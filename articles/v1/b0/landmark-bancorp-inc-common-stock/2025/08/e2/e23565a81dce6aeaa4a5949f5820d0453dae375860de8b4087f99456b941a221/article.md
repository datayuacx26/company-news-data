# Back-to-School Budgeting Tips for Families


Back-to-school time means back to the store for supplies, clothes, new tech, and even budgeting for food and activities as the kids head out of the house for another year. Excitement is high but so is stress for overwhelmed families managing their finances.

## How to set a realistic back-to-school budget


It’s easy to hit the store (or a website) and leave with hundreds of dollars of unnecessary items. Budgeting success begins by being precise with your shopping and knowing exactly what you need, what you intend to buy, and sticking to your list. To start, make a list of “must have” items your child will need as they head back to school. For example “clothes, school supplies, and accessories such as a new water bottle.” Next, set a budget for each item on your list. If you don’t intend to spend more than sixty dollars on new clothes, note that on your list. As you go about your shopping, in- store or online, hold fast and don’t go over your set budget.


Don’t forget about recurring school expenses like lunch money, extracurricular fees, sports gear, and school photos. These smaller costs can add up quickly if they’re not factored into your initial budget. Try setting aside a small amount each month to cover school expenses each year. Consider moving the money to a


[personal savings account,](http://banklandmark.com/personal/personal-savings-and-investment-accounts/) so it’s always there when you need it.


## Evaluate your list


Now that you have your list of back-to-school items, do a thorough evaluation of what you may already have before you head out to the store. There may be existing school supplies piled up in the basement or hand-me-down clothes you could pass down from one sibling to another, bypassing the need for additional cost. Money saving tip: Gather gently used clothes your child has outgrown and take them to a resale shop, adding money to your back-to-school budget.


## Money-saving tips


List in hand, you’re almost ready to head out the door and shop for your back-to-school items. First, look for deals and sales to help make your dollar go further. Here are a few ways to help you find cost-savings.


- Shop stores like Costco or Sam’s Club to gain savings on bulk items.


- Search for resale items on sites like eBay or Facebook Marketplace or even second hand stores.


- Ask friends about “swapping” items and clothing, passing down clothing from younger to older kids for money-savings and sustainability.


- Keep an eye out for “tax free weekends” and watch for coupons in the newspaper or online.

## Plan to stay on budget all year long


As the school year begins, look ahead to the full year of expenses. Go back through last year’s calendar and see what and where you spent money throughout the year. (ie. Halloween costumes, Christmas presents for teachers, etc). Make a budgeting calendar, planning for school expenses each month, so you’ll be prepared and have money set aside when the time comes.


## Involve the kids in budgeting and money decisions


Creating healthy financial behaviors starts by inviting kids into budgeting decisions early to help them understand the value of money. Take the list you’ve created so far and sit down with your child, telling them how much money is in your back-to-school budget and let them make a few adjustments, allowing for things they want or may not need. That way, kids feel a part of the process and may even contribute some of their own money or ideas for back-to-school budgeting.
