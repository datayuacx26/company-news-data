## How Can You Protect Your Home Computer?


Your home computer is often the first line of defense against cyber threats while protecting your financial information. Start by installing[reputable antivirus and anti-malware software](https://privsec.harvard.edu/choosing-personal-anti-virus-software) , as these tools detect and block potential threats before they compromise your system. Additionally, enable your firewall to provide an extra layer of protection by filtering incoming and outgoing traffic.


Regular backups are another crucial aspect of computer security. Back up your files to an external hard drive or a secure cloud storage service. If your system is compromised, having a backup ensures you won’t lose essential data. Remember, maintaining security in a computer begins with proactive measures to keep threats at bay.


## 1. Look out for Common Scams and Phishing Attempts


Phishing is one of the[most common cyber threats](https://www.banklandmark.com/blog/common-types-of-identity-theft/) targeting individuals. These scams often come in the form of emails, text messages, or phone calls pretending to be from legitimate sources. The goal is to trick you into revealing sensitive information, such as passwords, credit card details, or your social security number (SSN).


To spot a scam, look for red flags such as urgent language, misspellings, or suspicious links. If an email or message asks for personal information or directs you to a website, verify its authenticity by contacting the organization directly. Being vigilant can prevent you from becoming a victim of these schemes and getting into financial trouble.


## 2. Use Strong Passwords


Weak passwords are a gateway for hackers. To enhance your online security,[create strong and unique passwords](https://www.banklandmark.com/blog/online-banking-how-to-make-a-strong-password/) for each of your accounts. A strong password combines uppercase and lowercase letters, numbers, and special characters. Avoid using easily guessed information like your name or birthdate, opting instead for random phrases or strings of characters.


Consider using a password manager to generate and store complex passwords securely. This tool helps you maintain strong passwords without remembering them all, reducing the risk of reusing or forgetting them.


## 3. Multifactor Authentication (MFA)


Multifactor authentication (MFA) adds an extra layer of security to your accounts by requiring multiple forms of verification. For example, in addition to entering your password, you may need to provide a code sent to your phone or approve a login attempt through an app.


Enabling MFA makes it significantly harder for hackers to access your accounts, even if they have your password. Many banks and online services now offer two-factor authentication and similar services, and activating it is a simple yet effective way to enhance your cybersecurity.


## 4. Be Wary of Public WiFi


Public WiFi networks, such as those in cafes or airports, are convenient but often unsecured. Hackers can easily intercept data transmitted over these networks, potentially gaining access to your sensitive information.


To stay safe,[avoid using public WiFi to access](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/are-public-wi-fi-networks-safe-what-you-need-know)[financial accounts](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/are-public-wi-fi-networks-safe-what-you-need-know) or enter personal information. If you must use these networks, consider using a virtual private network (VPN) to encrypt your internet connection, ensuring your data remains secure.


## 5. Keep Devices and Apps Up-to-Date


Software updates often include patches for security vulnerabilities. Failing to update your devices and apps leaves them exposed to potential exploits. To maintain optimal security, check for updates regularly and install them promptly.


This practice applies to computers, smartphones, tablets, and any other devices connected to the internet. Updating everything ensures protection against the latest threats.
