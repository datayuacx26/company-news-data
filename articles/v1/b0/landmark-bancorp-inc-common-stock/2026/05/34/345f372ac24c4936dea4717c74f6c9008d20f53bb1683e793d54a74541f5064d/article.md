# Building a Business That Lasts: Succession Planning, Leadership, and Long-Term Growth

# Building a Business That Lasts: Succession Planning, Leadership, and Long-Term Growth


For many business owners, building a successful company is only part of the challenge.


The harder question often comes later:


How do you create something that continues to thrive beyond the founder?


For Scott Rosemann, that question has shaped much of his professional journey. As a second-generation leader at[Rosemann & Associates, P.C.](https://rosemann.com/?utm_source=chatgpt.com) , Scott has experienced firsthand what it looks like to step into a family business while preserving the values, culture, and relationships that made it successful in the first place.


And for other business owners thinking about succession planning, leadership transition, or long-term growth, he believes the process starts earlier than most people realize.


## A Business Built Through Relationships


Rosemann & Associates, P.C. was founded in 1987 by Scott’s father, Dan, during a difficult economic period marked by recession and tax reform changes.


At the time, many banks and developers were navigating distressed projects and uncertain futures. Through that work, Dan built a business focused not only on technical expertise, but on trust, relationships, and follow-through.


Those values became the foundation of the company.


Today, Rosemann & Associates operates offices in Kansas City, St. Louis, Denver, and Atlanta, employing more than 60 people with a primary focus on multifamily housing, including affordable housing, workforce housing, senior living, and student housing.


But according to Scott, growth alone has never been the goal.


## Succession Planning Starts with Culture


Many business owners think succession planning begins when retirement enters the conversation.


Scott sees it differently.


“I think it was much more modeled than stated,” he said when reflecting on the values his father passed down through the business.


For Scott, leadership transition wasn’t about inheriting a title. It was about learning how to lead by watching how relationships were handled, how decisions were made, and how people were treated long before he formally joined the company in 2008.


That experience shaped one of the biggest lessons he would offer other business owners: Culture cannot be created overnight during a transition. It has to be built consistently over time.


For companies preparing for future leadership changes, that means focusing not only on operational continuity, but also on the values and philosophies that define the organization itself.


## Building with the Long Term in Mind


Rosemann & Associates specializes in housing, but Scott is quick to point out they are not simply designing buildings.


“We focus on homes,” he explained. “Not just buildings.”


That mindset influences how the company approaches both design and business strategy.


“It’s where Christmas happens. It’s where birthdays happen. It’s where you sit and cry with people too,” Scott said. “It makes what we do a lot more personal.”


For Scott, thinking long term means understanding the real people impacted by every business decision. It also means creating a company built to evolve through changing markets, economic cycles, and future generations of leadership.


That perspective has helped position the company for continued growth, even amid current commercial real estate headwinds.


## Why the Right Banking Partner Matters


As businesses grow and leadership evolves, Scott says having trusted partners becomes increasingly important.


That is one reason Rosemann & Associates values its relationship with Landmark National Bank.


“You can always pick up the phone and get ahold of someone,” Scott shared. “If you have a question or need help, they’ll find the resources to help.”


For Scott, that accessibility matters, especially for growing businesses navigating major decisions.


“They’re very personal and approachable and at the same time have the resources and capability of a much larger bank,” he said.


It is a balance Rosemann & Associates strives for as well: maintaining the personalized service and culture of a smaller firm while continuing to scale strategically.


And for business owners preparing for future growth or succession planning, Scott believes relationships like those matter more than ever.


“It’s the ability to do business on a handshake and know the person you’re sitting across the table from is going to have your back,” he said.


## Advice for Business Owners Thinking About the Future


As Scott looks toward the next chapter for Rosemann & Associates, including continued growth over the coming years, his philosophy on leadership remains remarkably simple.


“Doing the right thing is never wrong,” he said. “Sometimes the right thing has the largest consequence, especially in business. But doing the right thing is never wrong.”


For business owners navigating growth, leadership transitions, or succession planning, that advice may be more relevant than ever.


Because long-term success is not just about building a profitable company.


It’s about building one strong enough to last.
