# Wire Fraud: What It Is, Why It’s So Common, and How to Avoid It


Wire transfers are fast, convenient, and widely used, especially during real estate closings, making business payments, and or conducting large personal transactions. Unfortunately, this also makes them a prime target for scammers.


Understanding what wire fraud is, why it’s on the rise, and how to protect yourself can help you stay confident when sending funds. At Landmark National Bank, we want you to feel informed, cautious, and empowered to verify your financial moves before acting.


## What Is Wire Fraud?


Wire fraud occurs when a scammer tricks someone into sending money to a fraudulent account. Because wire transfers move quickly — often within moments — recovering funds can be extremely difficult once they’re sent.


Most wire transfer scams involve impersonation. Criminals pretend to be someone you trust, such as:


- A real estate agent or title company.
- A vendor or supplier.
- A business partner.
- A company executive aka CEO fraud.


The scammer’s goal is simple: convince you to send money before you realize something is wrong.


## Why Wire Fraud Is So Common


Wire fraud has steadily grown in recent years due to increased complexity in tactics, including:


### Business Email Compromise


Scammers hack or mimic legitimate email accounts, making their messages look authentic. This is one of the most common forms of business email compromise and often leads to invoice redirection scams.


### Impersonation Tactics


Fraudsters study real transactions, communication styles, and timelines. They send messages that feel familiar, often right before a payment is due.


### Urgency and Pressure


Many scams rely on last‑minute changes, such as:


- “Use this updated account number.”
- “We need this payment today.”
- “Your closing will be delayed unless you act now.”


These tactics are designed to make you move quickly without verifying.


## Common Wire Transfer Scams


Here are the scenarios where wire transfer scams most often occur:


### Real Estate Wire Fraud


Scammers pose as your real estate agent or title company and send “updated” wiring instructions for your down payment or closing funds. Because these transfers are large, the financial impact can be devastating.


### Business Invoice Redirection


A business receives what looks like a legitimate invoice; however, the bank account has been changed. Small businesses are frequent targets of this type of scam.


### Executive Impersonation


A scammer pretends to be a CEO or manager and urgently requests a confidential wire transfer.


## How to Avoid Wire Fraud (Wire Transfer Safety Tips)


You can significantly reduce your risk with a few simple habits:


### 1. Always Verify Wiring Instructions


Call the sender using a phone number you already know — not one listed in the email. This is the most important step in[how to avoid wire fraud](https://www.banklandmark.com/privacy-security/) .


### 2. Never Rely Solely on Email


Email is the most common entry point for business email compromise. Always confirm instructions verbally.


### 3. Slow Down Before Sending Funds


Scammers rely on urgency and creating a lapse in judgement. Take a moment to double‑check details.


### 4. Look Closely at Email Addresses


A single letter or number may be altered. Example: “.co” instead of “.com.”


### 5. Be Cautious of Last‑Minute Changes


Any unexpected update to wiring instructions is a major red flag.


### 6. Contact Landmark Immediately If Something Seems Wrong


Quick action gives you the best chance of stopping or recovering a fraudulent transfer.
