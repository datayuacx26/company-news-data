# Credit Card vs. Debit Card Fraud: What’s the Difference and Who’s Responsible?

# Credit Card vs. Debit Card Fraud: What’s the Difference and Who’s Responsible?


When an unfamiliar charge appears on your account, it’s natural to feel stressed or confused. One of the most common questions customers ask is how credit card vs debit card fraud differs, and how to respond in each situation.


At Landmark National Bank, we want you to feel informed, reassured, and supported every step of the way. Understanding what differentiates these two types of card fraud can help you respond quickly, protect your money, and feel more confident if fraud ever occurs.


## How Debit Card Fraud Works (Debit Card Fraud Liability Basics)


Debit card fraud happens when someone uses your card or card number to make unauthorized purchases or withdrawals. Because[debit cards](https://www.banklandmark.com/personal/additional-personal-services/visa-debit-cards/) pull funds directly from your checking account, fraudulent charges can temporarily affect your available balance.


Here’s what to know about debit card fraud liability:


- Your liability depends on how quickly you report the unauthorized charge.
- Reporting within two business days offers the strongest protection under federal law.
- Waiting longer may increase your liability and delay reimbursement.
- While investigations are underway, your account balance may be impacted until the bank completes its review.


This is why regularly monitoring your account and immediately reporting suspicious activity are so important.


## How Credit Card Fraud Works (Credit Card Fraud Protection)


Credit card fraud works differently than debit card fraud. Because credit cards use borrowed funds, fraudulent charges don’t come directly out of your checking account.


Key points about[credit card fraud protection](https://www.banklandmark.com/privacy-security/) :


- Most credit card issuers offer zero liability for unauthorized charges, meaning you’re not responsible for fraudulent charges made with your card—as long as you report the activity promptly.
- Fraudulent transactions typically do not affect your bank balance.
- Disputes are handled through the credit card provider, not your checking account.


This difference in how funds are handled is one reason some customers choose to use credit cards for online purchases or travel.


## Debit vs Credit Security: Key Differences


Here’s a quick comparison to help clarify debit vs credit security:


Impact on your money:


- **Debit** : Fraud affects your checking account balance.
- **Credit** : Fraud affects borrowed funds, not your cash.


Reporting timelines:


- **Debit** : Faster reporting = stronger protection.
- **Credit** : Zero liability policies typically apply.


Investigation process:


- **Debit** : Bank investigates and may temporarily hold funds.
- **Credit** : Issuer removes the charge while investigating.


Understanding these differences can help you decide which card to use in different situations.


## Why Quick Reporting Matters (Unauthorized Charges: What to Do)


If you notice a charge you don’t recognize, acting quickly is essential. Here’s what to do about unauthorized charges:


- Contact your bank immediately.
- Review recent transactions for additional suspicious activity.
- Update your card credentials if needed.
- Set up account alerts to stay informed going forward.


Prompt reporting helps protect your account and ensures you receive the strongest possible protections under federal law.


## Prevention Basics: How to Reduce Fraud Risk


A few simple habits can help you stay ahead of fraud:


- Monitor your accounts regularly.
- Set up transaction alerts.
- Use secure payment methods.
- Avoid public Wi‑Fi for financial activity.
- Keep card information private.


These steps strengthen your overall protection and make it easier to report debit card fraud or credit card fraud quickly if something seems off.
