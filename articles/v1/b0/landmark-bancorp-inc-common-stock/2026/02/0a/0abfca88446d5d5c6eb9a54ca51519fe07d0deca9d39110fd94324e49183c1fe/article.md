# How Rising Interest Rates Can Affect Your HELOC


If you’re a homeowner, interest rates are always top of mind. And if you have a Home Equity Line of Credit (HELOC) or are considering opening one, interest rates are even more important to understand and manage.


At Landmark National Bank, we believe financial clarity leads to better outcomes. Below, we’ll help you understand how rising interest rates affect your HELOC interest rates and what you can do to be prepared.


## What Is a HELOC?


A home equity line of credit, also known as HELOC is a second mortgage you can take out on your home that grants you a revolving line of credit. When you qualify for a HELOC, you will be granted a spending limit that you can borrow against as often as you need to. Your HELOC rate will be dependent on the value of your home and will come with variable interest rates. Many people use the funds from their HELOC for debt consolidation, home renovations, education/tuition, medical bills or the acquisition of another property. HELOCs are typically not used to fund everyday expenses.


A HELOC differs from a traditional loan because it offers flexibility. Rather than receiving funds in one lump sum, a HELOC allows you to access money as you need it during the draw period. You can borrow, repay, and borrow again—similar to how a revolving line of credit works—while only paying interest on the amount you use.


Most HELOCs are variable-rate loan products, which means your HELOC interest rate can change over time. This is one of the key differences between a HELOC and other types of home equity loans.

## What Happens to Your HELOC When Interest Rates Rise?


Variable rate HELOCs are more sensitive to rising interest rates, but also offer advantages and flexibility home owners need. When interest rates increase, the most noticeable change for HELOC borrowers is often the monthly payment.


Here’s how rising interest rates can affect a HELOC:


1.


### Your Interest Rate May Increase


If your HELOC has a variable rate, an increase in the benchmark index (such as the prime rate) can cause your interest rate to rise. The timing and amount depend on the terms of your agreement.


1.


### HELOC payment changes


As interest rates increase, the interest portion of your payment also increases. This can result in higher monthly payments, especially if you’ve borrowed a significant portion of your available credit.


1.


### Your overall borrowing cost can change


Even small rate increases can add up over time. Understanding how rate changes affect your total interest paid can help you plan more effectively. It’s important to note that rate increases are typically incremental, not sudden or unpredictable. HELOC terms outline how often rates can change and whether there are caps on how high they can go.


Rising interest rates don’t have to be overwhelming. At Landmark National Bank, our team helps you understand how HELOC payment changes affect your specific situation so you feel confident and informed no matter the situation.


## How Often Can HELOC Interest Rates Change?


This is one of the most common questions homeowners ask. Most HELOC interest rates adjust monthly, based on changes to the underlying index. Your specific HELOC outlines these details, which is why reviewing your terms, or discussing them with a banker, can be helpful, especially during periods of rising interest rates. Your lending partner may include safeguards to protect against rising interest rates and HELOC payment changes. These include:


- Rate caps that limit how much the rate can increase at one time


- Lifetime caps that prevent the rate from exceeding a certain maximum


- Clear disclosures so you know what to expect


If you have questions about your HELOC, a trusted banker can help you understand your terms so you stay in control of your finances.


## Why Rising Interest Rates Don’t Automatically Mean Trouble


It’s natural to feel concerned when you hear about rising interest rates, especially if you have a variable-rate HELOC. But for many homeowners, a HELOC remains a flexible financial tool, acting as a reliable line of credit to manage large or unexpected expenses.


Why a HELOC may work for you:


- You only pay interest on what you use


- You can borrow as needed, rather than taking a lump sum


- You maintain access to funds for ongoing or unexpected expenses


- You can repay and reborrow during the draw period


## Ways to Prepare for or Manage HELOC Rate Increases


While you can’t control market interest rates, you can take steps to manage how rate changes affect your finances.


1.


### Review your current HELOC balance


Understanding how much you’ve borrowed, and how much remains available, helps you evaluate your exposure to rate changes.


1.


### Build rate changes into your budget


Planning for potential payment increases can reduce stress. Even setting aside a small buffer can help you stay confident if rates rise.


1.


### Pay more than the minimum when possible


Making additional principal payments can reduce your balance faster, which lowers the amount of interest you pay over time.


1.


### Use your HELOC strategically


HELOCs are often best used for long-term value investments, like home improvements, rather than everyday spending. Limiting your HELOC funds to these kinds of expenses can help you manage and pay down your loan.


1.


### Talk with a banker about your options


If you’re concerned about rising interest rates, work with a banking partner to understand alternatives, including fixed-rate options or repayment strategies.


## HELOCs vs. Other Borrowing Options in a Rising Rate Environment


When interest rates rise, homeowners often compare HELOCs to other types of loans. Each option has its place, depending on your needs.


- **HELOC:** Flexible access to funds, variable rate, interest paid only on what you use


- **Home equity loan:** Fixed rate, predictable payments, lump-sum funding


- **Personal loan:** Fixed rate, shorter terms, typically higher interest rates


- **Credit cards:** Convenient but often carry significantly higher interest rates
