An explainer video is the format you reach for when your product cannot be photographed and will not be read about. Software, financial products, medical devices, anything that happens inside a server or a sealed tank: a camera is useless and a landing page full of text goes unread.


The problem is that "explainer video company in USA" is one of the least trustworthy queries in this category. The phrase appears in the page titles of studios based in Ontario and Karnataka, and the roundups ranking for it repeat those studios without checking. We went looking for American animation studios and spent most of the research time disqualifying candidates.


What follows is the list that survived. We put ourselves at the top and then spent the rest of this piece marking out which briefs belong with a specialist studio instead. On a character-led film or a broadcast campaign, one of the nine below is the better call.


Treat the video as a working asset rather than a one-off expense, an argument we make at length about[design for advertising](https://www.awesomic.com/blog/5-reasons-you-should-invest-in-your-designs-for-advertising) , because the studios that price well on a single film often price badly on the variants you will need six months later.


## What counts as an explainer video company


The category is broader than it looks, and picking the wrong kind of supplier is the most common reason a project runs to double the estimate.


A dedicated explainer studio makes narrated 2D animation, 60 to 120 seconds, aimed at making an abstract product legible. That is the core product. Around it sit whiteboard and scribe animation, which trades polish for a sense of being walked through something; product demo animation built from real interface recordings; character animation, where the story carries more weight than the feature list; and motion graphics for social cutdowns.


Each has a different cost base. A whiteboard video and a character-driven brand film share almost no production steps, which is why a brief that just says "animated explainer" invites wildly different quotes from equally honest studios.


Decide which of those five formats applies, settle the runtime, and name the channel it plays on, all before you contact anyone. A clear brief is worth more than a longer shortlist, and it is the part most buyers skip. Our own[creative brief guide](https://www.awesomic.com/blog/crafting-a-good-creative-brief-unlock-business-results-faster) walks through the questions a studio will otherwise have to guess at.


## How we verified this list, and what it disqualified


Every competing roundup on this keyword republishes directory rankings without opening the sites. We did the opposite: we resolved each candidate's real domain by search, loaded it, and read the company's own contact or about page for a street address.


That single check removed more candidates than any quality judgment did.


**Hatch Studios** is in Toronto. Its own about page describes its founder as one of Canada's leading animation creative directors, and its client list includes The Heart and Stroke Foundation of Canada. It ranks on page one for the American keyword.


**MotionGility** publishes a Canadian address on Ellesmere Road in Scarborough, Ontario, while its page title reads "Best Explainer Video Company in USA." **Epic Video Factory** lists Dundas Street West in Toronto. **Studiotale** titles its homepage "Animated Explainer Videos Company in Bangalore, India." **Punchy Studio** , which appears in US-facing explainer searches, runs on a .com.au domain and calls itself Australia's leading visual communications agency.


Three more candidates failed for different reasons. **Grumo Media** publishes no location anywhere on its homepage, about page or contact page, so we could not confirm it either way and dropped it rather than guess. **The Draw Shop** now runs under the banner "Powered by Explainify," meaning it is no longer an independent choice. **Explainly** blocks automated access from three separate directions, so we could not verify its pages the way we verified everyone else's.


Most lists of the top explainer video companies in USA never run this check at all. None of this makes those studios bad, and several are excellent, so a Toronto animator may well be the right hire. But an article promising American companies should contain American companies, and a buyer who needs a US entity for procurement, contracting or time-zone reasons deserves to know before the first call.


The list runs to ten rather than the usual twelve because padding it would have meant putting a Toronto studio in a USA roundup.

## How to choose between them


Start with the format, not the vendor. Once you know whether you need a whiteboard walkthrough or a character film, half the list disqualifies itself.


Then apply four filters in order:


1. Ask for a street address and confirm it on the company's own site before the first call.
2. Watch three finished videos in your format, not a showreel of best moments from twenty projects.
3. Ask who writes the script, and what happens to the timeline if you supply your own.
4. Get the revision policy in writing, including what a round is and what triggers a new charge.


On length, there is better evidence than the usual unsourced claims about attention spans. Guo, Kim and Rubin analyzed 6.9 million video-watching sessions across four edX courses and found that shorter videos are substantially more engaging, and that informal, direct delivery outperformed polished studio production. The finding comes from educational video, but the mechanism is the same one your explainer depends on.


The practical read is to cut before you polish. A tight 70-second video from a mid-tier studio will usually outperform a beautiful 3-minute one, and it costs less to make. Deciding between commissioning this and building an in-house function is the same calculation covered in our comparison of[creative service models](https://www.awesomic.com/blog/integrated-creative-services) .


## Making the shortlist call


Three questions settle most of these decisions faster than a procurement process will.


Is this one video or the first of several? A single flagship film belongs with a specialist studio. A steady stream of product videos, cutdowns and the design that surrounds them belongs on a retainer or a subscription, because per-project pricing punishes volume.


Does the story need craft or clarity? Nathan Love and Trollbäck sit at the craft end, TruScribe and Next Day Animations at the clarity end, and most of the list in between. Buying craft when you needed clarity is the most expensive mistake in this category.


And does it actually need to be American? If procurement, contracting or security review requires a US entity, the verification above matters a great deal. If it does not, you have a much wider field, and some of the studios we disqualified for location are genuinely strong. ## FAQs


### How much does an explainer video cost in the USA?


Most American studios quote between $8,000 and $25,000 for a standard 60 to 90-second 2D animated explainer. Whiteboard animation runs lower, often $3,000 to $10,000, while character-driven or 3D work starts around $25,000 and climbs past $100,000 for technical animation. The two biggest variables are whether you supply a finished script and how many revision rounds are included. Subscription models price differently, charging monthly rather than per project, which changes the math once you need more than two videos a year.


### How do I check that an explainer video company is really based in the USA?


Open the company's own contact or about page and look for a street address, not a phone number or a "serving clients nationwide" line. Directory listings and roundups routinely describe firms as American when their own sites publish addresses in Toronto, Bangalore or Sydney. If a company publishes no address at all, treat that as a missing answer rather than a neutral one. This single check disqualified five candidates while we were building this list.


### How long should an explainer video be?


Between 60 and 90 seconds for most marketing uses. Research by Guo, Kim and Rubin covering 6.9 million viewing sessions found shorter videos substantially more engaging, and that holds across formats. If your script will not fit, the usual cause is trying to cover several audiences in one video rather than genuinely needing the time. Two short videos aimed at two audiences almost always outperform one long video aimed at both.


### Should I hire an animation studio or use a design subscription?


Hire a studio when you need one flagship piece with a distinctive visual signature, particularly character animation or a brand film. Choose a subscription when animation is one of several ongoing needs and you also require landing pages, social cutdowns, decks and brand assets. The deciding factor is volume rather than quality: per-project pricing works against you as soon as the second and third videos appear, while a monthly model works against you if you genuinely only ever need one.


### Who writes the script for an explainer video?


It varies by studio, and it is worth asking early because script development often accounts for a third of the schedule. Some companies include scriptwriting and treat it as the core of the engagement. Others expect a finished script and will quote a shorter timeline if you have one. Denario handed us a script it had already written and we produced the video from it, which compressed the project considerably. If you have strong internal writers, say so during scoping, because it should change both the price and the delivery date.
