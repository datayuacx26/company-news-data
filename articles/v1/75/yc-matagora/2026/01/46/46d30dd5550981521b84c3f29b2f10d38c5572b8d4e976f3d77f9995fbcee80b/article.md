# Shopify POS vs Square, Clover & Lightspeed: What’s Best for Small Businesses in 2026?

Choosing the best POS system for a small business isn’t just about taking payments. It affects your inventory, reporting, marketing, staff management, and customer experience — every single day.


We reviewed the top POS systems for small businesses in 2026: **Shopify POS, Square, Clover, and Lightspeed** .


Here’s what matters.


## 1. Shopify POS: Built for Unified Commerce


Shopify POS is the only platform that truly unifies **online and in-store sales in one system** .


That means:


-


One dashboard for ecommerce + retail


-


Real-time inventory sync across all locations


-


Unified customer profiles


-


Built-in marketing tools


-


Easy reporting across channels


Retailers using unified commerce platforms like Shopify report:


-


**22% lower total cost of ownership**


-


**25% lower annual software costs**


-


Faster implementation and fewer technical issues


Shopify is built to scale. Whether you run one store or 100 locations, the system grows with you.


## 2. Square POS: Simple but Limited


Square is popular for startups because it offers a free plan.


However:


-


Omnichannel capabilities are limited


-


Advanced reporting and integrations can require add-ons


-


Costs increase as transaction volume grows


-


Less scalable for multi-location retail


Square works for very small businesses — but many outgrow it.


## 3. Lightspeed POS: Feature-Rich but Complex


Lightspeed is designed for specialty retail with complex inventory.


However:


-


Higher monthly fees (starting around $89/month)


-


More complex setup


-


Online + in-store integration is not as seamless as Shopify


-


Higher total cost of ownership over time


It works — but often requires more management and higher investment.


## 4. Clover POS: Flexible Hardware, Fragmented System


Clover offers flexible hardware and basic retail features.


But:


-


Vendor-dependent contracts


-


Complex fee structures


-


Not truly unified for ecommerce + retail


-


Online integration often requires third-party tools


It’s functional, but not built for modern omnichannel retail growth.
