# Slashy vs Spark Mail: AI-Native vs Classic Email

Spark Mail and Slashy come from two different eras of email software. Spark, built by Readdle, started as a classic email client focused on a clean cross-platform experience across Mac, iOS, Android, and Windows, then layered AI features on top years later. Slashy was built AI-native from the first commit, with a proprietary memory system that adapts continuously based on emails you send, receive, and respond to. AI-draft acceptance climbs from around 30% on day 1 to over 80% by day 30 as the system learns your voice. This comparison breaks down how the two handle AI drafting, triage, follow-ups, calendar, team collaboration, free access, and price. For the broader picture, see[the AI email landscape in 2026](https://www.slashy.com/blog/the-ai-email-landscape-in-2026) .


## What's the difference between Slashy and Spark Mail?


The core difference is architecture and origin. Spark Mail is a classic email client. Readdle built it to be a polished, cross-platform inbox that works the same on iPhone, Android, Mac, and Windows, with features like Smart Inbox, snooze, send-later, and a shared team inbox. AI arrived later as an add-on: an assistant that can summarize threads and help compose, bolted onto an existing client.


Slashy was built AI-first. The memory system, drafting, auto-triage, follow-up tracking, and calendar sidebar were designed together as the foundation, not features stitched on after launch. A specialized memory agent learns your writing style, tone, recipients, response timing, and scheduling preferences from the emails you actually send and answer. The result is a client where the AI gets sharper the longer you use it, rather than a generic assistant sitting beside a traditional inbox.


Spark wins on breadth of platforms and a genuine free tier. Slashy wins on depth of AI: drafts in your voice, seven-label triage, and ghost-detection follow-ups that a classic client with bolted-on AI does not match.

## How does the AI compare?


Slashy's AI is the product, not an add-on. The in-house memory system learns continuously from emails you send, receive, and respond to. A specialized memory agent organizes those memories, so AI drafts match your tone, sentence structure, and how you talk to specific recipients. Acceptance climbs from around 30% on day 1 to over 80% by day 30 as the system refines its understanding. On top of drafting, Slashy auto-triages incoming mail into 7 labels, tracks whether recipients opened your email, and detects when a thread has gone quiet so it can remind you to follow up.


Spark's AI came later and is narrower. It can summarize long threads and help you compose a message, which is useful, but it is a general assistant rather than a memory system trained on your voice over weeks. Spark's compose help does not ramp toward your personal style the way a continuous memory system does, and it does not ship ghost-detection follow-ups or a learned seven-label triage model.

The practical gap shows up at volume. If you send 50 replies a day, a voice-trained draft that needs minimal editing saves real time, and ghost-detection follow-ups catch the threads that would otherwise slip. Spark's AI helps you write a single message faster, but it does not learn your patterns or watch your pipeline.


## Is Spark Mail free?


Yes. Spark Mail offers a genuine free tier, which is one of its clearest advantages over most AI email clients. The free plan covers core email across Mac, iOS, Android, and Windows, including Smart Inbox and basic features, at no cost. Paid tiers add more: Plus is $10/month and Pro is $20/month, unlocking higher limits and the fuller set of AI and collaboration features.


Slashy does not offer a free tier. Slashy Professional is $30/user/month month-to-month, or $300/year billed annually, with a 7-day free trial and no credit card required to start. The trade is straightforward. Spark gives you a free, cross-platform classic inbox. Slashy charges for an AI-native client where the memory system, voice-trained drafts, seven-label triage, and follow-up tracking are the core of the product. If price is the deciding factor and you mainly need a clean inbox, Spark's free tier is hard to beat. If the bottleneck is writing and chasing replies, Slashy's AI is what you are paying for.


## Which is better for founders?


For founders drowning in inbound, the answer usually comes down to whether your problem is organizing email or answering it. Spark's strengths help a small team share an inbox and stay tidy across devices. Its shared inbox and collaborative drafts let a founder and an assistant work the same mailbox, and the free tier keeps early-stage costs at zero.


Slashy is built for the founder whose bottleneck is the reply itself. The memory system drafts in your voice so you approve more than you compose, auto-triage into 7 labels surfaces what matters, the calendar sidebar lets you propose times without leaving the inbox, and ghost-detection follow-ups make sure a warm intro or investor thread does not die. Slashy ships a command palette for keyboard-driven control and a unified multi-inbox for founders running several accounts.


A founder who wants a free, cross-platform, collaborative inbox and does minimal AI drafting fits Spark. A founder buried in replies who wants AI that learns their voice and never lets a thread slip fits Slashy.

## Is Slashy secure?


Slashy maintains SOC 2 Type II and CASA Tier 2 compliance, with AES-256 encryption at rest and TLS 1.2+ in transit. Critically for an AI-native client, there is no AI training on your data and zero data retention from the AI providers Slashy uses, so your email content is not used to train models or stored by third-party LLMs.


Readdle, the maker of Spark, publishes its own privacy commitments for Spark Mail. Teams evaluating on a specific certification should confirm the current compliance posture for each product directly, since the relevant detail for an AI client is whether your content trains models. Slashy's documented position is that it does not.
