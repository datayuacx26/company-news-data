## Step 1: Connect Gmail to HubSpot and Set Your Logging Rules


Start by establishing the native connection between your inbox and your CRM. Most founders begin by installing the HubSpot Sales extension from the Chrome Web Store. This extension is the bridge that lets HubSpot see what is happening inside your Gmail compose window. Once installed, go to your HubSpot settings to define your global logging rules. Skip this step and you will be clicking the Log and Track checkboxes for every single email you send. That is a recipe for missing data.


Go to HubSpot settings, select Objects, then Activities, and find the Email Logging tab. Here you can specify which domains and email addresses should never be logged. You likely want to exclude your internal company domain and personal contacts to keep your CRM clean. The native integration works, with one practical constraint: it runs through the browser extension, so logging depends on that extension being installed and active wherever you send from.


Contact matching is worth checking too. If a prospect replies from a different address than the one on their HubSpot record, the thread can end up unassociated. You can reduce this by enabling the setting that automatically creates new contacts for unknown recipients, but this can cause CRM bloat. The goal is to establish this baseline connection so your[AI email for sales teams](https://www.slashy.com/blog/ai-email-for-sales-teams-2026) has a data source to pull from. This native setup is the foundation, but it is not the complete solution for high-volume, founder-led sales.

## What to Do If Emails Still Aren't Logging (Troubleshooting)


If you have followed these steps and your emails still are not appearing in HubSpot, the issue is usually a configuration conflict. First, check your HubSpot Never Log list. If your prospect's domain is on that list, HubSpot will ignore every interaction no matter how you send it. Sales operators often accidentally add common domains like gmail.com to this list, which breaks logging for a large portion of their leads. Keep your exclusion list limited to your own internal team and perhaps investors or personal contacts.


Second, verify that you do not have multiple Gmail extensions fighting for control. If you have the native HubSpot extension, a different CRM tracker, and Slashy all trying to manage the same thread, they can interfere with each other. Disable other tracking extensions and let Slashy handle the primary logging and tracking. This reduces the risk of duplicate logs or missing data. Also confirm your HubSpot account has not hit its technical API limits, though this is rare for early-to-mid-stage teams.


Finally, check the sync status inside Slashy settings. If your Google OAuth token has expired, the connection between Gmail and the AI layer may be broken. A simple re-authorization usually fixes this. If you continue to see gaps, look at the HubSpot activity logs to see if the emails are being received but not associated with the correct contact. This often happens when the contact record in HubSpot does not have the right email address listed. Clean data in your CRM is the prerequisite for a functional integration.
