# How growing biotechs can use software to drive process improvements in the lab

Are you setting your lab up for order or chaos? Progressing therapeutic candidates to IND submission and into the market involves navigating numerous sophisticated stages of the R&D life cycle—each generating an abundance of data and opportunities for optimization. With the right software, growing biotech companies can organize any scientific workflow—from cell therapy to fermentation—into trackable stages and efficiently test new variations to optimize your outputs and hit key milestones sooner.


Based on our experience with hundreds of life science R&D companies, we’ve identified the key software processes that drive laboratory improvements—described here so your team can accelerate the path to a successful IND filing and beyond.


## Organize and standardize your processes and experiments


Growing biotech companies face the transition from smaller scale discovery stages to tightly regulated bioprocessing and manufacturing stages. This transition brings new complex processes, new team members, and more departments, all of which need to fit seamlessly together to efficiently drive products forward and maintain compliance and safety. It’s no surprise that biotech companies are seeking digital solutions that can help them document, standardize, and contextualize processes to avoid chaos and boost productivity throughout R&D.


Here’s how you can use software to organize and standardize your processes and experiments:


-


Standardize notebook entry creation with pre-built or customizable templates


-


Register new samples directly from entries and link those samples to results data


-


Keep data organized by automating data entry through integrations and capturing results on a stage-by-stage basis


-


Structure experiment branching based on different inputs and conditions


-


Build workflow templates to align process execution across your organization

## Compare results across multiple runs of the same workflow


You can ensure the consistency of your outputs and optimize your workflow by comparing the results of multiple bioprocessing, purification, and analytical runs.


To do this efficiently, use software that unifies your entire workflow, centralizes your data, and integrates with third-party data visualization and analysis tools (e.g., Tableau and SpotFire). This will empower your organization to track the progress of any R&D process and compare historical data to identify areas of improvement. You’ll also be able to iterate your processes and quickly determine the impact of new variations to optimize your workflow, eliminate bottlenecks, and flexibly adjust processes to support evolving research needs.


Here are some ways you can use software to compare results across multiple runs of the same workflow:


-


Build custom dashboards to visualize and monitor progress and pinpoint areas for improvement


-


Monitor and visualize results in real time in order to identify, test, and assess new process variations


-


Keep track of process variations as you modify workflow templates so you can compare results across experiments, studies, and programs

## Coordinate work and resource utilization


Whether you are increasing the capacity of your bioreactors, expanding your portfolio of pipeline products, or initiating the manufacturing stages of your first therapeutic, maintaining productivity is a primary goal. However, with so many complex processes in life science R&D, a multitude of scientists, tasks, and resources need to be coordinated to establish and sustain a smooth-running workflow. Fortunately, modern software tools can help manage day-to-day activities and forecasting, even for research on the most cutting-edge therapeutic modalities.


The ideal software solution will give you insight into every active process, including the current status of each project, who’s working on each task, and the resources used and needed for each stage. Sophisticated software, such as Benchling, puts this information at your fingertips and offers seamless interfaces between research, process development, and manufacturing teams, so you can keep your projects on track and avoid unnecessary delays.


Here’s how you can use software to coordinate work and resource utilization:


-


Model any experimental process—even for complex large molecule R&D—as a digital, step-by-step workflow and orchestrate work across functions, teams, and instruments


-


Analyze long-term data to forecast resource requirements and identify and resolve bottlenecks to improve efficiency for each stage of a project


-


Establish repeatable processes to standardize data for scaling and automating experiments, surfacing deeper insights, and in preparation for regulatory filings
