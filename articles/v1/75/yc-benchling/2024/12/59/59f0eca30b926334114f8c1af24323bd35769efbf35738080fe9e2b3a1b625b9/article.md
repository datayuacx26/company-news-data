## What is sample management?


Sample management is a vital part of scientific R&D, that if done right, has the potential to improve product quality and patient health. It encompasses the processes and practices involved in handling samples of materials, products, or substances throughout their lifecycle.


While these practices may vary from company to company, common sample management tasks include:


-


**Sample collection:** This initial step establishes standardized procedures for collecting and documenting samples, including origin, collection method, and any relevant environmental conditions.


-


**Labeling and identification:** Using labels to assign identifying information, as well as the use of barcodes, ensures that each unique sample can be tracked in the lab and you’re not spending precious minutes trying to decipher half-smudged Sharpie.


-


**Storage:** Storing samples under appropriate conditions preserves integrity, while optimizing storage location means you’re not digging through the lab freezer on a regular basis.


-


**Tracking and analysis:** Consistent documentation of the handling of samples provides a clear record of sample movement. When analyzing samples, it’s important to follow protocols to drive consistency with results and insights so that you don’t have to repeat experiments when data is somehow misplaced.


-


**Quality control and compliance:** It’s a prerequisite for effective management that samples maintain standards for quality, commonly achieved through regular audits. Regulatory and industry standards such as Good Laboratory Practice (GLP) provide guidance for creating such conditions.


-


**Disposal:** Safe and compliant disposal, including records of disposed samples, ensures visibility and traceability for all parties and prevents you from having to sift through expired samples and reagents.

## Principles for effective sample management


It’s no question that managing samples effectively is critical for accurate, reliable experimental results. By sticking to the following principles, teams can ensure that their sample management practices support the integrity of their R&D activities — and avoid a dreaded experimental redo from a bad or lost sample.


1.


**Accurate labels:** Ensuring consistent, clear labels for all samples eliminates potential errors, while aiding in tracking and retrieval downstream. Best practice for identifying information includes criteria such as sample ID, date, time and any other relevant descriptors.


2.


**Standard operating procedures (SOPs)** : Establishing and adhering to standardized processes for each phase of sample management helps to further avoid inaccuracies.


3.


**Inventory and data management** : Integrating sample data with other data sources allows for more robust analysis. And by using inventory management systems to keep track of sample quantities, locations, and usage, the chances of sample loss or redundancy is greatly reduced.


4.


**Collaboration and communication** : Building the avenues for effective communication and collaboration across and within teams strengthens coordination for improved innovation.


5.


**Continuous improvement** : In order to scale your sample management practices, continuous review must occur. By auditing existing practices, opportunities arise to incorporate new technologies, address issues, and improve efficiency. Listening to your team, and seeking feedback from regular users, will contribute to the overall management process.
