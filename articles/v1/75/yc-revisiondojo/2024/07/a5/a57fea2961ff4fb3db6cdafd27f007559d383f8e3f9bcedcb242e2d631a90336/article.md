### The 2025 IB Chemistry map (so you don’t cram the wrong thing)


The 2025 structure is easiest to think of as two big stories:


### Structure: what matter is and how we model it


-


Particulate nature of matter, nuclear atom, electron configurations, the mole, ideal gases


-


Ionic, covalent, metallic bonding; then how models become materials


-


Periodic table and organic functional groups (classification)


### Reactivity: why reactions happen and what controls them


-


Energetics: enthalpy, energy cycles, fuels; plus entropy/spontaneity (HL)


-


Amount, rate, and extent: stoichiometry, kinetics, equilibrium ideas


-


Mechanisms: acid-base, redox, and organic pathways


When you cram **IB Chemistry** , this map matters because it tells you what compounds into everything else. The mole and bonding are like grammar. If your grammar is weak, every sentence costs double.

### The 7-day IB Chemistry cram plan (with realistic daily targets)


This plan assumes you have school, other subjects, and a human need for sleep. If you have more time, stretch the blocks. If you have less time, keep the structure and compress the notes.


### Day 1: Build the machine (diagnose, don’t drift)


**Goal:** Stop guessing. Start targeting.


-


Skim the syllabus map and mark: green (ok), yellow (shaky), red (panic).


-


Do a **30-45 minute mixed diagnostic** from a question bank.


-


Create a “Mistake Log” with 5 columns: topic, mistake type, correct method, why you missed it, what you’ll do next time.


Best place to practice immediately:[Questionbank](https://www.revisiondojo.com/feature/questionbank) , then use **AI Chat** to ask *why* the mark scheme wants that exact phrasing.


**Night task (15 minutes):** Start your one-pager (constants, key equations, recurring steps).


### Day 2: Structure sprint (atomic, mole, bonding)


**Goal:** Make basic questions automatic.


-


Learn fast from notes, then immediately switch to questions.


-


Prioritize: electron configuration logic, mole conversions, bonding models, intermolecular forces.


For targeted practice, pick a structured slice like[Structure 3 questionbank](https://www.revisiondojo.com/ib/ib-chemistry-new/ib-chemistry-new-structure-3-classification-of-matter/questionbank) to train classification and reasoning.


**Rule:** if you get a question wrong, don’t just read the solution. Rewrite the solution *as if teaching it to a tired friend* .

### Day 3: Reactivity foundations (enthalpy and energy cycles)


**Goal:** Turn energetics into a repeatable method.


-


Memorize the *shape* of common problems: calorimetry setup, Hess cycles, enthalpy definitions.


-


Do short sets: 10 questions, correct, then 10 more.


If you feel behind, don’t widen your scope. Narrow it: get 3 problem types perfect. **IB Chemistry** often rewards method consistency more than vague coverage.


### Day 4: Rate + equilibrium thinking (how fast, how far)


**Goal:** Stop treating kinetics and equilibrium as separate chapters.


-


Kinetics: rate graphs, order, half-life style reasoning (where applicable), factors, experiments.


-


Equilibrium: Le Chatelier logic, K expressions, what changes Q vs K.


A focused practice hub helps here:[R2.2 How fast? questionbank](https://www.revisiondojo.com/ib/ib-chemistry-new/ib-chemistry-new-r22-how-fast-the-rate-of-chemical-change/questionbank) .


**Night task:** Update your one-pager from memory. Don’t copy. Retrieve.


### Day 5: HL boosts or your weakest red zones


**Goal:** Spend today where marks are currently leaking.


If you’re HL, entropy/spontaneity is a common time-sink. Keep it practical:


-


Identify what each symbol means and which direction implies what.


-


Practice interpreting rather than re-reading.


If you’re SL, this day is still for your “red zones.” The fastest way to raise your **IB Chemistry** score is to fix recurring errors, not to skim new pages.

### Day 7: Calm review + strategy (protect the points you already earned)


**Goal:** Go into the exam steady, not fried.


-


Re-do your top 15 mistake-log questions.


-


Rewrite the one-pager one final time.


-


Practice command-term discipline: “state” vs “explain” vs “deduce.”


-


Pack your exam kit. Sleep.


A surprisingly high percentage of **IB Chemistry** points are lost to rushed reading and messy working. Day 7 is about reducing preventable mistakes.


### How to cram IB Chemistry without fooling yourself


Cramming fails when it becomes a performance for yourself: long hours, lots of highlighting, very little retrieval. Here’s the smarter pattern.


### Use the 3-pass rule for every topic


**Pass 1 (15-25 min):** Read concise notes, define key terms, copy one worked example.


**Pass 2 (35-60 min):** Do exam-style questions immediately. No “one more page” promises.


**Pass 3 (10-15 min):** Write a micro-summary from memory and add to your mistake log.


RevisionDojo’s **Study Notes** give you Pass 1 speed; **Flashcards** make Pass 3 automatic; the **Questionbank** forces Pass 2 honesty.


### Build “methods,” not memories


In **IB Chemistry** , you can often survive with 10-15 reliable methods:


-


unit conversions + significant figures discipline


-


mole ratios and limiting reagent structure


-


equilibrium change predictions (what affects K?)


-


energetics setup and common traps


-


organic identification patterns and reaction expectations
