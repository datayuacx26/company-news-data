# Upcoming DP1: Advice for GloPo and Math AI HL

If you are an upcoming DP1 student asking for advice on GloPo and/or Math AI HL, focus on **consistent skill-building rather than extensive summer preparation** . Global Politics rewards conceptual analysis, well-chosen contemporary evidence, and balanced evaluation. Mathematics: Applications and Interpretation HL rewards mathematical understanding, efficient technology use, accurate working, and interpretation in context. Begin DP1 with simple weekly systems for both subjects, and you will avoid the accumulation of gaps that makes the second year difficult.


### Understand what each course actually demands


The two subjects require different forms of thinking, but both assess application rather than memorization alone.


According to the current[IB Global Politics subject brief](https://www.ibo.org/globalassets/new-structure/university-admission/pdfs/global-politics-sl-hl-subject-brief-en.pdf) , HL students study the common core, three thematic studies, an engagement project, and the HL extension on global political challenges. The current course, first assessed in 2026, uses three external papers at HL. Paper 1 is source-based, Paper 2 requires extended responses, and Paper 3 uses stimuli and students' researched cases.


The[IB Math AI subject brief](https://www.ibo.org/contentassets/5895a05412144fe890312bad52b17044/subject-brief-dp-math-applications-and-interpretations-en.pdf) organizes the course around number and algebra, functions, geometry and trigonometry, statistics and probability, and calculus. It emphasizes modelling, real-world contexts, interpretation, and technology. Math AI HL is not an easier version of HL mathematics: it contains substantial statistics, calculus, functions, matrices, graph theory, and other higher-level material.

### Build a manageable DP1 system


DP1 becomes difficult when students rely on motivation and repeatedly postpone review. Instead, assign each subject a small weekly routine from the beginning.


For Global Politics, spend one session reviewing concepts and one applying them to a case or exam question. For Math AI HL, aim for three or four shorter sessions rather than one long weekend block. Mathematics improves through repeated retrieval, while political writing improves through regular planning and feedback.


A realistic weekly structure might be:


- **Math AI HL:** three 35-minute problem-solving sessions and one 20-minute error review.
- **Global Politics:** one concept review, one case-study update, and one essay or source-question plan.
- **Coursework:** one short block for organizing possible IA interests, sources, or questions.
- **Catch-up:** one flexible period reserved for unfinished work rather than automatically filled with new tasks.


Track completed questions, recurring errors, and teacher feedback. Do not track only hours, because two hours of rereading can produce less progress than thirty minutes of deliberate practice.


### How to approach Global Politics


### Learn concepts as analytical tools


Definitions matter, but exam answers require you to apply concepts such as **power, sovereignty, legitimacy, interdependence, rights, justice, development, sustainability, peace, and conflict** . For each concept, record a concise definition, a disputed interpretation, one relevant case, and one limitation.


For example, do not merely define sovereignty as state authority. Ask how refugee obligations, economic dependence, international organizations, multinational corporations, or armed groups can constrain that authority. This turns a definition into an argument.


The[RevisionDojo Global Politics notes](https://www.revisiondojo.com/ib/ib-global-politics/notes) can support this concept-first organization. After reading, close the notes and explain the concept independently before checking what you omitted.


### Build case studies, not news summaries


Following current affairs helps, but collecting articles is not enough. A usable case study should contain:


- The political issue and relevant context.
- Important state and non-state actors.
- Two or three precise pieces of evidence.
- Connections to several course concepts.
- Contrasting stakeholder perspectives.
- A judgment about outcomes, limitations, or trade-offs.


Choose cases that can support several arguments. A climate policy dispute, for example, might illuminate sovereignty, development, inequality, sustainability, legitimacy, and cooperation. Keep each case to one page and update it only when new evidence changes your analysis.


The current[Global Politics course update](https://www.ibo.org/university-admission/latest-curriculum-updates/global-politics-updates/) confirms that HL students investigate diverse cases connected to global political challenges. The extension emphasizes connections, tensions, and possible courses of action rather than memorizing a separate block of prescribed content.


### Practise argument before full essays


A strong paragraph usually contains a claim, a relevant concept, selective evidence, explanation, a counter-perspective, and evaluation. Evaluation means judging the strength or limits of an argument. It does not mean adding “however” followed by an unrelated fact.


Plan more essays than you write in full. A ten-minute plan can train question interpretation, case selection, counterclaims, and thesis construction without creating an unsustainable workload. Use the[Global Politics Questionbank and resources](https://www.revisiondojo.com/ib/ib-global-politics) to practise source questions and extended responses progressively.


### Treat the engagement project as a process


The **engagement project** combines research with meaningful engagement around a political issue. It should not be treated as a conventional research essay with one token interview added near the end. Suitable engagements might include interviewing stakeholders, observing political activity, attending a relevant meeting, or participating appropriately with an organization.


Do not finalize a question or activity without teacher approval, especially where safety, ethics, consent, or access may be an issue. In DP1, maintain a list of local political issues and potential stakeholders rather than trying to write the report prematurely. The[RevisionDojo engagement project guide](https://www.revisiondojo.com/ib/ib-global-politics/ib-global-politics-ia-engagement-project-guide) can help you understand the workflow, but your teacher's instructions and current official guide remain decisive.


### How to approach Math AI HL


### Strengthen algebra before the course accelerates


Technology is central to Math AI, but weak algebra still causes serious problems. Before or during early DP1, review rearranging equations, indices, logarithms, fractions, functions, graph transformations, and basic trigonometry. These skills appear inside longer modelling, calculus, and statistics questions.


When solving a problem, separate four stages: identify the mathematics, set up the model, calculate, and interpret. If you cannot explain why a method applies, calculator fluency alone will not protect you when the context becomes unfamiliar.


### Learn your calculator deliberately


The IB states that technology is used in all Math AI external assessments, and the official specimen papers require a graphic display calculator. Learn functions as they arise in class, including graphing, intersections, numerical solving, regression, distributions, matrices, and numerical calculus where relevant.


Keep a small calculator procedure log with the menu sequence, required inputs, output meaning, and one common error. The[RevisionDojo GDC guide for Math AI](https://www.revisiondojo.com/blog/gdc-calculators-in-math-ai) provides a useful starting point. Always follow your school and IB rules concerning permitted calculator models and examination settings.


### Show mathematics even when technology calculates


A numerical answer without setup, notation, or interpretation may not demonstrate the required reasoning. Write the equation, model, hypotheses, or relevant values before using technology. Then report appropriate precision and answer in context, including units where applicable.


For instance, a regression output is not a complete conclusion. You may need to identify the model, interpret a parameter, comment on fit, make a prediction, and recognize whether extrapolation is reasonable. This communication is part of the mathematics.


### Use an error log and mixed practice


After every test or practice set, classify mistakes as:


- Conceptual misunderstanding.
- Incorrect method selection.
- Algebraic or arithmetic error.
- Calculator entry error.
- Premature rounding.
- Missing working or notation.
- Weak contextual interpretation.


Reattempt the question without the solution, then complete a similar problem several days later. The[Math AI HL resource hub](https://www.revisiondojo.com/ib/ib-math-ai) supports topic review, while the[Math AI Questionbank](https://www.revisiondojo.com/ib/ib-math-ai/questionbank) can provide targeted and mixed practice. Jojo AI can clarify a step or question your reasoning, but you should still produce the final method independently.


### Prepare without overworking before DP1


You do not need to teach yourself both syllabuses during the summer. A better preparation plan is to establish foundations and arrive rested.


For Math AI HL, review prior algebra and learn the basic operation of your school's chosen calculator. For Global Politics, read reliable international reporting once or twice each week and practise identifying actors, interests, power relationships, and competing perspectives. Also confirm that your subject combination fits likely university prerequisites, particularly if you may apply for mathematics-intensive economics, science, computing, or engineering courses.


Avoid selecting an IA topic too early. Your understanding of feasible mathematics and political engagement will develop substantially during DP1.


### Common mistakes to avoid


Do not turn Global Politics notes into a chronology of world events. Organize knowledge around concepts, debates, actors, and arguments. Similarly, do not assume Math AI HL means every problem can be solved by pressing the correct calculator buttons.


Other avoidable mistakes include neglecting small misunderstandings, copying model answers instead of reconstructing their logic, using outdated Global Politics assessment advice, and beginning coursework without teacher guidance. The current Global Politics course replaced the former HL oral extension with **Paper 3** , so resources describing assessed HL presentations belong to the previous syllabus.


### Conclusion


Success in GloPo and Math AI HL comes from steady, active practice. Build adaptable case studies and evaluated arguments for Global Politics; strengthen algebra, calculator fluency, mathematical communication, and interpretation for Math AI HL. Keep routines modest enough to sustain alongside your other subjects, TOK, CAS, and later coursework.
