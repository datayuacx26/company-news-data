# Is One Extra IB Point on a Remark Really Rare?

### Is it rare to gain one extra point from an IB remark?


**It is indeed rare to get that one extra point to the next grade when remarking** , but it is possible. The important distinction is that an IB remark does not award a sympathetic point because a student is close to a boundary. It checks whether externally assessed work was marked correctly, and the mark changes only if the reviewing examiner identifies an error or reaches a different defensible judgment under the markscheme.


Being one scaled mark below the next grade makes a remark potentially worthwhile because even a small accepted change could raise the subject grade. It does not make the examiner more likely to find an additional mark. The IB does not publish a simple current success rate showing how often students who are exactly one mark short receive a higher grade, so claims that a particular percentage of remarks succeed should be treated cautiously.


The decision should therefore be based on **boundary distance, assessment type, component evidence, cost, risk, and consequences** . A university offer depending on the result may justify a request that would be difficult to justify for personal satisfaction alone.


### What an IB remark actually is


The official term is an **Enquiry Upon Results** , commonly abbreviated to **EUR** . A **Category 1 EUR** is a re-mark of all the candidate's externally assessed components in one subject, not merely a request to inspect one disputed question.


According to the IB's[assessment guidance for examiners](https://www.ibo.org/contentassets/4d92e48d38a4415a87e11555e143a39f/assessment-guide-for-examiners-en.pdf) , the re-marker is not instructed to find extra credit. The purpose is to check the original marking and correct identified errors. If no error is identified, the existing mark remains appropriate.


The request must be submitted by the school through the DP or CP coordinator. Students and parents cannot submit it directly to the IB. Written consent is required because a Category 1 re-mark can produce a **higher mark, a lower mark, or no change** .


Possible result What it means


Mark and grade unchanged The review does not produce enough change to cross a grade boundary


Mark changes, grade unchanged The revised result remains within the same grade band


Mark and grade increase The revised scaled total reaches the higher boundary


Mark and grade decrease The revised scaled total falls below the lower boundary


The IB's[official assessment FAQ](https://ibo.org/programmes/diploma-programme/assessment-and-exams/getting-results/assessment-faq/) confirms that the coordinator must explain the applicable fee and obtain consent. Fees, deadlines, categories, and administrative details can change between examination sessions, so the current coordinator guidance is more reliable than an old online post.


### Why gaining exactly the needed point is uncommon


### The original marking has already been quality controlled


IB examiners complete standardization and qualification before live marking. During marking, their work is monitored through definitively marked responses known as **seeds** , and examiners can be stopped if they repeatedly mark outside the permitted tolerance.


The IB also conducts additional quality checks before results are issued. Its published assessment guidance describes an **at-risk re-marking** process intended to identify candidates whose grades may be unexpectedly low in relation to available evidence. These safeguards cannot eliminate every disagreement, but they help explain why most later reviews do not produce dramatic changes.


### Examiners apply criteria, not proximity to a boundary


A re-marker assesses the work against the same markscheme, criteria, or markbands used during the examination session. The examiner is not supposed to know that the candidate needs one more scaled mark and then search for somewhere to award it.


This protects fairness. If proximity to a boundary influenced marking, two equivalent responses could receive different treatment simply because one candidate happened to be closer to a grade threshold.


### One raw mark is not always one final scaled mark


A final subject result combines components with different maximum marks and percentage weightings. Component marks may be scaled before being combined, so gaining one raw mark on a paper does not necessarily add exactly one point to the displayed scaled total.


For example, if a component is worth 20% of the final result but has 40 available raw marks, each raw mark contributes approximately half a scaled point before the IB's calculation and rounding rules are applied. A student displayed as one mark below a boundary might therefore need more than one additional raw mark across the re-marked components.


This is why the candidate portal's **marks required for a grade increase** should not be interpreted as a promise that one disputed question is all that stands between the student and the higher grade. RevisionDojo's explanation of[how IB grade boundaries are calculated](https://www.revisiondojo.com/blog/ib-grade-boundaries-how-theyre-actually-calculated) provides useful background on the difference between raw performance, weighting, scaled totals, and final grades.


### Which subjects are more likely to move?


No subject carries a guaranteed probability of success. However, there is a practical difference between responses governed mainly by objective markscheme points and those requiring holistic examiner judgment.


Assessment style Typical marking characteristics Practical remark implication


Essay or extended response Interpretation of criteria, quality of analysis, and best-fit markbands matter Some legitimate examiner judgment remains possible


Short structured response Credit is tied closely to specified points Movement usually requires a missed valid answer or misapplied markscheme


Calculation-heavy response Method, accuracy, and consequential marks are defined Changes are possible when working was overlooked, but broad reinterpretation is less likely


Multiple-choice component Answers are objectively recorded and processed Little or no examiner judgment is available to reconsider


It is therefore reasonable, as practical advice rather than an IB guarantee, to view essay-heavy components as potentially more movable. Nevertheless, an articulate essay is not automatically under-marked, and a teacher's higher estimate does not prove that the original examiner made an error.


A useful first step is to ask the subject teacher whether the result appears plausible in light of the student's established performance and the assessment criteria. ### When a one-mark remark is a rational decision


A request is strongest when several factors point in the same direction:


- The result is **one or two scaled marks below** the higher boundary.
- The externally assessed work includes substantial judgment-based responses.
- The subject teacher sees a credible marking issue, not merely an unexpectedly low result.
- The higher grade would affect the diploma, a university condition, scholarship eligibility, or another concrete outcome.
- The student accepts that the mark and possibly the grade can decrease.
- The fee is affordable if no grade change occurs.


The case is weaker when the student is several marks from the boundary, the assessment is predominantly objective, or the only evidence is that the examination felt successful. Predicted grades can provide context, but they are not evidence that a specific examination response was marked incorrectly.


If a university place is affected, contact admissions promptly rather than waiting silently for the EUR outcome. Explain that a review is being considered or has been submitted, but do not claim that the grade is certain to rise. ### A careful decision process


### 1. Confirm the numbers


Ask the coordinator for the scaled total, relevant grade boundaries, component information available to the school, applicable fee, and school deadline. Schools may impose an earlier internal deadline than the IB's final submission date.


### 2. Identify what Category 1 covers


A Category 1 request concerns externally assessed material for the subject. It is not an individual student request to re-mark an internally assessed component. **Category 3** is a different service involving re-moderation of a school's internally assessed sample and is initiated at school level under specific conditions.


### 3. Compare the upside and downside


A rise from 5 to 6 that confirms a university place has greater practical value than a rise that changes nothing beyond the transcript. Conversely, a student near the lower boundary must understand that a decrease could also have serious consequences.


### 4. Discuss alternatives


Possible alternatives include asking the university to reconsider, choosing another offer, or retaking a subject. A retake is a new assessment attempt, while a remark only reviews work already submitted. RevisionDojo's[IB retake guide](https://www.revisiondojo.com/blog/can-you-retake-ib) explains how these options differ.


### 5. Give informed consent


The IB's current[Diploma Programme general regulations](https://www.ibo.org/globalassets/new-structure/become-an-ib-school/pdfs/dp-2026-general-regulations.pdf) state that a re-mark may lead to a higher or lower subject grade. Consent should therefore reflect genuine acceptance of both possibilities, not an assumption that being one mark below the upper boundary makes the process risk-free.


### Common misconceptions


**“The IB will probably round me up because I am only one point short.”** Grade boundaries are cut scores. A result below the boundary receives the lower grade unless an official process changes the underlying result.


**“I am requesting one extra mark.”** A Category 1 EUR reviews eligible externally assessed components for the subject. It is not a request for a discretionary bonus point.


**“My grade cannot decrease because I am appealing.”** A Category 1 re-mark can lower the result, which is why written consent is required. An appeal is also not simply another opportunity to have the work marked repeatedly; it concerns whether applicable procedures were followed.


**“My IA will automatically be marked again too.”** Category 1 and Category 3 are distinct services. Individual students cannot use Category 1 to obtain a personal re-mark of ordinary internally assessed work.


**“One displayed point means one raw exam mark.”** Weighting and scaling break that simple equivalence. The required raw movement depends on the affected component and the subject's assessment model.


### Conclusion


Gaining the final point needed for a higher IB grade is possible, but it should not be treated as likely merely because the candidate is close to the boundary. A remark corrects marking where necessary; it does not award generosity to borderline candidates.


The best decision uses exact boundary information, teacher judgment, assessment type, financial cost, downside risk, and the real consequence of a grade change.
