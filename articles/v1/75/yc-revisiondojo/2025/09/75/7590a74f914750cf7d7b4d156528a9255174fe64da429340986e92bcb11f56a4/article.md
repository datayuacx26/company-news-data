### Why a 4 becomes a 7 faster than you expect


Most students try to “improve understanding.” Top students try to “improve conversion.” In **IB Computer Science** , conversion means taking what you know and packaging it exactly how the markscheme rewards it.


A 4 student often has:


-


partial knowledge of databases, networks, and system fundamentals


-


fragile algorithm tracing (one small slip breaks the whole answer)


-


programming solutions that work in their head but not on paper


-


theory answers that describe rather than explain, discuss, or evaluate


A 7 student is not perfect. They are *repeatable* . They can do the same types of questions with the same structures under time pressure.


### The four-week plan to level up IB Computer Science


### Week 1: Rebuild the floor (and stop guessing)


Your goal this week is to remove “mystery topics” in **IB Computer Science** .


**Daily routine (2 hours):**


-


30 min: Study Notes for one subtopic (fast, focused)


-


60 min: Questionbank practice on that subtopic


-


30 min: review mistakes, make 5--10 Flashcards for definitions, processes, and common traps


Pick foundational areas that show up everywhere:


-


data representation basics


-


networks fundamentals


-


databases vocabulary and keys


-


programming basics (variables, loops, arrays, conditionals)


RevisionDojo makes this week efficient because you can read, practise, and immediately grade in one place:[IB Computer Science Resources](https://www.revisiondojo.com/ib/ib-computer-science) .


### Week 2: Algorithms and Paper-style practice (marks live here)


Week 2 is where you start earning “free” marks: tracing, dry runs, and clean algorithm logic. In **IB Computer Science** , you do not need fancy code. You need unbreakable reasoning.


**Targets for the week:**


-


linear vs binary search (conditions for using each)


-


standard sorting logic (what changes each pass)


-


recursion basics (base case, step case, stack thinking)


-


pseudocode clarity: inputs, outputs, loops, conditions


**Two key habits:**


-


**Trace everything** . Write the table. Pretend you are the CPU.


-


**Fix one mistake pattern at a time** . If you keep forgetting loop bounds, drill only that until it stops happening.


If you need a quick emergency structure for compressing the week, borrow the pacing from[How to Cram IB Computer Science in 1 Week](https://www.revisiondojo.com/blog/how-to-cram-ib-computer-science-in-1-week) but stretch it across seven days with more practice.

### Week 3: Paper 2 style answers (the “say it like an examiner” week)


This is the week where 4 students bleed marks: they know the content, but their answers are too airy. **IB Computer Science** rewards specific language: definitions, cause-and-effect, and real-world context.


**High-yield areas to revisit:**


-


databases and SQL concepts (relationships, keys, integrity)


-


networks and protocols (what, why, trade-offs)


-


OS concepts and resource management


-


SDLC thinking: requirements, design, testing, evaluation


Use two supports:


-


For big-picture structure, skim[Mastering IB Computer Science Assessments](https://www.revisiondojo.com/blog/mastering-ib-computer-science-assessments) .


-


For content refresh, anchor your revision with[IB Computer Science Notes](https://www.revisiondojo.com/blog/ib-computer-science-notes) .


**One rule that upgrades answers instantly:**
After every theory point, add a “because” sentence. “Because” forces explanation, not description.


### Week 4: Simulate the exam, then sharpen one blade


Week 4 is not about learning new things. It is about performing **IB Computer Science** under constraints.


**Plan:**


-


Sit 2 timed mini-mocks (one programming-heavy, one theory-heavy)


-


Review with a strict method: for each lost mark, write the reason (concept gap, misread command term, time pressure, unclear explanation)


-


Spend the last 3--4 days on your top two weaknesses only

### Command terms: the hidden lever in IB Computer Science


Most “I deserved more marks” stories are actually command-term stories.


If the question says **evaluate** , you need criteria, pros/cons, and a justified conclusion. If it says **outline** , you need brief, accurate points, not a paragraph. If it says **explain** , you must show cause-and-effect.


Build this into your daily practice with[How to Use Command Terms Effectively in IB Exams](https://www.revisiondojo.com/blog/how-to-use-command-terms-effectively-in-ib-exams) . Then drill the habit: circle the command term before you write anything.
