# 11 Proven Tips to Improve IB Math AI Scores

### A quiet truth about IB Math AI scores


The biggest jump in **IB** Math AI rarely comes from learning one magical new topic. It comes from noticing what the exam has been asking you to do all along: model a real situation, choose a method confidently, use technology wisely, and then explain what the result means.


If that sounds simple, it’s because it is. But simple is not the same as easy.


This guide shares 11 proven tips to improve your **IB** Math AI scores (SL or HL) by focusing on what earns marks: method, interpretation, communication, and calm repetition.

### 11 proven tips for higher grades in IB Math AI


### Tip 1: Study the mark logic, not just the math


In **IB** Math AI, two students can get the same final number and different marks. Why? One shows the method clearly, follows command terms, and interprets. The other jumps, skips, and hopes.


A fast way to improve is to do short sets of exam-style questions, then compare your solution to what a markscheme would reward: setup, method steps, and a final statement.


Use RevisionDojo’s[Math AI Papers](https://www.revisiondojo.com/ib/ib-math-ai/papers) to practice full exam flow (and stamina) instead of isolated drills forever.


### Tip 2: Know the Paper 1 vs Paper 2 mindset


Paper 1 rewards structured reasoning without leaning on your calculator. Paper 2 rewards modelling, interpretation, and efficient technology use. HL students add Paper 3 style problem solving pressure.


So your revision should rotate: one day for clean reasoning, another day for calculator-heavy modelling.


If Paper 2 is your stress point, follow the strategy guide[Ace IB Math Applications and Interpretation Paper 2](https://www.revisiondojo.com/blog/ace-ib-math-applications-and-interpretation-paper-2-proven-study-strategies-and-revisiondojo-resources) .


### Tip 3: Get fluent with your GDC like it’s a language


Calculator fluency is not a bonus in **IB** Math AI. It’s part of the skill.


Train these until they feel automatic:


-


Regression + interpreting parameters


-


Solving equations numerically


-


Using trace, intersections, and window settings


-


Reading graphs and tables quickly


Pair practice with the[IB Math AI Data Booklet](https://www.revisiondojo.com/ib/ib-math-ai/data-booklet) so your formulas and calculator outputs speak the same “dialect” under time pressure.


### Tip 4: Build a mistake log that actually changes your score


Most students “review mistakes” by feeling bad for 20 seconds and moving on. High-scoring **IB** students do something more boring and more powerful: they record patterns.


Your mistake log should include:


-


The error type (setup, algebra, command term, interpretation, calculator)


-


The trigger (rushed, unfamiliar context, misread data)


-


The fix (a rule, a template sentence, a calculator step)


Then you retest the same skill 48 hours later.


Mistakes as compost


### Tip 5: Practice “interpretation sentences” on purpose


In **IB** Math AI, interpretation is where many marks quietly disappear. Train yourself to end solutions with one or two sentences that do three things:


-


Answer the question in context


-


Include units (if relevant)


-


Comment on reasonableness


This is especially important in statistics, modelling, and finance problems.


### Tip 6: Shrink your revision scope (and watch your accuracy rise)


One focused topic session beats a scattered “I touched everything” session.


Try this loop:


-


15 minutes: concept review


-


30 minutes: targeted questions


-


10 minutes: correction + mistake log

### Tip 7: Use Notes first, then questions (not the other way around)


When you start with questions before you understand the model, you end up memorising steps without meaning. In **IB** Math AI, meaning matters.


Use concise concept refreshers like[Functions Notes](https://www.revisiondojo.com/ib/ib-math-ai/math-ai-functions/notes) , then immediately reinforce with problems.

### Tip 8: Time yourself earlier than you think you need to


Time pressure changes everything: accuracy, decision-making, even basic algebra.


Start with 25-minute sprints before you attempt full papers. After each sprint, grade your performance by asking:


-


Where did I stall?


-


Did I choose the right model?


-


Did I explain the result?

### Tip 9: Master command terms as if they are mini-instructions


Words like “determine,” “explain,” and “interpret” are not decoration in **IB** questions. They’re your marking roadmap.


If you treat every command term like a different task, you’ll stop losing method marks through unclear communication.
