# Align Department Assessments with IB Criteria

If you have ever wondered why your friend got “great analysis” on an answer that earned you “too descriptive,” you have already met the hidden problem of inconsistent assessment.


In the **IB** , that inconsistency is not just annoying. It is expensive. It costs time, confidence, and revision energy because you start studying your teacher instead of studying the criteria.


The good news is that when a department aligns assessments with **IB** criteria, students feel it immediately: feedback becomes predictable, improvement becomes repeatable, and revision becomes calmer.


Same essay, three rubrics: the student experience


### What it means to align department assessments with IB criteria


Alignment is a simple promise: a “good response” stays good no matter which classroom you are in.


In practice, aligning departmental assessments with **IB** criteria means:


-


Your rubrics reflect the language of **IB** markbands (not a mystery “teacher rubric”).


-


Command terms are treated as skills, not vibes.


-


Two teachers can justify the same mark for the same reasons.


-


Feedback helps you improve the next task, not just explain the last one.
