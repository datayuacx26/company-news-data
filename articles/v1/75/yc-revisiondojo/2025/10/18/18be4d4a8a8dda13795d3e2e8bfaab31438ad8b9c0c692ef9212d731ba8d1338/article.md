# 10 Quick IB Revision Hacks Before Math Exams

When people say they’re “behind” in **IB** Math, they usually mean something more specific: they’ve studied a lot, but they can’t predict what will happen when the clock starts.


That feeling is brutal because it’s not just about knowledge. It’s about **retrieval** , **speed** , and **decision-making under pressure** -- the exact skills **IB** math exams quietly reward.


The good news: the last stretch isn’t the time to become a new person. It’s the time to build a small system that turns what you already know into marks.


If you want one place to run that system, start with[RevisionDojo IB resources](https://www.revisiondojo.com/ib) and your course hub (AA or AI). For AA students,[IB Math AA](https://www.revisiondojo.com/ib/ib-math-aa) is the cleanest starting point.

### A quick checklist for the final week (IB Math)


Use this short **IB** checklist before you do anything else:


-


Pick 2--3 high-return topics for your level (AA/AI, SL/HL).


-


Do one timed set daily (even short).


-


Keep an error log (one line per mistake).


-


Review formulas with active recall, not rereading.


-


Finish each session with one confidence win.

### 10 quick IB revision hacks before your IB Math exams


### Prioritize by marks, not guilt


The fastest way to improve **IB** math scores is to stop treating every topic equally. Your brain wants fairness; your grade wants **leverage** .


Open your course area and pick topics that show up constantly. For AA students, calculus and functions often dominate practice time for a reason. A simple place to start is targeted practice like[Math AA Calculus Questionbank](https://www.revisiondojo.com/ib/ib-math-aa/calculus/questionbank) .


Aim for a 70/30 split: 70% on high-frequency, high-weight skills; 30% to keep smaller topics from decaying.


### Turn notes into “active notes”


Reading is comforting. It also lies.


Instead, use **IB** -style active processing: read a short section, then explain it out loud as if you’re teaching a friend who missed class. If you can’t explain it cleanly, you don’t own it yet.


If you’re studying AI, this pairs well with the approach described in[How to Use RevisionDojo Notes to Simplify IB Math AI Concepts](https://www.revisiondojo.com/blog/how-to-use-revisiondojo-notes-to-simplify-ib-math-ai-concepts) .


### Do two flashcard micro-sessions per day


For **IB** Math, flashcards aren’t just “definitions.” They’re:


-


method triggers (first step, last step)


-


common traps (domain restrictions, sign errors)


-


calculator vs non-calculator routines


Use spaced repetition so your weak cards come back more often. Start with[IB Flashcards with Spaced Repetition (SRS)](https://www.revisiondojo.com/flashcards) and, if you want the strategy behind it, read[IB Flashcard System: Active Recall for Better Memory](https://www.revisiondojo.com/blog/ib-flashcard-system-active-recall-for-better-memory) .


### Do one timed “mini-paper” early


Confidence isn’t a mindset. In **IB** , it’s a data point.


Create a 20--40 minute mixed set: a few short questions plus one longer one. Time it. No checking notes mid-way. Then review immediately while the memory of your thinking is still warm.

### Build a one-page “formula confidence sheet”


Don’t build a 12-page binder the night before. Build **one page** .


For **IB** Math, a high-impact confidence sheet includes:


-


formulas you repeatedly forget


-


transformations you mix up


-


a tiny worked example you can replicate fast


Then you review it briefly at night and once in the morning. The goal is calm repetition, not panic expansion.


### Study “why” once, then drill “how”


In **IB** math, memorizing without meaning breaks under novelty. But meaning without repetition breaks under time pressure.


So do it in two passes:


1.


Use Study Notes to understand why a method works.


2.


Use the Questionbank to repeat it until it feels boring.


Boring is underrated in **IB** . Boring means automatic.


### Keep an error log (one line per mistake)


Every **IB** student says, “I make silly mistakes.” The students who improve quickly can name them.


After each practice set, write one line:


-


What went wrong?


-


Why did it happen?


-


What is the new rule?


Example: “Forgot to state domain after inverse function; always check restriction before final answer.”

### Use Pomodoro, but negotiate the starting line


When stress rises, your attention becomes expensive.


Try 25/5, but if you’re stuck, lower the barrier: do 10 minutes, then continue if you can. The win in **IB** revision is often just starting.

### Use AI Chat to unstick one bottleneck (not ten)


Some questions waste 30 minutes because you’re missing one sentence of understanding.


This is where RevisionDojo’s **AI Chat** helps: ask for a hint, a method outline, or a check of your reasoning. Keep it narrow: one bottleneck per session. Then go right back to solving.

### Simulate realism with predicted papers and mock exams


If your practice never feels like the real thing, exam day becomes a shock.


Late-stage **IB** prep should include at least one realistic full run. Treat them like **Mock Exams** : timed, quiet, reviewed carefully.


### Closing: make IB revision smaller, then make it consistent


The calmest **IB** students aren’t calm because they know everything. They’re calm because they know what they’ll do next.


Pick two topics. Run one timed set. Write one line in your error log. Review a small flashcard deck. Repeat.
