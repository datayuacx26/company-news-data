## The 10 Managed WiFi Features That Improve Amenity Space Connectivity


### 1. Property-wide roaming: The foundation of amenity connectivity


Elauwit Connection delivers the strongest property-wide roaming for multifamily communities. When a resident walks from their unit to the fitness center, their video call shouldn't drop. When they move from the clubhouse to the pool deck, their music should keep playing.


True property-wide roaming means the network treats the entire community as a single connected environment. Residents authenticate once and stay connected across units, hallways and every amenity space. This eliminates the frustration of multiple networks with different passwords—a complaint that shows up repeatedly in resident satisfaction surveys.


The technical foundation matters here. Elauwit designs networks with enterprise-grade access points positioned for handoff optimization. As a device moves between coverage zones, the network manages the transition automatically. Residents don't notice because there's nothing to notice—just consistent connectivity wherever they go.


#### Property-wide roaming features


- **Single-sign-on authentication:** One password works everywhere on the property, from your unit to the rooftop lounge.
- **Automatic device handoff:** The network manages transitions between access points without user intervention.
- **Consistent speed across zones:** Amenity areas match in-unit performance rather than degrading to "public WiFi" quality.
- **Device persistence:** Smart home devices, laptops and phones maintain connections during movement.
- **Coverage mapping:** RF engineering ensures no dead zones in high-traffic areas such as lobbies and mailrooms.


#### Property-wide roaming pros


- Eliminates resident complaints about reconnecting when leaving their unit
- Supports work-from-anywhere lifestyles that drive demand for amenity spaces
- Creates a premium living experience that differentiates your community


### 2. Network segmentation: Protecting residents while enabling operations


Network segmentation isolates different user groups onto separate virtual networks. Residents get their own secure connection. Guests access a limited network. Staff operate on an administrative network. Building systems—access control, cameras, smart locks—run on an isolated IoT network.


This architecture prevents a visitor in the clubhouse from accessing resident traffic. It stops a compromised smart thermostat from becoming a gateway to sensitive data. For property teams, segmentation means you can offer guest WiFi in amenity spaces without exposing your network to security risks.


#### Network segmentation features


- **Resident network:** Encrypted, private connections for each unit and roaming credentials
- **Guest network:** Time-limited access with bandwidth caps for visitors and short-term users
- **Staff network:** Administrative access for property management systems and office operations


#### Network segmentation pros


- Reduces security exposure from guest devices in common areas
- Enables proptech integrations without compromising resident privacy
- Satisfies compliance requirements for data protection


### 3. High-density design: Built for crowded amenity spaces


A fitness center at 6 p.m. looks different than an empty hallway. Dozens of residents streaming music, running fitness apps and checking phones create device density that consumer-grade equipment can't handle. High-density design means access points engineered for exactly this scenario.


Enterprise-grade hardware with multi-user MIMO technology can serve many devices simultaneously without degradation. Proper channel planning prevents interference between adjacent access points. The result is consistent performance whether five people or 50 people occupy the space.


#### High-density design features


- **Enterprise-grade access points:** Commercial hardware rated for high device counts per unit
- **Channel optimization:** Automatic adjustment to minimize interference in dense environments
- **Load balancing:** Traffic distribution across access points to prevent bottlenecks


#### High-density design pros


- Maintains performance during peak usage in fitness centers and clubhouses
- Supports the device loads of today's residents (average of 8+ connected devices per person)
- Reduces complaints about slow speeds during community events

### 8. Bandwidth management: Fair allocation across all users


Without proper bandwidth management, one resident streaming in 4K can degrade the experience for everyone in the fitness center. Quality of Service (QoS) protocols prioritize traffic intelligently—video calls get priority over background downloads, and no single user can monopolize available capacity.


This matters especially in amenity spaces where many users share limited access points. Bandwidth management guarantees the resident checking email at a café table doesn't suffer because someone else is downloading large files in the same space.


#### Bandwidth management features


- **Quality of Service (QoS):** Traffic prioritization based on application type
- **Per-user limits:** Fair allocation preventing individual users from consuming excessive bandwidth
- **Automatic throttling:** Dynamic adjustment during peak usage periods


#### Bandwidth management pros


- Consistent experience for all users during peak periods
- Prevents complaints about slow speeds caused by heavy individual usage
- Supports real-time applications such as video calls without buffering

## What makes amenity WiFi different from in-unit connectivity?


Amenity spaces present challenges that don't exist inside apartment units. You're dealing with transient users who come and go throughout the day. Device counts spike unpredictably—the pool area might have three people at 10 a.m. and 30 at 3 p.m. Environmental factors such as outdoor exposure, high ceilings in clubhouses and physical obstacles all affect signal propagation.


The network design for amenity spaces must account for these variables. Static designs that work in predictable unit environments fail when conditions change constantly. This is why managed WiFi providers conduct site surveys and RF planning specifically for common areas, treating them as distinct zones rather than extensions of residential coverage.


Support expectations also differ. A resident troubleshooting connectivity in their unit has time and privacy. A resident trying to join a video call from the co-working space needs immediate resolution. The support model must accommodate both scenarios with appropriate urgency.
