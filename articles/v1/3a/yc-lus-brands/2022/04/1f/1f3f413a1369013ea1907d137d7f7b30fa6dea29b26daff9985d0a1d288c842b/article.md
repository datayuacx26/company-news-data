# How to Repair Damaged Curly Hair, Once and For All

Between frizz, stringy strands and split ends, it can be hard to know what to do with damaged curly hair that just isn’t looking right. If you feel like you haven’t had a good hair day in forever, you may think the only option left is to reach for a pair of scissors. But not so fast! There are actually other steps you can take first to help repair your damaged curly hair and get it back on track — no snipping required. (Not sure if you’re dealing with damaged hair or just plain ol’ dryness? Check to see if you have any of these[10 Signs of Damaged Curly Hair.](https://loveurcurls.com/blogs/news/signs-of-damaged-curls) )


While dry hair isn't always necessarily damaged (it could just be dry due to the weather, for example), both damaged and dry hair have one thing in common: they lack moisture. Without proper moisture, you lose shine and definition. And as your hair becomes drier and drier, you’ll probably notice signs of damage creeping in, like split ends. Excessive dryness can cause damage, which can create even more dryness, which can cause even more damage and… well, you get the picture.


Before we get into how to repair damaged curly hair, you need to first identify the cause of the damage. While some culprits are obvious (think: heat-damaged hair or one too many bleach dye jobs), others (like using harsh products or over-washing) can fly under the radar and continue to wreak havoc on your curls.


### 5 Things That Cause Damaged Curly Hair


##### Color Damage


Color lifting agents are harsh, especially on curly hair, which tends to be more delicate and fragile. Lightening your hair too much (or too quickly) can break down the structure of your hair and make it even more porous than it naturally is. And while high porosity hair absorbs moisture from water and hair products quickly (yay!), it can lose that moisture just as easily (boo).


##### Over-Washing


Obviously, shampooing is important for lifting things like dirt and oil from your scalp and hair. However, too much shampoo can strip curly hair of its much-needed natural oils and moisture, particularly if you’re using a shampoo loaded with sulfates, which can be extra-drying on curly hair. ##### Mechanical Damage


Rough styling, detangling, using heat tools and even pillow friction can all cause breakage and suck moisture out of fragile curls. Treat your curls gently by wrapping freshly washed hair with a[cotton T-shirt](https://loveurcurls.com/collections/accessories/products/t-shirt-towel) instead of a towel, using a[detangling brush](https://loveurcurls.com/collections/accessories/products/detangle-style-brush) specifically designed for curly hair, and wrapping your hair in a[satin scarf](https://loveurcurls.com/collections/accessories/products/satin-scarf) at night to prevent breakage and frizz.


##### Using the Wrong Hair Products


Not everything on the shelf is good for your curls. Avoid products that are sticky, drying or create build-up and block moisture from penetrating your hair. (We’re looking at you, mousse and hairspray!)


##### Heat Damage


You didn’t think we’d leave out heat damaged hair, did you? Unsurprisingly, heat tools are a major cause of curl damage. If you absolutely must use tools like flat irons and curling irons, try adjusting the heat to the halfway mark to minimize the amount of damage to your curls. Better yet: go on a heat tools strike and embrace your curls.
