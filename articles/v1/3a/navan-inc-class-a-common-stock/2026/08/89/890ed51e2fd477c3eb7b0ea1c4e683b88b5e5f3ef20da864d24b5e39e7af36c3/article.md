# A business traveler's guide to KubeCon + CloudNativeCon NA 2026

KubeCon brings thousands of cloud-native professionals to Salt Lake City for four days of technical sessions, product demos, and community events.\[1\] A little planning before you arrive can make the week more productive.


## What is KubeCon + CloudNativeCon North America and who is it for?


KubeCon + CloudNativeCon North America is the Cloud Native Computing Foundation's (CNCF) flagship conference for professionals building, operating, and securing cloud-native infrastructure.\[1\] The event brings together the open-source community behind Kubernetes and other CNCF projects, along with the organizations that use and support those technologies.


KubeCon + CloudNativeCon North America drew more than 9,000 attendees when it last met in Salt Lake City in 2024, and nearly 9,400 attendees in Atlanta in 2025.\[4\]\[5\] You can expect to see platform engineers, SREs, DevOps practitioners, cloud architects, software engineers, CNCF project maintainers, open-source contributors, and technology vendors.


Whether you're evaluating new tools, contributing to upstream projects, or solving production challenges, KubeCon offers opportunities to learn directly from the people building and using the technologies that shape the cloud-native ecosystem. If you're making the case to attend, connect the trip to work your team already has planned. That could mean validating an upcoming Kubernetes migration, evaluating tools on your platform roadmap, or meeting directly with the maintainers and vendors behind the technologies you use every day.


## What makes KubeCon worth the trip


KubeCon + CloudNativeCon North America is where the cloud-native community comes together to share ideas, solve technical challenges, and shape the future of open source. You'll hear about new features and project roadmaps directly from the people building them, ask questions of maintainers, compare tools side by side, and learn how other engineering teams are solving problems similar to yours.


Plan time for these opportunities throughout the week:


- **CNCF co-located events:** If you have an All-Access Pass, spend Monday diving into a topic that aligns with your work, whether that's platform engineering, observability, security, GitOps, AI, Kubernetes at the edge, or another specialized area.\[1\]\[6\]
- **Solutions Showcase:** Explore the latest cloud-native platforms, infrastructure tools, and services in one place. Compare vendors side by side, ask detailed technical questions, and narrow your short list before you leave Salt Lake City.\[3\]
- **Project Pavilion:** Meet the maintainers and contributors behind CNCF projects, see live demonstrations, and ask implementation questions directly to the people building the technologies your team may already rely on.\[3\]
- **KubeCrawl + CloudNativeFest:** Included with registration, this signature networking event combines community programming, sponsor exhibits, food, and informal conversations that can lead to new partnerships and professional connections.\[7\]
- **Community meetups and sponsor events:** Watch the conference schedule and community channels (#KubeCon) for project meetups, happy hours, and technical gatherings announced closer to the event.


From hands-on technical discussions to informal community events, KubeCon offers far more than scheduled presentations. Planning ahead helps you make time for the experiences that matter most, whether you're evaluating new technologies, learning from project maintainers, or connecting with the broader cloud-native community.

Hotels closest to the convention center tend to sell out first, so book early if you want to stay within a few minutes of the venue.

### KubeCon hotel pricing


Navan analyzed anonymized hotel booking patterns for November 2025 near Salt Palace Convention Center to provide context for hotel pricing.\[16\] Use these benchmarks while planning your trip:


- **Median hotel rate:** The median rate was $257 per night.\[16\]
- **Booking windows:** Booking 2-plus months ahead pushed the median to $320 — the highest of the booking windows in this sample.\[16\]
- **Stay length:** Business travelers stayed an average of 2.1 nights.\[16\]
- **Late bookings:** Roughly 57% of bookings were made within two weeks of check-in.\[16\]


The Hyatt Regency block closes on October 13, 2026, and other properties are closing their blocks on October 16, 2026.\[2\] Room blocks often fill up early, and although an official block isn't always the lowest price during KubeCon, cancellation terms and walk time to Salt Palace often justify the premium for attendees.


### KubeCon flight trends


Most attendees fly into Salt Lake City International Airport (SLC), a Delta hub roughly 8 miles northwest of Salt Palace.\[2\] Navan flight data from the same November 2025 window shows 501 business flights into Salt Lake City, with 4.7% from international origins.\[16\] Most people flew from Denver, Phoenix, Orange County, Chicago, and San Francisco.


The 2024 KubeCon transparency report announced that registrants came from the U.S., Europe, Australia, Japan, India, and beyond.\[4\] If you're crossing time zones or attending Monday events, consider arriving on Sunday so you'll be rested for co-located programming or Project Lightning Talks.

## Set your Salt Lake City budget before traveling


Budgeting for KubeCon week means accounting for your conference pass, lodging, meals, SLC transfers, client dinners, and evening community events.


### Federal per diem for Salt Lake City


The U.S. General Services Administration (GSA) sets federal per diem rates by city and season. At the time of publication, the following November rates for FY2026 applied to Salt Lake City (Salt Lake and Tooele counties):


- **$222 per day combined:** Includes $142 nightly lodging plus $80 daily meals and incidental expenses (M&IE)\[8\]
- **M&IE breakdown:** Includes $20 for breakfast, $22 for lunch, $33 for dinner, and $5 for incidentals (reduce your meal allowance for any catering at the event)\[8\]
- **First and last travel days:** Includes M&IE allowance of $60 (i.e. 75% of the daily M&IE rate)\[8\]


Updated rates for FY2027 (October 2026–September 2027) are expected to be published in mid-August 2026 at gsa.gov. During conference week, rooms at hotels near Salt Palace often exceed the federal lodging rate, so budget for the difference or confirm with finance before booking.\[2\]


### Additional expenses for KubeCon


Budget for these additional expenses before you book so you can estimate the full cost of the trip and avoid surprises during conference week.


- **Conference registration:** A KubeCon + CloudNativeCon Only pass starts at $1,499, while an All-Access Pass starts at $1,798; check the official event website for current rates.\[7\]
- **Individual and Academic tiers:** Lower rates are available for qualifying registrants; visit the conference registration page for eligibility.\[7\]
- **Conference meals:** Registration includes lunch plus morning and afternoon refreshments during the main conference (Tuesday–Thursday); adjust your M&IE reimbursement accordingly.\[7\]\[9\]
- **Airport transfer:** Expect to pay $2.50 each way for the UTA TRAX Green Line between SLC and downtown or $25–$45 or more for a rideshare, depending on surge pricing.\[2\]\[10\]
- **Client dinners:** Private dining and upscale restaurants within walking distance of Salt Palace typically cost $75–$150 per person.
- **After-hours networking:** Leave room in your budget for team dinners, coffee meetings, sponsor-hosted receptions, or other informal networking events beyond the official conference schedule.


## Plan your KubeCon agenda


The Linux Foundation has published the overall schedule for KubeCon + CloudNativeCon North America 2026, with the full session catalog expected in August 2026.\[3\]\[11\] Build your schedule around the technical sessions, project discussions, and meetings that best match your goals. Leave room for time in the Solutions Showcase, conversations in the Project Pavilion, and the hallway discussions that are a hallmark of KubeCon.


Here are some of the conference highlights:


- **Monday (Nov. 9):** Optional CNCF co-located events (specialized one-day conferences held before the main event) for All-Access Pass holders, plus Project Lightning Talks for KubeCon + CloudNativeCon Only pass holders\[1\]\[3\]\[6\]
- **Tuesday (Nov. 10):** Opening keynotes, breakout sessions across multiple technical tracks, the Solutions Showcase, and Project Pavilion\[3\]
- **Wednesday (Nov. 11):** Keynotes, breakout sessions, the Solutions Showcase, Project Pavilion, and KubeCrawl + CloudNativeFest\[3\]\[7\]
- **Thursday (Nov. 12):** Final keynotes, breakout sessions, the Solutions Showcase, and the closing day of Project Pavilion\[3\]


### Build a plan based on priorities

## What to pack and wear at KubeCon


KubeCon is generally casual, so jeans or chinos, a polished T-shirt or collared shirt, and comfortable shoes will work for most conference days. Bring a slightly dressier option if you are presenting, hosting a dinner, or meeting with executives or customers.


Salt Lake City is typically cool and dry in early November, with average highs near 51°F and lows near 33°F. Temperatures can fall below freezing overnight, and early-season snow is possible.\[12\] Pack layers you can add for the walk to Salt Palace Convention Center and remove once you are inside. Salt Lake City also sits at an elevation of more than 4,200 feet, so the dry mountain air may leave you feeling thirstier than usual.


### Conference essentials


A few practical additions can make long conference days and cold walks through downtown Salt Lake City more comfortable:


- **Comfortable shoes:** Alternate between a few pairs of supportive, weather-appropriate shoes to give your feet a break after long days on convention-center floors and chilly or icy walks through downtown Salt Lake City.
- **Warm outer layers:** Bring a lightweight insulated jacket or warm coat for early mornings, evening events, and any unexpected cold snaps.
- **Light indoor layer:** Pack a cardigan, quarter-zip, or lightweight jacket for conference rooms that feel cooler than the temperature outside.
- **Refillable water bottle:** Dry air and altitude can make dehydration easier to overlook, especially when you are moving between sessions all day.
- **Portable charger:** Keep your phone powered for the conference app, messages, session notes, maps, and contact exchanges.
- **Lip balm and moisturizer:** Utah's dry climate can be noticeable within a day, particularly for travelers arriving from more humid areas.
- **Hand sanitizer and throat lozenges:** Keep both in your day bag for crowded sessions, frequent conversations, and dry indoor air.
- **Compact umbrella:** Pack a small umbrella if rain or early-season snow is in the forecast.


## Getting from SLC to Salt Palace Convention Center


Most attendees fly into Salt Lake City International Airport (SLC), roughly 8 miles from Salt Palace Convention Center.\[2\] The Utah Transit Authority (UTA) TRAX Green Line can take you from the airport to downtown in roughly 20 minutes; Arena Station and Temple Square Station are located within a half block of the convention center.\[2\]\[10\]


With checked luggage or a tight schedule, rideshare is usually the easiest door-to-door option. If you're traveling light and staying near a downtown TRAX stop, the Green Line can save you money. If your schedule and travel policy allow, arrive a day early so you have time to settle in and adjust to the elevation before the conference begins.

## Beyond sessions: Make time for the Solutions Showcase and networking


Some of the most valuable moments at KubeCon happen outside the presentation rooms. Leave time in your schedule to explore the Solutions Showcase, meet the maintainers behind CNCF projects, and connect with peers facing many of the same technical challenges as your team.


Here are a few ways to make the most of your time onsite:


- **Explore the Solutions Showcase and Project Pavilion:** Set aside dedicated time to compare tools, watch live demonstrations, and speak directly with vendors, maintainers, and CNCF contributors instead of trying to squeeze everything into short breaks between sessions.
- **Attend KubeCrawl + CloudNativeFest:** Included with registration, KubeCon's signature networking event combines sponsor exhibits, community programming, food, and informal conversations with attendees from across the cloud-native ecosystem.\[7\]
- **Make the most of Monday:** If you purchased an All-Access Pass, arrive in time for the CNCF co-located events. These focused programs offer deeper discussions on topics such as observability, platform engineering, GitOps, AI, Kubernetes at the edge, and open source security before the main conference begins.\[6\]
- **Schedule key conversations before you arrive:** Reach out to maintainers, partners, customers, vendors, or peers about two weeks before the conference to reserve time while everyone is still building their schedules.
- **Join community meetups:** Watch the conference app and CNCF community channels for project-specific meetups and informal gatherings announced throughout the week. They're a great way to connect with contributors and learn how to get involved beyond the conference.
- **Leave room for the unexpected:** Resist the temptation to fill every hour with sessions. Some of the best conversations happen while walking the show floor, attending community meetups, or simply introducing yourself between presentations.


KubeCon rewards curiosity as much as careful planning. The conversations you have with maintainers, contributors, vendors, and fellow attendees often become just as valuable as the sessions themselves, so give yourself enough flexibility to take advantage of those opportunities.

## Finish strong on departure day


The last few hours of KubeCon are a good opportunity to wrap up loose ends before you head back to work. A little preparation before your flight can make it easier to turn what you learned into action once you're back at your desk.


- **Pack the night before:** Charge your devices, set aside your travel clothes, and check your hotel room for chargers, conference swag, reusable water bottles, and anything else you may have picked up during the week.
- **Confirm your checkout time:** If you have a late flight, ask about late checkout or let Navan Edge help request it so you can work comfortably before heading to the airport.
- **Leave extra time for the airport:** Salt Lake City International Airport (SLC) is about 10–15 minutes from downtown, but plan to arrive at least two hours before a domestic flight or three hours before an international departure.
- **Submit your expenses while they're fresh:** Upload receipts and complete your expense report before you get pulled back into your regular workload.
- **Follow up with new contacts:** Send a quick LinkedIn message or email to the maintainers, vendors, customers, and peers you met while your conversations are still fresh.
- **Share what you learned:** Schedule time with your team to review key takeaways, compare notes, and decide which ideas, architectural discussions, or vendor evaluations deserve a closer look.
