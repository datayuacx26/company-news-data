## What is Web Summit and who is it for?


Web Summit is Europe's largest technology conference, hosted annually by Web Summit Ltd. This year, it runs Nov. 9–12, 2026 at Altice Arena (also called MEO Arena) and the Feira Internacional de Lisboa (FIL) exhibition center in Lisbon's Parque das Nações district.\[1\]


The 2026 edition is expected to attract 900-plus speakers, 2,300-plus startups, and 1,200-plus investors from more than 160 countries.\[1\] General attendee tickets are priced at €1,595, with group discounts available:


- 10% off for 5–9 tickets
- 15% off for 10–14 tickets
- 20% off for 15-plus tickets\[1\]


The crowd spans founders pitching on Centre Stage, investors scanning the startup exhibition, enterprise buyers evaluating SaaS and fintech vendors, developers in specialized tracks, and media covering the week's announcements. If your company sells to technology buyers, raises capital, or partners with startups, a meaningful share of your target market is accessible for four days.


November 9 is a pre-conference day featuring badge pickup, mentorship sessions, and opening night. The main conference runs November 10–12, with daily programming from 9 a.m.-5 p.m.\[1\]


Past speakers include Sam Altman (OpenAI), Geoffrey Hinton (University of Toronto), Ursula von der Leyen (European Commission President), Cristiano Amon (Qualcomm CEO), and Alexis Ohanian (Reddit co-founder).\[1\]


### Why Web Summit 2026 is worth the trip


Web Summit concentrates an unusual density of decision-makers in a four-day window. Your prospect's CTO could be walking the same expo hall. Your integration partner's product lead may be at the same Night Summit event. The investor who passed on your Series A could be attending and open to a conversation over espresso.


The conference spans 20-plus content tracks, including dedicated summits for fintech, SaaS, climate tech, marketing, and developer tools.\[1\] The real value is not only on the main stage. It's in the hallway track, partner dinners, and the conversations that happen after sessions end.


Beyond the official agenda, these are some can't-miss happenings:


- **Night Summit** , with curated networking events each evening across Lisbon's bars and restaurants
- **Partner-hosted dinners and demo nights** in the Chiado, Bairro Alto, and Cais do Sodré neighborhoods
- **264-plus additional events** during Web Summit week, listed on platforms like SideSummit and Luma\[2\]
- **PITCH competition finals** , where early-stage startups present on Centre Stage to global investors


Many of the week's best business conversations happen at these side events. The sessions give you context, but the dinners and Night Summit gatherings give you relationships. Plan your trip so you have the energy and calendar space to show up for both.

### Web Summit hotel pricing and when to book


Booking one to two months before check-in offered the best balance of price and availability in Navan's 2025 data. Despite having to pay nearly $100 more per night on average, 40% of conference travelers still booked within two weeks of arrival and 22.9% booked within seven days.\[14\]


Although you can book six or more months out to get your pick of rooms, the rate advantage is less predictable in a smaller sample. Here's when to book based on Navan data from 2025:


- **One to two months out:** Median rate of $404, with the best balance of price and availability
- **Two to four weeks out:** Median rate of $478, with inventory tightening
- **One to two weeks out:** Median rate of $502, as inventory drops


Compare any official hotel block against publicly available rates before booking. In some years the conference block offers the best value, but you may find lower prices by booking independently.


### Flights from the U.S. to Lisbon


Lisbon Humberto Delgado Airport (LIS) receives direct flights from several U.S. hubs, including New York/New Jersey (JFK/EWR), Boston, Washington Dulles, and Miami. Flight times range from seven to nine hours, depending on point of origin. For a conference starting Monday, November 9, arriving a day or two early — Saturday, November 7 or Sunday, November 8 — allows time to adjust to the time zone shift before the event begins.

## Set a realistic Lisbon budget


International destinations use per diem rates set by the U.S. State Department rather than domestic General Services Administration (GSA) rates. For Lisbon, the November 2026 allowance is:\[4\]


- **$350 per day:** Includes $249 for lodging and $101 per day for meals and incidental expenses (M&IE)
- **M&IE breakdown:** $81 for meals and $20 for incidentals
- **First and last travel days:** M&IE drops to 75% of the full rate ($75.75)


These rates are effective through December 31, 2026, and can be verified at[allowances.state.gov](http://allowances.state.gov/) .


### Budget considerations


Now compare that $249 lodging allowance to what Web Summit attendees actually pay. The median nightly rate near Altice Arena during Web Summit 2025 week was $444, a $195-per-night gap between the government allowance and conference-week market rates.\[14\] Booking one to two months ahead brought the median down to $404, but that still exceeds the per diem cap.


If your company reimburses at the State Department rate, you'll likely exceed the reimbursement limit unless you book early or stay in Chiado or Avenida da Liberdade. Be sure to also budget for:


- **Conference ticket:** €1,595 (approximately $1,820 USD)\[1\]
- **Public transportation:** Metro from LIS costs €1.90 each way
- **Rideshares and taxis:** Uber or Bolt runs €6–10; taxis cost €8–12\[5\]
- **Daily transit:** A daily Viva Viagem pass costs €6.80 and covers Metro, bus, and tram\[5\]
- **Client dinners:** €60–200 per person at Lisbon's business-caliber restaurants
- **After-hours events:** Some partner receptions and Night Summit-adjacent dinners carry separate costs beyond the conference ticket
- **Incidentals:** Coffee, snacks between sessions, and items you forgot to pack


Your $101 daily M&IE allowance goes further in Lisbon than in most U.S. conference cities. A quality lunch near the venue runs €12–18, and a dinner at a locally acclaimed restaurant runs €30–50 per person before drinks.

## Build your Web Summit agenda in advance


Web Summit typically publishes its session catalog several weeks before the conference, and the 1,000-plus speakers across 20-plus tracks creates two common challenges: over-scheduling (eight sessions per day with no gaps) or under-scheduling (winging it and missing the sessions you needed).\[1\] Most people do best with three to four sessions per day.


The Web Summit app is the single most important planning tool. It lets you browse the attendee list, schedule one-on-one meetings, and build a personal agenda before you land in Lisbon.\[6\] Download it as soon as registration is confirmed.


### Pre-conference checklist


- **Must-attend keynotes:** Centre Stage keynotes set the week's narrative and are recorded, so choose one or two relevant to your role to attend in-person.
- **Workshops and roundtables:** These fill fast and are not recorded, making in-person attendance essential.
- **Networking targets:** Identify 10–15 specific people you want to meet, send personalized outreach through the app or LinkedIn, and book meetings.
- **Badge pickup:** Get your badge on November 9 during the pre-conference period to avoid long queues on the first morning.\[6\]
- **Book dinner:** Confirm meetings with your networking targets and use Navan Edge to book dinner reservations for client meetings before and during Web Summit.
- **Email reminder:** Set your out-of-office auto-reply message with return date and emergency contact.
- **Prepare a concise introduction:** Be able to explain what you do, why it matters to others, and one specific ask.


Cognivix founder Daniele Bernardini, who has attended multiple Web Summits, was quoted on the conference blog, saying, “You should start preparing three months in advance. You should start contacting people, checking who is going to be there … preparation is the best investment of your time, because once you're here, it's too late.”\[7\]


## What to wear and pack for Web Summit, Lisbon


November highs in Lisbon average 17–19°C (63–66°F) and lows of 11–13°C (52–55°F), with a roughly 24% chance of rain on any given day.\[8\] The Parque das Nações area, situated along the Tagus River, can feel cooler in the evenings due to waterfront wind.


The dress code at Web Summit skews tech-casual, but you should match your audience — not the general crowd. A blazer over a fitted tee works for sessions and most dinners. Enterprise sellers trend slightly more formal, while startup founders trend toward branded hoodies.

- Snacks, supplements, and vitamins
- Hand sanitizer and electrolyte packets to stay hydrated and limit germ exposure
- A passport valid for at least three months beyond your departure date
- Digital copies of hotel confirmation, conference registration QR code, and transit passes

## The Web Summit schedule


With programming running from 9 a.m. to 5 p.m. across Centre Stage, the FIL exhibition halls, and specialized summit tracks, it's important to have a plan. Sessions in Altice Arena and the FIL halls can be a 10-minute walk apart, so build buffer time into your schedule or you'll spend half your day navigating corridors.


Consider this agenda:


- **Morning (9–10:30 a.m.):** Keynote or one high-priority session, then a scheduled 1:1 meeting over coffee
- **Midday (10:30 a.m.–1 p.m.):** Two sessions maximum, with breaks to process and note takeaways
- **Afternoon (1–4 p.m.):** Expo floor (startup demos, partner conversations, content track sessions)
- **Evening (5 p.m. onward):** One Night Summit event or client dinner


Workshops, roundtables, and sessions featuring speakers you want to meet afterward are one of the best uses of your time in person. For the expo floor, head there during Centre Stage keynotes when startup booths are less crowded, rather than immediately after lunch, when foot traffic peaks.

## Networking that's truly valuable


Post-conference connection requests sent within 48 hours outperform those delayed by a week or more, because the conversation is still fresh for both parties. Web Summit networking splits into three phases, and the preparation phase determines the outcome.


### Before the conference: Build your target list


Two weeks before the conference, identify 10–15 specific people to meet with. Look for prospects you've been trying to reach, partners whose product complements yours, and peers in your role at non-competing companies.


Use the Web Summit app to browse the attendee list and request 15-minute meetings.\[6\] A structured pre-event cadence suggests three touchpoints:


- Day -14 initial outreach
- Day -7 confirmation
- Day -1 logistics nudge\[9\]


### During the conference: Bring curiosity to the conversation


Capture details and context immediately after each conversation: name, company, what you discussed, and what you promised. Here's what to discuss:


- Ask what brought them to Web Summit this year (identifies their priority)
- Share one relevant insight from a session or trend you've observed (demonstrates value)
- Suggest a concrete next step before walking away (“I'll send you that case study by Friday”)


### After the conference: Follow up within 48 hours


Within two days of the conference ending, send personalized LinkedIn connection requests referencing your specific conversation. Deliver on anything you promised, and propose a concrete time to meet again with an agenda.


Following this exact pre-event targeting and post-event follow-up system, B2B outreach company hubsell reported generating 18 qualified opportunities from a single Web Summit.\[10\]

## How to avoid conference burnout at Web Summit


Four consecutive days with 15,000-plus steps, dozens of conversations, late-night events, and jet-lag adjustment create compounding fatigue that peaks on the final day. A seven-time Web Summit attendee from FusionAuth summarized the challenge for their company's blog: “Web Summit can be overwhelming. Schedule breaks, hydrate, and take time to relax to avoid burnout. Remember, it's a marathon not a sprint.”\[6\]


A few tips:


- **Take a break:** Every three hours, take a break. Walk along the Tagus River waterfront between sessions, return to your hotel for a 20-minute reset if staying in the venue district, or use the time to capture notes from your last conversation while details are fresh.
- **Get some sleep:** If you have a 9 a.m. meeting with a prospect, the Night Summit event that runs until midnight costs you the sharpest hours of the following day. Attend one evening event per night maximum. Choose based on who will be there rather than what is being served.
- **Prevent illness:** Use hand sanitizer, hydrate beyond what feels necessary, and carry a small bottle of saline nasal spray to combat dry air. If symptoms emerge, skip the evening event and get some extra sleep.

## Departure day: Late checkout, lounges, and making your flight


Most attendees leave Lisbon on November 13, which means hotels in Parque das Nações are busiest around the standard 11 a.m. checkout. If you have a later flight, request a late checkout when you book, or ask the front desk the night before. Hotel loyalty status may include complimentary late checkout, and most properties can store your luggage if you need somewhere to leave it while you squeeze in a final meeting or answer emails.


Pack the night before so your departure morning stays focused on wrapping up conversations rather than searching for chargers. When it's time to head to Lisbon Airport, the Metro Red Line from Oriente to Aeroporto is usually the fastest and most reliable option, taking about 10 minutes for €1.65. Rideshare services like Uber and Bolt are convenient, but expect higher prices during the late-morning checkout rush.


For international flights, arrive at LIS at least 2 hours before departure to allow time for security and passport control. If you have lounge access through your airline, credit card, or Priority Pass, arriving a little earlier can make the wait far more comfortable.

## After the summit: Re-entry and filing expenses


Returning from Lisbon to the U.S. means your body clock is five to eight hours ahead. Plan a light schedule for your first day back. Don't schedule a critical client call for 8 a.m. Eastern on the day you land from a transatlantic flight.


Be sure to file your expense report within 48 hours of returning while you still remember which dinner was a client meeting and which rideshare was to an event. Every receipt from Web Summit week involves EUR-to-USD conversion, but you still need to document the exchange rate used for each transaction, which most corporate cards provide automatically. Keep digital copies of all EUR receipts alongside their USD equivalents.
