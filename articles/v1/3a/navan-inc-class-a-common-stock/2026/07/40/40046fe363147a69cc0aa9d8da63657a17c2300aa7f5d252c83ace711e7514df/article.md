# Here’s why your 2026 T&E strategy needs a reality check

If your company is still managing travel and expenses with the tool you’ve always used, you may not just be dealing with a clunky interface — you may well be bleeding money.


After all, business travel is big business. According to the Navan[Business Travel Benchmark](https://navan.com/btb) , business travel volumes jumped 13.5% year-over-year in the first half of 2026. Companies are aggressively doubling down on face-to-face meetings to drive growth, even as average ticket prices climb.


But as travel activity accelerates, the friction points inside old, fragmented legacy architectures are fracturing. And as the industry’s pace of change also accelerates, companies still clinging to those legacy architectures are falling further behind.


To help travel and finance leaders navigate this ever-changing landscape, we just released our guide of the new realities defining corporate travel and expense now. It maps out the seven critical shifts reshaping corporate spending in real time.


[Download the full list now](https://navan.com/resources/ebook/new-realities-of-travel-and-expense-2026) , or read on for three of the biggest realities you can no longer afford to ignore.


## Real-Time Data Has Become Essential


Waiting weeks for a month-end close to find out which department blew past their budget is a relic of the past. In 2026, agile financial control requires real-time data architecture.


According to Skift and Navan’s[2026 State of Corporate Travel and Expense report](https://navan.com/resources/reports/state-of-corporate-travel-and-expense-2026) , 80% of companies believe they have access to the data they need, yet only 40% actually have real-time visibility into spending. That gap is exactly where overspending hides.


And it’s exactly why a unified platform is more important than ever. When your travel booking, corporate cards, and expense management system live in separate silos, you’re always looking backward. Modern platforms collapse these systems into a single loop. The moment an employee swipes a card or books a trip, the transaction data — including the traveler’s profile, trip purpose, and policy compliance status — flows automatically to finance.


The bottom line: Companies need to see spend as it happens, not weeks later.


## AI Automation Replaces Finance Team Busywork


For years, “AI” in expense management meant basic optical character recognition (OCR) that occasionally read a receipt correctly. In 2026, AI has moved past basic scanning into complex, proactive workflow automation.


It’s about time. A staggering 71% of business travelers still spend more than 30 minutes filing a single expense report, according to the Skift and Navan report, while 29% of T&E managers say their expense processing remains entirely manual.


Modern AI layers, like Navan’s[Expense Agent](https://navan.com/blog/ai-agents-for-finance) , eliminate this drag. Instead of forcing employees to manually code entries, AI automatically extracts more than 130 data points per transaction — handling general ledger (GL) auto-coding, cost centers, and tax categories based on historical organizational patterns.


The bottom line: Refocus your accounting team from auditing every single transaction to managing only flagged exceptions — it’s exactly the kind of efficiency AI was made for.


## Travel Support Finally Lives Up to Its Name


When travel disruptions hit, the gaps in legacy travel management companies (TMCs) become glaringly obvious. In 2026, a fragmented approach to travel support doesn’t just frustrate employees — it can drive up unmanaged spend and destroy traveler productivity.


Legacy platforms may route stranded employees through outsourced, tiered call centers with hours-long hold times. Modern travel systems, however, provide seamless, digital-first support directly inside the app, 24/7/365. With Navan, a proactive AI agent is the first line of defense, but humans are always on hand to help during flight cancellations or delays.


The bottom line: When travelers know their support system has their back, they’re more likely to book on-platform — that helps keep your travel program visible, compliant, and cost-effective.
