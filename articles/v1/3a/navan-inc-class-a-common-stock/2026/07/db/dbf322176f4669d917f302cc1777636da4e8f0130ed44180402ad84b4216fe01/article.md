# Black Hat USA 2026 travel guide: Where to stay, what to eat and more

## Key takeaways


Black Hat USA 2026 runs August 1–6 at the Mandalay Bay Convention Center in Las Vegas, and attendees who get the most from Hacker Summer Camp plan the trip as carefully as they plan their briefings.

- Las Vegas conference-week hotel rates often exceed the federal lodging per diem cap, so budget for the gap or consider Luxor or Excalibur for better value. (It's just a 10- to 15-minute indoor walk to the venue.)
- Harry Reid International Airport (LAS) sits about three miles from Mandalay Bay; rideshare or taxi transfers typically take 10–20 minutes when traffic on the Strip cooperates.

Black Hat USA 2026 brings security researchers, CISOs, red teamers, and vendor teams to Mandalay Bay for six days of trainings, summits, briefings, and the Business Hall.\[1\] The sessions are why you come, but it's the rest of your time that determines whether you leave with actionable threat intelligence and a full pipeline of follow-ups, or if you end up exhausted, over budget, and dehydrated. This guide walks through the full attendee lifecycle for Black Hat USA 2026, from booking your hotel to filing expenses after you land home.


## What is Black Hat USA and who is it for?


Black Hat USA is the cybersecurity industry's flagship event for researchers, practitioners, and executives, featuring peer-reviewed briefings, hands-on trainings, summits, and the Business Hall expo. The 2026 edition runs August 1–6 at the Mandalay Bay Convention Center in Las Vegas.\[1\]\[2\]


Black Hat reported 20,000 in-person attendees at its 2025 event, and the 2026 event is scheduled to feature 100+ briefings, 100+ trainings, 120+ sponsored sessions, and 80+ Arsenal demos.\[3\]\[4\] The crowd spans researchers presenting zero-day work, practitioners evaluating new tools, CISOs building vendor short lists, and red teamers comparing notes in the hallway.


### Why Black Hat USA 2026 is worth the trip


Black Hat is where the security community stress-tests ideas before they hit production blogs and vendor pitch decks. For practitioners, that means firsthand access to research, incident-response techniques, and AI-security frameworks you can't replicate by attending a webinar. For leaders, it means walking the Business Hall with a prioritized short list and the context to make budget decisions before locking in Q4 plans.


Beyond the official agenda, Hacker Summer Camp week includes:


- **August 1–4:** Intensive Trainings (Saturday through Tuesday) for hands-on skill-building
- **August 4:** Summit Day — CISO Summit, AI Summit, Healthcare Summit, Financial Threat Summit, and more invitation-only sessions\[1\]
- **August 4 onward:** Dozens of vendor parties along the Strip (community lists published on conferenceparties.com and #HackerSummerCamp)
- **August 5–6:** Briefings, Arsenal open-source tool demos, Business Hall expo, and keynotes
- **August 6:** DEF CON 34 badge pickup at Mandalay Bay, with the hacker conference opening the same day at the Las Vegas Convention Center, 15–20 minutes north\[5\]


If you're deciding whether the trip is worth approving internally, frame it around potential outcomes, like the specific threat research you'll need to brief leadership on, the vendor evaluations you'll complete in the Business Hall, or the Training certification that will advance your team's capabilities. Success means walking away with three to five vendor conversations that advance an evaluation, the technical clarity to brief your SOC on emerging threats, and lasting relationships.

We saw the biggest price spike in Navan's data one to two weeks before check-in; booking one to two months ahead can help avoid the priciest window. You can get lucky with last-minute rates, but inventory on the south Strip thins during Hacker Summer Camp. If Black Hat offers an official room block, compare that rate against open inventory before committing, because the block isn't always the cheapest option.

## Set a realistic Las Vegas budget before you go


Las Vegas may look affordable on paper, but Hacker Summer Camp can put you over budget, as it compresses three major security events into one week. So build your budget before finance asks for it, using the federal per diem as a floor and conference-week reality as the ceiling.


### GSA per diem for Las Vegas


The U.S. General Services Administration (GSA) sets federal per diem rates by city and season. For Las Vegas (Clark County) in August 2026, the standard figures are:


- **$212 per day:** Includes $126 per night for lodging and $86 per day for meals and incidental expenses (M&IE)
- **M&IE breakdown:** $18 for breakfast, $20 for lunch, $30 for dinner, and $5 for incidentals (deduct conference-provided meals from your meal claim as applicable)
- **First and last travel days:** M&IE is typically reimbursed at **75%** of the daily rate ($64.50) when you're in transit\[6\]


Note: GSA updates rates each October, so verify the current figure at gsa.gov before you travel.


Conference-week hotel rates near Mandalay Bay often exceed the federal lodging cap. Navan booking data from the August 2025 Black Hat week showed a $201 median nightly rate near the venue — roughly $75 above the $126 GSA lodging rate.\[9\] Budget for that gap or plan to stay at Luxor or Excalibur, where rates are often lower but still put you within a 10–15 minute indoor walk to the venue.


### Budget considerations


Set a daily spend target before you fly so you're not reconstructing costs from memory on the flight home.


- **Conference registration:** Briefings Pass, $2,999; Business Pass, $999; Summit Pass, $2,099; Training Pass, priced per course; Academic Pass, $1,999 (eligibility rules apply)\[15\]
- **Conference-provided meals:** These reduce your M&IE claim for those days, so track which lunches or receptions your pass includes.\[6\]
- **Airport transfer:** Regional Transportation Commission (RTC) buses from Harry Reid International Airport (LAS) cost about $6 to $10 with transfers; rideshares run $20 to $35 or more with surge pricing during Hacker Summer Camp arrival windows.\[7\]\[8\]
- **Client dinners:** Sit-down venues like STRIPSTEAK or Orla at Mandalay Bay will run you $75 to $150 per person.
- **After-hours vendor parties:** These are often free, but you may need to RSVP and budget for rideshare costs, cover charges, or hosted-bar minimums.
- **DEF CON crossover:** If you extend into DEF CON 34 (August 6–9), factor a potential hotel switch or additional rideshares between Mandalay Bay and the Las Vegas Convention Center.
- **Training pass add-ons:** Extras and last-minute briefings upgrades are easy to forget in a per diem spreadsheet but show up on personal cards by day three.


Note: Verify current badge rates at blackhat.com/us-26/registration.html before you book your trip.


## Build your agenda before you land: Sessions, side events, and the hallway track


If you're attending Black Hat USA for the first time, you need to build a sustainable conference schedule. Cap yourself at three to four sessions per day and protect white space for the Business Hall, Arsenal, and hallway lines where vendor and research conversations actually happen.


Before you fly to Las Vegas:


- **Star must-see briefings and Arsenal demos** in the Black Hat USA mobile app and schedule builder at blackhat.com/us-26; skip anything you'll watch on demand later.
- **Pre-book 10 to 15 target meetings** two weeks out, using a three-sentence LinkedIn or Black Hat outreach with a concrete 15-minute ask.
- **Download the app** , set your out-of-office email responder, and confirm that booked meetings have all the necessary details.
- **Choose a contact-sharing method** (QR, NFC, or LinkedIn with a note); when you save a contact, add one line of context about where you met and what you discussed.

### Networking that turns a conference into a pipeline


The highest-value work at Black Hat happens outside the briefing rooms. Treat networking as a before-during-after system, not something that happens spontaneously on the Business Hall floor. Aim for three to five genuine conversations per day, not badge volume.


- Use the **Black Hat mobile app** for real-time navigation, session capacity, and room changes across Mandalay Bay's convention levels.
- Official programming shifts by phase: Trainings (August 1–4), Summit Day (August 4), then Briefings and the Business Hall (August 5–6).\[1\]
- Unofficial Hacker Summer Camp parties fill the Strip from Tuesday onward — watch **#HackerSummerCamp** and community lists at conferenceparties.com.
- Crossing into **DEF CON 34** on August 6? Badge pickup runs 7 a.m. to 4 p.m. at the Mandalay Bay Ballroom Foyer before the conference moves to the Las Vegas Convention Center — plan the venue switch before Thursday night.\[2\]\[5\]


Leading up to Black Hat, build your target list, research recent disclosures or product launches, and send personalized outreach two weeks out with a specific 15-minute ask. At the event, avoid pitching products and instead ask questions and lean on colleagues for introductions.


Within 48 hours of connecting, send a short message referencing what you discussed, propose one concrete next step, and connect on LinkedIn with the same context. Block two hours on your calendar for follow-up before you leave Las Vegas — reply rates drop sharply after 48 hours.\[11\]


## What to wear and how to pack for Black Hat USA


Black Hat skews more casual than a banking conference but more polished than DEF CON. Think chinos or dark jeans with a collared shirt or technical tee, comfortable shoes, and an optional blazer for CISO Summit sessions or client dinners. Vendor reps wear company polos, and researchers lean toward jeans and sneakers.


Las Vegas in early August is brutal, with average highs around 102°F and lows around 78°F, with minimal rainfall.\[10\] You'll spend most of your day in air-conditioned convention halls, but the parking-lot walk and tram transfers mean breathable fabrics beat blazers outdoors. Keep a light layer for over-air-conditioned briefings rooms.


### The experienced traveler's packing list


If you're presenting or hosting executives, don't forget to pack a dressier outfit alongside your comfortable day-to-day clothing. Additionally, be sure to pack:


- **Two pairs of shoes:** Convention floors are hard, and the Mandalay Bay complex is sprawling. Rotating shoes prevents blisters after 15,000-step days.
- **Portable battery (20,000mAh or larger):** Your phone is your badge, map, calendar, and contact exchange tool.
- **Refillable water bottle:** Desert heat and dry convention air dehydrate you faster than you notice.
- **Hand sanitizer and basic immune support:** Thousands of attendees in enclosed spaces make “conference crud” a real risk by day four.
- **Earplugs:** Useful for evening vendor parties and light sleepers in busy hotels on the Strip.
- **Light sweater or jacket:** Briefings rooms run cold despite the heat outside.

Plan to arrive the night before trainings or briefings begin if your schedule allows. You'll start day one rested instead of recovering from a red-eye, and you can collect your badge at the Bayside Ballroom Foyer on Level 1 before Saturday training registration opens.\[2\]

## Dinners, dining, and staying fueled


Try to eat a filling breakfast before morning trainings, and grab lunch away from the longest Business Hall lines. Keep protein bars or nuts in your bag for sessions that run long. Hydration matters more than caffeine stacking by day four, especially in the August heat, so keep your water bottle full.


Depending on your meal goals, here are some restaurant suggestions:


- **Client dinners:** STRIPSTEAK by Michael Mina inside Mandalay Bay handles business dinners with private dining rooms steps from the convention center.\[12\]
- **Team meals:** Border Grill sits between the Shark Reef and the Mandalay Bay Convention Center, with two floors of patio dining for group meals without white-tablecloth formality.\[13\]
- **Quick fuel/business meals:** Orla, Chef Michael Mina's Mediterranean restaurant at Mandalay Bay, works for sit-down lunches and pre-briefings dinners; request a back table if you want quieter conversation away from the casino floor.\[14\]

## How to avoid conference burnout


Black Hat packs six days of technical depth into a city that runs 24/7. Schedule less than you think you can handle, especially when lodging already stretches your per-diem cap.


- **Take a break every three hours:** Find a quiet corner in the Business Hall, take a walk through Mandalay Place, or enjoy a 20-minute hotel reset in air conditioning.
- **Prioritize rest:** If you have morning trainings or CISO Summit sessions, get a good night's rest rather than hitting another vendor party.
- **Hydrate:** Load your water up with electrolytes and avoid walking outdoors during peak heat.
- **Urban awareness at night:** Stick to well-lit routes between Mandalay Bay and your hotel, and use rideshare from evening events if you're unfamiliar with the area.


## Departure day: Late checkout, lounges, and making your flight


Pack the night before you head to the airport, because Friday checkout at Mandalay Bay-aligned hotels is busy during Hacker Summer Camp. Lay out travel clothes and chargers, and keep your badge by the door.


- **Late checkout:** Ask at check-in and remind the front desk the night before; loyalty status helps, and Navan Edge's 24/7 human travel experts can request late checkout when your flight is in the evening.
- **LAS lounge access:** If your credit card includes it, Harry Reid International Airport (LAS) has lounge options in multiple terminals.
- **Buffer time:** Conference-week security lines run longer, so arrive at least two hours before domestic departures and three for international travel.


## After Black Hat USA: Expenses, re-entry, and the wrap up


Submit receipts within a few days, not weeks, because rideshares, meals, training add-ons, and DEF CON badges add up fast. Some additional post-conference tips:


- **Approvals:** If lodging ran **above your per-diem cap** , flag it for your approver when you submit.
- **International travelers:** Keep the original currency you paid with on receipts and convert only per your employer's policy.
- **Re-entry:** Whether you're traveling domestically or internationally, block your first day back for email triage — not back-to-back internal meetings.
- **Social media takeaway:** While Black Hat USA is still fresh in your mind, share one LinkedIn post with the most powerful takeaway and tag a relevant researcher, colleague, or vendor.
