# Dreamforce 2026: Your complete attendee travel guide

Dreamforce draws more than 40,000 people to San Francisco every September at the Moscone Center for Salesforce's ecosystem, its partners, and anyone selling to, buying from, or competing with them. The sessions are valuable. The hallway conversations are even more valuable. And the dinner you have with a prospect during the conference might be the most valuable moment of the entire week.


This guide covers the logistics that make the conference worth attending: how to book smart, budget accurately, build a session strategy that won't leave you burned out by day two, and follow up in a way that turns conversations into pipeline.


## Why Dreamforce 2026 is worth the trip


Dreamforce 2026 runs September 15 through 17 at Moscone Center (North, South, and West halls) in San Francisco. This year's theme is “Agentic Enterprise,” and Marc Benioff will deliver the opening keynote alongside speakers including Sterling K. Brown, Swan Sit, Tony Robbins, Sheryl Lee Ralph, Matthew McConaughey, and Renée Richardson Gosline.


The scale is staggering: 1,600+ sessions, 150+ hands-on trainings, and an attendee count that regularly tops 40,000. A full conference pass costs $2,299, though early-bird pricing drops to $1,499 if you register before July 16, and groups of five or more pay $999 per pass. Every pass includes all keynotes, breakout sessions, theater sessions, workshops, roundtables, the Welcome Reception, Dreamfest, and daily refreshments plus lunch.


But the real value isn't on the main stage. It's in the hallway track, the partner receptions, and the community meetups. It's also in the dinners that happen after the sessions end, as Dreamforce week extends well beyond the boundaries of the convention center: dev happy hours, exec dinners, and Dreamfest (a concert event included with every pass) fill the evenings.


### Who attends


The crowd is broad; it includes Salesforce admins, developers, CTOs, sales leaders, consultants, startup founders, and partner ecosystem executives. If your company uses Salesforce or sells to companies that do, a significant share of your target market walks Moscone's halls for three days straight. The density of decision-makers per square foot is hard to replicate at any other single event.


For anyone considering a pre-event deep dive, the Trailblazer Bootcamp runs September 12 through 14. It's three additional days of hands-on training before the main conference begins.


## Book your flights and a hotel near Moscone Center early


Conference-week hotel prices near Moscone don't behave like regular San Francisco rates. An anonymized analysis of 1,337 hotel bookings near Moscone Center during Dreamforce 2025 week shows a median nightly rate of $569 — an 87% premium over a normal September week's median rate of $305.


The booking window matters more than most travelers realize. Those who booked two or more months before check-in paid a median of $483 per night. Those who waited until the last minute paid $587. Yet 36.4% of travelers still booked within two weeks of the conference, and 22.8% booked within seven days. The sweet spot is clear. Book early or pay the procrastination tax.


The official hotel block deadline is August 21, 2026. Compare the block rate against open-market pricing before committing. In some years, early open-market rates undercut the block; in others, the block guarantees availability when everything else sells out. Check both.


### Where to stay


Three hotel zones work for Dreamforce, each with a different trade-off:


SoMa (5-minute walk to Moscone). Marriott Marquis, InterContinental, Hyatt Regency, The Clancy. Highest convenience, highest price. If walkability is non-negotiable, this is your zone.


Union Square (10 to 12 minutes on foot). Better restaurant selection, BART and Muni access at Powell Station. One caveat: The east side of Union Square borders the Tenderloin, so check your hotel's specific block before booking, especially if you'll be walking back late.


Embarcadero (15-minute commute). Best waterfront views and waterfront dining along the bay. Ideal if you're comfortable with a short rideshare or Muni ride and want a quieter neighborhood for the evenings. The Ferry Building has strong breakfast and coffee options in this zone.

## Set a realistic San Francisco budget before you go


San Francisco's GSA per diem rate for September 2026 is $272 for lodging and $92 for meals and incidental expenses (M&IE), totaling $364 per day. The M&IE breaks down to $23 for breakfast, $26 for lunch, $38 for dinner, and $5 for incidentals. On your first and last travel days, M&IE drops to $69 (75% of the full rate). Rates are subject to change; verify current figures at GSA.gov.


Now compare that $272 lodging allowance to what Dreamforce attendees actually pay. The median nightly rate near Moscone during Dreamforce 2025 week was $569 — a $297-per-night gap between what the government considers reasonable and what the market charges during the conference.


If your company reimburses at the GSA rate, you're structurally underfunded unless you book early (2+ months out brought the median down to $483) or stay farther from Moscone. If you're self-funding, budget $500 to $600 per night for a walkable SoMa hotel, or $350 to $450 for Union Square or Embarcadero.


For a 3-night stay (the most common), hotel costs alone run $1,450 to $1,800 near Moscone at conference-week rates. Add the $2,299 conference pass, flights, meals, and ground transportation, and a realistic all-in budget for Dreamforce is $4,500 to $6,000 per attendee. The early-bird pass ($1,499 through July 16) and early hotel booking ($483 median at 2+ months out) are the two biggest levers for reducing that number. Groups of five or more get an even steeper conference discount at $999 per pass, which is worth coordinating if multiple team members are attending.


### Hidden costs to plan for


- **Rideshare surge pricing.** Conference week pushes Uber and Lyft prices up across SoMa, especially during morning arrivals and evening event rushes.
- **Premium dinners.** Client dinners near Moscone run $75 to $200+ per person at the restaurants worth visiting.
- **After-hours events.** Some partner receptions and exec dinners are ticketed separately from the conference pass.
- **Conference-provided meals.** Your pass includes daily refreshments and lunch, which offsets the M&IE deduction. Factor that savings into your daily budget.

## Build your agenda before you land


Since Dreamforce features 1,600+ sessions and 150+ hands-on trainings, the temptation may be to pack every minute with one of them. Resist it. Three to four sessions per day is the sustainable cap; beyond that, retention drops and fatigue compounds.


Prioritize formats that only work in person. Workshops, roundtables, and hands-on training require physical presence. Keynotes and theater sessions are often available on replay via Salesforce+ after the conference. Skip the replayable ones live and rather use that time for conversations that can only happen face-to-face.


### Pre-conference checklist


- **Download the Dreamforce mobile app.** Session schedules, venue maps, and real-time capacity updates live there. Build your agenda in the app before you land.
- **Set OOO messages.** Block your calendar for the conference dates and the travel day on either side.
- **Confirm meetings.** If you've scheduled coffees or 1:1s, reconfirm 48 hours before. No-shows spike at large conferences.
- **Prepare your contact-sharing method.** QR codes and NFC-based sharing (via Apple Wallet or LinkedIn) are faster than business cards. Whatever method you choose, add a note about the conversation topic within an hour of each exchange. The note is what makes the contact useful later.


Build a target list of 10 to 15 people you want to meet. Send personalized outreach two weeks before the conference. Include something specific: Reference a session they're speaking at, a product they launched, or a mutual connection. Generic “let's connect at Dreamforce” messages get ignored.


If you're bringing a team, assign coverage. Who attends which sessions? Who targets which contacts? Divide the conference so each person covers different ground, then merge insights at the end of each day. Three people attending the same keynote produces one takeaway — three people in three different rooms produces three.


## What to wear and what to pack for Dreamforce


The dress code at Dreamforce is tech-casual: blazer over a tee, clean sneakers or loafers, no ties required. Adjust upward if you're in client-facing meetings or pitching investors; a sport coat and collared shirt still signals “I take this seriously” without overdressing for the crowd.


September is San Francisco's warmest month, with average highs of 70 to 73 degrees Fahrenheit, lows of 56 to 57 degrees, and minimal rain. But SF's microclimates mean SoMa (near Moscone) runs warmer while western neighborhoods are cooler and foggier. Evening temperatures drop noticeably. Bring layers.


### Experienced-traveler packing list


- **Two pairs of shoes.** Bring broken-in walking shoes for the convention floor (expect 20,000+ steps per day) and a dinner-appropriate pair for evening events.
- **20,000mAh battery pack.** Your phone is your conference app, your map, your contact-sharing tool, and your camera. It won't last three days on its own.
- **Reusable water bottle.** Moscone has refill stations. Hydration is the single easiest burnout prevention tool.
- **Hand sanitizer and immune support.** 40,000+ people in enclosed spaces means “conference crud” is a real risk. Zinc, vitamin C, and hand hygiene reduce your odds of getting sick.
- **Earplugs or noise-canceling headphones.** For sleeping and quiet breaks during the conference.
- **Light jacket.** For evening walks and air-conditioned session rooms.
- **Chargers and adapters.** International attendees: the U.S. uses Type A/B plugs.

### When attendees actually arrive


Anonymized flight data from Dreamforce 2025 week shows 5,920 flights carrying business travelers to SFO and OAK, with 25.2% originating internationally. The NYC metro area was the largest corridor with 907 flights, followed by Melbourne (279), Atlanta (277), Toronto (266), and Seattle (257).


Most hotel check-ins happened on Monday, with Thursday being the most common checkout day. Most people arrive Sunday evening or Monday morning and departing Thursday.


Arrive the night before. If your first session is Tuesday morning, fly in Monday evening. The cost of one extra hotel night is a fraction of the productivity you lose by arriving jet-lagged on day one. For cross-country or international arrivals, shift your sleep schedule 30 minutes per day in the days before departure, get daylight exposure immediately after landing, and skip the night-zero social events. A 25% international attendee base means a quarter of the room is fighting jet lag during Tuesday morning's opening keynote. Don't be one of the attendees who slept only four hours.

## During the conference


The hallway track is the real conference. The sessions give you content. The conversations between sessions give you relationships, deals, and intelligence you can't get from a slide deck.


Target three to five quality conversations per day. That's not “scan a badge and move on.” That's a genuine exchange where you learn something, share something, and walk away with a reason to follow up. The sessions give you knowledge, but the hallway makes way for new relationships and competitive intelligence that never make it into the keynote slides.


### Session strategy


- **Attend in-person-only formats.** Workshops, hands-on training, and roundtables where the interaction is the product.
- **Skip sessions that will be on replay.** Keynotes and theater sessions are typically available via Salesforce+ within days. Watch them at 1.5x speed from your hotel.
- **Leave gaps.** A packed schedule eliminates the serendipity that makes conferences valuable. Leave at least one open hour per half-day.


### Side events and after-hours


Dreamforce's orbit includes partner receptions, community-led meetups, dev happy hours, exec dinners, and Dreamfest, which is included with every conference pass. To find them:


- Check the Dreamforce mobile app for official side events.
- Search the conference hashtag on LinkedIn and X (formerly Twitter).
- Ask exhibitors and sponsors directly; many host invite-only dinners that are announced at the booth.
- Watch your email the week before; partner invitations spike in the final 10 days.


The Welcome Reception on opening night is worth attending. It's low-pressure, well-organized, and gives you a chance to get oriented before the sessions begin. Use it to scope out the venue layout, test your contact-sharing method on someone low-stakes, and identify the quiet corners you'll retreat to later in the week.


## Networking that turns Dreamforce into pipeline


Networking at a 40,000-person conference without a plan is just socializing. The framework that works is simple: before, during, and after.


### Before Dreamforce


Build a target list of 10 to 15 people. Use the attendee list (if published), speaker bios, and LinkedIn to identify who you want to meet. Send personalized outreach two weeks before the conference. Reference something specific: a session they're presenting, a product they launched, a shared connection. Schedule coffees and 1:1 meetings outside the main session blocks.


### During Dreamforce


Lead with curiosity, not a pitch. Ask what they're working on, what sessions surprised them, what problems they're trying to solve. The goal is information exchange, not a sales call.


Use a buddy system if you're attending with colleagues. Split up and work different rooms, different sessions, different dinners. Debrief each evening to share contacts and insights.


Capture context immediately. After every meaningful conversation, take 30 seconds to write a note about who you spoke with, what you discussed, what they care about, and what you promised to send. A phone photo of their badge plus a voice memo works. The context is what makes the follow-up land.


One underrated tactic: Schedule a 15-minute debrief with your team or travel companion at the end of each day. Share contacts, compare notes on sessions, and flag anyone the other person should meet tomorrow. Two people covering the same conference should produce twice the output, not twice the overlap.


### After Dreamforce


The 48-hour follow-up window is the single most important networking rule. Within two days of the conference ending, send a personalized email referencing the specific conversation you had. Instead of saying “Great to meet you at Dreamforce!”, send something that proves you were paying attention.


After 48 hours, the memory fades, the inbox fills, and your message becomes noise. The window is real.


The follow-up doesn't need to be long; three sentences will do it. Remind them who you are, reference what you discussed, and propose a next step. “Great meeting you at the Data Cloud session. You mentioned struggling with attribution across Salesforce instances. Here's the case study I mentioned, and I'd love to set up a 20-minute call next week to walk through it.”

## How to avoid conference burnout


Three days at a 40,000-person conference will test your stamina. The attendees who leave Dreamforce with energy and results instead of exhaustion and regret follow a few deliberate patterns.


Schedule less than you think you can handle. If you can attend six sessions, plan for four. The margins are where the best conversations happen.


Break every three hours. Moscone has quiet areas on the upper levels. Your hotel room is a 5-minute walk if you're in SoMa. A 20-minute reset between session blocks makes the afternoon productive instead of performative.


Sleep beats afterparties. If you have a 7 a.m. meeting or a session you flagged as must-attend, the 11 p.m. partner reception is not worth it. Choose deliberately.


Hydrate and eat real food. A reusable water bottle and a real lunch are more effective than any productivity hack. Convention center air is dry, the walking is relentless (20,000+ steps per day on those floors), and skipping meals compounds fatigue fast.


Conference crud prevention. Use hand sanitizer before and after every handshake. Take zinc and vitamin C starting two days before arrival. Bring earplugs for better hotel sleep. These aren't paranoid measures; they're practical tips in a crowd of 40,000.


Personal safety in SoMa. San Francisco's SoMa neighborhood is generally safe during conference hours. After dark, stick to well-lit streets and avoid the 6th Street corridor. Rideshare back to your hotel if you're out late.


## Tips for departure day


Most Dreamforce attendees check out on Thursday. The morning rush at SoMa hotels is predictable, and so is the rideshare surge to SFO.


Pack the night before. Don't wait until the morning of — you might want the final morning for a last coffee, a quick meeting, or simply a calm start.


Get a late checkout. Ask at check-in, not at checkout. Loyalty status (even mid-tier) often qualifies you for a 2 p.m. checkout. If you're using Navan Edge, its 24/7 human travel experts can negotiate late checkout directly with the hotel, freeing you to focus on your last conference meetings instead of haggling at the front desk.


Use airport lounge access. SFO's terminals have multiple lounge options. An Amex Platinum card gets you into the Centurion Lounge in Terminal 3. Priority Pass opens up several other options. If you have a long wait, a lounge beats a gate seat after three days on a convention center floor.


Buffer your travel time. Conference-week rideshare surge is real. Add 30 minutes to whatever Google Maps says. If you're taking BART, the trip is predictable, but factor in the walk from your hotel to Powell Station with luggage.


Skip the hotel breakfast rush. Thursday morning at SoMa hotels is chaotic. Every Dreamforce attendee is checking out, eating breakfast, and calling rideshares at the same time. Grab coffee to go, get to the airport early, and eat at the lounge or gate. You'll save 30 minutes of lobby congestion.


## After Dreamforce 2026


The conference ended, but there's still work that can be done to ensure you get the most out of it.


### The 48-hour follow-up window


This is the anchor of your post-conference week. Within 48 hours, send every follow-up email you committed to. Each one should reference the specific conversation: what you discussed, what they mentioned caring about, and what you're sending or proposing. After the 48-hour window, response rates drop sharply. Treat it as a deadline, not a suggestion.


### Share what you learned


Post on LinkedIn within 24 hours. Tag the speakers who impressed you. Share one concrete takeaway, not a generic “great event.” The visibility compounds: Your contacts see the post, the speakers see the tag, and people who couldn't attend get value from your perspective.


Schedule a team debrief within one week. Cover key themes, contacts made, follow-ups in progress, and competitive intelligence gathered. One person's Dreamforce trip should generate insights the whole team can use. Structure the debrief around three questions: What did we learn that changes our approach, who did we meet that we should engage, and what did competitors announce that we should respond to?


### File your expense report


File while the details are fresh. Capture receipts for every client dinner, rideshare, and incidental before they blur together. If your company uses per diem reimbursement, document the dates and meals clearly to ensure you're reimbursed accurately.


### Gentle re-entry


Don't fill your calendar the day after you return. Leave the first day back for processing: follow-ups, expense filing, and decompression. The worst post-conference move is diving into back-to-back meetings with a jet-lagged brain and an overflowing inbox.


Block the Monday after Dreamforce as a “recovery and action” day. Use the morning for follow-up emails (the 48-hour window is closing). Use the afternoon for your team debrief. By Tuesday, you're back in normal operating mode with every contact captured, every expense filed, and every takeaway shared.
