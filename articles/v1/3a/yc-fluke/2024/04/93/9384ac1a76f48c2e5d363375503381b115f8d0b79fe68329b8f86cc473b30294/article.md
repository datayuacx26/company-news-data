# Instrument Calibration and Why It Matters

# Instrument Calibration and Why It Matters


[Calibration](https://www.fluke.com/en-us/learn/blog/calibration)


Manufacturing requires accuracy for consistent outputs. But that isn’t easy to achieve if you don’t know what your inputs are. While instrument calibration may only slightly affect inputs, that can still significantly impact your outputs, the longevity of your equipment, and more. Calibrating your equipment ensures your readings are accurate and allows you to make necessary adjustments or repairs before any issues arise.


Instrument readings can change over time as instruments are exposed to harsh working environments, vibrations, temperature changes, and even accidents such as being dropped. Calibration ensures that your instruments produce accurate readings and that your equipment runs at peak performance.


## What Is Instrument Calibration?


Instrument calibration compares an instrument’s readings to those on a calibrator or reference instrument. The instrument being tested may need to be adjusted to ensure it provides correct readings.


Many different types of instruments require calibration, and the process may look different depending on the readings being measured and the type of equipment. Regardless of the instrument being tested, calibration steps include:


- Ensuring the correct testing environment
- Running the calibration test, preferably more than once, to ensure accuracy
- Recording results
- Adjusting the instrument or sending it to be adjusted by an instrument calibration service, if necessary
- Testing again and recording the results after the adjustment


The proper calibration of instruments is key to your organization’s production and overall functionality. But there are many additional reasons to ensure your instruments are properly calibrated.


## Why is instrument calibration important?


Regularly calibrating your instruments is necessary for many reasons. Depending on your industry, instrument calibration could be heavily regulated, and you could be subject to fines, product recalls, or other costly consequences if your instruments aren’t properly calibrated.


**Safety:** Inaccurate calibrations can cause safety issues for your employees. Machines and processes running at incorrect pressures or temperatures can create an unsafe work environment and even lead to employee injury, worker’s compensation claims, missed work, and other hardships for your employees.


**Compliance:** Certain industries have regulations that require calibration standards for production factors. For example, humidity and temperatures must be carefully controlled in food or pharmaceutical applications, and inaccurate readings can cause wasted or unsafe products.


**Consistency:** No matter what type of product you produce, your customers expect a certain level of consistency and quality. As the calibration of your instruments drifts over time, this can cause a decrease in the quality of your products, resulting in unhappy customers and recalled products, as well as damage to the reputation you’ve built for yourself.


**Longevity:** When temperatures, pressure, and other measurements are improperly calibrated, it can cause unnecessary wear and tear on your equipment. This can reduce the life of your machines, increase downtime, increase time spent on maintenance and repairs, and ultimately cost you money.


**Cost savings:** Calibrating machines takes time, but this is not an area where you want to cut corners. Properly calibrated machines waste less raw material, resulting in higher-quality outputs. They also function more smoothly, which will result in cost savings over the long haul.


## When Should an Instrument Be Calibrated?


Now that we’ve discussed what calibration is and why it’s important, you may wonder[how often you should calibrate your instruments](https://www.fluke.com/en-us/learn/blog/calibration/how-often-should-you-calibrate) . The answer varies widely depending on the instruments, the applicable outside regulations (such as by the FDA or other governing authority), the frequency with which the instrument is being used, and more.


Here are a few guidelines you can use to help determine your calibration frequency:


- **Manufacturer’s recommendations:** The manufacturer that created the instrument typically has a calibration frequency recommendation. However, compliance with regulatory agencies may require more frequent calibration.


- **Before or after a critical measuring project:** If you’re making decisions based on the output of your equipment, you’ll want to ensure everything is calibrated correctly so you can ensure your decision is made using the most accurate measurements.


- **After an event:** Instruments should be calibrated after an event, such as an accident or exposure to excessive vibration or other environmental stresses.


- **At regularly scheduled intervals** : These intervals could be monthly, quarterly, semiannually, or some other set interval time.

## What are the Types of Calibration?


Calibration can be categorized into several types based on the instruments and parameters being measured.


Electrical calibration involves instruments that measure electrical properties such as voltage, current, resistance, and capacitance, with standard instruments including multimeters, oscilloscopes, power supplies, and signal generators.


Temperature calibration focuses on instruments measuring temperature, like thermometers, thermocouples, RTDs (Resistance Temperature Detectors), and temperature sensors, ensuring accurate temperature readings across their operating range, which is essential for food processing, pharmaceuticals, and HVAC industries.


Pressure calibration applies to instruments measuring pressure, including pressure gauges, transducers, barometers, and manometers, ensuring accurate pressure measurements for critical applications in sectors like oil and gas, aviation, and manufacturing.


Mechanical calibration involves devices that measure mechanical properties such as mass, force, torque, and dimensions, including balances, scales, force gauges, and torque wrenches. This ensures precise mechanical measurements crucial for engineering and manufacturing processes.


Dimensional calibration pertains to instruments measuring geometric properties, such as calipers, micrometers, and gauges, ensuring the accuracy of dimensional measurements in precision engineering and manufacturing.


Lastly, flow calibration focuses on instruments that measure the flow rate of liquids or gases, such as flow meters and anemometers, ensuring accurate flow measurements for applications in fluid dynamics, chemical processing, and HVAC systems.


### When Should Instrument Calibration be Performed?


To maintain accuracy, instrument calibration should be performed at specific intervals and under certain conditions. When a new instrument is first put into service, it should be calibrated to establish a baseline of accuracy, ensuring it meets the manufacturer's specifications and industry standards. Regular, periodic calibration is essential and should be conducted based on the manufacturer's recommendations, industry standards, or regulatory requirements, with common intervals ranging from monthly to annually, depending on the instrument's usage and criticality.


Calibration should also be performed after any repair, servicing, or component replacement to verify the instrument's accuracy and performance. For instruments used in critical applications or those sensitive to environmental changes, pre-use calibration ensures the instrument provides accurate readings for critical measurements. Calibration should also be performed after significant events, such as exposure to extreme temperatures, shocks, or vibrations, to identify and correct any potential impact on the instrument's performance. Lastly, there are indications that an instrument's readings are not accurate or consistent. In that case, recalibration should be performed to diagnose and rectify any issues, preventing erroneous measurements and maintaining data reliability.


### Basics of Instrument Calibration


Understanding the fundamentals of instrument calibration is crucial for maintaining measurement accuracy. Calibration should be traceable to national or international standards to ensure the reliability and comparability of measurements, providing a chain of comparisons linking the instrument to recognized standards. Using reference standards with known and documented accuracy is essential, as these standards serve as the benchmark for comparing and adjusting the instrument's readings. These reference standards themselves should be regularly calibrated to maintain their accuracy.


Standardized calibration procedures ensure consistency and accuracy, typically comparing the instrument's readings with the reference standard and making necessary adjustments. Conducting calibration in controlled environmental conditions minimizes external factors, such as temperature, humidity, and electromagnetic interference, that could affect measurement accuracy. Maintaining detailed records of the calibration process, including the date, conditions, reference standards used, and results, is essential for traceability, compliance, and future reference. Additionally, assessing and documenting the uncertainty associated with the calibration process helps understand the instrument's accuracy and reliability limits.


#### Calibration Requirements


Calibration requirements vary based on industry standards, regulatory guidelines, and specific applications. Adhering to industry-specific standards and procedures for calibration, such as ISO/IEC 17025 for testing and calibration laboratories or ANSI/NCSL Z540 for Calibration in the United States, ensures that calibration processes meet recognized quality and accuracy criteria. Ensuring compliance with regulatory requirements relevant to your industry, such as FDA regulations for medical devices or EPA standards for environmental monitoring instruments, ensures that instruments meet legal and safety standards.


Following the calibration intervals and procedures recommended by the instrument's manufacturer is crucial for optimal performance and longevity. Implementing a robust quality management system, such as ISO 9001, which includes calibration as a critical component, ensures consistent and accurate measurements and helps maintain systematic and reliable calibration processes. Maintaining comprehensive calibration records, including certificates of calibration, is essential for demonstrating compliance and traceability during audits and inspections, helping track the calibration history, and identifying any recurring issues. Lastly, ensuring that calibration is performed by qualified personnel with the necessary training and expertise is crucial for maintaining the integrity and accuracy of the calibration process.


#### The Calibration Process Explained


The calibration process involves several detailed steps to ensure accuracy and reliability. First, preparation consists of gathering all necessary tools and reference standards and guaranteeing the calibration environment is stable and free from disturbances. Initial testing measures the instrument's output against the reference standard to establish its current accuracy. If discrepancies are found, adjustments are made to the instrument's settings to align with the reference standard. Verification then re-tests the instrument to confirm that the adjustments have corrected errors. Finally, documentation of all measurements, adjustments, and results is maintained, providing a comprehensive record for traceability and future reference.


## Common Challenges in Instrument calibration


Several challenges can arise during the calibration process. Environmental factors such as temperature, humidity, and pressure can affect measurement accuracy, making it crucial to control these factors during calibration. Instrument drift over time can cause instruments to deviate from their calibrated settings, necessitating more frequent calibrations. Calibration uncertainty is an inherent aspect of the process, and understanding and minimizing this uncertainty is essential for accurate measurements. Ensuring compliance with relevant industry standards and regulations can also be complex, requiring specialized knowledge and training.

Choosing the right calibration service provider is crucial for ensuring the accuracy and reliability of your instruments. The provider's accreditation and certification by a recognized body such as ISO/IEC 17025 are vital factors to consider. Evaluate the provider's experience and expertise, looking for a proven track record in calibrating the specific types of instruments you use. Consider the services offered, including on-site calibration, repair, and recalibration services. Assess the provider's turnaround time to ensure timely services without compromising quality and evaluate the quality of customer support, including the availability of technical assistance and responsiveness to inquiries.


### Impact of Calibration on Quality Management


Calibration plays a critical role in quality management systems (QMS). Accurate measurements are fundamental to maintaining product quality and consistency. By integrating regular calibration into your QMS, you can ensure that all measurement instruments used in production and quality control are reliable and accurate. This integration helps maintain compliance with international standards such as ISO 9001 and contributes to continuous improvement efforts. Regular calibration supports root-cause analysis by providing reliable data for identifying and addressing process variations and defects.


#### Understanding Calibration Certificates


Calibration certificates are essential documents that provide proof of calibration and details about the calibration process. They typically include information such as the instrument identification, make, model, serial number, and calibration date. Information on the reference standards used and their traceability are also included. Measurement results are documented, showing initial and final readings and any adjustments made. The uncertainty associated with the calibration process is also recorded, providing an understanding of the limits of the instrument's accuracy. Information about the calibration technician is included, ensuring accountability and traceability.


#### Future Trends in Instrument Calibration


Advancements in technology are continuously improving the calibration process. Some emerging trends include the increasing use of automated calibration systems to enhance accuracy and efficiency. Wireless calibration devices are being developed to allow for remote calibration and monitoring. The transition to digital calibration records is also gaining traction, making it easier to manage and trace calibration data. Additionally, artificial intelligence is being used to predict calibration needs and optimize calibration schedules, further enhancing the efficiency and accuracy of the calibration process.
