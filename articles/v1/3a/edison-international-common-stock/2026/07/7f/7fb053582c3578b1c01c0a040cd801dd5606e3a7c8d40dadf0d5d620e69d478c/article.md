# SCE Marks 140 Years of Innovation

Imagine a world where turning the lights on with a flip of a switch wasn’t an option. Before July 4, 1886, that was the reality.


On that day, electric lights illuminated Visalia’s Independence Day celebration for the first time, kicking off a[140-year legacy of innovation](https://www.edison.com/about-us/our-history) that now powers over 15 million people across Southern California.


SCE continues to build on its 140-year legacy of innovation, safely delivering reliable, clean and affordable electricity to millions.


**The evolution of electricity in Southern California** :


**July 4, 1886:**


Holt and Knupps — later becoming Visalia Electric Light and Gas Company — use a steam engine to power arc lights so town festivities could commence on the evening of July 4, 1886.


**1896-1899:**


Southern California Edison is born through the merging of numerous local power companies, driving innovation with the powering of electric trolley cars, building hydroelectric facilities and adopting three-wire conduit technology to minimize power line clutter.


**1902-1950:**


SCE[brings transmission lines to Los Angeles](https://energyhistory.yale.edu/southern-california-edison-co-transmission-system-map-1947/) and neighboring communities. Steam, electric and hydroelectric facilities help strengthen California's agricultural industry.


**1968-1984:**


Three nuclear units are built at the[San Onofre Nuclear Generating Station](https://www.pbssocal.org/shows/lost-la/san-onofre-the-epic-journey-to-close-a-nuclear-plant) , generating roughly 4,850 megawatts of power.


**1980:**


SCE announces a[commitment to renewable and alternative power](https://www.sce.com/regulatory/regulatory-information/renewable-alternative-power) , using wind, solar, geothermal, fuel cells, hydroelectricity and combined heat and power.


**1989-1996:**


SCEcorp — SCE's parent company — is renamed Edison International and expands globally with the opening of its subsidiary, Edison Mission Energy, in London. Operations later expanded to Australia.


**2000-2016:**


SCE increases rooftop solar generation for residents, invests in and expands wind power generation infrastructure, and upgrades 5 million meters with smart meter technology.


**2017:**


SCE launches "[Energy for What's Ahead](https://www.edison.com/about-us/our-history/our-vision-the-clean-power-and-electrification-pathway) ,” placing[clean power](https://energized.edison.com/stories/dam-upgrades-boost-safety-in-the-sierra-nevada) ,[electrification](https://energized.edison.com/stories/the-big-blue-bus-goes-green) and[wildfire mitigation](https://energized.edison.com/stories/replacing-risky-trees-for-wildfire-safety) as the top priorities.


**2018-2026:**


SCE reimagines the grid to reduce greenhouse gases, leads the electrification of transportation infrastructure, increases reliability and addresses climate risks — all in support of California's goal to reach of net-zero greenhouse gas emissions by 2045 while also meeting rising demand for electricity.
