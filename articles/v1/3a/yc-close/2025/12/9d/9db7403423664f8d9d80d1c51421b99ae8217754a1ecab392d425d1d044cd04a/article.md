## The AI SDR category in June 2026, honestly


"AI SDR" is not a product category. It is a marketing term that got applied to at least four different types of software. Grouping them together is how buyers end up with the wrong tool.


Type one is inbound conversational AI. Piper from Qualified is the mature example. It engages website visitors in real time via chat, voice, and video, then hands qualified leads to a human.


Type two is outbound agent-as-a-service. Ava from Artisan and Alice/Jordan from 11x sit here. You buy an AI seat, feed it an ICP and a domain, and it sends thousands of cold emails and (sometimes) cold calls.


Type three is signal-based prospecting. Factors.ai identifies which accounts are showing buying intent so a human rep can act on that signal. It is not really an SDR at all, but it keeps getting compared to them.


Type four is embedded AI teammate. Chloe from Close fits here, along with the built-in AI layers in HubSpot's Sales Hub and Salesforce Agentforce. These agents live inside your CRM and handle work you were already doing, rather than replacing a human seat.


Each type solves a different problem. Buying the wrong type is expensive. We will walk through all four with real pricing and honest limits.

## Before you pick a tool, run the speed-to-lead math


The single biggest driver of pipeline lift from AI SDR tooling is not "more messages sent." It is faster first response on inbound leads that already came to you. Harvard Business Review has cited this for years. Teams that respond within 5 minutes are 21 times more likely to qualify the lead than teams that wait 30 minutes. In our own Chloe beta with 306 businesses across 818,000-plus calls, the median first-touch time dropped by more than half once outbound and inbound work lived in the same CRM as the AI agent.


If your median inbound first-touch is under 10 minutes and your outbound is already running, an AI SDR is a scale lever. If your median is 30 minutes or more, buying an AI outbound seat is like buying a bigger sink when the drain is clogged. Fix the drain first.

### 2. Qualified (Piper): the inbound heavyweight


Piper is the most mature product in the category, with[~4.9 / 5 across 1,487 G2 reviews](https://www.g2.com/products/qualified/reviews) as of June 2026 (the highest rating in the conversational marketing category). Piper engages website visitors in real time via chat, voice, and video, qualifies them, books meetings on real reps' calendars, and hands warm handoffs to Salesforce. It works. It also costs six figures once you add the required Salesforce stack.


Pricing is quote-only. Based on Vendr negotiation data and multiple public buyer reports, Qualified Premier lists around $68,000 per year for 25 users and negotiates to roughly $40,000–$50,000. Salesforce CRM is required (no exceptions), adding $30,000–$60,000 per year for a 10-seat team, plus intent data and implementation. Enterprise deployments run anywhere from $50,000 to $500,000+ per year depending on tier.


In Q2 2026, Qualified announced PiperX, a multi-modal upgrade with cross-channel engagement and deeper CRM integration. If you are already on Salesforce and your inbound pipeline is the bottleneck, Piper is the gold standard. If you are on HubSpot, Pipedrive, or Close, you cannot use Qualified at all. If you are outbound-heavy, Piper does not cover that motion. For teams weighing the underlying CRM decision itself, our[speed benchmarks against Salesforce, HubSpot, and Pipedrive](http://close.com/blog/close-crm-vs-pipedrive-hubspot-salesforce-speed-test) cover how the platform choice affects rep throughput.


### 3. 11x (Alice and Jordan): read the coverage before you sign


11x sells Alice for outbound (email, LinkedIn) and Julian/Jordan for inbound voice. Pricing is quote-only. Vendr median deal size is $40,125 per year. Public reports put Alice at roughly $50,000 to $60,000 per year, with the Jordan voice add-on in the $50,000-plus range.[G2 sits at 4.5 / 5 across ~10 reviews](https://www.g2.com/products/11x/reviews) , a small sample size worth weighing against the coverage below.


This is the part where we would normally list features. Instead, we are going to link the coverage.


In March 2025,[TechCrunch reported](https://techcrunch.com/2025/03/24/a16z-and-benchmark-backed-11x-has-been-claiming-customers-it-doesnt-have/) that 11x had displayed customer logos, including ZoomInfo and Airtable, for companies that were not customers. ZoomInfo issued a cease-and-desist. The story also cited claims of $10M ARR against actual figures closer to $3M, and reported that the company was seeing high churn within a three-month break clause. In Q2 2026, 11x brought in a new CEO, Prabhav Jain, and has been rebuilding the product and go-to-market story. Multiple buyer forums continue to report churn rates in the 70–75% range within the first three months, though 11x disputes these figures.


We do not have a position on whether 11x is fixed under the new CEO. We do have a position on signing a $50,000 annual contract for outbound email that you can accomplish for a fraction of the cost with a well-configured stack. If you evaluate 11x, ask for named customer references you can call, verify the logos on the website, and read the current coverage before you commit.


### 4. Artisan (Ava): the reputation problem


Ava is Artisan's outbound AI SDR. Pricing runs around $2,000 per month on annual-only contracts. Artisan raised significant funding and put its brand on Bay Area billboards with a provocative "Stop hiring humans" campaign in 2024.


The buyer feedback is where things get uncomfortable. As of mid-2026,[public writeups collecting Artisan reviews](https://aisalesreport.com/posts/why-teams-are-leaving-artisan/) point to zero-star G2 ratings citing brand damage and "extremely bland and obviously AI" copy, Hacker News threads reporting 0% effective response rates and hallucinated fake company news in outbound emails, Reddit posts describing 1,400 emails sent with zero responses, and multiple buyers reporting difficulty canceling contracts. Gizmodo and other outlets have written about Artisan as an example of "software to automate spamming."


We are not saying Artisan cannot work for anyone. We are saying that if you are considering a $24,000-per-year annual contract, you should read the current coverage first and demand named customer references before you sign. The category has better options.


### 5. Regie.ai: enterprise pricing, mid-market pain point


Regie sells an AI SEP (sales engagement platform) at $180 per user per month with a 10-seat minimum ($21,600 per year floor) and a Force Multiplier Rep tier at $499 per user per month with a five-seat minimum ($29,940 per year floor). Add-ons include a Parallel Dialer at $1,800 per user per year and mailbox rotation at $50–$100 per user per month. The product includes a 220M-plus contact database and 339 G2 reviews as of June 2026.


Regie is a reasonable pick if you already have an SEP motion (Outreach, Salesloft) and want to add an AI generation layer on top. It is a bad pick if you are a five-person team looking for a first outbound tool, because the seat minimums put you at $22,000 to $30,000 per year before you know whether the motion works.


### 6. AiSDR: the quarterly-billing option


AiSDR sells two plans plus custom enterprise. Explore is $900 per month billed quarterly ($2,700 upfront, or $8,640 per year on annual). Grow is $2,500 per month quarterly ($7,500 upfront, $24,000 annual). Explore includes 1,200 AI-generated messages and 1,200 lead search credits per month.[G2 sits at ~4.7 / 5 across ~76 reviews](https://www.g2.com/products/aisdr-inc-aisdr/reviews) as of early 2026.


The quarterly billing is the honest differentiator. If you want to test AI outbound for 90 days without committing to a full year, AiSDR is the shortest commitment window in the outbound-agent category. Expected output on Explore, per AiSDR's own projections, is around 3 meetings per month, which comes out to roughly $300 per meeting booked. Whether that math works depends on your deal size and close rate. Below a $10K ACV, it usually does not.


### 7. Factors.ai: intent signals, not an SDR


[Factors.ai](https://www.factors.ai/) identifies which accounts are showing buying intent by combining anonymous website visitor de-anonymization, LinkedIn signals, G2 engagement, and CRM data. It scores accounts so a human rep knows who to call. It does not send messages or place calls.


Pricing (verified June 2026): Free tier covers 200 companies per month. Basic is $399 per month ($4,788 per year) for 3,000 companies. Growth is $999 per month for 8,000 companies. Enterprise is custom, typically $25K–$30K per year. Add-ons are where costs balloon: LinkedIn AdPilot is $1,000 per month, Interest Groups is $750 per month. A Basic plan with both add-ons is $2,149 per month, not $399.[G2 sits at 4.5 / 5 across ~179 reviews](https://www.g2.com/products/factors-ai/reviews) .


Best fit: marketing teams running ABM with a rep team ready to act on the signals. Weak fit: teams expecting Factors to do the outbound work itself. It will not.


### 8. MarketOwl: the LinkedIn-first budget option


MarketOwl runs autonomous LinkedIn plus email outbound campaigns tied to one LinkedIn profile per campaign. Pricing is $500 per month per campaign on monthly, or $300 per month with a 12-month commitment. An "AI SDR + AI SMM" bundle is $329 per month with a one-year commitment. Free-form trial available.


MarketOwl is the cheapest option in this comparison and it is aimed at solo founders and one-person sales teams testing outbound. It is not a serious competitor to Chloe, Piper, or Regie for teams with even three reps, but it does what it says on the label at a price a bootstrapped founder can absorb for one quarter of testing.


## Our honest recommendations by team shape


The right AI SDR depends more on your team shape than on the vendor's feature list. Here is how we actually advise founders when they ask.


**Solo founder or 1–3 person team, mixed inbound and outbound.** Skip the standalone AI SDR bots. Get a calling-first CRM with an embedded AI teammate.[Close with Chloe is the cleanest fit](https://app.close.com/signup/) . If you want a lighter-weight LinkedIn-only motion for one quarter, MarketOwl at $300/month is a reasonable test.


**Founder-led team, 4–10 reps, outbound-heavy.** Same recommendation, with the outbound motion moved into Power Dialer and Chloe handling call summaries and follow-up drafts. This is the profile where Chloe's design center pays off most, because the after-call ritual for 10 reps compounds into hours per day of admin that Chloe removes. If you are ready to compare Close's plan tiers, the[Growth plan](http://close.com/persona-founder-owner) at $99/user/mo is where most teams in this profile land.


**Marketing-led inbound engine already running on Salesforce, 20+ reps.** Qualified with Piper is the right tool. Yes, the total cost of ownership is $70K–$128K per year. If your inbound pipeline is worth several million in ARR, the math works. It also stacks well with signal-based prospecting like Factors.ai. If you are on any CRM other than Salesforce, this recommendation does not apply. See our[Pipedrive comparison](https://close.com/compare/pipedrive-alternative) and other CRM comparisons for the right alternative.


**Mid-market outbound team with a mature SEP motion, 15+ reps.** Regie.ai is the most defensible pick, provided you already have Outreach or Salesloft in place. If you do not, do not buy Regie as your first outbound tool. Buy a CRM with native outbound first, then layer AI on top when you know the motion works.


**Enterprise outbound, budget is not the constraint.** We would still not recommend 11x or Artisan without reading the current coverage and demanding named customer references you can call. If those check out for your specific use case, fine. If they do not, the enterprise outbound category is not actually mature enough yet to justify a $50K-plus annual seat.

The category is moving fast enough that a comparison written today has a short shelf life. Two shifts are worth watching before your next AI SDR evaluation.


The reputational read on 11x is unsettled. TechCrunch's reporting on disputed customer logos and the gap between publicly claimed and actual ARR is the reason 11x sits neutral-to-cautious on our list. New CEO Prabhav Jain took over in Q2 2026, and the product and go-to-market story is being rebuilt. The embedded-agent model is pulling ahead of the standalone-agent model for small teams. Chloe's first six months of production data across 306 businesses and 818,000-plus calls point to consistently better outcomes for teams under 10 reps compared to bolting a separate SDR bot onto a legacy CRM. Bigger teams and inbound-heavy motions are a different story (Qualified still wins that segment), but the small-team default has shifted. Expect HubSpot and Salesforce to lean harder into their own embedded agents through the back half of 2026.

## Where to start tomorrow


Before you evaluate any AI SDR tool, run three quick checks against your own team.


One, measure your current median time to first human touch on inbound leads. Pull last month's form fills and time the gap. If it is over 15 minutes, no AI SDR will fix that.[Speed-to-lead is a CRM problem](https://close.com/blog/b2b-follow-up-speed-test) , not an outbound-agent problem.


Two, count your current sales stack. If you already have a dialer, a sequencer, a notetaker, an[enrichment tool](https://close.com/blog/ai-enrich-crm-tips) , and a CRM, adding an AI SDR seat on top will not help. Consolidation almost always beats addition. A single[CRM plus email plus calling plus SMS](https://close.com/communication) workspace is the cleanest setup for teams under 10 reps.


Three, sit with a rep for one day and time the after-call ritual. If it is over five minutes per call, an embedded AI teammate is the highest-leverage change you can make.[Native calling with AI notes](https://close.com/blog/3-ways-to-call-in-close) collapses that ritual to under 90 seconds in the workflows we have measured.
