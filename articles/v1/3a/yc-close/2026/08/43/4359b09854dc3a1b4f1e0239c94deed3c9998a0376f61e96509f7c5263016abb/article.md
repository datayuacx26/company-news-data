# From Founder's Instinct to Sales Engine: Meghan Watchorn's Playbook to Scaling Sales Teams

At the start of almost every company, founder-led sales isn't a strategy. It's how almost every company gets off the ground. In the early days, the founder embodies the vision, speaks with conviction, and closes every deal that matters.


But what gets a company off the ground rarely gets it to the next stage. As the business grows, the cracks start to show. Customer context lives in the founder's head, inbox, and Slack DMs. Growth becomes fragile, and that's when most founders realize what got them here won't get them there.


That's where Meghan Watchorn, Chief Growth Officer at 6P Marketing, comes in. Throughout her career she's been the person growing companies trust to turn founder-led sales into repeatable, scalable systems. The results back it up: she's scaled a sales team from 3 reps to 22 and driven 4X revenue growth on Close. She's learned that the best sales organizations aren't built around star performers. They're built around clear process, strong coaching, and technology that helps the whole team execute, and she keeps choosing Close to hold it together.


At 6P Marketing, that philosophy shaped everything, from spending her first month listening instead of making changes to leading a thoughtful migration from Pipedrive to Close.


Her story offers a practical playbook for any company ready to move beyond founder-led sales without losing what made it successful in the first place. Check out the full conversation below.


## **Listen First, Systematize Second**


When founders decide it's time to scale, they don't just need more salespeople. They need someone who can take everything in their head and turn it into a system a team can actually run. That's the work Meghan loves most.


Unlike the stereotypical hot shot sales leader, she doesn't walk in and start ripping things out. She starts by listening. Then she maps the customer journey and watches how information moves between people, or, more importantly, where it breaks down. Before recommending a single change, she wants a clear picture of how work actually gets done and where the team lacks visibility.


> "If you do not have your CRM fully integrated with the communication tools you use, then you basically have a very fancy post-it note on a wall, and there's not a lot of substance behind it."


It's a deliberate approach in a business world obsessed with moving faster. Meghan has learned that taking the time to listen, observe, and understand how work actually gets done is often the fastest path to lasting improvement.


## **Scale People, Process, Technology — In That Order**


Every sales leader has a philosophy for scaling a team. Meghan believes in the time tested framework of people, process, and technology. Getting them in the right order makes all the difference.


### People First


For Meghan, people come first, and everything else exists to serve them. She spent her first decade in sales chasing the top of the leaderboard, but somewhere along the way the thing that lit her up changed. "Coaching others to be their best self and to see them grow sparked way more joy in me than my own success," she says.


That shift changed how she leads. She hires for character over credentials because skills can be taught, and she leads by being open about her own failures. A team that sees its leader as human is more willing to learn, collaborate, and grow together.


### Then Process


When it comes to process, Meghan doesn’t chase a perfect playbook. She focuses on building a shared one, shaped by continuous input from the people using it every day.


When everyone follows the same process, the team can see what’s happening, step in when someone is unavailable, and keep opportunities from slipping through the cracks. Without that consistency, success depends on individual memory instead of a system.


> “Everything relies on that one human being being present and knowing all the things they need to do. We all know life doesn't work like that. People get sick, people get hurt… they quit and walk out the door, and you're starting from square one.”


The benefits extend well beyond the sales team. Shared processes create smoother handoffs between departments, preserve customer context, and give leaders visibility into the activities , not just the revenue, that drive growth. That's how teams identify problems and improve the process before those problems show up in the numbers.


### Technology to bring it home


Only after people and process are in place does Meghan think about technology. After two decades inside CRMs, she's clear about what it can and can't do. And with the emergence of AI, finding the right technology for your business is evolving daily.


> "Technology is a force multiplier, not a savior. The people are the drivers — but the right system enables the team, gives us visibility into the pipeline, and lets people take that vacation because we can own it as a team and help each other out."


Good technology creates visibility, automates busywork, keeps customer history so no one starts from zero, and gives a team a shared place to[coach each other and share what's working](https://close.com/coaching) . What it can't do is rescue an unclear strategy. "Technology can't fix a broken strategy or process." That's why she sorts out the fundamentals first, because get them wrong and software just helps a team fail faster.


> "We can't expect people to make the CRM another administrative task. I want people prospecting and talking to people, not copying and pasting emails from one system to another."


## **Replace Friction, Not Just Software**


One of Meghan's first priorities at 6P Marketing was understanding the systems the team already relied on. The team was using a lower tier of Pipedrive, and one issue stood out almost immediately: customer communication lived outside the CRM.


> "One of the things I noticed even in the interview process was that emails weren't integrated into Pipedrive at all — other than a copy-and-paste into a note from time to time. So immediately I was like, well, that's got to change."


Every copied email was another manual task pulling reps away from customers, while valuable context remained scattered across inboxes instead of becoming part of a shared system the whole team could rely on.


> "I wanted a system that would help our team not just record where we are, but forecast revenue and pass leads to someone else — and that I could do myself, rather than having to hire a Salesforce engineer to do it."


Despite spotting an obvious problem right away, Meghan didn't rush to replace the CRM. She spent her first month learning how the team actually worked before evaluating Close alongside Pipedrive and several other options. She wasn’t looking for the platform with the most features, but rather one that could support a more collaborative sales process, give the team better visibility into the pipeline, and scale alongside the business.


Not everyone embraced the change immediately. Moving from siloed work to a shared system creates more visibility, and with it, more accountability. But for Meghan, that was the point.


## **Pick Tools That Help Teams Sell**


Over the years, she kept encountering it in more sophisticated sales organizations, watching how features like Smart Views, workflows, email templates, and built-in communication helped teams stay aligned without adding complexity.


By the time she joined 6P Marketing, she'd implemented Close more than once. But rather than defaulting to what she already knew, she started the evaluation from scratch. Armed with a clear list of requirements, and another teammate to keep her honest, she compared Close against five other CRMs.


> "I went to market armed with my key functional requirements, and brought someone else along for the ride so I wasn't too biased. We looked at five CRMs, and Close won by a landslide."


The aim was to find a solution that removed friction, putting calls, emails, texts, and notes, automation, and reporting in one place instead of a stack of disconnected tools, so her team could spend less time managing software and more time selling.


> "Having phone, text, and email all within one tool, plus the ability to record notes and the productivity hacks — I've seen people light up because they don't have to do three extra steps. Everything is just there."


That range is what keeps her loyal. "Some of the other systems out there are really good at one thing. Close is really great at many. I really do feel like Close would be part of that dream stack." That confidence has only grown as the product has evolved.


> "Close is constantly innovating — there are constantly new things, I can't even keep up sometimes. It's a very product-led organization building for sales teams, and you feel that."


## **Build for the Next Stage**


A year from now, Meghan wants a team that operates with consistency. Everyone follows the same process. Customer context is easy to find. Handoffs happen without dropping the ball. And instead of reacting to the next opportunity, the team has the time and visibility to proactively build relationships across every channel… including the phone.


That last part is personal for her. Too many salespeople, she says, have gone quiet behind a keyboard.


> "I heard somewhere that 73% of people in sales don't pick up the phone anymore. They're hiding behind a keyboard. But where's the human connection in that? Email is great, I love it, but it's not the only thing that's going to get someone's attention."


Her goal for the team is to build a process that meets buyers where they actually are, whether that's the phone, in person, or a Slack community, with the technology tying it all together.
