# 6 No-Brainer Workflows Every Sales Team Needs to Save Time and Sell More

Would you describe your sales process as organized and efficient? If not, you're not alone.


For many small businesses, sales reps wear a lot of hats. Between logging activities, updating records, sending follow-ups, scheduling meetings, and remembering the next step for every deal, admin work quickly eats into selling time.


The good news? You don't need a RevOps team or technical expertise to build a repeatable, automated sales process anymore.


With[Close Workflows](https://close.com/blog/how-to-use-close-forms-with-workflows) , you can automate the repetitive tasks that slow your team down. Instead of relying on sticky notes, spreadsheets, or memory, use automations so every lead gets a consistent experience, nothing falls through the cracks, and your team spends more time talking to customers.


Here are six practical workflows, inspired by real businesses, to automate every stage of your sales process.


## 1. Inbound Lead Qualification


Every new lead feels exciting, but not every lead needs your attention today.


Whether inquiries come from your website, referrals, paid ads, or social media, some people are ready to buy and others are still exploring. The challenge isn't generating leads, it's knowing who deserves a phone call now and who just needs more time.


Take a growing marketing agency. New inquiries arrive daily from multiple channels. Without a repeatable process, account executives spend their mornings sorting submissions, deciding who to contact first, and trying to remember who still needs a follow-up—time that could be spent talking to prospects.


An inbound qualification workflow takes those decisions off your team's plate. New inquiries get an immediate welcome message, and engaged prospects can book a discovery call right away. For the first call, Close can even[hand off to Chloe](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OVSdXTKHjUo) , our AI voice agent, who reaches out the moment a lead comes in, qualifies them, and books the meeting.


The result? Faster response times, fewer missed opportunities, and more time selling.


**A common workflow might look like this:**


- **Day 1:** Send a welcome email and/or SMS with a link to schedule a discovery call.
- **Day 1:** Create a task to connect on LinkedIn and send a case study or testimonial.
- **Day 7:** Send an email addressing common objections.
- **Day 9:** Create a task for a follow-up call.
- **Day 14:** Send a friendly breakup email.
- **Day 15:** Change the lead status from **Interested** to **Cold** and optionally enroll them in a long-term nurture sequence.

## 2. Automated Outbound Prospecting


Consistency wins in outbound sales. The problem is, consistency is hard when every day starts with "Who should I call first?"


Imagine a B2B SaaS company prepping for a major industry conference. The team has a list of 2,000 attending companies, but only three reps to reach them before the event.


Without a system, every morning starts the same way: filtering spreadsheets, checking who was contacted last, deciding who deserves another follow-up, and trying not to email the same prospect twice. That's time that could be spent having conversations.


An outbound prospecting workflow keeps outreach moving automatically. Leads who haven't been contacted recently rise to the top of the call list, cold emails continue in the background, and prospects stop receiving outreach once they reply or book a meeting. No morning triage required.


**A common setup might look like this:**


- **Daily:** Surface a[Smart View](https://close.com/blog/crm-automation-prevent-lost-leads) sorted by least-called and least-contacted leads.
- **Daily:** Work that list in the[Power Dialer](https://help.close.com/docs/power-predictive-dialing) .
- **Ongoing outreach:** Run automated cold email sequences alongside calls.
- **After a reply:** Automatically remove the lead from the sequence.
- **After maximum outreach attempts:** Change the lead status to **Never Reached** and remove them from the active calling queue.

## 3. Call No-Show Recovery


A missed meeting doesn't always mean a missed opportunity.


People get pulled into another meeting. A customer gets sick. An emergency pops up. Most no-shows aren't a "no". They're just life getting in the way.


Take a coaching business that books consultation calls after every webinar. Every conversation is a chance to earn a new client. But when someone doesn't show, the coach has to remember to send a follow-up email, text a reschedule link, and reach out personally—all while prepping for the next session and serving existing clients. If that follow-up doesn't happen fast, the opportunity often disappears.


When a meeting is marked as missed, Close can kick off emails, texts, and follow-up tasks without anyone lifting a finger. Prospects receive a friendly email and text with a link to reschedule, while reminders prompt the coach to personally reach out at the right time. If they book another meeting, they're automatically removed from the workflow. If not, they can continue receiving occasional check-ins until the timing is better.


**A common workflow might look like this:**


- **Day 1:** Send a "No worries—let's find another time" email with a reschedule link.
- **Day 1:** Send an SMS encouraging them to rebook.
- **Day 2:** Create a follow-up call task.
- **Day 2:** Send a reminder email and SMS.
- **Day 7:** Create another call task and send a "Sorry I missed you" email.
- **Day 14:** Move the lead into a long-term nurture campaign if they haven't responded.

## 4. Proposal & Contract Follow-Up


Sending a proposal isn't the finish line. It's often the moment your prospect starts making a decision.


Maybe they're comparing quotes. Maybe they're waiting on a spouse, a business partner, or an insurance claim. Silence doesn't always mean they're not interested.


Take a roofing company that sends estimates after every inspection. If you've ever had work done on your house, then you know decision are rarely made on the spot. Customers are comparing contractors, waiting on insurance, or discussing it with family. Meanwhile the sales team is juggling dozens of open estimates, trying to remember who received what and when to follow up. The companies that follow up consistently are usually the ones that win the work.


Rather than leaving follow-up to chance, Close can automatically send reminders, create call tasks, and keep every proposal moving until the prospect decides. Reminder emails, texts, and scheduled call tasks help prospects take the next step while giving them plenty of chances to ask questions.


**A common workflow might look like this:**


- **Day 7:** Send a friendly "Just checking in" email.
- **Day 8:** Send a quick SMS reminder.
- **Day 9:** Create a follow-up call task.
- **Day 10:** Create a task to review communication history and resend the proposal if needed.
- **Day 11:** Send an email offering to answer questions or schedule a walkthrough.
- **Day 14:** Create a final follow-up call task before pausing outreach.

## **5. New Customer Onboarding**


Winning a customer feels great. Keeping them excited after they buy is where long-term relationships begin.


The first few days after a sale are when customers ask themselves: *Did I make the right choice? What happens next? Who do I contact if I need help?* A smooth onboarding answers those questions before they're even asked.


Take an insurance agency signing dozens of new policyholders a month. Every customer expects clear next steps, but agents are also quoting new business and helping existing clients. Without automation, it's easy to forget a welcome email, delay an introduction, or miss a follow-up. Those little moments shape first impressions, and first impressions shape relationships.


The moment an opportunity is marked Closed Won, Close can automatically kick off onboarding—welcome emails go out, internal tasks are assigned to the right people, and customers get the information they need without waiting for someone to remember.


**A common workflow might look like this:**


- **Trigger:** Opportunity is marked **Closed Won.**
- **Day 1:** Update the lead status to **Customer.**
- **Day 1:** Send a welcome email with next steps.
- **Day 1:** Create onboarding tasks for the account owner.
- **Day 3:** Send helpful resources and FAQs.
- **Day 7:** Create a customer check-in task.
- **Day 14:** Send educational resources or schedule an onboarding review.

## **6. Lost Deal Nurturing**


Not every deal is supposed to close today.


Sometimes the budget disappears, another project takes priority, or a prospect just decides "not yet." That's very different from "never."


Take a manufacturer selling custom components to other businesses. A prospect loves the proposal, but their production schedule shifts and the project slips to next quarter. Another sticks with their current supplier because switching feels like too much work right now. Neither is a lost cause. It’s just not the right time. Mark them **Closed Lost** and forget them, you'll be starting from scratch when they circle back.


Close can automatically capture why an opportunity was lost, enroll the contact in a nurture sequence, and remind your team when it's time to reconnect. When the prospect is finally ready, they remember you—not because you sent the most emails, but because you consistently showed up with something useful.


**A common workflow might look like this:**


- **Day 1:** Record the reason the opportunity was lost.
- **Day 1:** Send a courteous follow-up thanking them for their time.
- **Day 30:** Share an educational resource or customer success story.
- **Day 60:** Send a product update or industry insight.
- **Day 90:** Check in to see if priorities have changed.
- **Ongoing:** Continue quarterly nurture until the prospect re-engages.

## Conclusion: Start Small and Build From There


The best sales automations aren't the flashiest. They're the ones that quietly eliminate the work your team does over and over.


Start by looking for the moments where people have to stop and think:


- Who should I follow up with today?
- Did we send that welcome email?
- Did anyone check back on that proposal?
- What happens when someone misses a meeting?


If the answer depends on someone's mental to do list, it's probably a great candidate for automation.


You don't need to automate everything at once. Pick one repetitive process, build a simple workflow, and let it run. Once your team sees the time it saves, and how much more consistent the customer experience becomes, you'll naturally find the next thing to automate.


Because the goal isn't to replace the human side of selling. It's to remove the busywork so your team has more time for the conversations and relationships that actually grow your business.
