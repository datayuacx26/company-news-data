## Why Free Credit Numbers Lie to You


Free credits sound generous until they vanish mid-build. That’s not a bug. It’s how every AI app builder’s business model works.


Here’s the problem nobody warns you about: a “credit” on one platform is not the same as a “credit” on another. Bolt.new measures in tokens (think characters of code processed). Lovable uses build credits (roughly one generation per credit). Replit charges based on effort (the complexity of what you ask for). Taskade counts AI operations. Comparing 1,000,000 Bolt tokens to 30 Lovable credits is like comparing kilometers to pounds.


As one r/SideProject thread put it bluntly: “Every AI app builder free tier is designed to show you what the tool can do, not to let you build anything real for free.” That framing is honest and worth keeping in mind as you read through every platform below.


The real question isn’t “who gives the most free credits?” It’s “what can I actually ship for $0?” This guide answers that question for 13 platforms, organized by what they produce: native iOS apps, web apps, business tools, and design prototypes. ## What “Free Credits” Actually Mean


Before evaluating any platform, you need to understand the four credit models in use today:


**Build credits** (used by Lovable, Blink, x1, RationalGo): One credit typically equals one generation or one prompt-to-output cycle. You write a prompt, the system builds or modifies your app, and one credit is consumed. These are the most predictable, but the scope of what counts as “one build” varies.


**Tokens** (used by Bolt.new): Tokens measure the volume of text processed, both your prompt and the AI’s response plus all the project context the system reads. A typical vibe coding session burns 10,000 to 50,000 tokens per prompt, and complex multi-file edits can exceed 100,000 tokens. Most token consumption comes from the builder reading and syncing project files, so larger projects eat tokens faster per message.


**AI credits** (used by Taskade, Zite, Figma Make, Softr): A general-purpose counter for AI operations. What counts as “one AI credit” is platform-specific and often opaque.


**Effort-based pricing** (used by Replit): The cost scales with the complexity of your request. A simple file edit costs less than a multi-step architectural change. This is the hardest model to predict.


The consistent pattern across all of them: almost none of the platforms tell you what a credit actually produces before you sign up. The vague language is intentional.

## How to Make Free Credits Count: 5 Practical Tips


Most people burn through free AI app builder credits in a single afternoon. Here’s how to stretch them.


**1. Write specific prompts.** Vague instructions like “make it look better” force the AI into guessing, which means more revision cycles and more credits burned. Specify exactly what you want: “Change the header background to #1a1a2e and increase the font size of the CTA button to 18px.” Practitioners report that 63% of developers spend more time debugging AI-generated code than writing it themselves. Precise prompts reduce that overhead.


**2. Plan before you build.** Decide on your screen count, navigation flow, data model, and authentication approach before spending a single credit. Platforms like x1 build this planning phase directly into their workflow, which prevents expensive mid-build pivots.


**3. Use visual editing where available.** Softr’s visual editor is unlimited and free. Figma Make gives 500 monthly AI credits but unlimited manual design. Several platforms separate AI-assisted generation from manual editing. Do everything you can manually first.


**4. Don’t burn credits on debugging.** If the AI generates broken code, export the project and fix issues locally instead of spending credits on iterative “fix this error” prompts. This is especially true on token-based platforms like Bolt, where each debugging cycle consumes context tokens.


**5. Test two or three platforms, then commit to one.** Use free credits across multiple tools to evaluate workflow fit, output quality, and credit burn rate. Then invest your paid budget in the winner. Spreading paid subscriptions across platforms is how[indie hackers end up spending $70/month](https://x1.new/post/ai-app-studio-for-indie-hackers-glossary) before writing any business logic.

## FAQ


### How many free credits do AI app builders typically give?


It varies wildly by platform and credit type. Build-credit platforms range from 5 per month (Softr) to 100 at signup (x1). Token-based platforms like Bolt.new offer up to 1 million tokens per month. Taskade gives 3,000 AI credits. The numbers aren’t directly comparable because each platform defines “credit” differently. Focus on what a credit produces (screens, features, revisions) rather than the raw number.


### Can I build a complete app with free AI app builder credits?


For most platforms, no. Free tiers are designed for evaluation, not production. As one Reddit community member noted, free tiers show you what the tool can do, not let you build anything real for free. You might complete a very simple landing page or single-feature prototype, but anything with authentication, a database, or multiple screens will typically exceed free allowances.


### Which AI app builder has the most generous free tier?


By raw numbers, Bolt.new’s 1 million free tokens per month is the largest allowance. By credit count, Taskade’s 3,000 AI credits leads. By practical output for business apps, Zite’s 50 monthly credits with unlimited users offers the best ratio. For native iOS apps, x1’s roughly 100 credits is the only option in the category. “Most generous” depends entirely on what you’re building.


### Do I need a credit card to sign up for free AI app builder credits?

### What’s the difference between tokens, build credits, and AI credits?


Tokens measure text volume processed (your prompts plus AI responses plus codebase context). Build credits count discrete generation events (one prompt, one output). AI credits are a general-purpose counter for various AI operations. Effort-based pricing (used by Replit) scales cost with request complexity. Token-based systems are hardest to predict because larger projects consume more tokens per interaction.


### Can I build an iOS app with free credits from web-based AI app builders?


No. Platforms like Bolt, Lovable, and Replit produce web applications using React, Next.js, or similar frameworks. They do not generate native Swift code or Xcode projects. If you want a native iPhone app in the App Store, x1 is currently the only AI builder offering free credits toward that output. Using web builder credits for an iOS goal wastes those credits entirely.


### What hidden costs should I expect beyond free credits?


Beyond the platform subscription, common costs include: hosting ($10 to $20/month on Vercel or similar), database services ($10+/month), API costs for third-party integrations, domain registration ($10 to $15/year), and for iOS apps, Apple’s $99/year Developer Program fee. One community calculation put the total stack cost at $70/month before any business logic was written.


### Should I use multiple AI app builders or pick one?


Use free credits on two or three platforms to test workflow fit, output quality, and credit efficiency. Then commit your paid budget to one. Spreading paid subscriptions across multiple tools is the fastest way to overspend without shipping. The 2026 community consensus is clear: pick the right builder for your project type rather than trying to make one tool do everything.
