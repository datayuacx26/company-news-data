## What Are App Store Screenshots?


App Store screenshots are promotional images displayed on an app's App Store product page. They showcase the app's interface, highlight important features, and communicate the primary value users receive after installing the app. Unlike screenshots taken during development, App Store screenshots are marketing assets designed to increase conversion rates and encourage downloads.


## What to Look for in an App Store Screenshot Tool


Before picking a tool, know what actually matters.


**Correct sizes and auto-export.** Apple’s requirements are pixel-exact. For 2026, the primary size is 1320 x 2868 pixels for the iPhone 17 Pro Max (6.9"). Even small discrepancies, like exporting at 1284 x 2778 instead of 1290 x 2796, trigger upload errors. Your tool needs to handle this automatically.


**Localization support.** This is the highest-ROI upgrade most indie developers skip. Case studies show 33% to 36% conversion gains from screenshot localization alone, with broader language coverage driving 101% to 128% more downloads. An app supporting just five languages needs 50 unique screenshot assets for a single release.


**Pricing model.** Subscription, one-time, or credits? One practitioner flagged a common trap: “Canceling subscription is not an option, since you need it to access your project and you’ll likely need to access it again from time to time in order to update your screenshots.”


**Design quality without design skills.** Templates, AI composition, and guided editors all solve this differently.


**Direct App Store upload.** Eliminates the most tedious manual step. Without it, you’re downloading files and re-uploading them through App Store Connect yourself.

## Screenshot Best Practices That Actually Move Downloads


Tools matter, but what you put in the screenshots matters more. Users decide whether to install in roughly three seconds, and around 90% never scroll past the third screenshot.


**Lead with emotion, not features.** One indie developer guide from 2026 nails this: “The first screenshot must do the heavy lifting. Strip it down to one strong headline and one hero visual. Lead with the emotional benefit rather than a list of features.” Show what the user *gets* , not what the app *does* .


**Show the app at its best state.** Never use empty states, loading screens, or settings menus. The screenshots should show the app populated with realistic, attractive data.


**Portrait for most apps, landscape only for games.** Unless your app is a game or video player, portrait orientation is standard and expected.


**Localize your top 3 markets first.** You don’t need all 50 App Store languages on day one. Start with the markets where your analytics show the most interest, then expand. The 33-36% conversion lift from localization compounds across each additional language. This kind of preparation connects to broader[App Store readiness](https://x1.new/post/x1-ai-app-studio-idea-to-app-store-readiness) , which goes well beyond screenshots.


**Don’t forget 50% of installs come from list-view snapshots.** That means the first screenshot and your app icon together are doing most of the work. Treat them as a pair.

## Frequently Asked Questions


### What size should app store screenshots be in 2026?


Start with 1320 x 2868 pixels for the iPhone 17 Pro Max (6.9"), which is now the primary flagship size. Apple requires PNG or JPEG format, RGB color space, no alpha channel, and exact pixel dimensions. You can upload between 1 and 10 screenshots per supported device class. Even small pixel discrepancies will trigger upload errors in App Store Connect.


### How many app store screenshots should I create?


Apple allows up to 10, but the data suggests front-loading your best work. Around 90% of users don’t scroll past the third screenshot, and 50% of installs come from the list-view snapshot (your first image). Create at least 5, but pour your effort into making the first 3 exceptional.


### Do app store screenshots actually affect downloads?


Yes, significantly. Roughly 60% of users make install decisions based on the app name, rating, and screenshots. Screenshot localization alone drives 33-36% conversion gains in case studies, and broader localization has produced 101-128% download increases. Screenshots are the highest-impact visual asset on your listing.


### Are free app store screenshot tools good enough?


It depends on the tool. AppMockUp Studio is genuinely free with no watermarks and produces clean results. Launch Shots also offers a usable free tier with direct upload. But many tools (like AppLaunchpad) add watermarks to free exports, making them useless for production. Always check the watermark policy before investing time in a free tool.


### Should I use Fastlane for app store screenshots?


Only if you’re a technical developer comfortable with Ruby, Xcode, and CI/CD pipelines. Fastlane automates screen capture and upload but produces raw screens, not designed marketing assets. Practitioners consistently report that setup takes longer than expected and maintenance is ongoing. Most teams pair Fastlane capture with a separate design tool.


### What’s the difference between template-based and AI-generated screenshots?


Template tools (AppLaunchpad, Screenshots Pro) give you pre-designed layouts to customize. AI-generated tools (Nuvex) compose the entire design from scratch based on your inputs. Templates offer more control and predictability. AI tools are faster but less precise. A third category, template-plus-AI tools (AppScreens, Launch Shots), sits in between.


### How long does it take to create app store screenshots?


With a dedicated screenshot tool, most developers finish a set in 1-2 hours. The DIY Figma approach takes 3-4 hours per set. Using an integrated studio like x1, screenshots are created as part of the launch workflow, so there’s no separate time block. Without any tool, practitioners report spending 1-3 full days per release, especially when factoring in localization and multiple device sizes.


### Do I need screenshots for both iPhone and iPad?


If your app supports iPad, Apple requires iPad-specific screenshots at iPad dimensions. Most screenshot tools handle both, but it doubles your asset count. If you’re launching an iPhone-only app, you only need iPhone screenshots. Every tool in this comparison covers iPhone dimensions; iPad support varies by tool.
