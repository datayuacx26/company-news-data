## 2.[Rork](https://rork.com/)


**Best for:** Cross-platform MVP validation and native iOS apps through a no-code chat interface.


### What Rork Is


Rork AI is a no-code platform where you describe an app idea in plain English and get a functional mobile app in return. Founded by Levan Kvirkvelia and Daniel Dhawan, the company has raised roughly $15 million in funding and reportedly hit $1.5 million in annual recurring revenue within three days of launching its Max tier in February 2026.


The platform runs on two distinct engines. Rork Pro generates cross-platform apps using React Native and Expo, covering iOS, Android, and web from a single codebase. Rork Max generates native SwiftUI apps targeting the Apple ecosystem, including iPhone, iPad, Apple Watch, and Vision Pro.


Everything compiles on Rork’s cloud Mac fleet, so you never need to install Xcode or own a MacBook to ship an app.

### Weaknesses and Real User Feedback


Credit consumption is the most common complaint. Practitioners on Reddit and review forums report that iterative builds burn through credits fast because every refinement, bug fix, and follow-up prompt costs a message. A real app needs dozens or hundreds of refinements, not just one prompt.


One practitioner who tested multiple vibe coding tools summed it up: “The logic layer is where it struggles. Streak calculations, state management, anything beyond surface-level CRUD needs significant manual rework before it’s production-ready.” They added that credit-based pricing “gets expensive fast on iterative builds.”


A solo developer’s three-month review highlighted another risk: “Once features accumulate, you cannot move quickly through fixes in code whose structure you never internalized.” This is the hidden cost of no-code generation. You get speed upfront but lose control as complexity grows. Understanding this tradeoff is important when[comparing AI app builder types](https://x1.new/post/ai-app-builder-types-how-they-work) .


ICON POLLS rates Rork Max 3.8 out of 5, reflecting a tool that’s impressive but not yet polished.


### Rork Verdict


Rork is the strongest option if you need cross-platform coverage (iOS and Android from one codebase) or want deep Apple hardware integration through Rork Max. The credit system makes it expensive for iterative development, and the chat-only interface provides limited design control.

### Critical Issue: Apple App Store Removal


In March 2026, Apple removed apps built with Vibecodeapp (and Replit) from the iOS App Store under Guideline 2.5.2, which prohibits apps that execute arbitrary downloaded code. Apple also blocked Vibecodeapp from distributing its app-building tools on iOS under the same guideline.


This is the single most important piece of context in any Rork vs Vibecodeapp comparison. If you’re building an iOS app, the platform you use to build it has been flagged by Apple’s review team. That doesn’t necessarily mean every app created with Vibecodeapp will be rejected, but it introduces a layer of risk that didn’t exist before March 2026.

## The Problem Both Rork and Vibecodeapp Share


Winning the Rork vs Vibecodeapp comparison doesn’t mean Rork is without serious limitations. Both tools share structural problems that become obvious once you try to ship a real, polished app.


### Chat-Only Interfaces Hit a Wall


Both Rork and Vibecodeapp are chat-first tools. You type what you want, the AI generates code, and you iterate through more prompts. This works well for the first 80% of an app. It breaks down badly for the last 20%.


AI models have a limit on how much context they can hold at once. Exceed that limit and the model starts “forgetting” earlier parts of the conversation. It drops requirements you mentioned ten prompts ago. It contradicts decisions it made twenty prompts ago. Features that were working start breaking because the model no longer remembers they exist. If you want to understand how different[AI app builder approaches](https://x1.new/post/ai-app-builder-types-how-they-work) handle this, the distinction between chat-only and modular workflows is fundamental.


### The “Last Mile” Is Where Apps Die


The vibe coding community talks about the “1% finish” problem. Getting from 0% to 90% of an app is easy with AI tools. Getting from 90% to 100%, handling edge cases, authentication flows, payment processing, App Store metadata, compliance, and deployment, is where most projects stall or die.


Apple’s App Review team rejected approximately 1.93 million submissions out of 7.77 million in 2024. That’s nearly 1 in 4 apps. Many of those rejections involve exactly the kind of details that chat-based AI builders don’t address: metadata formatting, screenshot requirements, permission justifications, and paywall compliance.


### No Launch Assets


Neither Rork nor Vibecodeapp generates App Store screenshots, writes listing copy, or handles ASO metadata. After spending hours or days building your app, you still need to figure out screenshots (with proper device frames), write a compelling description, choose keywords, and navigate Apple’s submission requirements manually. This[App Store launch checklist](https://x1.new/post/app-store-launch-checklist) covers what’s actually required.


### Security Gaps in AI-Generated Code


A fractional CTO who specializes in rescuing vibe-coded apps noted a disturbing pattern: “The average vibe-coded app has zero error handling for network failures, zero error boundaries for rendering failures, and zero user-facing error messages.” This isn’t unique to Rork or Vibecodeapp. It’s a systemic issue with chat-generated code that no one reviews line by line.

## FAQ


### Is Vibecodeapp still available on the iOS App Store?


As of March 2026, Apple removed Vibecodeapp (along with Replit) from the iOS App Store under Guideline 2.5.2, which prohibits apps that execute arbitrary downloaded code. Vibecodeapp’s web version at vibecodeapp.com still works, but the mobile app that made it unique is no longer distributed through Apple’s platform.


### Is Rork Max worth $200/month compared to Rork Pro at $25/month?


It depends on your platform needs. Rork Max generates native SwiftUI code with access to deep Apple APIs like HealthKit and ARKit, while Rork Pro generates cross-platform React Native code. If you’re building exclusively for Apple devices and need hardware-level features, Max offers genuine native capability. But $200/month adds up fast when combined with credit consumption for iterative development.


### Can I export source code from Rork or Vibecodeapp?


Rork provides access to your codebase. Vibecodeapp requires the Pro plan ($50/month) to download source code, which drew complaints from users who felt basic ownership was paywalled.


### What does “Guideline 2.5.2” mean for apps I’ve already built with Vibecodeapp?


Guideline 2.5.2 prohibits apps from downloading and executing code not included in the original App Store submission. Apps already live may face scrutiny during updates or re-review. The enforcement signals that Apple is drawing a firmer line around what qualifies as a native app, which has implications for any tool generating code that runs through intermediary frameworks.


### How does x1’s pricing compare to Rork and Vibecodeapp?


x1 starts at $99/month ($66/month if billed yearly) for its Builder plan, which includes the full five-stage workflow and native Swift/Xcode output. That’s more than Vibecodeapp’s $20/month Plus plan or Rork’s $25/month Junior plan, but x1 includes launch assets (App Store screenshots, listing copy) that neither competitor offers. For native iOS output comparable to what Rork Max provides, x1’s Builder plan is less than half the cost of Rork Max’s $200/month.


### Do any of these tools handle App Store screenshots and metadata?


Only x1 includes built-in App Store screenshot creation and listing copy generation as part of its workflow. Both Rork and Vibecodeapp stop at code generation, leaving you to handle all launch assets separately using other tools or manual effort.


### Which tool is best for someone with zero coding experience?


All three tools are designed for non-technical users. Vibecodeapp is the easiest to start with (free, from your phone), but its limitations surface quickly. Rork is more capable but relies on a chat interface that can be disorienting as projects grow. x1’s staged studio approach provides the most structure, walking you through each decision in sequence rather than expecting you to know what to prompt for next.


### Can I build an Android app with any of these tools?


Rork Pro generates cross-platform apps that work on iOS, Android, and web. Vibecodeapp also generates React Native code for both platforms, though the Apple removal complicates iOS distribution. x1 is iOS-only, generating native Swift/Xcode projects exclusively for iPhone. If Android support is a requirement, Rork Pro is the clear choice among these three options.
