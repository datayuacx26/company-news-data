# Goodwood, Innit?


**LiveWire Takes on the Greatest Motorsport Garden Party on Earth**


Some events are races. Some are trade shows. Some are festivals. Goodwood Festival of Speed is all three, crammed into the rolling hills of West Sussex and turned up to eleven.


For four days, the grounds of the Duke of Richmond's estate become a temporary capital of speed, where Formula 1 cars, prototype race machines, vintage motorcycles, million-dollar supercars, EVs, customs, choppers, and everything in between converge on a single hillclimb. It is somehow polished and completely bananas at the same time.


## A Little Bit of Everything


One of the things that makes Goodwood special is its refusal to pick a team. Combustion and electric. Vintage and futuristic. Factory race teams and backyard builders. Hypercars and chopped motos. Walking through the event feels like scrolling through the world's most unhinged motorsports mood board. It's all here.


For anyone who loves machines, Goodwood isn't just a show. It's sensory overload.


As Max of Reload Land put it:


“It’s the absolute overflow of dreams for any enthusiast. Everything you've imagined is there and you can basically touch it.”


## Up the Hill


Of course, it wouldn't be Goodwood without the hillclimb.


The 1.16-mile ribbon of pavement that cuts through the Goodwood estate isn't just the centerpiece of the Festival of Speed. It's sacred ground. Every manufacturer, race team, and driver arrives wanting their moment on that stretch of tarmac.


The start line crackles with anticipation. Mechanics make last-minute adjustments. Supercars bark through rev limiters. Historic race bikes cough to life. Crowds pack every available viewing spot. Then, flags go down and one by one, machines launch toward the trees.


In the middle of all that noise and spectacle, LiveWire's electric motorcycles delivered their own kind of drama. Aaron Guardado piloted the S2 Alpinista Corsa up the famed hill, demonstrating the kind of instant acceleration that can make even seasoned motorsport fans stop and take notice. While V12s and race engines announced themselves with thunderous noise, the LiveWire machines attacked the course with something different: an almost calm sense of urgency. No waiting. No hesitation. Just pure torque.


As Aaron put it:


“Fast AF, as per usual.”


For spectators seeing the bikes in action before visiting the display, the hillclimb served as proof that these weren't simply motorcycles to admire behind a barrier. They were machines built to perform. The result was a steady stream of visitors arriving at the booth with curiosity.


## Charley Being Charley


One of the biggest crowd-pleasers of the weekend came courtesy of an old friend of the brand: Charley Boorman.


Long before he was charging up the Goodwood hill aboard a LiveWire, Charley helped introduce electric motorcycles to millions of viewers around the world in Long Way Up, the acclaimed adventure series in which he and longtime riding partner Ewan McGregor rode from Ushuaia, Argentina to Los Angeles aboard prototype LiveWire motorcycles.


At the time, the idea of crossing continents on electric motorcycles felt impossible to many riders. Charley and Ewan proved otherwise, showcasing not only the capability of electric motorcycles, but the spirit of adventure behind them.


That connection makes Charley's appearances with LiveWire feel more like family reunions than celebrity guest appearances.


And true to form, he didn't disappoint.


The veteran adventurer spent the weekend wandering the paddock, chatting with marshals, riders, and friends as if he'd known everyone there for decades. Which, frankly, he probably has.


Then came the hill.


Then came the summit.


Then came the burnout.


While everyone else was busy navigating procedures and waiting for instructions, Charley casually rolled into position and let the rear tire do some talking. And if there is a more fitting way to celebrate the partnership between one of motorcycling's greatest storytellers and one of motorcycling's most disruptive brands, we haven't found it yet.


## Enter the Honcho


While the S2 lineup won people over with performance and design, the S4 Honcho won hearts.


Kids climbed aboard and instantly started imagining adventures. Adults pictured rides around campgrounds, properties, neighborhoods, and weekend escapes. The Honcho Van became a magnet for curious attendees all weekend long.


**The Stuff You Can't Put on a Spec Sheet**


The funny thing about Goodwood is that the cars and motorcycles are only part of the story. It's the random conversations. The police officer who sat on the Alpinista and lit up like a kid. The children imagining electric motorcycles under the Christmas tree. The autograph seekers trying to determine whether members of the LiveWire team were actually famous. The England football match watched in England. The ice cream consumed while waiting at the start line. The mosquito attack that forced creative engineering solutions involving napkins, jacket sleeves, and eventually an electric bug zapper that became an unofficial team initiation device.


These are the moments you remember long after the event goes quiet.


**Done By Enthusiasts**


Perhaps the best observation came from Max, who described Goodwood in three words:


“Done by enthusiasts.”


That's what makes the event special.


It's built by people who genuinely love motorcycles, cars, racing, engineering, and all the wonderfully irrational reasons humans create machines that go faster than they need to.


It's also what makes LiveWire feel at home there.


Whether people arrived curious about EVs, searching for their next motorcycle, or simply looking for the coolest thing they'd see all weekend, the conversations, reactions, and smiles told the story.


The people made the weekend.


And after four scorching days, countless conversations, several hillclimb runs, more than a few laughs, and at least one mosquito-fueled survival exercise, the team packed up and headed home. Probably a couple pounds lighter. Definitely a few stories richer.


See you next year, Goodwood.
