# Alex vs. Paradox

Paradox is the best-known conversational assistant in hiring, and for high-volume hourly work it earns that reputation: text-to-apply, chat-based qualification and self-service scheduling that move thousands of applicants with almost no recruiter time. Alex is built for the next step: conducting the actual interview over voice or video, scoring it, checking the candidate is who they claim to be, and syncing the result to your ATS. If getting hourly applicants to apply and book is the problem, Paradox. If nobody has time to interview them, Alex. Here's the side-by-side.


## What each tool actually is


**Paradox** is a conversational assistant. Candidates interact through chat and SMS. They apply by text, answer knockout questions in a conversation, and pick an interview slot themselves. It has native ATS and HRIS connections behind it. It is a front-of-funnel automation product, and the funnel is where it stops.


**Alex** is an[AI recruiter](https://www.alex.com/platform) . It runs the screening interview itself over video, phone or WhatsApp, with SMS handling invitations and reminders, scores the conversation against the role, verifies integrity and identity,[coordinates scheduling](https://www.alex.com/product/coordinator) , and writes results back to your ATS.


These can be complementary rather than either/or depending on what your team needs.

## Where Paradox fits


**High-volume hourly conversion.** Text-to-apply and conversational qualification move applicants who would abandon a long form interview.


**Franchise-scale self-service booking.** Store and site managers booking their own slots is well-trodden in Paradox, but it stops at the booking.


If conversational apply for high-volume hourly roles is your only constraint, Paradox will do the job.


## Where Alex pulls ahead


### A scored interview, not a chat transcript


This is the whole distinction. Paradox's candidate interaction is chat and SMS: structured questions, knockout logic, a booked slot. That is filtering, and filtering still leaves the interviewing to be done.


Alex conducts interviews via a voice or video conversation that follows up on vague answers, probes a claim on a résumé, and produces a scored, reviewable record tied to the role's criteria. Chat tells you a candidate says they hold a forklift certification. A conversation tells you how they describe the last time they used it.


That difference decides what a hiring manager receives: a shortlist of people who cleared the filters and still need interviewing, or interviews already done.


### Integrity signals on the conversation


Alex's[cheat detection](https://www.alex.com/product/verify) scores video, audio and transcript separately for external assistance, AI-generated answers, multiple speakers, eye and head tracking and voice modulation, against a threshold you set per role anywhere from 0 to 100. Fraud verification scores identity separately — disposable emails, VOIP numbers, VPNs, CNAM mismatches, résumé-to-profile inconsistencies — and a Stripe-backed document-and-selfie ID check can be switched on per job. If an interview score is going to displace a recruiter phone screen, it has to be defensible.


## What to test in a pilot


Run one high-volume requisition through both and measure:


- Completion rate on your hardest-to-reach role, not the one that fills itself
- Days from application to a scored interview, and how much interviewing work still sits with recruiters afterwards
- What integrity signals exist on a completed screen, and the false-positive rate on review
- Whether results write back to your[ATS](https://www.alex.com/integrations) without a manual export


## The verdict


Choose Paradox if conversational apply, texting reach and self-service scheduling across a very large hourly funnel is the job. Choose Alex if you need the interview itself conducted, scored and verified before a human spends a minute on it.
