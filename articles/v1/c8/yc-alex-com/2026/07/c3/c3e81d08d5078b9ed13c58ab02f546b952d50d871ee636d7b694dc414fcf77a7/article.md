# Alex vs. Bullhorn

Bullhorn is the system of record for a large share of the staffing industry, and if your recruiters live in it all day that gravity is real. Alex is an AI recruiter: it runs the screening conversation itself across channels and pushes scored results back into the ATS you already run. Which is why this probably isn't an either/or. Bullhorn is where the record lives. Alex is what happens to a candidate before there's anything worth recording. Here's the side-by-side.


## What each tool actually is


**Bullhorn** is a staffing ATS/CRM. Requisitions, candidate records, client and contact relationships, submittals, placements and redeployment history: it's the operational spine of an agency. Its candidate-facing automation is chat plus scheduling.


**Alex** is an[AI recruiter](https://www.alex.com/platform) . It conducts interviews itself over video, phone or WhatsApp, scores the answers,[verifies](https://www.alex.com/product/verify) integrity and identity,[coordinates scheduling](https://www.alex.com/product/coordinator) against live calendars, and writes the result back into your ATS.


Bullhorn holds the record; Alex runs the conversation that produces something worth recording. Alex is built to[integrate directly with Bullhorn](https://www.alex.com/blog/partnership-announcement-alex-x-bullhorn) . For most agencies the realistic setup is both.

## Where Bullhorn fits


**It owns the record.** Every candidate, client, submittal and placement in one auditable place, which is exactly why Alex writes into it rather than around it.


**Redeployment depth.** Placement history lets a recruiter put last quarter's contractor back to work this week.


If a single system of record for candidates, clients and placements is your only constraint, Bullhorn does a great job.


## Where Alex fits


### The screening conversation, not the intake form


Alex interviews: it asks a follow-up because an answer was thin, probes a gap in a work history, or hands a recruiter a scored transcript instead of a completed form.


Alex does that part over phone or video, on the channels field and hourly candidates actually use. WhatsApp carries both calls and messages and is the strongest channel outside the US, and SMS handles invitations, reminders and scheduling around it.


### Integrity signals, not just document checks


Bullhorn offers document verification — a real capability, and the right one for right-to-work and credential paperwork. It's a different question from whether the person in the interview answered their own questions.


Alex's cheat detection scores video, audio and transcript for external assistance, AI-generated answers, multiple speakers, eye and head movement and voice-changing tools, against a threshold you set per role. Fraud verification scores identity separately — disposable email addresses, VOIP numbers, VPNs, résumé-to-profile inconsistencies — and optional ID verification adds a document-and-selfie check. The report breaks signals out by analysis type, so a recruiter can see why something was flagged.


In contract staffing, where a placement can start remote inside a week, that distinction is money.


## What to test in a pilot


Point both at the same desk for a month and measure:


- Completion rate on your hardest-to-fill requisition, not your easiest
- Hours from application to a scored, reviewable interview
- Share of interviews flagged for integrity review, and the false-positive rate on review
- Whether scores and transcripts reach the[ATS record](https://www.alex.com/integrations) without a manual export


## The verdict


Keep Bullhorn. It's your record, your CRM and your redeployment engine. Add Alex if what's slowing your desks down is the gap between an application arriving and a recruiter knowing quickly whether it's worth a call — screening at volume, on channels candidates answer, with integrity.
