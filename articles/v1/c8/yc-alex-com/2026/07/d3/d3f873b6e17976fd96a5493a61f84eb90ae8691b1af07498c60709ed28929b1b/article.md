# Alex vs. Eightfold

Eightfold is a talent-intelligence platform, and its skills graph is impressive. It sits underneath sourcing, matching, internal mobility and workforce planning, and few vendors match that depth. Alex is an AI recruiter, running the hiring journey from start to finish. It runs the screening interview, scores it, confirms the candidate is who they say they are, and puts the result in your ATS. On Eightfold's side of the line, voice and video interviewing is an add-on module rather than the product. If you're buying a talent-intelligence backbone, look at Eightfold. If funnel throughput is what's breaking, Alex is built for that. Here's the side-by-side.


## What each tool actually is


**Eightfold** is a talent-intelligence platform. Its centre of gravity is inference about people, their skills, adjacencies, career trajectories, applied across your applicants, your employees and the wider market, with native ATS and HRIS connections underneath. Voice and video interviewing is available as an add-on module.


**Alex** is an[AI recruiter](https://www.alex.com/platform) . It conducts interviews over video, phone or WhatsApp, with SMS handling invitations and reminders, then scores the interview, verifies integrity and identity,[coordinates scheduling](https://www.alex.com/product/coordinator) against live calendars and syncs results back to your ATS so your team can interview everyone and hire the best.


These aren't the same purchase, and plenty of teams could run both: Eightfold to decide who's worth a conversation, Alex to have it at volume.

## Where Eightfold fits


**Skills-graph depth.** Eightfold's inference layer models what your workforce can do next. It's useful for internal mobility planning, though it decides who to interview rather than doing the interviewing.


**Platform breadth.** One contract across sourcing, matching and planning, with interviewing as one module among many.


If a talent-intelligence backbone spanning sourcing and internal mobility is your only constraint, Eightfold will do the job.


## Where Alex pulls ahead


### Interviewing as the product, not the add-on


Eightfold's public documentation lists voice and video interviewing as an add-on, which has a predictable consequence: the interview is one module competing for roadmap attention inside a very large product.


Alex's whole surface area is the hiring funnel. Interviews run over video, phone or WhatsApp, with SMS carrying invitations and reminders. WhatsApp does most of the work for global and frontline hiring — it handles calls as well as messages, and it runs on wifi rather than billable minutes.


### Integrity signals you can tune


Alex's[cheat detection](https://www.alex.com/product/verify) scores video, audio and transcript separately for external assistance, AI-generated answers, multiple speakers, eye and head tracking and voice modulation, against a threshold you set per role anywhere from 0 to 100. Signals land in the candidate report broken out by analysis type, so a reviewer sees *why* something was flagged rather than a single composite number.


Fraud verification runs as its own score — disposable emails, VOIP numbers, VPNs, CNAM mismatches, résumé-to-profile inconsistencies — and a Stripe-backed document-and-selfie ID check can be switched on per job.


## What to test in a pilot


Run one live requisition through both and measure:


- Completion rate on your hardest-to-reach role, not the one that already fills
- Days from application to a scored, reviewable interview
- Share of interviews flagged for integrity review, and the false-positive rate once a human looks
- Whether scores and transcripts land in your[ATS](https://www.alex.com/integrations) without a manual export


## The verdict


Choose Eightfold if you're buying a talent-intelligence platform and interviewing is one workflow inside it. Choose Alex if interviewing is the constraint, if you need candidates reached where they actually are, integrity checked before a human spends time, and scored results in your ATS the same day.
