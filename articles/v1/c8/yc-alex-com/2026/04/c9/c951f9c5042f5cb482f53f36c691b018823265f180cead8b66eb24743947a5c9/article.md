## **What Are Recruitment KPIs? (And Why Most Teams Track the Wrong Ones)**


A recruitment KPI measures how well your hiring process drives business results. The right metrics reveal bottlenecks that directly impact revenue, retention, and client satisfaction.


Most teams track the wrong numbers. Total resumes collected, social media impressions, and job board views look impressive on dashboards, but don't predict hiring success or profit margins.


The KPIs that matter connect daily recruiting activities to measurable outcomes. Time-to-hire shows where delays cost placement fees. Cost per hire reveals whether you're burning budget on low-return channels. Quality of hire proves your process actually works.


Your team probably spends 8-12 hours weekly pulling these numbers from spreadsheets and[Applicant Tracking System](https://docs.alex.com/integrations/overview) reports. That's time stolen from candidate relationships and client development. Real-time ATS integration eliminates this reporting burden, automatically updating KPIs while you focus on strategic work.


## **Efficiency & Speed KPIs That Protect Your Margin**


Speed is money. Every extra day a requisition lingers costs revenue per recruiter and opens the door for competitors to steal your candidates. These KPIs focus on the levers that shorten hiring cycles, expose bottlenecks, and protect margin.


### **1. Time to Hire**


**Description:** Calendar days from the first outreach until the candidate signs the offer.[Industry data shows](https://www.shrm.org/content/dam/en/shrm/research/benchmarking/Talent%20Access%20Report-TOTAL.pdf) that median timelines vary by role type: executive hires typically take around 60 days, while nonexecutive roles average about 44 days. Sector and seniority still influence these ranges, but these medians offer a more accurate benchmark for most teams.


‍ **Calculate:** Offer acceptance date minus first contact date.


**Why it matters:** Exposes every delay between first contact and signed offer. When a software engineer takes 52 days from initial screen to acceptance, that's 18 days of competitive risk where another company can swoop in with a faster offer.


Break into stages (sourcing-to-screen: 3 days, screen-to-interview: 5 days, interview-to-offer: 7 days, offer-to-acceptance: 3 days) to identify bottlenecks.[Self-service scheduling](https://www.alex.com/blog/automated-interview-scheduling-guide) drops time-to-hire by 5-7 days by eliminating coordination overhead.


### **2. Time to Fill**


**Description:** Broader window from requisition approval to acceptance of start date. Best-in-class staffing firms: 30 days, while enterprise[averages 45 days](https://www.shrm.org/content/dam/en/shrm/research/benchmarking/Talent%20Access%20Report-TOTAL.pdf) .


Long fill times hurt client satisfaction and forecast accuracy. Track it the same way finance tracks days' sales outstanding. Automating interview scheduling and pushing real-time status updates to hiring managers often saves a whole week.


**Calculate:** Start date acceptance minus requisition approval date.


**Why it matters:** Directly impacts forecasting and client satisfaction. When you promise a client 30 days and deliver in 45, you break the commitment and erode trust. Staffing operations directors live and die by fill rate accuracy, because every missed timeline results in frustrated clients and delayed revenue recognition.


Segment by urgency tier: emergency backfills (15-20 days), standard roles (30-35 days), specialized searches (60-75 days). Automating interview scheduling often saves a whole week.


### **3. Cost per Hire**


**Description:** Total recruiting spend divided by hires closed. SHRM reports an average of[$4,700 per hire](https://www.shrm.org/topics-tools/news/talent-acquisition/real-costs-recruitment) , with role complexity driving variation. Total costs can reach 3-4x salary when accounting for training and lost productivity.


**Calculate:** (Internal costs + external costs) ÷ total hires. Break into internal (recruiter salaries, technology) and external (ads, agencies, referral bonuses).


**Why it matters:** Exposes profit margins on every placement. A staffing firm charging $20,000 per placement but spending $6,500 in recruiting costs operates on thinner margins than competitors spending $3,800. If your recruiter, making $75,000, spends 60% of their time on admin, that's $45,000 in non-revenue activities. Reducing admin from 60% to 20% triples productive capacity without adding headcount.


### **4. Source of Hire**


**Description:** Identifies which channels produce accepted offers, not just applications. Referrals deliver a[46% higher retention rate](https://www.managementpaper.net/archives/2025/vol7issue1/PartC/7-1-34-199.pdf) than job boards.


**Calculate:** Hires from each channel ÷ total hires. Track conversion and cost-per-hire side-by-side.


**Why it matters:** Shows where the budget actually works versus where it generates noise. A job board generating 500 applications but zero hires isn't productive. Track first touch AND converting touch: a candidate who applied via Indeed 6 months ago and then responded to LinkedIn shouldn't be credited to LinkedIn. Industry patterns: healthcare staffing sees 50% from referrals, and technology roles are split between LinkedIn and specialized tech boards.


### **5. Applicant-to-Hire Ratio**


**Description:** This metric compares the total number of applicants to the number of successful hires. It helps you understand whether your talent pool is wide enough and if your screening process is effective.


**Calculate:** Total applicants ÷ number of hires.


**Why it matters:** Reveals whether you're attracting the right candidates or just collecting applications. A 60:1 ratio on entry-level retail might be expected, but 40:1 on senior engineering signals major targeting problems.


Processing 500 applicants to find 12 interview-worthy candidates means recruiters spend 20-30 hours reviewing resumes that never had a chance.[Automated qualification questions](https://www.alex.com/blog/automated-candidate-screening) can filter out 60-70% of unqualified applicants, cutting screening time from 30 hours to 8 hours per role.


### **6. Interview-to-Offer Ratio**


**Description:** Offers are extended in proportion to the number of candidates interviewed. Efficient pipelines: 10-15 interviews per offer. Ratios above 15 signal ineffective screening.


**Calculate:** Offers extended ÷ total candidates interviewed.


**Why it matters:** Interviewing 20 candidates to extend one offer wastes recruiter time, candidate goodwill, and hiring manager patience. Root causes: screening criteria don't match manager expectations, interview rubrics differ from job requirements, or you're passing candidates to hit activity targets.


Comparing ratios across recruiters, one running 6:1 while others average 14:1 signals either superior screening or suspiciously low standards. The goal isn't the lowest ratio but the efficient frontier where quality meets speed.


## **Quality of Hire Metrics That Prove ROI**


Speed alone doesn't keep clients coming back; you need proof that placements perform, stay, and make hiring managers happy. These metrics translate post-hire outcomes into actionable data.


### **7. Quality of Hire Score**


**Description:** A composite metric that evaluates how well new hires perform and stay with the company. Scores above 80 indicate strong hiring quality, while anything below 70 suggests the need for a deeper review of your screening and selection process.


**Calculate:** (Performance rating × 0.4) + (manager satisfaction × 0.3) + (retention rate × 0.3). Scale to 100 points.


**Why it matters:** The ultimate validation metric proving whether your hiring process works. Every other metric measures efficiency; this measures outcomes. A recruiting team filling roles in 25 days at $3,500 per hire looks efficient until 30% of placements fail within six months.


Build measurement systems: schedule 90-day and 180-day check-ins, collect performance ratings at standard intervals, and track one-year anniversaries. Segment by recruiter, source, and role type to identify what drives quality.


### **8. Offer Acceptance Rate**


**Description:** Accepted offers divided by total offers extended, multiplied by 100. Gold standard: 85-90%. Below 75% means burning recruiter hours on candidates who never start.


**Calculate:** (Accepted offers ÷ total offers) × 100.


**Why it matters:** Every declined offer represents 20-40 hours of wasted recruiter time, damaged client relationships, and reset timelines. A rate below 80% signals fundamental problems: overselling during interviews, uncompetitive compensation, or making offers to candidates who weren't genuinely interested.


Fix by compressing the gap between the final interview and the offer with automated approvals, sharing transparent compensation bands early, and adding a genuine-interest qualifier: 'What would need to be true for you to consider leaving seriously?'


### **9. First-Year Turnover Rate**


**Description:** Percentage of hires who leave within 12 months. Best-in-class firms: below 10%. Many industries: 20%.


**Calculate:** (Hires who left within 12 months ÷ total hires) × 100.


**Why it matters:** Most expensive recruiting failure. You've invested 30-50 hours sourcing and closing. Client paid placement fees. The new hire consumed onboarding resources, then left before delivering ROI. For staffing firms, trigger-fee refunds are hitting their profit margins directly.


Three failure windows: first 90 days (hiring mistakes), months 4-8 (onboarding or manager problems), months 9-12 (career development issues). Compare turnover across sources and hiring managers to identify root causes.


### **10. 90-Day Retention Rate**


**Description:** Employees still on payroll three months after start, divided by total hires. Target: 90% or higher. Below 80%: cultural or role mismatch.


**Calculate:** (Employees remaining after 90 days ÷ total hires) × 100.


**Why it matters:** An early warning signal before problems compound. A candidate leaving after six weeks signals that screening failed to identify fundamental mismatches that should've been obvious during interviews. Compare exit reasons against interview transcripts to pinpoint which competencies predict quick departures.


Familiar patterns: technical candidates leave when the tech stack differs from what they were told, sales candidates when quota expectations shift post-hire. For historically high-turnover roles, implement realistic job previews showing the actual work environment.


### **11. New Hire Performance Ratings**


**Description:** Manager ratings of each new hire at 90 and 180 days are then averaged. A rating around 80 on a 100-point scale is commonly considered strong.


**Calculate:** (90-day rating + 180-day rating) ÷ 2. Average across all new hires.


**Why it matters:** Validates whether candidates who looked strong in interviews actually deliver results. Closes the feedback loop between screening criteria and job success. When the 90-day average is 72, but your quality target is 80, you're either screening for the wrong competencies or interviews aren't predictive.


Calibrate by identifying the top 20% performers, analyzing their interview transcripts for common patterns, and then weighting those distinguishing factors more heavily. Separate subjective satisfaction from objective performance metrics.


### **12. Hiring Manager Satisfaction Score**


**Description:** NPS-style question: 'How likely are you to request this recruiting team again?' A net score above +50 keeps internal clients loyal. Below +20 warns of frustration.


**Calculate:** % promoters (9-10) minus % detractors (0-6).


**Why it matters:** A Leading indicator of recruiting team credibility, low scores signal that managers see recruiting as a burden rather than a service. Common complaints: poor candidate quality, slow turnaround, and lack of pipeline communication.


Track alongside hire quality to catch misalignments. If satisfaction runs +65 but quality scores 73, you're delivering speed but not rigor. If quality scores 85 but satisfaction sits at +28, it's good candidates, but a painful process. Segment by manager tenure and role level.


## **Candidate Experience Metrics That Protect Your Brand**


Candidates decide how they feel about your company long before they sign an offer. When the process drags, or interviews get rescheduled three times, even the[perfect match will disappear](https://www.alex.com/blog/recruiting-black-hole) . Track the right experience KPIs to keep your pipeline moving and employer brand intact.


### **13. Candidate Satisfaction Score**


**Description:** After every interview, candidates rate their experience on a 1-10 scale, and the ratings are then averaged. An NPS-style score above 40 is strong across industries.


**Calculate:** Average all candidate ratings (1-10 scale) across interviews.


**Why it matters:** Satisfaction matters even for rejected applicants. The software engineer you reject today might be perfect for a different role in six months, or refer qualified friends. Organizations treating rejected candidates with respect see 2-3x higher application rates on future postings.


Survey every candidate, hired, rejected, and withdrawn, to avoid sampling bias, and break scores by interview stage to identify where the process fails. Real insights come from rejected candidates who still rated 7/10 or higher.


### **14. Interview Completion Rate**


**Description:** No-shows waste recruiter hours and lengthen time-to-hire; completion rates below 80% signal broken scheduling or lack of engagement.


**Calculate:** Completed interviews ÷ scheduled interviews initially.


**Why it matters:** When completion dips below 75%, pipeline velocity suffers, and recruiters spend more time rescheduling than interviewing. Common causes: too much time between scheduling and interview date, poor logistics communication, and overscheduling passive candidates who weren't serious.


Send three touchpoints: immediate confirmation when scheduled, a reminder 24 hours before, and a final reminder 2 hours before. No-show rates drop 40% with three touchpoints versus one; track patterns by source and stage to reveal where candidates drop off.


### **15. Application Completion Rate**


**Description:** Measures submitted applications divided by started applications. Best-in-class funnels: 10-15% conversion from started to submitted.


**Calculate:** Submitted applications ÷ started applications.


**Why it matters:** Clunky forms turn paid traffic into abandoned carts. Each additional required field drops completion rates by 3-8%. A 15-field application with 45% completion could hit 62% by cutting to 6 fields. Essential fields only: name, contact information, resume/LinkedIn, and work authorization.


Everything else waits until after the initial screen. Best candidates and employed people considering a switch apply during the commute or lunch break. If it takes more than 90 seconds on mobile, you're losing candidates.


### **16. Time in Stage**


**Description:** Average days candidates wait at each funnel step, ideal targets: screening-to-interview (3 days), interview-to-offer (7 days).


**Calculate:** Average days between stage entry and exit for each funnel stage.


**Why it matters:** Exposes specific bottlenecks that aggregated metrics hide. When candidates wait 11 days between the final interview and the offer, the problem might be approval workflows requiring three VP signatures, compensation negotiations taking too long, or background check delays.


You can't fix 'time-to-hire is slow', but you can fix 'offer approval takes 11 days because CFO reviews every offer manually.' Set stage-specific targets by urgency tier to catch delays immediately.


### **17. Candidate Net Promoter Score**


**Description:** Single-question survey: 'How likely are you to recommend applying here?' Anything above +30 is good; +50 is exceptional.


**Calculate:** % promoters (9-10) minus % detractors (0-6).


**Why it matters:** Predicts the trajectory of the employer brand. A score below +20 means you're losing referrals, getting negative Glassdoor reviews, and struggling to attract top talent over time. A score above +50 indicates candidates actively recommend your company even when they don't get hired; that's when your recruiting process becomes a competitive advantage.


Detractors are often angry about ghosting, poor communication, or misleading job descriptions. Turn promoters into referral sources by asking: 'Who else in your network should we know about?'


## **Diversity & Fairness Metrics That Reduce Legal Risk**


Hiring faster means nothing if your process quietly sidelines qualified people. These KPIs protect your organization from litigation risk and reputation damage while keeping your funnel fair and data-driven.


### **18. Diversity Hiring Rate**


**Description:** Reveals whether your hires reflect your applicant mix. Matching or exceeding the applicant pool diversity shows real progress. Falling short flags hidden bias in screening.


**Calculate:** (Diverse hires ÷ total hires) compared to (diverse applicants ÷ total applicants).


**Why it matters:** Context matters. If your hires are 28% diverse but the applicant pool is 42% diverse, you're losing diverse candidates somewhere in your funnel. If hires are 31% diverse and the pool is 26% diverse, your screening process actually favors diversity through[structured interviews](https://www.alex.com/blog/standardizing-interviews) .


Break metrics by role type and level. If entry-level diversity is 38% but senior leadership is 12%, you don't have a recruiting problem; you have a retention and advancement problem.


### **19. Pass-Through Rate by Demographic**


**Description:** Number of candidates from each demographic who advance to interview, divided by total applicants in that group. Healthy funnels show minimal variance, ideally less than 10 percentage points.


**Calculate:** (Demographic candidates advancing ÷ total demographic applicants) by demographic group.


**Why it matters:** Reveals where bias enters your process. If female candidates pass from application to screen at 18% while male candidates pass at 31%, your resume screening process has a gender bias. Common causes: resume keyword matching favoring specific educational backgrounds, 'culture fit' assessments defaulting to 'people like us,' years of experience requirements excluding career-changers.


Fix combines blind screening (removing names, universities, and dates) with structured assessment (the same questions, the same rubric). Typically narrows gaps from 20+ points to under 10 points within one quarter.


### **20. Adverse Impact Ratio**


**Description:**[The EEOC's 80% rule](https://www.eeoc.gov/laws/guidance/questions-and-answers-clarify-and-provide-common-interpretation-uniform-guidelines) : if the protected group's selection rate drops below 80% of the highest group's rate, this signals potential adverse impact and may prompt investigation.


**Calculate:** Protected group's hire rate ÷ majority group's hire rate. Anything under 0.80 demands immediate action.


**Why it matters:** The 80% rule isn't a legal threshold; it's an investigative trigger. If the EEOC investigates, you'll need to prove that the selection criteria are job-related and business-necessary. Structured interviews with documented scoring provide that defense.


Example: You hire 15% of white applicants and 9% of Black applicants. Ratio is 0.09 ÷ 0.15 = 0.60, well below the 0.80 threshold. Test each funnel stage separately; you might pass on final hires but fail at the interview stage.


### **21. Structured Interview Adoption Rate**


**Description:**[Structured interviews](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/380930509_Exploring_Interview_Dynamics_in_Hiring_Process_Structure_Response_Bias_and_Interviewee_Experience) , same questions, same scoring rubric,[cut bias more effectively](https://www.alex.com/blog/how-to-reduce-bias-in-hiring-process) than any other single change. Also, improve the quality of hire through structured conversations.


**Calculate:** Structured interviews ÷ total interviews.


**Why it matters:** The Single highest-impact bias reduction tool is to assess competencies systematically rather than relying on the interviewer's intuition. Most organizations resist because interviewers claim it feels 'robotic' or limits judgment; the data says their judgment is the problem, track adoption by interviewer and role.


When the engineering team with 95% adoption shows 84 quality-of-hire and 35% diverse hiring, while the sales team with 40% adoption shows 71 quality-of-hire and 19% diverse hiring, the data becomes impossible to ignore.
