# Avoid Errors in Duty Drawback Claims: 6 Common Mistakes and How to Fix Them

## 6. Lack of expertise or professional assistance


Duty drawback isn’t something most companies handle every day, and that’s exactly why so many claims are filed incorrectly. Without a strong understanding of customs law, classification codes, documentation requirements, and filing protocols, mistakes are almost inevitable.


Yet many companies assume drawback is a straightforward refund form, something that can be handed off to someone in accounting, finance, or logistics. The reality is far more complex. To avoid errors in duty drawback claims, it’s critical to have deep regulatory knowledge or a trusted partner who does.


### Drawback law is technical and nuanced


Duty drawback is governed by Title 19 of the U.S. Code and administered through highly specific regulations from U.S. Customs and Border Protection. These rules change over time and how they’re enforced can vary based on port, officer, or claim type.


Without professional expertise, companies often:


- Misinterpret eligibility requirements
- Apply the wrong HTS codes or substitution standards
- Fail to track claimable inventory correctly
- Submit incomplete or non-compliant documentation
- Lose claims to missed deadlines or invalid matches


These aren’t just clerical errors—they’re legal and regulatory missteps that can cost you the entire refund.


### Filing internally often means leaving money behind


Even if a claim gets accepted, many internal teams fail to maximize refund amounts. For example, they may file under direct identification when substitution could deliver higher payouts, or ignore destruction claims entirely.


Some companies submit partial claims on just a handful of imports, unaware that they could have filed for five years of retroactive refunds across multiple ports. These missed opportunities add up fast, especially for high-volume importers and exporters.


### Brokers help, but even they have limits


Working with a customs broker is a step in the right direction, but not all brokers are created equal. Some lack specialization in drawback; or only support filing without offering deeper audit preparation, compliance checks, or refund optimization. Others operate with outdated systems that require heavy manual inputs and long back-and-forth communication chains.


In many cases, brokers still ask you to do most of the heavy lifting: assembling documents, tracking deadlines, managing records, and ensuring match eligibility.


### Why modern support matters more than ever


CBP audits are increasing in recent years, and refund values are higher than ever. That means relying solely on spreadsheets or old-school brokers is no longer good enough. The combination of regulatory complexity and financial upside demands a more proactive, tech-enabled approach.


Having the right expertise on your side can mean the difference between a rejected claim and a six-figure refund.
