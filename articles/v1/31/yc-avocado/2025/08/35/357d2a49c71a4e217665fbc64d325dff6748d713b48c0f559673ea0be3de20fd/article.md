# Texas Just Made It Easier to Run a Food Truck: What You Need to Know (August 2025 Update)

Good news, food truck owners! Texas just passed **HB 2844** — signed June 20, 2025 — and it’s a game-changer for food trucks.


Starting **July 1, 2026** , you’ll only need **ONE** state license to run your truck anywhere in Texas. No more buying a new health permit every time you roll into a different city.


If you’re a small-scale food business (under $1.5M/year and with the right state permit or food manufacturer license), cities can’t make you pay their permit fees or get extra local licenses.


## **🛻 Your New State License**


-


**Required July 1, 2026**


-


One license per truck (list multiple trucks on one application)


-


Inspection within 14 days after your complete application — must pass to operate


-


Renew once a year; you can keep working if your renewal’s pending


-


Lets you work in multiple cities without those extra local health permits


## **🚫 What Cities Can’t Do to You (If You Have the State License)**


-


Cap the number of state licenses


-


Ban propane beyond state rules


-


Make “distance from restaurant” rules


-


Force a commissary if your truck’s fully equipped & waste is handled right


-


Require fingerprints or GPS to get licensed


-


Demand constant movement except when serving


-


Add extra fire/health inspections if you already passed within the year


-


Charge local permit fees if you’re a qualifying small-scale food biz


## **🧼 What Inspectors Will Look At**


-


Truck is safe & clean for storing, prepping, and serving food


-


Food safety rules followed


-


License & inspection cert posted where customers can see


-


CDL on file if required for your truck


## **📍 Local Rules Still in Play**


-


Fire code, zoning, and where you can park/serve


-


Non-conflicting operational rules


-


Small-scale exemption only covers permits/fees — you still follow safety & zoning laws


## **🗓️ Timeline**


-


**June 20, 2025** – Law signed


-


**Sept 1, 2025** – Rulemaking starts


-


**May 1, 2026** – Rules finalized


-


**July 1, 2026** – State license required, small-scale local permit relief kicks in


## **✅ Your To-Do List**


-


Figure out your license type (Type I/II/III)


-


**Type I** – Vendors who **dispense only prepackaged foods** , don’t serve time- or temperature-controlled or potentially hazardous beverages, and present a **low risk** to public health.


-


**Type II** – Vendors who serve foods that require **limited handling and preparation** (e.g., assembling sandwiches, warming items) but do **not perform full cooking or extensive food prep** .


-


**Type III** – Vendors who **prepare, cook, hold, and serve** food directly from their vehicle—essentially full-service operations with higher complexity and risk.


-


Get inspection-ready: temps, propane setup, waste disposal, clean equipment


-


Gather paperwork: registration, insurance, permits, menu, planned stops


-


Keep following local fire/zoning rules


-


If you’re under $1.5M revenue, confirm your small-scale status & get your state permit or manufacturer license


**Bottom line:** One license, statewide freedom. Get your truck inspection-ready and apply before **July 1, 2026** so you can roll anywhere in Texas without the local permit hassle.
