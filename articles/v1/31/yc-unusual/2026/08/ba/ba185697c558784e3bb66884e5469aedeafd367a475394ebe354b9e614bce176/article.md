# Microsoft Clarity splits AI citations into branded and generic queries, its third AI-visibility release in 25 days

Microsoft[announced on August 3](https://clarity.microsoft.com/blog/branded-non-branded-queries/) that Clarity's AI Citations dashboard now labels each query as branded or non-branded. "Not all AI visibility comes from the same kind of query: some citations are driven by queries that reference a brand directly, while others come from broader, generic queries an AI system uses to help answer a prompt," the announcement reads.


The release has three parts. Individual queries in the Queries card now carry a branded or non-branded label. Share of Authority, Clarity's measure of how much of the cited ground a site holds for a given query, is now broken out by query type. And a new filter applies the split across the whole dashboard, so a team can compare how it performs when an AI system looks the brand up by name against how it performs in generic category research. Microsoft's stated goal: "By separating brand-led demand from generic discovery, you can better assess brand strength, identify discovery and consideration opportunities, and interpret changes in citation performance with greater confidence."


It is Clarity's third AI-citation release in 25 days, following Topic Insights on July 9 and automatic query-topic grouping on July 22, a cadence[PPC Land tracked](https://ppc.land/microsoft-clarity-splits-ai-citations-by-brand-in-3rd-release-in-25-days/) .[Search Engine Land confirmed](https://searchengineland.com/microsoft-clarity-adds-branded-and-non-branded-ai-queries-484185) the feature is live in the AI Visibility dashboard. In the sample dashboard Microsoft published (its own example, so treat the numbers as illustrative), branded queries drove roughly 43% of about 8,100 citations and held the two highest Share of Authority figures, at 24.2% and 22.3%; the highest-volume non-branded query sat fourth at 20.3%.


Clarity is free, and Microsoft is now shipping AI-visibility features monthly. Citation tracking, the entry product for much of the AI-visibility tool market, is becoming a standard feature of a mainstream analytics suite.


The takeaway: the split matters more than the dashboard. Branded queries measure demand a brand has already earned, buyers asking AI about it by name. Non-branded queries are the open category research where[AI models decide who makes the shortlist](https://www.unusual.ai/blog/the-two-hurdles-every-brand-must-clear-to-win-ai-recommendations) in the first place, and that is where market position is won or lost. A citation count still only shows what AI models retrieve,[not why they weigh a brand the way they do](https://www.unusual.ai/blog/ai-share-of-voice-is-meaningless) or what they tell a buyer about it. Read Clarity's new split as a free early-warning gauge, not a diagnosis.
