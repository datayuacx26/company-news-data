# Where Personalization Meets Patient Privacy

On average, healthcare firms invest[nearly a third](https://www.lob.com/state-of-direct-mail/2026-report-healthcare) of their marketing budget into direct mail. And it’s easy to see why. From nurturing existing relationships to winning back high-value patients, direct mail continues to deliver unmatched trust and relevance.


The proof is in the performance.


According to a[recent report by USPS](https://www.uspsdelivers.com/direct-mail-insights/health/metrics) , most healthcare businesses report[direct mail response rates](https://www.lob.com/blog/direct-mail-response-rates) of 11 to 15%. What’s more, out of all industries surveyed, **healthcare organizations are most likely to have response rates of more than 25%** .


These exceptional response rates are just the beginning. Healthcare companies have an opportunity to drive engagement even further by providing the[in-depth personalization](https://www.lob.com/blog/personalization-in-direct-mail-proven-response-rate-results-and-insights) that consumers have come to expect.


However, healthcare faces a unique challenge. Unlike other industries, direct mail in healthcare serves a dual purpose – it's as much about[compliance](https://www.lob.com/blog/compliance-workflows-for-regulated-direct-mail) as it is marketing communication. Here’s how to make your next direct mail campaign more[personalized without overstepping](https://www.lob.com/blog/how-to-personalize-healthcare-direct-mail-without-compromising-patient-trust) :


## Start with context, not sensitive data


Personalization doesn’t require extensive personal information or a huge dataset to create relevance – it just needs more context.


Take sensitive data out of the equation by avoiding overly detailed health references, sensitive assumptions, or excessive personal data. You can make patient mail more timely and meaningful by including information such as provider relationships, appointment status, care journey stage, or geography.

## It’s all about the experience


The most effective direct mail personalization goes beyond just adding a patient’s name or inserting basic details. True personalization uses context – everywhere from the copy to the imagery – to reduce friction and make every interaction more useful.

## Build trust through transparency


Trust is never a given – especially in healthcare. Personalization works best when it’s built on a foundation of transparency. Patients are[more likely to engage](https://www.lob.com/blog/how-to-earn-trust-with-your-marketing-leads-and-customers) when communications are clear, expected, and consistent. You can earn their trust by including recognizable branding, clear purpose, and obvious next steps in every mail piece.


## The future of patient communications


Making your mail more relevant doesn’t have to come at the cost of speed, compliance, or[patient privacy](https://www.lob.com/blog/how-healthcare-teams-keep-phi-safe-in-mailing-workflows) . Advancements like predictive modeling and no-code design are making[personalization at scale](https://www.lob.com/blog/how-to-automate-personalized-direct-mail-at-scale) more accessible than ever before.


Healthcare organizations that harness these technologies to strike the right balance between personalization and patient privacy are reshaping the future of patient communications – creating[omnichannel experiences](https://www.lob.com/content/omnichannel-marketing) that are more relevant, trusted, and patient centered.
