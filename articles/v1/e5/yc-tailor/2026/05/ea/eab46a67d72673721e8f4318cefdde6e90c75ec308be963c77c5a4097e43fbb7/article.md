## How AI Chats Are Changing Ecommerce


Depending on your customer’s AI experience of choice (Gemini, Perplexity, ChatGPT, Claude, etc.), they’ll encounter different shopping interfaces — some rely on **API systems** to translate a website’s shopping experiences to the chat interface, others feature **retailer-built apps** for shopping within a chat, and a few open a **mini web-browser** and access your site as they normally would.


If you’re tech-minded ([or in Marketing, Sales, and Ops](https://www.tailor.tech/use-cases/omni-channel-retail) ), you may already be cringing at the wrinkle to this new convenience: purchases that happen outside your curated experience disrupt your funnels, data tracking, and customer retention strategies.


Before you panic, no, you don’t have to lose your tracking data, new customers don’t have to miss your post-purchase funnel, and you certainly don’t have to worry about fulfillment woes losing your new customer.

API (UCP & ACP) ACP and UCP allow AI chat users to interact with your website through a standardized API, providing your customer with the information they need to make a decision and purchase a product. By integrating both standards into your ecommerce experience, you allow new customers to be targeted through AI chat, while also providing AI agents with a standardized method of traversing your shop.


Web-Browser AI chat opens a mini-browser within the chat, allowing potential customers to be exposed to your brand the way you intended. Having a customer experience that's tailored to work in a smaller form factor helps you control how you present, while giving potential AI-shopping customers a frictionless experience.


Retailer App Retailer-built apps are customized experiences constructed with AI chat in mind. These interfaces are completely within your control, but require a developer to create. When you develop a custom AI chat app, you're extending your brand experience into a platform these new customers are using every day — ensuring you maintain complete touch on every step of the agentic commerce process.


## Kickstarting Your Agent-Ready Storefront: What You Should Know


Before we dig into the more technical aspects of how agent-ready storefronts, agentic commerce, and headless ERP interact, let’s take a look at a few of the key changes you’ll need to consider when developing your existing storefronts to accommodate AI agents:


### 1. Pretty Interfaces Won’t Woo Agents


Your store might look incredible, but there’s a chance it ends up looking like a mess to an AI agent. Brilliant graphic design and avant-garde typography choices may win over customers and solidify your brand, but these choices might steer agents in the wrong direction — frustrating your potential customer and creating a data point that may prompt that AI chat tool to not recommend your products in the future.


### 2. Frictionless Navigation Keeps the Ball Rolling


Designing your storefronts for human customers is always the right move, but it’s important not to get caught up in the flourishes that make a site feel great to browse — because many of the design choices made to appeal or make a site more accessible to humans are confusing for AI agents. Creating an agent-ready storefront means having a site that looks good while removing friction and barriers for agents. This may look like:


-


Implementing clean CSS and other code elements within the website that are clearly sign-posted for agents


-


Removing difficult-to-resolve pop-ups that would require human customers to enter information to proceed


-


Simplifying UX elements that follow a logical flow from adding items to cart to checking out


### 3. Other AI Standards to Keep in Mind


Clean code, logical UX, and peeling back design flourish are all great starts, but there are a few other AI-related standards that can make or break your agent-ready storefront:


-


Keep your copy **descriptive and focused** on the features of the product, making things easier for agents to parse.


-


Ensure your web developers have the right documentation available on your site, so agents know how to maneuver and whether they’re even allowed to operate for their user (these files are called **llms.txt and robots.txt** , respectively).


-


Ensure you have attribution fields set up so that AI agents operating through API connections deliver all the data you would usually get from a customer browsing — and making a purchase on — your site.
