## 3 Reasons Your Inventory Tracking Isn’t Quite Working For Your Retail Brand


On a good week, your inventory numbers check out, and all systems are green — but there’s a catch. These “good” weeks are actually when your traffic is low or within expectations.


Your actual “big picture” good weeks — when you’re managing rushes, huge orders, and exceeding sales projections — are fraught with emergency recounts, unexpected out-of-stock notices, and overselling inventory. As a scaling retailer, this state of affairs is unsustainable and prevents you from operating at peak efficiency.


While these conditions are problematic, they don’t have to become the status quo. Before we identify some solutions, let’s explore the three main reasons your inventory tracking isn’t working for your business.


### System Visibility Is Siloed


The first reason that your ecommerce inventory tracking might not be working for you is a problem that plagues many growing retail brands: **system visibility.** In the same way you might struggle to read a road sign in the early hours of a foggy morning, your inventory tracking software struggles to pull accurate data from other parts of your business. When your system visibility is low, it’s because the rest of your tech stack is siloed (or blocked off).


Because your stack is blocked off, each part of your business is isolated, with each part operating in its own bubble. This breaks system cohesion and results in your tech stack having several points of fractured authority — or different batches of data claiming to have the truth about your inventory (without knowing what other parts of the system have).


In order to fix this problem, you need to remove silos from your business and establish **a single source of truth.**


### Data Isn’t Flowing Properly


A knock-on effect of having a siloed tech stack is that none of the data in your business flows as efficiently as it should. Ideally, a growing retailer would have a system where data is automatically reconciled by a single source of truth and automatically moved where it needs to go.


Instead, retailers often end up with stagnant data that’s prone to errors caused by lagging reports and manually reconciling data in spreadsheets. Having to move data between parts of your tech stack with spreadsheets as a translation layer often results in a few major ecommerce inventory tracking issues:


-


**Transposed inventory numbers.** This leads to discrepancies between actual inventory in the warehouse and what’s on the books.


-


**Lost data.** When multiple people are working in one spreadsheet, the incident rate for accidentally deleted data goes up — creating opportunities for inventory tracking oversights.


-


**Slow system-wide reconciliation.** Spreadsheets, while an invaluable and powerful tool, just aren’t meant to do system-wide inventory management. Because data needs to be copied to multiple software, reconciliation delays build up across your system.


### Operations Stalled by Disconnected Tools


The final reason your ecommerce inventory tracking isn’t quite working for your business is that your tech stack and necessary tools aren’t fully connected to each other. They may have limited connections between a handful of tools, but they’re cut off from the rest of your tech stack.


This results in having to create workarounds (which are often inefficient and prone to breaking), pay for expensive, custom translation layers, or, once again, rely on spreadsheets to fill the gaps across your business. When any of these temporary solutions break, it causes your day-to-day operations to stall — creating headaches for your Ops team.
