## What Is a Healthcare Claims Audit?


A healthcare claims audit is a structured review of submitted claims to verify that medical billing, coding, and documentation comply with payer and regulatory standards.


Healthcare claims audits run at four stages:


- **Pre-claim submission auditing (claim scrubbing):** Catches errors before the claim reaches the payer.
- **Retroactive claim auditing:** Reviews submitted or paid claims to surface undercoding, overcoding, or documentation gaps.
- **Compliance auditing:** Evaluates adherence to CMS guidelines, payer contracts, and coding standards.
- **CDI auditing:** Identifies clinical documentation gaps that affect coding accuracy upstream.

## How to Choose the Right Claims Audit Software


The right claims audit software should do more than flag errors after the fact. Here are the capabilities to look for when evaluating a platform:


- **Retroactive coding audit:** Reviews pre-coded charts to identify undercoding, missing services, and coding discrepancies against full clinical documentation.
- **Pre-submission claim scrubbing:** Validates claims against payer-specific rules before submission, catching billing discrepancies and[modifier](https://www.combinehealth.ai/blog/modifiers-in-medical-billing) errors before they cause denials.
- **Compliance auditing:** Audits coded claims against current CMS manuals, LCDs, NCDs, and commercial payer policies — not last quarter's rules.
- **CDI / documentation gap detection:** Surfaces incomplete clinical details and missing diagnoses upstream, before they affect code assignment and reimbursement.
- **Explainable audit outputs:** Provides line-by-line rationale for every finding, making results reviewable, defensible, and actionable.

## FAQs


**What are common issues found during a healthcare claims audit?**


The most common findings are insufficient documentation, lack of medical necessity, coding errors (upcoding, unbundling, modifier misuse), administrative errors (eligibility lapses, duplicate claims), and regulatory compliance failures like timely filing violations.


**What are the key steps in a medical claims audit?**


Define scope, pull a sample, gather documentation, review charts for coding accuracy and medical necessity, analyze findings, report results, implement corrective action, and schedule follow-up audits within 3–6 months.


**How often should a healthcare organization conduct claims audits?**


OIG guidelines recommend ongoing monitoring, not just periodic reviews. High-risk code types and high-volume providers warrant quarterly or monthly audits while broader population reviews typically run annually.

**What's the difference between a prospective and retrospective claims audit?**


A prospective claim audit catches errors before submission to prevent denials. A retrospective claim audit reviews claims after payment or denial to identify coding errors, underpayments, and documentation gaps for corrective action.
