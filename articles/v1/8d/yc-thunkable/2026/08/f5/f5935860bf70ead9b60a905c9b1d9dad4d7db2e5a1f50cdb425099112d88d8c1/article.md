# Thunkable vs. the AI App Builder Field: How the 2026 Options Actually Compare


Every AI app builder in 2026 will tell you it's the fastest way to go from idea to app. Most of that is true, as far as it goes. What it usually leaves out is *what kind of app* you end up with — and whether "app" means something that lives in a browser tab, or something a real person finds in the App Store, downloads, and opens from their home screen.


That distinction is the fault line running through this entire category. Here's where the major players actually land, and where Thunkable fits.

## The pattern nobody markets directly


Look past the individual feature lists and a pattern shows up fast: **most AI app builders generate web apps.** Full-stack web apps, sometimes very good ones — but web apps. Getting from there to something in the[App Store](https://www.apple.com/app-store/) and[Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps?hl=en_US) usually means a second tool, a wrapper, or handing the project to a developer.


‍


Gartner projects low-code tools will account for 75% of new application development by 2026. A huge share of that is real, durable value. It's also concentrated in web and internal tooling — not the specific job of "I want an app people can search for and download."


‍


Thunkable was built around that specific job from the start, and it's the reason the platform can publish directly to both app stores without a handoff to anyone else.

## What actually differs between the tools


**Who they're built for.** Lovable and Bubble assume you're comfortable iterating either entirely through prompting or reading some code. Quick tweaks like changing a color on a button requires changes through prompts or code. Replit assumes a developer's mental model — files, a console, version control — even with an AI agent doing the writing. Thunkable assumes none of that: describe your idea, get a working app, refine it in plain language. Thunkable also adds a visual design tool that makes it easy for a user to see all the screens on a canvas (similar to popular design tools like Figma or Canva) to edit and finalize your designs.


‍


**What "done" looks like.** For a web-first tool, done means deployed to a URL. For Thunkable, done means live in the App Store and Google Play — the step every other tool on this list treats as somebody else's problem.


‍


**How pricing behaves as you grow.** Credit-based models (Lovable, Replit, and most of the field) charge for generation, hosting, and usage from a shared balance. Independent reviews of Replit specifically report real bills landing well above the advertised plan cost once an app has actual users. Plan-based pricing, like Thunkable's, is easier to predict before you commit.


## So which one should you actually use?


**If your product is a web app, dashboard, or internal tool** — something people will only ever open in a browser — Lovable or Bubble are all reasonable starting points depending on how technical your team is.


**If you're comfortable in a developer environment and want an AI agent that writes real code inside it** — Replit is a legitimate, capable choice.


**If you have a mobile app idea and no interest in becoming a developer to ship it** — that's the specific gap Thunkable fills. Describe it, build it, publish it to both app stores, without a second tool or a handoff.

The AI app builder field solved "how do I get something built fast" a while ago. What most of it hasn't solved is "how do I get a real app into the App Store without hiring anyone." That's still the harder problem — and it's the one Thunkable was built to solve.
