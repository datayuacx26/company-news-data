# Canada as a Strategic Entry Point to North America [VIDEO]

As global supply chains evolve, Canada continues to stand out as a reliable and strategically positioned market for international trade. Its extensive transportation infrastructure, access to three oceans, and close integration with the United States give businesses several options for reaching customers across North America and beyond.


For companies entering the Canadian market, this combination creates a stable environment for growth. Businesses can benefit from established distribution networks, modern infrastructure, and connections to major markets in the Americas, Europe, Asia, the Middle East, and Africa.


A Multimodal Network Built for Trade


Canada’s size demands a diverse transportation network. More than 1.1 million kilometers of public roads support regional transportation and last-mile distribution across the country, while Canada’s rail system connects major inland markets with ports and border crossings.


Canada also has 17 major international airports, providing global connectivity for high-value and time-sensitive shipments. Businesses requiring faster transportation options can use[air cargo services](https://www.expeditors.com/services/transportation/air) alongside ocean, rail, and ground solutions to build a more flexible supply chain.


This multimodal network helps companies select the routes and transportation modes that best suit their cargo, timelines, and customers.


Ports Connecting Canada to Global Markets


Canada’s access to the Pacific, Atlantic, and Arctic oceans supports trade with markets around the world. On the West Coast, the Port of Vancouver serves as the country’s largest port and an important connection to Asia. On the East Coast, the[Port of Halifax](https://info.expeditors.com/horizon/port-of-halifax-a-strategic-atlantic-gateway-for-global-supply-chains?hsLang=en-us) operates year-round without ice restrictions, supporting consistent cargo movement across the Atlantic.


The Port of Montreal provides another major gateway for trade between North America and Europe. Together, these ports give importers and exporters multiple options for[ocean transportation](https://www.expeditors.com/services/transportation/ocean) , helping reduce dependence on a single gateway or trade corridor.


Continued investment in port infrastructure is also expected to expand capacity and improve the long-term efficiency of Canada’s transportation network.


Connected to the United States


Canada’s logistics network is closely integrated with the United States. The countries share more than 100 land border crossings, allowing cargo to move between major manufacturing, distribution, and consumer markets throughout North America.


Canada has also introduced a[land preclearance operation](https://www.canada.ca/en/border-services-agency/news/2025/01/the-cbsa-announces-first-canadian-land-preclearance-operation-in-the-us.html) designed to modernize border processes and support the secure movement of people and goods.
