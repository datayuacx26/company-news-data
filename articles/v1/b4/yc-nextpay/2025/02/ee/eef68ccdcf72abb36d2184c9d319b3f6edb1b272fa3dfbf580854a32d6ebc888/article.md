# Invoice vs Receipt vs Bill: Philippine Business Guide

For Philippine businesses, the simple distinction is:


- An **invoice** records a sale and requests or supports payment.
- A **receipt** acknowledges that payment was received.
- A **bill** is a practical payment request, often from the buyer’s point of view or for immediate-payment situations.


Under the Ease of Paying Taxes Act and BIR Revenue Regulations No. 7-2024, the invoice is now the primary sales document for both goods and services. Official receipts and similar documents are generally supplementary documents used to acknowledge payment.


This matters because a customer may casually ask for a “receipt,” while the BIR rules may require an invoice as the primary document for the sale.


## Quick Comparison


Document What It Means Typical Timing Current BIR Role


Invoice Evidence of the sale of goods or services Issued for the sale, whether payment is immediate or later Primary sales document


Receipt Acknowledgment that money was received Issued after collection or payment Supplementary payment document


Bill Request for payment or amount due Usually before payment, often immediate Business/payment term, not the main BIR category


This guide is a general explainer for business owners and finance teams. For tax filing, document registration, or audit positions, confirm details with the BIR, your accountant, or tax counsel.


**Business Takeaway**


Use the invoice to document the sale, the receipt or acknowledgment to document collection, and the bill to request payment before or outside a registered sales-document workflow.


## What is an Invoice?


An invoice is a written account that evidences a sale of goods or services. It tells the buyer what was sold, how much is due, who sold it, and the transaction details needed for accounting and tax records.


In the Philippines, invoices became more important after the Ease of Paying Taxes Act. RR No. 7-2024 implements the updated invoicing rules and treats invoices as the primary written evidence for sales of both goods and services.


Depending on the transaction, the invoice may be called a sales invoice, cash invoice, charge invoice, credit invoice, billing invoice, service invoice, or another name that describes the transaction. The important point is that the document is an invoice.


Common invoice uses:


- Billing a customer for a service project
- Selling goods to a customer
- Recording a credit sale with payment terms
- Supporting a VAT or non-VAT sale
- Tracking accounts receivable


## What is a Receipt?


A receipt acknowledges payment. It shows that money was received, either in full or in part.


Before EOPT, many service businesses used official receipts as their primary sales document. The current framework changed that. BIR guidance says official receipts are treated as supplementary documents after RR No. 7-2024. They can still help document collection, but they are not the primary document that supports the sale for BIR purposes.


Examples of supplementary payment documents include:


- Official receipt
- Collection receipt
- Acknowledgment receipt
- Cash receipt
- Other similar documents showing payment received


If you are still using old official receipt booklets, check the transitory rules carefully. BIR RMC No. 77-2024 clarifies options for remaining unused official receipts, including using them as supplementary documents or converting them into invoices if requirements are followed.


## What is a Bill?


“Bill” is usually an everyday business term. It means an amount due or a payment request.


For example:


- A restaurant gives a customer the bill before payment.
- A supplier sends a bill for materials delivered.
- A utility company issues a monthly bill.
- A client says they received your bill, even if the formal document is an invoice.


In accounting, what one side calls a bill may be the other side’s invoice. If you issue it as the seller, it is usually your invoice. If you receive it as the buyer, you may record it as a bill payable.


The BIR compliance question is not usually “Is this called a bill?” The better question is: “Is the correct invoice issued for the sale, and is payment properly acknowledged when collected?”


## When Should a Philippine Business Issue an Invoice?


RR No. 7-2024 implements invoicing requirements under the Tax Code as amended by the Ease of Paying Taxes Act. In general:


- VAT-registered persons issue VAT invoices for sales of goods or services.
- Non-VAT taxpayers issue invoices for sales, barter, exchange, or lease transactions covered by the rules.
- For non-VAT registered persons, an invoice is required for covered sales valued at PHP 500 or more, and when the buyer asks for one.
- For lower-value transactions, rules may allow aggregate invoicing at the end of the day when conditions are met.


The exact requirements depend on VAT status, transaction type, and BIR registration. Use official BIR guidance or your accountant’s advice before changing registered documents.


## What Information Usually Belongs on an Invoice?


RR No. 7-2024 and related BIR guidance set out invoice information requirements. A practical invoice checklist usually includes:


- Seller’s registered name
- Seller’s TIN and branch code
- VAT or non-VAT status
- Registered business address
- Invoice date
- Invoice serial number
- Buyer’s name, address, and TIN when required or applicable
- Description of goods or services
- Quantity, unit cost, and total amount
- VAT information when applicable
- Required non-VAT wording when applicable
- Authority to Print or system details where applicable


Do not treat a pretty PDF or payment link as automatically BIR-compliant. The document still needs to follow the rules for your registered invoice format.


## How Invoices and Receipts Work Together


For a credit sale, the sequence usually looks like this:


1. You deliver goods or render services.
2. You issue an invoice to record the sale and bill the customer.
3. The customer pays later.
4. You issue or keep a supplementary payment acknowledgment, such as a collection receipt, if your process requires one.
5. Your accounting records match the invoice, payment, and deposit.


For an immediate-payment sale, the invoice may also show that payment was made. The key is to distinguish between documenting the sale and acknowledging collection.


## Common Mistakes to Avoid


- Treating an official receipt as the primary sales document after the EOPT changes.
- Sending an informal bill or PDF without checking if it matches the registered invoice format.
- Assuming a receipt is enough for a buyer’s input VAT claim.
- Using old official receipt booklets without following BIR transitory rules.
- Forgetting that a customer’s casual word “receipt” may still require a proper invoice.
- Keeping invoices and payment records in separate tools with no reconciliation trail.

## Frequently Asked Questions


### Is an Invoice the Same as a Receipt in the Philippines?


No. An invoice is the primary sales document under current EOPT/BIR invoicing rules. A receipt generally acknowledges payment and is treated as a supplementary document.


### Is an Official Receipt Still Valid After EOPT?


Official receipts may still be used as supplementary payment documents or, in some cases, converted into invoices if BIR requirements are followed. Check RR No. 7-2024, RR No. 11-2024, and RMC No. 77-2024 before relying on old receipt booklets.


### What Should I Give a Customer Who Asks for a Receipt?


Ask what they need it for. If they need the primary sales document, issue the proper invoice. If they already have an invoice and only need proof of payment, a supplementary receipt or collection acknowledgment may be appropriate.


### Is a Bill Legally Different From an Invoice?


In everyday business language, a bill is a payment request. In BIR invoicing rules, the important document category is the invoice. A document called a bill may still need to be issued as or supported by a proper invoice.


### Can a Digital Invoice be BIR-compliant?


It can be, but only if the business follows the applicable BIR rules for invoices, systems, serial numbers, and registration. Do not assume any digital invoice template is compliant by default.
