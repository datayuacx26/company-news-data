## **Juicebox Agents have real autonomy**


This is the real product difference, and you can see it in how each company describes its own agent.


**Juicebox Agents 2.0** are built for autonomy *with* direction. You brief an agent the way you'd brief a sourcer: discuss trade-offs, refine the ideal profile, challenge its plan. Then it works continuously, adjusting its sourcing strategy in real time based on what it finds and what you approve or reject. It can also write multi-step outreach in your team's voice, and report back through a dashboard where you can run multiple agents at once. When the search drifts,you just talk with the agent and it changes course mid-search.


**Noon's agent** , based on its public product materials, is autonomy you calibrate after the fact. It selects candidates and initiates outreach, and you review its picks and give thumbs-up/thumbs-down signals. Noon's own content says match accuracy "typically reaches 70-80%+ positive signal rates" after "20-30 reviews." That's a real machine-learning loop, but it means your first weeks are teaching the agent by watching it be wrong, one shortlist at a time.


For a role you've hired ten times, calibration may be fine. For the searches that matter (new roles, ambiguous profiles, demanding hiring managers), you want an agent that can be told, not just graded.


In each demo, try asking:


- A must-have criterion changed *after* the search has started, and how the agent updates its strategy in response.
- The agent explaining what it searched, what it learned, and what it will try next.
- Several searches running in parallel, managed from one dashboard.
- A search redirected through conversation, without restarting the calibration loop.


Juicebox can complete all four.

An autonomous agent inherits the quality of the data it searches through.


Juicebox searches 800M+ profiles aggregated from 30+ sources, including GitHub and research publications, for technical talent. Profiles come with email and phone contact details, so outreach doesn't depend on InMail credits.


Noon describes its own coverage inconsistently across its public content: some pages claim large aggregated profile counts, others describe real-time web crawling.
