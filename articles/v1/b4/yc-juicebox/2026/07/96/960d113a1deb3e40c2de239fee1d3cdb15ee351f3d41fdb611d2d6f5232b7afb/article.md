# Juicebox vs Findem: A guide for TA teams

Juicebox and Findem agree on the future of recruiting: keyword search is dead, and the future of sourcing is understanding people, not matching strings.


The difference is what it takes to get a shortlist: with Juicebox, you describe who you're looking for and go. With Findem, you assemble the search from an attribute library, after a sales cycle and an implementation plan.


## **TL;DR**


- **Natural language vs attribute assembly:** Juicebox search is AI-assisted from the start: describe who you need in plain language and the AI builds the search with you. Findem's core model is attribute-based search over a large filter library, and its own natural-language assistant (Fia, announced December 2025) is still in early access.
- **Agents you direct vs. a copilot that ranks:** Juicebox Agents take a briefing through a conversation, adjust strategy mid-search, and learn from your feedback. Findem's Copilot compiles and ranks candidates from a job description or ATS requisition, and per[Findem's own documentation](https://findem.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/22714060654103-What-is-Findem-s-Copilot) , it "is not learning based on a user's activity." While its newer agents (via the Getro and Glider acquisitions) focus on job posts, inbound screening, and scheduling rather than outbound sourcing.
- **Features for advanced sourcing use cases:** Juicebox builds market compensation data and talent-flow mapping into every search, plus network sourcing for warm intros and hiring manager workflows. Findem's market intelligence runs at the analytics level; warm paths and in-platform hiring manager loops aren't in its documented product line.
- **A platform you can actually trial:** Juicebox has self-serve options with published pricing and a free trial, and scales from a solo recruiter to 5,000+ customers, including Notion, Ramp, Reddit, and more. Findem is sales-led: no public pricing, no trial, annual contracts, and reported multi-week implementations.
- **Secure on both sides:** Both companies publish independent Warden AI bias audits, and both hold SOC 2 Type II. Juicebox adds ISO 42001 readiness, a 5/5 G2 rating, and $115M+ in backing including an[$80M Series B in March 2026](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260310781820/en/Juicebox-Raises-%2480M-at-%24850M-Valuation-to-Help-Businesses-Reach-Top-Talent-Before-Anyone-Else-Does) .

## **Search you speak vs. search you assemble**


Findem's core product functionality is centered around pre-computed "attributes" built from career histories, company milestones, patents, and funding events, so you can search for patterns like startup experience or scaling exposure rather than keywords.


The tradeoff is the ramp. Because searches are composed from a large attribute library, proficiency takes time, and Findem's G2 reviews reflect that: "It isn't very intuitive for first-time users," and with so many options it's "difficult to know which filters to use."


Juicebox took the other path from the start: AI-assisted natural language search is the primary interface, not a layer. "Staff ML engineer, recommendation systems, open to Series B startups, not currently at FAANG" is a complete query. Juicebox interprets it and builds the structured search with you, and reasons about context (company stage, team, trajectory) much the way Findem's attributes aim to, without the assembly step.


The practical difference is in the speed to value: every recruiter on your team can run a real search and build a strong shortlist on day one.


## **What "AI agent" actually means**


**Juicebox Agents** are directable sourcing agents. You brief an Agent directly, like a sourcer: discuss trade-offs, challenge its plan. Then it runs, continuously sourcing, evaluating, and sending multi-step outreach in your team's voice, adjusting strategy in real time as results come in and as you redirect it in natural language.


**Findem's Copilot** , per its own help documentation, compiles and ranks top candidates from a job description or an ATS requisition, and "is not learning based on a user's activity." That's a deliberate design choice with a fairness rationale, and it works well for its purpose: the copilot produces a strong first slate. The interaction model is brief-and-rank rather than directable mid-search.


Findem's newer agents, added through its Getro and Glider acquisitions, focus on job posts, screening, and scheduling. However, they are more inbound-focused, priced per outcome, and recently integrated into the platform.


When assessing whether Findem fits, a few capabilities are worth pressure-testing:


- A must-have criterion changed after the search has started, and how the system updates its strategy in response.
- The agentic product’s ability to learn from feedback and improve overtime
- Managing several searches in parallel by one recruiter
- How much of the agent lineup is native product versus recently acquired and still integrating


## **Deep features for advanced use cases**


Juicebox surrounds sourcing with a layer built for the individual search:


- **Comp data and talent-flow mapping.** See what candidates actually earn and where talent is moving (compensation ranges, company context, talent-flow maps) before you write the first message. Findem offers market intelligence as an analytics module; Juicebox puts it in the recruiter's flow.
- **Network sourcing.** Juicebox surfaces the warm paths your team already has to a candidate, so outreach can start with an intro instead of a cold email. Not in Findem's documented product line.
- **Hiring manager workflows.** Structured calibration and review loops inside the platform. Their feedback produces a summary you can convert into a search or push to an agent in a single click. Also not documented at Findem.
- **Research signals:** Identify top research talent from their publication and citation record, making it easy to find deep technical experts and rank them by real scientific impact.


Speed to value shows up in our shipping cadence too. Juicebox Agents, Research Signals, Network Sourcing, and Hiring Manager Workflows all launched within the past year, and we post product updates biweekly on[our blog](https://juicebox.ai/blog/product-updates) .


## Sourcing today vs. sales cycle


Findem is enterprise software sold the enterprise way: without published pricing, no free trial, annual contracts, and implementations that reviewers and analysts report at two to six weeks onboarding process.


Juicebox offers published pricing, a free trial, and a first shortlist the same afternoon, plus business plans with dedicated support for larger organizations. For teams that source every day, the payoff shows up in week one, and again every time a new recruiter joins and is productive on day one.


## **The data under the search**


Both platforms claim 800M+ profiles. However, at these sizes, the raw counts stop mattering; what matters is whether the profile is current and reachable.


Juicebox manages best-in-class contact waterfalls across phone and email data with 10+ data partners. Findem claims continuous verification, but reviewers still report friction: contact information that "does not match up," limited contact details on some profiles, and data that can lag profile updates.
