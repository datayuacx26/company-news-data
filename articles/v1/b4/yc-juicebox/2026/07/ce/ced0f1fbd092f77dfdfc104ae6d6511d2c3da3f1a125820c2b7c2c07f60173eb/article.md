## **What does an HR manager do?**


HR managers lead the people function for an organization. Core responsibilities include hiring, policy creation and adherence, employee development, and workforce and strategic planning.


In practice, this means they touch the full life-cycle of the employee experience, from recruitment and onboarding to compensation and retention. HR managers must also be as comfortable dealing with the day-to-day demands of running a people org, like conflict resolution, as they are with HR strategies, like internal mobility planning and outlining hiring projections based on evolving needs.


## **HR manager duties and responsibilities**


Typical day-to-day responsibilities for HR managers include:


- **Hiring:** Writing job descriptions, running phone screens, scheduling interviews, and extending and negotiating offers with candidates.
- **Onboarding:** Supporting employees during the new-hire experience across the first 1–2 weeks, defining 30-60-90-day goals (and checking in throughout that time period), and intaking candidate paperwork and documents before submitting to the HRIS.
- **Employee relations:** Receiving and actioning complaints, running investigations, mediating conflicts between employees, and structuring performance management flows with managers.
- **Compliance:** Keeping personnel files up to date, tracking adherence to policies, and updating them as needed.
- **Workforce planning:** Taking inventory of skills gaps, building plans to address them, and forecasting future headcount in conjunction with company leadership.
- **Recruiting strategy:** Identifying and tracking the success of candidate sourcing channels, prioritizing hiring needs against competing demands, and designing competitive offer packages based on market data.
- **Advising leadership:** Keeping a pulse on factors both internal and external to the organization impacting talent, and keeping leadership apprised.


## **HR manager requirements and qualifications**


HR manager qualifications boil down to two distinct groups: The must-have requirements that constitute the baseline needed for success, and the nice-to-haves that make a candidate particularly well-suited for the job.


Over-index on the nice-to-haves, and you risk needlessly narrowing your applicant pool. But the inverse also applies; overly relaxed standards increase the chance of a poor-fit hire.


Must-have qualifications usually include:


- Several years of progressive HR experience with at least some team leadership
- Working knowledge of HR principles and employment law
- Hands-on familiarity with an HRIS
- Solidly developed interpersonal communication skills
- Conflict resolution experience


The nice-to-haves that many organizations also like to see are:


- A bachelor’s degree in HR, business, psychology, or a related field
- HR certifications from professional and continuous education organizations like SHRM or HRCI
- Change management experience
- Backgrounds that align with a company’s specific industry


If a candidate lacks specific experience as an HR manager, look for titles on their resume that come from the typical feeder roles; examples include HR generalist, HR analyst, or recruiter.

## **Salary and benefits for HR managers**


According to[BLS data](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/management/human-resources-managers.htm#tab-5) , the median salary for HR managers in 2024 was around $140,000. The lowest 10% of earners made less than $84,000, while the top 10% made upwards of $239,000.


The key factors driving variable compensation:


- **Location:** HR managers based in high-cost-of-living metros, like New York City, San Francisco, and Boston, typically earn more than those living in less expensive areas.
- **Company size:** Typically, HR managers working at large enterprises earn more than those working at small-to-medium-sized businesses.
- **Experience:** Like other professions, HR managers in charge of more strategic functions or with larger responsibilities usually earn more than early-career professionals.
- **Industry:** HR managers in certain industries earn more. According to the BLS, professional, scientific, and technical services pay more than industries like healthcare and social assistance or government.


As full-time employees, HR managers can expect to receive a suite of competitive benefits, including health insurance, retirement plan contributions, PTO, and flexible working arrangements at some companies. Others might also receive growth-related perks, like bonuses.

### **What are the duties of an HR manager?**


HR managers run the people and administrative side of organizations. Typical duties include hiring, talent acquisition, complaint investigation, conflict resolution, policy setting, workforce planning, and communicating internally to both leadership and team members.


### **What qualifications does an HR manager need?**


According to the BLS, the typical entry-level education requirement to be an HR manager is a bachelor’s degree in a relevant field. Other common qualifications include several years of work experience in a related field, certifications from professional organizations such as SHRM, and demonstrated knowledge of employment law, HR software, and leadership, as well as strong communication skills.
