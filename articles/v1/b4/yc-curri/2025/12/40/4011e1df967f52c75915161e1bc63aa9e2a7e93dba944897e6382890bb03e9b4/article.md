# Just-in-time delivery for construction: Benefits and risks

Construction has long been an industry of delays, stalls, and slow turnaround times. As project demand rises, however, many construction material distributors are looking to improve their delivery performance. Here’s what just-in-time delivery is, and what the benefits and risks are with getting started.


## What is just-in-time delivery?


Just-in-time (JIT) is an inventory and delivery strategy in which parts, materials, or products are delivered at the moment they’re needed for a construction project. The alternative, storing large quantities of product beforehand, creates more waste and drives up inventory holding costs.


By using a JIT approach, distributors can minimize inventory while maximizing operational efficiency. The JIT strategy requires a routine, reliable[supply chain](https://www.curri.com/article/how-construction-supply-chains-win-with-on-demand-delivery) and meticulous coordination between receivers and suppliers to ensure deliveries are, well, just in time.


### Why it’s a must-have in modern construction


Today, everyone is aiming for greater efficiency and faster project completion to keep up with construction demand. With retail delivery faster than ever, other industries, such as the trades, are expected to keep up to date. To keep up with an increasingly competitive market, savvy distributors must adopt a JIT strategy to outperform the competition.


## Just-in-time delivery: 5 benefits


### 1. Improved cash flow and cost efficiency


When materials are delivered exactly as needed, contractors avoid paying for items too far in advance of installation. This helps preserve a modest cash flow, which is ideal for managing multiple jobs or a multi-phase megaproject. JIT also reduces hidden fees and costs, including storage fees, damaged goods, double-handling, and missing materials.


By ordering right when the materials are needed, teams avoid over-ordering, and distributors avoid overproduction.


### 2. Reduced on-site material storage


JIT delivery reduces the amount of excess material on site, immediately reducing worksite congestion and clutter. Less material on hand also means a lower risk of theft, damage, weather exposure, and worker injuries from a lack of pathways and workspace.


This improves site coordination and allows subcontractors to work with fewer delays. The site can be organized for productivity rather than storage, with a focus on smoother operations.


### 3. Greater adaptability to design changes and real-time needs


Construction projects often face schedule changes, material substitutions, and ad hoc adjustments. JIT delivery gives teams the flexibility to order the right materials without being stuck with slow turnaround times.


This is particularly important for fast-paced projects with evolving designs. By reducing delivery operations to a JIT model, contractors can respond more confidently to owner requests and revisions.


### 4. More accurate scheduling and project coordination


JIT better supports tight construction schedules, because materials arrive in tandem with the build. This helps coordinators avoid bottlenecks caused by missing materials arriving too early. It also improves collaboration between crews, dispatchers, and distribution. When everyone is working from the same, aligned schedule, it becomes easier for all to anticipate and adjust to unexpected changes.


### 5. Increased safety on the jobsite


On any construction site, safety is everything. Fewer materials on site mean fewer tripping hazards, less clutter, and safer movement around equipment on pathways. The JIT strategy also prevents unsafe stockpiles that can spill, topple, and obstruct emergency equipment and access points.


Better timing also reduces the chance of workers cutting corners or rushing to make extra space for unexpected deliveries. With a cleaner, more controlled environment, workers using the JIT strategy meet compliance requirements and regulations with ease.


## Risks to consider


The main risk to consider with a JIT strategy is supply chain vulnerability, from a lack of buffer stock, over-reliance on suppliers and distributors, and limited flexibility when distributors fail to produce on demand. Any delay from suppliers, severe weather, road closures, or other factors can halt projects and leave little room for scheduling errors.
