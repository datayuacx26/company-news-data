These days, the construction industry is kicking off and finishing projects at breakneck speed. ## What is fleet elasticity?


Fleet elasticity means rapidly adjusting the size and composition of your fleet to reflect the changing delivery demand. It’s all about being agile and adapting to your business needs on a day-by-day basis.


To achieve fleet elasticity, companies must either work with multiple delivery couriers or find a flexible partner that can adjust to demand. Businesses in cities of all sizes, from[Denver](https://www.curri.com/location/denver-colorado) to[Houston](https://www.curri.com/location/houston-texas) to[Chicago](https://www.curri.com/location/chicago-illinois) , run into barriers searching for the right provider that supports an[elastic strategy](https://www.curri.com/article/what-is-elastic-logistics-how-does-it-support-the-trades) . Many companies lean into conventional couriers. Here’s why that approach presents problems.


### The challenge with conventional construction fleets


Many businesses stick with traditional delivery methods, as that’s “the way it is”. Although they may be used to it, some pitfalls come with conventional delivery, including:


- **No visibility:** Distributors and construction companies alike are in the dark about when their materials are expected to arrive, and if there are any delays on the road, leading to wasted time and project delays on-site.
- **No delivery confirmation:** If an order is mis-delivered, again, neither party is notified. This disrupts work on site and causes additional stress for project managers as they locate the missing shipment.
- **Slow turnaround times:** “ASAP” for many conventional couriers means 2-5 business days. If a business needs an emergency shipment, same-day delivery simply isn’t an option.
- **Limited delivery radius:** If your delivery crosses city or state lines, you may be out of luck in completing your delivery.
- **Lack of hotshot support:** Simple point A to point B deliveries (aka[hotshots](https://www.curri.com/article/hotshot-delivery-cost-factors-what-impacts-the-final-price) ) are a pipe dream with conventional delivery. Everything is part of a larger, sluggish route.


On top of that, conventional delivery methods are often costly. If you need a specialty vehicle, rush shipment, or long-distance delivery, you can be certain that your total delivery cost will skyrocket. But by choosing to partner with a company that owns flexibility as part of its business model, you can expect fair prices and elastic fleets that work for you. Enter: Curri.
