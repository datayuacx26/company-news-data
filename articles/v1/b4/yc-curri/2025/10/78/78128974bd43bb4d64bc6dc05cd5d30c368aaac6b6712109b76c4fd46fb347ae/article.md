## Where most delivery strategies fall short


Distributing your snacks across the country should be as simple as making a couple of calls and scheduling the right pickups. In theory. The reality, however, is that businesses[face countless challenges](https://www.curri.com/article/navigating-weather-terrain-and-traffic-challenges-in-regional-construction-material-delivery) when it comes to ironing out delivery logistics. They might include:


- Parcel carriers jacking up the price for long-distance deliveries
- Turnaround times not quick enough for the product's shelf life
- Large truckloads of heavy product fees eating into your profit margins
- A lack of visibility with[route status](https://www.curri.com/route-planner) and delivery confirmation


From the consumer perspective, fast, cheap shipping from companies like Amazon and Temu sets unrealistic expectations. This creates a margin versus customer satisfaction dilemma. Rising to the occasion of instant delivery is difficult, but it shouldn’t feel impossible.


Additionally, for new brands especially, anticipating where you will need to house the most product is tricky. A faraway warehouse with low inventory might see a surge in demand next week thanks to a viral affiliate video or effective regional marketing campaign. These are the moments where you need to act quickly, without blowing your budget.
