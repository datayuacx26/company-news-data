# A complete guide to Full Truckload shipping (FTL) | Curri

When construction projects demand the movement of large-scale materials and equipment, full truckload shipping emerges as the most efficient solution. Unlike other delivery methods that involve multiple stops and shared cargo space, FTL shipping dedicates an entire truck to your shipment, ensuring faster transit times, reduced handling, and enhanced security for valuable construction materials.


For construction and industrial supply chains, where timing and material integrity are paramount, FTL shipping provides the reliability and efficiency needed to keep projects on schedule. Whether you're transporting heavy machinery, bulk lumber, or specialized equipment, understanding how FTL works and when to use it can significantly impact your project's success and bottom line.


## What is Full Truckload (FTL) shipping?


Full truckload shipping refers to a logistics service where an entire truck is dedicated to a single shipment, regardless of whether the cargo physically fills the entire trailer. This method contrasts sharply with[less-than-truckload (LTL) shipping](https://www.curri.com/ltl) , where multiple shippers share space on the same truck, requiring additional stops, transfers, and handling along the route.


FTL shipping is particularly advantageous for companies with high-volume shipments or those requiring expedited delivery to a single destination. The dedicated nature of FTL means your materials travel directly from origin to destination without the delays and potential damage risks associated with multiple loading and unloading cycles. This direct-route approach makes FTL the preferred choice for construction companies managing time-sensitive projects or transporting fragile, high-value materials that require minimal handling.


## Benefits of using FTL for construction logistics


Construction logistics present unique challenges that make FTL shipping particularly valuable. Large-scale materials like steel beams, lumber bundles, heavy machinery, and specialized equipment require careful handling and often exceed the size limitations of standard LTL services. FTL shipping provides the space and weight capacity necessary for these oversized loads while maintaining the structural integrity of materials during transport.


The speed advantage of FTL cannot be overstated in construction contexts. With direct routing and no intermediate stops, FTL deliveries significantly reduce transit times compared to LTL alternatives. This efficiency is crucial when project timelines are tight or when materials are needed to prevent costly work stoppages. Additionally, the reduced handling inherent in FTL shipping minimizes the risk of damage to expensive construction materials and equipment, protecting your investment and avoiding project delays caused by damaged goods.

## Comparing FTL to LTL and Parcel delivery


Choosing between FTL, LTL, and parcel delivery depends on several key factors including shipment size, urgency, destination requirements, and cost considerations. FTL becomes the optimal choice when shipments exceed 10,000 pounds, require expedited delivery, or consist of items too large or valuable to share truck space with other shippers' goods. Construction materials often fall into these categories, making FTL the natural choice for many building supply deliveries.


LTL shipping may seem cost-effective for smaller shipments, but the hidden costs of delays, additional handling, and potential damage often offset initial savings. Parcel delivery, while suitable for small tools and supplies, lacks the capacity and specialized handling capabilities required for construction materials. FTL eliminates these concerns by providing dedicated space, direct routing, and specialized equipment matching, ensuring your materials receive the attention and care they deserve throughout the shipping process.
