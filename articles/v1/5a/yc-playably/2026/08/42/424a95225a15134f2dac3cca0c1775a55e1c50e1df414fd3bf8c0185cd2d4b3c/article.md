# Zero party data collection for Shopify brands

Every Shopify brand is sitting on a pile of data it does not really own. Pixel data, cookie data, lookalikes built on signals that get thinner every year. Then a browser update lands and half of your targeting quietly stops working.


Zero party data is the answer merchants keep hearing about and rarely operationalize. Here is what it actually is, why the standard email popup collects almost none of it, and four ways to gather it that shoppers genuinely enjoy.


## What zero party data actually is


Zero party data is information a customer deliberately and proactively gives you. Not observed. Not inferred. Not bought. Given.


The distinction matters because the other categories are all degrading at once. Third party data (bought or brokered) is the most exposed. First party data (what you observe on your own site) is safer, but it is still inference: someone viewed three jackets, so you guess they want a jacket. Maybe they were shopping for a gift. Maybe they hated all three.


Zero party data is the shopper saying it out loud. My size is medium. I buy for two kids under five. I care about the fit, not the price. Nothing to model, nothing to guess.


## Why it matters more in 2026 than it did in 2023


Three things happened at the same time. Signal loss made paid acquisition more expensive and less precise. Inbox competition made generic email worth less per send. And shoppers got sharply better at recognizing a bribe for their address.


The result is that the average Shopify brand now has a very large list and a very thin profile behind each address. You know an email. You do not know why that person is on the list, what they want next, or what would move them.


Zero party data closes that gap, and it is the one input that competitors cannot copy, buy, or scrape.


## Why the 10 percent off popup collects almost nothing


The standard capture flow asks for an email and gives away margin in exchange. It has three problems.


It collects one field, and that field is not a preference. It attracts the most price sensitive segment on the site, because a discount hook selects for discount buyers. And it trains the shopper to expect a code before every purchase, which means the next full price visit feels like a mistake.


So you pay margin, you get an address, and you learn nothing. We wrote more about the mechanics of fixing that in[gamified email capture for Shopify brands](https://playably.ai/blogs/about-gamification/gamified-email-capture) . The short version: the exchange has to be worth something other than money.


## Four ways to collect zero party data shoppers will actually give you


### 1. A quiz that recommends something real


A quiz works when the shopper gets a genuinely better answer at the end. Not a coupon. A recommendation they would have struggled to reach on their own.


The Sak ran a product finder quiz and saw 5x the conversion rate of the site average, because every question was doing double duty: it narrowed the recommendation for the shopper and captured a stated preference for the brand. Style, use case, price comfort, all volunteered.


Keep it to five or six questions. Every question past that costs you completions.


### 2. A game that trades a play for a preference


Games are the cheapest way to make a data ask feel like a reward instead of a toll. The shopper plays, the shopper answers one or two questions inside the flow, and the brand gets a profile field plus a memory of the brand that a popup never creates.


The mechanics that work here (instant win, streaks, milestone unlocks, seasonal formats) are covered across our[games library](https://playably.ai/pages/games) . The rule is simple: the game has to be fun on its own terms. If the game is only a wrapper for a form, shoppers feel it immediately.


### 3. A gamified rebate that captures intent at the moment of purchase


This is the underrated one. Most data collection happens before the purchase, when the shopper is least invested. A[gamified rebate](https://playably.ai/pages/rebates) flips it: the customer buys at full price, then enrolls in a promotion tied to a real world event, and the enrollment step is where they tell you things.


Because the reward is real money back if the event hits, enrollment rates are high and the answers are honest. You learn who they are rooting for, what they are shopping for next, which product they would buy again. That is intent data attached to a confirmed order, which is the most valuable version of it.


The commercial side holds up too. Buckle Me Baby Coats saw a 23 percent sales lift on launch. BlackBoxMyCar ran a rebate campaign to 18.25x Meta ROAS and a 57 percent lift in store sales. Full price, full margin, and Playably carries the risk on the payout.


### 4. A winback ask that leads with a question, not a code


Dormant segments are where brands reflexively reach for the deepest discount, and it is the worst place to spend it. A lapsed customer who returns for 30 percent off has just told you their reactivation price forever.


Asking instead works better. Jones New York reactivated a dormant list with a gamified send and generated 5x the revenue per send compared to their standard campaign, without a sitewide code. The ask itself (what were you looking for, what did we get wrong, what would bring you back) is both the data and the reason to reengage.


## What to collect, and what to skip


Collect what changes what you would send or show. Category preference, size or fit, purchase occasion (self or gift), replenishment timing, budget comfort, the team or event they care about.


Skip anything you cannot act on within 30 days. Birthday fields, favorite color, open text boxes nobody reads. Every field you request costs completion rate, so each one has to earn a place in a flow, a segment, or a merchandising decision. And tell people what you will do with it in the same sentence you ask. Response rates go up, not down, when the payoff is explicit.


## How to put it to work once you have it


Route stated preferences into segments the day they land. A shopper who says gift buyer should not get the same November send as a shopper who says self purchase. A shopper who names a size should never see an out of stock hero in that size.


Then feed it back into acquisition. Stated preferences make far better seed audiences than pixel derived lookalikes, and they are portable across ad platforms in a way that inferred segments are not.


## How to measure whether it is working


Three numbers tell you most of it. Profile completeness (what share of your list has at least two stated fields). Revenue per send on segmented flows versus your batch sends. And full price conversion rate on the segments you built from stated data, which is the number that proves you are not simply discounting under a new name.

## FAQ


### Is zero party data the same as first party data?


No. First party data is what you observe on your own properties, like pages viewed or products added to cart. Zero party data is what the customer intentionally tells you, such as size, budget, or purchase occasion. First party data is inference. Zero party data is a statement.


### Will shoppers really answer questions without a discount?


Yes, when the exchange is worth something. A better recommendation, a game worth playing, or a chance at real money back all outperform a code, and none of them train the shopper to wait for the next sale.


### How many questions can I ask before people drop off?


Five or six inside a quiz, one or two inside a game or a rebate enrollment. The ceiling is not a fixed number, it is the point where the shopper stops seeing a payoff. Ask for one more field only when you can name the flow it will trigger.


## The takeaway


Zero party data is not a compliance project. It is the difference between a list of addresses and a list of people you can actually sell to at full price. The brands winning right now are not the ones with the biggest lists. They are the ones who asked better questions and made answering worth the shopper's time.
