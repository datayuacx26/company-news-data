# What the 2026 Exposure Gap Report Reveals About Remediation

Some security teams are reducing critical exposure within hours, while others are leaving similar issues open for days. The 2026 Exposure Gap Report shows that many organizations can identify, validate, and


[prioritize](https://www.checkpoint.com/exposure-management/exposure-prioritization/?_gl=1*1w8demz*_gcl_au*NzM1OTgyNzUuMTc3NjkzMjE0Ny4yMDM4NjE5ODcuMTc4MjExMDM1Mi4xNzgyMTExMTUz) exposure, but the real challenge begins when teams need to turn those insights into


[remediation](https://www.checkpoint.com/exposure-management/safe-remediation/?_gl=1*1q3tfpm*_gcl_au*NzM1OTgyNzUuMTc3NjkzMjE0Ny4yMDM4NjE5ODcuMTc4MjExMDM1Mi4xNzgyMTExMTUz) .


Across environments, organizations are often working with similar types of exposure, yet their outcomes vary significantly. The difference depends on how quickly validated findings move into remediation and how consistently teams can repeat that process at scale.


##### **Remediation Speed Varies Significantly**


According to the report,


[Utilities organizations resolve exposure in about 12.6 hours on average, while Healthcare environments are closer to 158 hours](https://intelligence.checkpoint.com/exposure-management-gap-report/) . These timelines show how long similar exposure can remain open depending on the way security teams structure and execute remediation.


Faster remediation reduces the window in which exposures can be exploited. Longer remediation timelines leave validated exposure open for more time, even after teams understand the issue and know it needs attention.


##### **The Delay Starts Before Remediation**


Remediation delays often begin before a fix is assigned. Teams first need to validate findings, confirm ownership, and decide which exposures should move forward. When that process is clear, exposure can move into remediation with fewer delays. When it is less structured, decisions take longer, backlogs grow, and response slows across the workflow.


This is where validation becomes essential.[Agentic Exposure Validation (AEV)](https://www.checkpoint.com/solutions/agentic-exposure-validation/) supports that process by narrowing the field before remediation begins.


##### **Execution Drives Outcomes**


The data shows that strong remediation performance is achievable at scale


[. Financial Services organizations implemented 91.7% of recommended fixes](https://intelligence.checkpoint.com/exposure-management-gap-report/) , which illustrates that high exposure volume does not have to prevent consistent execution.


Performance depends on how remediation workflows are structured. Teams with connected validation, prioritization, and remediation processes can maintain speed as volume grows. Security teams with fragmented workflows often struggle to maintain the same pace as the volume of findings grows.


##### **Remediation Reflects How Teams Operate**


Remediation depends on more than knowing which exposures need attention. Teams also need a clear process for assigning work, tracking progress, and confirming when action is complete. When that process is well structured, exposure moves through the workflow more quickly. When it is not, delays build and unresolved exposures remain open longer.


This is where[exposure management](https://www.checkpoint.com/exposure-management/) becomes critical, since it connects validation and remediation so teams can act on exploitable exposure without unnecessary delay.


##### **Closing the Remediation Gap**


Remediation performance reflects how efficiently exposure moves through the security workflow. Organizations working with similar exposure can achieve very different outcomes depending on how well validation, prioritization, and remediation are connected.


When the process is structured, exposure is resolved more consistently and at a faster pace. When the process is fragmented, exposure remains open longer as delays build across the workflow.


Vulnerabilities continue to increase, and actionable exposure remains clearly defined. Progress depends on recognizing that boundary and moving from signal to action with greater consistency.
