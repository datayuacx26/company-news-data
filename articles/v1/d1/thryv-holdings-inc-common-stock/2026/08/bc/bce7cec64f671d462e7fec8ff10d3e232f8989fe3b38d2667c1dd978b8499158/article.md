## Why Is Social Media Important for Small Businesses?


Social media can support business growth in several ways. The most effective strategies often combine relationship-building content with campaigns designed to reach new audiences.


### Build Brand Awareness


Customers are more likely to remember a business they see regularly. Sharing[consistent content](https://www.thryv.com/blog/brand-consistency-adjust-messaging-without-losing-trust/) can help your business become more familiar to people in your community.


Your social content should reflect your brand and show customers what makes your business different. This could include your expertise, customer experience, team, values, or the quality of your work.


Over time, that familiarity can help build recognition and trust.


### Connect With Current Customers


Social media gives businesses an opportunity to maintain relationships after a customer has made a purchase or completed a service.


You can use your social channels to answer questions, respond to comments, share updates, and provide useful information. These ongoing interactions can help customers stay connected to your business and encourage repeat business.


### Demonstrate Your Expertise


Helpful content can show customers that your business understands their needs.


For example, a home services business might share seasonal maintenance tips. A salon could provide advice about caring for a new hairstyle. A legal practice might answer common questions about its services.


Educational content does more than fill your social calendar. It can help establish credibility and give potential customers a reason to learn more about your business.


### Reach New Customers


Organic posts primarily help you stay connected with your existing audience, but paid social media advertising can expand your reach.


Social ads allow businesses to promote content to specific audiences based on factors such as location, interests, and other targeting options available through the platform.


This can help your business reach people who may be interested in your services but have not yet followed your page or discovered your business.


## Organic Social Media vs. Paid Social Ads


Organic social media and paid social advertising serve different purposes. A strong strategy may include both.


### Organic Social Media


Organic social media includes the content you publish directly to your business profiles without paying to distribute it.


Organic content can help you:


- Maintain relationships with followers
- Share your business personality
- Provide useful information
- Highlight customer experiences
- Build credibility over time
- Keep your business active and visible


Organic social media is valuable for long-term relationship-building, but the reach of individual posts can vary.


### Paid Social Media Ads


[Paid social ads](https://www.thryv.com/blog/what-to-expect-from-social-ads-first-90-days/) are promoted campaigns designed to reach a broader or more targeted audience.


Social ads can help businesses:


- Increase awareness in a local market
- Promote a specific service or offer
- Reach new potential customers
- Drive traffic to a website or landing page
- Generate calls, messages, or other customer actions


Paid advertising can provide more control over who sees your content and how much you spend. However, successful campaigns still require strong creative, clear messaging, thoughtful targeting, and ongoing performance monitoring.


The most effective approach depends on your goals. If you want to strengthen customer relationships and maintain visibility, consistent organic content may be the priority. If you want to reach new audiences or promote a time-sensitive offer, paid advertising may be a better fit.

## How to Create a Social Media Strategy for Your Small Business


You do not need to be active on every platform or publish new content every day. Start with a[focused strategy that supports your business goals](https://www.thryv.com/blog/social-media-tactics-for-ai-search/) .


### Define Your Goal


Decide what you want social media to help your business accomplish.


Your goal may be to:


- Build local awareness
- Attract new customers
- Increase website traffic
- Promote a new service
- Generate more calls or inquiries
- Encourage repeat business
- Stay connected with existing customers


A clear goal can help you determine what content to create and how to measure success.


### Focus on the Platforms Your Customers Use


You do not need to maintain a profile on every social media platform.


Consider where your target customers spend their time and which platforms fit your business. Visual businesses may benefit from image- and video-focused platforms, while professional service providers may prioritize platforms that support educational or industry-related content.


It is usually better to maintain a consistent presence on a few relevant channels than to spread your efforts too thin.


### Create Content That Is Useful and Relevant


Your content should provide value to your audience rather than focus only on selling.


Consider creating a mix of:


- Helpful tips
- Frequently asked questions
- Customer reviews
- Before-and-after photos
- Team introductions
- Seasonal reminders
- Business updates
- Promotions and special offers
- Educational videos


This variety can keep your content useful while giving customers multiple ways to connect with your business.


### Use Clear Calls to Action


Tell customers what to do next.


Depending on your goal, your call to action may encourage people to:


- Visit your website
- Request an estimate
- Book an appointment
- Call your business
- Send a message
- Learn more about a service


A clear next step can help turn attention and engagement into meaningful business activity.


### 5. Review Your Results


Social media performance is about more than likes and follower counts.


Review metrics that align with your goals, such as:


- Website visits
- Calls and messages
- Clicks
- Leads or inquiries
- Engagement
- Reach and impressions
- Customer actions


Use those insights to understand which topics and campaigns connect with your audience.
