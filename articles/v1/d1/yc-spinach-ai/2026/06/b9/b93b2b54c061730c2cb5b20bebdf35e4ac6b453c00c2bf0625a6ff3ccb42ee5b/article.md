## What Is Gong and Why Companies Seek Alternatives


Gong built its reputation on revenue intelligence: recording sales calls, analyzing rep performance, and surfacing deal risk signals for sales managers. It does those things well for sales teams. The problem is that most companies buying Gong have more than a sales team.


Engineering standups, product reviews, customer success calls, and executive syncs all generate decisions and action items that disappear the moment the call ends. Gong was not built to capture any of that. Its analytics are sales-pipeline-centric, its integrations route to CRM, and its pricing reflects an enterprise sales tool budget, not a company-wide meeting intelligence one.


That scope mismatch is why teams go looking for alternatives.


### Who Actually Needs Something Different


The search for Gong alternatives tends to come from a few distinct situations:


- Teams outside of sales who need meeting intelligence but have no use for pipeline forecasting or rep coaching scores, and do not want to pay for features built for a completely different workflow.
- Companies that want[action items, decisions, and tickets](https://www.spinach.ai/blog/ai-meeting-notes) to surface automatically from any meeting, beyond sales calls routed through a CRM.
- Smaller or faster-moving organizations where Gong’s enterprise contract structure and seat minimums do not fit the budget or the buying process.


The right alternative depends on which of those gaps you are actually trying to close.

## Fireflies.ai: Accessible Transcription with Team Collaboration Features


Fireflies.ai targets teams that want meeting transcription without a steep learning curve. Its free tier covers unlimited transcription (capped at 800 minutes of storage per seat), and paid plans start at around $10 per seat per month, making it one of the more accessible options in this category.


The tool records meetings across Zoom, Google Meet, and Teams, then generates transcripts, searchable notes, and a meeting summary. Teams can leave comments on transcript snippets, create soundbites, and share notes across channels. For cross-functional teams that want a shared record without building new habits, that collaboration layer is genuinely useful.


Where Fireflies stops is at the transcript. There is no ticket creation, no action item routing to Jira or Linear, and no native Scrum ceremony support. If your team’s next step after a standup or sprint review is filing work, someone is still doing that by hand.


Fireflies works well for teams that need a lightweight, searchable record of conversations across departments. It falls short for engineering and product teams where the output of a meeting should be a set of assigned tickets, not a document to skim later. Teams in this position should consider[Fireflies alternatives](https://www.spinach.ai/blog/fireflies-alternatives) built for agile workflows.


## Otter.ai: General-Purpose Transcription for Individuals and Small Teams


Otter.ai focuses on transcription and note-taking for individuals and small teams. It captures spoken audio and produces searchable transcripts, making it a go-to for journalists, students, and solo professionals who need a record of what was said.


Where it stops short is familiar: Otter gives you a transcript, not a decision log or a set of assigned tickets. For engineering teams running standups or sprint reviews, that gap matters. Someone still has to read the transcript, pull out the action items, and file them somewhere useful.


Otter’s free tier covers basic transcription with limited monthly minutes. Paid plans unlock longer recordings, more integrations, and team features, though the output stays documentation-focused throughout. Before committing, review[Otter AI pricing](https://www.spinach.ai/blog/otter-ai-pricing) to understand the full cost structure.


## Read.ai and Granola: Next-Generation Meeting Intelligence Options


Read.ai layers real-time engagement analytics on top of transcription, tracking participation patterns and meeting dynamics alongside notes and summaries. Granola takes a different approach entirely, processing audio locally instead of routing it through cloud servers, so no bot joins the call and recordings stay on the device.


Both options appeal to teams uncomfortable with visible AI participants or wary of sending audio to third-party servers. The tradeoff is integration depth. Neither tool carries the CRM connections of Avoma or the native Jira and Linear workflows of Spinach, which makes them harder fits for teams whose post-meeting work lives in a project management tool. Smaller user bases also mean thinner ecosystems for support resources and third-party add-ons.


## Key Selection Criteria: Matching Tools to Your Team’s Workflow


Choosing the right meeting intelligence tool depends on your team’s actual use case, beyond feature checklists. A few factors worth weighing before you decide:


- How far beyond the transcript do you need the tool to go? Some teams need verbatim records; others need decisions and tickets filed before the call ends.
- Which integrations are non-negotiable? If your team lives in Jira or Linear, a tool that stops at Slack exports adds manual work back into your day.
- Who needs meeting intelligence, and in what roles? Sales, engineering, recruiting, and leadership each pull different value from the same call.
- What does your compliance posture require? SOC 2, HIPAA, and data residency requirements narrow the field quickly.
- What’s the real cost at your team size? Per-seat pricing compounds fast, and free tiers rarely cover full teams.
