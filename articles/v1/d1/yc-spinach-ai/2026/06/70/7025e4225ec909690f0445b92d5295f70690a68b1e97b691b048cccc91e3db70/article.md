## What Is a Meeting Recorder


A meeting recorder captures spoken conversation during a call or in-person session and converts it into a searchable record. That record might be a raw audio file, a timestamped transcript, an AI-generated summary, or a set of action items depending on the tool.


The category spans hardware devices you carry into a conference room, software apps on your phone or laptop, and cloud-connected bots that join your Zoom or Google Meet automatically. What they share is the starting point: audio in, structured output out.


Where tools diverge is what happens after[the transcript and meeting notes](https://www.spinach.ai/blog/ai-meeting-notes) .


## Meeting Recorder Apps vs Meeting Recorder Devices


Apps and devices solve different problems, and mixing them up leads to buying the wrong thing.


Meeting recorder apps run on your phone, laptop, or browser. They capture audio through your device’s microphone or integrate directly with Zoom, Google Meet, or Teams to record calls at the source. Most include[AI transcription](https://www.spinach.ai/blog/ai-transcription-tools) , speaker identification, and some form of summary output.


Meeting recorder devices are standalone hardware, like the Plaud AI recorder, designed for in-person conversations where pulling out a laptop feels awkward. They excel in boardrooms, one-on-ones, or field interviews where no screen is welcome.


### Which format fits which situation


- Apps are the right call for remote and hybrid teams. They connect to your meeting stack, sync automatically, and can push summaries to Slack or Jira without manual steps.
- Devices make sense for in-person settings where audio quality from a phone mic would suffer, or where recording discretely matters.
- Some teams run both: a device for the weekly leadership offsite, an app for every other call.


The distinction matters because the keyword “meeting recorder” covers both categories, and the best choice depends entirely on where your meetings happen. If most of yours are virtual, a dedicated device collects dust.


## AI Meeting Transcription Accuracy in 2026


Word Error Rate (WER) is the standard benchmark: one error per ten words at 10% WER. That sounds tolerable until you factor in a 45-minute sprint planning session where corrupted action items end up filed in Jira.


According to January 2026 testing published by summarizemeeting.com, Fireflies.ai led the field at 91.3% accuracy, followed by Otter.ai at 89.7% and Sembly at 87.2%. Independent[AI notetaker accuracy comparisons](https://www.assemblyai.com/blog/top-ai-notetakers) confirm these benchmarks across real-world meeting scenarios.


Those figures reflect controlled audio conditions, though choosing[the best AI note taker](https://www.spinach.ai/blog/best-ai-note-taker) depends on more than accuracy alone. Noisy conference rooms, heavy accents, dense technical jargon, and overlapping speakers all drag real-world accuracy down. A tool scoring 91% on clean audio can drop several points during a five-person architecture review where people are finishing each other’s sentences. For teams whose meetings include product names, system identifiers, or acronyms, that gap matters more than the headline number suggests.

## Meeting Recorders for Specific Platforms


Each major video conferencing tool has its own recording ecosystem worth knowing before you pick a solution.


### Zoom


Zoom’s built-in recorder saves locally or to the cloud, depending on your plan. Cloud recording requires a paid Zoom subscription, and storage limits apply. Third-party apps like Otter connect via Zoom’s app marketplace for automatic transcription on top of native recording.


### Google Meet


Free Google accounts get limited recording access. Workspace plans unlock full recording to Google Drive. Apps like Spinach connect directly to Meet to capture decisions and action items beyond the audio file.


### Microsoft Teams


Teams records to SharePoint or OneDrive on eligible plans. Transcription runs through Microsoft’s own engine, though accuracy drops on technical vocabulary. Third-party integrations fill that gap for teams needing cleaner output or downstream ticket creation.
