# Should I Become an Auto Mechanic in Tennessee?

If you love cars, enjoy working with your hands and want to work in[one of the nation’s leading automotive manufacturing hubs](https://www.thinktennessee.org/blog/the-road-ahead-steering-tennessees-auto-manufacturing-growth-and-workforce-challenges/) , you may want to consider an automotive mechanic career in the great state of Tennessee. The Volunteer State boasts a growing automotive industry, steady demand for skilled workers, and accessible training options to jumpstart your career in the automotive industry1.


## Skills You Need to be an Auto Tech in Tennessee


Today's automotive technicians do much more than perform basic maintenance and repairs. Modern vehicles rely on advanced electronics, computerized systems, and diagnostic technology, which means you need to develop a broad set of technical and problem-solving skills.


Automotive employers look for technicians with practical technical skills such as:


- Engine performance and diagnostics
- Electrical and electronic systems
- Brake, steering, and suspension repair
- Transmission and drivetrain service
- Computerized vehicle diagnostics
- Heating and air conditioning systems
- Hybrid and electric vehicle technology


Developing these skills in a training program can help prepare you for entry-level opportunities and create a pathway for future specialization and career growth.


## Tennessee Has a Strong Demand for Auto Mechanics


The need for skilled automotive technicians remains steady across the country,1 but the outlook is even brighter in Tennessee. Compared to the rest of the nation, Tennessee’s demand for skilled automotive mechanics is projected to grow 5 times faster through 20342. With the number of vehicles on the road expected to rise, Tennessee drivers need reliable mechanics to keep their cars road ready.


## Tennessee is One of the Largest Auto Manufacturers in the Nation


Tennessee plays a major role in the U.S. automotive industry. The state is home to several large manufacturing plants, including the[Nissan North America Regional HQ](https://www.nissan-global.com/EN/COMPANY/PROFILE/EN_ESTABLISHMENT/NORTH_AMERICA/) and[Mitsubishi Motors North America](https://www.mitsubishicars.com/) , both in Franklin, TN. This strong manufacturing presence helps create a ripple effect throughout the state’s economy3. And with more than 3,000 dealerships in the state, like[Audi of Nashville](https://www.audinashville.com/en/) ,[Beaty Chevrolet](https://www.beatychevrolet.com/) ,[Lexus of Nashville](https://www.lexusofnashville.com/) , and[Wyatt Johnson Ford](https://www.wyattjohnsonford.com/) , there are many existing opportunities for you to break into the industry4.


You may also find work in repair shops and fleet service operations, which rely on trained automotive techs to maintain and service vehicles. If you’re an aspiring mechanic, this means more potential job opportunities and a stable industry where you can build your career.


## Automotive Techs Can Specialize and Grow in Tennessee


One of the advantages of an auto mechanic career is the ability to specialize as you progress. As an entry-level tech, you may begin with general maintenance tasks like oil changes, brake service, and inspections. Over time, you can build expertise in specific areas, such as:


- Engine performance and diagnostics
- Electrical and electronic systems
- Transmission repair
- Heating and air conditioning systems
- Hybrid and electric vehicle technology


With[Tennessee’s continued efforts toward cleaner energy in transportation](https://www.tn.gov/environment/program-areas/energy/state-energy-office--seo-/programs-projects/programs-and-projects/sustainable-transportation-and-alternative-fuels.html) , technicians with specialized knowledge in areas like EVs are increasingly valuable. Modern cars also rely heavily on computerized systems, sensors, and diagnostic tools, meaning you need both mechanical and technical skills.


With experience, you may be able to move into more established roles such as shop foreman, service manager, or even open your own repair business someday.


## Does Tennessee Require Mechanics to Have Degrees?


Unlike many careers, auto technology doesn’t require a degree to get started. Instead, you can prepare for your career as an automotive technician at a trade school or technical training program.


[Automotive training schools](https://www.lincolntech.edu/news/automotive/automotive-technology/what-to-look-for-auto-mechanic-school) provide programs that focus on hands-on learning and practical skills in areas like:


- Engine repair
- Brakes, steering systems and suspensions
- Performance tuning
- A/C and Electrical systems
- Computerized diagnostics
- Transmissions


These programs are designed to prepare you for entry-level roles in less time than a traditional college path—often in a year or two. You gain experience working with the tools, equipment, and real-world scenarios that mirror what you encounter on the job.


Employers often value this kind of structured training because it shows you’ve developed both technical knowledge and safety awareness before you get into the workforce.


## Is an Auto Mechanic Career in Tennessee Right for You?


Choosing a career is a personal decision, but Tennessee offers several advantages for aspiring automotive technicians. A strong manufacturing presence, steady demand for repairs, and a shift to cleaner energy all contribute to a favorable job outlook. Not to mention the benefits of working and living in[a state that offers a growing economy](https://www.usnews.com/news/best-states/tennessee) , diverse cultures, great outdoor spaces, and a world-renowned music industry.


If you enjoy problem-solving, working with technology, and seeing the results of your work firsthand, auto mechanics can offer both stability and growth. With the right training and experience, you can build a career that keeps you engaged and in demand.
