# Can You Start a Healthcare Career Without a Degree?

If you only think of doctors and nurses when you hear “healthcare career,” you’re missing hundreds of other healthcare career paths. And while some of them require four-year degrees, many do not. You can find entry-level healthcare roles with:


- A high school diploma
- Career-focused training
- Certification
- Clinical experience


With nearly 2 million healthcare job openings projected each year through the decade1, there are plenty of healthcare career paths where you can train for the skills employers need without spending four years in college.


## Healthcare Career Training vs Healthcare Degree


Healthcare training programs help you develop the practical, hands-on skills you need for entry-level roles often in less time than degree programs. These programs typically focus on the procedures, equipment, and patient care responsibilities you need for a specific job. Degree programs generally include a broader education that combines healthcare coursework with general education requirements and may be preferred—or required—for some advanced clinical, administrative, leadership, or specialized healthcare careers.


## Healthcare Jobs You Can Start Without a Four-Year Degree


Support roles—those helping doctors, nurses, dentists, and other medical professionals—are critical to the healthcare system. And for most of those positions, you just need a passion to help others, some training and understanding of the field, and a bit of experience. Consider some common options that don’t require a four-year degree:


- Medical assistant
- Dental assistant
- Patient care technician
- Nursing assistant
- Home health aide
- Phlebotomy technician
- EKG technician
- Medical administrative assistant


## What Is Healthcare Career Training?


Many healthcare careers require practical skills that are developed through training and hands-on practice. For example, as a medical assistant, you take vital signs, update patient records, prepare exam rooms, assist with mobility, help collect samples, or support clinical procedures depending on your role. And as a dental assistant, you’re part of a[four-handed dentistry](https://www.lincolntech.edu/news/health-sciences/dental-assistant/what-is-four-handed-dentistry) team working chairside with the dentist. To perform either role effectively, you need to learn the required techniques and practice them in real-world settings.


Training helps you build your skills before you walk into a real workplace. A strong healthcare training program may include:


- Medical terminology
- Anatomy and physiology basics
- Infection control
- Patient communication
- Vital signs
- Electronic health records
- Clinical procedures
- CPR or Basic Life Support preparation
- Externship or lab experience


## Examples of Non-Degree Healthcare Roles: Medical Assistant


Medical assisting is a strong healthcare entry point if you want a mix of patient care and office support. Medical assistants work in physicians’ offices, hospitals, outpatient clinics, and other healthcare settings. Demand for the profession is expected to grow much faster than average throughout the decade2.


## Examples of Non-Degree Healthcare Roles: Dental Assistant


Dental assisting makes good sense if you want healthcare work with a strong patient-facing component. Dental assistants support dentists, prepare patients and work areas, sterilize instruments, process X-rays depending on state rules, and help keep dental offices organized. Demand for dental assistants is expected to grow faster than average throughout the decade3.


[Dental assistant requirements vary by state](https://www.danb.org/state-requirements) , so you should always check your state’s rules before choosing a program or certification path.


## Examples of Non-Degree Healthcare Roles: Home Health Aide


Home health aides provide care for people who need assistance with daily living because of age, illness, or disability. They help clients with bathing, dressing, meal preparation, light housekeeping, and mobility while also monitoring basic health changes and providing companionship. Home health aides often work in patients' homes, helping them maintain independence and quality of life. As the population ages and more people choose to receive care at home, employment of home health aides is projected to grow much faster than average over the coming decade4.


## How to Launch Your Career Beyond College


1. **Choose Your Healthcare Focus:** Think about whether you prefer direct bedside care, dental care, clinical office work, or administrative support. Your answer can point you toward dental assistant, medical assistant, or another healthcare support role.
2. **Check Local Requirements:** Healthcare rules vary by state and role. Nursing assistants often need state-approved training and a competency exam. Dental assistant duties and credential requirements vary by state.
3. **Pick a Healthcare Training Program:** Look for hands-on labs, experienced instructors, certification preparation, externship opportunities, and career support. Schools accredited by the[Accrediting Commission of Career Schools and Colleges](https://www.accsc.org/) must meet established standards for career school quality and student outcomes.
4. **Apply for Financial Aid:** You do not have to attend a four-year college to explore financial aid. Students can use the[Free Application for Federal Student Aid](https://studentaid.gov/h/apply-for-aid/fafsa) to apply for federal grants, loans, and work-study funds at eligible schools.
5. **Prepare for certification:** Certification may not always be required, but it can help show employers that you have job-ready knowledge. The[National Healthcareer Association](https://www.nhanow.com/certification/nha-certifications) offers certifications for roles such as Certified Clinical Medical Assistant, Certified Patient Care Technician, Certified Phlebotomy Technician, Certified EKG Technician, and Certified Medical Administrative Assistant.
