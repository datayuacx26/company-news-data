# The Proposed Lease Sale Offshore Virginia: Jobs, Workforce &#38; Ocean Industries

Responsible offshore mineral exploration relies on a diverse workforce with expertise spanning marine science, engineering, environmental management, logistics, and offshore operations. From scientists conducting environmental surveys to vessel crews, engineers, GIS specialists, hydrographers, and data analysts, a wide range of professionals contribute to improving our understanding of the offshore environment while evaluating potential mineral resources.


Universities and research institutions also play an important role. For the proposed Mid-Atlantic exploration lease, activities such as baseline environmental studies, habitat mapping, geophysical surveys, and long-term environmental monitoring create opportunities for collaboration among industry, academia, and government agencies. The scientific information collected can support ongoing research while helping regulators, resource managers, and coastal planners better understand offshore ecosystems and inform future decision-making.


Many of the skills required already exist within Virginia's established maritime economy, including ports, shipping, offshore construction, dredging, marine engineering, environmental consulting, and ocean technology. As exploration activities progress, these industries may provide specialized services, vessels, equipment, and technical expertise, creating opportunities for local businesses throughout the maritime supply chain. Workforce opportunities could include marine scientists, environmental specialists, hydrographers, subsea engineers, naval architects, vessel crews, shipyard trades, port operators, logistics providers, and other skilled maritime professionals.


Partnerships with universities, community colleges, vocational programs, and workforce development organizations could also help prepare future generations for careers in marine science, engineering, environmental management, and maritime operations. Many of the technical skills developed through offshore exploration—including vessel operations, hydrographic surveying, environmental monitoring, dredging, and offshore engineering—are transferable across other maritime and coastal industries, strengthening Virginia's long-term workforce capacity.


If future scientific, environmental, and regulatory reviews support commercial development, additional employment opportunities could emerge through mineral processing, transportation, equipment maintenance, manufacturing support, environmental services, and other supply chain activities. Because any development would occur in multiple phases over many years, these opportunities would be expected to support sustained economic activity for maritime businesses, ports, suppliers, and coastal communities rather than short-term economic growth. The scale of these opportunities would ultimately depend on future regulatory decisions, project approvals, and market conditions.


Regardless of whether commercial development ultimately occurs, scientific exploration itself provides lasting value. The environmental and geological data collected can expand scientific understanding of the Mid-Atlantic offshore environment, support academic research, inform future ocean planning and resource management, and strengthen collaboration among industry, academia, government agencies, and coastal communities. Through investments in science, partnerships, and workforce development, offshore exploration has the potential to strengthen Virginia's maritime expertise while contributing valuable knowledge that benefits future generations.


**Project Background:** Odyssey has submitted an unsolicited request asking the Bureau of Ocean Energy Management (BOEM) to consider holding a competitive lease sale for offshore critical mineral exploration in a carefully selected area off the Virginia coast.
