# Environmental Stewardship Guides Every Stage of Exploration

Environmental stewardship means building environmental protections into the way exploration is planned, conducted, and reviewed.


For offshore mineral exploration, safeguards begin before vessels enter the water. Project teams review environmental data, identify sensitive areas, assess potential risks, and design measures to reduce disturbance. These measures can include defined operating areas, vessel operating procedures, environmental monitoring, reporting requirements, and activity-specific controls.


Environmental protection continues throughout exploration. Environmental conditions are evaluated before field activities begin, monitored during operations, and reviewed afterward to confirm safeguards are working as intended. When new information becomes available, operators can adapt their approach, using scientific evidence to strengthen environmental protections and improve future operations.


Regulatory oversight is also an important part of responsible exploration. In the United States, offshore marine mineral activities are subject to federal environmental review, scientific studies, and permitting requirements. In the Cook Islands, seabed mineral activities are governed by a dedicated regulatory framework that includes environmental assessment, monitoring, compliance, and enforcement.


Good environmental stewardship means identifying potential impacts early, reducing them where possible, monitoring environmental conditions throughout operations, and being transparent about what is learned.


For Odyssey, responsible exploration means gathering the scientific information needed to support informed decisions while protecting the marine environment throughout every stage of the exploration process.
