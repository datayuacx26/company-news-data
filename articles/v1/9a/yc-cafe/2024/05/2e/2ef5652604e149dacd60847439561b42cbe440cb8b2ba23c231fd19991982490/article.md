# The true cost of Employee Disengagement in a Hybrid Workplace

In the fast-paced business world, where profit margins and quarterly reports dominate conversations, it's easy to lose sight of a company's most valuable asset: its employees.


Any organization will claim that a strong team is its most valuable asset. Employees are the foundation of every business, setting its foundation for success. Investing in their workers becomes critical as CEOs attempt to lead their companies toward success. After all, firms that prioritize employee experience and culture have excellent retention rates and create a more lively work environment, whether hybrid or not.


Corporations tend to see employees as data rather than what they bring to the table, which is a company's vitality. Employees are the foundation of every firm, setting the platform for success via creativity and productivity. As a result, traditional organizations treat employees as just numbers, and employee engagement plummets, with severe consequences for the company’s work culture.


‍


## **The post-pandemic and RTO area**


Take a step back four years and zoom out to a global scale. Understanding employee disengagement often begins with a moment of revelation, a realization that challenges our preconceived notions. The COVID-19 pandemic was a wake-up call, with the start of hybrid work prompting a deeper introspection into workplace satisfaction and productivity dynamics.


"While employee engagement had been on the rise for the past decade, the COVID-19 pandemic disrupted this upward trajectory," observes Dr. Michael Wong, a leading economist specializing in labor markets. "Now, more than ever, companies must prioritize engagement initiatives to foster resilience and adaptability in the face of global challenges."


Contrary to popular belief, employee engagement is not a default condition in which companies do not need to commit their time. While some firms thrive at it, many need to heighten their employee engagement. It's an outlier, not the standard. **The average US workforce includes 16.2% actively disengaged** employees. The economic implications are considerable, with billions of dollars wasted each year due to disengagement difficulties.


‍


## **Effects of Turnover Rates:**


This leads to dissatisfaction, lack of motivation, and a weak company culture, leading to a higher turnover rate. While cutting corners on employee satisfaction may seem to save money, the reality paints a different picture within work culture.


High turnover rates are a common consequence of disengagement, which inflicts costs on organizations regarding recruitment expenses and lost productivity. The Center for American Progress reveals that the average turnover of an employee costs about one-third of the worker's salary. This figure can skyrocket to an alarming **200% for highly skilled, long-term employees.**


> The costs associated with employee turnover extend far beyond the initial hiring process


explains Dr. Sarah Johnson, a leading researcher in organizational psychology.


> From recruitment expenses to lost productivity during the transition period, the financial implications are staggering.


Consider a scenario in the technology sector where the turnover rate is **13%.** For a 1,000-person company, the cost of losing 130 employees annually is astronomical. Factoring in recruitment, training, development, and lost productivity expenses, the total expense balloons a staggering $6.5 million annually. **‍**


‍


## **The Financial Implications**


The economic impact of disengaged employees remains alarmingly consistent, and the financial implications of lost productivity alone are massive. Disengaged employees worldwide cost the economy $8.8 trillion in lost productivity, equivalent to[9% of global GDP](https://www.gallup.com/workplace/393497/world-trillion-workplace-problem.aspx) .


Overall, employee engagement has declined, with the number of actively engaged employees dropping by[two percentage points since 2019](https://www.forbes.com/sites/karlynborysenko/2019/05/02/how-much-are-your-disengaged-employees-costing-you/?sh=44db942f3437) . This downward trajectory is concerning, as it predates the pandemic, especially considering remote work, signaling deeper systemic issues within organizations.


To put these figures into perspective, let's consider a hypothetical scenario. Imagine a 200-person company in the Atlanta metro area with an average salary of $74,000 annually. Assuming that 17% of employees are actively disengaged, each costing[34% of their annual salary](https://threewill.com/total-cost-of-disengaged-employees-to-your-organization/#:~:text=They%20may%20take%20longer%20to,billion%20annually%20in%20lost%20productivity.) in disengagement costs, the yearly financial burden amounts to a staggering $855,440.


However, this figure only scratches the surface of the actual cost of disengagement. Gallup's statistics predominantly focus on actively disengaged employees, neglecting the substantial portion categorized as "not engaged." Even a conservative estimate of the impact of this group underscores the magnitude of the challenge facing organizations.


‍


## **More than a Financial Loss**


" **The negative impact of disengagement goes beyond financial losses," remarks Dr. Emily Che** n, a renowned expert in organizational behavior. "A toxic work environment, characterized by low morale and diminished productivity, can erode company culture and drive talented employees away."


While effective in attracting talent, financial incentives need to foster genuine engagement. While a competitive salary may make employees feel secure, there needs to be employee engagement, a sense of ownership, and passion about the company for sustained productivity. Otherwise, productivity takes a nosedive as disengaged employees contribute less, make more errors, and are prone to absenteeism. Disengaged employees cost[U.S. companies](https://threewill.com/total-cost-of-disengaged-employees-to-your-organization/#:~:text=They%20may%20take%20longer%20to,billion%20annually%20in%20lost%20productivity.) $450 billion to $550 billion annually in lost productivity.


Teams rating high on engagement metrics enjoy many benefits, including heightened customer loyalty, increased profitability, and enhanced productivity. Engaged work cultures outperform their peers and demonstrate resilience in economic downturns, underscoring the strategic significance of fostering a culture of engagement.


Despite these statistics, companies can proactively combat employee disengagement and its associated costs. Investing in employee experience, cultivating a positive work culture, and offering competitive benefits boost engagement and retention rates.


> **Strong leadership, coupled with a supportive work environment, lays the groundwork for employee engagement**


**emphasizes Dr. Rachel Martinez, a seasoned HR consultant. "By prioritizing employee well-being and fostering a sense of belonging,** companies can mitigate the financial losses incurred by disengagement."


‍


Furthermore, transparency and accountability are paramount in engaging employees, with leaders making changes based on input. Regular check-ins and pulse surveys enable organizations to gauge progress and adjust strategies accordingly, fostering a positive work culture of continuous improvement.


Investing in employee engagement is not just a moral imperative; it's a sound business decision with tangible returns on investment. The costs of inaction far outweigh the resources required to cultivate a highly engaged workforce. Organizations can unlock untapped potential by prioritizing engagement, driving innovation, productivity, and sustainable success in today's competitive landscape.


## **Conclusion**


As we continue to see companies transform in every way, leadership must take this opportunity to redefine the future of work. The road ahead may be fraught with challenges, but the rewards of a highly engaged workforce are undeniable. Together, let us embark on this adventure, armed with the knowledge that the value of employee engagement transcends mere economics—it's the cornerstone of organizational vitality and resilience in an ever-changing world.
