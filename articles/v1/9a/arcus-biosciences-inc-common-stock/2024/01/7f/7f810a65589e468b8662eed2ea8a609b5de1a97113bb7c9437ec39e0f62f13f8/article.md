# The Fc-silent Treatment: Pursuing a Known Target in a New Way

# The Fc-silent Treatment: Pursuing a Known Target in a New Way


By


**Juan Jaen, Ph.D.** | President at Arcus Biosciences


Reading Time:


minutes


The biological interactions between cancer and immune cells are complex across tumor types. In some cases, multiple treatments with different functions or targets can be combined to provide options that address this underlying complexity. We are developing novel immunotherapy[treatment combinations](https://arcusbio.com/editorials/investigating-combination-therapies-to-treat-cancer/) that harness the body’s natural immune defenses to kill cancer cells.


TIGIT (T cell immunoreceptor with immunoglobulin and ITIM domains) has emerged as a promising new immune checkpoint to inhibit because it is expressed on immune cells in several types of cancer.1 TIGIT can deactivate immune cells and prevent them from recognizing and killing cancer cells.2


Antibodies used to target TIGIT are[designed in different ways](https://arcusbio.com/editorials/taking-the-brakes-off-the-immune-system-exploring-immunotherapy-combinations/) , with their Fc region (the part of the antibody that faces away from TIGIT) either “enabled” (able to interact with various Fc receptors) or “silent” (mutated to prevent interaction with the Fc receptors). The vast majority of anti-TIGIT antibodies in clinical development were Fc-enabled, meaning they can activate immune processes that could eliminate cancer-killing immune cells. Until recently, there were no clinical data to support whether Fc activity is needed for clinical benefit. Yet, the hypothesis that Fc activity is required has been the prevailing belief in the field.


In an Fc-silent antibody, the Fc-region, or tail, is not functional. In an Fc-enabled antibody, the tail is functional and can send signals that may eliminate cancer-killing immune cells.


Arcus was among the first to take a different approach and pursue an Fc-silent anti-TIGIT antibody based on our own scientific understanding of how this biological pathway works. Both Fc-silent and Fc-enabled antibodies can block TIGIT’s inhibition of the immune system, but unlike Fc-enabled antibodies, an Fc-silent one may also reduce the possibility of killing two types of cells: TIGIT-expressing immune cells that are capable of killing cancer cells, and regulatory T cells (Treg) outside the tumor where they control unwanted immune responses against healthy tissue.3


## Designed to be Combined


Since blocking TIGIT alone may not promote full activation of immune cells, we are studying an investigational Fc-silent anti-TIGIT antibody specifically designed to be used in combination with other treatments. Our intention is to combine the Fc-silent antibody with other immunotherapy options without compounding the potential harmful side effects often associated with multi-treatment regimens.


Blocking both PD-1 and TIGIT could enhance activation of immune cells because these checkpoints appear to play distinct but potentially complementary roles in anti-tumor activity. Combination with Fc-silent anti-TIGIT might not compound potentially harmful side effects associated with multi-treatment regimens.


In preclinical studies, the investigational Fc-silent anti-TIGIT antibody in combination with an investigational anti-PD-1 antibody increased production of pro-inflammatory proteins important for anti-tumor immunity.4 In other preclinical studies, Fc-silent anti-TIGIT combined with anti-PD-1 also promoted tumor control without depleting other helpful immune cells, suggesting an Fc-enabled antibody may not be required for anti-tumor immunity.5


Two Phase 2 clinical studies in non-small cell lung cancer and upper gastrointestinal cancers have been published at medical conferences and a peer-reviewed journal that characterize the safety and efficacy of this investigational combination. 6,7,8,9 Phase 3 studies are ongoing.


## Changing the Treatment Paradigm


Together, our encouraging early data represents our commitment to exploring a novel way to target TIGIT in pursuit of changing the way combination treatments are used in immunotherapy. Everything we do is in service of one goal: improving care for people with cancer to help them live fuller lives. I take immense personal pride in contributing to Arcus’s mission.


Our researchers’ objective approach to evaluating the Fc activity hypothesis, combined with a significant amount of scientific intuition, led us to postulate that Fc activity does not appear to be needed for clinical benefit of anti-TIGIT antibodies. The development of an Fc-silent anti-TIGIT antibody is a testament to the extraordinary efforts of our talented team, who were recently recognized at the[Pharmaceutical Technology Excellence Awards](https://www.pharmaceutical-technology.com/excellence-awards/winners/) .


The entire Arcus team is passionately dedicated to continuing to investigate combinations of treatment in different cancer types with high unmet need.
