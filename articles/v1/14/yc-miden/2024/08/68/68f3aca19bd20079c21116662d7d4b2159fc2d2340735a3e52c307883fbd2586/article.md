### What Are Corporate Cards?


corporate cards for businesses in Africa


Think of corporate cards as company cards issued to your employees. Employees can use these cards to cover business needs, like software subscriptions, travel costs, or office supplies, without needing to use their own cash or personal cards.


This simplifies things for everyone – employees don't have to wait for reimbursements, and you gain better control over company spending.


Corporate cards come in two main types: physical cards and virtual cards. Both options allow employees to make authorized purchases on the company's behalf.


Virtual cards offer some extra perks:


**Enhanced Security:** Since there's no physical card to lose or steal, virtual cards are generally considered more secure.


**Budget Control:** You can easily set spending limits for each virtual card, giving you more control over company funds.


### Virtual Cards: A Growing Trend


Cash used to be king, but electronic transactions are becoming increasingly popular. Virtual cards are at the forefront of this trend.


They are the second most popular payment method in over five Sub-Saharan African countries, including Nigeria, after physical cash.


In Nigeria, about 35% of people and businesses use virtual cards. Physical cash is still the most common way to pay, especially among older people and in rural areas. But it's clear that virtual card adoption rates are steadily growing.


### Things to Look Out for Before Choosing a Corporate Card


These four key factors will make or break your business. Don't overlook any of them.


### Security


Security matters most when picking a virtual corporate card. Find robust fraud protection to guard your business against unauthorized transactions. You don't want customers losing trust in your brand over "suspicious" activity.


Security isn't simple - it covers many areas. Let's break down the key aspects:


**Data Security:** Protect cardholder information like card numbers and CVV codes. Use encryption, tokenization, and secure transmission protocols to safeguard sensitive data.


**Fraud Prevention:** Implement advanced fraud detection algorithms and real-time alerts. Use multi-factor authentication and biometric verification to ensure only authorized users access accounts.


**Access Control:** Restrict access to virtual card accounts. Use role-based controls, strong password policies, and two-factor authentication to prevent unauthorized access and internal breaches.


**Compliance and Regulations:** Follow industry standards like PCI DSS and GDPR. Conduct regular audits and train staff to maintain compliance.


**Vendor Security:** Assess the security practices of third-party vendors. Check their certifications and data protection measures. Include security requirements in contracts and service agreements.


**Employee Training and Awareness:** Train employees on security best practices. Help them recognize phishing attempts and understand their role in protecting card information.
