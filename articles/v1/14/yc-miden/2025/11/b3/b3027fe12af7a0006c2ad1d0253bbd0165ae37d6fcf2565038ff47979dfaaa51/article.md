## Best Practices for Choosing a Payment Processing Provider in Nigeria


### 1. Access Your Business Needs


To choose the right payment processing provider, start by determining:


- **Transaction Volume:** The higher your transaction volume is, the higher you want to aim.
- **Geography** : Are you focused solely on Nigerian customers, Africa, or beyond
- **Target Audience Needs:** Which payment method does your customer prefer? You can choose to offer stablecoin, bank transfer, or card payment services.
- **Scalability** : Ensure the provider supports high-volume transactions, provides enterprise-grade features, and is capable of meeting future market demands.


### 2. Prioritise Reliability Over Everything Else


Reliability is the watchword. Choose a processor with:


- A proven track record of high uptime (98%+)


- Real-time transaction monitoring


- Clear, transparent customer support services


### 3. Check Their Fraud Prevention Measures


Reports have constantly shown that financial institutions have[lost up to N42.6 billion to fraud in Nigeria](https://www.arise.tv/nigerian-banks-lose-n42-6bn-to-fraud-in-q2-2024-reports-fitc/#:~:text=6bn%20in%20Q2%202024%20compared,3billion%20in%20Q2.) . Hence, this is a very essential factor to consider when choosing a payment processor.


Ensure that the provider has:


- Advanced fraud detection
- AI/ML risk scoring
- Real-time/Automated fraud checks, etc.


###


### 4. Determine Settlement Speed & Transparency


Don’t fail to ask your provider salient questions about their settlement features.


- How fast do they respond to settlement charges - Same day, T+1, T+2, or more?
- Are they available during weekends and holidays?
- Are they transparent about all relevant fees?


### 5. User-Friendly Integration Setup


Every good payment processing provider should have:


- A Clean API documentation
- Sandbox environment


This helps you understand their product offerings properly and simplifies your onboarding process.


### 6. Compliance & Regulation Review


A good payment processing provider in Nigeria must be **PCI-DSS compliant** , **licensed by the Central Bank of Nigeria** (CBN), **NDPR-compliant** for data protection, and more.


Hence, make sure to do your due diligence.


### Red Flags to Watch For


1. Frequent downtime or unexplained transaction failures
2. Poor or unresponsive customer support
3. Hidden fees and unclear pricing
4. Lack of proper licensing or non-compliance with relevant regulations.
5. Poor reviews
