## 2. Entry Requirements: Visa on Arrival & eVisa in Sierra Leone


Sierra Leone offers two main entry routes for tourists and business travelers; a visa on arrival at official ports of entry, and an eVisa applied for online before you fly. Whichever one applies to you depends on your nationality.


### On-Arrival Visa Sierra Leone: What to Know


- Available at Freetown International Airport (Lungi) and at land borders including Jendema (Liberia) and Gbalamuya (Guinea).


- Standard fee is roughly USD 80 for most eligible nationalities (some third-party facilitators quote higher all-in prices, so always check the official rate first).


- ECOWAS/African Union citizens often pay less — some nationalities are charged around USD 25, and a handful (e.g., Botswana, Eswatini, Kenya, Lesotho, Malawi, Mauritius) are issued a free visa on arrival under reciprocal agreements.


- Visas on arrival are typically valid for 30 days, single entry.


- You'll need: a passport valid 6+ months beyond your stay, proof of onward/return travel, accommodation details, and your yellow fever vaccination card (mandatory, checked on landing).


### eVisa Option


- Citizens of countries not eligible for visa-on-arrival must apply for an eVisa in advance through the official portal.


- Cost is around USD 80, with processing usually completed within a few business days.


- Apply only through the official government portal — third-party sites often charge extra "service fees" for the same visa.

3. Money, Payments & the Best Virtual Cards for Sierra Leone


Sierra Leone remains a largely cash-based economy outside major hotels and businesses in Freetown. For anything you need to pay online before or during your trip — flights, hotels, visa fees, conference tickets — a virtual visa card for international payments is generally the fastest, safest option, especially if your home bank card gets blocked for "unusual" overseas or online activity.


### Local Currency & Cash


- The Official currency is the Sierra Leonean Leone (SLL).


- Carry cash (USD or EUR travel in clean, newer bills) for markets, local transport, and smaller restaurants.


- ATMs exist in Freetown but are not universally reliable outside the capital. Don't depend on them as your only source of funds.


### Mobile Money


Mobile money is the backbone of everyday digital payments in Sierra Leone:


- Orange Money and Africell's Afrimoney are the two dominant providers, used for everything from utility bills to hotel and restaurant payments.


- Both now offer prepaid Visa cards linked to mobile money wallets, letting users without a traditional bank account make local and international online payments. A useful context if you're paying local vendors, staff, or partners while in-country.


- Setting up a local mobile money account as a short-term tourist is possible but usually requires a local SIM and ID, so it's more practical for longer stays, NGO staff, or business travelers than a one-week beach holiday.


### How to Choose the Best Virtual Card for Online Payments in Sierra Leone


Because card acceptance is still uneven and international transfers to Sierra Leone can be slow or costly, many travelers, event organizers, and businesses use a virtual card to handle end-to-end travel logistics.


When comparing the best virtual card for online payments in this context, look for:


- Global acceptance with Visa or Mastercard networks (important since local card infrastructure is still developing)


- Instant issuance so you're not waiting days before a trip or event deadline.


- Multi-currency support to avoid repeated conversion fees between USD, EUR, and SLE.


- Low or no cross-border fees, since you'll likely be paying vendors, hotels, or organizers based outside your home country.


- Spending controls — the ability to load a fixed amount, which is useful for managing event budgets or per-trip spending caps.


- Works for one-off or recurring payments, since some virtual cards are single-use only

## Frequently Asked Questions


### How much is the visa fee for Sierra Leone?


The standard visa fee — whether visa on arrival or eVisa — is around USD 80 for most nationalities. Some African Union/ECOWAS citizens pay a reduced fee (around USD 25), and a small number of countries with reciprocal agreements get a free visa on arrival. Fees can change, so confirm the current amount on the official eVisa portal or with your nearest Sierra Leone embassy before you travel.


###
Does Sierra Leone use mobile money?


Yes. Mobile money is the primary way most Sierra Leoneans pay for everyday things. Orange Money and Africell's Afrimoney are the two major providers, used for bills, market purchases, transport, and increasingly hotel and restaurant payments. Both now also offer prepaid Visa cards linked to mobile money wallets for online and international payments. Short-term tourists can find it hard to register without a local SIM and ID, so cash and a reliable international virtual/travel card are usually more practical for a short trip.


###
How long does the Sierra Leone eVisa take?


Processing typically takes around 3 business days once you submit your application through the official eVisa portal, though it can take longer during busy periods. Apply well ahead of your travel dates, and avoid unofficial third-party visa sites that add extra service fees for the same result.
