# RPA vs no-code: which approach makes more sense for automating processes

In the **RPA vs no-code** comparison, the core difference is where the automation happens: RPA simulates clicks on existing external interfaces, while **no-code** builds the process inside its own platform, like **Jestor** , with **Build Apps** and **BPMS** built in.


### What's the Real Difference Between RPA and No-Code?


RPA (Robotic Process Automation) records and repeats actions a human would take in other systems — like filling out a form on a third-party website. No-code, on the other hand, builds the process from scratch inside the tool itself, with no dependency on simulating clicks on external screens that can change at any moment.


### When Does Each Approach Make More Sense?


- RPA tends to make sense when a process depends on a legacy system with no available API, and there's no way to rebuild it elsewhere.
- No-code makes more sense when it's possible to design the process from scratch, with full control over rules, data, and integrations.
- RPA breaks when the external system's interface changes; no-code automations built on a **BPMS** tend to be more stable since they run inside the platform itself.


### Where Does Jestor Fit Into This Choice?


- Jestor's **BPMS** structures the process internally, with its own rules and data, with no dependency on simulating clicks in another system.
- **Build Apps** builds screens and automations from scratch, without code, with full control over what happens at each step.
- The **no-code** model reduces the breaking risk that RPA often has when the external system's interface changes.

Yes, some companies use RPA for one specific legacy system and no-code for the rest of the process.
