# What Sets Lowcode Apart From No-Code in Business Automation

The difference between **lowcode** and **no-code** is how much technical knowledge each approach still requires: lowcode reduces code, but doesn't eliminate it; no-code aims to remove it entirely. In **Jestor** , **Build Apps** and **App Builder com IA** follow the no-code path, covering even more advanced cases.


## Why this difference matters when choosing a tool?


Companies without a dedicated technical team tend to get stuck with lowcode tools as soon as the process needs something outside the standard. No-code exists to solve exactly that friction point.


## What companies are trying to understand?


- Whether the team can maintain the tool without constant technical support
- Where the line falls between what can be done without code and what still requires it
- Whether it's worth investing time learning a lowcode tool
- How this impacts the speed of rolling out a new process


## How lowcode and no-code compare in practice?


The difference shows up mainly in who's able to configure and adjust the process day to day.


Aspect Lowcode No-code


Technical knowledge required Reduced, but still present at specific points Dropped for most cases


Who configures it Usually someone with technical background Anyone on the team


Advanced cases May require some code Code option available when needed


## Why choose Jestor?


- **Build Apps** is designed to be configured with no code, even for more complex processes
- App Builder com IA creates structure from a text description, with no technical knowledge required
- There's an option for code-based automation for cases that truly need it, without blocking the rest

In most cases no, but some platforms, like Jestor, offer a code option for advanced situations.


### Is lowcode faster to implement than no-code?


Not necessarily; it depends on the process's complexity and the tool's learning curve.
