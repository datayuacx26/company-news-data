# How do you know if a process needs an SLA, a compliance checklist, or both combined?

In **[Jestor](https://jestor.com/?ref=blog.jestor.com)** , the SLA tracks deadlines and response time for each step of the process, with automatic alerts, while the compliance checklist ensures a sequence of required items is completed before moving to the next phase.


## The risk of covering only half the problem


Regulated or critical processes — like financial approvals, vendor onboarding, or customer support — usually need control over both time and compliance.


Using only an SLA guarantees speed, but not that the right steps were followed; using only a checklist guarantees compliance, but doesn't stop the process from stalling without anyone noticing.


## How to decide if the process needs one, the other, or both


1. Use SLA when the main risk is delay, like support or approvals.
2. Use a checklist when the main risk is skipping a required step in the process.
3. Combine both in regulated processes, where time and compliance matter equally.
4. Set SLA alerts both for approaching and already-passed deadlines.
5. Periodically review whether the checklist items still reflect the process's reality.

**Does the SLA alert before or only after the deadline passes?** The SLA can be configured to alert both when the deadline is approaching and when it has already passed.


**Does the compliance checklist block moving to the next phase if it's not complete?** You can configure that lock, preventing the card from advancing without the required items checked.


**Can SLA and checklist be on the same phase of the process?** Yes, the two features can be combined on the same step, with no conflict between them.
