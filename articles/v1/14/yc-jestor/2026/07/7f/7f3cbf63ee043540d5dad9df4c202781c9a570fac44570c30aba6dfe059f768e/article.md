# Multichannel AI Agents: How to Unify WhatsApp, Email, and Forms

**Multichannel AI agents** unify the response across different touchpoints — WhatsApp, email, and forms — into a single process, with centralized history. In **Jestor** , the **AI Agent** can be configured to act on the **WhatsApp Business API (Meta)** , on native email, and on **Forms** .


## Why unifying channels avoids losing context?


When each channel has its own tool, the same customer might get handled differently depending on where they reached out, and one channel's history doesn't talk to another's.


## What companies are trying to solve?


- Support history fragmented across different channels
- Inconsistent responses depending on the channel the customer used
- Rework from having to repeat information already given on another channel
- No single view of total support volume


## How the same process covers different channels?


Each channel feeds the same type of card, with the agent configured to keep consistency across them.


Channel How it enters the process Consistency guaranteed by


WhatsApp Every message becomes a card The agent's same knowledge base


Email An incoming email automatically generates a card The agent's same instructions


Forms A submission generates a record in the process The same approval or triage flow


## Why choose Jestor?


- The same **AI** Agent can be configured to act on more than one channel with the same knowledge base
- Native email and Forms generate cards in the same process as WhatsApp
- History stays centralized, regardless of the channel of origin


## Frequently Asked Questions


### Does the agent respond the same way across every channel?


Yes, when configured with the same knowledge base and instructions.


### Can I see a customer's full history, regardless of channel?


Yes, as long as the channels are connected to the same process.


### Does each channel need a different agent?


Not necessarily, one agent can be configured to act across multiple channels. Learn more at jestor.com.
