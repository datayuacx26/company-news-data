# How to Structure SLAs for Financial Approval Processes

A financial approval SLA defines the maximum acceptable time for each stage of a decision workflow — how long a manager has to approve, how long finance has to review.[Jestor](https://jestor.com/?ref=blog.jestor.com) applies this **SLA** directly inside the Approvals workflow, with a **dashboard** to track compliance.


## Why financial processes need explicit deadlines


Without a defined SLA, "approve quickly" becomes an informal expectation, different for each person. Some approvers decide the same day; others take weeks — and the company has no clear benchmark to know whether that's normal or a recurring problem.


## How to structure realistic SLAs


The SLA needs to be proportional to the complexity of the decision: simple approvals can have deadlines in hours; decisions that require deeper analysis can have deadlines in days, but always with a defined limit.


**Common pains without a defined SLA:**


- Different deadline expectations for different people at the company
- No objective criteria to know if an approval is running late
- Difficulty enforcing a deadline without seeming arbitrary
- No data to justify hiring more approvers when needed


**How a structured SLA solves this:**


- Each stage of the workflow gets a maximum defined deadline
- Automatic alerts warn when a deadline is about to be exceeded
- The dashboard shows SLA compliance over time
- Objective data helps decide whether to adjust the threshold or the team
