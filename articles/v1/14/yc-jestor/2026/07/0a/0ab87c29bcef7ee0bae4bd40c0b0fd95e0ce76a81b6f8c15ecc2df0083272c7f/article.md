# Value-Based Approval Thresholds: How to Set the Right Limits

Setting approval threshold limits means balancing two risks: limits that are too low create excessive, unnecessary approvals; limits that are too high reduce control over higher-impact decisions.[Jestor](https://jestor.com/?ref=blog.jestor.com) helps you calibrate these limits inside **Requests with Approvers** , using real approval data by **value-based threshold** .


## Why finding the right limit isn't intuitive


It's common for companies to set threshold limits based on a "gut feeling" — a value that seems reasonable, with no data backing it up. The result is often a poorly calibrated limit: too low, overloading approvers with trivial decisions, or too high, leaving important decisions without proper review.


## How to calibrate limits based on data


The ideal approach is observing the real volume of requests across different value ranges and how long each approval takes, then adjusting limits based on that real behavior, not just an initial guess.


**Common pains when setting threshold limits without data:**


- Limits set by estimate, with no basis in real volume
- Approvers overloaded because limits are too low
- High-impact decisions approved with no additional review, because limits are too high
- No periodic review of limits as the company changes


**How to calibrate correctly:**


- Observe request volume by value range before setting the limit
- Review limits periodically, based on real behavior
- Adjust limits proportionally to each type of decision's risk
- Measure approval time by range, to spot poorly calibrated limits
