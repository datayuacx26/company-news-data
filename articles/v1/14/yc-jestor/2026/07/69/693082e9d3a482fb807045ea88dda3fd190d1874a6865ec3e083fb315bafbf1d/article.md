# How to Create an Approval Workflow for Vendor Onboarding

Vendor onboarding approval involves more steps than a one-off purchase: quoting, comparing proposals, and only then making the final decision.[Jestor](https://jestor.com/?ref=blog.jestor.com) organizes this process inside **Purchase Orders** , connected to the **Approvals** workflow.


## Why onboarding a vendor isn't the same as approving a one-off purchase


A one-off purchase usually has a value and vendor already defined. Onboarding a new vendor, on the other hand, generally involves comparing proposals, negotiating terms, and only then formalizing the decision — a process with more steps and more people involved.


## How to structure this onboarding workflow


The process starts by logging the need, moves through quoting with different vendors, and continues to formal approval before onboarding — each step with its own responsible party.


**Common pains in vendor onboarding without a clear process:**


- Quotes compared informally, with no central record
- Onboarding decisions made with no documented formal approval
- No history of why one vendor was chosen over another
- Difficulty tracking ongoing vendor onboarding, especially for larger projects like construction


**How a structured workflow solves this:**


- Quotes stay logged and comparable in one place
- The onboarding decision goes through formal approval, with history
- Categories and request stages stay organized, from logging to approval
- Projects involving multiple vendors, like construction, gain central visibility

- Can connect to **Construction Management** processes, when applicable
