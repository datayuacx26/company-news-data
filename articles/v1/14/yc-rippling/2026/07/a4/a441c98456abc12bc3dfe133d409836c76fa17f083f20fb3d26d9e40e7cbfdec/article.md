A payroll company handles the complex task of paying your employees accurately and on time.

## Why does your business need a payroll software provider?


Running payroll manually might seem manageable at first, but it quickly becomes a liability.


Beyond the sheer time investment, manual payroll exposes your business to costly risks: miscalculations, missed deadlines, and compliance errors that can trigger penalties or damage employee trust.


A payroll software provider removes these risks and builds a foundation for efficiency, compliance, and scalability:


### Save time and reduce errors


Payroll software automates repetitive, error-prone tasks like wage calculations, overtime tracking, and tax withholdings. Instead of spending hours crunching numbers each pay period, you can run payroll in minutes.


### Ensure tax compliance


Tax rules change constantly at the federal, state, and local levels. Payroll providers track these changes for you, automatically apply the correct rates, and file payroll taxes on time.


### Streamline benefits and deductions


Modern payroll platforms connect directly with benefits providers, ensuring deductions for health insurance, retirement plans, and commuter benefits are accurate. Employees can also manage their benefits online through self-service portals, reducing HR’s administrative burden.


### Gain actionable insights through reporting


Accurate reporting transforms payroll from a back-office function into a decision-making tool. Real-time dashboards and custom reports give you visibility into labor costs, turnover, overtime trends, and compliance metrics — helping you control costs and plan for growth.


### Scale with confidence


Whether you’re hiring your 10th employee or your 1,000th, payroll software adapts to meet complexity. Multi-state compliance, industry-specific rules, and[international hiring](https://www.rippling.com/blog/hiring-international-employees) all become manageable with the right provider.

## Frequently Asked Questions


How much do payroll services cost?


Payroll services usually include a monthly base fee plus a per-employee charge. In most cases, the base fee falls between $40–$60, with per-employee rates ranging from $4–$12. Exact costs vary by provider, since some include features like tax filing and benefits in the base price while others charge separately.


Are there different types of payroll companies?


Yes, payroll companies generally fall into these categories: basic payroll processors that handle calculations and tax filing, full-service providers that include HR features and benefits administration, and comprehensive platforms like Rippling that integrate payroll with other business functions.


What services are included in the base price?


Most base plans include payroll processing, payroll tax calculations and filing, direct deposit, employee self-service portals, and basic reporting. Advanced features like benefits administration, time tracking, and HR tools often cost extra or require higher-tier plans.


Is payroll data secure?


Reputable payroll providers use bank-level encryption, maintain SOC 2 compliance, and follow strict data protection protocols. They're often more secure than handling payroll in-house, as they invest heavily in cybersecurity infrastructure and undergo regular security audits.


What are the best payroll services for small businesses?


The best small business payroll services offer comprehensive payroll services with automated tax calculations and transparent pricing. Rippling, OnPay, Gusto are top choices for small business payroll processing that simplify payroll management and handle payroll expenses effectively.


How does payroll software work?


Payroll software automates the calculation and processing of employee wages, tax withholdings, deductions, and direct deposits. It also handles payroll tax filing with federal, state, and local agencies, generates pay stubs, and maintains records for compliance purposes. Most platforms include employee self-service portals and integrate with accounting, HR, and time-tracking systems to reduce manual data entry.


Can payroll software handle both employees and contractors?


Most payroll software can handle both employees and contractors, but the features differ. Employees require tax withholding, benefits deductions, and W-2 generation. Contractors are paid gross without withholdings, and most platforms generate 1099s at year-end. Some providers also support international contractor payments across multiple currencies, which is useful for remote or globally distributed teams.


Can I switch payroll providers without losing historical data?


Yes. Switching payroll providers is common, and most modern platforms support data migration from prior systems. You'll typically need to export historical payroll records, YTD figures, and employee data from your current provider before importing into the new one. The cleanest time to switch is at the start of a new calendar year or quarter, though mid-year transitions are possible with careful YTD reconciliation. Most providers offer onboarding support to help with the migration.
