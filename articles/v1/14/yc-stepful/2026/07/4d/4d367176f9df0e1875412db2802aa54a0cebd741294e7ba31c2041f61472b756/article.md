# Radiology Tech Salary: Hourly and Annual Pay by State

[Radiology techs](https://www.stepful.com/post/what-is-a-radiology-tech) have one of the best jobs in healthcare. Besides being in demand, they make a good salary right from the start without needing a four-year degree.


The[Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS) reports](https://data.bls.gov/oesprofile/?major_group=290000&occupation=292034&measure=13&areas=STATE) that radiologic technologists and technicians earn an average of $83,840 a year, or about $40.31 an hour. The median amount they make is $80,110 per year and $38.52 per hour.


The lowest-paid 10% earn under $55,980, while the top 10% make $118,660 or more.


Pay can vary a lot depending on your experience, location, specialty, and work setting. One of the best things about this career is how many ways there are to grow your income.


This guide covers all the important details about radiology tech salaries. We’ll look at pay by state, experience level, specialty, and workplace so you can see the impact of each factor.

## Radiology Tech Salary by Experience Level


The BLS doesn't break out radiology tech pay by years of experience, but combining what we know from industry sources with BLS data can give us some idea.


[Payscale](https://www.payscale.com/research/US/Job=Radiologic_Technologist/Hourly_Rate) has collected 2,800 self-reported salary profiles, suggesting how pay climbs over a career. They report that entry-level techs earn 17% below the national average, and pay climbs steadily until techs are considered “experienced” and make 13% above average.


Applying their percentages to the BLS national average gives us an estimated picture of how pay tends to progress. These are estimates, not survey results. Payscale’s percentages are calculated against its own self-reported average, which runs lower than the BLS figure, so treat the dollar amounts as directional.


- **Entry-level (0 to 2 years):** New rad techs make roughly $56,000 to $70,000 a year while they develop skills and confidence with patients and equipment.
- **Mid-career (3 to 9 years):** These techs make roughly $84,000 a year, at or near the national average. Many add a specialty credential during this time, which pushes pay higher.
- **Experienced (10+ years):** Experienced techs make roughly $89,000 to $95,000 a year on average. Senior techs and those with multiple specialty credentials can reach the top tier of salaries, which would mean $99,000 to $118,660 or more.


## Radiology Tech Salary by Specialization


Specializing is one of the fastest ways to raise your pay as a radiology tech. Some specialties build on the[core duties](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/healthcare/radiologic-technologists.htm#tab-2) of general radiography and can be added to an existing RT certification. Others are separate imaging careers with their own training path and primary credential.


This section shares the average salaries of different radiology tech specialties.


In some cases, the BLS tracks a specialty as its own occupation, so we have their data. Where they do not, we have used the American Society of Radiologic Technologists’[2024 Wage and Salary Survey](https://www.asrt.org/main/news-publications/news/article/2024/10/31/asrt-wage-and-salary-survey-shows-wage-increases-across-all-major-disciplines) .


Because these two sources survey different populations, the figures below are best read as ballpark comparisons rather than exact rankings.


For reference, ASRT put the average general radiographer’s salary at $73,274 in 2024, while the BLS national average for all radiologic technologists and technicians is $83,840.


### CT Technologist


- **Average salary:**[$87,785 per year](https://www.asrt.org/main/news-publications/news/article/2024/10/31/asrt-wage-and-salary-survey-shows-wage-increases-across-all-major-disciplines)
- **Training path:** Post-primary credential added to an existing RT certification


CT techs operate computed tomography (CT) scanners, which use rotating X-ray equipment to capture detailed, layered images of organs and tissue.


They earn more than general radiographers because CT requires additional cross-sectional anatomy training and a separate certification. To move up, techs complete CT-specific clinical experience and pass ARRT's CT certification exam.


### Mammographer


- **Average salary:**[$88,468 per year](https://www.asrt.org/main/news-publications/news/article/2024/10/31/asrt-wage-and-salary-survey-shows-wage-increases-across-all-major-disciplines)


Mammographers perform breast imaging exams used to screen for and diagnose breast cancer.


This specialty pays more because it's federally regulated under the[Mammography Quality Standards Act](https://www.fda.gov/radiation-emitting-products/mammography-quality-standards-act-mqsa-and-mqsa-program) , which requires documented training hours and a minimum number of supervised exams. Techs then earn ARRT mammography certification.


### Vascular Interventional Technologist


- **Average salary:**[$95,317 per year](https://www.asrt.org/main/news-publications/news/article/2024/10/31/asrt-wage-and-salary-survey-shows-wage-increases-across-all-major-disciplines)


Vascular interventional techs assist physicians with minimally invasive procedures, like angioplasty and stent placement. They use real-time imaging to help the surgeon guide small tools through blood vessels.


The role pays more because of its procedural complexity and frequent on-call demands. Techs need ARRT’s Vascular-Interventional Radiography credential, which requires additional clinical training and an exam.


### MRI Technologist


- **Average salary:**[$96,120 per year](https://data.bls.gov/oesprofile/?major_group=290000&occupation=292035&measure=04&areas=STATE)
- **Training path:** Available as a post-primary add-on or as a primary credential with its own program


MRI techs operate magnetic resonance imaging scanners, which use magnetic fields instead of radiation to produce detailed images.


Pay is higher because MRI involves specialized safety protocols and equipment knowledge beyond general radiography. Techs need clinical MRI experience and an ARRT MRI certification to move into the role.


### Diagnostic Medical Sonographer


- **Average salary:**[$97,240 per year](https://data.bls.gov/oesprofile/?major_group=290000&occupation=292032&measure=04&areas=STATE)
- **Training path:** Separate accredited program and primary credential (ARDMS RDMS)


Sonographers use high-frequency sound waves, rather than radiation, to produce real-time images of organs, tissue, and blood flow.


Pay is higher than general radiography in part because sonography is a distinct, hands-on skill. Techs manually position an ultrasound probe and assess image quality in real time rather than capturing a single static shot.


Most sonographers complete a dedicated training program and earn the Registered Diagnostic Medical Sonographer (RDMS) credential from ARDMS, which is the credential most employers require. ARRT also offers a sonography credential, though it is more common among rad techs adding sonography to an existing radiography career.


### Nuclear Medicine Technologist


- **Average salary:**[$105,160 per year](https://data.bls.gov/oesprofile/?major_group=290000&occupation=292033&measure=04&areas=STATE)
- **Training path:** Separate accredited program and primary credential (NMTCB or ARRT)


Nuclear medicine techs prepare and administer small amounts of radioactive material. Then, they use special cameras to capture images of how organs and tissues function.


This is one of the highest-paid imaging specialties because of the extra training required to safely handle radioactive materials.[Nuclear medicine](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/healthcare/nuclear-medicine-technologists.htm#tab-4) typically requires completing an accredited training program and earning certification from either the Nuclear Medicine Technology Certification Board (NMTCB) or ARRT.


### Radiation Therapist


- **Average salary:**[$114,990 per year](https://data.bls.gov/oesprofile/?major_group=290000&occupation=291124&measure=04&areas=STATE)
- **Training path:** Separate accredited program and primary ARRT credential


Radiation therapists deliver targeted radiation treatments to patients with cancer and other diseases, following a plan set by a radiation oncologist.


Pay is high because the role carries direct treatment responsibility and typically requires its own accredited program plus ARRT certification in radiation therapy.


### Registered Radiologist Assistant (RRA)


- **Average salary:**[$126,428 per year](https://www.asrt.org/main/news-publications/news/article/2024/10/31/asrt-wage-and-salary-survey-shows-wage-increases-across-all-major-disciplines)
- **Training path:** Radiography credential, approved RA program, and a master's degree


RRAs are experienced radiographers who take on advanced duties under a radiologist's supervision, such as performing certain procedures and offering initial observations on images.


This is the highest tier a radiographer can reach without becoming a physician, which is reflected in the pay. It requires an ARRT radiography credential, completion of an approved radiologist assistant program, a master’s degree, and qualifying clinical experience before earning ARRT’s RRA certification.


## Radiology Tech Salary by Work Setting


Where a radiology tech works affects pay almost as much as specialty does.


The BLS tracks[pay by industry](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/healthcare/radiologic-technologists.htm#tab-5) and reported these median annual salaries for 2024:


- **Federal government:** $93,970 was the median salary for government rad techs. It ranks as the highest-paying employer, but it employs only a small share of the radiology techs in the U.S.
- **Outpatient care centers:** $81,000 was the median annual salary of techs who worked at outpatient centers. This is higher than hospital pay and offers more predictable weekday hours than hospital jobs.
- **Hospitals:** $78,560 was the median annual salary for techs working in hospitals. While this is lower than the two employers above, hospitals employ the majority of radiology techs,[about 60%](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/healthcare/radiologic-technologists.htm#tab-3) .
- **Medical and diagnostic laboratories:** $76,770 was the median annual salary of techs who worked at imaging centers and labs.
- **Offices of physicians:** $66,060 was the median annual pay for those working at doctors’ offices. These roles typically involve a narrower range of exams and a lighter daily patient volume.


### Travel Radiology Techs


There is one group of techs who work in multiple settings and can earn better-than-average pay. These are travel radiology techs who fill short-term staffing gaps at hospitals and imaging centers around the country.


Contracts usually run 8 to 13 weeks and are set up through a staffing agency. According to[Nomad Health](https://nomadhealth.com/allied/travel-allied/radiology-tech) , most travel assignments require one to two years of clinical experience and an active ARRT certification. Many travel techs hold licenses in several states to qualify for a wider range of contracts.


Travel radiology tech roles often pay significantly more than permanent staff positions. According to[Vivian Health](https://www.vivian.com/allied-health/radiology-technologist/travel/salary/) , travel radiology techs average $2,248 a week, about 35% more than the staff average of $1,670 a week.


That higher pay can come with tradeoffs, though. Travel techs often cover their own temporary housing costs (though many contracts include a stipend), and they don't have the job stability of a permanent staff position.


## How to Increase Your Pay as a Radiology Tech


There are many ways radiology techs can boost their income over the course of their careers. These strategies range from simple to complex but are available to everyone:


- **Pick up shifts that pay overtime:** Evening, weekend, and overnight shifts typically pay extra per hour on top of base pay, which adds up over a full year.
- **Add a specialty credential:** CT, MRI, mammography, and other specialties consistently pay more than general radiography. They may require additional education, clinical hours, and/or another certification exam.
- **Consider travel assignments:** Even one or two short-term travel contracts can significantly boost pay compared to a permanent staff role, especially in high-demand locations.
- **Take on a leadership role:** Lead technologist, supervisor, and director roles come with a substantial pay bump. This move up[the career ladder](https://link.ahra.org/Article/building-a-career-ladder-for-medical-imaging-technologists) involves cross-training, mentorship duties, and managing others.
- **Relocate strategically:** Pay can vary by tens of thousands of dollars between states, so researching regional pay before a move can meaningfully affect your income.
- **Earn an advanced degree:** Rad techs with a bachelor’s degree[earn 7% more](https://www.asrt.org/docs/default-source/research/radiologic-technologist-wage-and-salary-survey-2024.pdf) than those with an associate degree. The top-paid RTs are Radiologist Assistants who have a master’s degree.


## Radiology Tech Salary FAQs


**How much does a radiology tech make per hour?**
The national average for radiologic technologists is[$40.31 per hour](https://data.bls.gov/oesprofile/?major_group=290000&occupation=292034&measure=13&areas=STATE) , as of May 2025. Pay varies by location, employer, experience, and more, so it’s a good idea to check wages in your area.


**Do travel radiology techs make more money?**
Often, yes. According to[Vivian Health](https://www.vivian.com/allied-health/radiology-technologist/travel/salary/) , travel radiology techs average $2,248 a week, about 35% more than the average of $1,670 a week for full-time staff positions.


**Which radiology tech specialty pays the most?**
[Registered Radiologist Assistants](https://www.arrt.org/pages/earn-arrt-credentials/credential-options/registered-radiologist-assistant) (RRAs) earn the most of any role covered in this guide, averaging[$126,428 per year](https://www.asrt.org/main/news-publications/news/article/2024/10/31/asrt-wage-and-salary-survey-shows-wage-increases-across-all-major-disciplines) , but this certification requires a master’s degree. Among credentials you can add to an existing RT certification without an advanced degree,[MRI](https://www.arrt.org/pages/resources/earn-additional-credentials/earn-additional-credentials-postprimary/magnetic-resonance-imaging) pays the most, averaging[$96,120 a year](https://data.bls.gov/oesprofile/?major_group=290000&occupation=292035&measure=04&areas=STATE) .


**What's the starting salary for a radiology tech?**
Entry-level radiology techs typically earn between $56,000 and $70,000 a year, depending on location, employer, and any specialty certifications they may have.


**Which state pays radiology techs the most?**
California pays the most, with an average annual wage of[$117,950](https://data.bls.gov/oesprofile/?major_group=290000&occupation=292034&measure=04&areas=STATE) , followed by Massachusetts, Washington, Oregon, and New York.


**Is it worth specializing as a radiology tech? ‍** Yes. Every role covered in this guide pays more on average than general radiography. Some, like CT, mammography, and MRI, are[post-primary credentials](https://www.arrt.org/pages/earn-arrt-credentials/rt-credential-options) you can add to an existing RT certification through structured education, documented clinical experience, and an exam. Others, like nuclear medicine and radiation therapy, are separate careers with their own accredited programs—a bigger commitment, but they also sit at the higher end of the pay range.
