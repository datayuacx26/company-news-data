# Medical Assistant in Texas Career Guide: Schools, Certifications, and More

Medical assistants (MAs) are in high demand nationwide, including in Texas. In fact, the[Texas Workforce Commission](https://texascareercheck.com/OccupationInfo/OccupationSummary/31-9092.00) projects that MA employment in the state will grow about 22% between 2024 and 2034. That’s much faster than the[12% growth rate](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/healthcare/medical-assistants.htm#tab-6) predicted for MAs in the nation as a whole.


Demand for medical assistants reflects the country's growing healthcare needs as our population ages. Because MAs handle both clinical and administrative tasks, they are a vital part of healthcare teams in many different settings.


In doctors’ offices, hospitals, and outpatient centers, MAs provide clinical support like taking vital signs and assisting with exams. They also provide administrative support, such as scheduling patient appointments and handling insurance paperwork.


Texas offers a wide range of training options, from in-person programs at community colleges to online programs you can complete in a few months.


This guide covers medical assistant training programs in Texas, how to find a job, salary and job outlook, and where most MAs work in the state.


We’ll also explain the path you need to follow to become an MA, what you need to know about certification and licensing, and break down what MAs can and cannot do in Texas.

## Where Medical Assistants Work in Texas


[Different work settings](https://www.stepful.com/post/where-do-medical-assistants-work) provide different day-to-day experiences. Physician offices tend to have a steadier, more predictable pace, while hospitals and outpatient centers move faster and offer more variety in the patients and procedures they see.


Medical assistants in Texas can find work in a variety of healthcare settings. However, most medical assistants work in doctors’ offices.


Nationally, the BLS says[57% of MAs work](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/healthcare/medical-assistants.htm#tab-3) for physicians. This might include primary care,[pediatrics](https://www.stepful.com/post/pediatric-medical-assistant) ,[cardiology](https://www.stepful.com/post/cardiology-medical-assistant) , and[OB-GYN](https://www.stepful.com/post/ob-gyn-medical-assistant) offices.


[Hospitals](https://www.stepful.com/post/hospital-medical-assistant) employ 17% of MAs nationwide. The benefit of hospital work is that it offers so many different styles of work.[Emergency medicine](https://www.stepful.com/post/emergency-medical-assistant) is great for MAs who like a faster pace and a lot of variety. Other units may focus on women’s health or supporting cancer patients. There are also roles with more administrative work if you prefer that.


Outpatient care centers, like urgent care clinics and imaging centers, employ another 10% of MAs nationwide. The rest work in a variety of other settings, from the offices of health practitioners like chiropractors to nursing homes, labs, and schools.


## How to Become a Medical Assistant in Texas


[Becoming a medical assistant in Texas](https://www.stepful.com/post/how-to-become-a-medical-assistant-in-texas) doesn't require a college degree or a state license, which makes it one of the more accessible healthcare careers to enter.


Here are five steps you can follow to get started in this career:


- **Step one:** Earn your high school diploma or GED. This is the only formal educational requirement to start training.
- **Step two:** Complete a training program. We’ve already talked about the variety of programs you can choose from, with different costs, timelines, and certifications. Completing a program takes the longest. Once you get that done, you’re almost there.
- **Step three:** Pass a certification exam. Certification isn't required by the state of Texas, but most employers prefer or require it.
- **Step four:** Complete a clinical externship. Programs[like Stepful’s](https://www.stepful.com/program/texas-medical-assistant-certification-program) require 80-160 in-person hours in a clinical setting. This can help you gain hands-on experience and make connections with employers.
- **Step five:** Apply for jobs. Use job boards, your externship connections, and healthcare facility websites to find openings.


Most people can become job-ready in Texas in anywhere from 5 months to 2 years, depending on the program they choose.


## Certification & License Requirements for Medical Assistants in Texas


Texas does not require medical assistants to hold a state license. There's also no legal requirement to be certified in order to work as an MA in Texas.


That said, most employers prefer to hire certified medical assistants. According to the National Healthcareer Association,[96% of employers](https://aci.edu/blog/nha-industry-outlook-shows-growing-healthcare-jobs-updated/) require or encourage certification since it shows you've been trained and tested on the core skills the job requires. Getting certified can also help you earn more from the start.


Several[medical assistant certifications](https://www.stepful.com/post/top-medical-assistant-certifications-to-advance-your-career) are available. These are the most widely recognized across the country.

## What Medical Assistants Can & Can't Do in Texas


In most healthcare roles, rules define what your training allows you to do and not do. This is called “scope of practice.” Most states, and sometimes the federal government, make rules about scope of practice for those working in healthcare.


[Scope of practice for medical assistants](https://www.stepful.com/post/medical-assistant-scope-of-practice-by-state) varies by state. A few states, like[Texas](https://www.aama-ntl.org/docs/default-source/state-sop-laws/scope-of-practice-for-medical-assistants-under-texas-law.pdf?sfvrsn=4a9ff775_1) , are vague about the specifics and go by the rule of “delegation.” They trust the supervising doctor or nurse to delegate only the tasks the medical assistant is properly trained for.


In practice, this means what a Texas MA can do often comes down to their training and their supervising provider's judgment. Still, some general limits apply to everyone.


Medical assistants in Texas can:


- Take vital signs and record medical histories
- Draw blood and collect lab specimens
- Give injections and administer medications as directed by a physician
- Assist with procedures and prepare patients for exams
- Perform basic clinical tests and operate equipment like EKG machines
- Handle administrative tasks like scheduling, billing, and maintaining records


Medical assistants in Texas cannot:


- Examine, diagnose, or treat patients independently
- Prescribe medications
- Perform tasks outside what their supervising provider has delegated to them
- Represent themselves to the public as being licensed to practice medicine
