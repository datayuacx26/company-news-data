# Pre-Med Volunteer Opportunities in San Diego

Volunteering is an essential part of the pre-med experience. Medical school admissions committees want to know you have been out in the world seeing how healthcare affects real people, not just holed up in the library studying.


They know that interacting with patients and healthcare teams will give you empathy and build the communication skills that will make you a better doctor.


The bonus is that it gives you material for your personal statement and letter-of-recommendation opportunities that go beyond grades and test scores.


**San Diego is a great place to find hands-on, pre-med volunteer experiences** . The city and greater county are home to major health systems, a VA hospital, a dedicated children's hospital, and a wide network of community clinics serving diverse patient populations.


Finding the right opportunity here isn't always simple, though. Popular hospital programs can have waitlists, strict age or hour requirements, or application windows that only open once or twice a year.


This guide is designed to help. It covers where to volunteer around San Diego, how to apply, and what to expect once you start. We’ll also talk about a paid role that can complement your volunteer work and strengthen your med school application.


## Volunteer Opportunities for Pre-Med Students in San Diego


San Diego's[pre-med volunteer opportunities](https://www.stepful.com/post/best-volunteer-opportunities-for-premed-students) generally fall into four categories: hospitals and health systems, hospice, community clinics and nonprofits, and academic and research.


Each builds a different kind of experience, and the strongest applications usually combine more than one.


Here's where to start looking around San Diego:


### San Diego Hospital and Health System Volunteer Programs


San Diego's major hospital systems all run structured volunteer programs. Most require an application, interview, and health clearance before you can start, so factor in an on-ramp period when considering your timeline.


#### [UC San Diego Health](https://health.ucsd.edu/support/volunteer/)


- **Roles:** Volunteers support patients and staff in helping with wayfinding, errands, stocking, deliveries, and visiting with patients.
- **Requirements:** Applicants must be at least 16 and commit to a weekly four-hour shift for a minimum total of 100 hours over six to eight months. You must provide immunization/vaccination records, a TB test, and a background check.
- **Application process:** Onboarding takes about a month. You must complete an online training course, attend an in-person orientation, and do any other training required by the department where you will volunteer.


#### [Sharp HealthCare](https://www.sharp.com/volunteers)


- **Roles:** Sharp places volunteers across its San Diego hospitals in roles like visitor greeting, patient companionship, and gift shop support.
- **Requirements:** Each hospital has its own requirements, which you can find on their site. They have minimum age and GPA requirements for students, as well as a minimum time commitment.
- **Application process:** The process includes an interview, orientation, and health screenings, which they will specify during onboarding.


#### [Scripps Health](https://www.scripps.org/about-us/volunteer)


- **Roles:** Scripps volunteers help with tasks ranging from welcoming guests to cleaning and clerical support. They even have special programs related to canine therapy, crafts, and music.
- **Requirements:** Applicants must be at least 15 years old and commit to two to four hours a week for a minimum of six months or 100 hours.
- **Application process:** Onboarding takes several weeks and includes an interview, a mandatory orientation, and immunization requirements.


#### [Kaiser Permanente San Diego](https://healthy.kaiserpermanente.org/southern-california/doctors-locations/san-diego-volunteer-services)


- **Roles:** Volunteers may work at hospitals, medical offices, or hospice locations. Support includes wayfinding, visiting with patients, deliveries, pet therapy, arts, and other support for patients and families.
- **Requirements:** Applicants **** must be 16 or older and commit to a three- to four-hour shift per week. Volunteers over 18 must commit to 120 total hours of service and pass a health clearance and background check.
- **Application process:** Training sessions and onboarding requirements vary by location and role.


#### [Rady Children's Hospital](https://radyfoundation.org/get-involved/volunteer/opportunities/hospital-volunteer/hospital-volunteer-application/)


- **Roles:** Support is needed in a variety of settings, including inpatient, outpatient, and emergency departments.
- **Requirements:** Applicants must be 18 or older and commit to one weekly three-hour shift for a minimum of 100 hours over 12 consecutive months. You must pass certain health screenings, a drug screening, and a background check. There are also required vaccinations.
- **Application process:** Rady's application process isn't always open, so it's worth checking the page regularly for the next window.


#### [VA San Diego Healthcare System](https://www.va.gov/san-diego-health-care/work-with-us/volunteer-or-donate/)


- **Roles:** Clinical opportunities include supporting staff in nursing, pharmacy, speech, and radiology. You may also support patients and families as an ambassador or greeter in surgical waiting rooms.
- **Requirements:** Volunteers may work at a community-based clinic or the main hospital. Requirements vary by location and role. Some require health screenings and a background check.
- **Application process:** Applications are accessed online by linking to a specific facility.


Volunteer program requirements, application cycles, and minimum hours at these hospitals change often, so confirm current details directly on each site before you apply.

### San Diego Hospice Volunteer Programs


Hospice work gives pre-med students a different kind of exposure than a hospital floor, one centered on companionship, comfort, and end-of-life care. It's one of the highest-impact volunteer categories for medical school applications.


#### [Hospice Volunteering with VITAS Healthcare](https://www.vitas.com/volunteer/volunteer-faqs)


- **Roles:** Volunteers can fill administrative or patient-facing roles, like one-on-one visits with patients and their families in their homes or at hospice facilities. The site lists many opportunities, including Direct Patient Care.
- **Requirements:** Volunteers who are 18 or older can join the interdisciplinary team and are eligible for many different roles. Other requirements may include health screenings, depending on the role and location.
- **Application process:** Complete a volunteer application, attend an orientation session, and complete VITAS's free volunteer training.


#### [Sharp HospiceCare](https://www.sharp.com/volunteers/hospice)


- **Roles:** Nonmedical support for patients and families, including caregiver relief, companionship, light housekeeping, and help with outings and errands. There are also administrative and clerical roles.
- **Requirements:** Applicants must commit to at least four hours a week for one year, provide a CA driver's license and proof of auto insurance, two letters of recommendation, and a $25 registration fee ($10 for students). There are also required health clearances. Volunteers 18 and older also must undergo a background check.
- **Application process:** Submit the application by email or mail, complete an interview, then attend the three required training sessions.


#### [The Elizabeth Hospice](https://elizabethhospice.org/about/volunteering/)


- **Roles:** Volunteer patient companions provide caregiver relief, social visits, light housekeeping, and run errands. There are also other roles available.
- **Requirements:** Applicants must commit for at least one year. Hours are scheduled around a patient’s needs, but a typical weekly shift is four hours or less. Training is provided.
- **Application process:** Call or email to sign up for a volunteer orientation, where you'll learn about the application and training process.


### San Diego Community Clinics and Nonprofit Health Organizations


Community clinics and nonprofits can offer more direct exposure to underserved patient populations than a large hospital. Some roles may be administrative or event-related, but some may provide even closer contact with patients than a typical hospital volunteer shift.


#### [Family Health Centers of San Diego](https://www.fhcsd.org/volunteer-opportunities/)


- **Roles:** General volunteer positions support health education, patient engagement, and clerical work in FHCSD's clinics. There are also a limited number of student internships each year.
- **Requirements:** Applicants must be at least 18, commit to a minimum of 100 total hours, and complete a background check and health screening, including TB testing and proof of COVID-19 vaccination.
- **Application process:** Apply online, attend an information session, and complete a department interview. After finishing the 100-hour minimum, FHCSD can provide a letter documenting your role and hours for med school applications.


#### [Volunteers in Medicine (VIM) San Diego](https://vim-sandiego.org/volunteer-application)


- **Roles:** Volunteer front desk managers support the clinic by checking in patients, answering the phone, assisting doctors and nurses, scheduling, and more.
- **Requirements:** Not listed publicly; VIM reviews applications and follows up directly about current openings.
- **Application process:** Submit the online volunteer application. VIM will reach out with details on available positions.


#### [UC San Diego Student-Run Free Clinic Project](https://familymedicine.ucsd.edu/education-training/freeclinic/support/volunteer.html)


- **Roles:** Undergraduate volunteers help patients access low-cost or free medication, support patient intake, manage inventory and supply ordering, collect and enter patient data, and more. They work alongside medical student volunteers and supervising physicians.
- **Requirements:** Requirements vary by role and will be posted with open positions.
- **Application process:** Announcements about open positions and application procedures are posted in their[Facebook group](https://www.facebook.com/groups/ucsdsrfcpvolunteers/) .


#### [Mama's Kitchen](https://mamaskitchen.org/volunteer/)


- **Roles:** Volunteers deliver medically tailored meals or prep food in the kitchen for San Diegans living with HIV/AIDS, cancer, and other chronic illnesses.
- **Requirements:** Delivery drivers must complete a background check and use their own vehicles. Weekly commitments are encouraged. Kitchen volunteers have no specific requirements and can sign up for shifts on an online schedule.
- **Application process:** Complete a volunteer registration form and a virtual orientation.


#### [San Diego Blood Bank](https://sandiegobloodbank.org/volunteers-organizers/volunteer-opportunities/)


- **Roles:** Volunteer bloodmobile greeters greet and support donors, make them feel at ease, help them fill out intake forms, and answer their questions.
- **Requirements:** Applicants must be 15 or older. Shifts run four to six hours.
- **Application process:** Submit a volunteer application and attend an orientation before your first shift.


#### [American Red Cross](https://www.redcross.org/local/california/southern-california/volunteer.html) , Southern California Region


- **Roles:** Volunteers support work like disaster relief response and blood drives.
- **Requirements:** Background checks are required for all adult volunteers, but other requirements vary by role. Time commitments are also variable.
- **Application process:** Complete an online application and submit a background check, then a volunteer screener will contact you with next steps.


Organizations may change their roles, requirements, or application cycles, so confirm this information before applying.


### San Diego Research and Academic Volunteer Opportunities


Unpaid research roles at San Diego's universities and medical schools build a different kind of experience, one that MD/PhD and research-focused MD programs value.


There are a few ways to find these positions. The most common is direct outreach to the faculty member running the research project. However, some scholar programs and individual research projects may also have online applications.


University pre-health advising offices are a good source of curated, regularly updated listings, even for students not enrolled at that school. The University of San Diego's[pre-health resources page](https://www.sandiego.edu/cas/student-resources/advising/pre-health/resources/volunteer-and-internship-opportunities.php) , for example, lists many local options.


Here are a few specific opportunities you can explore.


#### [UC San Diego Emergency Medicine Research Associates Program](https://emergencymed.ucsd.edu/research/research-associates.html)


- **Roles:** Students screen, enroll, and interview emergency department patients for clinical research studies under faculty and resident supervision.
- **Requirements:** Applicants must first become Health System Volunteers through[UC San Diego Health Volunteer Services](https://health.ucsd.edu/support/volunteer/) . You must commit to at least 100 hours in six months and show proof of health screenings and a background check. Then, you must complete a variety of training requirements depending on the role and location.
- **Application process:** The program accepts applications only at certain times of year and then reviews them on a rolling basis. Check the site to find out when applications are being accepted.


#### [San Diego State University Department of Psychology Research Labs](https://psychology.sdsu.edu/research/become-a-research-assistant/)


- **Roles:** Students volunteer as unpaid research assistants in faculty labs studying a variety of factors related to human behavior. They help with data collection, literature review, and other ongoing lab work.
- **Requirements:** Students must be in good academic standing. The site recommends getting involved as early as possible in your academic program.
- **Application process:** Students identify a lab of interest, then contact the principal investigator directly by email or during office hours to ask about openings. Some labs require a formal application, which you can download.


## How to Find and Apply for Pre-Med Volunteer Opportunities


Once you know which kind of opportunities interest you, the actual search-and-apply process looks pretty similar across most San Diego organizations.


1. Search the sites of organizations directly. Don’t rely on an aggregator or third-party volunteer site. Organizations also list the most up-to-date requirements. Here’s a list of categories to get you started:


- The volunteer pages on hospital and health system websites


- Websites of community clinics and nonprofits
- Hospice websites
- Pre-health advising offices at universities
- Pre-med clubs and organizations, like[AMSA](https://www.amsa.org/) chapters or[Alpha Epsilon Delta](https://www.aednational.com/) ‍
- Websites for volunteers like[Idealist](https://www.idealist.org/)


1. Submit an application and prepare for an interview with a volunteer coordinator.
2. Complete any required health clearances, immunizations, and background checks. Requirements vary by organization and change over time, so confirm exactly what's needed for each volunteer role. Keep in mind that they may need to be done before you submit your application.
3. Attend orientation and any required training.
4. Start volunteering and track your hours. Review[what medical schools want to know](https://students-residents.aamc.org/applying-medical-school/how-demonstrate-service-orientation) about your experience and keep a log of those items. That will make filling out your[AMCAS application](https://students-residents.aamc.org/applying-medical-school-amcas/publication-chapters/adding-work-experience-and-activities) and prepping for interviews much easier later on.

## What to Expect When Volunteering: Best Practices for Pre-Med Students


Volunteering rarely looks like the medical dramas that got you interested in the field in the first place. Going in with realistic expectations helps you get more out of the experience and avoid burning out.


Here are some tips that may help:


- Expect administrative or indirect tasks at first. Remember that even roles like greeting patients or restocking supplies put you in a clinical environment and teach you how care teams actually function.
- Keep a short journal or notes after each shift. Specific moments, not general impressions, are what make personal statements and interview answers memorable.
- Ask questions and build relationships with staff and physicians when appropriate (not in front of patients). These connections can turn into a letter of recommendation down the road.
- Show up reliably and follow through on your commitment. Consistency matters more to admissions committees than raw hours.
- Reflect on what you learn about yourself. Given how[competitive medical school admissions](https://www.stepful.com/post/is-it-hard-to-get-into-medical-school) have become, being able to explain specifically why an experience confirmed your interest in medicine matters as much as the experience itself.


## Balancing Volunteering with Academics and MCAT Prep


Volunteering matters, but not at the expense of your GPA or MCAT score. Those numbers carry more weight in the admissions process than the volunteer hours themselves.


Here are some tips for keeping things in balance:


- Don’t commit to more than you can handle. Most organizations ask for one shift a week of a few hours. Being consistent with that commitment counts more than just piling on hours.
- Communicate with your volunteer coordinator when you have exams or tougher class loads. Most coordinators will let you pause or scale back if you just let them know your needs.
- Avoid signing up for more than you can reasonably sustain. A shorter list of consistent commitments looks stronger on an application than a long list of one-time events.

## Pre-Med Volunteering in San Diego FAQs


**How many volunteer hours do medical schools expect from applicants?** The general consensus is that somewhere between[150 and 300 volunteer hours](https://www.stepful.com/post/how-many-volunteer-hours-for-medical-school) is the right amount. Doing more doesn’t make your application better. What matters most is consistent, meaningful involvement rather than hitting a specific number.


**Do I need to volunteer at a hospital specifically, or do other settings count?** Other settings count. Community clinics, hospice organizations, and research labs all give admissions committees a fuller picture of your interest in medicine and your willingness to serve others.


**How early should I start volunteering as a pre-med student?** Starting during your first or second year of college gives you time to build meaningful, long-term commitments instead of scrambling to log hours right before you apply. Consistency over multiple years matters more than an early start alone.


**Can shadowing count as volunteering?** No. AMCAS tracks shadowing and volunteering as separate categories, since shadowing involves observing rather than actively helping. Most applicants include both. You can[learn more about shadowing a doctor here](https://www.stepful.com/post/how-to-shadow-a-doctor) .


**Do ride-alongs with fire departments or EMS count as volunteer hours?** Not usually. A typical ride-along, where you observe without performing hands-on duties, falls under AMCAS's shadowing category rather than volunteering. It's still worth doing and worth including on your application, but it would be logged separately from your volunteer hours.


**Is paid clinical work like being a medical assistant a substitute for volunteering?** It's not a substitute, but it strengthens your application. Admissions committees generally value paid clinical roles as strong evidence of hands-on patient care experience. Most applicants benefit from having both paid work and volunteer service on their application.


**What if a hospital's volunteer program is closed to new applicants?** Don't wait around for one program to reopen. Community clinics, research labs, and hospice organizations often have more flexible timelines. Applying to a few different types of programs improves your odds of getting started sooner.


**How does volunteering affect my overall chances of getting into medical school?** Volunteering alone won't[get you into med school](https://www.stepful.com/post/chances-of-getting-into-med-school) , but its absence can hurt you. Combined with strong grades, a competitive MCAT score, and clinical experience, it rounds out the profile admissions committees look for.
