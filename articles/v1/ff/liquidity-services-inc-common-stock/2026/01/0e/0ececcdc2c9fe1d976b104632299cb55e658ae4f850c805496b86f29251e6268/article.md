# A Practical Guide To Inventory Traceability and Inventory Tracking

Public Works and Utility departments manage some of the most complex inventories in local government. Their inventory often includes fleet vehicles, heavy equipment, meters, valves, pipe, transformers, tools, and tons of scrap materials. These assets are all critical to daily operations, public safety, and regulatory compliance.


When assets reach the end of their service life, agencies often find it challenging to keep clear records, maintain accountability, and ensure transparency during the surplus disposal process. Our partnerships with Public Works and Utility agencies across the country reveal a frequent source of confusion between **inventory tracking** and **inventory traceability** . Traceability is especially important in surplus, when assets are subject to audits, sales, or public scrutiny.


## **Tracking vs. Traceability: What’s the Difference?**


Although related, tracking and traceability serve different operational and compliance needs.


**Inventory tracking** is the process of monitoring an asset’s current location, quantity, and status. It focuses on day-to-day operational visibility.


Tracking answers questions such as:


- Where is this backhoe or service truck today?
- How many spare water meters are in the yard?
- Is this transformer in storage, in use, or surplus?


**Inventory traceability** is the ability to document and retrieve an asset’s entire lifecycle, from acquisition through final disposal.


Traceability answers questions like:


- When and why was this asset taken out of service?
- Who approved it as surplus?
- What condition was it in when taken out of service?
- How was it sold or disposed?
- Can we prove compliance during an audit or public records request


**Tracking tells you what you have today. Traceability tells you what happened yesterday and why.**


## **Why Inventory Traceability Matters in Surplus Sales**


Inventory traceability is about more than recordkeeping; itis about protecting public assets, ensuring compliance, and maximizing value throughout the asset lifecycle. By maintaining documented proof of asset history, agencies meet federal and state regulatory requirements while reducing risk through rapid responses to audits or recalls. Traceability also enables effective root cause analysis when failures occur, helping identify sources and prevent future issues. Most importantly, it promotes transparency and accountability, building public trust and guaranteeing the ethical disposal of taxpayer-funded assets.
