# Optimizing nitrogen fertilizer in agriculture

## Introducing Messium


[Messium](https://www.messium.co.uk/) is a UK-based precision agriculture company optimizing wheat growth and fertilizer use across the globe. By monitoring crop health, and leveraging hyperspectral satellites and state-of-the-art crop growth models, Messium helps farmers apply the minimal necessary fertilizer to maximize the growth of the crop and reduce the impact on the surrounding environment, all while optimizing farmer profit.


## Addressing the problem – Fertilizer Overuse


Determining the optimal amount of nitrogen fertilizer to enhance wheat yields is a difficult task. Fertilizer overuse is a common problem, resulting in increased nitrous oxide emissions (273x more damaging per tonne released than carbon dioxide) and reduced farm profits.


Crop Nitrogen concentration maps. *Credit: Messium*


The pre-fertilizer map to the left displays homogenous crop nitrogen distribution as yellow. Meanwhile, the post-fertilizer map to the right shows areas of over-fertilization in red.


Messium detects excess nitrogen concentrations (red) in real-time with laboratory-level precision, allowing customers to tailor fertilizer use with less manual effort, reducing nitrous oxide emissions and increasing farm profitability.


## Choosing Wyvern for Hyperspectral Data


For Messium and its customers, space-based hyperspectral data represents a paradigm shift in what is possible within agriculture. Decades of academic research have generated advanced crop growth modelling and weather forecasting. However, commercial obstacles have limited implementation, including access to frequent, affordable, accurate and scalable crop monitoring. High-resolution hyperspectral satellite imaging from[Wyvern](https://www.wyvern.space/) helps bring this research to reality, enabling companies like Messium to build real solutions. For Messium, the ability to offer a cutting-edge, data-driven solution using Wyvern hyperspectral data has improved their offerings and created opportunities for further growth and innovation.
