# Ruba Doleh or: How I Learned to Stop Worrying and Love Coding

# **Ruba Doleh or: How I Learned to Stop Worrying and Love Coding**

My name is Ruba Doleh and I am working remotely as an Infrastructure Software Engineer with RelationalAI.


What is curious about my story is that I don’t really know how I got here. It all started off with a simple thought: I don’t like working with software. I don’t like coding. I would rather work in the hardware field. And while studying in Birzeit University in Ramallah, Palestine, that was my intention. Then I did an internship program during my fourth year with Exalt Technologies, a local company that Nokia used as a contractor. I even chose to work with Shell Scripting when no one was interested in that field. I didn’t know what I was getting myself into!

And that is where it clicked for me. I rather enjoyed working on the software side of this world (more than studying it, at least). It came like a calling to me.


The internship turned into a part-time job, and I was unofficially on the Nokia team. I say “unofficially” because my status at the company as a part-timer did not allow me to be an actual member of the team, but I still ended up helping them in any way I could.


The thing about my life, and especially my career, was that it was a series of large steps. Nothing came in small doses. I’m referring to being offered a full time position just before starting the second semester of my final year at university. And because of all the great work I did, I was OFFICIALLY placed on the Nokia team working with amazing people!


Press enter or click to view image in full size


I graduated with distinction from Birzeit University, and the first thing I did after that was to apply to the Manara program. I had heard about it before, but my plate was full. There was something about Manara though: it was daunting. I started doubting myself because up to that point in my career, I saw myself as a lucky woman: from an internship to a part-time job to a full-time job all in a span of a year. No interviews. No fuss. Just offer after another.


Manara was going to take me to uncharted waters. And it wasn’t easy, for many reasons, but mainly because the day I started the Manara program was the day I was promoted as Scrum Master with the Nokia team. I was the only woman on the team and the youngest: the next youngest person was a man who was 10 years older than me! So you understand how it was a challenge to fit Manara into my list of priorities.

But I was insistent. I needed that challenge in my life, and Manara certainly offered me that challenge. I had to remind myself everyday to switch between my work and personal laptops but it was all nonstop!


What helped was the amazing Manara community. Everyone was there for me, anytime and anywhere. And any doubt about my skills and abilities disappeared during the program. We were constantly told that we ARE good enough for anything that came our way.


There were several responsible for that: my soft skills TA Michael Wagner, my mentors Laamia Islam and Hailey Yoon, and of course from Manara Mai Temraz, the Program Manager, and Lisa Virtue, the Talent Placement Manager. The sessions with Michael were my favorite because he always encouraged me to talk, talk, talk. And naturally, that boosted my confidence.


Press enter or click to view image in full size


Then came the moment when I received an email from RelationalAI. They wanted me to interview for a remote position there, and it came shortly after completing the Manara program. It was a truly happy moment for me! I took a screenshot of that email and sent it out to my friends! But then I realized that I’ve never really had interview experience before, and this is where my experience with Manara kicked in. And I passed all the interviews with flying colors!


Despite getting the offer from RelationalAI, it was a bittersweet moment for me. I had built myself a solid ground to stand on with Nokia that it was difficult to say goodbye. But it was something my manager told me that made me move forward confidently. He said, “Ruba, you are a very talented woman. Always take big strides in your career.”


And that is what I’m doing.


I don’t know what my future holds. But I know one thing: I’m going to take it one step at a time. One BIG step at a time.
