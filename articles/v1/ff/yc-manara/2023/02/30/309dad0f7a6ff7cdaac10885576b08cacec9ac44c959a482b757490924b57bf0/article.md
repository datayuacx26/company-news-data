# From Tunisia to Spain: A Senior Engineer’s Route of Determination

My name is Wefa Hakem and I am from Tunisia. I want to tell you my story. My intention is not selfish. On the contrary, I am doing this in the hope that someone out there reads it and follows in my footsteps. Someone who would look at the challenges in front of them, and instead of saying “not today”, they would say “bring it on, everyday!”

Wefa Hakem, Tunisia


I found out about Manara in 2021when I heard their co-founder and CEO Iliana Montauk speak at an online conference about their program. I was inspired; it was exactly what I was looking for in my life. You see, I was always infatuated by computers, even from a young age. I participated in competitions, and even built my first website when I was 7 years old.


I was a senior engineer in a local tech company when I heard Iliana speak, and had set my sights on something bigger and better, because I knew I had it in me.


However, I had a problem. Well, it wasn’t really a “problem”, but let’s call it a “problem in disguise”. I had just given birth a couple of months before that conference, and my little angel was taking up most of my time. So when the time came to apply to the Summer Cohort of 2021, I hesitated. I stopped myself, saying “I will not have time!”. I had a job to keep, a baby to take care, and a home to maintain.


So I did not apply. *I just did NOT have the time!*


But regret instantly settled in me right after the application deadline had passed. I KNEW it was an opportunity I could not miss, and yet I still missed it. I was angry at myself and was adamant that I would apply to the next cohort.


Before doing so, I did something I had never done before: I set a strict, daily routine for myself, and then shared it with my husband, my family and my friends. I drew my own boundaries, and set my own schedule. My priorities in life were three: work, daughter, family. Each would get their own time of day, and none would encroach on the other!

With that settled, I set off on my journey. With a lot of practice, I was accepted into the Manara program. The most challenging part was the mock interviews. By nature, I am a shy person. I found it difficult to market myself. But I had a full set of truly inspirational women around me to help me through it: my mock interview trainer Amna Javid, and my mentors Einas Haddad and Rim Yazigi. What I like to call my support system! They all coached me on how to speak with no fear. I became a confident woman. I wouldn’t have done it without them!


The job hunting phase was a little stressful because, for me, that was time I had to *add* to my daily agenda. So here’s what I did: I lined up several interviews during the same week, and I would prepare for them all in one go.


This was when Amazon Spain came into the picture. My ‘support system’ had taught me so well that I smashed the interviews! I was so proud of myself, yet extremely exhausted! After that week, I threw my routine away and spent the whole week locked in a room by myself. I needed a break!


In the end, I am grateful for Manara to allow me the opportunity to better my life; to move abroad and experience a new culture and start afresh with my husband and daughter. It *will* be a very exciting journey that I can’t wait to embark on.


And I hope a woman reads this, tights up her hijab, and gets to work. Maybe one day she would say, “I am a better person now than I previously was because I did what Wefa did.”


That is my hope.
