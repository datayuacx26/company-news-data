***Note:*** *this data set is not intended to be representative of averages or norms in the region… though we’ve heard a lot of interest on this topic from both our talent and our hiring partners, so we may do more research & share it with you in the future! ### **Shouldn’t we compare living expenses and taxes too?**


Yes, absolutely! Often salaries are high in parts of the world where costs are higher. Make sure you take this into consideration before deciding where to seek a job.


For example, many of our alumni who have moved to Western Europe have been surprised at how high their **taxes** are… they may only get ~70% of the salary in their paycheck. This is referred to as “take-home pay.” In addition, **living expenses** can be very high in some locations. The biggest impact on your salary is the rent you pay.


If you are seeking to maximize your savings, consider staying in a low cost of living country and getting a highly paid remote job (if you can find one). For our alumni:


- **Those living in Palestine** and earning $40k remotely **are usually able to save more money** than those living in London and earning $70k. A salary in the UK of $70k ends up being closer to $50k after taxes, and rents are expensive.
- In the **UAE taxes are low** but living expenses are likely higher than in your home country
- **The West Bank has higher expenses than in Egypt,** which partially explains why their salaries are higher


### **Tips to learn from Manara alumni to increase your salary**


Our alumni in this sample have had a special leg up when it comes to getting high salaries because they’ve received custom training and referrals to companies through Manara’s hiring partnerships or our community.


Nevertheless, there are some tips that they’ve learned that we can share with you:


1. **Research companies in depth**


- Most companies have **salary bands** that they use, and that will be the biggest determinant of the salary you get if you join them.
- Some remote companies have a policy of **paying everyone in the world the same salaries** , which means that if you stay in the Middle East or North Africa, you’ll end up with a really high salary by local standards (as much as $100k for a junior engineer!)
- Most remote companies have a policy of **paying salaries based on the cost of living, but higher than local companies** to attract talent, but their rates would be at the lower ends of our ranges above
- Some remote companies **pay local salaries** , especially those hiring large amounts of people through outsourcing


**2. Understand the market**


- **Salaries depend a lot on supply & demand:** There are times in history when the demand for talent globally exceeds the amount of talent available, in which case companies are willing to pay more for talent. This was the case most recently in 2020–2022.
- When you hear news about companies laying off employees, it’s a good signal that demand for talent is lower… during such times you likely won’t have the ability to negotiate your salary much and will need to take jobs at the lower end of the salary spectrum. This has been the case in 2023–2024.


**3. Prepare to ace your interviews… in English!**


- A lot of companies that pay higher salaries have very high expectations for people during the application process & in their interviews. You will be competing with a global talent pool for these jobs and need to meet those expectations.
- Don’t underestimate the amount of time it takes to prepare for interviews. Even in the United States, software engineers applying to switch from one top tech company to another (e.g., working at Meta and applying to Google or Amazon) often spend 3+ months preparing for interviews. Manara alumni usually spent 6–12 months preparing for interviews at 10–20 hours/week.
- Don’t forget to practice English too! You will need to write and speak clearly (it’s ok if you make some mistakes).


**4. Be prepared to job hunt a long time**


- Even in the United States, it’s not uncommon for people to spend 12 months applying to jobs, doing interviews, etc especially during a slow market. In a hotter market, it still takes most qualified candidates 6–12 months.
- If your resume has skills missing in it that would help you land an excellent role, work on building those skills in your current job, freelance projects, internships, open source projects, etc. Don’t assume people will just give you a chance because you’re interested in learning new things… they want you to demonstrate your drive, curiosity, and experience.


**5. Take stepping stones towards your goal**


- To avoid job hunting a long time, take stepping stones towards your goal. For example if your goal is to get a remote job that pays you $100k or more and you’re a junior engineer, realize that these roles are extremely rare in the current environment. Focus instead on getting a great job at world-class company (locally or remotely) and building your skills for now. Once you are a mid-level or senior engineer, you will have many more opportunities.


**6. Practice negotiating**


- Especially in a hot market, you will have the chance to negotiate… and should usually ask for an amount higher than you feel comfortable asking.
- At Manara between 2020–2022, we advised our alumni to negotiate to receive ~30% more than offered.
- In this slower job market, make sure you answer honestly about your expected salary range when you’re asked so you’re not surprised at the end of the interview process. Companies hiring remotely these days are often paying salaries similar to local salaries, so if you are expecting to double or triple your salary, you may be disappointed when you get your job offer and they may feel frustrated that your expectations were so far off.
- **In** **the UAE, it’s standard to** **share what your current salary is** if a potential employer requests it. They may even request proof from your current employer. **In the United States and Europe, this is not a standard process** and most coaches recommend you do not disclose your current salary, just your expectations. In fact in some places it’s illegal for an employer to ask this question.


**7. Finally, if you can… consider which currency you want to be paid in**


- When our alumni took their jobs over the past few years, they didn’t realize that there would be so much inflation in currencies around the world. It’s not something you can always plan.
- Earlier this year, the devaluation of the Egyptian pound was on everybody’s minds. People who got remote jobs paid in dollars, or salaries pegged to the dollar, have been grateful.


### But wait, salary isn’t all that matters!


Especially if you are a junior engineer, make sure you **focus on building your skills and resume** instead of worrying about salary too much. If you get a high-paying role in which you aren’t developing your skills, it will be harder to maintain your salary in the future.


On the other hand, engineers who are truly senior engineers by global standards are in high demand consistently and can almost always command a salary of $90k or above when working remotely with a US startup or tech company.
