# International Import Shipping: 2026 Tariff Guide

**Update on tariffs and international trade**


U.S. tariffs changed on February 20, 2026. Here’s what you need to know:


- The U.S. Supreme Court[invalidated tariffs](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/25pdf/24-1287_4gcj.pdf) imposed in August 2025 under the International Emergency Economic Powers Act (IEEPA), including the reciprocal tariffs and additional IEEPA-based duties on Canada, Mexico, and China. U.S. Customs and Border Patrol stopped collecting IEEPA tariffs on February 24.
- The administration signed a new[temporary 10% import duty](https://www.whitehouse.gov/fact-sheets/2026/02/fact-sheet-president-donald-j-trump-imposes-a-temporary-import-duty-to-address-fundamental-international-payment-problems/) under Section 122 of the Trade Act of 1974, effective February 24 for 150 days. Some goods are exempt, including USMCA-compliant imports from Canada and Mexico, goods already subject to Section 232 tariffs (steel, aluminum, autos), and certain electronics, pharmaceuticals, agricultural products, and passenger vehicles.
- Section 232 tariffs on[steel, aluminum](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/02/adjusting-imports-of-steel-into-the-united-states/) ,[automobiles](https://www.whitehouse.gov/fact-sheets/2025/03/fact-sheet-president-donald-j-trump-adjusts-imports-of-automobiles-and-automobile-parts-into-the-united-states/) , copper, and lumber remain in effect and are not impacted by the court ruling.
- The de minimis exemption remains suspended. Low-value shipments under $800, including those through postal carriers, are subject to the Section 122 duty. Congress also eliminated the exemption in statute as part of the One Big Beautiful Bill Act.


Check with your local trade authority for the latest information on current tariffs—and[learn more about Shopify’s position](https://www.shopify.com/news/open-doors-open-trade) on trade.

## Find the right HS code


A crucial first step to navigate tariffs is finding the right HS codes for your products. HS codes (Harmonized System codes) are standardized numerical identifiers that classify traded products worldwide and determine their import duties.


The right HS code could mean the difference between a 0% and 15% duty rate or higher. In order to receive accurate tariff rates, you'll need to[add an HS code](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/international/duties-and-import-taxes/charging-duties#add-hs-codes) and country of origin to each product in your Shopify admin. You should also use the HS code from the guide on commercial invoices when importing into the US.


Shopify's[built-in calculator](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/international/duties-and-import-taxes/charging-duties#collect-duty-and-import-taxes-at-checkout) for taxes and duties can help you find applicable rates.


## Optimize duties collection


The next step is implementing a strategy for handling costs imposed by duties and import taxes. When shipping internationally, you have two main approaches:


1. **Delivered At Place (DAP):** Your customer pays duties and taxes upon delivery. While this requires no upfront cost from you, it could lead to surprise fees for customers and potential delivery delays.
2. **Delivered Duty Paid (DDP):** Duties and taxes are calculated and collected at checkout. This creates a transparent experience for your customers with no surprise fees at delivery. With Shopify, you can either absorb these costs yourself or collect them from customers at checkout.


### Managing duties and taxes in international markets


Shopify merchants can use the[duties calculator in the admin](https://admin.shopify.com/settings/taxes#duties) to estimate duties and import taxes. You can build duties into your prices to cover expected costs, or choose to transparently display duties to customers at checkout. By communicating that no additional fees will apply at delivery, your customers will feel more confident in completing their purchase.


Here's how:

2. Add a[country of origin](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/international/duties-and-import-taxes/charging-duties#add-hs-codes) to your products so the correct import tax and duty rates are applied at checkout.
3. Add[HS codes](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/fulfillment/shopify-shipping/hs-codes) to your products to identify what the product is and help avoid border delays.


You can bulk-add HS codes and country of origin via CSV, or add them manually on the product's page.

### Purchase Delivered Duty Paid (DDP) labels


Once you calculate and collect duties and import taxes at checkout, you need to purchase and use Delivered Duty Paid (DDP) shipping labels instead of DAP (Delivered at Place) shipping labels for international orders. This ensures your customer is not double-charged for duties and import taxes.

When fulfilling orders, select DHL Express or DHL eCommerce and choose the prepaid duties option.

### Purchase Delivered at Place (DAP) labels


If you choose not to collect duties at checkout or use DDP labels, you can use Delivered at Place (DAP) labels (also called Delivered Duty Unpaid or DDU). With DAP shipping, you take responsibility for shipping the product, while your customer pays any import or duties fees upon delivery.


You can[buy DAP shipping labels](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/fulfillment/shopify-shipping/shipping-labels) in the U.S. and Canada through Shopify when you're fulfilling an order in your admin. For merchants based elsewhere, check with your preferred carrier and assess DDP and DAP options for your region.


Tips for using DAP shipping labels:


- Clearly communicate that customers are responsible for paying duties, taxes, and potential handling fees upon delivery.
- Include this information in your shipping policy and order confirmation emails.

## International Import Shipping FAQ


### What's the difference between a tariff and an import duty?


A tariff is a specific type of import duty, and import duty is the broader tax customs authorities collect on goods moving across borders. Import duty, or customs duty, is a tax collected by customs authorities on all goods sold across borders. Tariffs typically target specific product categories or countries as part of trade policy, while general import duties and taxes apply more broadly based on a product's classification and declared value.


### Is the de minimis exemption for low-value imports still available?


No, the de minimis exemption for low-value shipments is currently suspended in the U.S. Shipments under $800, including those sent through postal carriers, are now subject to duty under the Section 122 import duty, and Congress has also eliminated the exemption in statute. Businesses shipping internationally should build duty and tax collection into every order regardless of its declared value, rather than assuming smaller packages will clear duty-free.


### What documents do I need for international import shipping?


International import shipments generally require a commercial invoice, a certificate of origin, and a packing list. A commercial invoice states the commercial price and the cost of freight, insurance, packing and other miscellaneous charges. A certificate of origin identifies the country where goods were manufactured, which some countries require, and a packing list is a detailed inventory of items contained in a shipment that helps customs process the shipment faster. Pairing these with accurate HS codes reduces the risk of delays at the border.


### How long does international import shipping typically take?


International shipping times range from a few days to several weeks, depending largely on the mail service or carrier used. International shipping delivery times are dependent on which mail service is used and can take anywhere from a few days to several weeks for a package to arrive. Air-based services move faster but cost more, while ocean or standard postal options take longer but keep per-unit shipping costs lower for bulkier orders.


### How can I avoid delays at customs when importing internationally?


Avoid customs delays by pairing accurate HS codes with specific, detailed product descriptions on every shipment. Generic terms like "merchandise," "gift," or "clothes" are no longer accepted, and shipments with vague descriptions face delays or rejection, so use precise wording such as material and item type. Double-check the country of origin and commercial invoice details before a package ships, since mismatched or incomplete paperwork is a common cause of border holdups.
