# Best Influencer Marketing Campaigns: Examples + Expert Tips

The best influencer marketing campaigns help you boost brand visibility, expand your target market, and drive sales.


It’s a tactic trusted by marketers and customers alike. A[2025 Sprout Social study](https://media.sproutsocial.com/uploads/2025/05/influencer-marketing-report_2025_influencer-marketing-has-changed-how-we-shop.pdf) found that 16% of consumers rank influencer marketing as having the most impact on their purchasing decisions, and a[2026 Aspire survey](https://www.aspire.io/guides/the-state-of-influencer-marketing-2026#Download) of nearly 900 marketing professionals reports that influencer marketing spend is expected to exceed $12 billion in the US in 2026, up more than 15% from the year before. ## What are influencer marketing campaigns?


An[influencer marketing campaign](https://www.shopify.com/blog/marketing-campaign-influencer) is a marketing strategy in which a brand partners with an influencer or content creator to promote a product, service, or event. Influencer campaigns typically run on social media platforms such as Instagram,[TikTok](https://www.shopify.com/blog/tiktok-influencer-marketing) , and[YouTube](https://www.shopify.com/blog/youtube-influencer-marketing) . They can take various forms, such as product reviews, promotions, giveaways, or social media takeovers (when a creator temporarily posts content on a brand’s account). These strategic partnerships let brands connect with customers via people they already follow and trust, increasing the likelihood of conversions.


Influencer campaigns begin with a business identifying a creator (or multiple creators) whose personal brand and niche audiences align with the company’s brand identity and[target audience](https://www.shopify.com/blog/target-audience) . The next steps involve outreach and sending influencer briefs that outline details like deliverables, compensation, and timeline. Then, the creator executes their influencer content. Brands can measure the[key performance indicators (KPIs)](https://www.shopify.com/blog/what-is-kpi) of these campaigns by tracking metrics like[affiliate link](https://www.shopify.com/blog/affiliate-marketing-metrics) and[discount code](https://www.shopify.com/blog/discounts-and-coupons) conversions, post engagement, and impressions. [Successful influencer marketing](https://www.shopify.com/blog/influencer-marketing-smb-playbook) campaigns begin with a clear match between the brand’s target audience and the influencer’s reach. “It all starts at the top,” says Kendall, whose clients have included Graza, Canopy, Immi, and more. “I feel like where most people get it wrong is not paying attention to their[ideal customer persona (ICP)](https://www.shopify.com/blog/ideal-customer-profile) and recognizing whether the influencers support their ICP testing.”


The campaigns on this list demonstrate how complementary ICPs, creative ideas, and thoughtful approaches can create something effective and memorable. Here are six top influencer marketing examples.


### Graza: Seeding campaign


Source:[Molly Baz](https://www.instagram.com/mollybaz/?hl=en)


When[Graza](https://www.graza.co/) was preparing to launch its squeeze bottle Sizzle and Drizzle olive oils in 2022, Kendall (their social media marketing manager at the time) leaned heavily on[influencer product seeding](https://www.shopify.com/blog/seeding-sales) . This influencer strategy entails gifting products to creators with relevant audiences in the hopes that they share the product on their channels. What resulted was a seriously effective influencer marketing campaign that created significant buzz: Graza sold out of olive oil in its first week.


The campaign succeeded in part because the creators that were chosen to receive products—like beloved home cooks[Molly Baz](https://mollybaz.com/) and[Samin Nosrat](https://ciaosamin.com/) —had a natural connection with the brand. This led to more authentic, organic influencer-generated content.


“I feel like the work I did with Graza changed the perspective of influencer seeding,” Kendall says. “I feel like there’s more of an open-mindedness to being OK with saying, ’We’re sending this out, we’re not asking for people to post, it’s not a transaction.’ It’s important to be able to test the market, to then say, ’Hey, these people are fantastic; they keep sharing the product.’ And then inviting them to an affiliate program or ambassador program and tiering them up. If they’re advocating for you, keep rewarding them.”

### Made In: Platform-native strategy

Rather than chasing short-form viral posts, Made In has invested in long-form creative partnerships with chefs and food personalities like[Claire Saffitz](https://www.youtube.com/@CSaffitz/featured) , collaborating on content that mirrors how people actually research cookware.

[Whitelisting](https://www.shopify.com/blog/influencer-whitelisting-retail) , or Partnership Ads, is an influencer marketing tactic in which a brand runs targeted paid ads directly through the influencer’s own social media account. Unlike a typical sponsored post, which appears organically in an influencer’s feed and is left to perform on its own, whitelisted content is treated as paid media. The brand puts ad spend behind the post and distributes it to target audiences through the creator’s account. This also allows the brand to test different audiences, optimize performance, and scale spend, while still retaining the influencer’s likeness and voice.

## Best practices for managing influencer marketing campaigns


- Keep outreach simple
- Have a clear brief
- Embrace seeding
- Dig into influencers’ analytics
- Consider adding a paid usage clause
- Regularly communicate with and incentivize long-term collaborators


Read on for tips from Kendall to help you create the best influencer marketing campaigns possible.


### Keep outreach simple


When initiating an influencer marketing campaign, introduce your brand to the influencer first before sending any unique links or codes. “Don’t spew an affiliate link to someone when they haven’t even tried your product yet,” Kendall says. “Don’t say, ‘We’d love to send you this, and by the way, we made you a custom code.’ They don’t even know if they like your product.”


Instead, Kendall advises sending straightforward outreach messages and following up a few times if needed.

### Have a clear brief


To achieve the desired influencer campaign results, brands need to provide clear briefs. Kendall recommends getting detailed about the number of deliverables, tone, and talking points. “Sometimes I see briefs that say ‘Make a Reel,’ and that’s it,” Kendall says. “You’re not giving them any info. And then brands say, ‘What we’re paying for and what we’re getting are so wrong.’ And the immediate reaction is that the creator didn’t understand. But it’s not the creator’s fault.”


At the same time, Kendall suggests brands avoid overly rigid briefs that limit a creator’s voice. “It’s a balance of creative freedom,” she says. “It’s important that it’s collaborative—that it doesn’t feel like a demand, but a concept.”


A strong brief requires setting financial parameters upfront. Kendall recommends getting quotes from creators early on in the process to better understand their payment structures. “Even if it takes a little bit longer for the budgeting process, it’s easier to then activate the influencers because you have their fees already,” she says. This also means you’re only putting effort into influencers you can actually afford to work with.


Finally, Kendall advises building target CPM (cost per thousand impressions) into the negotiation process from the start, asking clients: “What’s our[CPM](https://www.shopify.com/blog/influencer-pricing) that we aim for, so when I’m negotiating with influencers, I can make sure that we’re working within this ideal range?”


### Embrace seeding


Kendall encourages brands to embrace product seeding as an early influencer marketing campaign strategy—“especially if you’re a day-zero, scrappy brand that’s finding influencers manually and not paying for a ton of multi-use platforms for managing UGC and paid marketing and search.”


Though it can feel nerve-wracking to gift products without a guaranteed post in return, it’s a great way to capture authentic UGC, and it’s still possible to track performance. “There are tools that we can use to track the content, like[Archive](https://archive.com/) and[Superfiliate](https://www.superfiliate.com/) , so that I’m able to then show earned media value of that influencer seeding, and what impact it’s having across our competitive market.”


Seeding isn’t the right fit for all brands. It’s generally more effective for lower-cost products rather than higher-priced items. Kendall notes that food and beverage brands are especially good candidates for this marketing technique.


### Dig into influencers’ analytics


To choose the right influencer partners for your campaigns, it’s critical to have a strong sense of their demographic, reach, and engagement. Kendall recommends using a tool like[CreatorIQ](https://www.creatoriq.com/next-generation-creator-marketing) , Superfiliate, or Archive to do a deep dive into a creator’s analytics or to simply ask them for their back-end analytics. She also stresses the importance of considering non-follower reach, which indicates whether an influencer’s content is spreading beyond just their followers.


### Consider adding a paid usage clause


A paid usage clause is a section of the contract that gives your company the right to use an influencer’s branded content how you see fit. For example, it could enable you to turn an influencer’s UGC into a paid ad down the road.


Kendall finds that paid usage clauses make influencer partnerships more valuable and easier to justify internally. “It’s a little more tolerable to the broader team, because even if we pay $6,000 for a paid partnership, we could actually make $20,000 because we had paid usage on that,” she says.


### Regularly communicate with and incentivize long-term collaborators


Building healthy influencer partnerships doesn’t end once you’ve onboarded creators to your affiliate or influencer program. “Holding retention within affiliate programs is really important,” Kendall says. “Community in all regards is key.”


She suggests sending out a biweekly or monthly newsletter to creators, asking them about challenges they’re encountering in their work, which creates an opportunity for your brand to get feedback on your process. Offering incentives, like free products, to top performers can also help reinforce long-term relationships.


## Best influencer marketing campaigns FAQ


### What is an influencer marketing campaign?


An[influencer marketing campaign](https://www.shopify.com/blog/marketing-campaign-influencer) is a marketing technique in which a brand partners with an influencer or creator to promote a product, service, or event, usually on social media platforms.


### What are the three Rs of influencer marketing?


The three Rs of influencer marketing are relevance (How well does the influencer’s personal brand align with yours?), reach (How big is the audience or how visible will the content be?), and resonance (How well does the influencer engage their audience?).


### What are the four types of influencers?


The four types of influencers are:


- [Nano- influencers](https://www.shopify.com/blog/nano-influencer) (500 to 10,000 followers)
- **Micro-** **influencers** (10,000 to 100,000 followers)
- **Mid-tier** **influencers** (100,000 to 500,000 followers)
- **Macro-** **influencers** (500,000 to 1 million followers)
- **Mega-** **influencers** (More than 1 million followers)
