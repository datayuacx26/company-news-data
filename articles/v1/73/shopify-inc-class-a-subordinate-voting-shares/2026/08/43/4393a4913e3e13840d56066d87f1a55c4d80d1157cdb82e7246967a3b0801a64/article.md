# How To Use Contextual Commerce: Guide for SMBs

Contextual commerce integrates online buying opportunities into places where people spend time but are not necessarily shopping. When you see the[Shopify Buy Button](https://www.shopify.com/buy-button) embedded in a blog post that mentions a specific product, that’s contextual commerce in action.


Contextual commerce is ushering in a new era of ecommerce and influencing purchasing decisions. On an episode of the[Shopify Masters podcast](https://www.shopify.com/blog/ridge-wallets-using-ai-to-grow-revenue) ,[Ridge](https://ridge.com/) SEO Sean Frank says he believes “the future is more agnostic commerce,” in which customers prioritize convenience rather than staying committed to a particular channel or shopping context. This expands transactions from a typical website checkout into social posts, voice-command smart assistants, and agentic experiences like[Perplexity Shopping](https://www.shopify.com/blog/perplexity-shopping) .


This guide explains the basics of contextual commerce, provides examples of shoppable touchpoints, and details how to implement contextual commerce for your brand.


## What is contextual commerce?


Contextual commerce is the practice of integrating the opportunity to buy something into online experiences outside of your ecommerce store. It lets consumers shop in the context of their typical online behaviors. For example, you can tag a product in a shoppable Instagram post, the user can tap to see it in your Instagram Shop, and then can click through to your store checkout.


Traditional ecommerce aims to get shoppers to visit retailers in their online store; contextual commerce meets the customer where they are—in the content, apps, and new technologies they’re already engaging with.


## Examples of contextual commerce


- Social media shopping
- Shoppable blog content
- Agentic commerce


Here are some essential ways contextual commerce is fueling the future of shopping and how you can use them for your store:


### Social media shopping


One form of contextual commerce is shoppable posts on social platforms, in which someone scrolling through apps like Instagram or TikTok sees a post in their feed, taps a button, and then browses your products without leaving the app.


[Gogo Sweaters](https://gogosweaters.com/) , for example, made its Instagram content shoppable by tagging products in their posts. This creates a “View shop” banner that takes shoppers directly to the company’s Instagram Shop, allowing consumers to browse Gogo Sweaters products without leaving the Instagram app. When customers are ready to make a purchase, they’re directed to your ecommerce site’s checkout page.


Source: Instagram via[Gogo Sweaters](https://www.instagram.com/p/DXHoLCtAoer/)


### Shoppable blog content


Shoppable blog content uses buy buttons and links inside relevant content to take someone into a purchasing flow. A food brand could create a[shoppable recipe](https://www.shopify.com/blog/shoppable-recipes) with links that help readers quickly purchase the ingredients they need.


You can also create shoppable how-to blog posts by embedding visual[buy buttons](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/online-sales-channels/buy-button) or[permalinks](https://community.shopify.com/c/Shopify-Design/Cart-Use-permalinks-to-pre-load-the-cart/td-p/613702#adding-a-buy-this-link-to-a-blog-post) that take a user directly to the first page of the checkout screen with items pre-loaded into their cart. This approach places your items in the context of someone learning more about the best running shoes for their specific needs.


By placing a Buy button right next to the item in the blog post—rather than having them search for it after they’ve read the content—you move potential customers at the top of the[marketing funnel](https://www.shopify.com/blog/marketing-funnel) downward, toward a sale.


### Agentic commerce


Over the past few years, shoppers have increasingly turned to generative artificial intelligence (AI) platforms and[AI agents](https://www.shopify.com/blog/ai-agents) for assistance with the product discovery and purchase process. According to[Shopify’s 2025 Global Holiday Retail Report](https://www.shopify.com/news/global-holiday-retail-report-2025) , 64% of shoppers said they were likely to use AI to some extent when making purchases. This increased to 84% among shoppers aged 18 to 24.


For example, after their coffee machine breaks down, a consumer might ask ChatGPT for recommendations on a good replacement. ChatGPT would offer options and pricing with tappable images to take the shopper directly into the purchase flow.


AI platforms like Microsoft’s Copilot Checkout and Perplexity’s Instant Buy take this a step further by delivering agentic commerce, in which customers can complete a purchase without leaving the chat.

## How to build a contextual commerce strategy


1. Determine where your customers spend their online time
2. Set up stores and shoppable posts on social
3. Make your blog shoppable
4. Prepare your site for agentic commerce
5. Monitor performance


With this step-by-step process, you can develop a strategy to embed opportunities for commerce within your consumers’ online habits:


### 1. Determine where your customers spend their online time


To prioritize your efforts, make sure you understand which platforms your audience spends time on. “It’s about knowing where your audience is,”[Sock Candy](https://sockcandy.com/) founder Mary Gui says on[Shopify Masters](https://www.shopify.com/blog/sock-candy-reddit-and-creative-marketing-tips) .


Sock Candy was active on TikTok, but Mary saw that videos weren’t converting for its largely 25-and-up consumer base, and shifted more of the brand’s efforts to Instagram. “You also have to know when to stop and when to realize, ‘OK, this is just not my platform,’” Mary says.


Dig into your metrics to see how much traffic you get on your main channels:


-


**Blog and AI answer engines.** Use[Google Analytics](https://www.shopify.com/blog/14681601-google-analytics-for-ecommerce-a-beginners-guide) to see how many people are visiting your blog pages, as well as the traffic you receive from AI answer engines like ChatGPT, Gemini, and Claude.


-


**Facebook and Instagram.** Log in to Meta Business Suite. On the left sidebar, see views and interactions by clicking on the Insights tab.


-


**TikTok.** In the TikTok app, tap the three lines on the top right corner of your profile, then select “Business suite.” Next, tap “Analytics” to view audience metrics.


Based on your findings, prioritize the contextual spaces where you’ll integrate shopping functionality.


### 2. Set up stores and shoppable posts on social

This is where you can set up an online shop on Facebook or Instagram and import your product catalog. Once your shop is approved and stocked, use Meta Business Suite to add a shoppable button to new posts. Shopify’s[Facebook & Instagram app](https://apps.shopify.com/facebook) does this for you, syncing your ecommerce site’s inventory to your presence on those platforms.


Within[Meta Business Suite](https://www.shopify.com/blog/instagram-tools) , create shoppable posts in the post creation screen via the shopping bag icon. Then, select the product from your catalog you wish to feature on that post. You can also create shoppable posts and ads through Shopify’s free Facebook & Instagram app.


#### TikTok


First, set up your[TikTok Shop](https://www.shopify.com/blog/tiktok-shopping) and link it to your brand’s TikTok account. After you’ve configured shipping options and submitted tax information, add products to your TikTok Shop. TikTok has[Shopify integration](https://seller-us.tiktok.com/university/essay?knowledge_id=6184952698373890&from=portal) , so you can easily import your shop to the app.


To tag products in TikTok posts, go to the Post module and tap Add Link, then select Products. In the Showcase module, browse through your available items and click Add on the product you want to feature. Then, enter a product name, click Add, and then post the video.


Shopify’s[TikTok app](https://apps.shopify.com/tiktok?search_id=d59d06f1-dff9-407f-865f-0b9da59c1dcf&shallow_install_type=search&surface_detail=tiktok&surface_inter_position=1&surface_intra_position=1&surface_type=search) connects your shop to your TikTok account and lets you create posts and ads that feature your designated products


### 3. Make your blog shoppable


Use[buy buttons](https://www.shopify.com/blog/60670213-5-ways-you-can-use-shopify-buy-buttons-to-sell-on-your-website-or-blog) in relevant content to drive customers to purchase products in your ecommerce store or to send customers straight to checkout. To do this[within Shopify](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/online-sales-channels/buy-button/create-buy-button) :


-


Add the[Buy Button sales channel](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/online-sales-channels/buy-button/setup) in your admin dashboard.


-


Then, create a Buy Button for a product or collection by going to Sales channels, then Buy Button in the Shopify admin.


-


Next, click Create a Buy Button and choose the option to make one for a single product or a collection.


-


Once you’ve created the Buy Button, click Copy Code. This gives you HTML code you can place where you need it to be in a blog post.


### 4. Prepare your site for agentic commerce


Shopify store owners can access AI channels like ChatGPT, Microsoft Copilot, and AI mode in Google search through[Agentic Storefronts](https://www.shopify.com/agentic-plan) . This feature syncs your Shopify store to AI chat platforms.


On the back end of your site, make sure your robots.txt file is set up for AI platform crawlers, much like you would for Google’s search crawlers. Then ensure product information, like titles, prices, materials, and dimensions, is organized in standard, machine-readable fields rather than embedded in marketing copy or page layouts.[Shopify Catalog](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/promoting-marketing/seo/shopify-catalog) provides the same schema fields automatically for all stores on Shopify. Finally, use[Shopify’s free audit tool](https://www.shopify.com/agentic-readiness) to make sure your site is agent-ready.


### 5. Monitor performance


Continually check vital metrics such as the number of adds to cart, product sales, and sales from tagged content in your posts with contextual commerce elements. For Meta properties (Instagram and Facebook), track this in Meta Business Suite in the Commerce Manager.


For blog posts with embedded buy buttons, you can track the click-through rate to see how often customers are going from the blog to the purchase flow. Shopify’s content metrics dashboard shows key performance indicators, like conversion rate and average order volume, that illustrate how well your contextual commerce features are driving conversions.


## Contextual commerce FAQ


### What is contextual commerce?


Contextual commerce is the practice of placing buying elements within content that your customers regularly interact with, like social media posts, blog posts, and AI chat.


### How does contextual commerce work?


Contextual commerce works by using tools within social media platforms, ecommerce services, and content management systems to embed or place commercial elements like a buy button or banner, taking someone to an in-app shop. The goal is not to drive someone to your ecommerce site, but to complete more sales within the app they’re using.


### What is an example of contextual marketing?


Contextual marketing makes ads and other marketing content feel like it’s naturally part of the context the consumer is in online, whether that’s within blog articles, social posts, or web pages. For example, a display ad might show an ad for pet toys in search results for pet travel advice, creating a personalized experience for the customer.
