# A Retailer’s Guide to Returns Management Software in 2026

Return volumes continue to grow. Per Statista Market Insights,[7.4%](https://www.statista.com/statistics/1613801/e-commerce-return-rate-worldwide/) of global ecommerce revenue in 2025 was returned to store owners, and the figure is forecast to reach 9% by 2029.


According to Radial, nearly[half of shoppers](https://www.radial.com/insights/the-new-guide-to-returns-2026) say they’ll stop buying from a retailer that flags a genuine return as fraudulent. This leaves you in a bind: protect the margin, lose the customer; please the customer, lose the margin.


To resolve this tension, treat each return on its own terms. This is something policy documents can’t do, but returns management software can. Ahead, learn why returns management solutions are increasingly important for customer engagement, plus the key capabilities you need to scale.


## What is returns management software?


A returns management software solution applies your return rules to determine whether an item qualifies for a refund, exchange, or store credit. The platform can then generate a label, offer a refund or exchange, calculate fees, and keep the customer updated. Once the item comes back, it helps teams track its condition and decide whether to restock, repair, resell, recycle, or dispose of it.


The category often gets confused with three adjacent systems that either sit upstream or downstream of it:


- **Reverse logistics.** A[reverse logistics](https://www.shopify.com/enterprise/blog/reverse-logistics) software solution handles what happens physically after a return is authorized. Your returns management software typically sits a layer above this; it owns the customer-facing decision of whether a return is eligible and what the customer gets back, then hands the physical movement to reverse logistics systems or a 3PL.
- **Order management system (OMS).** An OMS is built for the forward transaction: placing, processing, and fulfilling a purchase. It can integrate returns management software to keep inventory and orders in sync.
- **Help desk.** A help desk gives customers a place to ask about a return and gives agents a ticket to answer it. But it doesn’t enforce return policy automatically, check eligibility rules, flag fraud patterns, or route items to a warehouse.


That means returns can belong to several teams at once. For instance, customer experience teams manage shopper questions and exceptions; ecommerce and operations teams set return rules and workflows; logistics and warehouse teams receive, inspect, and route products; finance reconciles refunds and fees; while fraud teams review suspicious behavior.


## Benefits of returns management software


Returns management software helps owners protect margins, keep more customers, and reduce manual work. In 2026, it also helps businesses meet new customer experience and compliance expectations.


Since[June 19, 2026](https://finance.yahoo.com/small-business/articles/returns-compliance-becomes-retail-priority-074145005.html?guccounter=1&guce_referrer=aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cuZ29vZ2xlLmNvbS8&guce_referrer_sig=AQAAAClXozsreqmDWO6m0bp3KWlPPo4uhZRpArMsPCtA5WxN03g3N8e07OQi3aEZuOmGtxGxUItjNc07YG_fiTCQDOSfa3ALLaY7SYI5QXm0rbvt1BFpVzp8YY64d-5Moz2JC_iw8hMOWE2102UCke-bvhl-5HpeBZu6m5lcZlM2jg60) , businesses selling online to consumers in the European Union have been required to offer a simple digital way for shoppers to cancel eligible purchases and exercise their right of withdrawal. The function must be easy to find, collect the required order details, and send the customer an electronic confirmation.


Email and customer service can still support the process. But they can no longer be the only way customers exercise this right. The withdrawal option has to be built into the digital experience itself.


A reason retailers are looking at this category now is compliance; it’s projected to grow to $8.53 billion by 2026. The bigger opportunity is operational, the right system can help businesses recover value, control costs, improve customer experience, and apply stronger fraud controls.


But other than that, four pressures are converging on returns at once:


### Recover more value from returned inventory


Returned products need both processing and a clear path back into the business. McKinsey estimates that retailers spend[$200 billion each year](https://www.mckinsey.com/industries/logistics/our-insights/from-cost-center-to-competitive-advantage-modernizing-reverse-logistics-with-ai) trying to recover value from returned products. Software can make those decisions easier when returns move across warehouses, stores, markets, and third-party logistics providers.


### Protect margin beyond the refund


The National Retail Federation (NRF) and Happy Returns estimated that retailers received[$849.9 billion in returns](https://nrf.com/research/2025-retail-returns-landscape) from 2025 sales. Of those, online return rates were higher than the overall average, at an estimated 19.3% of ecommerce sales. Returns software helps reduce manual work and added costs tied to return shipping, inspection, customer support, restocking, and fraud and policy abuse.


### Make returns easier to keep customers coming back


A smoother return experience can support loyalty. Radial found that[61%](https://www.radial.com/insights/the-new-guide-to-returns-2026#:~:text=44%25%20of%20brands%20describe%20managing%20returns%20fraud%20and%20abuse%20as%20the%20biggest%20pain%20point%20in%20their%20current%20returns%20process) of consumers become less interested in shopping with a retailer if they have to pay return fees. It also found that up to 71% would rather keep an unwanted product and not buy from the brand again than deal with a frustrating return. Returns software helps reduce that friction with clearer workflows and faster resolution.


### Apply stronger controls as volume grows


Manual review becomes harder to maintain as return volume increases. As ReBound returns product manager Wouter ten Heggeler[says in Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260618001633/en/Retailers-Lose-%C2%A329-Million-to-Returns-Fraud-Across-1-Million-Orders-as-New-ReBound-Data-Reveals-Industry-Blind-Spot) , that the returns process has become a blind spot for retailers. That’s why 44% of brands now call returns fraud and abuse their single biggest operational pain point, per Radial. Returns software can help businesses enforce policies more consistently and spot abuse earlier.


If you’re on Shopify, a Built for Shopify–certified app like[EU Withdrawal Button & Form](https://apps.shopify.com/eu-widerrufsbutton) builds the withdrawal button directly into checkout, without requiring your customers to log in, alongside broader return and exchange automation.


The payoff, however, isn’t limited to just returns.[Asphalte](https://www.shopify.com/case-studies/asphalte) , a French apparel brand built on a pre-order model, resells surplus pre-order returns and cancellations through Le Magasin, their first permanent store.


“We adopted Shopify for what it does best: the smooth processing of orders, exchanges, and returns, as well as its efficient payment process,” says Benjamin Mateo, chief revenue officer.


## Key features of the best returns management software


[G2’s Returns Management category](https://www.g2.com/categories/returns-management) sets six qualifying criteria a product must meet to be listed in the category at all. So, when comparing vendors, test each one against all six categories individually.


### 1. Configurable return policies


The platform has to let you create and manage return policies for different product categories. That means the policy engine needs to flex across product type, order value, customer segment, and geography.


Shopify’s[return and cancellation rules](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/fulfillment/managing-orders/returns/return-rules#return-rules) cover this at the settings level. You set a return window—14, 30, 90 days, unlimited, or a custom number—and choose whether it starts from each item’s own delivery date or from delivery of the last item in the order.


For sellers operating across regions,[rules can be set per market](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/fulfillment/managing-orders/returns/return-rules#per-market-rules) instead of storewide. For example, if you’re selling to EU consumers, you can set a return window of at least 14 days from delivery of the final item, allow cancellations until fulfillment, and extend deadlines that fall on weekends or holidays. This supports the[EU right-of-withdrawal requirements](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/fulfillment/managing-orders/returns/return-rules#eu-right-of-withdrawal) that took effect on June 19, 2026, although these Shopify settings do not, by themselves, guarantee legal compliance.


### 2. Self-service and automated return workflows


G2’s second criterion covers workflows to create, confirm, or cancel a return—the mechanics of what happens after a request comes in. Shopify[lets customers request cancellations for unshipped items](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/fulfillment/managing-orders/returns/self-serve-returns/setup) and returns for delivered items through their customer account or order status page.


On the automation side,[Shopify Flow](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/shopify-flow) handles the bulk of the work.


Shopify Flow triggers on the specific status a return is in—[requested](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/shopify-flow/reference/triggers/return-requested) ,[approved](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/shopify-flow/reference/triggers/return-approved) ,[declined](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/shopify-flow/reference/triggers/return-declined) ,[cancelled](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/shopify-flow/reference/triggers/return-cancelled) ,[reopened](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/shopify-flow/reference/triggers/return-reopened) , or[closed](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/shopify-flow/reference/triggers/return-closed) —alongside the existing[refund-created](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/shopify-flow/reference/triggers/refund-created) trigger. A[cancel-return](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/shopify-flow/reference/actions/cancel-return) action pairs with these to handle returns that stall out, like when a customer says they’ll send an item back and never do.


You can use these triggers to build workflows such as[alerting a team in Slack](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/shopify-flow/reference/connectors/send-slack-message) when a customer requests a return,[cancelling inactive returns](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/shopify-flow/reference/actions/cancel-return) after a set period, or[releasing exchange items for fulfillment](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/shopify-flow/reference/actions/release-fulfillment-hold) once the original return has been closed and the order has no outstanding balance.


Take[SilkSilky](https://silksilky.com/) . The apparel brand used Shopify Flow for flagging high-risk orders and placing them on payment hold before fulfillment, so a team member could request partial card verification before the order shipped. The brand’s return rate fell from 7% to 8% to[less than 1%](https://www.shopify.com/case-studies/silksilky) as a result.


### 3. Flexible refunds, credits, and replacements


G2’s third criterion is about coordinating refunds, credits, or product replacements. Shopify supports these options[natively in the admin](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/fulfillment/managing-orders/returns/creating-returns#refund) . Your staff can add an exchange or replacement item to a return, refund the customer to their original payment method, issue store credit, or split the refund between both.


The store credit can also be given an expiry date. Shopify automatically works out the balance when the returned and replacement products have different values.


Shopify’s[Spring 2026 Editions](https://www.shopify.com/editions/spring2026) extends this flexibility in stores. Refunds, exchanges, and new purchases can now run through[one Shopify POS cart](https://www.shopify.com/editions/spring2026#:~:text=Returns%20and%20exchanges,Shopify%C2%A0POS.) rather than separate transaction flows. Your staff adds the replacement or newly purchased products, and Shopify POS calculates whether the customer should receive a refund or pay the remaining balance.


The same release adjusts two things on the admin side:


- [Discounts can now be applied directly on the refund page](https://changelog.shopify.com/posts/apply-discounts-to-items-on-the-refund-page) , so a partial refund with a goodwill discount attached doesn’t throw off sales and tax reporting.
- Returns and upsells now calculate pricing the same way checkout does, so an exchange with a price difference charges the right amount automatically.


### 4. Commerce and operations integrations


G2’s fourth criterion is integration with ecommerce or related platforms; integration deep enough to power capability that wouldn’t otherwise exist. G2 notes that returns platforms commonly integrate with ecommerce, order management, warehouse management, inventory, and accounting software.


[Princess Polly](https://us.princesspolly.com/) , for example, integrated[Loop](https://apps.shopify.com/loop-returns) with Shopify so customers could use their return credit to select another product directly from its website. The integration passed data between the customer-facing return experience and back-office operations, while giving the brand returns insights and customer feedback.


Princess Polly reported a[55% year-over-year reduction in out-of-stock rates](https://www.shopify.com/case-studies/princess-polly-2) through instant exchanges and an average upsell of $1.23 per return through Loop’s Shop Now feature.


“Thanks to Shopify Plus and Loop, we’ve seen a huge improvement in our return experience, with customers telling us first-hand that they appreciate how easy it is. And not only is it improving our customer loyalty—it’s also driving a significant uptick in revenue,” says Alexandria Collis, director of operations.


When comparing Shopify’s almost[200 returns and exchanges apps](https://apps.shopify.com/categories/orders-and-shipping-returns-and-warranty-returns-and-exchanges/all) , look for the[Built for Shopify](https://www.shopify.com/partners/blog/built-for-shopify) designation. From[December 1, 2026](https://shopify.dev/changelog/built-for-shopify-requirements-for-returns-and-exchanges-and-subscription-apps) , returns and exchange apps offering buyer-facing self-service must use Shopify’s Customer Account API to retain that status. This gives customers one secure sign-in across their account, storefront, and connected app rather than requiring a separate login for the returns portal.


### 5. End-to-end return tracking


G2’s fifth criterion is tracking returned items from the moment they’re picked up to the moment they’re received.


On the customer side, Shopify’s[order status page](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/fulfillment/setup/order-status-page/understanding-order-status-pages) updates in real time as a return moves through its stages. When something’s gone wrong in processing, rather than leaving the customer guessing, the return is flagged with a distinct “Return failed” status.


Returns tracking works with[hundreds of carriers automatically](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/fulfillment/setup/order-status-page/order-tracking) —DHL, UPS, FedEx, Canada Post, Australia Post, and EVRi among them—so this functionality isn’t limited to labels purchased directly through Shopify.


On your side, Shopify[assigns each order a return status](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/fulfillment/managing-orders/order-status#return-status-desc) : Return requested, Return in progress, Inspection complete, or Returned. You can filter orders by these statuses to find requests that need approval, returns awaiting processing, or refunds that still need to be issued.


### 6. Returns analytics and business impact


G2’s sixth and final criterion is analyzing the value of returns and their impact on revenue.


Shopify now uses[category-specific return reasons](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/fulfillment/managing-orders/returns/creating-returns#create-a-return) across the admin, Shopify POS, self-serve returns, and compatible third-party apps. So, a footwear customer might select “too narrow,” while a beauty customer can report an issue with shade or coverage. You can then use those patterns in data to improve product descriptions and sizing guidance or work with suppliers on recurring quality issues.


[Shopify’s Analytics](https://www.shopify.com/analytics) further connects those reasons to physical and financial return metrics. Merchants can review the quantity and percentage of units physically returned, the reason attached to each line item, and the value of sales reversals by product.


Returns reduce net and total sales in Shopify’s[sales reports](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/reports-and-analytics/shopify-reports/report-types/default-reports/sales-report) , making their effect on revenue visible alongside the products that generated it.


And because return reason data feeds into the same operational picture as sales and receiving data, you can ask Shopify’s AI assistant,[Sidekick](https://www.shopify.com/sidekick) , direct questions—what’s not moving, what to reorder this week—and get an answer informed by why products are coming back.


## How do you choose the best returns management platform?


While popular options in G2’s returns management category include Narvar, ReturnGO, ClickPost, parcelLab, AfterShip Returns, and Happy Returns, these platforms aren’t interchangeable. Some focus on self-service portals and workflow automation, while others extend into broader post-purchase communication, carrier management, physical drop-off networks, and reverse logistics.


### Decide whether you need another platform


Before shopping for software, check what your commerce platform already supports.


Shopify can manage return and cancellation rules, customer return requests, refunds, store credit, exchanges, return shipping information, inventory updates, and return-reason analytics natively. Shopify Flow can automate tasks when a return is requested, approved, declined, canceled, closed, or reopened. And Shopify’s rich network of apps and APIs extends the platform:


“We have around 18% EBITDA margins, which is way above average for an apparel brand,”[says SuitShop cofounder Diana Ganz](https://www.shopify.com/case-studies/suitshop) . “We attribute much of this to the efficiencies enabled by Shopify and its ecosystem of apps.”


A dedicated returns platform becomes more useful when:


- You need more advanced self-service exchanges and exchange incentives
- You manage complex routing across warehouses, stores, and 3PLs
- You need a large international carrier or physical drop-off network
- You run returns management across several ecommerce platforms


### Ask the same questions of every vendor


Once you’ve answered the question of whether or not to add more software, use G2’s six category criteria to compare shortlisted platforms consistently:


1. **How configurable are your return policies?** Can you set different windows, fees, eligibility rules, and final-sale exceptions by product, market, channel, or customer?
2. **What parts of the workflow can you automate?** Can customers start a return themselves? Which requests can be automatically approved or declined? Can the platform generate labels, notify teams and customers, close inactive returns, and send unusual cases to an employee for review?
3. **Which resolutions do you support?** Can you offer refunds, store credit, replacements, variant exchanges, or exchanges for another product?
4. **How well do you integrate with our operations?** Check the vendor’s connections with your ecommerce platform, POS, order and inventory management systems, ERP, warehouse software, 3PLs, carriers, customer support tools, and accounting systems.
5. **Can you track the complete return?** The platform should show what has happened from the initial request through shipping, receipt, inspection, restocking, exchange, and refund.
6. **What can you learn from the returns data?** Look for return rates, reasons, product-level trends, processing times, shipping costs, refund value, exchange uptake, retained revenue, and inventory outcomes.

## Returns management software FAQ


### What is return management?


Return management is the returns process for receiving, reviewing, tracking, refunding, exchanging, and restocking returned products.


Returns software can use automated workflows to approve requests, generate return labels, provide real-time tracking, and update warehouse management systems, which improves operational efficiency and increases customer satisfaction.


### How big is the return management market?


Estimates vary because research firms define the category differently. The Insight Partners valued the global returns management software market at $1.58 billion in 2023 and projects it will reach[$3.29 billion by 2031](https://www.theinsightpartners.com/reports/returns-management-software-market) .


### What is the difference between customer return and RTO?


A customer return happens after an order has been delivered and the buyer sends it back.


Return to origin (RTO) happens when delivery cannot be completed, so the carrier sends the parcel back to its original pickup location.


### What is the average customer return rate?


US retailers estimated that[15.8%](https://nrf.com/media-center/press-releases/consumers-expected-to-return-nearly-850-billion-in-merchandise-in-2025) of sales would be returned in 2025. The rate for ecommerce returns was higher, at an estimated 19.3% of online sales, although rates vary by product category and retailer.


### What is RTO dropshipping?


RTO dropshipping occurs when a supplier ships an order directly to the customer, but delivery fails, and the parcel returns to the supplier or fulfillment origin.


Dropshippers may still pay outbound and return shipping costs, so order verification, fraud prevention, and AI-powered risk screening can help identify high-risk returns, mitigate risk, and support revenue retention.


### What is RTO and RVP in ecommerce?


RTO, or return to origin, happens when an order cannot be delivered and is sent back to the seller. RVP, or reverse pickup, happens after delivery, when a carrier collects an item that the customer wants to return or exchange. Some logistics providers use different terms for this second journey, such as DTO.


The best returns management solution should separate these flows so teams can analyze customer behavior, offer a flexible return resolution, and optimize returns. At higher support volume, automated updates can reduce support ticket volume, while fraud detection tools help flag fraudulent post-purchase activities.
