# AR Viewer Guide for Ecommerce: Implementation Tips

Augmented reality (AR) entered the public imagination with whimsy in the form of the game Pokémon Go, but its practical applications have since made it a valuable tool in a range of industries, including ecommerce. By projecting realistic 3D images into a customer’s own space or field of vision, AR viewer technology can make try-before-buying simpler, accessible, and more convenient for customers.


The AR-in-retail market is on a growth trajectory,[valued at $9.1 billion in 2025](https://www.kingsresearch.com/report/augmented-reality-in-retail-market-2981) and projected to grow to $67.7 billion by 2032. This expansion is being driven by AR viewers in furniture visualization and virtual try-on tools, which have been shown to[boost customers’ confidence](https://www.limelightdigital.co.uk/augmented-reality-statistics/) before purchase and to reduce return rates.


In this guide, learn how AR viewers work, how ecommerce brands use them, and steps to consider as you integrate AR features into your own online store.

## What is an AR viewer?


An AR viewer is technology that layers virtual objects over a real-world scene or image, blending digital content with a user’s physical environment. Through a combination of software and hardware built into many phones, tablets, and some AR-enabled wearables (like smart glasses and smart watches), an AR viewer maps a user’s surroundings so it can place 3D objects realistically within a scene.


In ecommerce, it helps shoppers visualize products in their own space or on themselves before purchasing and is designed to reduce uncertainty in buying decisions. AR viewers rely on several technologies working together:


-


**Sensors.** Device hardware collects information about the user’s surroundings and movement so the AR viewer can understand the environment.


-


**Scene** **mapping software.** Software analyzes that information to identify light and shadow, horizontal and vertical surfaces, and distances, creating a digital understanding of the environment.


-


**3D models** **.** The AR viewer places 3D models of products into the scene, giving users a real-world look at the items. These models can be created with 3D scanning tools or professional rendering software.


### Ecommerce applications of AR viewers


While AR viewers have applications across a variety of sectors, their ecommerce applications solve a long-running challenge for online shopping: increasing customer confidence when shoppers can’t see, test, or try products in person.


Common ecommerce applications for AR viewers include:


-


Showing how a sofa or desk looks in your living space


-


Visualizing a roof-mounted bike rack on your car


-


Previewing how a jacket or sweater might look while worn


-


Comparing paint colors on an accent wall


AR viewers can reduce the need for customers to order items like clothing or eyeglasses solely to evaluate them at home. That can lower return rates while reducing the shipping, packaging, and processing footprints associated with those returns.

### 1. Identify products that benefit most from AR


Not every product is equally suitable for AR viewing. But your catalog and recent analytics are both valuable troves of information that can help to shape your AR viewing strategy and 3D catalog.


AR viewers work especially well for products that benefit from trying on (like[clothes](https://www.shopify.com/blog/ar-try-on-clothes) ), or trying out in a customer’s own context that can be cumbersome or resource-intensive (like furniture). Style products and accessories that need to be matched to face shape or size are also great candidates for AR-based virtual try-on.


Other products that could benefit from AR viewing:


-


Ceiling fans, pendant lights, and other home décor that requires installation


-


Patio furniture


-


Area rugs


-


Accent tiles and wall art


-


Sunglasses and eyeglasses


-


Cosmetics


Your own content can offer helpful direction for curating your AR offerings, too. Do you frequently find the same products in abandoned carts? Are there size charts and item details on product pages that receive more clicks than others? These clues could point to products that customers need more contextual information about.


And your current returns and abandoned cart data offer valuable baselines around which to structure goals and metrics that will help you track the effectiveness of adding AR mode to your offerings.

Before shopping third-party vendors, it’s worth checking out what your existing stack has for AR compatibility. ### 3. Ensure your AR models depict your products accurately


To create an AR experience, you first need 3D models of your products that customers can place and view in their own environment. Depending on the tools you choose, you can make these models in-house or with[a professional partner](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/products/product-media/expert-3d-model) .


If your team is working with an AR software development kit (SDK), the SDK will provide specs and file types needed to build the 3D models. High-quality models start with accurate dimensions (in millimeters) and well-lit photos of each product. Use a professional camera and take clear, well-labeled photos from many angles. Small details or textures that are important to the product may require additional photos.


Once the 3D models are built, testing the model viewer will help verify whether the images load easily for customers and are accurate, believable, and clear. Ideally, a brand’s viewing experience will be similar across device types, so previewing 3D models across as many devices and operating systems as possible will help to identify whether any file revisions or adjustments are needed before going live.


Here are some key criteria to check:


-


Does the product appear at scale when placed in the room?


-


Are the edges of the object well-defined?


-


Are there any areas that are blurry or distorted?


### 4. Protect customer privacy and data


By gathering environmental or biometric data and accessing a user’s smartphone, desktop, or tablet camera, AR viewers also raise important privacy issues. While a brand’s privacy protections may vary depending on region and product, here are some general guidelines to follow to protect your customer’s data and maintain their trust:


-


**Data limits.** Limit what data you collect to the minimum required for the AR experience to function, and anonymize or aggregate user data to reduce the chance of identification.


-


**Encryption.** Use encryption and industry best practices such as two-factor authentication for any data you collect.


-


**Compliance.** Understand your region’s privacy laws and regulations. If you operate in multiple jurisdictions, adhere to the stronger of the regulations.


-


**Transparency.** Treat your customers as partners by offering a straightforward, simple[privacy notice](https://www.shopify.com/blog/website-privacy-policy) about what data you collect and what choices they have in how it is collected, accessed, and stored.


-


**Collaboration.** Make sure third-party vendors and partners are on the same privacy page. Since AR viewers can involve multiple companies and service providers, choose partners whose privacy and data policies align with your brand’s.


### 5. Promote adoption and gather feedback


Integrating[augmented reality](https://www.shopify.com/blog/what-is-ar) into your ecommerce store is meant to make your customers’ lives easier, so be sure they know about the feature and how to use it once it’s live. Ensure that AR features are visible in your website navigation bar and that relevant product pages and FAQs are updated to drive customers to the tool and instruct them how to use it. A simple “View In Your Space” button can direct visitors to try out the feature.


Stage a soft launch phase and gather feedback from trusted customers and peers before broadcasting the AR viewer more widely. For instance, you could ask customers how well the AR experience worked on their device, if there were any delays in loading the files, and whether they understood intuitively how to use the tool or needed more direction.


A soft launch also gives you the chance to celebrate your shop and offer[discount codes](https://www.shopify.com/blog/discounts-and-coupons) or prizes as you gather customer feedback.


### 6. Measure performance and refine your strategy


As your customers begin to interact with the AR technology and your featured products, the scene viewer on your site can also yield valuable digital insights for your brand. As you roll out AR viewing in your online store, ask:


-


Did abandoned cart rates decrease for AR-compatible products?


-


Did return rates change on AR-viewed products?


-


Which products did shoppers try out or try on most often?


-


On average, how much time did customers spend with products in augmented reality?


-


Did AR capabilities influence conversion rates?


Your developer team can also help identify what insights your AR tools can provide and how they can inform[key performance indicators (KPIs)](https://www.shopify.com/blog/what-is-kpi) . Study at least two quarters of AR-informed data before making significant changes to your strategy.


## AR viewer FAQ


### What does AR stand for?


AR stands for “augmented reality,” a versatile technology in which 3D images of objects are overlaid on a viewer’s environment to create a believable blended scene. It differs from virtual reality, which immerses a viewer in a wholly separate 3D environment through a headset or wearable tech.


### Does my phone have AR?


If you have a smartphone, there’s a good chance it can act as an AR viewer. Depending on the AR software that powers the AR app or program, it may run best on certain models or operating systems. Some viewers created for ecommerce allow you to build 3D models directly from your phone. For instance, users can create no-code AR models of products and adjust lighting directly through the Shopify app on iPhone 12 or later.


### How does an AR viewer work?


An AR viewer uses a mobile device’s hardware to map out the space and surfaces near the device. Specialized software code then processes that data to interpret lighting conditions, horizontal and vertical planes, and motion. The AR viewer then projects a rendered 3D model of an object into the scene.
