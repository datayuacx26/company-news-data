# Mobile vs. Desktop Conversion Rates: 2026 Comparison

Conversion rates measure how many visitors make a purchase on your website. Knowing your mobile versus desktop conversion rates helps you understand where your customers are finding you and how they shop.


Conversion rates matter because most online stores only convert a small fraction of the traffic they work hard to attract. According to[Dynamic Yield](https://marketing.dynamicyield.com/benchmarks/conversion-rate/) , the average global ecommerce conversion rate is 2.69%, meaning for every 1,000 shoppers who land on your site, roughly 27 buy something, while the other 973 leave without making a purchase. Even small improvements to that rate translate directly into more revenue from the same traffic.


This article breaks down benchmarks for desktop versus mobile conversion rates, explains why they matter for ecommerce businesses, and outlines the steps you can take to improve the mobile and desktop customer experience and drive sales.


## Mobile vs. desktop conversion benchmarks


A[conversion rate](https://www.shopify.com/blog/ecommerce-conversion-rate) is the percentage of visitors who complete a desired action on your website. In ecommerce, this usually means purchases—the share of visitors who buy after visiting your site. When analyzing this data, global benchmarks (average conversion rates across different devices and industries) can offer a useful point of reference.


Recent ecommerce benchmarks place global conversion rates in the low single digits, with desktop usually outperforming mobile.[Contentsquared](https://contentsquare.com/guides/digital-experience-benchmark/engagement/) ’s 2026 report found that desktop conversion rates averaged 3.4%, compared to 2% for mobile shoppers. Desktop users also spent over twice as much time on ecommerce sites as mobile users, even though mobile made up 70% of all ecommerce traffic.


That said, customers don’t always experience mobile and desktop as separate journeys. Many browse on their phone, then switch to a laptop to compare products or buy.[Recent studies](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1509/jm.17.0113) show shoppers are increasingly using multiple devices in a single purchase, making it important to design for both.


Benchmarks are most useful in context. According to[Dynamic Yield](https://marketing.dynamicyield.com/benchmarks/conversion-rate/) , industries with expensive products, like luxury and jewelry, saw conversion rates below 0.9% in 2025, while food and beverage averaged around 5.5%. What you sell, and who you sell to, should guide which industry benchmarks you use.

## Factors that impact conversion rates


- Checkout complexity
- Product discovery and navigation
- Website speed and performance
- Cost transparency
- Consumer trust
- CTA effectiveness


Here are common issues that can affect both desktop and mobile browsers, and approaches for addressing them:


### Checkout complexity


A complicated checkout process is one of the most common drags on conversion rates. Long forms, confusing payment options, or unexpected questions create friction points that push customers away.


[One-page checkouts](https://www.shopify.com/blog/one-page-checkout) and accelerated checkouts that support Google Pay, Apple Pay, and[Shop Pay](https://www.shopify.com/shop-pay) can significantly expedite the process. Adding[autocomplete](https://www.shopify.com/blog/google-autocomplete) functionality is another way to save customers time—especially for mobile shopping, where typing in personal data is more tedious than on desktop.


“Something that Shopify launched that I think has been transformational has been Shop Pay,” says[Curious Elixirs](https://curiouselixirs.com/) founder JW Wiseman, on an episode of the[Shopify Masters podcast](https://www.shopify.com/blog/curious-elixirs-raising-the-bar-for-non-alcoholic-beverages) . “Shop Pay has been absolutely incredible. I mean, not only as a business owner, but also as a consumer of different products. It just makes it so much easier to say yes.”


### Product discovery and navigation


A visually distinctive website can attract new customers and help define your brand, but that means little if the design gets in the way of the user’s journey. Product and site navigation build the pathway that guides customers to checkout. Menus, search pages, product filters, categories, and page layouts should all help customers find the right product quickly—not force them to decode how your site works.


For Erika Geraerts, founder of the Australian beauty brand[Fluff](https://www.itsallfluff.com/) , this meant rethinking what she’d originally seen as a bold creative vision. “We had a very intricate custom-built website when we first launched Fluff seven years ago, and I really wanted to do something that was aesthetically different,” Erika says on *[Shopify Masters](https://www.shopify.com/blog/fluff-opens-four-times-a-year)* .


“I think we were ahead of our time and actually created a website that was not usable for the consumer.” Her customers, she says, “weren’t familiar with something that was more of an art piece or a digital experience than just a straightforward checkout.” After identifying several friction points, Erika decided to redesign the website for a more user-friendly shopping experience.


### Website speed and performance


Website performance and[site speed](https://www.shopify.com/blog/site-speed-importance) can have a major impact on conversion rates, especially for mobile users. A site that loads quickly for desktop users may feel sluggish for mobile customers who have slower connections and smaller screens.


When[optimizing your site’s speed and performance](https://www.shopify.com/enterprise/blog/site-performance-page-speed-ecommerce) , start with the most heavily visited webpages—landing pages, product pages, and carts. Focus on pages that anchor major moments in the[customer journey](https://www.shopify.com/blog/how-to-map-out-the-customer-journey-for-your-retail-business) , like a first visit or the final steps to purchase. Ecommerce platforms like Shopify offer a fast underlying infrastructure and a library of mobile-friendly,[responsive themes](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/online-store/themes/theme-support/troubleshooting) .


### Cost transparency


A shopper may be ready to buy a product based on the product price, then hesitate once taxes, shipping, handling, and other fees appear. The result is often higher[bounce rates](https://www.shopify.com/blog/bounce-rate) and abandoned carts. Clearly displaying these fees early—ideally on the first checkout page—or building fees into the product price helps customers see the full expense upfront.


Claudia Snoh, cofounder of coffee concentrate company[Kloo](https://drinkkloo.com/) , noticed this firsthand. “We did have a lot of abandoned checkouts during our soft launch period. Not abandoned carts—abandoned checkouts. Which means that they had the full intention of purchasing, but they probably saw the shipping fee,” she says on[Shopify Masters](https://www.shopify.com/blog/kloo-coffee-pre-launch-strategy-tips) . After reviewing its conversion data, Kloo changed how it distributed the costs. “I think the key takeaway that we learned is that no one likes to pay for shipping.”


### Consumer trust


Customers need to trust your product descriptions, reviews, payment options, and that your business will follow through on return and shipping policies. Overpromising can create short-term interest but can lead to frustrated customers, bad reviews, and fewer repeat purchases.


Charlotte Palermino, cofounder and CEO of skin care brand[Dieux](https://www.dieuxskin.com/) , spoke to the dangers of “holy grail” marketing on *[Shopify Masters](https://www.shopify.com/blog/dieux-skin-build-trust-nyc-on-location)* : “I find that when a customer feels duped into buying something, there’s a lot of bad blood there, and I don’t want that with consumers. I want people to understand what our products do and what they don’t do.”


In her experience, setting clear expectations builds loyalty and opens up honest conversations with customers. As Charlotte puts it, transparency “creates a different playing field, and a different conversation, where it’s more open.” She’s especially critical of companies that use definitive product claims: “I cringe when I hear ‘holy grail.’ Like, for who?”


### CTA effectiveness


Charlie Bowes-Lyon, cofounder and CMO of the bath and body care retailer[Wild,](https://wearewild.com/) describes improving conversion rates by[A/B testing](https://www.shopify.com/blog/ab-testing) the website’s calls to action: “We’re constantly doing A/B tests or looking at ways to improve our website and convert higher and higher,” he says on an episode of[Shopify Masters](https://www.shopify.com/blog/wild-launch-strategy) . “It could be as simple as changing your CTA—your button on the homepage which gets people to buy—from ‘Buy Now’ to ‘Learn More.’ Test those two against each other, split the traffic, and see which converts better.”


For retailers, a practical approach is to make CTA testing specific and measurable. If you A/B test “Buy Now” versus “Add to cart,” for example, you can compare results across different device types. If mobile users respond better to “Buy Now” and desktop devices prefer “Add to cart,” update your mobile pages to feature "Buy Now" more prominently.


## Mobile vs. desktop conversion rates FAQ


### What is meant by mobile conversion rate?


Mobile conversion rates measure the percentage of visitors on mobile devices who complete a desired action on a website. In mobile commerce, this usually means completing a purchase. Tracking this metric can help you understand how customers interact with your mobile site and identify opportunities to improve mobile conversion rates and increase ecommerce sales.


### How do I calculate mobile conversion rate?


To calculate your mobile conversion rate, divide the number of mobile conversions by the total mobile traffic, then multiply that number by 100. For example, if 50 out of 1,000 visitors purchased an item from your mobile site, then your mobile conversion rate is 5%. This means 5% of website visitors completed a mobile purchase.


### What is a good conversion rate?


A good conversion rate depends on your industry, product prices, customer base, mix of desktop usage and mobile usage, and the customer’s journey. The average global ecommerce conversion rate is 2.69%. For useful benchmarks, it is often more helpful to compare conversion rates to similar ecommerce businesses in your industry, or to compare against your own previous conversion rates to track rate changes over time.
