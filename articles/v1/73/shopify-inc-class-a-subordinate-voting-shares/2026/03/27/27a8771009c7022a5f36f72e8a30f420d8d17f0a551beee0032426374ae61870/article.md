# How To Start a Jewelry Business: 9-Step Guide (2026)

For many creative people, a hobby like jewelry making is a great way to relax after a long day. But if your creations start getting attention from friends and strangers, you might ask yourself: Could my[hobby become a business](https://www.shopify.com/blog/hobby-to-business) ?


Learning to start a jewelry business requires understanding the industry. For one thing: It's growing. The worldwide jewelry market is expected to reach[almost $579 billion by 2033](https://www.grandviewresearch.com/industry-analysis/jewelry-market) . A huge market, yes, but still one with exciting opportunities for newcomers to find an untapped niche or bring a fresh perspective.


If you're looking for a[creative way to make money](https://www.shopify.com/blog/creative-ways-make-money) , read on. This guide contains expert advice on how to start your jewelry business, based on the experiences of jewelry designers who built their brands from scratch.


## How to start a jewelry business in 9 steps


Follow these nine steps to start your jewelry business.


## 1. Find your niche in the jewelry industry


[Wolf & Moon](https://www.wolfandmoon.com/)


The jewelry industry is full of niches and subcategories—everything from cheap and cheerful plastic beads to fine-art precious metals and gems. One of the first questions to ask yourself is what type of jewelry you want to sell.


Opt for fine jewelry, costume jewelry (also known as fashion jewelry), or something in between. Each category has its own materials, production process, price point, and customer profile.


Let's look at the qualities associated with the main types of jewelry.


#### Fine jewelry


Fine jewelry is at the top end of the market and has the following qualities:


- Made with precious metals and gems.
- Higher price points.
- Made with master craftsmanship.
- Rarely mass-produced.
- For luxury, wedding, and special occasion customers.


#### Fashion or costume jewelry


Most often, you'll find costume jewelry sold at chain boutiques and[online stores](https://www.shopify.com/blog/opening-an-online-store-tips) . You can spot it by these properties:


- Designs based on current fashion trends.
- Made of beads, wire, plated metal, plastic, and synthetic gems.
- Lower price points.
- Often mass produced.
- For the everyday customer.


#### Artist-designed jewelry


This category captures everything in between, like handmade jewelry requiring specialized skill and mid-range materials. Bespoke jewelry will have the following qualities:


- Varied materials mixing inexpensive and precious (textile, metals, semi-precious gems, wood,[3D printing](https://www.shopify.com/blog/3d-printer-business-ideas) , etc.).
- Unique designs (collectible, made to order, one of a kind).
- Skilled craftsmanship.
- Mid-range price points correlated to a designer's popularity.
- Occasion or statement pieces for collectors and gift-giving customers.


[Camille Enrico](https://www.camilleenrico.com/) jewelry is known for its unique combination of metals and textiles—giving pieces instant recognizability and cohesiveness with each other. *[Camille Enrico](https://www.camilleenrico.com/)*


Once you've narrowed down the broad category for your jewelry business, it's time to[carve out a niche](https://www.shopify.com/blog/6187532-dont-follow-your-passion-a-smarter-way-to-find-a-product-to-sell) for your products.


Start by defining your[ideal customer profile](https://www.shopify.com/blog/ideal-customer-profile) (budget, values, preferences, location, shopping habits, etc.) and decide if your products are occasion-specific (weddings, parties) or intended for everyday wear. This will determine how you market them to your[target audience](https://www.shopify.com/blog/target-audience) .


[Enso](https://ensorings.com/) found its niche with an audience looking for alternatives to metal rings: active customers and those with metal allergies. *[Enso](https://ensorings.com/)*


#### Jewelry products and categories


Once you've narrowed down your market, consider whether you'll offer an entire product line or focus on a single item. For example, you may want to niche down and specialize in engagement rings, or go broad with a collection of mix-and-match fashion pieces.


Consider the following product types for your jewelry line:


- Rings (sized or adjustable)
- Necklaces (chokers, pendants)
- Bracelets (bangles, cuffs)
- Earrings (drop, clip-on, studs)
- Engagement and wedding rings
- Watches
- Toe rings, body chains, and other body jewelry
- Piercings (nose, navel)


Also consider complementary products and categories. Jewelry brand[Wolf & Moon](https://www.wolfandmoon.com/) sells its signature styles in both hair accessories and bags.

## 2. Research market trends


Before you[start a business](https://www.shopify.com/blog/how-to-start-a-business) , it's essential to know how to reach your audience. In other words, you'll need to find a market fit for your products.


#### Following jewelry trends


Researching trends is one way to validate your chosen market. Read fashion and jewelry blogs and follow fashion influencers and popular TikTok creators in the space to stay on top of trends for the upcoming season.


Look to other jewelry designers for inspiration. And, don't be afraid to dig into some data. With[Google Trends](https://www.shopify.com/blog/trends-google) , you'll find global search volume for different search queries.


*Google Trends*


Another approach is to analyze adjacent[consumer trends](https://www.shopify.com/blog/consumer-trends) , such as the appetite for customization, like engraving. Or tap into the growing trend of sustainable shopping habits by using ethically sourced materials.


Of course, you don't have to just follow jewelry trends—you could start your own. That's what[Biko](https://www.ilovebiko.com/) founder and designer Corrine Anestopoulos did, launching a collection based on her personal style.


[Biko](https://www.ilovebiko.com/) jewelry sits somewhere in between costume and fine, selling high-quality designs made for everyday wear. *[Biko](https://www.ilovebiko.com/)*

## 3. Define your brand

Fashion purchases are often emotional, and emerging brands can win customers by connecting on a personal level, even through something as simple as a[business name](https://www.shopify.com/tools/business-name-generator) .

Your brand guidelines will inform everything you do as a brand. This essential document will capture everything from voice and tone to[mission and values](https://www.shopify.com/blog/difference-between-mission-and-vision) . ## 4. Produce or source your jewelry products


How you'll develop your products—by hand or in a factory, at home or outsourced—depends on their complexity, price point, materials, and required skill level. Generally speaking, there are five ways to[produce](https://www.shopify.com/blog/production-planning) or[source](https://www.shopify.com/blog/product-sourcing-apps) jewelry:


- Handcrafted jewelry production: fine and one of a kind.
- Handcrafted jewelry production: fashion and costume.
- Jewelry production: factory outsourced.
- Jewelry production: print on demand.
- Jewelry curation: dropshipping and resale.


#### Handcrafted jewelry production: fine and one of a kind


[Rachel Boston](https://www.rachelboston.co.uk/)


Handcrafting one-of-a-kind jewelry can be one of the most involved yet versatile of the production methods.


Depending on materials and design, some methods of bespoke jewelry production require specialized training/certification and expensive equipment. You may need to learn some of these methods, including:


- Soldering
- Silver/goldsmithing
- Casting
- 3D printing
- Laser cutting
- Leather tooling
- Weaving
- Gemstone setting


Consider starting with free online tutorials to learn how to make jewelry. You can then upgrade to paid classes or apprentice under an experienced craftsperson to expand your skill set.


#### 2. Handcrafted jewelry production: fashion and costume


One Chew Three creates silicone fashion jewelry for moms with teething babies. *One Chew Three*


Handmade costume jewelry often involves the assembly of existing elements—chain, wire, beads, cast pendants—and doesn't require special training or equipment beyond basic hand tools.


This type of business is easier to scale, as you can purchase elements in bulk and create templates for assembly by hired production staff.


If design and production are the elements bringing you joy—and not the actual task of making jewelry—outsource or hire for other roles to help you scale the creative work.


Biko founder Corrine reaches out to local universities with renowned fashion communication programs to find her interns.


"I don't ever look for anyone who's interested in design," she says. "What I need is someone to help me free up my time so that I can be designing more."

#### Jewelry production: factory outsourced


If you choose not to make jewelry with your own hands, have your designs[manufactured](https://www.shopify.com/blog/what-is-manufacturing-definition) by someone else. While not appropriate for fine or one-of-a-kind jewelry, it's a cost-effective option for fashion jewelry produced in larger quantities.


- **Local manufacturing** has the benefit of a "made local" appeal and creates opportunities to build relationships and have factory oversight. The downsides can include higher costs, limited choice, and production volume limitations.
- **Overseas manufacturing** can be a good solution if you want to keep costs low (for those doing high volumes) and have more choice in factories. Possible challenges include communication barriers, less oversight, and fulfillment issues caused by supply chain delays.


Accurate sketches or 3D renderings of your designs, along with technical specifications, may be necessary if you're[outsourcing to a manufacturer](https://www.shopify.com/blog/13975985-how-to-find-a-manufacturer-or-supplier-for-your-product-idea) .


#### Jewelry production: print on demand


The most hands-off jewelry production method involves uploading your designs to a[print-on-demand](https://www.shopify.com/blog/print-on-demand) service. With print on demand, you create a mockup of your product, which is then 3D-printed or recreated in stainless steel, wood, plastic, gold, or silver and shipped directly to your customers.


The[Shopify App Store](https://apps.shopify.com/categories/finding-products-sourcing-options-print-on-demand-pod) is home to print-on-demand apps that can produce and ship custom products, from jewelry to t-shirts and mugs.


#### Jewelry curation: dropshipping and resale


If you're not creative but still have a passion for jewelry, consider curating jewelry pieces from other designers to sell in your online store. You can carry inventory and ship it yourself, or opt for a[jewelry dropshipping](https://www.shopify.com/blog/dropshipping-jewelry) model.


With[dropshipping](https://www.shopify.com/blog/what-is-dropshipping) , there's no need to carry inventory, making it a popular way to[start a business with minimal funds](https://www.shopify.com/blog/start-business-without-money) . Receive orders through your store, then pass them to your dropshipping supplier for fulfillment and shipping.


Jewelry entrepreneurs can also[source products from verified Shopify brands](https://www.shopify.com/source-products) using Shopify Collective—product sourcing built into Shopify—with better margins and faster domestic shipping than traditional overseas suppliers.


## 5. Set up a jewelry business studio or workspace


If you elect to make your jewelry in-house, you'll need a workshop space for you and your team. When setting up this space, consider the following:


- **Maneuverability.** Consider the flow of the space, especially if the assembly has multiple steps. For example, you might choose to move from one station to the next in a logical sequence.
- **Safety.** Some chemicals or tools involved in jewelry making require proper ventilation and safety precautions. Check with local laws governing the use of these substances and processes.
- **Storage.** Well-organized storage with multiple compartments is essential for small parts.


"A jeweler's bench, a flex shaft, and your safety would be the most important considerations," says Gillian Johnson, founder of Canadian jewelry brand[Hawkly](http://www.hawkly.ca/) . "The flex shaft is a wonderful and versatile tool that you can use for drilling, finishing, and polishing your pieces."


She also advises using a face mask and safety goggles, and for those with long hair to pull it back so it doesn't get caught.


If your production team is larger or your requirements are complex—as with metal work or silversmithing workshops—consider using a commercial space. If it's out of your budget at launch, look into co-op studio space or[share the costs](https://www.shopify.com/blog/119535173-day-in-the-life-present-day-gifts) of space and equipment with other creatives. Gillian splits her studio with two other businesses.


Space sharing also helps combat[entrepreneur loneliness](https://www.shopify.com/blog/118533829-feeling-lonely-5-ways-to-cure-the-entrepreneurship-blues) , something Corrine is familiar with.


"After doing Biko full-time for a while, I ended up working at a retail clothing store once a week just to not be lonely at home in my studio," she says.


#### Jewelry equipment and tools


There are several online resources for jewelry-making materials and equipment, including[wholesale](https://www.shopify.com/blog/selling-wholesale) sellers, equipment leasers, and suppliers of precious gemstones.


Gillian Johnson, founder of[Hawkly](http://www.hawkly.ca/) , relies heavily on word of mouth when it comes to her[wholesale suppliers](https://www.shopify.com/blog/wholesale-suppliers) .


"Don't be afraid to ask other designers where they find their supplies," she says.


She also attends trade shows and industry events to continue to expand her network and discover new materials.


"My favorite place to source the newest stones and silver findings is the Tucson Gem and Jewelry Show," Gillian says. "There are thousands of vendors from all over the world."

## How to start a jewelry business FAQ


### How do you start a jewelry business online?


To start a successful jewelry business online, follow these steps:


1. Find your niche.
2. Research the market and trends.
3. Write a business plan.
4. Define your brand.
5. Produce or source jewelry products.
6. Take professional photos of your products.
7. Build your online store to sell jewelry.
8. Market your jewelry company.
9. Scale your business.


### Do I need a business license to sell jewelry online?


It's important that you consult local regulations governing small businesses of any description. In many cases, though, you do not require a[business license](https://www.shopify.com/blog/business-license) to set up an online jewelry store to sell your products. In some regions, you may need a tax number, but this also may depend on how much income you're generating. Consult an accountant and/or business lawyer while you establish your business. Remember to ask about business insurance and any other legal requirements.


### What are the basic tools for jewelry making businesses?


The basic tools for a jewelry making business depend on the type of jewelry you plan to produce. You can start a jewelry company with almost no upfront cost or equipment if you take the[print-on-demand](https://www.shopify.com/blog/print-on-demand) approach. Fashion jewelry that involves mostly the assembly of pre-made components may require simple hand tools like pliers. However, if you plan to create fine jewelry products, you may require expensive and highly specific equipment.


### How much does it cost to start a jewelry business?


The cost of starting a jewelry business is relative to the complexity of the production model you choose. You can start your own business with a couple hundred dollars to cover basic supplies and make jewelry from your kitchen table. Or, you can invest tens of thousands in equipment and expensive raw materials and start a fine jewelry-making business from a dedicated studio. A successful business relies on understanding[how much money you will need](https://www.shopify.com/blog/cost-to-start-business) upfront and an accurate picture of your[cash flow](https://www.shopify.com/blog/cash-flow-management) .
