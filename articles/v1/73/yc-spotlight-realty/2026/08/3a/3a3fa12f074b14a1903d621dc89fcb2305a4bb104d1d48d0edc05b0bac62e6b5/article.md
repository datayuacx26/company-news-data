# The Worst Things About Living in Durham, NC

# The Worst Things About Living in Durham, NC (According to Someone Who's Lived Here for 32 Years)


If you search for videos or articles about Durham, North Carolina, you'll find plenty that highlight the city's award-winning restaurants, thriving downtown, beautiful parks, and booming job market.


Those things are all true.


But if you're considering moving to Durham, it's just as[important to understand the downsides.](https://youtu.be/yEPT7noECG8?si=Q7aKR10zgt7GHCDo)


After all, no city is perfect.


Having lived in Durham for more than 30 years and helping hundreds of buyers relocate to the Triangle, I've learned that the best decisions happen when people have realistic expectations—not just the highlight reel.


Here are the biggest challenges of living in Durham and why, despite them, so many people still choose to call it home.


## 1. Traffic Hasn't Kept Up with Growth


[One of Durham's biggest growing pains is traffic.](https://youtu.be/yEPT7noECG8?si=Q7aKR10zgt7GHCDo)


As the Triangle has expanded, roads like US-15-501 and Interstate 40 have become significantly busier, especially during rush hour.


If you're relocating from a major metro area like Atlanta or Washington, D.C., Durham's traffic will probably feel manageable.


If you're coming from a small town, however, the congestion can come as a surprise.


The reality is simple: Durham has grown rapidly, and the transportation infrastructure is still catching up.


## 2. Public Transportation Never Reached Its Potential


Many longtime residents remember the[proposed Durham-to-Chapel Hill light rail project.](https://youtu.be/yEPT7noECG8?si=Q7aKR10zgt7GHCDo)


It promised to connect two of the Triangle's largest employment centers while reducing traffic throughout the region.


Unfortunately, after years of planning and significant public investment, the project was ultimately canceled.


Today, most residents still rely on their cars for daily transportation, making commute times and traffic an important consideration when choosing where to live.


## 3. Property Taxes May Surprise You


Many buyers are attracted to Durham because homes are often more affordable than comparable properties in Chapel Hill.


However, purchase price is only part of the equation.


Durham County has one of the higher property tax rates in the Triangle, and recent property reassessments substantially increased taxable home values for many homeowners.


While Durham may still represent excellent value, it's important to consider both your mortgage payment and your long-term tax obligations when planning your budget.


## 4. Durham's Reputation Doesn't Match Today's Reality


One of the most common questions relocation buyers ask is:


["Is Durham safe?"](https://youtu.be/yEPT7noECG8?si=Q7aKR10zgt7GHCDo)


Durham's reputation was shaped decades ago, and for many people, that perception hasn't caught up with how much the city has changed.


Today's Durham is home to thriving neighborhoods, nationally recognized restaurants, major healthcare systems, growing technology companies, and one of the country's leading research universities.


Like any city, some areas experience more crime than others, but evaluating individual neighborhoods provides a much more accurate picture than relying solely on citywide statistics.


## 5. Summers Are Hot and Humid


There's no polite way to say it.


July and August in North Carolina are hot.


Very hot.


Combined with high humidity, stepping outside can feel like walking into a warm blanket.


Fortunately, this intense heat typically lasts only a few weeks before giving way to one of the biggest reasons people love living here: beautiful spring and fall weather.


For many residents, it's a small price to pay.


## The Real Worst Thing About Living in Durham


Ironically, the biggest downside isn't traffic, taxes, or humidity.


It's that everyone else discovered Durham too.


For years, Durham was considered the Triangle's hidden gem.


Housing was affordable.


Historic homes could be purchased for prices that now seem impossible.


Downtown warehouses sat largely empty.


Then people started paying attention.


Award-winning restaurants opened.


Downtown experienced a remarkable revitalization.


Technology companies expanded throughout the Triangle.


More people discovered everything Durham had to offer.


As demand increased, so did home prices.


The secret got out.


## What That Means for Buyers Today


Does that mean you've missed your opportunity?


Not at all.


It simply means buyers need to be more strategic.


Some of Durham's best opportunities aren't always the neighborhoods everyone already knows.


Often, tremendous value exists just outside the most recognizable areas, where prices haven't climbed quite as quickly but the lifestyle remains exceptional.


Local market knowledge becomes increasingly valuable in a competitive market.


Understanding neighborhood trends, upcoming development, school districts, commute patterns, and home values can make a significant difference in finding the right home.


## Is Durham Still Worth It?


Absolutely—for many people.


Durham continues to attract professionals, families, healthcare workers, university employees, and remote workers because it offers an outstanding combination of:


- A vibrant restaurant and brewery scene
- Excellent healthcare and higher education
- Convenient access to Research Triangle Park
- Diverse neighborhoods with unique personalities
- Strong long-term housing demand
- Easy access to Raleigh, Chapel Hill, and Raleigh-Durham International Airport


No city is perfect, and Durham certainly has its challenges.


But for many buyers, the advantages continue to outweigh the drawbacks.


## Final Thoughts


The "worst" thing about Durham may actually be a reflection of its success.


People want to live here.


That increased demand has brought higher home prices and more competition, but it has also fueled investment, revitalization, and continued growth.


If you're considering a move to Durham, don't focus solely on headlines or rankings.


Take time to explore the neighborhoods, understand the market, and think about the lifestyle that best fits your goals.


Every neighborhood tells a different story, and finding the right one can make all the difference.
