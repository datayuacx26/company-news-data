# Living in Amberly, Cary, NC

# [Living in Amberly, Cary, NC](https://youtu.be/NX2za76BnxA?si=lYYKbFCRI_-Ag9Pr) : What Buyers Really Need to Know


If you're considering buying a home in[Cary, North Carolina](https://youtu.be/NX2za76BnxA?si=lYYKbFCRI_-Ag9Pr) , chances are Amberly has come up on your list.


It's easy to see why.


Amberly is a large, master-planned community with resort-style amenities, convenient access to Research Triangle Park, strong schools, trails, pools, fitness facilities, and a location that works particularly well for people relocating to the Triangle.


But there's one thing about Amberly that can be confusing for buyers— **the price.**


Search online and you'll find articles suggesting that homes in Amberly start in the $400,000s.


That number isn't necessarily wrong.


It's just not telling you the whole story.


There are essentially **two very different housing markets inside Amberly:** single-family homes and townhomes. And the difference between them is significant.


## [Amberly, Cary](https://youtu.be/NX2za76BnxA?si=lYYKbFCRI_-Ag9Pr) : Two Very Different Price Points


According to MLS data referenced in our video, 82 single-family homes sold in Amberly between January 2025 and the time of recording.


The median sales price was approximately **$822,000** , with an average of about $811,000.


Only 17 of those 82 homes sold for less than $600,000, and just 27 sold for less than $700,000. At the time of recording, there were 12 single-family homes for sale, and none were priced below $600,000.


So where does that $400,000-ish price you see online come from?


**Townhomes.**


Over the same general period, 21 townhomes sold with a median price of approximately **$458,000** . That's where those lower price points come from—and they're legitimate.


The problem occurs when buyers see that number and assume it represents the cost of a typical single-family home in Amberly.


It doesn't.


### What Does Your Budget Buy in Amberly?


Here's a simple way to think about it:


**Around $450,000–$550,000:**
You're primarily looking at townhomes.


**Around $700,000+:**
You're entering the single-family market.


**Around $822,000:**
You're around the current median for an Amberly single-family home.


That distinction is incredibly important if you're relocating to Cary and building your home search around a particular budget.


## Why Do People Want to Live in Amberly?


If the prices are higher than some online articles suggest, what are you actually paying for?


Quite a bit.


Amberly is a master-planned community in west Cary with amenities designed to make everyday life convenient.


There are pools, a fitness center, trails, common areas, and a village square with restaurants.


And then there's the location.


Amberly is approximately 1.5 miles from Research Triangle Park, making it particularly attractive to people who work in RTP. It's also roughly 12 minutes from Duke University and within reasonable driving distance of Durham, Raleigh, and Chapel Hill.


For a household where one person works in RTP and another works at Duke, UNC, or in Raleigh, that central location can be a major advantage.


It's one of the reasons Amberly is so popular with relocation buyers.


## [Amberly and Carolina Preserve](https://youtu.be/NX2za76BnxA?si=lYYKbFCRI_-Ag9Pr)


Another unusual feature of the broader Amberly community is **Carolina Preserve** , a Del Webb 55+ active-adult community located within the larger development.


That creates an interesting option for families who want multiple generations to live nearby without necessarily sharing a home.


Grandparents can live within walking distance of their children and grandchildren while still having their own community, amenities, and lifestyle.


It's a setup that can be particularly appealing to families relocating to the Triangle together.


## Don't Forget the HOA Fees


One of the advantages of a master-planned community is the amenities.


But those amenities aren't free.


The master association dues can be around $100 per month, depending on the property and current fee structure, and those fees support things such as pools, fitness facilities, trails, and common areas.


For townhome buyers, there's another layer.


You may have both the **master association fee and a separate townhome HOA fee** .


That second fee generally covers things such as exterior maintenance and can be considerably more than the master association fee.


So if you're comparing a $458,000 Amberly townhome with a single-family home elsewhere in Cary, don't compare only the purchase prices.


Look at the **actual monthly cost** , including HOA dues, property taxes, insurance, and mortgage payment.


## The Trade-Off: Convenience vs. Character


Amberly is designed.


That's part of the appeal.


The homes relate to one another, the streets are planned, the amenities are centralized, and the community has a cohesive appearance.


For some buyers, that's exactly what they want.


For others, it may feel too planned.


If your dream home has a huge mature oak tree in the front yard, a large lot, an unusual floor plan, and 75 years of history, Amberly probably isn't going to scratch that itch.


The trade-off is essentially **convenience for character** .


You're getting a newer, highly planned community with amenities and easy access to major employment centers.


You're giving up some of the individuality, mature landscaping, and older-home character you'll find in established Cary neighborhoods.


Neither is inherently better.


It just depends on what you're looking for.


## The Schools Are Excellent—But There's a Catch


For relocating families, schools are often the biggest consideration.


Amberly feeds into some highly regarded Wake County schools, including Mills Park Elementary and Panther Creek High School. Panther Creek has a graduation rate of approximately 99%, and the broader west Cary area includes schools such as Green Hope and Green Level.


But there is an important caveat for anyone moving from outside Wake County.


**Wake County school assignments don't always work the way you might expect.**


Every address has a designated base school, but schools can become capped when they reach capacity. When that happens, new students may be assigned to an overflow school.


Attendance boundaries can also change.


That means you should never buy a home in Wake County based solely on the assumption that your child will attend a particular school.


If schools are a major factor in your home search, verify the current assignment directly with Wake County Public School System for the **specific property and school year** before making an offer.


## The Two Ways Into Amberly


Ultimately, there are two primary ways to buy into Amberly.


### Single-Family Homes


The median single-family sale price is approximately **$822,000** , with the upper end of the market reaching around $1 million or more.


If you're shopping seriously for a single-family home in Amberly, a budget of $700,000 or more is a much more realistic starting point based on the sales data discussed in the video.


### Townhomes


Townhomes provide a much more accessible entry point.


The median townhome sale price was approximately **$458,000** , with a working range of roughly $419,000 to $550,000 after accounting for outliers.


That means you can potentially get the Amberly location, amenities, and community lifestyle for hundreds of thousands less than a typical single-family home.


But you're making a trade.


You'll have less square footage and generally less private outdoor space.


For a young couple, single professional, downsizer, or empty nester, that might be an excellent trade.


For a family with three kids and a dog who needs a backyard, maybe not.


## [Who Is Amberly Best For?](https://youtu.be/NX2za76BnxA?si=lYYKbFCRI_-Ag9Pr)


Amberly may be a particularly good fit if you:


- Are relocating to the Triangle and want an easy landing spot
- Work in Research Triangle Park
- Need convenient access to RDU Airport
- Want newer construction
- Value community amenities
- Prefer pools, trails, fitness facilities, and planned gathering spaces
- Want strong school options
- Are comfortable with HOA rules
- Have a budget of $700,000+ for a single-family home
- Or are comfortable purchasing a townhome in the mid-$400,000s to low-$500,000s


On the other hand, Amberly may not be right for you if you:


- Want a large lot
- Want mature trees
- Prefer historic or unique homes
- Don't like HOAs
- Want to walk to a traditional downtown
- Need a single-family home around $600,000


And that's okay.


The important thing isn't whether Amberly is "the best" neighborhood in Cary.


It's whether **Amberly is the right neighborhood for you.**


## The Bottom Line


Amberly is a very good example of why buyers shouldn't rely solely on broad online real estate numbers.


The $400,000s price point is real—but it's primarily describing the townhome market.


The single-family market is a completely different story, with a median sale price around $822,000 based on the MLS data discussed in the video.


And that's actually good news if you're shopping in Amberly.


If you know which market you're in, you can make a much more informed decision about whether the neighborhood fits your budget and lifestyle.


Amberly isn't trying to be an old, quirky, tree-lined Cary neighborhood.


It's trying to be **convenient, amenitized, newer, well-located, and easy to live in.**


And for a lot of Triangle buyers—especially those relocating from out of state—that's exactly what they're looking for.
