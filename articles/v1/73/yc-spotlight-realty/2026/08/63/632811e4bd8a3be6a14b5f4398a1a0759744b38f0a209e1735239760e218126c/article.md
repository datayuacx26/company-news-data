# Last Fridays, First Saturdays: A Resident's Late-August Weekend in Historic Hillsborough

The last Friday of August in downtown Hillsborough starts before dinner. By 5:30 p.m., the North Lawn of the Old Orange County Courthouse is filling with makers' tents, a band is tuning on the South Lawn, and Churton Street is already thick with the kind of crowd that shows up rain or shine. If you live here, you know the rhythm. If you're still treating it as an occasional outing, you're using the town at half strength.


The argument of this post is simple. In late summer, the calendar in Historic Hillsborough is not a scatter of unrelated events. It's a single walkable weekend that begins Friday at dusk and ends Sunday on the Riverwalk, and the businesses worth your time are the ones that plan around it.


## The last Friday is the town's real anchor


The 2026 Last Fridays dates are April 24, May 29, June 26, July 31, August 28, September 25, and October 30. That means one date left after this month before the season shifts. The last Friday of every month, rain or shine, downtown Hillsborough showcases its art scene with the Last Fridays Art Walk from 5:30 p.m. to 9 p.m., and galleries, studios, boutique shops, and restaurants all participate.


The evening has three layers stacked on top of each other:


- **Art Walk** , 5:30 to 9 p.m., across roughly 19 downtown venues.
- **Makers Market** , on the Old Courthouse North Lawn, running May through October with 20-plus curated vendors.
- **Live on the Lawn** , the performance series on the South Lawn, 5:30 to 8:30 p.m., May through October.


Most of it happens on and around Churton Street between West Margaret Lane and West Queen Street, which is a compact enough footprint that you can loop it twice in an evening without repeating a block. The August 28 edition is your last chance to catch the Makers Market and Live on the Lawn together with genuinely warm weather, since the fall dates start compressing daylight quickly.


If you have out-of-town friends staying with you that weekend, this is where you take them. You don't need reservations. You don't need a plan. You need shoes.


## The newest reason the crowds are heavier


For veterans of the Art Walk, a specific detail is worth naming: Serratore's Pasta and Pizza opened its doors during a recent August Last Fridays, and it hasn't slowed since.


The restaurant sits at 112 N. Churton Street, in the space Radius Pizzeria occupied for 11 years before closing in December 2024. Chef and owner Josh DeCarolis runs it with partners Pietro Costanza and Phil Bey, the same trio behind Mothers & Sons Trattoria in Durham. The menu features a full slate of Neapolitan pizzas and a standout cutlet section with chicken or veal in several styles, the ambient-fermented dough is light and crispy with a little chew, and all pastas from spaghetti to gnocchi are made fresh in-house.


> "I want people to experience a genuinely warm and fulfilling experience — like going to grandma's house for dinner," DeCarolis has said about the space.


What this changes for residents: the block anchored by Serratore's, Nomad at 122 W. King in the old Osbunn movie theater, and Yonder now functions as a three-stop dinner route on Art Walk nights. You can eat at one, take a drink at another, and hit a gallery in between without moving your car.


## The shorter version of the same walk


Not every Friday needs to be a five-hour outing. If you're pacing yourself, the walkable core is small enough to do in ninety minutes. A tighter route:


- Start at Vesta, the boutique Francesca Morgan opened after moving to Hillsborough from the Monterey Bay area, bringing a California-meets-European sensibility to downtown's retail scene with clothing, home goods, and perfumes curated around quality over quantity.
- Cross to Volume Records, where special gatherings draw music lovers of all ages.
- End at Matthew's Chocolates or Town Scoop for something cold before the walk home.


That's the resident's version. It leaves the two-hour dinner-and-gallery loop for the visitors.


## Saturday morning belongs to the market


The rhythm doesn't stop at midnight. The Eno River Farmers' Market in downtown Hillsborough is open every single Saturday year-round, hosts a live music series and children's programming, and accepts and doubles the value of SNAP, FMNP, and WIC payments, with nearly all farmers and artisans sourced from within 60 miles of the market pavilion.


Late August is peak variety. The peppers, tomatoes, okra, and eggplant are all at their fullest, and the transition to winter squash hasn't started yet. Regular shoppers plan their week's cooking around what's on the tables that morning rather than the other way around.


If you want the slower Saturday, drive fifteen minutes out to RambleRill Farm at 913 Arthur Minnis Road. RambleRill hosts its Saturday Slowdown on the first Saturday of the month, from May to December 2026, from 10 a.m. to 2 p.m., with several farms and local artisans joining. September's is the next one. Mark it now.


## The Riverwalk connects everything else


Once the market wraps around noon, you can walk off breakfast on the Riverwalk without ever getting back in the car. The greenway is an accessible urban path stretching about three miles along the Eno River between Occoneechee Mountain State Natural Area and the Historic Occoneechee Speedway Trail, popular for walking, jogging, and cycling, with pedestrian connections between several neighborhoods. Its downtown portion is part of the N.C. Mountains-to-Sea Trail, and it's open daily from 7 a.m. until a half hour after sunset.


The move most residents don't make often enough: use the Riverwalk to get to Eno River Brewing rather than driving. The brewery sits in an unassuming blue building at 329 Allison St., where you hear the rushing Eno before you see it. Removing most of the fence around the property was intentional, so anyone walking the Riverwalk can stumble upon the brewery, a five-minute stroll from Gold Park.


That's the geography most guides get wrong. Eno River Brewing is not a destination you drive to. It's a stop on a walk.

None of these rely on each other, but they're stronger together. The Friday crowd feeds the Saturday market. The market feeds the Riverwalk. The Riverwalk feeds the brewery. That's the loop.


## The small print residents actually care about


A few practicalities that only matter if you're the one going, not reading about it:


Parking on Last Fridays gets tight after 6 p.m. The free deck next to Weaver Street Market is the standard move, and it fills in that order. If you're coming from a neighborhood on the west side, park at Gold Park and walk in on the Riverwalk. It's less than a mile and you skip the deck entirely.


Riverwalk closes a half hour after sunset. In late August that's roughly 8:30 p.m., which means the Friday evening loop and the Art Walk are on the same clock. Plan the walk before the galleries, not after.


Details for each Last Fridays lineup are typically finalized the week before the event on the Hillsborough Arts Council's site at[hillsboroughartscouncil.org/last-fridays](https://www.hillsboroughartscouncil.org/last-fridays) , which is worth checking Thursday if you have a specific gallery you want to see.


If you're the kind of resident who's lived here five years and still hasn't made the Riverwalk-to-brewery walk, or hasn't set foot inside Serratore's, this weekend is the one. The August 28 Art Walk is the last of the summer schedule with full daylight. What's here right now is different from what was here a year ago, and it will keep changing.
