# Durham Townhome and Condo Living: A Buyer’s Guide

Wondering whether a Durham condo or townhome is the right move for you? If you want lower-maintenance living, a more in-town location, or a price point that can differ from detached homes, attached housing can be a smart option. The key is knowing how ownership, monthly costs, and association rules work before you buy. Let’s dive in.


## Why condos and townhomes matter in Durham


Durham has made room for attached housing as part of its long-term growth. The city’s Comprehensive Plan identifies Apartment and Townhouse Neighborhoods as a formal place type and says these residential areas should be found throughout Durham.


That planning approach lines up with what many buyers want today. Durham’s Expanding Housing Choices initiative notes that fast population growth, limited developable land, and a renewed preference for in-town living have all contributed to housing availability and affordability challenges. For you as a buyer, that helps explain why condos and townhomes remain an important part of the local market.


## Where you may see attached housing in Durham


Attached housing appears in several parts of Durham, often in areas tied to compact-neighborhood or transit-oriented planning. City planning materials point to places such as Leigh Village, Patterson Place, South Square and MLK Jr. Parkway, Erwin Road near Duke University and the VA Medical Center, and Alston Avenue.


In Southside, just south of downtown and north of NCCU, the city describes a redevelopment area of about 125 acres that includes homes, apartment communities, and commercial development. Southside phases have included townhouses and live-work apartments, which shows how attached housing fits into Durham’s evolving urban fabric.


Around Erwin Road, the city describes a dense mixed-use area with large multi-family apartment complexes, many built between the 1970s and 2000s. The city also notes an area north of University Drive and west of MLK Jr. Parkway with apartment and townhome communities generally built around 1990, with nearby redevelopment focused around South Square, Parkway Plaza, and West Gate.


## Condo vs. townhome ownership


Before you compare listings, it helps to understand what you are actually buying. A condo and a townhome can look similar from the outside, but the ownership structure can be very different.


### How a condo is owned


Under North Carolina’s Condominium Act, each condo unit and its interest in the common elements is a separate parcel of real estate. Each unit is also separately taxed and assessed.


In a condo community, the association can adopt budgets, collect assessments for common expenses, regulate common elements, and maintain, repair, or replace common elements. In practical terms, that often means the association takes on more responsibility for shared structures and common areas.


### How a townhome is owned


Durham’s development ordinance defines a townhouse as a building made up of three or more attached dwelling units on individual lots that share side walls. That legal definition matters because townhome ownership often feels more like owning a small-lot house than owning a condo.


Under North Carolina’s Planned Community Act, which covers many newer HOA-governed townhome communities, the association can budget, collect assessments, and regulate common elements. But the owner is generally responsible for the lot and improvements on it unless the declaration says otherwise.


### Why the difference matters to you


This is where many buyers get tripped up. Two attached homes with similar prices can come with very different maintenance responsibilities.


A condo often shifts more shared-structure responsibility to the association. A townhome can range from near full owner responsibility to partial exterior maintenance by the association, depending on the community documents. That is why reviewing the declaration, rules, and maintenance responsibilities is so important.


## Monthly costs to budget for


The sticker price is only part of the story. When you buy a Durham condo or townhome, your monthly housing cost usually includes several moving parts.


### Core monthly expenses


You should expect to budget for:


- Principal and interest on your mortgage
- Property taxes
- Homeowners or unit-owner insurance
- HOA dues
- Utility bills if they are not covered by the association


HOA dues are typically paid separately from the mortgage payment. That means a home that looks affordable at first glance may feel very different once dues and insurance are added in.


### Durham property taxes


Durham’s FY 2026-2027 combined city and county tax rate is $1.0163 per $100 of assessed value. That works out to about $1,016.30 in annual property tax for every $100,000 of value before exemptions.


For example, if you are looking at a home assessed at $300,000, the annual property tax at that rate would be about $3,048.90 before any applicable exemptions. That is a useful number to build into your planning early.


### Utilities and service costs


If utilities are not included in your HOA dues, ask for a clear breakdown. Durham water is billed on a tiered usage schedule, and sewer is billed on a flat volume basis with added service charges.


The city also notes that customers outside city limits can face higher service charges. Even if a listing looks straightforward, utility structure can still affect your true monthly cost.


### Insurance for condos and townhomes


Insurance is another area where buyers should ask detailed questions. Condo association dues usually include master insurance for common areas, but you still need your own coverage for the unit.


Freddie Mac now requires an HO-6 unit-owner policy when the master property policy does not cover all or part of the unit interior or when the master policy includes a per-unit deductible. In simple terms, do not assume the association’s policy covers everything inside your walls.


## How to evaluate the HOA


In condos and townhomes, the association is a big part of what you are buying. Your future costs, maintenance experience, and resale potential can all be affected by how the HOA is run.


### What the HOA should tell you


North Carolina disclosure rules require sellers to disclose whether the property is subject to association rules, the amount of regular dues or assessments, the services paid for by those dues, and any approved special assessments or pending lawsuits involving the association.


That gives you a starting point, but it should not be your stopping point. You want to go beyond the summary and understand how the community operates in practice.


### Smart HOA questions to ask


These are some of the most useful questions for buyers:


- What do the dues cover?
- How well funded are the reserves?
- Are any special assessments planned?
- Is there litigation involving the association?
- What part of the building is insured by the association versus the owner?
- Are there rental limits or occupancy restrictions that could affect future marketability?


These questions can help you spot both value and risk. A higher monthly HOA fee is not automatically bad if it supports strong reserves, meaningful maintenance coverage, and stable operations.


## Financing and resale considerations


When you buy a condo or townhome, you are not just buying a home. You are also buying into a project or community structure that a future buyer and lender will evaluate later.


Fannie Mae’s project review framework looks at legal documents, budgets, financial statements, reserve studies, litigation, and insurance. Freddie Mac also says project review should address marketability, financial stability, litigation, occupancy restrictions, common elements and amenities, and insurance adequacy.


One especially important point is reserves. Freddie Mac notes that underfunded reserves can lead to special assessments or higher HOA assessments, which can create financial hardship for owners. For you, that means the health of the association can influence both your monthly costs now and your resale options later.


## What this means for first-time and relocating buyers


If you are buying your first home or moving to Durham from outside the Triangle, a condo or townhome can offer a simpler entry point into the market. You may find options in locations that support a shorter commute, easier access to daily needs, or less exterior upkeep than a detached home.


At the same time, attached housing asks you to pay closer attention to the fine print. The right fit is not just about the floor plan or finishes. It is also about ownership structure, dues, insurance, maintenance responsibilities, and how well the association is managed.


## How to shop smarter in Durham


If you are comparing Durham condos and townhomes, it helps to evaluate each one through the same lens. That makes it easier to separate a good fit from a home that only looks appealing on the surface.


### A practical buyer checklist


As you tour homes, keep these points in mind:


- Confirm whether the home is legally a condo or a townhome
- Ask what the HOA fee covers and what it does not
- Review current dues and any approved special assessments
- Ask about reserves, litigation, and insurance coverage
- Estimate taxes, insurance, utilities, and dues together
- Look at the surrounding area and redevelopment context
- Think about future resale, including buyer and lender appeal


This approach can help you make a more confident decision. It also gives you a clearer picture of your real cost of ownership before you get too far into the process.


Buying a condo or townhome in Durham can be a great move if you match the home, the community, and the ownership structure to your goals. ## FAQs


### What is the difference between a condo and a townhome in Durham?


- In general, a condo gives you ownership of your unit plus an interest in common elements, while a townhome is typically an attached home on an individual lot with shared side walls. Maintenance responsibilities can differ a lot based on the community documents.


### What costs should you budget for with a Durham condo or townhome?


- You should usually budget for mortgage principal and interest, property taxes, insurance, HOA dues, and any utilities not covered by the association.


### What do HOA dues usually cover in a Durham attached home community?


- Coverage varies by community, but dues commonly help pay for shared expenses such as common-area maintenance, landscaping, and association operations. You should always confirm the exact services and responsibilities.


### Why do HOA reserves matter when buying a Durham condo or townhome?


- Reserve funding matters because underfunded reserves can lead to higher dues or special assessments, which can affect your monthly budget and future resale.


### What HOA questions should a Durham condo or townhome buyer ask?


- Ask what the dues cover, how well funded the reserves are, whether any special assessments are planned, whether there is litigation, what insurance the association carries, and whether rental or occupancy restrictions apply.


### Where are some Durham areas with condos and townhomes?


- City planning materials identify attached-housing or compact-neighborhood areas including Leigh Village, Patterson Place, South Square and MLK Jr. Parkway, Erwin Road near Duke and the VA Medical Center, Alston Avenue, and Southside.
