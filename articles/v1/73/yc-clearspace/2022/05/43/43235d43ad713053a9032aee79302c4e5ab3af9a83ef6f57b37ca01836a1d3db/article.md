Our third installment of ClearSpace Stat of the Week mixes things up a little bit![Stat of the Week #1](https://medium.com/clearspace-blog/stat-of-the-week-15-000-hours-saved-3d23e0964320) showed how the hours saved by our users have accumulated over time, while[Stat of the Week #2](https://medium.com/clearspace-blog/half-your-trips-to-facebook-arent-worth-it-c68840e8810e) depicted the differences across social media apps in how often people decide to actually use the app they opened after we encourage them to be intentional about their app use. This week’s stat is a little different because the information it provides is, frankly, unexpected.


One of the bigger questions that we’ve had as a team is if there is a time of day when people most need a reminder to be intentional about their phone use. Many of the users we’ve talked to and other stories and experiences we’ve come across expressed an interest in reining in their phone use in one window of the day; often, people were most unsatisfied with how much they use their phones either first thing in the morning or just before they go to sleep at night.

The image below shows two sets of data: the percentage of intercepts where the user decides not to use the app they originally opened (blue) and the percentage of the day’s total intercepts during each hour (green), both broken down by the hour of the day in which they occur.


Press enter or click to view image in full size


Two of the three hours with the highest percentage non-use intercepts fall when users are most likely trying to fall asleep — 47.5% of intercepts in the 1 AM hour result in a non-use, as do 46.9% of intercepts in the 12 AM hour. Surprisingly, the second-highest hour for non-use intercepts comes in the middle of the day — 47.4% of intercepts in the 11 AM hour result in a non-use. On the other end of the spectrum, the three hours with the lowest non-use percentage are 5 AM (30.0%), 4 AM (35.4%), and 7 AM (63.7%). It’s hard to know for sure, but there are multiple possible reasons for this distribution of non-uses: intercepts in the morning are more likely to be part of a morning routine, intercepts at night might stem from reflexive restlessness instead of purposeful use, and a fresh stream of unseen content released overnight that users encounter during their first, and therefore more valuable, app use of the day.


The green line shows that phone use (or at least, opens of apps connected to ClearSpace) is pretty consistent throughout the day, with each hour between 10 AM and 11 PM including between 5 and 7% of the day’s total intercepts. The top three hours by percent of the day’s opens are 9 PM, 8 PM, and 5 PM, while the three lowest hours are 4 AM, 3 AM, and 5 AM, respectively.
