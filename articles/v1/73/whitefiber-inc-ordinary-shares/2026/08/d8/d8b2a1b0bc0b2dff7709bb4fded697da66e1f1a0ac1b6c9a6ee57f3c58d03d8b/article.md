# The Future of AI in Healthcare: Trends Driving Cloud, Colocation, and Private Infrastructure

## Trends, Demands, and Infrastructure Implications


‍


Artificial intelligence is reshaping healthcare, life sciences, and biotech at an extraordinary pace. From drug discovery to hospital operations, AI is moving from pilot projects to mission-critical systems. The implications are massive, not only for patients and researchers but also for the infrastructure required to power this transformation.


‍


In this post, we’ll break down the **current and future AI trends in healthcare** and explore what they mean for **AI cloud, GPU clusters, and colocation strategies.**


‍


‍


‍


## AI Trends Transforming Healthcare


‍


### 1. Drug Discovery and Clinical Trials


‍


Nearly **30% of new drug candidates by 2025 are expected to be discovered using AI.**


AI-first biotech startups raised **$5.6 billion in 2024, triple the prior year.**


Companies like Insilico Medicine designed novel drugs in under 18 months at a fraction of traditional costs.


‍


**Why it matters for infrastructure:** AI-driven drug discovery requires massive parallel GPU computing. Simulating molecules, running generative protein models, and processing trial datasets generate workloads measured in **millions of GPU hours** . The demand for dedicated, scalable clusters will only increase as pharma integrates AI deeper into its pipelines.


‍


‍


### 2. Diagnostics and Medical Imaging


‍


As of 2024, the FDA had cleared **882 AI-enabled medical devices** , 76% in radiology and ~10% in cardiology.


Pathology is catching up, with the first AI digital pathology systems cleared for **primary diagnosis in 2025.**


In China, the AI imaging market expanded to **¥44B (~$6B) by 2025** , addressing severe radiologist shortages.


‍


**Infrastructure impact:** Imaging AI workloads involve high-throughput data processing and real-time inference. Scaling these models globally requires l **ow-latency GPU clusters** , robust networking, and cost-effective data management, a natural fit for private cloud or colocation strategies.


‍


‍


### 3. Hospitals and Predictive Analytics


### ‍


**65% of U.S. hospitals now use predictive models** , often via EHR integrations, to forecast readmissions, sepsis risk, and ICU transfers.


China’s Tsinghua University even launched an **AI “Agent Hospital” with 42 AI doctors** operating at near-human diagnostic accuracy.


AI is also being used for scheduling, supply chain forecasting, and fraud detection, saving millions annually.


‍


**Infrastructure impact:** Hospitals will increasingly need **secure, compliant AI clouds and sovereign colocation solutions** to process sensitive patient data while scaling predictive models. Edge-to-core architectures will matter most, with GPUs running models close to where the data is generated.


‍


‍


### 4. Remote Monitoring and Wearables


‍


**72% of EU healthcare organizations** plan to deploy **AI-powered patient monitoring systems.**


FDA and EMA have approved **AI-driven digital endpoints** from wearables for use in clinical trials.


Closed-loop insulin pumps and ECG wearables are already saving lives with AI-driven insights.


‍


**Infrastructure impact:** Continuous monitoring generates streaming data that must be analyzed in real time. This creates demand for **low-latency inference environments** , hybrid setups where edge devices connect back to private or colocated GPU clusters for heavier processing.


‍


‍


‍


## Cloud vs. Colocation: The Next Stage of Healthcare AI


‍


Healthcare organizations are beginning to ask hard questions: should AI workloads run in public cloud, private cloud, or dedicated colocation clusters?


‍


Public cloud offers agility but can become cost-prohibitive for sustained, GPU-heavy research.


Private cloud provides better control and compliance, critical for healthcare and life sciences where data sovereignty is non-negotiable.


GPU cluster colocation is emerging as a middle ground, offering the cost efficiencies of owned infrastructure with the flexibility of cloud-like scaling.


‍


As AI models get larger, p **ower consumption is a looming factor** . A single GPU cluster for imaging AI can consume **several megawatts of power** . Healthcare providers and biotechs will look to colocation partners who can deliver **energy efficiency, sustainability, and high-density GPU hosting.**


‍


‍


‍


## What This Means for the Future


‍


The healthcare AI market is not just expanding, it’s accelerating. Diagnostics, drug discovery, and hospital operations are producing **real-world results,** backed by regulatory approvals and billions in investment.


‍


### To keep pace, organizations will need infrastructure that can:


‍


- **Scale GPU compute elastically for training and inference**
- **Handle petabyte-scale data pipelines from genomics, imaging, and wearables**
- **Deliver compliance-ready environments for sensitive patient data**
- **Optimize power and cost efficiency at cluster scale**
