# NGX T+1 Settlement: When Do You Get Your Money After Selling Shares?

If you sell shares on the Nigerian Exchange today, your money should be settled by the next business day.


That is the simple meaning of T+1 settlement.


The “T” means trade date, the day your buy or sell order is executed. The “+1” means one business day after that. So if you sell shares on Monday, settlement should happen on Tuesday. Sell on Friday, and the next business day is Monday, weekends do not count. If the next day is a public holiday, settlement moves to the next working day after that.


From June 1, 2026, Nigeria’s stock market moved to this T+1 cycle. Before now, the market ran on T+3, then T+2, meaning investors waited longer for trades to fully settle. Now the standard is one business day.


## **What happens after you sell shares?**


Selling looks simple from your end:


- You open your trading app
- You choose the stock you want to sell
- You place your sell order
- Your order is matched with a buyer
- Your broker confirms the trade


But the transaction is not fully complete at that point.


Behind the scenes, the shares need to move from you to the buyer, and the money needs to move from the buyer to you. Brokers, custodians, clearing systems, and settlement infrastructure all have roles in that process. Settlement is the point where it is finally done: buyer gets shares, seller gets cash.


Under T+1, that process should complete by the next business day.


## **So when exactly do you get your money?**


Here is the practical answer:


**Day you sell shares** **Expected settlement day**


Monday Tuesday


Tuesday Wednesday


Wednesday Thursday


Thursday Friday


Friday Monday


Saturday/Sunday Not trading days


T+1 does not mean instant payment. It means settlement, the full completion of the transaction, should happen one business day after you trade.


A simple example **:** You sell ₦500,000 worth of shares on Wednesday. Under T+1, the trade settles on Thursday. Depending on your broker, that ₦500,000 may appear in your trading wallet, portfolio cash balance, or bank account. Once it does, you decide what happens next, withdraw it, reinvest, or move it toward a specific goal.


That decision matters.


## **Where does the money actually go after settlement?**


This is where many retail investors get confused.


When your shares are sold, the proceeds do not always land in your bank account first. Depending on your broker or trading platform, the money may appear in:


- Your broker account
- Your portfolio wallet
- Your cash balance on the trading app
- Your linked bank account


Some platforms move the proceeds to your bank account automatically. Others keep the money in your trading wallet until you request a withdrawal. Some investors set their accounts so proceeds stay within their portfolio, making it easier to buy another stock quickly.


So when people say “you get your money under T+1,” what they mean is that the proceeds become *available* after settlement. Where you see them first depends on how your broker’s platform works.


If you sell shares and do not see the money in your bank account the next business day, check your trading wallet or broker cash balance before assuming there is a delay. That is often where it is sitting.


## **What should you do once the money arrives?**


This is the question most investors skip.


T+1 gives you faster access to your capital. But access is not the same as progress. Once your proceeds are available, you still need a plan, does the money go into another stock, a money market fund if you want relatively low-risk access while you decide, your emergency reserve, or a goal you are actively building toward?


Faster settlement just means the decision arrives sooner. The quality of that decision is still yours to make.


## **Why T+1 matters for retail investors**


For everyday investors, the clearest benefit is speed.


You no longer wait two or three business days for a sale to fully settle. If you need to reinvest, access your proceeds, or move money toward something, the waiting period is shorter.


That matters. Market opportunities move quickly. If you sell one stock because you want to buy another, faster settlement means you can act faster. If you need cash from an investment, T+1 cuts the gap.


It also reduces what is called counterparty risk, the risk that one party in a transaction fails to deliver. The longer a trade takes to settle, the more room there is for something to go wrong. Shortening the cycle to one business day tightens that window.


T+1 is also in line with settlement reforms adopted in major global markets. Nigeria joining that standard is an important upgrade for the market’s settlement infrastructure.


For retail investors, the practical point is simple: proceeds from share sales should now be available faster.


## **Can there still be delays?**


Yes, but they should be the exception now, not the expectation.


In the early period of implementation, some brokers may still experience operational gaps as they adjust. If your proceeds are not visible by the next business day, here is what to check:


1. Check your trading wallet or portfolio cash balance, the money may be there, not in your bank account.
2. Confirm whether the day after your trade was actually a business day, accounting for weekends and public holidays.
3. Check whether your broker requires a manual withdrawal request before proceeds move to your bank.
4. If the money is still not visible after all of the above, contact your broker directly.


The bigger shift is this: under T+1, the normal expectation has changed. Waiting two or three business days is no longer standard. One business day is. If it is taking longer without explanation, that is worth following up on.


## **Does T+1 affect buying shares too?**


Yes.


When you buy shares, the same cycle applies. Your trade may be confirmed immediately, but the full transfer of ownership completes at settlement. Under T+1, shares you buy should be settled in your name by the next business day.


## **Final thought**


T+1 changes when your money moves. It does not change what your money should do next.


For short-term needs, keep the money accessible. If it is for an emergency, separate it from your spending money. Long-term growth calls for an investment that matches your risk appetite.
