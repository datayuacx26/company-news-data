# 5 New Charts: ARR, MRR, Active Subscribers, Auto Renew Status & Cohorted Proceeds

Starting today, we've shipped five new powerful charts to help you keep tabs on your app's growth, overall health and subscriber movement:


1. **ARR (Annual Recurring Revenue)** shows you exactly how much yearly revenue your app would pull in if all your current subscribers kept their subscriptions running.
2. **MRR (Monthly Recurring Revenue)** represents how much revenue is generated each month from active subscriptions.
3. **Active Subscriptions** tracks the count of unexpired, paid subscriptions at any point in time.
4. **Auto Renew Status** shows how much of your MRR is set to *renew* vs *churn* .
5. **Cohorted Proceeds** represents net proceeds cohorted by install date — after refunds, taxes, and fees.


Like our existing charts, each of these support our powerful filters, breakdown segments, time ranges, and natural language input for data selections. That means you can answer questions like:


- How much of my ARR comes from a particular paywall, but only in the United States?
- How much MRR comes from a certain product?
- Was my ad spend profitable for a certain day after refunds and Apple's commission factor in?


Let's check them out.


## ARR


Being able to see your revenue at a yearly clip is essential information. You can think of it as your app's annual earning power right now.


ARR chart in Superwall


**Here's a pro tip:** You can breakdown our ARR, MRR and Active Subscriptions charts by "Auto Renew Status", making it easy to see how much of your revenue and subscriber base is actually set to automatically renew versus how much is going to churn when the current billing cycle ends.


Most analytics focus on total numbers, but using the auto-renew breakdown with those charts gives you the delta between your current performance and your future performance. Instead of simply knowing "we have 1,000 subscribers," you can see the full, realistic picture: "700 subscribers will stick around, and 300 are about to churn."
