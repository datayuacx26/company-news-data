## Semrush


Semrush is the data suite most SEO teams already know. It's been the default research layer for years, and it earns that spot. If you've ever opened a keyword gap report or run a site audit, there's a good chance you were in Semrush.


### What They Offer


- Keyword research, rank tracking, site auditing, and backlink analysis across a database of 43+ trillion backlinks
- AI visibility tracking through Semrush One or the AI Visibility Toolkit add-on, covering ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Gemini
- Content optimization via the SEO Writing Assistant (a separate paid add-on)


Good for: teams with a dedicated internal SEO hire or agency relationship who need solid research infrastructure to inform strategy.


Limitation: Semrush surfaces data and recommendations, then stops. Someone still has to act on every output, stacking hidden headcount or agency cost on top of the subscription fee. Full AI visibility tracking requires the add-on or Semrush One, starting at $199/month.


Bottom line: strong data tool if you already have the people to execute against it. The loop stays open.


## Ahrefs


Ahrefs built its reputation on link data, and that reputation holds. If your work revolves around who links to whom and why the competitive link profile in your space looks the way it does, this is the reference set.


### What They Offer


- One of the largest backlink indexes available: 42+ trillion links, with a crawler processing roughly 8 billion pages a day
- Keyword research, content gap analysis, and site auditing
- AI visibility tracking through Brand Radar, a paid add-on starting at $199/month
- Content Explorer for finding top-performing content in any niche


Good for: SEO pros and agencies whose workflow revolves around backlink analysis, competitor research, and link building at scale.


Limitation: Ahrefs reports, it doesn't act. It won't push a fix or publish a page. You still need a team to turn the data into shipped work. Brand Radar sits behind an add-on fee, and independent accuracy tests have flagged heavy undercounting in its AI citation detection across ChatGPT and Perplexity (see our[Ahrefs vs. Maintouch comparison](https://www.maintouch.com/compare/ahrefs) and this Ahrefs vs. Semrush breakdown).


Bottom line: a strong research tool for link-focused SEO. The loop reopens the moment it hands you the data.

### What They Offer

## Surfer SEO


Surfer SEO does one thing and does it well: it scores your drafts against what's already ranking. The Content Editor feeds you live optimization signals as you write (keyword density, heading structure, word count), and for writers who want that feedback loop baked directly into the drafting experience, the setup is clean.


### What They Offer


- SERP-based content scoring and keyword density guidance for individual pages
- Content Editor with live optimization scoring against top-ranking competitor pages
- Keyword research and content brief generation
- AI article writing priced per article on a credit model


Good for: writers and small content teams optimizing individual pages who want live on-page scoring while they draft.


Limitation: Surfer doesn't learn from your edits. Every piece starts from scratch with no improvement to brand voice over time, no institutional memory. Bottom line: a solid on-page scoring tool at low-to-moderate volume. If you want a system that improves as it runs and doesn't charge more as you scale, Maintouch is the better fit.
