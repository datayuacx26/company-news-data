## What Is Keyword Competitor Analysis


Keyword competitor analysis is the process of finding the keywords your competitors rank for that you don't, then using that intel to close ranking gaps and shape what you publish next.


Traditional research starts from a blank page: you brainstorm terms, check volumes, and guess at what your buyers type. Competitor analysis skips all of that. Every keyword a rival ranks for is one Google already decided deserves traffic. You're not guessing at demand. You're reading the results sheet.


## Why Keyword Competitor Analysis Matters


Skip this step and you're competing on instinct. Your rivals already published the content that ranks, and every day you spend guessing which topics matter is a day they compound their lead.


Run it and four things follow:


- A real benchmark. You see exactly where your visibility stands against specific competitors, not a vague sense that you're "behind."
- Untapped keywords. Terms your buyers search that you've never targeted show up as clear gaps, ready to claim.
- A defensible content plan. You publish against proven demand instead of a list built on hunches.
- Less wasted effort. You stop pouring hours into topics your market doesn't actually search for.


None of that matters if you run the analysis against the wrong domains.


## How to Identify Your True SEO Competitors


Most teams assume their business rivals are their search rivals. They're often not the same list, and that's the first place this analysis goes sideways.


Your business competitors sell what you sell. Your search competitors rank for the terms you want. A tool directory, a Reddit thread, or a trade publication can sit above you for your highest-value queries without ever competing for your customers. They're not your enemy in market, but on the SERP, they're taking your spot.


How to find who you're actually up against:


- Take your 10 to 15 most important target keywords.
- Search each one in an incognito window.
- Note every domain in the top ten.
- Tally which domains keep showing up.


The domains that surface repeatedly are the ones you're fighting for visibility, whatever they sell. Get this list wrong and every step after it points at the wrong targets.


## How to Collect Competitor Keywords


With your competitor list set, the next job is pulling the actual keywords those domains rank for. Two routes: free by hand, or paid at scale.


The free route:


- Open each competitor's top-ranking pages and note the terms they target in titles, headers, and URLs.
- Harvest the "People Also Ask" boxes and related searches under your core queries. Every question there is a keyword someone typed.
- Skim their blog and resource sections to see which topics they keep coming back to.


The paid route is faster and deeper. Drop a competitor domain into Semrush, Ahrefs, or SpyFu, and you've got their full organic keyword footprint in one export, positions and volumes included. If you're still deciding between tools, the[best keyword research tools 2026](https://maintouch.com/blogs/best-keyword-research-tools) breakdown covers how they compare on database size, difficulty scoring, and gap coverage.


Run this across every competitor you named. The combined output is the raw material for the gap analysis coming next.


## How to Run a Keyword Gap Analysis


Once you have every competitor's keyword export, the analysis is a sorting job. Overlay your own ranking data against theirs and drop every keyword into one of three buckets.


Bucket What it means Why it matters


Missing Competitors rank, you don't appear The biggest opportunity


Weak You both rank, but they sit above you A foothold to defend and improve


Shared You rank at roughly equal positions Low priority, already competitive


Do this across two or more competitors at once. A keyword that shows up missing against three separate rivals is proven demand you've ignored. That overlap is the signal worth acting on.


Two questions drive the output: where do multiple competitors rank while you're absent, and where are you stuck on page two behind one beatable rival? The first is content you haven't written. The second is a page you can push with a targeted rewrite.


## Keyword Competitor Analysis Tools


The tools split into paid intelligence suites and free options that get you partway there.


SpyFu is the budget entry point, built to surface competitor keyword portfolios, ad spend history, and historical ranking data.[SpyFu's pricing plans](https://www.spyfu.com/mainpurchase/?page=plans) run $29-$187/month on annual billing across three tiers, which keeps it within reach for freelancers and small teams.


- **Ahrefs and Semrush** are the deep suites. Bigger keyword databases, dedicated gap-analysis views, and a price tag to match, well above SpyFu.
- **Google Search Console** is free, but it only shows your own impressions and rankings. You'll never see a competitor's data in there.
- **Free trials:** Semrush, Ahrefs, and SpyFu all offer limited access, enough to run through the gap workflow before committing.


## How to Score Gap Keywords and Build a Content Plan


A raw gap list isn't a plan. It's 200 rows in a spreadsheet. You need to filter it down before you write a single word.


Score each gap keyword against four things:


- **Intent match.** The query should point at content you can actually convert on. Traffic for traffic's sake is noise.
- **Difficulty vs. your domain authority.** Skip terms your domain can't compete for yet.
- **Search volume.** Enough demand to warrant the write.
- **Commercial relevance.** Closer to a buying decision beats vanity volume every time.


Stop treating keywords as one-off targets. Group related gaps into topic clusters, build the pillar first, then fill in the supporting posts around it. The pillar captures the broad ranking. The supporting posts capture the long tail and pass link equity back up to anchor it.


## Keyword Competitor Analysis for AI Search Visibility


Your keyword gap doesn't stop at the blue links. A keyword gap on Google is a missing organic ranking. An AI citation gap is a missing mention inside the answer ChatGPT, Perplexity, or Google AI Overviews generates for that same query. Running[AI visibility tracking](https://maintouch.com/blogs/best-ai-brand-visibility-tracking-tools) alongside your organic gap analysis is how you catch the second kind before a competitor locks it in.


The two overlap heavily. A[seoClarity study (data as of 2025)](https://www.seoclarity.net/research/aio-rankings-overlap) found that 94% of keywords showing a Google AI Overview had at least one citation overlapping the top 20 organic results. Strong organic rankings feed AI citations, so your organic gap list is a solid starting point for your citation gap list.


They're not identical, though. A competitor can rank organically and still get skipped in the AI answer. Or earn the citation without ranking first. The surfaces are related, not interchangeable.


When a rival earns the citation for a cluster you're missing, the gap compounds. They collect the organic click and the AI mention. You collect neither. Run your gap analysis against both surfaces, and pair it with a plan for[getting cited in AI Overviews](https://maintouch.com/blogs/how-to-get-cited-in-ai-overviews) , going beyond the blue links.


## How to Track Competitor Keywords Over Time


The gap analysis you just ran is a snapshot. Competitors publish new content, algorithms shift, and fresh domains break into your SERPs. The picture you built today drifts within weeks.


Set a cadence and stick to it. Monthly works for most teams. In a fast-moving niche where rankings churn week to week, go weekly. The same cadence applies to AI search:[tracking LLM search rankings](https://maintouch.com/blogs/llm-visibility-complete-guide-tracking-ai-search-rankings) alongside organic positions gives you the full picture.


Three things to watch between runs:


- Competitors picking up new positions on keywords you haven't touched.
- Your rankings slipping on shared keywords a rival just improved.
- New domains entering your top ten that weren't there last month.


A lightweight system beats an elaborate one you abandon. One spreadsheet, your priority keywords, each competitor's position, updated on your cadence. Flag any row that moved more than a few spots. The movement tells you where to look next.

## FAQ


### SpyFu vs Semrush vs Ahrefs for keyword competitor analysis: which one should I actually use?


SpyFu is the right call if budget is the constraint. At $29/month on annual billing, you get competitor keyword portfolios and ad history without the enterprise price tag. Semrush and Ahrefs go deeper on database size and have dedicated gap-analysis views, but you're paying a steeper premium for that coverage. If you're doing this at scale or need the most complete organic footprint data, Semrush or Ahrefs wins. If you're on a tight budget and just need the gap workflow to run, SpyFu gets you most of the way there.


### How do I run a keyword gap analysis without paying for a tool?


Open your top 10 to 15 target keywords in incognito, note every domain appearing repeatedly in the top ten, and manually read competitor titles, headers, URLs, and "People Also Ask" boxes to pull the terms they're targeting. It's slower than dropping a domain into Semrush or Ahrefs, but it works. You're just trading time for money. Google Search Console gives you your own ranking data for free, so the gap becomes visible when you line up your positions against what competitors are visibly targeting.


### What's the difference between a keyword gap analysis and regular keyword research?


Regular keyword research starts from a blank page: you brainstorm terms, check volumes, and guess at what buyers type. Keyword gap analysis starts from proven demand. Every keyword a competitor ranks for is one Google has already validated as worth traffic. You skip the guessing entirely and reverse-engineer a keyword list from sites already winning the searches you want. Your content plan is built on real signal, not hunches.


### Can a competitor rank organically and still win the AI citation while I don't?


Yes, and this is where a standard competitor keyword analysis misses the full picture. Strong organic rankings feed AI citations, but the two surfaces aren't identical. A competitor can rank organically and get skipped in the AI answer, or earn the citation without ranking first. Running your gap analysis against both organic results and AI-generated answers closes that blind spot; if you only track the blue links, you're missing half the competitive intelligence.


### How often should I redo a competitor keyword analysis to keep the data fresh?


Monthly works for most teams. That cadence catches new competitor content, algorithm changes, and fresh domains breaking into your top ten before they compound a lead you can't close quickly. In fast-moving niches where rankings move week to week, shorten it to weekly. The system doesn't need to be elaborate: one spreadsheet, your priority keywords, each competitor's position, updated on your cadence, with any row that moved more than a few spots flagged for action.
