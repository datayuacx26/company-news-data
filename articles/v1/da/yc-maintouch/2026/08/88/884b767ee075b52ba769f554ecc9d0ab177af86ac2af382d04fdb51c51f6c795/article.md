## What Are GEO Tools?


GEO (generative engine optimization) is the practice of structuring your content and schema so AI systems pull your words directly into their answers. Ask ChatGPT, Perplexity, or Google AI Overviews a question and they assemble a response from passages extracted off the open web through a process known as[retrieval-augmented generation](https://aws.amazon.com/what-is/retrieval-augmented-generation/) . GEO is about whether one of those passages comes from your site.


It's easy to conflate GEO with AEO, so let me draw the line. AEO covers whether you show up across AI answer engines at all: domain authority, citation share, presence in the retrieval set. GEO is the execution layer underneath — it works at the passage and schema level, deciding which specific text gets extracted and surfaced inside a generated answer.


AEO is the scoreboard. GEO is the plays that move it.

### What's the difference between GEO and AEO, and do I need both?


GEO (generative engine optimization) works at the passage and schema level — it's the execution layer that decides whether an AI engine extracts and cites your content inside a generated answer. AEO (answer engine optimization) is the broader scoreboard: your domain authority, citation share, and presence across AI retrieval systems. You need both, but they're not separate strategies. Strong SEO feeds AEO directly because AI engines run a web search under the hood before generating answers — the same content and technical work drives both.


### How does schema markup actually affect whether an AI engine cites my content?


Schema is a binary qualifier for AI citation. Without it, a page gets deprioritized from the retrieval set before content quality is even evaluated, and your writing never gets a chance to compete. Schema types like FAQPage, Article, HowTo, and Organization, the vocabulary defined in Google's[structured data markup](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/intro-structured-data) guidelines, tell AI engines exactly what a page is about and how to extract relevant passages. The subtler problem is schema drift: your content gets updated, the schema doesn't, and citations disappear without any visible change in content quality. It's one of the most common silent losses I see. The fix is automating schema regeneration on every publish, which is what Maintouch does.


### Do I need to optimize for each AI engine separately, or does one strategy work across all of them?


The foundation is shared: passage-level content optimized for direct extraction, structured data, and a strong backlink profile all improve citation rates across ChatGPT, Gemini, Google AI Overviews, Perplexity, and Claude simultaneously. Where the engines diverge is in how they weight freshness, authority, and structured formats. The practical answer is one content strategy executed well beats five fragmented ones — the engines are picking from the same open web, and the same signal quality that earns a citation in Perplexity tends to earn one in ChatGPT too.
