## What Are Retinoids and Why Should You Use Them?


[Retinoids are a class of vitamin A derivatives](http://dearbrightly.com/retinol-retinal-and-tretinoin-whats-the-real-difference/) that accelerate your skin’s natural renewal process. They increase cell turnover,[stimulate collagen production](http://dearbrightly.com/what-is-collagen-and-how-to-rebuild-it/) , and help fade discoloration, making them one of the most effective ingredients for treating signs of aging, uneven texture, acne, and dullness.


Among them, the most clinically effective is **Tretinoin** , a prescription-strength Retinoid that works faster and more deeply than over-the-counter versions like **Retinol** . Because it’s already in its active form, Tretinoin begins working immediately—no conversion needed—giving you a head start on visible improvements.


## Retinol vs. Tretinoin: What’s the Difference Among Retinoids?


Though often used interchangeably, Retinol and Tretinoin are both Retinoids, but they act very differently in the skin. **Retinol** is widely available over the counter and must convert into retinoic acid within the skin to become active. **Tretinoin** , on the other hand, is retinoic acid—meaning it works immediately and at a deeper level.


Here’s how they compare:


**Feature** **Retinol (OTC)** **Tretinoin (Rx Retinoid)**


Strength Low High (up to 20x more potent)


Conversion in Skin Must convert to retinoic acid Already in active form


Availability Over-the-counter Prescription required


Speed of Results Slower (2–6 months) Faster (visible in 6–8 weeks)


Irritation Potential Lower (less effective) Moderate (buffered with correct support)


Ideal For Mild concerns, preventive care Mild to advanced skin concerns


While Retinol may offer gentler results, many users become frustrated by the slow progress or underwhelming outcomes. Tretinoin bridges that gap, offering clinical efficacy and long-term transformation—especially when paired with proper care and support.
