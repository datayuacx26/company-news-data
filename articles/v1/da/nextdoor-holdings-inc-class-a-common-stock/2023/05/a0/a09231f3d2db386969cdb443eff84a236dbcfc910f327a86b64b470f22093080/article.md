At Nextdoor we build technology that empowers resilient, safe, and kind neighborhoods all over the world. Securing a product that empowers global communities requires diverse and inclusive teams, reflective of the communities we support.


Yet hiring and retaining the diverse talent needed to achieve our purpose remains an industry challenge. The gap is particularly evident in the cybersecurity field where[25% of the workforce](https://cybersecurityventures.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/Women-In-Cybersecurity-2022-Report-Final.pdf) and[16% of CISOs](https://www.forrester.com/report/ciso-career-paths-3-0/RES178976) identify as female. According to the[WiCyS State of Inclusion report 2023,](https://www.wicys.org/initiatives/wicys-state-of-inclusion/) women cite lack of respect and limited opportunities for growth in cybersecurity as top challenges accompanying lack of representation. We must keep working on it.


That is why Nextdoor welcomed the chance to celebrate diversity, alongside[RSAC 2023](https://www.rsaconference.com/usa) , in Nextdoor HQ’s backyard this week and to partner with our neighborhood[Women in Cybersecurity](https://www.wicys.org/) (WiCyS)[Silicon Valley chapter](https://www.wicyssiliconvalley.org/) . We are committed to building a diverse and inclusive workplace, and we are proud to work with organizations like WiCyS, who share the same values.

Nextdoor’s CISO TC Niedzialkowski kicked off with a warm welcome. CEO Sarah Friar framed the discussion by sharing how she launched her career by building a network at her first RSA conference as an equity analyst for Security Software at Goldman Sachs. She emphasized that diverse teams bring a variety of perspectives and experiences to the table, which ultimately leads to better problem-solving and innovation.
