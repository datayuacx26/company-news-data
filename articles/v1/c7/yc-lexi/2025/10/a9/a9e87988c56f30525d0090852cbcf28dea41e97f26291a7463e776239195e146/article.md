## **Harvey AI: Enterprise Power for BigLaw**


### **Overview and Market Position**


Harvey is built for the world's largest law firms. Backed by significant venture capital and partnerships with firms like Allen & Overy (now A&O Shearman), Harvey has positioned itself as the AI platform of choice for Am Law 100 firms. Its focus is depth, not reach—LLM-powered legal research, document generation, and complex custom workflows for firms with thousands of lawyers.


Harvey's approach is uncompromising: build the most powerful legal AI possible, then deploy it within enterprise environments that can support its complexity. The result is a platform that delivers impressive results for the firms that can afford it and have the technical infrastructure to run it.


### **Core Capabilities**


Harvey's feature set is tailored for high-stakes, high-volume legal work. Key capabilities include:


-


**Advanced legal research:** Harvey leverages custom large language models fine-tuned on legal corpora. It can analyze case law, statutes, and regulatory frameworks across multiple jurisdictions with remarkable depth.


-


**Document generation and review:** From drafting complex transactional documents to reviewing thousands of contracts during due diligence, Harvey handles document-heavy workflows that would take human teams weeks.


-


**Custom workflow automation:** Harvey's enterprise clients can build bespoke AI workflows that mirror their existing processes—meaning the platform adapts to the firm, not the other way around.


-


**Knowledge management:** Harvey can be trained on a firm's proprietary work product, precedent banks, and internal guidelines, creating a firm-specific AI that reflects institutional knowledge.


### **Pricing and Accessibility**


Harvey's pricing reflects its enterprise positioning. Industry estimates place Harvey's annual cost at approximately **$288,000 per year** for a full firm deployment—a figure that includes licensing, onboarding, customization, and ongoing support. For a firm generating hundreds of millions in annual revenue, this is a rounding error. For a 20-attorney firm, it is a non-starter.


The implementation timeline is equally enterprise-grade. Deploying Harvey typically requires months of data integration, security reviews, and custom configuration. Firms need dedicated IT resources and often a project manager to oversee the rollout.


### **Strengths and Limitations**


Harvey's strengths are clear: unmatched depth for complex legal work, strong security posture for enterprise environments, and the ability to customize at scale. However, these strengths come with trade-offs that matter for the majority of[law firms](https://www.getlexi.io/solutions/law-firms) :


-


**High cost of entry:** At ~$288K/year, Harvey is priced for BigLaw budgets only.


-


**Long implementation cycles:** Expect 3–6 months before the platform is fully operational.


-


**Requires internal technical resources:** Smaller firms without dedicated IT teams will struggle with deployment and maintenance.


-


**Limited small-firm features:** Harvey does not prioritize workflows common in smaller practices, such as client intake automation, billing integration, or solo-practitioner dashboards.

## **Legora: Collaboration-First Legal AI**


### **Overview and Market Position**


Legora takes a different approach entirely. Rather than building a monolithic enterprise platform, Legora focuses on making legal collaboration smarter and faster. It integrates directly into the tools lawyers already use—Microsoft Word and Outlook—making adoption frictionless for mid-sized firms and corporate legal departments.


Legora's philosophy is pragmatic: meet lawyers where they are, rather than asking them to learn a new system. This approach has earned it a loyal following among firms that value incremental improvement over wholesale transformation.


### **Core Capabilities**


Legora's feature set centers on document collaboration and review:


-


**In-document AI assistance:** Legora embeds directly into Word, offering real-time suggestions, clause analysis, and drafting assistance without leaving the document.


-


**Collaborative review workflows:** Multiple team members can review, comment, and approve documents within a structured workflow, reducing version-control chaos.


-


**Due diligence automation:** Legora accelerates due-diligence review by flagging key clauses, risks, and anomalies across document sets.


-


**Clean user interface:** Legora's UI is consistently praised for its simplicity. Lawyers who are skeptical of AI find Legora approachable and intuitive.


### **Strengths and Limitations**


Legora excels at what it does. Its clean integration with Microsoft Office, focus on collaboration, and approachable learning curve make it a strong choice for firms that want to improve document workflows without disrupting their practice. Its pricing is more accessible than Harvey's, and onboarding is measured in days rather than months.


Still, Legora—like most legal AI tools—sits in a single lane. It is excellent at document review and due-diligence tasks, but it is not designed to automate the *entire legal lifecycle* . Firms that need AI to handle client intake, legal research, case management, court filing preparation, and analytics will find Legora covers only a portion of their needs. The result is that many Legora users end up supplementing it with additional tools, creating the very tool fragmentation they were trying to avoid.
