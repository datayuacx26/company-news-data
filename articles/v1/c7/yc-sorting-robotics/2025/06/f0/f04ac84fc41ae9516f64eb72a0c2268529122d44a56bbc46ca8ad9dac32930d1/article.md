**Thank you so much for your time! I know that you are a very busy person. Before we drive in, our readers would love to “get to know you” a bit better. Can you tell us a bit about your ‘backstory’ and how you got started?**


I


have always possessed an entrepreneurial spirit even as a child. Whether it was starting a neighborhood car wash company, selling Olympia door-to-door to eventually managing and scaling a Malibu winery–I find business and its inherent complexities fascinating. I also find science and the natural world fascinating as my undergraduate degree from Pepperdine University is in Biology. Even after completing my MBA, I still felt there was more to learn — thus, my participation at The Harvard Business School in their “Succeeding as a Strategic CFO” Executive Education program. Being able to learn alongside 59 of the world’s greatest CFOs was truly inspiring and helps continue to push Sorting Robotics to new heights of success.


**It has been said that our mistakes can be our greatest teachers. Can you share a story about the funniest mistake you made when you were first starting? Can you tell us what lesson you learned from that?**


I was lucky enough to be the 7th employee at Sorting Robotics, back when we were working out of a cozy little 4,000 square foot flex space — no conference room in sight. In fact, we had a giant TV perched on a folding table–Ah yes, peak startup vibes! One day, our CEO, Nohtal, casually says, “Hey, build a computer for the TV so we can run our remote meetings.” Now, I’m no engineer, but I love a good challenge.


So, I rolled up my sleeves, grabbed a Raspberry Pi, flashed the drive, and connected it to the TV. Boom! Success… or so I thought. Once I loaded Windows and fired up Zoom, the meeting looked like an abstract art piece, pixelated and lagging like it was stuck in a time warp. Turns out, a Raspberry Pi doesn’t exactly have the horsepower for Zoom. Who knew, right?


But here’s what I took away from this glorious misadventure:


1. When faced with a challenge, step one is to define the problem and take action. If you fail, no sweat — just figure out what went wrong and try again.


2. Even in failure, there’s room for laughter, especially when your technical team has your back. And now, I can proudly say I know how to build a computer that almost works…just not for Zoom.

**Let’s now move to the central part of our interview. How have recent technological advancements such as AI made your job easier?**


Easy is a false parameter here. Large Language Models (LLMs) are incredibly useful in business as they will allow you to work with much higher efficiency. Productivity and efficiency are where the current returns can be found for businesses that are looking to implement AI tools. Understanding where to implement these tools in your business is the next phase.


**In which processes do you utilize automation the most?**


In my role I use these LLM tools as digital consultants –I run ideas by them. I named my GPT Aldous after Aldous Huxley. Aldous helps me think in ways that I may not have considered when thinking through a problem–unlocking new ideas and boosting creativity. We utilize automation for marketing and soon some customer support functions.


**What should people bear in mind when automating processes?**


Automating mundane processes is a good place to start. Do not simply launch these tools without garnering feedback from departments impacted by such tools’ use –bring them in to see if there are new ways to reshape SOPs through automation implementation. Most importantly, keep improving since these tools are rapidly improving every day.


**What are your “Top Five Tips For Harnessing AI Technology to Propel People Operations”? Please share a story or an example for each.**


1. **Implement AI-focused recruitment tools.** As a high-growth company, we are hiring for multiple roles at any given time. Typically, we can tailor our interviews with candidates in ways that allow us to gain better insights into their knowledge by generating questions for our interviews. This helps us be more efficient and effective in our process.
2. **Ask questions about AI to potential new hires.** We value candidates who make it through our hiring process to our on-site interviews. These onsite interviews typically have a presentation component and it is always a positive for us when someone explains how and why they used AI as a tool to complete the project. Be mindful, however, about having AI do all the work. Trust me we know what to look for.
3. **Start off with small tasks.** Before you fully implement any AI Technology tools do a small test to see how it works before rolling it out to the larger organization.
4. **Ensure data privacy.** LLM’s and other AI tools are definitely using your data. However, check settings to know exactly how it is being used as this could be violating company policy and/or the privacy rights of your candidates. If possible, implement a custom version of the commercially available AI models which can help ensure data is anonymized or kept locally.
5. **Use for strategic workforce planning.** Creating models is never a fun exercise so upload your forecasts and ask questions about future needs. This can take multiple hours of refining and training your AI model but ultimately you’ll be in a much more valuable position with a full model to help you make strategic decisions.


**What are your favorite “I couldn’t live without these” tools?**


Like most, ChatGPT has changed the way we can access and manipulate information. Also, Leonardi.ai is a brilliant image creation tool that can quickly iterate through concepts and help you craft stunning visuals with minimal effort.


**How do you see technology impacting the HR space in the future?**


I see this technology taking over and improving many Applicant Tracking Systems (ATS) which we all must admit candidates despise. In the very near future, your first interview may very well be with an AI bot via Zoom or on the phone. This will allow managers to better filter candidates to move on in the process vs. the current systems that are very one-dimensional and will potentially exclude many otherwise good candidates.
