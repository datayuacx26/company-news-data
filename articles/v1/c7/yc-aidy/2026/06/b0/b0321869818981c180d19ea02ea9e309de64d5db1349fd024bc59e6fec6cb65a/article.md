# Workplace Restroom Accommodations for IBD: Example Requests

Inflammatory bowel disease can turn bathroom access into a daily logistical problem at work. Crohn's disease and ulcerative colitis often bring sudden, urgent bowel movements, and the unpredictability of a flare can make an ordinary shift a source of real anxiety. Many employed patients want to ask for support such as quick restroom access or a flexible schedule, yet they stall because they do not know what to say. This article gives example request wording you can adapt, explains the legal framework behind these requests, and offers a short checklist to prepare. It is general information, not legal advice, and your own situation may call for guidance from an attorney or a disability advocate.


## Why IBD Can Qualify for Workplace Accommodations


The symptoms that make work hard are the same ones that can support an accommodation request. Ulcerative colitis frequently causes diarrhea and[an urgent need to have a bowel movement](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/digestive-diseases/ulcerative-colitis/symptoms-causes) , along with tenesmus, the feeling of a constant urge even when the bowel is empty. Crohn's disease commonly produces[diarrhea, abdominal cramping, and pain](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/digestive-diseases/crohns-disease/symptoms-causes) , and both conditions cycle between flares and remission, so needs can change from week to week.


Under federal law, these limitations can make an employee a qualified individual with a disability. The[Americans with Disabilities Act requires employers with 15 or more employees](https://www.ada.gov/resources/disability-rights-guide/) to provide reasonable accommodation to qualified individuals with disabilities unless doing so causes undue hardship. The Equal Employment Opportunity Commission, which enforces this part of the law,[confirms the 15-employee threshold](https://www.eeoc.gov/publications/small-employers-and-reasonable-accommodation) . A reasonable accommodation is[any change to a job or the work environment](https://www.ada.gov/resources/employment-seeking-guide/) that lets you perform your role, and examples the government lists include modifying a workspace and time off for treatment.


## How to Make the Request


You do not need a lawyer's vocabulary to start. The EEOC guidance is explicit that a request[does not need to be in writing and need not mention the ADA or the phrase reasonable accommodation](https://www.eeoc.gov/laws/guidance/enforcement-guidance-reasonable-accommodation-and-undue-hardship-under-ada) . You simply need to connect a workplace need to a medical condition. Even so, putting the request in writing helps both sides keep a clear record, and the Crohn's & Colitis Foundation[recommends preparing in advance, consulting HR, and gathering medical paperwork](https://www.crohnscolitisfoundation.org/patientsandcaregivers/managing-the-cost-of-ibd/tips-requesting-ibd-work-accommodations) such as a letter from your doctor.


After you ask, the employer should begin what the law calls the interactive process,[an informal exchange to clarify what you need](https://www.eeoc.gov/laws/guidance/enforcement-guidance-reasonable-accommodation-and-undue-hardship-under-ada) and to identify a workable accommodation. Cost is rarely the obstacle people fear. The Department of Labor reports that[most accommodations cost nothing, and the rest usually run about $500](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/odep/ada/MythsandFacts) .


## Example Wording for a Restroom Access Request


Keep the request specific, factual, and focused on function rather than on the details of your diagnosis. You might write:


"I have a medical condition that can cause sudden, urgent needs to use the restroom, sometimes with little warning. To perform my job reliably, I am requesting prompt access to a restroom whenever symptoms occur, and I would like my workstation moved closer to the nearest restroom. I am happy to provide documentation from my gastroenterologist."


Relocating a workspace closer to facilities is among the options the Crohn's & Colitis Foundation[lists as reasonable](https://www.crohnscolitisfoundation.org/patientsandcaregivers/managing-the-cost-of-ibd/tips-requesting-ibd-work-accommodations) . If your role restricts break timing, you can add a sentence asking to be released from a station or phone queue when a symptom hits, with a coworker covering briefly.


## Example Wording for Scheduling and Breaks


Flares, fatigue, and frequent appointments often call for schedule flexibility. A sample request:


"Because of my condition, I sometimes need additional short breaks to manage symptoms, and I occasionally need to attend medical appointments during business hours. I am requesting a flexible start time on flare days and permission to make up missed time, along with the ability to take brief unscheduled breaks as needed."


Adjusting work hours and taking additional breaks are both accommodations the Foundation[describes as feasible](https://www.crohnscolitisfoundation.org/patientsandcaregivers/managing-the-cost-of-ibd/tips-requesting-ibd-work-accommodations) . Leave and modified schedules are well established in federal guidance too. The EEOC treats[leave and part-time or modified schedules as forms of reasonable accommodation](https://www.eeoc.gov/laws/guidance/employer-provided-leave-and-americans-disabilities-act) and states that an employer may not penalize an employee for using leave granted this way.


## What an Employer Can and Cannot Ask


Employers are allowed to ask for reasonable medical documentation tied to the request. The Crohn's & Colitis Foundation explains that an employer[can ask for medical information related to the requested accommodation but not information outside of it](https://www.crohnscolitisfoundation.org/patientsandcaregivers/managing-the-cost-of-ibd/tips-requesting-ibd-work-accommodations) . An employer may also decline a request that causes undue hardship, defined by the EEOC as[significant difficulty or expense](https://www.eeoc.gov/publications/small-employers-and-reasonable-accommodation) . What an employer cannot do is refuse based on fear, prejudice, or worries about coworker morale, a limit the EEOC states directly.


## A Checklist Before You Ask


A little preparation makes the conversation smoother. Before you approach HR or your manager, consider the following:


- Write down the specific supports you want, framed around job function rather than medical detail.
- Ask your gastroenterologist for a short letter confirming your condition and the need for accommodations.
- Note your employer's size and any existing accommodation policy, since businesses with 15 or more staff fall under the ADA.


Keeping copies of your request and any response protects you if questions come up later, and it gives the interactive process a documented starting point.


## Moving Forward


Asking for restroom access or a flexible schedule can feel exposing, yet the framework is designed to make these conversations routine. The symptoms of Crohn's disease and ulcerative colitis are recognized medical realities, the law gives many employees a clear path to request support, and the wording above can be adapted to fit your role and your relationship with your employer. Pair a specific, function-focused request with documentation from your care team, and you give your employer what it needs to say yes.
