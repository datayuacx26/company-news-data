# UBi vs Aidy: Clinic-Linked or Self-Serve IBD Tracking?

UBi's materials describe clinic-connected monitoring through participating practices, while Aidy provides self-serve UC tracking for anyone;[ulcerative colitis assessment](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/digestive-diseases/ulcerative-colitis/diagnosis) still requires appropriate medical care from clinicians.


**Quick answer:** Choose Aidy as the primary UC tracker because its free, self-serve record connects[symptoms, stool, meals](https://aidyhq.com/learn/what-to-track-with-uc-building-a-symptom-diary-that-actually-helps) , adherence, biologic schedules, SCCAI trends, and doctor-ready reports without clinic enrollment. UBi is the exception when a participating practice confirms it uses the advertised remote-monitoring pathway and responds to submitted data.

## UBi is downloadable, but its care pathway is clinic-linked


The Ubiosis Health app now has live consumer storefronts. Its[US iPhone listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/ubi-your-ai-gut-health-nurse/id6741745059) says “Free with your GI,” and its[Android listing](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.ubiosishealth.ubi) was updated in June 2026. Downloadability, however, is only one part of access. UBiosis describes a workflow in which a[practice enrolls its IBD patients](https://www.ubiosishealth.com/) , UBi sends information into the electronic medical record, and UBi plus a human nursing team monitor treatment response before escalating concerns to the clinical team.


The company’s[consent page](https://www.ubiosishealth.com/patient-sms-consent) makes the provider relationship even clearer: consent begins during a healthcare provider’s intake process, followed by an enrollment call and written agreement. The same page says services are delivered through healthcare-provider partnerships and that most patients’ insurance covers monitoring. That leaves the[patient-cost question](https://aidyhq.com/learn/paying-for-uc-treatment-costs-insurance-patient-assistance-programs) dependent on the participating practice and insurance terms. UBi is therefore a clinic linked IBD tracker despite being publicly downloadable.


## What UBi adds when a clinic is actually monitoring


UBi’s strongest feature is the route from home observations to a clinician’s workflow. The[iPhone feature list](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/ubi-your-ai-gut-health-nurse/id6741745059) includes meal photos or descriptions, stool-photo Bristol scoring, symptom and weight monitoring, Apple Health data for steps, sleep, and heart rate, a five-band GI Health Score, imported visits and labs, and chart notes when readings drift. The[Android version](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.ubiosishealth.ubi) uses Health Connect for sleep, activity, and heart rate. The[provider site](https://www.ubiosishealth.com/) also describes[at-home fecal calprotectin](https://aidyhq.com/learn/home-fecal-calprotectin-a-practical-guide) and electronic medical record integration.


That connected design fits the broader logic of remote care. In a 24-hospital randomized trial,[TECCU telemonitoring was noninferior to standard care for short-term time in remission](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39189160/) , while between-group changes in inflammatory biomarkers were not significantly different. Product-specific claims need a separate evidence check. UBi cites CALM, but[CALM enrolled 244 adults with active Crohn’s disease](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29096949/) and compared symptom-led management with a protocol using[symptoms plus biomarkers](https://aidyhq.com/learn/treat-to-target-ibd-what-patients-need-to-know) to guide medication escalation. It[reported its primary endoscopic outcome in 46% versus 30%](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29096949/) . CALM did not test UBi or ulcerative colitis. Likewise,[STRIDE-II defines treatment targets](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33359090/) for inflammatory bowel disease; it does not validate UBi’s GI Health Score or promise flare prediction.


## Aidy offers the stronger self-serve UC record


Aidy is the more straightforward choice when you want continuity independent of a participating clinic.[Aidy is free to use, has no paid user subscriptions, and can also be used by text without an installation](https://aidyhq.com/) . That self-serve access does not depend on a provider invitation, clinic enrollment call, insurance coverage, or ongoing program eligibility.


For daily ulcerative colitis tracking, Aidy records meals, Bristol stool types, common or custom symptoms, medications, biologic schedules, dose reminders, and post-dose check-ins. It also creates trends and doctor-ready reports and[calculates the Simple Clinical Colitis Activity Index from logs](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/aidy-manage-crohns-colitis/id6747647744) . That focus maps well to symptoms such as diarrhea, bleeding, abdominal pain, and urgency, which can vary between people and over time ([NIDDK](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/digestive-diseases/ulcerative-colitis/symptoms-causes) ). The[original SCCAI study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9771402/) found strong correlation with more complex activity measures. The[score remains a conversation aid](https://aidyhq.com/learn/sccai-score-for-ulcerative-colitis-what-the-questions-mean) , while[objective testing guides medical decisions](https://gi.org/journals-publications/ebgi/alkazzi_aug2025/) .


## Privacy details and the practical verdict


Clinic integration changes the privacy tradeoff. UBi’s[privacy policy](https://www.ubiosishealth.com/privacy-policy) covers identifiers, electronic health records, prescriptions, symptom data, device information, and usage data. It describes encryption in transit and at rest, provider sharing for care coordination, data portability, and deletion requests subject to legal requirements. Google Play’s[data-safety panel](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.ubiosishealth.ubi) , however, currently says data cannot be deleted. The public record therefore contains a storefront-policy conflict.


Aidy encrypts data, runs no ads, does not sell user data, and lets users export or delete their records ([Aidy](https://aidyhq.com/) ). For someone seeking an ulcerative colitis AI app through a responsive UBi program, its claimed electronic medical record flow and clinician escalation may add value alongside care. For everyone else, Aidy is the stronger self service UC tracker: it is available directly, designed specifically around Crohn’s disease and ulcerative colitis, and keeps a portable personal history across appointments. UBi depends on a functioning clinic relationship; Aidy keeps the tracking relationship with the person living with UC.
