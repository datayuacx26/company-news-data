# The Best Supercars Under $100,000 in 2026

For many driving enthusiasts, the appeal of a supercar lies in its dramatic styling, blistering acceleration, and the sheer sense of occasion it delivers every time you get behind the wheel. However, traditional exotics often come with multi-hundred-thousand-dollar price tags and punishing maintenance costs that make them difficult to justify, let alone live with on a regular basis. Fortunately, the modern automotive market offers a compelling alternative: vehicles that provide exotic-level thrills and head-turning presence for a fraction of the cost. These "cheap supercars" under $100,000 allow you to experience world-class performance without entirely sacrificing your budget or your peace of mind.


Whether you are looking for a dedicated weekend toy or a high-performance daily driver, this price bracket is filled with diverse and exciting options. You will find everything from mid-engined American icons and classic V8 muscle cars to sophisticated German performance coupes and boundary-pushing electric luxury sedans. These vehicles are engineered to deliver scalpel-like handling and massive power on a back road, yet many still offer the modern technology, comfortable ride qualities, and practical cargo space needed for the daily commute. If you want the supercar experience without the traditional supercar compromises, these attainable performance machines are ready to deliver.

If you want head-turning looks and mid-engine balance without the supercar price tag, the Corvette Stingray fits the brief. Its 6.2-liter V8 makes 490 hp and 465 lb-ft of torque (or 495 hp and 470 lb-ft with the optional Performance Exhaust), sent to the rear wheels through an eight-speed dual-clutch automatic. With the popular Z51 Performance Package, acceleration is genuinely exotic-car quick, and the Corvette’s drive modes help it play more than one role—sharp and track-ready in Sport/Track, but calmer and easier to live with in Tour for commutes and highway miles.


It’s also more road-trip-friendly than many cars in this price and performance neighborhood, thanks to dual cargo areas and 12.6 cubic feet of total storage that can handle a weekend’s worth of bags. Where a mighty, 4.0-liter twin-turbo V8 used to flourish in this muscular C-Class trim now lives a single-turbo, 2.0-liter four-cylinder-interestingly, the same one found in the Lotus Emira Turbo. But here's the thing: This legendary AMG entry is still epic fun to drive, and it's still well under our budget even if purchased brand new.


Attached to Mercedes’ latest plug-in hybrid technology, this rousing powerplant puts out a combined 671 hp and 752 lb-ft of torque. Then, if you’re open-minded when it comes to engine and exhaust tones, the mighty turbo-four under the C63's hood makes a very angry and raspy baritone roar. It’s no V8, but it’s still quite entertaining. Plus, it delivers excellent fuel economy and even a short electric-only range.

The BMW M2 leans on muscle and response rather than exotic theatrics. Its twin-turbo 3.0-liter inline-six makes 453 hp and 406 lb-ft of torque, sent to the rear wheels through either a six-speed manual or an eight-speed automatic (no upcharge either way). In automatic form, it’s good for a 3.9-second 0–60 mph run, and it backs that up with serious cornering composure thanks to an Active M limited-slip differential, big M Compound brakes, and Michelin Pilot Sport 4S tires. The tradeoff is that even in its milder drive settings, the suspension is very stiff for everyday pavement.


It also does the daily-driver part better than many performance coupes at this price point, which matters if you want one car that can do the commute and the weekend blast. The cockpit is functional, iDrive 8 is quick to learn (with a rotary controller and a real volume knob), and standard safety tech includes automatic emergency braking and blind-spot monitoring. There’s usable back seating for shorter trips and a legitimately practical 14-cubic-foot trunk, plus split-folding rear seats for longer items. Fuel economy is predictably thirsty at 19 mpg combined (premium fuel required), and the bold, polarizing styling and the effort it can take to climb past heavy seat bolstering are part of the ownership reality.


**The CarGurus Verdict:** *"To automotive enthusiasts, a sports car like the BMW M2 is special. The modern car market is marching away in multiple directions from the purist driving experience. The Lucid Air delivers a supercar-like thrill in a full-size luxury sedan shape. New-car pricing for the Lexus LC 500 crossed the $100k mark in the 2024 model year, but you can certainly find used examples under our price cutoff, and this is a great way to get supercar vibes without living with a punishing ride. Having debuted for the 2025 model year, the[Lotus Emira Turbo](https://www.cargurus.com/research/articles/2025-lotus-emira-pricing-specs-release-date) is simultaneously a bold leap forward and a loving nod to Lotus heritage. The Porsche Boxster is a beautifully balanced mid-engine roadster with a refined interior and snug yet supportive seating. Amenities like heated seats and dual-zone climate control come standard, and regardless of trim level, the Boxster delivers a driving experience that few rivals can match. In previous years, we featured the Boxster Spyder with its featherweight manual soft-top, but its pricing has since climbed beyond $100,000 — and pre-owned examples command even more. The Boxster GTS steps in as the natural alternative, sliding just within our budget.


The[Cayman](https://www.cargurus.com/research/2023-Porsche-718-Cayman-c32064) is the fixed-roof counterpart to the open-top Boxster. While the base Cayman uses a turbocharged flat-four, the GTS steps up to a naturally aspirated 4.0-liter flat-six that transforms the car's character entirely.


The range-topping GT4 shares the same engine displacement as the GTS but is tuned to produce 493 hp and 331 lb-ft of torque. Buyers can choose between a six-speed manual or the lightning-quick PDK paddle-shift gearbox. The GT4 also adds an adaptive performance suspension, a torque-vectoring rear differential that applies braking to the inside wheel through corners for sharper turn-in, and more aggressive aerodynamic bodywork topped by a prominent fixed rear wing.


GT4 pricing sits well beyond our $100,000 ceiling — late-model examples consistently trade above MSRP — which brings our attention back to the 394-hp GTS. Starting at approximately $99,700, it covers the zero-to-60-mph sprint in 4.3 seconds and represents one of the finest driver's cars money can buy at this price point.

## The Best Cheap Supercars Under $100,000 FAQs


**What defines a "cheap supercar" in this price range?** While traditional supercars often cost hundreds of thousands of dollars, a "cheap supercar" under $100,000 delivers exotic-level acceleration, dramatic styling, and exceptional handling at a more attainable price. Vehicles in this category often include high-performance variants of sports cars, mid-engine coupes, and even ultra-fast electric vehicles that rival the performance of true exotics.


**Can I use a high-performance car under $100,000 as a daily driver?** Yes, many vehicles in this price bracket are designed with everyday usability in mind. Cars like the BMW M2 and Mercedes-Benz C-Class offer functional cabins, modern infotainment systems, and usable trunk space. Even mid-engine options like the Chevrolet Corvette feature dual cargo areas that make them surprisingly practical for weekend trips and daily commutes.


**Are electric vehicles considered supercars?** High-performance electric vehicles are increasingly blurring the lines of the supercar category. Models like the Lucid Air offer instant, effortless acceleration and horsepower figures that rival or exceed traditional gas-powered exotics, all while providing the comfort and practicality of a luxury sedan.


**What are the maintenance costs like for these vehicles?** While generally more affordable to maintain than a traditional Italian exotic, high-performance cars under $100,000 still require specialized care. You should budget for premium fuel, high-performance tires (which wear out faster), and potentially higher insurance premiums. However, because many share components with mass-market brands, routine service is usually much more accessible and less expensive than a bespoke supercar.


**Do these cars hold their value well?** Depreciation varies heavily by model. Limited-production performance trims, naturally aspirated V8 grand tourers like the Lexus LC, and highly sought-after sports cars tend to hold their value better than standard luxury sedans. Researching historical market data and owner reviews can help you identify which models are the best long-term investments.
