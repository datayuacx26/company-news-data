# The Best Gas Mileage SUVs of 2026

Fuel costs are a real consideration for most car shoppers, and SUVs have historically been among the thirstier vehicles on the road. That's changed significantly in recent years, with hybrid powertrains now available across a wide range of SUV sizes and price points—from budget-friendly subcompact crossovers to near-luxury options. The best gas mileage SUVs of 2026 can deliver 40 mpg or more in combined driving, which translates to meaningful savings at the pump over time, especially for drivers who log a lot of miles on their daily commute.


This guide covers 10 SUVs that stand out for their fuel efficiency, drawing on real-world testing, EPA ratings, and CarGurus market data to help you find the right fit. The list includes options at different price points and sizes, from the Kia Niro Hybrid's class-leading 53 mpg combined to the Toyota RAV4's newly standard hybrid powertrain projected at up to 44 mpg combined. Whether you're prioritizing cargo space, all-wheel drive, or a near-luxury cabin, there's likely a fuel-efficient SUV here that matches your needs without requiring you to make major compromises.

Toyota's redesigned 2026 RAV4 leans hard into efficiency by making a hybrid powertrain standard across the lineup, with Toyota projecting up to 44 MPG combined for both FWD and AWD versions. That hybrid setup also brings a useful bump in everyday power: FWD models pair a 2.5-liter four-cylinder with two electric motors for 226 hp, while AWD adds a third motor at the rear for 236 hp, giving the RAV4 quick low-speed response in traffic without demanding much at the pump. If you move up to heavier AWD trims like XSE and Limited, Toyota expects about 42 MPG combined, while the taller, all-terrain-tire Woodland trim is projected at 39 MPG combined.


For families shopping efficient SUVs, the RAV4 keeps its familiar footprint and passenger space, but it's still easy to live with thanks to smart storage and a square cargo area rated at 37.8 cubic feet with the rear seats up and 70.4 cubic feet with them folded. Tech also gets a meaningful refresh with a standard 12.3-inch digital instrument cluster, wireless Apple CarPlay and Android Auto, and Toyota's quicker Arene operating system; available features like a built-in dash cam add day-to-day convenience. Safety is another strong point, with Toyota Safety Sense 4.0 standard (including upgraded automatic emergency braking and lane-centering assistance), and Toyota expects pricing to start in the low $30,000s—helpful context when the standard hybrid setup is designed to lower fuel costs over time versus a gas-only SUV.

## The Best Gas Mileage SUVs of 2026 FAQs


**Which SUV gets the best gas mileage in 2026?**


Among the SUVs on this list, the Kia Niro Hybrid leads the pack with an EPA-estimated 53 MPG combined on non-Touring trims—the highest figure of any model covered here. Its 11.1-gallon tank also provides a real-world range of around 588 miles between fill-ups. If you need a larger SUV, the Toyota Corolla Cross Hybrid and Toyota RAV4 are strong runners-up, with combined ratings of 42 MPG and up to 44 MPG combined, respectively.


**Do I have to choose between fuel efficiency and cargo space?**


Not necessarily, but there are trade-offs depending on which model you choose. The Honda CR-V Hybrid and Kia Sportage Hybrid offer some of the most generous cargo volumes on this list—39.3 and 39.5 cubic feet behind the rear seats, respectively—while still delivering strong mpg figures. Smaller options like the Lexus UX Hybrid and Toyota Corolla Cross Hybrid prioritize efficiency but come with more limited cargo areas, so it's worth measuring your typical hauling needs before deciding.


**Does choosing all-wheel drive significantly hurt fuel economy in a hybrid SUV?**


It depends on the model, but AWD can meaningfully reduce mpg. The Kia Sportage Hybrid, for example, drops from 42 MPG combined in FWD form to 35 MPG combined with AWD. The Honda CR-V Hybrid goes from 40 MPG combined (FWD) to 37 MPG combined (AWD). Some models, like the Toyota Corolla Cross Hybrid and Lexus UX Hybrid, come standard with AWD and are still rated at 42 MPG and 39 MPG combined, respectively, so the impact varies. If maximum fuel economy is your priority, check the specific FWD vs. AWD ratings for the model you're considering.


**Are these hybrid SUVs more expensive to buy than non-hybrid versions?**


Generally, yes—hybrid versions typically carry a price premium over their non-hybrid counterparts. However, several models on this list are designed to offset that cost over time through fuel savings. Mazda estimates the CX-50 Hybrid's premium pays back in about four years for a driver covering 15,000 miles annually. The 2026 Toyota RAV4 takes a different approach by making the hybrid powertrain standard across the entire lineup, starting in the low $30,000s, which removes the choice entirely and makes the efficiency benefit available from the base trim.


**What safety features should I expect on a fuel-efficient SUV in 2026?**


Most of the SUVs on this list come with a strong suite of standard driver-assistance features. Toyota models include Toyota Safety Sense 3.0 or 4.0, which covers automatic emergency braking, lane-centering assistance, and adaptive cruise control. The Kia Niro Hybrid includes blind-spot monitoring and forward collision avoidance as standard equipment. The Lexus NX Hybrid earned an IIHS Top Safety Pick+ award and includes Lexus Safety System+ 3.0. In general, you can expect automatic emergency braking and some form of lane-keeping assistance across the lineup, though features like blind-spot monitoring and rear cross-traffic alert may be reserved for higher trims on some models.
