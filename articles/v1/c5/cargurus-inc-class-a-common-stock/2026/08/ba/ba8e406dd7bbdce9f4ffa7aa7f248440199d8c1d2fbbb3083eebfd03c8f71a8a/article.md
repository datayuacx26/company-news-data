# The Best 7-Passenger SUVs of 2026

When the time comes to transport more than a handful of people, a 7-passenger SUV is often the perfect solution. These vehicles offer the versatility to handle everything from the daily school run to a cross-country family road trip, blending the passenger capacity of a minivan with the rugged styling and available all-wheel-drive capability of an SUV. With three rows of seating, they provide the flexibility to carry a full load of passengers or fold down the rear seats to create a cavernous cargo area for luggage, sports equipment, or home improvement supplies.


The modern 7-passenger SUV market is more diverse than ever, offering a wide range of options to suit nearly any need or budget. Shoppers can choose from efficient hybrids and fully electric models like the Hyundai Ioniq 9, luxurious and powerful options such as the BMW X7 and Lincoln Aviator, or practical and value-packed choices like the Kia Telluride and Honda Pilot. Whether you prioritize a premium interior, engaging driving dynamics, cutting-edge technology, or maximum cargo space, there's a three-row SUV designed to meet your family's demands.

Few three-row SUVs have cracked the passenger space puzzle quite like the 2026 Honda Pilot. Its standout feature is the removable second-row center seat — giving owners the flexibility to switch between bench and captain's chair configurations without sacrificing either option permanently. The seat stows neatly under the cargo floor in a dedicated compartment, and the outboard second-row seats are genuinely spacious for adults, sliding fore and aft by a generous amount and reclining for longer trips. Third-row access is as easy as a single button push, which folds and slides the second row forward automatically. With up to 112.4 cubic feet of cargo space when both rows are folded, the Pilot's versatility rivals a minivan. Powering it all is a 285-horsepower 3.5-liter V6 paired with a 10-speed automatic, with AWD standard on most trims.


The Pilot's appeal on this list goes beyond raw seating numbers, though — it's the thoughtful details that set it apart from less imaginative rivals. A Cabin Talk feature uses the audio system as an intercom so the driver can communicate with rear-row passengers without raising their voice, and multiple camera angles assist with tight parking and light off-roading. For 2026, Honda upgrades to a standard 12.3-inch touchscreen and a 10.2-inch digital instrument panel across all trims, with wireless Apple CarPlay and Android Auto included. The Honda Sensing suite of safety features — adaptive cruise control, lane-keep assist, blind-spot monitoring, and automatic emergency braking among them — comes standard on every Pilot. The 2025 model earned a five-star NHTSA overall rating and IIHS Top Safety Pick recognition, and with no structural changes for 2026, similarly strong results are expected.

Few three-row SUVs make as strong a first impression as the Volkswagen Atlas, and fewer still back it up with genuinely competitive passenger and cargo space. The cabin is one of the segment's most upscale at this price point, with quilted diamond-pattern leather, metallic accents, and warm wood trim in upper trims that put it in the same visual league as vehicles costing significantly more. All three rows are thoughtfully executed: the front seats offer heat, ventilation, and massage on the top trim, the second-row captain's chairs fold and tip forward without collapsing (a godsend when a car seat is installed), and the third-row bench sits high enough off the floor to provide real thigh support—something many competitors miss. Cargo capacity is a genuine competitive advantage, with 20.6 cubic feet behind the third row, 55.5 cubic feet with it folded, and a class-competitive 96.6 cubic feet total. A five-star NHTSA overall safety rating and a standard IQ.Drive suite—bundling adaptive cruise control, automatic emergency braking, blind-spot monitoring, lane-keep assist, and semi-autonomous Travel Assist across every trim—round out a strong value proposition, with base pricing starting below most rivals in the segment.


On the road, the turbocharged 2.0-liter four-cylinder delivers 269 hp and 273 lb-ft of torque with a wide, usable powerband that keeps highway driving relaxed and composed. AWD is available across the lineup and standard on the Peak Edition and above, with a dedicated Snow mode for added confidence in slippery conditions. The main trade-offs are well-documented: ride quality on larger wheel-and-tire packages can feel stiff and harsh over rough pavement, and the native MIB3 infotainment system is genuinely frustrating to use. The good news on both counts is that smaller wheels on lower trims may smooth things out, and wireless Apple CarPlay and Android Auto work flawlessly as a daily alternative to the native system. For families who want a seven-passenger SUV that looks premium, hauls people and cargo with ease, and undercuts much of the competition on price, the Atlas makes a compelling case.
