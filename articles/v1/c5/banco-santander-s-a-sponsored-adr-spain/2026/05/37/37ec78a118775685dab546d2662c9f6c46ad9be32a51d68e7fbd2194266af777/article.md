# EU–Mercosur: Beyond trade in a fragmented global order

##


EU–Mercosur: Beyond trade in a fragmented global order


The report released by the Spanish Institute for Strategic Studies, authored by Ramón Casilda Béjar, examines the EU–Mercosur Association Agreement from an economic, geopolitical and strategic perspective. The report argues that the agreement goes far beyond trade by representing a commitment to multilateralism, European strategic autonomy, and the creation of a shared economic and political space amid growing global competition between the US and China.


Key takeaways:


- **One of the largest free trade areas globally:** The agreement connects 720 million people, removes over 90% of tariffs, and accounts for around 25% of global GDP.


- **Strong geopolitical dimension:** It reshapes supply chains and strengthens both blocs’ strategic positioning in an increasingly fragmented world.


- **Access to critical resources:** The EU secures strategic raw materials, while Mercosur gains access to technology and industrial upgrading opportunities.


- **Values-based framework:** It is the first agreement to integrate Paris Agreement commitments, labour standards, and sustainability requirements.


- **Three structural pillars:** Commercial (liberalization), political (strategic alliance), and cooperation (sustainable development and institutional strengthening).


- **Strategic response to the US and China:** The EU promotes a “third way” based on rules and multilateralism, which contrasts with US protectionism and China’s state-led model.


- **Significant economic impact:** Estimated annual tariff savings of €4 billion to boost trade and competitiveness.


- **Ratification challenges:** Political resistance, particularly in agriculture, has led to safeguard mechanisms and delays.


- **Latin America as a key partner:** Critical for Europe’s strategic autonomy, especially in energy, raw materials, and supply chain relocation.


- **A broader global vision:** The agreement is a commitment to a rules-based international order grounded in cooperation, sustainability, and democratic values.
