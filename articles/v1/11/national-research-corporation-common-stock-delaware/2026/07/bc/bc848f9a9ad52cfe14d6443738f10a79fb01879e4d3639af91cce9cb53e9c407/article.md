# Transforming Pediatric Provider Communication


Healthcare leaders continue to search for better ways to strengthen trust, improve communication, and support care teams. Many training programs focus on theory, scripts, or peer review. Yet one voice often remains underused in provider development. That voice is the voice of the patient and family.


For


[pediatric healthcare leaders](https://nrchealth.com/blog/navigating-healthcare-transformation/) , this gap is even more complex. Communication often involves both the child and the caregiver at the same time. Each interaction carries emotional weight and requires clarity, empathy, and trust across two audiences.

## Why Provider Communication Remains a Challenge in Healthcare


[Communication shapes every healthcare experience](https://nrchealth.com/nsight/building-trust-during-care-transitions/) . In


[pediatric care](https://nrchealth.com/blog/nemours-tour-pediatric-collaborative/) , trust must be built with both the child and the caregiver at once. A provider may explain a diagnosis to a parent while helping a child feel calm and safe.


Strong communication supports outcomes and


[employee experience](https://nrchealth.com/solutions/employee-experience/) . It also reduces confusion and stress for families who face difficult decisions.


Yet many clinicians rarely receive direct feedback on how their communication feels in real time. They may review survey data weeks later. That delay limits growth; small behaviors that define the experience often go unnoticed.


Organizations can close this gap by bringing the patient and family voice into the moment of care and using it to guide improvement.


## A Patient and Family Perspective on Provider Communication


This HUB26 session will drawn upon the work of


[Anand Petigara, MSW, Senior Family Consultant at Children’s Hospital of Philadelphia](https://www.linkedin.com/in/apetigara) . His role connects his professional training with his lived experience as a caregiver of a child with complex medical needs.


That dual lens shapes how Petigara works with providers. He partners with teams to strengthen relationships with patients and families across care settings. His work includes family advisory councils and programs that place the family voice at the center of care decisions.


At


[HUB26](https://nrchealth.com/events/hub) , Petigara will share how these ideas come to life through FLOURISH, a provider coaching program built on observation and feedback from the family perspective.


In pediatric settings, this model helps providers adjust communication across age groups while guiding caregivers through complex or emotional situations.


## How Observation-Based Coaching Improves Provider Communication


Traditional coaching often relies on self-reflection or peer input. Observation-based coaching adds a direct view of real interactions in clinical settings, where trained observers watch encounters and capture details that shape experience. Tone, pacing, body language, and clarity all matter in pediatric care; providers must respond to both a child’s emotional state and a caregiver’s need for clear information.


These encounters often carry strong emotion. Families may be managing fear, confusion, or long-term conditions. Observation helps identify how providers respond in these moments and how small changes can improve trust.


Participants in this session will learn how direct observation reveals blind spots and strengthens emotional awareness. They will see how feedback rooted in real interactions helps providers improve with clarity.


This model focuses on strengths. Coaches highlight what works and build from there, creating a supportive environment where clinicians feel safe to grow.

## Why Patient Experience and Communication Matter Now More Than Ever


Healthcare organizations face growing pressure to improve


[patient experience](https://nrchealth.com/solutions/patient-experience/) and support staff well-being. In pediatric care, families expect clear guidance, compassion, and consistency across every interaction.


Small shifts in behavior can change how families experience care. A clear explanation helps a parent make decisions; a calm tone helps a child feel safe. A moment of attention builds trust that lasts beyond the visit.


Programs like FLOURISH show that improvement starts with real interactions. Teams can learn from what happens in the room and apply those lessons across the organization.


This session shows how to turn everyday moments into opportunities for growth.
