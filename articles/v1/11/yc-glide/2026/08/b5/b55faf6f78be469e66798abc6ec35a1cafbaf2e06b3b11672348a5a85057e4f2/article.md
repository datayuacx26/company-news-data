## What the Bending Spoons acquisition could mean for Airtable users


Bending Spoons has a well-known playbook. Its public strategy, by its own account, is to acquire software companies at a discount, cut costs, and "streamline" products to make them profitable more quickly. That approach has meant real, visible changes at the brands it's bought: fewer resources devoted to free tiers, leaner support teams, and product roadmaps that shift toward monetization over new capability.


Airtable fits that description: it was valued at over $11 billion in 2021, and its shares had reportedly been trading on secondary markets at around $4 billion[earlier this year,](https://www.axios.com/2026/08/04/bending-spoons-airtable) well above the roughly $2.25 billion equity value implied by this deal.


Nothing has been announced about specific product or pricing changes at Airtable, and the deal isn't expected to close until later in 2026, pending regulatory approval. But it's worth noting that Bending Spoons frames its own approach around acquiring companies and then focusing on operational efficiency, and its[co-founder described](https://investors.bendingspoons.com/newsroom/bending-spoons-agrees-to-acquire-airtable) the Airtable deal around "doubling down" on the product's "core strength", language that, at other acquisitions, has often preceded a narrower, more consolidated roadmap rather than an expanding one.


But it's reasonable for any business that depends on Airtable for its databases, apps, and automations to start asking a practical question: what happens if support quality drops, prices rise, or product investment slows, as it has at other Bending Spoons companies?


For a business that has built operations, client portals, or internal tools on top of Airtable, that uncertainty alone is reason enough to have a backup plan, or to start moving now, on your own timeline, rather than later under pressure. The good news is that migrating off Airtable doesn't have to be disruptive, and GlideOS makes it fast.
