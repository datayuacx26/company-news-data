For the past five years, my daily work has focused on creating custom software tools for growing businesses. I have spent thousands of hours working alongside business owners, translating operational bottlenecks into clean, functional digital dashboards. Historically, this meant spending days mapping out databases, configuring conditional visibility rules, and manually connecting backend tables.


The introduction of AI engineering and[GlideOS](https://www.glideapps.com/os) changes this process entirely.


No-code platforms already made development visual, but AI engineering takes accessibility a step further. Instead of manually aligning components or wiring data structures cell by cell, business leaders can now use natural language and existing data to build software much faster. This change allows operations teams to shift their focus away from technical configuration and toward high-level strategy and system design.


In this article, I’d like to share some of my insights that can help business leaders make the most of new AI engineering tools.


## Why AI engineering is becoming more popular


AI engineering is growing in popularity because it removes the technical friction of software development, allowing businesses to launch custom applications in a fraction of the time. While traditional no-code requires a baseline understanding of database logic, modern platforms let you interact directly with an intelligent system that does the heavy lifting for you.


The unique advantage of GlideOS lies in how it interprets business data. If you use generic AI coding tools, you often receive raw snippets of code that you must still host, secure, and maintain. GlideOS works differently by looking directly at your operational data.


Many businesses run on spreadsheets that have been modified by multiple teams over several years. These sheets are often filled with scattered notes, formulas, and unorganized rows. GlideOS can ingest these files, analyze the relationships between columns, and automatically create the app structure. You do not need to become a prompt engineer; you simply bring your existing business information, and the platform interprets your operational reality.


## How to get started with your data


Business leaders can start building custom tools either by using natural language prompts or by uploading an existing spreadsheet into GlideOS. Both methods allow you to quickly go from a rough operational idea to a functioning prototype, provided you follow a methodical approach.


### How to start with an existing spreadsheet


If you choose to use your company's existing spreadsheets as a starting point, you can optimize the build by following three practical rules:


- Start with a smaller version: Do not upload a massive workbook containing dozens of tabs on your first day. Create a simplified version of your spreadsheet with just a few rows of data. This approach lets you see how the system interprets your data structure while keeping token usage low.
- Keep your formulas active: Ensure your spreadsheet contains real numbers and active calculations. The system reads these active formulas to understand how your numbers are connected, making it easier to automatically recreate those calculations in your new application.
- Organize your columns: While the intelligence layer can parse messy sheets, providing clean headers and clearly defined rows will give you a much more accurate initial application structure.


### How to start with a chat prompt


If you prefer to start with a blank canvas and build your application entirely through conversation with the GlideOS Agent, you can avoid disorganized configurations by using a structured, two-step method:


1. Use an interview prompt: Instead of asking the system to build a complex tool all at once, begin the conversation by defining your goal and asking for feedback. Try a prompt like: "I want to build a system to track multi-location warehouse inventory. Act as an expert database architect and interview me before making the plan." This forces the system to ask clarifying questions about your specific business rules first.
2. Build in clear stages: Once you agree on the plan, instruct the system to build the application in a sequential, traceable series of steps. Developing features one by one makes it easy to verify that each requirement works perfectly and prevents the underlying logic from becoming a tangled mess.


## Make an admin app to manage your applications safely


Building a secondary admin console alongside your frontline tools allows your managers to update app workflows without touching any underlying code. When software requires a developer to change a simple dropdown selection or update an employee role, the business faces operational delays.


To give companies true autonomy, I recommend using an administrative management app that controls your primary production tools.


Instead of hardcoding static variables, like workflow stages, user permission tags, or dropdown lists, directly into the main visual interface, you store these settings as rows in a dedicated configuration table. You then build a simple, secure dashboard that lets your managers update those rows.


Through this management console, an administrator can safely handle daily software updates:


- Modify workflows: Rename pipeline stages dynamically (for example, changing a status from "In Progress" to "Under Review") without opening a software builder.
- Manage the team: Update employee access flags and toggle specific software features on or off based on user roles.
- Run operations: Broadcast team-wide announcements, receive internal bug reports, and monitor user activity logs across the company.


Because the primary production tool reads its layout rules directly from this configuration table, the software adapts instantly to your changes. The business gains total operational control, and the underlying application architecture remains safe from accidental clicks.
