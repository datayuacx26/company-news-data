## 3 common ways to send money in Dubai


Here’s the thing: depending on whether you're paying rent or just splitting the bill for a late-night shawarma, the "best" way to move your money changes. Too often, people default to what they already know – but that usually means dealing with clunky interfaces and long wait times.


Here are the 3 different ways to send money in Dubai based on what you need:


### 1. Use payment apps to split bills and send money to friends in seconds


Payment apps let you send, receive, and manage money directly from your phone.


You download the app, create an account, and connect a bank account or card. From there, you can send money using a phone number, username, QR code, or payment link – without having to exchange bank details.


### Pros and cons


Pros Cons


Opening an account with a payment app can be much faster than opening a traditional bank account.


Not always preferable for large or formal payments like rent, deposits, or business transactions.


Payments often arrive instantly, making it easier to settle up with friends.


Some apps have transfer limits or restrictions on certain payment types.


There may be multiple payment options available, from direct transfers using a username or a phone number to payment links and QR codes.


You’ll still need to top up your wallet or link a bank account/card to your app, which adds a small extra step.


Payment apps are built for small everyday moments when money needs to move quickly.


You might use one to:


- Pay a friend back after dinner or a taxi ride


- Split groceries


- Collect money for a group gift or a trip


- Get paid for a small, quick sale (like a second-hand top)

### 2. Use direct bank transfers for larger or formal payments


As you probably already know, bank transfers allow you to send money straight from your bank account into another.


To make a transfer, you'll need to enter your payee’s bank details (such as their IBAN or routing number) into your banking app and confirm your payment details. Then your bank will process the transfer and send the funds into your payee’s account.


### Pros and cons


Pros Cons


Highly secure because your money moves through regulated banking networks


It can take anywhere from a few minutes to 5+ business days to settle payments


Typically low-cost for local AED transfers


It’s easy to enter the wrong IBAN, which can delay or even ‘fail’ your payment


Leaves a formal record of the transaction


Awkward for small everyday payments like splitting bills or paying friends back


International transfers can be expensive, especially when routed through the SWIFT network, which can cost upwards of 150 AED1 per transaction.


Traditional bank transfers suit best when you’re making a large payment or want a formal paper trail, for example, when you’re sending rent to a landlord every month or paying a large one-off bill like medical or legal fees.


### 3. Use money transfer services to send money in Dubai and overseas


Money transfer services are specialized financial platforms (such as Wise, Western Union, or Al Ansari Exchange) that help you move money within the UAE and overseas.


To use a money transfer service, you pay using your card, bank, or cash, and they deliver the money to the recipient's bank account, mobile wallet, or a physical cash pickup location.


### Pros and cons


Pros Cons


Can be faster than a standard bank wire, with options for instant or same-day delivery.


Transfer limits may apply, which can restrict how much money you can send in a single transaction.


Often offers transparent pricing, so you can see fees and exchange rates before sending.


Fees and charges can vary between providers and destinations. Some services also charge a remittance service fee.2


Widely available, which makes it easy to send money to friends and family in the UAE and in other countries.


Exchange rates may be marked up, so your recipient could receive less than expected.


Money transfer services can be used for local payments, remittances, and larger international transfers. For example, you might:


- Support family or friends abroad


- Pay overseas tuition fees


- Transfer savings between countries


- Make property-related payments overseas
