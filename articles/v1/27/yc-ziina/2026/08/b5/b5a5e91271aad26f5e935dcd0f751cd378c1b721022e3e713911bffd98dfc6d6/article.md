## How to integrate Apple Pay with a website builder using a payment provider’s plugin


It’s usually quite easy to integrate Apple Pay when you’re using a pre-built website such as WordPress or Shopify.


This is because Apple Pay itself doesn’t process payments – it only handles the payment authorization through Face ID, Touch ID, or your customer’s device passcode on compatible iOS devices. Your payment provider is the one who actually processes the transaction in the background.


As such, many payment platforms make it simple for you to integrate Apple Pay on your website using a plugin.

## How to integrate Apple Pay with a custom website using a payment provider’s API


If you have a custom website, you typically don’t need to integrate directly with Apple Pay yourself. Instead, you can use your payment service provider’s Application Programming Interface (API) to add a checkout that already supports Apple Pay.


The exact integration process depends on the checkout options your payment provider offers, but some common approaches include:


### Hosted checkout


A hosted checkout is usually the simplest API integration. Your website creates a payment session or


[payment link](https://ziina.com/blog/payment-links) through your provider’s API and redirects the customer to a checkout page hosted by the payment provider.


The provider manages the checkout experience and payment methods, including Apple Pay, so you don’t need to build or maintain the payment interface yourself.


### Embedded checkout


Some payment providers also let you embed their checkout directly into a page on your website.


In this setup, you control the surrounding page and customer experience, while the payment provider controls the secure payment widget itself. Depending on your provider, you may need to submit your website domain so it can be registered and verified for Apple Pay.


### More customized integrations


Some payment providers offer additional APIs that give developers more control over the checkout experience. For example, a provider could offer custom card components that allow you to adapt parts of the payment interface to your website’s design.


The setup and level of customization available vary by provider, so you’ll need to follow your PSP’s documentation for the specific integration you’re using.
