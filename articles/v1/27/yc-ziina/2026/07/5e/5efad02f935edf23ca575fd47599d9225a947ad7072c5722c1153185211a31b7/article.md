## How does a multi-currency payment gateway work?


A multi-currency payment gateway lets customers pay in their local currency while you receive your funds in AED.


Let's say someone visits your website from Saudi Arabia. Instead of seeing prices only in AED, they can view and pay in SAR. The same goes for customers in the US paying in USD or customers in Europe paying in EUR.


The flow for a UAE business usually looks like this:


1. 1.


You enter the price in AED:


You set your pricing in your base currency.


2. 2.


The customer sees their local currency:


When they open the checkout page, they see the equivalent amount in a currency they recognize based on their IP address or their choice, such as GBP or SAR.


3. 3.


The customer pays in their currency:


They complete the transaction without having to manually convert the price or guess what the final charge on their bank statement will be.


4. 4.


The gateway converts the funds:


The platform processes the payment and handles the conversion back to AED.


5. 5.


You receive an AED settlement:


Your funds are deposited in Dirhams, making your accounting and reconciliation much cleaner.


Note:


Some payment providers let you hold balances in multiple currencies. That can make sense for larger businesses operating across several markets, but for many SMEs, it can add extra accounting work. With payment apps like Ziina, your funds automatically settle in AED.


## How different businesses can use a multi-currency payment gateway: 3 examples


- E-commerce brands selling globally:


Fashion, skincare, and specialty retail brands often find that international shoppers hesitate when they see AED. A multi-currency gateway lets a customer in London pay in GBP while the brand keeps its settlement in AED.


- Travel, tourism, and holiday rentals:


If you’re selling tours or rentals to tourists before they land in Dubai, they need to pay deposits or full balances in their home currency. This reduces friction and helps build trust before the trip begins.


- Consultants and creative agencies:


If you have international clients, you don't want to rely on slow manual bank transfers where payments can be delayed for days. Sending a link to your multi-currency payment gateway is faster, more professional, and saves you from chasing payments across different time zones.


## What to look for in a multi-currency payment gateway


When you're looking for the right payment gateway, ask yourself the following questions:


### 1. Does the gateway support currencies my customers actually use?


Your gateway should support the currencies where your customers actually live. For UAE-based businesses,


[this often includes AED, USD, EUR, GBP, and SAR](https://ziina.com/help-center/8271680-can-i-receive-payments-in-currencies-other-than-aed-on-my-website) .


It's not just about showing the right symbol, either: the entire payment experience should feel familiar. If a customer can pay in their local currency using a recognizable method like


[Apple Pay](https://ziina.com/card/apple-pay) , for instance, they’re far more likely to finish the transaction.


Think ahead, too. If you plan to expand into new markets, make sure your provider supports the regions and currencies you’ll need later.


Switching payment gateways down the line isn’t easy – it often means a lot of technical setup, migrating product or SKU data, and potentially disrupting the SEO or SEM performance you’ve already built.


It’s simpler to just pick a payment partner that can grow with you from the start.


### 2. What are the fees for international cards and currency conversion?


You should understand the true cost of accepting international payments before you commit to any particular payment provider.


Standard transaction fees are only one part of the equation – most providers have a base fee, an additional fee for international cards, and currency conversion fees.


At Ziina, for example, we keep things simple and transparent: 2.6% + 1 AED per transaction, plus a 1.5% fee for international cards and/or non-AED currency.


### 3. How quickly can I cash out into my bank account?


If you have to wait several days to access your money, it can put pressure on cash flow, especially if you need to restock inventory, pay suppliers, or cover day-to-day operating costs.


That's why it's worth paying attention to how quickly a provider settles funds. While timelines vary between payment gateways, some offer settlements within a few business days, and might even provide instant cashouts for some users.


### 4. Can I integrate with a website builder of my choice and customize my checkout?


You've worked hard to build trust throughout the buying journey, and a clunky checkout can undo that in seconds.


This is especially true for international customers, who might be purchasing from your brand for the first time.


When comparing payment gateways, look for one that lets you:


- Easily integrate with


[Shopify](https://docs.ziina.com/business/shopify) ,


[WooCommerce](https://docs.ziina.com/business/woocommerce) , or a


[custom website](https://docs.ziina.com/business/other-platforms)
- Create a clean, branded checkout with a clear currency display and minimal payment steps that works well across desktop and mobile


### 5. Does the provider offer fraud protection in line with high security standards?


Selling internationally opens the door to more customers, but it can also introduce more risk.


When payments are coming from different countries, it's often harder to verify customer details and resolve disputes if something goes wrong. That can increase the risk of fraud and chargebacks.


The right payment gateway can help reduce that risk. Look for features like:


- Real-time fraud monitoring detection to stop suspicious activity before it becomes a real problem


- PCI-DSS certified technology and end-to-end encryption to keep sensitive cardholder data safe


- Dedicated support for managing chargebacks and payment disputes effectively in your time zone


- Regulatory oversight from the Central Bank of the UAE to ensure your funds are handled according to strict financial standards


### 6. Can I collect payments across multiple channels, not just a website checkout?


Your website might be your main sales channel today, but that may not always be the case.


Maybe you start selling through Instagram and WhatsApp. Maybe you start attending pop-up events. Maybe you eventually open a physical store.


The issue is that every new sales channel tends to come with another payment tool and another dashboard to track transactions. Before you know it, you're juggling payment links in one platform and in-person POS transactions in another.


The good news is that some providers bring all these functions together, which can make your payment operations much simpler.
