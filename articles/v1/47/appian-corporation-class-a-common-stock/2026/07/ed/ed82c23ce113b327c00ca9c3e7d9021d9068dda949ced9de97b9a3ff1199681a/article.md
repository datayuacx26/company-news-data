# 5 Continuous Improvement Process Methods for IT Leaders

Many organizations adopt a method or combination of methods, such as PDCA, Kaizen, Six Sigma, lean, or TOC as the framework for continuous improvement processes.


Regardless of your specific approach, successful implementations of a continuous improvement process typically involve a variation of the following steps:


1. **Define the scope -** Identify a process that needs improvement and establish goals and objectives for the improvement process. Remember that the best CIPs are those that focus on making ongoing, iterative changes, so try to start small.
2. **Select a team -** Identify who will lead the CIP effort and choose representatives from different areas and levels of the organization. This helps build employee engagement and ownership, a key step in creating a continuous improvement culture.
3. **Identify opportunities for improvement -** Continuous improvement processes rely on[process transparency](https://appian.com/process-mining/process-transparency) to understand how current processes are executed—including who does what, when, and where. Analyze the processes to identify opportunities by gathering data and using tools such as


[process mining software](https://appian.com/products/platform/process-intelligence) or process maps and cause-and-effect diagrams to identify suggestions for improvement.
4. **Develop the improvement plan -** Once you’ve identified opportunities for improvement, create a plan for making changes that defines clear priorities, identifies measurable goals, and establishes a timeline.
5. **Implement -** With the plan defined, it’s time to implement. Be sure to involve all stakeholders and communicate any changes as the project unfolds. And remember, continuous improvement is about making changes and learning from them—it’s okay if you run into some bumps in the road, as these can be addressed in the next iteration.
6. **Monitor -** Track the progress of the improvements that you implemented and measure the results against the established goals and objectives. This step is the key to understanding what can be improved in the next iteration.
7. **Continuously improve -** Identify new areas for potential improvement by reviewing the processes on a continual basis and documenting what you’ve learned from failures or setbacks. As new opportunities are identified, repeat the cycle or standardize the results to apply them to another area for improvement.
