## **What is a Grandma Pizza?**


Grandma pizza is a thin-crust, square pizza baked in an olive-oil-coated sheet pan. Invented at Umberto’s Pizzeria in New Hyde Park, Long Island, this “Pizza alla Nonna” (Italian for grandmother’s pizza) recreates the simple, home-style pizzas Italian grandmothers made at home. What sets it apart is the crust: pressed straight into an oiled pan, it bakes up crispy on the outside and fluffy on the inside. Here is how a classic Grandma pizza comes together:


- **The crust:** unproofed dough pressed into a heavy steel pan coated in olive oil, giving it a golden, crispy bottom
- **The cheese:** sliced mozzarella laid directly on the dough
- **The sauce:** dollops of chunky plum tomato spooned on top, rather than a full layer
- **The finish:** a drizzle of olive oil with a sprinkle of oregano and Pecorino Romano


Grandma pizza is just one of several distinct New York pizza styles:


- **Grandma pizza:** thin, crispy, square pizza baked in an oiled pan
- **New York pizza:** round, thin-crust cheese pizza with slices so big they are often folded when eaten
- **New York Sicilian pizza:** thick, dense, focaccia-like pizza with a chewy crust, cut into rectangles

## **What is the Difference Between New York and Grandma Pizza?**


The main difference between New York and Grandma pizza is shape and crust: New York pizza is round with a thin, hand-tossed crust cut into foldable triangles, while Grandma pizza is square with a thin, pan-baked crust cut into rectangles. Both are New York staples, but they differ across four key traits:


- **History:** New York pizza was created in 1905 at Lombardi’s, the first pizzeria in the U.S. Grandma pizza came later, invented by Italian immigrant Umberto Corteo on Long Island and popularized in the 1990s.
- **Crust:** New York pizza uses proofed dough that is stretched and tossed before baking, yielding a thin, sturdy crust with a slightly puffy outer ring. Grandma pizza uses unproofed dough pressed directly into an oiled steel pan, yielding a flat crust that is golden and crispy on the bottom.
- **Shape:** New York pizza is round and sliced into triangles. Grandma pizza is rectangular and sliced into squares.
- **Cheese and sauce order:** New York pizza is topped with sauce first, then shredded mozzarella, for an even cheese-to-sauce ratio. Grandma pizza is topped with sliced mozzarella first, then dollops of chunky tomato, leaving parts of the dough exposed for its signature look.

## **Grandma Pizza FAQs**


**Why is it called Grandma pizza?**


It is called Grandma pizza because it recreates the simple, home-style pizzas that Italian grandmothers baked in sheet pans at home. Its Italian name, “Pizza alla Nonna,” literally means grandmother’s pizza.


**Where did Grandma pizza originate?**


Grandma pizza was created on Long Island, New York, at Umberto’s Pizzeria in New Hyde Park. Founder Umberto Corteo based it on the pizzas his mother made in Italy, and it grew popular through the 1980s and 1990s.


**Is Grandma pizza the same as New York-style pizza?**


Not exactly. Grandma pizza is one style within the broader New York pizza family. Classic New York-style pizza is round, hand-tossed, and cut into foldable triangles, while Grandma pizza is square, pan-baked, and thin with a crispy bottom.
