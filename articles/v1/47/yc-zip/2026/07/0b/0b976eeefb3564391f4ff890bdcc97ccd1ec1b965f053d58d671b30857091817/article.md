## **What does good AP automation look like in 2026?**


Good AP automation in 2026 raises touchless invoice processing as close as possible to the 49.2% best-in-class benchmark, while using AI to handle the exceptions that rules-based systems can't. The best platforms reduce manual work, lower invoice processing costs, and shorten cycle times without forcing AP teams to resolve the same issues repeatedly.


According to the Ardent Partners data, top-performing AP teams can process 49.2% of invoices straight-through, or "touchless," compared to a 32.6% industry average. They also process each invoice for around $2.78, while the average team pays $9.40. Cycle time shows a similar gap: Best-in-class teams can clear an invoice in 3.1 days compared to 17.4 days on average.


AP teams have to ask themselves how high their AP software's AI can raise their touchless rate before it plateaus. In practice, that ceiling is set by three things: how clean the invoice data is when it arrives, how well the platform's AI codes and matches non-standard invoices, and how it resolves exceptions when matching fails. The 10 platforms provide solutions in very different ways.

## **How to choose an AP automation solution for your business**


Finding the right AP automation requires a bit of legwork. Let's explore some practical things to keep in mind as you figure out what truly fits your business.


#### Know your needs and pain points


Before you even consider any software demos, take a good look at what's slowing you down right now. What's complicating the current AP process? Is it the endless cycle of typing in invoice details, the frustration of chasing down approvals, or the mystery of where a payment actually is? Pinpointing these inefficiencies is the very first step.


Understanding your current AP journey will shed light on the specific features you need in an automation solution. What works (and what doesn't) for a small, local business will look very different from the priorities of a larger company dealing with multiple entities and complex approval hierarchies.


#### Evaluate your current system


Once you've got a handle on your pain points, the next step is to think about how a new AP automation solution will play with the tools you already use. Your AP process likely doesn't live in isolation. Do you have a separate system for managing purchase orders or contracts? What about where your invoices are stored before they enter the AP workflow? Consider how easily a new solution can connect with these existing platforms.


Think beyond just the immediate integrations, too. As your business evolves, you might adopt new software. Understand if a potential AP automation solution offers[open APIs](https://ziphq.com/capabilities/integration-ecosystem) . This flexibility can save you a lot of headaches down the road, allowing for smoother data flow and a more connected overall business ecosystem.


#### Consider vendor reputation and support


Choosing an AP automation partner means you also have to look at the company behind it. Before you commit, do a little digging into its reputation. Online reviews and[customer stories](https://ziphq.com/customers) can offer valuable insights into the brand's reliability and how well it supports its customers.


Don't underestimate the importance of responsive and helpful customer support. When questions arise or you encounter a technical snag, you'll want to know that help is readily available. Consider the support channels on offer (phone, email, chat), the response times, and if the company provides adequate training and onboarding resources. A strong supplier with excellent support can make all the difference in ensuring a smooth implementation and a positive long-term experience.


#### Understand pricing and ROI


Getting a handle on the cost of an AP automation solution means looking beyond the sticker price. You'll want to review the different pricing models, too. Is it per invoice, per user, or a tiered structure? Make sure you understand what you're paying for and how it aligns with your transaction volume and team size.


Beyond the initial outlay, really consider the[potential return on investment (ROI)](https://ziphq.com/calculator) . Think about the time your team currently spends on manual tasks and how much you could save by automating data entry and approvals. Factor in the cost of errors, late payment penalties, and the potential to snag early payment discounts. Don't forget to ask about any hidden costs, like implementation fees or charges for integrating with your other systems.


As a sanity check on ROI math, consider Ardent Partners' 2025 benchmarks that peg the spread between average and best-in-class AP processing at $6.62 per invoice ($9.40 average vs. $2.78 best-in-class). Multiply by your annual invoice volume to size the prize.


#### Request demos and pilot programs


Once you've narrowed down your options, don't hesitate to request personalized demos. This is your chance to see the software in action, tailored to your specific workflows and pain points. Pay close attention to the user interface: Is it intuitive and easy to navigate? Can you envision your team quickly adopting it?


Even better than a demo is a pilot program, if the provider offers one. This lets you test the software in your actual environment with real data, albeit often on a limited scale. A pilot provides invaluable insights into how the solution integrates with your existing systems, how your team adapts to it, and the tangible benefits it delivers. It's a fantastic way to de-risk your decision and ensure the solution truly meets your needs.


#### Future-proof your decision


Beyond solving today's AP challenges, picking the right automation solution sets your business up for future success. Think about where your company is headed, and make sure the solution you choose can easily scale to accommodate these changes without becoming a bottleneck itself.


Think beyond scalability and evaluate the vendor's long-term vision, too. Is the company actively investing in research and development? Does it have a clear roadmap for new features and improvements? A supplier committed to innovation is more likely to provide a solution that remains cutting-edge and continues to meet your evolving needs. Asking about future plans can give you confidence that your investment will continue to deliver value as your business grows and technology advances. In 2026, the most important roadmap question is whether the vendor's AI is built to lift your touchless processing rate over time, or whether it plateaus at the rules-based ceiling.

## **Frequently asked questions**


#### What is AP automation software?


AP automation software reduces the manual work involved in invoice capture, data extraction, coding, matching, approvals, and payment. Modern platforms use AI to read invoices, match them to purchase orders, route exceptions, and post approved invoices to the ERP. The goal is to shorten invoice cycle time and raise the share of invoices that move through AP without human intervention.


#### What is touchless invoice processing?


Touchless invoice processing is the percentage of invoices that move from receipt to ERP posting without human involvement. According to Ardent Partners data cited by Tungsten Automation, the industry average is 32.6%, while best-in-class AP teams sit at 49.2%. Stronger AI can raise that ceiling by handling invoice exceptions that rules-based matching often sends back to AP.


#### How much does AP automation save?


Top-performing AP teams process invoices for $2.78 each, compared with the $9.40 industry average, which saves $6.62 per invoice. For a company processing 10,000 invoices a year, that would mean roughly $66,200 in annual processing savings. The Ardent Partners study also noted that best-in-class teams clear invoices in 3.1 days, compared with 17.4 days on average. For a rough estimate, multiply your per-invoice cost gap by your annual invoice volume.


#### What's the difference between AP automation and procure-to-pay software?


AP automation covers the invoice-to-payment process. It captures invoices, codes line items, matches invoices to purchase orders, routes approvals, and supports payment. Procure-to-pay software covers the broader process, starting with intake, sourcing, supplier onboarding, contracts, and purchase orders. Platforms like Zip include AP automation, but also manage the upstream context that determines whether an invoice arrives clean.


#### Does AP automation software work with my ERP?


Yes, most major AP automation platforms integrate with leading ERPs and accounting systems. Common connectors include NetSuite, Sage Intacct, Microsoft Dynamics 365, Workday, Oracle, QuickBooks, and Xero. Before choosing a vendor, confirm which fields sync, how often data updates, and whether the integration supports two-way communication. ERP-native AP modules are another option for teams that prefer one core system.


#### What should I look for in AP automation software in 2026?


Look for AP automation software that can improve touchless processing on real invoice volume. The most important factors are AI accuracy, ERP fit, approval control, global payment coverage, and whether the platform connects AP to upstream procurement data. The last feature is important because invoice exceptions often start with missing approvals, incomplete supplier records, or mismatched purchase orders.
