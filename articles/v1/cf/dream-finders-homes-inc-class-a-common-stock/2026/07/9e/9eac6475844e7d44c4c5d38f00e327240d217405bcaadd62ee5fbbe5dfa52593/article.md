## What Are the Most Common Types of Houses?


Here are some of the most common types of houses you’ll encounter:


- Single-Family Homes: Standalone houses designed for one family, featuring privacy and space both indoors and out.
- Townhomes: Multi-floor homes that share walls with neighbors but have their own entrances.
- Condos: Individual units within a larger building or complex, sometimes with shared amenities.
- Duplexes and Triplexes: Buildings divided into two or three separate living units, often rented out or owned by different families.
- Manufactured and Mobile Homes: Prefabricated homes built in a factory and transported to the site.
- Cottages and Bungalows: Smaller, cozy homes with a charming, traditional feel.

## What Are the Different Categories of Housing?


Housing categories typically group homes by their structure and ownership.


- Detached Housing: Fully independent buildings which include single-family homes and cottages
- Attached Housing: Homes that share walls but have separate entrances such as townhomes or row houses.
- Multi-Family Housing: Apartment buildings, duplexes, triplexes, and condos where multiple families live in one structure.
- Mobile/Manufactured Housing: Factory-built homes that are movable or set in communities.


## What Type of House Is Best for a Family?


For families, the best type of house balances space, safety, and community. Single-family homes top the list due to their larger living areas and private yards that are ideal for kids to play and families to grow. However, some families find townhomes or duplexes appealing for their sense of neighborhood and often more affordable pricing.


## What’s the Difference Between a Townhome and a Single-Family Home?


Single-family homes stand alone on their own lot, with no shared walls or structures. A townhome is one unit in a row of similar homes attached side-by-side, sharing one or two walls with neighbors. Townhomes have multiple floors and may come with shared community spaces, while single-family homes have more independence but usually at a higher price.
