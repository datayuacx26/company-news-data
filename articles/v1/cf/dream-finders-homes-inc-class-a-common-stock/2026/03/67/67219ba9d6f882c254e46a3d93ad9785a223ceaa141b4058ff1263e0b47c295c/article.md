# What Is a Retirement Community?


- A retirement community is a broad term for housing designed for older adults, ranging from independent living neighborhoods to communities that offer higher levels of care.
- A senior community or retirement village may include amenities, social opportunities, dining, transportation, and in some cases healthcare support.
- Age-restricted communities require residents to meet a minimum age, while age-targeted communities are designed to appeal to older adults but may not have the same formal age requirements.
- Senior living homes can include independent living, assisted living, memory care, and continuing care communities, depending on the level of support a resident needs.

If you have ever asked, what is a retirement community, the simplest answer is this: it is a community designed for older adults, often with housing, shared amenities, and opportunities for social connection. In many cases, residents are largely independent, though some communities also offer access to supportive services or higher levels of care.


That broad definition matters because the term covers a wide range of living options. Some retirement communities are centered on independent living and an active social calendar. Others are built for people who need help with daily routines, medical oversight, or memory care. That is why it is important to understand the different categories before deciding where to live.

## Age-Restricted vs. Age-Targeted Communities


Another important distinction is age-restricted versus age-targeted housing.


- Age-Restricted Communities


Age-restricted communities are intended for residents who meet a minimum age requirement, commonly 55+. These neighborhoods are specifically developed to serve older adults and typically follow rules that help preserve that focus.


- Age-Targeted Communities


Age-targeted communities are designed and marketed to appeal to older adults, but they do not always enforce the same formal age limits. The floor plans, amenities, and overall environment may still be ideal for this stage of life, but the residency rules can be more flexible.


This distinction matters because two communities can look similar at first glance while offering very different living experiences. Buyers who want a neighborhood built around peers often prefer age-restricted 55+ living.


## What Are Senior Living Homes, Assisted Living, and Related Options?


The term senior living homes can describe several housing types for older adults. Independent living is best for those who want convenience, community, and fewer household responsibilities without personal care. Assisted living adds support with daily activities such as dressing, bathing, meals, medication reminders, and housekeeping. Memory care offers a more structured setting for people with cognitive needs. Continuing care communities combine multiple levels of living and care in one place.


In other words, a retirement community can range from fully independent to highly supportive. That is why it helps to think first about your lifestyle and then about your current or future care needs.

### **FAQs**


##### **What is the difference between a retirement community and assisted living?**


A retirement community is typically designed for independent adults seeking lifestyle, convenience, and social connection, while assisted living provides additional support with daily care needs.


##### **What age requirements are typically associated with retirement communities?**


Age requirements vary, but many retirement communities are designed for adults age 55 and older, while some may have different minimum age thresholds.


##### **What types of retirement communities are available to buyers?**


Options can include 55+ active adult communities, independent living communities, continuing care retirement communities, and other age-targeted neighborhoods designed around different lifestyle needs.


##### **What costs are generally associated with living in a retirement community?**


Costs may include the home purchase price or monthly rent, HOA or community fees, utilities, maintenance, and any optional lifestyle or service-related expenses.


##### **What are the residency options for couples when only one partner meets the age requirement?**


In many retirement communities, one qualifying resident may allow a younger spouse or partner to live in the home, though specific occupancy rules vary by community.
