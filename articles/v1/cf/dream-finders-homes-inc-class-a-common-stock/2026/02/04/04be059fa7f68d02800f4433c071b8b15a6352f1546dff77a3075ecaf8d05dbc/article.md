# Moving to Florida: A Guide to Costs, Cities, and Home Buying


If you have been thinking about moving to Florida, you are not alone. People relocate to Florida for a lot of reasons, including career opportunities, warmer weather, and a lifestyle that makes it easier to spend time outdoors year-round. The key is choosing the right area for your day-to-day life and building a budget that matches the reality of where you want to live.


This guide covers the big topics that matter most when you are[relocating to FL](https://www.moving.com/tips/why-moving-to-florida-is-a-great-idea/) : cost of living basics, taxes, climate, moving tips, and what to know about buying a new construction home.


## What does it really cost to live in Florida?


Florida can be a great value compared to many high-cost states, but costs still vary a lot by region, commute, and lifestyle. When you plan your budget, it helps to break things into a few categories.


### Housing


Housing is usually the largest line item. Florida has a wide mix, from low-maintenance townhomes close to city centers to larger single-family homes in master-planned communities. Your monthly payment can look very different in a coastal area versus a growing suburb, so it is smart to choose your target city first, then shop neighborhoods within it.


### Utilities and day-to-day expenses


Air conditioning is a real part of Florida life, especially in summer, so utilities can be higher during warm months. Many buyers like the idea of new construction because modern building practices and energy-efficient features can help support comfort and potentially reduce energy use over time. Dream Finders Homes highlights energy-efficient features as a benefit of buying new.


### Taxes


Taxes are a major reason many people move to Florida. Florida does not have a state income tax, and the state constitution forbids it, which is one reason Florida often appeals to people relocating from higher-tax states.


You will still want to plan for other taxes, including:


• Sales tax: Florida’s general state sales tax rate is 6%, and local surtaxes may apply depending on the county.


• Property taxes: Property taxes vary by county, and Florida offers homeowner benefits like the homestead exemption and the Save Our Homes assessment limitation for eligible primary residences.


A quick tip if you are relocating to FL: if you plan to make Florida your primary residence, learn the local rules and deadlines for filing for exemptions in the county where you buy.

## Florida climate: what to expect and how to plan


Florida is warm and sunny, but it is smart to prepare for the full picture.


• Summers are hot and humid, and afternoon thunderstorms are common.


• Winters are mild, which is a big reason many people move to Florida.


• Spring and Fall are the perfect Florida weather months.


• Hurricane season runs from June through November, so it is wise to plan ahead with a basic home and storm readiness checklist.


Practical moving tip: if you have flexibility, many movers and employers find shoulder seasons helpful for scheduling, and you may find more availability for moving services and home tours.


## Moving tips to make relocating to FL easier


Relocating does not have to feel chaotic if you plan in phases.


1. Pick your target metro first, then narrow to neighborhoods. Your commute, school preferences, and lifestyle goals will matter more than a statewide “best place” list.


2. Budget for the “first month” expenses. Think utility deposits, moving supplies, temporary housing if needed, and basic setup costs.


3. Plan your Florida timeline. If you are switching jobs, leasing temporarily, or buying new construction, map out realistic milestones for tours, financing, and move-in.


4. Build a short list of must-haves. For example: a first-floor primary suite, home office, garage space, or low-maintenance living.


5. Schedule trips with purpose. If you can, combine community tours with drive-time tests. Visit in the morning and evening to understand traffic and daily routines.


## Buying a new construction home in Florida: what to know


If you are ready to relocate Florida and buy, new construction can be a great way to get modern layouts, today’s design preferences, and the benefit of a home that is brand new.


Here is a simple path many buyers follow:


Step 1: Get clear on budget and financing


Start with a realistic monthly payment goal and get pre-qualified early. This helps you move faster when you find the right home or homesite.


Step 2: Decide between quick move-in and build-from-the-ground-up


Many buyers like quick move-in homes if they need a faster timeline. Others prefer choosing a homesite and selecting finishes. Dream Finders Homes offers both community options and quick move-in inventory in Florida markets.


Step 3: Tour communities and compare lifestyle


When you tour, look beyond the model home. Check the drive to grocery stores, schools, and major roads. Ask about community amenities and what is planned nearby.


Step 4: Personalize where it matters


One of the biggest advantages of buying new is the ability to personalize your space. Dream Finders Homes emphasizes flexibility and the ability to personalize your home, with support like a design studio experience.


Step 5: Understand the timeline and the process


New construction has milestones like contract, design selections, pre-drywall walkthroughs, and closing. Ask your sales counselor for a clear roadmap so you can plan your lease end date, movers, and utilities.
