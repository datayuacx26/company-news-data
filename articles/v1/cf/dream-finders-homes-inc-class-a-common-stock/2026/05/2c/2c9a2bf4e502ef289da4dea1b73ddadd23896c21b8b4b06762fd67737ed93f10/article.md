## What Happens During Each Phase of the Home Building Process


### Pre-Construction and Planning


This stage includes choosing the homesite, selecting a floor plan, reviewing options, securing financing, and finalizing your contract. For a more personalized home, this phase may also include blueprint drafting and structural approvals. Dream Finders Homes highlights the role of its Design Studios and professional designers in helping buyers make cohesive selections before construction begins.


### Permitting and Site Preparation


Before construction can start, the builder must secure permits and complete required prep work. Depending on the location, this can take several weeks. For custom homes the permitting timeline alone can take six to eight weeks.


### Foundation, Framing, and Major Systems


Once permits are approved, the home moves into the visible construction phase. This includes the foundation, framing, roofing, windows, plumbing, electrical, and HVAC. This is often the stage buyers are most excited about because progress becomes easy to see week by week.


### Interior Finishes and Final Details


After the mechanical systems are in place, crews move into drywall, flooring, cabinetry, countertops, paint, trim, and fixtures. Final inspections, punch-list items, and orientation also happen near the end, followed by closing and move-in. During this final stretch, buyers attend a new home orientation and prepare for closing day.


## Custom vs. Semi-Custom vs. Production Homes


### Why Fully Custom Homes Usually Take Longer


A fully custom home often requires more design work, more decisions, and more one-off coordination. Larger homes also tend to take longer. National data shows build times increase for bigger homes, especially above 4,000 square feet.


### Why Streamlined New Construction Can Move Faster


If your goal is to shorten how long to build a house, a production or semi-custom home may be the better fit. Standardized floor plans, established construction schedules, and a structured design approach help reduce slowdowns. ### The Most Common Causes of Delays


Weather is one of the biggest variables, especially during site work, foundation pours, and exterior construction. Permitting can also extend the timeline depending on local requirements. Labor shortages, material lead times, and last-minute change orders can all add time as well. The National Association of Homebuilders reports that even though timelines improved in 2024, construction still takes longer than it did a decade ago due in part to regulation and labor constraints.

### **FAQs**


##### What is the average time it takes to build a new home from start to finish?


On average, building a new home can take around 7 to 12 months, depending on the size of the home, level of customization, permitting, and local conditions. Production and semi-custom homes often move faster than fully custom builds.


##### What are the most common causes of delays in home construction?


The most common delays come from permitting, weather, labor shortages, material availability, and change orders made after construction begins. Even small changes during the build can create a ripple effect that slows multiple phases of the project.


##### Does building a custom home take longer than a semi-custom or production home?


Yes, custom homes usually take longer because they involve more design decisions, approvals, and one-of-a-kind details. Semi-custom and production homes typically have more streamlined plans and processes, which can help shorten the timeline.


##### What happens during each phase of the home building process?


The process typically includes planning and design, permitting and site prep, foundation and framing, installation of major systems, interior finishes, inspections, and final walkthroughs. Each phase builds on the one before it, which is why timing and coordination matter so much.


##### How can I speed up the timeline when building a new house?


You can help keep the process moving by finalizing your floor plan, design selections, and financing as early as possible. Choosing an experienced builder with a clear process and avoiding major changes during construction can also make a big difference.
