# No-Code AI Agents for Operations and Finance Teams

Operations and finance teams lose hours to relay work: pulling numbers out of systems, reconciling them across spreadsheets, and rewriting the same reports every cycle. A no-code AI agent can draft that work from your data and route it to a workflow owner to approve. The agent removes the gathering and drafting; the person keeps the judgement. This piece covers where to start and what to keep human.


## The relay problem


The slow part is rarely the analysis. It is the relay: a market coordinator exports demand, an allocations team pulls production estimates, someone reconciles them against shipping capacity, and the numbers degrade and lag at each handoff. By the time a planning view is current, it is already a little stale. The work is real, repetitive, and exactly the kind a grounded agent can draft.


## Where to start


Pick one recurring report or planning view that a person assembles by hand today:


- A weekly allocation pack matching demand against production and capacity.
- A reconciliation summary across two or three systems.
- A recurring management report pulled from the same sources each month.
- An exception list that flags what does not match for a human to review.


Run it as a draft the agent produces and a person approves. Each figure traces to the source it came from, so the validator checks rather than rebuilds. A European fresh-produce importer runs exactly this for weekly allocations, replacing a multi-day manual relay with a drafted pack and a human validating the exceptions.


## Working with the files you already have


Operations data is messy: spreadsheets with locale-specific number formats, exports that do not line up, headers that differ by system. A good agent reads the files your systems already produce and maps them, rather than forcing everyone onto a new format. The point is to remove the relay between documents, not to make people re-onboard their tools or their vendors.


## What stays human


The validation of exceptions and the sign-off on anything that drives a decision or leaves the team. The agent proposes the matches and drafts the views; a person approves them. The operator moves from typist to control tower, spending time on the cases that need judgement instead of the assembly that does not.
