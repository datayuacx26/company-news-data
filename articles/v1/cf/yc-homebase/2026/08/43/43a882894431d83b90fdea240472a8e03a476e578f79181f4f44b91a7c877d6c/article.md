## **What to Look for in a Timesheet App for Small Business**


The most important features in a timesheet app aren't always the most obvious. For hourly teams, the ones that protect your payroll and keep you compliant are worth understanding before you commit. Here's what to look for.


### **Clock-in options that fit your team.**


Not every team clocks in the same way. A restaurant might use a[time clock kiosk](https://www.joinhomebase.com/blog/time-clock-kiosk) at the host stand. A retail store might use[mobile time tracking](https://www.joinhomebase.com/blog/mobile-time-tracking) . A cleaning crew spread across job sites needs GPS verification to confirm someone was on location.


The[clock in clock out apps](https://www.joinhomebase.com/blog/clock-in-clock-out-apps) you're choosing between differ more than their interfaces suggest. The verification method determines whether your hours are actually accurate, not just recorded. A[geofencing time clock](https://www.joinhomebase.com/blog/best-geofencing-time-clock) creates a virtual boundary around a worksite. Employees can only clock in from within that radius, which stops remote clock-ins and buddy punching.


### **Overtime visibility before it hits your paycheck.**


Overtime errors happen when you find out about them on payday. A good timesheet app tells you before the threshold is crossed, not after the check is cut.


Automatic overtime alerts protect your labor budget. Labor cost reports by department show you what a shift actually costs before payroll closes, not when you're reconciling at the end of the week. Homebase has sent more than 45 million overtime alerts to small business owners. It's a reminder that[overtime happens](https://www.joinhomebase.com/blog/overtime-happens-avoid) in even well-managed teams, and catching it before the paycheck closes is the whole point.


Overtime rules also vary. Federal law requires overtime after 40 hours per week, but[overtime laws by state](https://www.joinhomebase.com/blog/overtime-laws-by-state) can be stricter: California and Alaska require it after eight hours in a single day.


### **Payroll integration that works.**


There's a difference between "connects to payroll" and "connects to payroll without you manually moving data." The first describes a CSV export. The second describes a native integration where hours sync automatically, with no re-entry required.


When you evaluate any timesheet app, confirm your payroll provider is on the supported list.[Homebase payroll integrations](https://www.joinhomebase.com/integrations) cover QuickBooks, Gusto, ADP, Paychex, and Square Payroll natively.


One pricing detail most comparison guides skip: per-location pricing versus per-user pricing. For a business with 15 or more employees at a single location, per-location pricing can cost significantly less. It's worth asking before you sign up.


### **Timesheet editing and approval.**


Missed punches are part of running a shift-based team. Someone forgets to clock out, a start time needs correcting, a manager adjusts hours after a no-show. The question isn't whether it'll happen. It's how fast you can fix it and whether there's a record of the change.


Look for an app that lets managers correct[time cards](https://www.joinhomebase.com/blog/time-card) directly, from a phone or computer, adjusting clock-in and clock-out times without needing to log in on a desktop. Every edit should be timestamped and attributed to whoever made it. That audit trail isn't just good practice. It's what the FLSA requires for wage and hour recordkeeping. It also means timesheets go through manager approval before hours reach payroll, so mistakes get caught before the check is cut. A[clocking in and out policy](https://www.joinhomebase.com/blog/clocking-in-and-out-policy) makes the approval step consistent across your team.


### **Break tracking.**


Most states require paid or unpaid breaks at set intervals, and the rules vary by state, shift length, and industry. California requires a paid 10-minute rest break for every four hours worked. Missing a required break is a compliance liability, not just a scheduling gap.


A timesheet app with built-in[break tracking for small business](https://www.joinhomebase.com/blog/break-tracking-small-business) removes that risk automatically. Look for configurable break rules by state or location, alerts when a break is missed mid-shift, and a record of breaks taken that you can pull up for an audit.


### **Scheduling integration.**


A time tracking app that doesn't connect to your schedule creates two systems that need to agree with each other by payroll. When they don't, someone is manually reconciling the difference.


An integrated tool connects schedule to clock-in to payroll in one workflow. You can see scheduled hours versus actual hours in real time, prevent early clock-ins before a shift starts, and catch when someone works outside their scheduled hours. For shift-based hourly teams, that connection between[scheduling and time tracking](https://www.joinhomebase.com/scheduling) is what separates a basic time tracker from a complete team app.

## **Timesheet App Compliance: What the FLSA Actually Requires**


The[FLSA recordkeeping requirements](https://www.joinhomebase.com/hr-compliance/flsa) are specific: track hours worked each day and workweek for non-exempt employees, store payroll records for three years, and keep time cards for two. Paper systems and spreadsheets often fail this standard. Not from dishonesty, but because they create gaps that can't be verified later.


Here's what compliant recordkeeping looks like in practice:


- **Full edit history:** Every change to a time card should be logged with a timestamp and a record of who made it.
- **State-specific overtime rules:** These fall under the broader scope of[hourly employee labor laws](https://www.joinhomebase.com/blog/hourly-employee-labor-laws) that govern your team. Federal laws set the floor at 40 hours per week; California and Alaska require overtime after eight hours per day. A timesheet app with configurable overtime rules handles the calculation automatically.
- **Accessible records:** The Department of Labor can audit back two years for wage records. Records need to be retrievable on demand.


Homebase stores time cards with full edit history, tracks overtime per applicable state rules, and keeps records in a format that's accessible if you ever need them. One important note: a timesheet app helps you stay compliant. No app can guarantee compliance. What you're looking for is a tool that removes the manual steps where errors sneak in.
