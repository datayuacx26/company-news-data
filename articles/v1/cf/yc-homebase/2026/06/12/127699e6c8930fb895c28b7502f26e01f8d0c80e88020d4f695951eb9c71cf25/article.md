## **The two types of time tracking tools (and which one you need).**


Most "Hubstaff alternatives" lists mix two completely different tool categories together. Picking the wrong one is the most common mistake *and* the most expensive, because it means switching again in six months.


**Shift-based time clocks** track when hourly employees arrive and leave. GPS and geofencing, break compliance, overtime alerts, attendance records, and payroll-ready timesheets are all designed around one job: making sure the right person showed up at the right place and left at the right time. If you run a restaurant, retail shop, cleaning company, or any business with scheduled hourly workers, this is the category you need.


[Project and task trackers](https://www.joinhomebase.com/blog/best-time-tracking-softwares) log how long work takes against clients or deliverables. Timers, project tagging, utilization reporting, and invoicing are all designed around billing and productivity. If you run an agency, consulting practice, or a fully remote team where everyone bills by the hour, this is your category.


Hubstaff tries to cover both, but it optimizes for neither, and it charges for monitoring features that shift-based teams don't need.

## **Frequently asked questions about Hubstaff alternatives**


**What is the best free alternative to Hubstaff?**


Clockify has one of the most generous free tiers in the category: basic time logging, kiosk clock-in, and attendance tracking at no cost with no expiration. For hourly shift teams, Homebase also has a free plan covering one location with up to 20 employees including scheduling and time tracking, which is a better fit if payroll-ready timesheets are the goal.


**Is Clockify better than Hubstaff?**


For teams that need time logging without monitoring, Clockify is a better fit, especially at the free tier. Hubstaff has stronger GPS and monitoring features for distributed teams that need proof-of-work visibility. The better choice comes down to whether your priority is simplicity and cost, or accountability and oversight.


**What is the best Hubstaff alternative for hourly teams?**


For small businesses with hourly employees, Homebase and Buddy Punch are the strongest fits. Both are built for shift-based work with GPS clock-in, overtime tracking, and payroll integration. Homebase's per-location pricing is more cost-effective for single-location businesses that are adding headcount over time.


**Can I switch from Hubstaff without losing my data?**


Yes, but export your time data from Hubstaff before canceling. Some users have reported difficulty accessing historical records after their subscription ends. Most alternatives support CSV import, and running both tools in parallel for two weeks before cutting over significantly reduces the risk of gaps.


**What time tracking app is best for small businesses?**


It depends on your team type. For hourly workers clocking in and out of scheduled shifts, a shift-based time clock—Homebase, Buddy Punch, or QuickBooks Time—is the right fit. For remote teams logging billable project hours, a project tracker like Toggl Track, Harvest, or TimeCamp works better; TimeCamp in particular if you want automatic tracking without manually starting timers.


## **The bottom line on Hubstaff alternatives**


Hubstaff isn't a bad tool. It just isn't built for every kind of business. The right alternative depends on one thing: what your team is actually doing. Hourly workers clocking in and out of shifts need a time clock with GPS verification, break tracking, overtime alerts, and a clean path to payroll. Remote teams logging billable hours need a project tracker with strong reporting and no surveillance overhead.


Pick the right category first, then pick the best tool within it. That's the decision that won't need to be remade in six months.
