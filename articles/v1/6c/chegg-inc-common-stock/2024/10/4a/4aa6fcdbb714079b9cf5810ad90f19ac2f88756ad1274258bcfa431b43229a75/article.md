# Voting Like Their Future Depends On It, Because It Does: Young Voters’ Insights Into Today’s Political Landscape

-


### Share:


-
-
-


*In-depth Conversations with Chegg’s Student Advisory Council*


Young adults possess significant voting power. In 2020, voters aged 18-29[comprised](https://www.census.gov/content/dam/Census/library/publications/2022/demo/p20-585.pdf) only 16.5% of the electorate. The 2024 election could be a turning point, though.


With 41 million Gen Z adults now[eligible](https://circle.tufts.edu/latest-research/41-million-members-gen-z-will-be-eligible-vote-2024) to vote, their impact could be substantial. Since 2020, Gen Z voters are already outperforming previous generations. In the 2022 midterm elections, 28.4% of Gen Z adults[voted](https://circle.tufts.edu/latest-research/gen-z-voted-higher-rate-2022-previous-generations-their-first-midterm-election) – a slight increase in turnout compared to older Millennial adults (23%) and Gen X adults (23.5%) when they were around that age.


**So, What’s Driving Young Voters to the Polls?**


Racial justice, climate change, mental health, gun control, reproductive rights, inflation, and jobs with livable wages top the list of concerns for young adults. To better understand young voters, we spoke with members of[Chegg’s Student Advisory Council](https://www.chegg.com/about/center-for-digital-learning/) . These conversations helped us capture authentic voices and experiences, offering a clearer picture of what motivates young voters today. Several themes emerged.


Our student advisors view the 2024 election as critical for marginalized communities, particularly around reproductive health, Title IX protections, and the future of affirmative action (i.e., race-conscious admissions). They also believe the election will have a significant impact on higher education, with a strong emphasis on college affordability, freedom of speech, and generative AI (GenAI).


With these issues at stake, our student advisors urge their peers to go beyond voting. They champion a full commitment to advocacy, community involvement, and holding elected officials accountable. They also outlined strategies to engage young voters, from ensuring access to voting to colleges investing in civic engagement.


**The Election’s Impact on Marginalized Communities**


Our student advisors view the upcoming election as an opportunity to reshape their future and protect historically marginalized communities. One student advisor noted that young adults bring their unique and diverse identities to the forefront of civic engagement:


*“When I think about my community as a student, as a young person inheriting this world, as an LGBTQIA+ person fighting for the rights and dignity of everyone, and as a person of color, I believe this election will determine the future for those of us who hold all of these identities.”*


**Reproductive Rights**


In 2022, the U.S. Supreme Court[overturned](https://www.npr.org/2022/06/24/1102305878/supreme-court-abortion-roe-v-wade-decision-overturn) *Roe v. Wade* , ending the nearly 50-year constitutional protection for the right to abortion.[Nearly three in four (71%)](https://news.gallup.com/poll/611453/state-reproductive-policies-important-enrollment-decisions.aspx) of college students and prospective college students say access to reproductive healthcare impacts their college choices. Another student advisor emphasized the crucial impact this election will have on her as a young woman, highlighting how the outcome will directly influence her rights and those of other young women:


“ *This year’s election will have a deep impact on me and my community, both as a woman and as a young person. As a woman, the rights that I have in this country will be impacted by who wins this election, which is not something that you ever hope to say.”*


**LGBTQIA+ Rights**


In 2023, state legislatures[introduced](https://www.hrc.org/press-releases/roundup-of-anti-lgbtq-legislation-advancing-in-states-across-the-country) over 520 bills targeting LGBTQIA+ rights. These measures include restrictions on[gender-affirming care](https://www.hrc.org/resources/attacks-on-gender-affirming-care-by-state-map) ,[bathroom bills](https://www.hrc.org/resources/state-maps) ,[healthcare access](https://www.hrc.org/resources/state-maps) limitations, and[sports bans](https://www.hrc.org/resources/state-maps) for transgender athletes. As a result, 90% of LGBTQIA+ youth[reported](https://www.thetrevorproject.org/survey-2024/assets/static/TTP_2024_National_Survey.pdf) negative impacts on their well-being due to recent politics. Many worry these policies threaten their rights and safety, increasing anxiety about their future on college campuses. A student advisor stressed the vital role elected officials play in shaping non-discrimination policies, especially regarding LGBTQIA+ rights in education.


“ *I think that the President and the members of Congress who we elect are going to be really important in shaping what the future of non-discrimination looks like in our schools. Another facet of Title IX or of non-discrimination rather, is when we look at LGBTQIA rights. Whether or not schools are going to be barred from discriminating against LGBTQIA+ students is often a policy that has a narrative, and a directive set from the top, but also something that’s decided by many of our local school boards and our city councils.”*


**College Affordability**


College affordability is a major concern for younger voters in this upcoming election, as tuition rates climb, and student debt hits record levels. For most young voters, economic issues and the rising cost of living will be the primary factors[influencing](https://www.insidehighered.com/news/government/politics-elections/2024/10/09/harris-leads-trump-38-points-among-students?utm_source=Inside+Higher+Ed&utm_campaign=690ecf0423-DNU_2021_COPY_02&utm_medium=email&utm_term=0_1fcbc04421-690ecf0423-235163529&mc_cid=690ecf0423&mc_eid=e0ae31a60f) their decisions in November. In the 2023-24 academic year, the average published tuition at public four-year institutions has[more than doubled](https://research.collegeboard.org/media/pdf/Trends%20Report%202023%20Updated.pdf) compared to what it was three decades ago. Similarly, at public two-year colleges, tuition has[increased](https://research.collegeboard.org/media/pdf/Trends%20Report%202023%20Updated.pdf) to more than 1.5 times the amount from thirty years ago. Increases in tuition likely contributes to the[$1.6 trillion](https://www.newyorkfed.org/microeconomics/hhdc.html) student loan debt crisis — a critical issue for young voters. A recent survey[revealed](https://www.cnbc.com/2024/03/26/canceling-student-loan-debt-a-key-issue-ahead-of-election-survey.html) nearly half (48%) of all voters consider canceling student loan debt a key issue in the 2024 presidential and congressional elections, with this concern being even more pronounced among young adults – seven in ten (70%) Gen Z respondents viewed canceling student loan debt as “very” or “somewhat” important. One student advisor expressed student loan debt as a social justice issue:


*“I think student loan debt cancellation is one of the most important moral issues of our time. It’s a question of equity and how to make sure that people who are low-income are able to also pursue this dream of finding higher education and receiving the credentials they need to succeed in the workplace.”*


**Free Speech and Campus Protests**


During the last academic year, college campuses[experienced](https://www.insidehighered.com/news/governance/executive-leadership/2024/05/31/protests-are-mostly-over-whats-next-colleges) widespread protests centered on free speech, notably triggered by discussions on the longstanding Israeli-Palestinian conflict. As these debates grew more intense, the responses from colleges prompted concerns about students’ freedom of expression. While 92% of college students[consider](https://knightfoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/Knight-Fdn_Free-Expression_2024_072424_FINAL-1.pdf) free speech essential, their confidence in its protection has[dropped](https://knightfoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/Knight-Fdn_Free-Expression_2024_072424_FINAL-1.pdf) sharply, from 73% in 2016 to 43% in 2024. This decrease reflects increasing anxiety among students about expressing diverse viewpoints. A student advisor underscored the importance of ensuring safety and fostering open dialogue during campus protests:


*“We saw some interesting footage in some situations where students were put into unsafe situations, and at times, protests did get out of hand. And so, it’s important to understand that we all have this right to protest, and we should be able to use it. You should be able to discuss these things on college campuses because that’s what they’re for. College campuses should be able to foster diverse opinions, and the ability to debate each other and to agree to disagree and do it in a safe manner.”*


**The Role of Generative AI in Education**


Generative AI (GenAI) is becoming a pivotal topic in education, especially with the upcoming election. As GenAI transforms various sectors, its potential to reshape educational spaces is increasingly clear. In our[recent](https://8dfb1bf9-2f43-45af-abce-2877b9157e2c.usrfiles.com/ugd/8dfb1b_e9bad0aef091478397e6a9ff96651f6d.pdf) Global Study Survey, 40% of students worldwide reported using GenAI-enabled tools for their studies, with 53% stating it helps them learn faster and 43% saying it frees up time for other activities. Despite these benefits,[concerns](https://www.insidehighered.com/news/tech-innovation/artificial-intelligence/2023/06/05/how-ai-tools-both-help-and-hinder-equity) about equitable access are rising. Students in under-resourced schools, who may have limited internet access or cannot afford paid platforms, are less likely to benefit from or receive training in GenAI-enabled tools. A student advisor echoed these concerns, asking:


*“How are you going to make sure that AI is spread to low-funded schools? How are you going to make sure that the idea of prompt engineering is taught to students that don’t have access to computers.”*


**Barriers to Voting**


Despite the importance of their votes, young adults frequently encounter obstacles to voting. Many[struggle](https://circle.tufts.edu/latest-research/major-report-how-drastically-increase-youth-voter-participation-and-reduce) with foundational tasks like registering to vote. Limited polling hours, coupled with confusing or inaccessible rules around early or absentee voting, particularly affect students who must navigate varying state regulations. New restrictive laws[introduced](https://www.insidehighered.com/news/government/politics-elections/2024/09/27/new-election-laws-could-suppress-student-voters-2024?utm_source=Inside+Higher+Ed&utm_campaign=a848d4895f-DNU_2021_COPY_02&utm_medium=email&utm_term=0_1fcbc04421-a848d4895f-235163529&mc_cid=a848d4895f&mc_eid=e0ae31a60f) by lawmakers could complicate student voting even more. One student advisor even noted demanding school and work schedules make it difficult for students to reach the polls:


*“One of the biggest reasons of why Gen Zers are not turning out to the ballot box is because they literally can’t get time off of work or they can’t leave their classes in order to go and vote.”*


**Political Disillusionment and Youth Voter Turnout**


Political disillusionment contributes to low voter turnout among young adults. Many feel excluded from the political process, diminishing their motivation to participate. In 2020, almost half (46%) of young adults[reported](https://circle.tufts.edu/latest-research/major-report-how-drastically-increase-youth-voter-participation-and-reduce) not being contacted by any major political party or campaign, heightening their sense of exclusion, as campaign engagement is crucial for voter participation. Direct outreach markedly boosts turnout. Young adults who receive outreach from a candidate’s campaign or organization are[more likely](https://circle.tufts.edu/latest-research/youth-engagement-2018-election) to express their intent to vote compared to young adults who don’t receive any outreach –49% versus 28%, respectively. Highlighting the emotional impact of this exclusion, one student advisor expressed their frustration:


*“We don’t feel represented, we don’t feel like we’re a part of this conversation, and so with that, it’s very easy to ignore everything that’s going on because it feels like our vote doesn’t count.”*


**Five Strategies to Boost Youth Voter Participation**


As the election approaches, our student advisors are dedicated to encouraging their peers to vote by tackling existing challenges and emphasizing the urgency of these efforts. They propose five key strategies to remove barriers, foster engagement, and reach traditionally underrepresented groups:


1. Automatic Voter Registration: Automatically register individuals to vote when they turn 18, simplifying the process and ensuring they are ready to participate.
2. Make Election Day Accessible: Advocate for a national holiday or guaranteed time off for students and workers to eliminate scheduling conflicts and increase voter turnout.
3. Peer-to-Peer and Social Media Outreach: Leverage peer-led conversations and engaging content on platforms like TikTok and Instagram to make voting more relevant and accessible.
4. Targeted Outreach to Underrepresented Groups: Provide tailored resources and information to marginalized communities and first-time voters to help overcome voting barriers.
5. Enhance Civic Education: Strengthen civic education in schools to cultivate long-term voting habits, teaching young people about their rights and the importance of voting.


**The Final Push: Why The Youth Vote Matters in 2024**


As the 2024 elections approach, the voices of young adults are more important than ever. Our student advisors have provided insightful and refreshing ideas that emphasize the vital role young voters play in shaping the future. Their contributions reflect a deep commitment to civic engagement and underscore the power of this generation to drive meaningful and lasting change.
