# A Boxer’s Guide To A Strong Immune System

With the unfortunate rise in infectious diseases and current public health concerns, there has been an increased interest in how to maximize the immune system. The immune system is one of the most extraordinary systems in the body. It is so highly intricate, yet it functions mostly on its own, as our inner military defense, protecting us from the harms of the external world—bacteria, viruses, chemicals, etc.—also known as pathogens. For all individuals, including the health-conscious ones in our[FightCamp](https://joinfightcamp.com/?utm_source=organic&utm_medium=blog&utm_campaign=homepage&utm_content=) family, the immune system is pretty darn important. Here’s what you need to know.


## NUTRIENTS FOR THE IMMUNE SYSTEM


Micronutrients, such as vitamins and minerals, play a special role in keeping a healthy immune system. Vitamins, such as vitamins A, C, D and E, along with minerals, such as zinc and selenium, have crucial functions in different parts of the immune system to ensure it operates properly.[Research](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6162863/#:~:text=Vitamin%20A%20(VitA)%20is%20a,role%20in%20enhancing%20immune%20function.) shows that the body requires a constant intake of vitamin A through food in order to support immunity. Antioxidants, such as vitamin C, vitamin E and selenium, protect and strengthen cells of the immune system by neutralizing reactive oxygen species—molecules that may cause severe damage to our cells ([Source 1](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5707683/) ,[2](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7011499/) ,[3](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3723386/) ). Vitamin C also plays a role in stimulating the production of white blood cells, a.k.a. soldiers who fight intruders in the body ([Source](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3921/7/3/41/htm) ), while zinc aids in the growth and development of immune cells ([Source 1](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1568997214002808?via%3Dihub) ,[2](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5748737/) ). Lastly, there is a clear link between vitamin D deficiency and adverse health outcomes due to the significant role of vitamin D on immunity ([Source 1](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-6643/12/5/1248/htm) ,[2](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fphys.2014.00151/full) ).

## DO I NEED TO TAKE SUPPLEMENTS?


Are you one of those who pop the vitamin C pills when you feel that cold coming? You’re not alone. Contrary to popular belief, research indicates that supplements such as vitamin C and zinc do not reduce the chances of getting a cold. However, these supplements can be helpful in reducing the duration and severity after the onset of cold symptoms ([Source 1](https://www.cochranelibrary.com/cdsr/doi/10.1002/14651858.CD000980.pub3/full) ,[2](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6124957/) ,[3](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3273967/) ). It’s important to know that vitamins and minerals are more bioavailable (better absorbed) when consumed through foods versus supplements. In addition, foods provide other important nutrients and health benefits that supplements lack.


## THE ROLE OF EXERCISE


##


Several studies have looked at the link between physical activity and the immune system.[One study](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2095254618301005) found an inverse relationship between moderate exercise training and risk for illness. Similarly, the study found that habitual exercise may have an anti-inflammatory effect, improving defense activity and metabolic health, which in turn also reduces illness risk. Another[study](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7387807/) suggested that long-term physical activity improves the immune system and may prevent infections, while a[meta-analysis](https://www.cochranelibrary.com/cdsr/doi/10.1002/14651858.CD010596.pub3/full) indicated that regular exercise may reduce the severity of respiratory tract infections.
