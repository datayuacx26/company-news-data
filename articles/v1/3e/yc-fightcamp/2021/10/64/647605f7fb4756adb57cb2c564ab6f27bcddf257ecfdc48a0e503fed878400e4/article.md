# The Fight Camp | How To Train Like a Fighter

There is nothing harder in the world than a good, old fashioned boxing fight camp. My name is Aaron Swenson, and I have been through 34 of them. Throughout that process, I have trained with different coaches, with different philosophies, and discovered that everyone takes a different approach to getting ready for a fight.


## What Is a Fight Camp?


A


[fight camp](https://blog.joinfightcamp.com/blogs/boxing-training/fight-camps-how-to-train-like-a-pro-boxer-at-home)


is traditionally a 6-8 week training camp leading up to a fight. The camp can be shorter or longer depending on how much notice a fighter gets in advance. Usually, a fighter uses that time to cut weight, get in peak conditioning, and spar people that resemble their opponent. Fighters use a variety of tactics to accomplish this. In this article, I will share a few.


## Fight Camp Training


Depending on your goals and your schedule, you and your coach or team will adjust your fight camp training accordingly. Here are the basic elements and parts of a fight camp that fighters do to stay in


[fighting shape](https://blog.joinfightcamp.com/blogs/news/why-training-like-a-boxer-gets-you-in-amazing-shape)


and ensure they’re ready for their


[next big bout](https://blog.joinfightcamp.com/blogs/boxing-training/how-do-boxers-train-for-a-fight) .


### Roadwork


[Running](https://blog.joinfightcamp.com/blogs/boxing-workouts-drills/hit-the-pavement-roadwork-for-boxing)


is essential for fighters because it conditions your legs and your lungs. Some people believe in sprinting, while others prefer running long distances. There are pros and cons to each. Short sprints work your fast-twitch muscles and resemble the short spurts of action in a fight. Long distance running helps condition your legs, which is necessary, especially for long fights that last 8-12 rounds. Running long distances is also good for your mind because it builds mental fortitude which is key for boxers


[before](https://blog.joinfightcamp.com/blogs/boxing-training/inside-a-boxer-s-mind-how-to-mentally-prepare-for-a-fight)


and


[during](https://blog.joinfightcamp.com/blogs/boxing-training/inside-a-boxer-s-mind-how-to-stay-in-the-zone-during-a-fight)


a fight.


### Jumping rope


A


[jump rope](https://blog.joinfightcamp.com/blogs/boxing-workouts-drills/how-to-jump-rope-like-a-boxer)


is a staple piece of equipment in most fight gyms. Skipping rope is a great way to warm up your legs and calves both for boxing workouts and before for a fight. Plus, it’s amazing


[cardio](https://blog.joinfightcamp.com/blogs/boxing-workouts-drills/hiit-jump-rope-workout-for-quick-cardio-training) . I love it too because it gives you the opportunity to express yourself with tricks and different rhythms.


Here is a quick


[10-minute jump rope workout](https://blog.joinfightcamp.com/blogs/boxing-workouts-drills/quick-cardio-10-minute-jump-rope-workout)


you can do at home for cardio training.


### Shadowboxing


[Shadowboxing](https://blog.joinfightcamp.com/blogs/boxing-training/why-you-should-start-shadowboxing)


is crucial for a fighter to warm up and perfect techniques in the mirror. When you shadow box you watch yourself in the mirror as you refine your punches and kicks. Shadowboxing is also a tool that fighters use to simulate a fight with an imaginary opponent, and warm up.


### Sparring


[Sparring](https://blog.joinfightcamp.com/blogs/boxing-workouts-drills/how-to-start-sparring-beginner-boxing-tips-drills)


is essential for a fighter’s timing. You can hit a


[heavy bag](https://blog.joinfightcamp.com/blogs/boxing-training/how-to-train-on-a-heavy-bag-boxing-tips)


and use focus mitts all you want, but a


[punching bag](https://blog.joinfightcamp.com/blogs/home-boxing-equipment/at-home-boxing-equipment-the-free-standing-punching-bag)


does not move that much or hit back. When you spar, you really get an idea of where your strengths and your weaknesses are, and it is the closest thing you can get to simulating an actual fight.


### Padwork


Fighters will hit focus mitts with their coach to practice different


[boxing punch combinations](https://blog.joinfightcamp.com/blogs/boxing-training/boxing-101-2-punch-combos) ,


[defense](https://blog.joinfightcamp.com/blogs/boxing-workouts-drills/4-defensive-blocking-drills-for-boxing) , and


[footwork](https://blog.joinfightcamp.com/blogs/boxing-workouts-drills/boxing-footwork-drills-for-defense-and-sparring) . Hitting mitts is a good way to resemble sparring without taking all the damage from getting punched in the head.


### Heavy bag training

[heavy bag](https://blog.joinfightcamp.com/blogs/boxing-training/why-you-should-start-training-with-a-punching-bag)


is for developing power and conditioning your punch muscles. That's one of the reasons why our product is called

technology, where you can actually measure your output as you hit the bag, and compare your score with other users.


### Speed ball or double end bag training


The


[speed ball](https://blog.joinfightcamp.com/blogs/home-boxing-equipment/what-is-a-speed-bag-in-boxing)


is great for conditioning the shoulders. The


[double end bag](https://blog.joinfightcamp.com/blogs/home-boxing-equipment/how-to-choose-the-right-punching-bag-for-your-workout)


is for practicing speed, timing, and head movement. Just a few minutes a day training on the speed ball or double end bag is enough to build up the muscle memory needed to tackle any situation in the ring.


### Strength and conditioning


Every fighter has a different philosophy on strength and conditioning. Some fighters prefer just doing bodyweight movements like push-ups, sit-ups, and pull-ups. Others love functional movements with weights like clean and jerks and snatches. It really depends on what your weaknesses are and what you need to improve. For instance, if you are fast but lack power, you might want to consider adding some weight training into your regimen to get stronger.


### Recovery


People often overlook the importance of


[recovery](https://blog.joinfightcamp.com/blogs/boxing-workouts-drills/active-recovery-day-exercises-for-boxers) . After a tough training session, your muscles are inflamed and the faster you can


[recover](https://blog.joinfightcamp.com/blogs/boxing-training/what-you-need-to-know-about-rest-days-q-a-with-coach-pj) , the faster that you can get back to work. There are many different recovery modalities like


[ice baths](https://blog.joinfightcamp.com/blogs/boxing-training/5-benefits-of-an-ice-bath-for-recovery) , massage, hyperbaric chambers, foam rolling, lots of sleep, etc.


## What Does a Day In a Fight Camp Look Like?


Fight camps are generally a combination of all of these training modalities that are scheduled out in advance. Here are two examples of what a day in a fight camp might look like.


### Fight Camp Schedule 1


**6am**


- 3 mile run
**7am**


- 4 rounds of shadowboxing + 4 rounds of padwork + 4 rounds of heavy bag work
**6pm**


- 3 rounds jump rope + strength and conditioning + abs
**8pm**


- Massage


### Fight Camp Schedule 2


**9am**


- 3 rounds of jump rope + 4 rounds of shadowboxing + 8 rounds of sparring + 1 round of speed ball + abs and core work
**6pm**


- 3 mile run + strength and conditioning
**8pm**


- Ice bath


Usually, a striking coach would work directly with a strength and conditioning coach to curate this plan for the fighter and adjust accordingly.
