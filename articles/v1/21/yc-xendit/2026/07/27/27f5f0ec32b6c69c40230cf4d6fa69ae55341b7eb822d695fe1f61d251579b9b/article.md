# Why Southeast Asia Is Becoming a High-Growth Market for Subscription Businesses

Southeast Asia has spent the past decade building one of the world's most active digital economies, and that foundation is now giving rise to a


**fast-expanding market for subscriptions** .


More consumers across the region are choosing to pay on a repeating schedule for the products and services they use most, and businesses are following that demand toward


**recurring revenue** . For subscription businesses looking to enter or expand into Southeast Asia, the timing is worth understanding, because the market is still early in its growth.


A


**subscription** business charges customers on a


**recurring schedule** in exchange for ongoing access, instead of depending on one-time sales. The appeal for your business is steadier, more predictable income and longer customer relationships. Whether that model pays off in Southeast Asia comes down to what is driving demand across the region and how well you can collect on it.


**Here is what this article covers at a glance:**


- Southeast Asia's digital economy gives subscriptions a ready foundation, with most transactions in the region now digital and consumers already used to paying by phone


- A fast-growing middle class is widening the pool of customers with both the income and the interest to commit to ongoing services


- Subscription demand reaches well beyond streaming, into software, e-commerce, beauty, wellness, and recurring essentials


- Turning that demand into recurring revenue depends on offering the payment methods each market already uses, since preferences change from one country to the next


The sections below work through each point in turn, starting with the digital foundation the region has already built.


## **Southeast Asia's digital economy creates room for subscription growth**


The starting point for any subscription business here is the scale of the region's digital economy.


Southeast Asia is set to


**pass US$300 billion** in gross merchandise value in


**2025** , with revenues reaching


**US$135 billion** , according to the


[2025 e-Conomy SEA report](https://blog.google/company-news/inside-google/around-the-globe/google-asia/sea-economy-2025/) by Google, Temasek, and Bain & Company.


The region has also added more than


**200 million new internet users** since 2016, and


**three in five people** now buy something online. What matters most for recurring revenue is how normal digital payments have become. Over


**60%** of all transactions in the region are now


**digital** , a reversal from 2019 when most value still moved in cash.


This everyday comfort with


**paying by phone** is reinforced by high device access.


[Smartphone penetration](https://www.weforum.org/stories/2022/02/digitalization-south-southeast-asia/) reaches 87% in Singapore, 83% in Malaysia, 75% in Thailand, 70% in Indonesia, and 64% in the Philippines, alongside fast adoption of digital financial services.


For your business, this foundation lowers one of the hardest barriers a subscription model faces. When customers already shop, pay, and manage money digitally by default, asking them to set up a recurring payment feels familiar instead of unusual, which makes it easier to convert first-time buyers into paying subscribers.


## **A growing middle class is driving demand for flexible services**


A large digital economy creates the conditions for subscriptions, and a


**growing middle class supplies** the customers.


[ASEAN is projected to add](https://www.mastercard.com/mea/en/news-and-trends/stories/2025/mastercard-ASEAN-inclusive-growth-summit.html) 112 million middle-class consumers by 2035, which would rank it


**third worldwide for middle-class additions** , behind only India and China. More than half the population in Singapore, Thailand, and Vietnam already counts as middle-class, a figure that reaches 89% in Malaysia, while Indonesia and the Philippines are expected to reach a middle-class majority between 2031 and 2032.


Income growth changes how people spend.


As earnings rise, households put more toward education, health, recreation, and financial services, and the region's discretionary


[spending is forecast to climb](https://www.mastercard.com/mea/en/news-and-trends/stories/2025/mastercard-ASEAN-inclusive-growth-summit.html) from


**US$23 trillion** in


**2025** to


**US$35 trillion** in


**2035** . These categories line up closely with subscription offers, since a recurring plan spreads cost over time and makes ongoing services easier to afford than a single large payment.


For your business, a larger middle class means a widening pool of customers with both the income and the willingness to commit to ongoing services. Reaching them consistently is what turns a promising market into steady recurring revenue, and that depends on meeting local expectations around pricing and payment.


## **Subscription opportunities are expanding across industries**


The subscription model is no longer tied to a few categories, which broadens where your business can compete.


According to the


[Subscription Economy Market Report](https://www.grandviewresearch.com/industry-analysis/subscription-economy-market-report) , the


**global subscription economy** was valued at US$492.3 billion in 2024, with business-to-business services accounting for 55.2% of it on the back of demand for


**enterprise software** ,


**cloud platforms** , and


**data services** .


The report also projects


**software** and


**technology** as the fastest-growing segment, expanding at a compound annual rate of 15.8% through 2033, with Asia Pacific the fastest-growing region overall.

Southeast Asia reflects this breadth in its own market.


**Streaming** remains the most visible example, growing

Demand now reaches well past media into e-commerce, software, beauty, food, wellness, and recurring daily essentials, as brands build their offers around


**regular delivery** and


**ongoing access** .


For your business, this range means recurring revenue is realistic across many models, not only content platforms. Whether you sell software, physical products, or services, there is a path to a subscription offer here, provided the experience fits how customers in each market expect to pay.


Turning that offer into steady revenue depends on the experience around payment, which comes into focus once you look at how people across the region are already using and managing their subscriptions.


## **Why payment flexibility matters in subscription payments**


As subscription adoption grows across the region,


**customer behavior is maturing** in two ways at once: more people subscribe, and each subscriber manages more services than before.


In Indonesia,


[93%](https://voiceofasean.com/newsroom/pr-business/the-demand-for-convenience-94-of-indonesian-consumers-want-a-single-hub-to-manage-all-subscriptions/) of consumers now hold more than two subscriptions and 20% hold more than six. Malaysia follows a similar pattern, with


[79%](https://voiceofasean.com/newsroom/pr-business/overloaded-91-of-malaysian-consumers-think-there-are-too-many-subscription-services-to-choose-from-now/) holding more than two and 11% holding more than six across video, music, food delivery, and sports.


[Adoption is climbing](https://retailasia.com/exclusive/asia-pacific-retailers-tap-subscribers-drive-growth) in the region's newer markets too, reaching 53% in the Philippines and 51% in Vietnam.


For your business, these figures describe a market that has moved from early curiosity into everyday habit, which is part of what makes it worth entering now. They also change what wins customers. When people already run several subscriptions, they become more selective about which ones stay in the budget. A service that feels effortless tends to keep its subscribers, while one that adds friction at any step, including payment, is easy to cancel.


**Payment** is one of the clearest places that friction appears. Depending on the market, many customers prefer e-wallets, direct debit, bank transfers, or QR payments to cards, so a card-only checkout narrows reach and makes renewals harder to collect where cards are less common.


**Offering the payment methods each market prefers** to use keeps sign-up smooth and gives every renewal a better chance of clearing, which is how a growing market becomes recurring revenue you can rely on.
