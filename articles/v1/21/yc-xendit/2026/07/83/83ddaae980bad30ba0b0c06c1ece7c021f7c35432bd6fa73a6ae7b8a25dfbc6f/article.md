## **What makes scaling payments across Southeast Asia complex**


Each country in the region handles payments differently, across regulation, technology, and day-to-day operations. Building for those differences one market at a time is what makes regional expansion difficult to manage.


**Six challenges** come up most often when scaling e-commerce payments across Southeast Asia:


### **1. Different regulatory and compliance requirements**


Every market sets its own rules for business verification, anti-money laundering (AML) and know-your-customer (KYC) checks, tax, data localization, and settlement, and each one affects how quickly you can start accepting payments and moving money in a new country.


Indonesia has its own payment licensing and domestic payment infrastructure requirements, Singapore pairs a more mature regulatory environment with strict financial and data protection standards, and Vietnam's digital payment and e-commerce rules continue to change.


For your business, approval and onboarding timelines differ by country, so what works in one market will not be the same in another.


### **2. Fragmented payment rails and customer behavior**


Checkout preferences vary by country, so a payment mix that converts well in one country can underperform in another.


Customers may expect mobile wallets, QR payments, bank transfers, cards, or account-to-account systems depending on where they shop, with Thailand on PromptPay, Malaysia on DuitNow, Singapore on SGQR and PayNow, Indonesia on BI-FAST and its wallet ecosystems, and the Philippines relying heavily on GCash.

Offering the wrong payment method mix in a market limits conversion before pricing or promotion can make any difference.


### **3. Technology integration challenges**


As you add markets, payment integrations get harder to maintain, since each new country can bring its own providers, banking partners, dashboards, and reporting systems.


This shows up clearly in live commerce, which drives a large share of sales in Indonesia, Thailand, Vietnam, and the Philippines, where customers expect to check out instantly during a live session.


Supporting that takes a payment system able to handle high transaction volumes while reconciling and separating financial reporting across different sales channels. When every market needs its own setup, engineering teams spend more time maintaining connections than supporting growth.


### **4. Cross-border settlement and currency complexity**


Cross-border complexity does not end once the customer pays. Selling across markets brings different currencies, settlement timelines, FX considerations, transaction limits, and domestic settlement rules.


A business selling across Indonesia, Singapore, and Malaysia, for example, may collect in several currencies, track settlements market by market, and process refunds under each country's rules. Handled poorly, this creates cash flow uncertainty and makes reconciliation slower and more error-prone.


### **5. Operational visibility gaps**


Payment visibility connects checkout to fulfillment, customer support, and finance, so your teams need to know whether each order has been paid, failed, refunded, settled, or still needs follow-up.


A business could be selling through marketplaces in one country, a brand website in another, and social commerce in a third, and it still needs to track every order across those channels and markets at once. Without a clear view, order processing, customer issue resolution, and financial reporting slows down.


### **6. Mobile-first expectations**


Southeast Asia is a mobile-first region, which puts mobile checkout performance at the center of e-commerce growth.


In mobile-heavy markets like Indonesia and the Philippines, many shoppers buy through apps, marketplaces, and social commerce, where speed and simplicity decide whether the sale closes. Slow payment pages, too many redirects, or unreliable confirmations create friction at the exact moment a customer is ready to pay.


These challenges point in the same direction. Regional expansion needs infrastructure that can support different markets, payment rails, compliance needs, and workflows without adding complexity each time you scale.
