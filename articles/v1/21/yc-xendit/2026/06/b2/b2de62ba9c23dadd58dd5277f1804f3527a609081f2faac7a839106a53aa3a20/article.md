# Beyond Authentication: Building Trust Through Identity Intelligence

Building trust has always been a shared responsibility across the payments ecosystem, with issuers, acquirers, merchants, and networks all playing a role in protecting customers while enabling seamless transactions.


Typically, this takes the form of authenticating that the cardholder is the one authorizing the payment. However, the traditional methods of authentication focus on challenging the user with passwords and OTPs which create friction for the user, add steps to the checkout process and also create points of failure which can lead to lower conversion rates.


In the worst case, they may also fail to thwart the fraudsters, who regularly adapt their methods, leading to both higher friction and lower trust for the users. Increasingly, the issuers and acquirers are realizing that additional friction can no longer provide adequate protection and a change in approach is needed.


Today’s customers no longer view security and convenience as separate priorities. They increasingly expect both, making


**frictionless and secure experiences** a competitive differentiator for digital businesses.


## **Trust Starts Before the Transaction**


Every interaction an issuer or a merchant has with their customer is an opportunity for them to


**learn more about the customer.**


Through these interactions they can gather a wealth of data about their behavioral patterns, including:


- Device usage


- Browsing behavior


- Purchase history


- Location signals


- Other contextual customer interactions


Together, these signals from the Customer 360 Signal Wheel can create a compelling profile of the customer, which can be used to establish their identity every time they visit the merchant’s storefront. This continuous assessment enables the merchants to establish the trust level even before the customer reaches the checkout and tailor their browsing and checkout experience.


In many cases, the most effective authentication experience is the one customers barely notice. By establishing trust before a transaction takes place, businesses can reduce unnecessary challenges while maintaining strong security outcomes. In the increasing convergence between customer experience and risk management, this is a potent tool available to the merchants.


While identity intelligence helps establish trust before the transaction begins, innovations in authentication and payment credentials have been helping to reduce friction during the transaction itself.


## **How Passkeys and Tokenization Are Redefining Seamless Commerce**


At the other end of the payment chain, the issuers are facing similar challenges in balancing the customers’ demands of a frictionless payment experience and protecting them from fraud and misuse.


With the growth in ecommerce, there has been an explosion of stored-on-file card information which makes the customer checkout journey faster, but this also increases the risks of breach and compromise. Especially if the merchants do not have robust processes and security. The proliferation of personal devices has also led to a remarkable shift in customer preference to transact using their personal devices and store payment information on them. This presents both a risk and an opportunity.


Since the overwhelming majority of such devices are personal in nature and remain with their owners, device fingerprinting and facial recognition has emerged as a major tool to silently establish the customer identity. The rich data available on how customers use their devices paints a unique personal profile of a customer and helps to identify them with a degree of confidence.


###


**How Passkeys Simplify Authentication**


As the next step in this evolution,


[Passkeys](https://fidoalliance.org/passkeys/) have emerged as a preferred solution for making the customer journey smoother. Passkeys allow customers to leverage their existing device biometrics and authentication methods, which they already use frequently, to authenticate themselves during a transaction. Once registered, it works seamlessly and quickly, leading to a familiar experience, less friction, and better conversions.


###


**Why Tokenization Has Become the New Standard**


Similarly, to solve the issue of card data security at the merchants,


[tokenization](https://docs.xendit.co/docs/cards-network-tokens) has become the new standard. The merchants no longer need to store the card data and the tokens can be saved with the networks, leading to improved portability and automatic refresh of card information. All of these measures have made a significant impact on the checkout process leading to less friction, improved security and higher conversion rates.


## **Balancing the Relationship Between Security and Customer Experience**


With these new tools, data and methods, the sophisticated merchants and issuers can now build continuous trust in the identity of their users and they can leverage it to separate high-risk transactions from the low-risk ones much more precisely. This has enabled a number of progressive payments players to adopt a


**risk-based approach to authentication,** where they can decide to remove the authentication friction for lower risk transactions and customers altogether.


This risk-based approach allows for more adaptive customer journeys where the low-risk customers can receive a hassle-free check-out experience and the friction can be dialed up for high-risk customers based on their profile and transaction history. This also acts as a powerful differentiator for the companies to retain their best customers and generate repeat business.


[Risk-based authentication (RBA)](https://www.onespan.com/topics/risk-based-authentication-rba) complements passkeys and network tokens very well to make the customer journey seamless. As such, businesses can now improve their customer experience while at the same time reducing lost conversions and associated overhead costs.


## **Building an Ecosystem of Shared Trust**


Despite the improvements in authentication, transactional trust still remains an ecosystem-wide responsibility. The other hurdle is that the digital payment ecosystem still remains fragmented, which is an impediment to creating a fully optimized payment journey for the customers. Merchants, issuers and acquirers have visibility to different parts of the payment journey, have access to different data points about a customer, and can come to different conclusions about the risk of the same customer or transaction.


### **Different Players, Different Views of Risk**


****


The challenge is that every participant in the payment ecosystem sees a different piece of the customer journey:


- **Merchants** have visibility into browsing behavior, cart activity, and purchase history on their own platforms


- **Acquirers** have visibility into payment processing activity, merchant transaction performance, and payment acceptance and settlement data.


- **Issuers** can see cardholder transaction history across multiple merchants, broader spending patterns, and cross-merchant behavior insights


**No single participant has a complete view of the customer** . As a result, different parties may reach different conclusions about the same customer or transaction, creating unnecessary friction within the payment journey.


Therefore, while a merchant may want to provide a friction-free checkout experience to a trusted user, the issuer may treat them differently, or vice-versa, leading to additional steps and risk of abandonment.


### **Aligned Interests for Shared Trust**


Even though there are differing views, the interests of all participants still are aligned: Getting Completed transactions. Therefore, it is essential that we all work together to facilitate the good customers while protecting the payment ecosystem from fraud and abuse. But this will take a great deal of coordination and trust among payment ecosystem participants: issuers, merchants, and acquirers. We will need a way to share their risk assessments with each other and a common standard to report and interpret the risk ratings and other signals so that there is consistent assessment and treatment of the customers.


The networks need to be a central part of coordinating this exchange by setting the standards and guidance for treatment of shared information. Some steps have already been taken with the network offerings such as


[Mastercard Identity Check](https://developer.mastercard.com/product/identity-check/) and


[Visa Secure](https://www.visasecureservices.com/overview) to enable risk-based, frictionless authentication but a lot more must be done to effectively bring together these fragments of information which can together create a more secure and seamless environment for everyone, most of all for the customers.


## **What the Future of Digital Trust Could Look Like**


With better collaboration, the future of digital payments could look like a well-tuned machine where a customer is recognized across issuers, acquirers and merchants, their identity and transaction history travels with them, and they receive a consistent, hassle-free experience across various points of payment.


In the future,


**trust** may become increasingly


**portable across the ecosystem** . Rather than repeatedly proving their identity at every step, legitimate customers should be able to carry trusted signals across merchants, issuers, and payment providers. The result would be a payment experience that feels increasingly invisible, while


[maintaining strong protection against fraud.](https://www.xendit.co/en/products/fraud-detection/)


Passkeys, network tokens, risk-based authentication, continuous identity monitoring and trust scores are all key milestones in that journey to reduce the steps, increase security and enhance the customer experience for a seamless checkout.


### **The Business Case for Digital Trust**


For businesses, digital trust is no longer just a fraud prevention objective. It has become a driver of conversion, customer retention, and long-term growth.


The future of digital trust will not be defined by stronger passwords or additional authentication steps alone. It will be shaped by how effectively the entire payment ecosystem combines identity intelligence, shared trust signals, and seamless customer experiences to create security that works quietly in the background.


Everyone, including the customer, benefits when a transaction is completed smoothly and securely, and the silent, proactive and collaborative approach to digital trust will enable the next phase of expansion in the industry. The winners in this journey will be the ones who adopt the new capabilities and address the customers’ needs proactively by


**reducing friction without compromising security.** Are you standing still and watching others, or are you working on retaining your best customers by improving their experience?
