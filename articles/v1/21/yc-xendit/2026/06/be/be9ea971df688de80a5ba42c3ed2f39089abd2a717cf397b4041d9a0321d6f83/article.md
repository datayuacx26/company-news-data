# Third-Party Integration Fees Explained: What E-Commerce Businesses Need to Know

Running an e-commerce store means making a lot of decisions about tools and platforms, and those decisions come with costs that are not always obvious upfront. One of the most commonly overlooked is the fee that comes with connecting a third-party payment provider to your e-commerce platform.


When you use a payment provider other than the platform's built-in solution, both the platform and the payment provider may charge fees for that integration. These fees exist for legitimate reasons, but understanding them and how they stack on top of each other is essential for accurately calculating your total cost of accepting payments.


This article breaks down what third-party integration fees are, how they work on platforms like Shopify, why payment providers charge them, and what to look for when evaluating the total cost of your payment setup.


**Key Takeaways**


- Third-party integration fees are charges applied when you connect an external payment provider to an e-commerce platform


- E-commerce platforms like Shopify charge an additional transaction fee when merchants use a payment provider other than their built-in solution


- Payment providers may also charge their own integration or plugin fees on top of standard processing fees


- In markets where the platform's built-in payment solution is unavailable - such as most of Southeast Asia - using a third-party provider is not optional


- Understanding the full fee stack helps businesses make more informed decisions about their payment setup


## **What are third-party integration fees?**


Third-party integration fees are charges that arise when you connect an external payment provider to handle payment processing for your store.


These fees can come from two directions. First, the e-commerce platform itself may charge an additional fee for routing transactions through a provider outside its ecosystem. Second, the payment provider may charge a fee for maintaining the technical integration with the platform, covering the costs of building, maintaining, and supporting the connection between the two systems.


Together, these charges sit on top of the standard payment processing fee your provider charges per transaction.


They are separate line items


, and they need to be factored into your total cost of accepting payments to get an accurate picture of what each transaction actually costs your business.


## **How third-party fees work on Shopify**


**Shopify** is one of the most widely used e-commerce platforms in the world, and its fee structure is a useful illustration of how third-party integration fees work in practice.


Shopify has its own built-in payment solution called Shopify Payments. When merchants use Shopify Payments, they pay a processing fee per transaction - and no additional platform fee applies. But when merchants choose to use a third-party payment provider instead, Shopify charges an additional transaction fee on top of the provider's own processing fees.

## **Why do payment providers charge integration fees?**


Beyond the transaction fee that Shopify charges, payment providers themselves may charge a fee for maintaining the integration with platforms like Shopify. This is sometimes referred to as a plugin fee, partner fee or platform integration fee.


These fees exist because building and maintaining a reliable payment integration with a major e-commerce platform requires ongoing technical investment. The integration needs to stay current with platform updates, support new features, handle error cases, and maintain security standards - all of which carry a real cost for the payment provider.


For merchants, this fee is the cost of accessing a payment provider's capabilities through a platform they are already using - without needing to build a custom integration from scratch. It is infrastructure cost, passed through in a structured way.


The specific amount and structure of integration fees varies by payment provider. Some charge a flat monthly fee, others charge a percentage of transaction volume processed through the integration, and some bundle integration costs into their processing fees without a separate line item.


At Xendit, a Shopify partner fee is charged as a percentage of the transaction volume processed through the Xendit integration on Shopify. It then reflects the cost of maintaining a reliable, up-to-date connection between Xendit's payment infrastructure and Shopify's platform.

## Additional e-commerce costs merchants should account for


Third-party integration fees are just one part of the cost structure for e-commerce businesses. Other charges that commonly affect profitability include:


1. App and plugin subscriptions


Shopify's app ecosystem includes thousands of tools for marketing, inventory, customer service, and more. Paid apps typically range from $10 to $300 or more per month, and costs can accumulate quickly across multiple tools.


2. Currency conversion fees


Selling internationally involves currency conversion, which typically carries a fee of around 1.5% per transaction on top of standard processing costs.


3. Chargeback and dispute fees


When customers dispute transactions, chargeback fees apply on top of the disputed amount. For a full breakdown of how these work, refer to our guide on

4. Refund fees.


Issuing refunds typically means absorbing the original processing fee. Some providers also charge a separate refund fee.

## **How integration fees affect profitability**


For businesses with low transaction volumes, third-party integration fees may be a manageable cost. But as volumes grow, the cumulative impact becomes more significant - and worth tracking as a distinct line item rather than burying it in general payment costs.


A merchant processing $50,000 per month on Shopify's Basic plan with a third-party provider pays $1,000 per month in Shopify partner fee alone - before any processing or integration fees from the payment provider. Upgrading to the Advanced plan at $299/month reduces the transaction fee to 0.5%, saving $750/month in Shopify fees while adding $270/month in plan cost, a net saving of $480/month at that volume.


Mapping this out for your specific volume and plan helps identify where fee optimization has the biggest impact on your bottom line.


## **How to evaluate third-party payment providers for your e-commerce store**


When choosing a third-party payment provider for your e-commerce store, the processing rate alone is not enough to make a good decision. Here is what to evaluate across the full cost picture.


1. **Total fee stack**


Add up the platform transaction fee, the provider's processing fee, and any integration or plugin fees. This gives you the true per-transaction cost, not just the headline processing rate.


2. **Local payment method coverage**


Especially for businesses in Southeast Asia, the ability to accept local payment methods - e-wallets, bank transfers, QRIS, virtual accounts - directly affects your conversion rate. A provider with strong local coverage can recover more revenue than a lower processing rate saves.


3. **Fraud detection and dispute management**


A provider with strong fraud detection reduces the chargeback exposure that adds to your total cost of payments.


Dispute management capabilities


affect how efficiently your team handles cases when they arise.


4. **Settlement speed and reliability**


Processing fees matter less if settlements are slow or unreliable. Evaluate the provider's settlement track record alongside their cost structure.


5. **Multi-market support**


If your store sells across multiple Southeast Asian markets, a provider with licensed entities and local infrastructure in each market reduces complexity and compliance risk.
