The promotion runs from June 1 through June 30, 2026.


However, because this bonus is being offered directly on the Qatar Airways end rather than by the card issuers themselves, there is a massive catch you need to know about before hitting "transfer."


#### The Deal & Key Terms


When you initiate a transfer during the campaign window, you will receive the standard 1:1 transfer rate immediately. The extra 30% will be calculated per transaction and tacked on by the airline later.


The Promo Window: June 1, 2026, through June 30, 2026 (GMT+3).


The Math: A transfer of 1,000 bank points will net you 1,300 total Avios.


The Catch: According to the official terms, the bonus Avios are slated to hit your account by July 31, 2026.


Irreversible: Once you trigger the conversion, the transaction is final and cannot be reversed.


#### The "Delayed Posting" Warning


Because the terms state the bonus points can take until the end of July to post, this promo is fundamentally useless if you need to book an available award ticket right this second. If you see an open seat today, transferring only the base amount and waiting a month for the bonus to arrive means that seat will likely be long gone.


*Note: While some early data points from users indicate the bonus has been posting immediately for certain accounts, the official terms give Qatar a massive window. Proceed with caution if timing is tight.*


#### Why the Avios Ecosystem Changes Everything


Usually, a delayed transfer bonus is an automatic pass. However, Avios is a highly unique currency because it is shared across a global family of airlines.


You can seamlessly link your loyalty accounts and shift your Avios back and forth at a 1:1 ratio between:


- Qatar Airways


- British Airways


- Iberia


- Aer Lingus


- Finnair


Because you can move the points freely, a 30% bonus to Qatar is effectively a 30% bonus to any of these airlines' sweet spots. Even if you just use Qatar as a middleman, you can send those points over to British Airways or Iberia to book travel across the entire Oneworld alliance.


#### Top Sweet Spots to Target


If you are willing to wait out the bonus, or treat it as a point rebate on a future trip, here are some of the absolute best ways to spend Avios:


**1. Qatar Qsuites Business Class (54k Citi Points)**


Qatar Airways notoriously releases extra Business Class award space to its own Privilege Club members that partner airlines can't see. A one-way flight from major US hubs (like New York, Los Angeles, or Chicago) to Doha costs a flat 70,000 Avios. With the 30% bonus factored in, you're looking at just 54,000 credit card points one-way (plus $200–$300 in taxes).


**2. Iberia Business Class to Spain (27k Citi Points)**


For off-peak travel from Northeast US gateways (like Boston or New York) to Madrid, Iberia charges a mere 34,000 Avios one-way. Shifting your points through Qatar over to Iberia means you can secure a lie-flat business class seat across the Atlantic for an astonishing 27,000 transferable points.


**3. Aer Lingus to Dublin (10k / 39k Citi Points)**


Aer Lingus offers incredible value during off-peak windows from US cities like Boston, Chicago, and Minneapolis to Ireland. Economy seats go for 13,000 Avios, while Business Class goes for 50,000 Avios. With the transfer bonus, that drops to just 10,000 points for economy or 39,000 points for business.

#### The Verdict: Should You Transfer?


Our golden rule is almost always to never transfer points speculatively. Frequent flyer programs can devalue their award charts overnight without warning, and sitting on a massive stash of locked airline miles is a dangerous game. Furthermore, Avios will expire if your account sees absolutely zero activity over an extended period.


However, the flexibility to shift Avios between five major global airlines makes a speculative transfer much more palatable here than it would be with a single airline. If you are a frequent traveler who books Oneworld award flights on a regular basis, pulling the trigger to bag a 30% rebate for future travel is a highly defensible move.


Just ensure you don't need those bonus points to book a flight departing next week!
