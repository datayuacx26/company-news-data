American Express is offering a 30% transfer bonus to Virgin Atlantic Flying Club through July 31, 2026, updating the conversion ratio to 1:1.3. This promotion drops the cost of luxury ANA First Class flights down to just 56,000 Amex points, making a premium first class ticket cheaper than a regularly priced business class award.


American Express has launched its latest summer promotion, giving cardholders a 30% bonus when converting Membership Rewards points into Virgin Atlantic Flying Club. The promotion is active through Friday, July 31, 2026, offering an excellent window to stretch the value of your credit card points.


Under standard conditions, Amex points move over to Virgin Atlantic at a 1:1 ratio. This temporary promotion bumps the conversion up to a 1:1.3 ratio, meaning a transfer of 10,000 Amex points will yield 13,000 Virgin Points in your loyalty account. Point transfers must be completed in increments of 1,000 and typically process instantly, allowing you to secure flights without a lengthy waiting period.

## The ANA Multiplier Phenomenon: First Class for Less Than Business


While Virgin Atlantic points can be used to book travel across the SkyTeam alliance, the ultimate sweet spot of this promotion lies with its partner airline, All Nippon Airways (ANA). Virgin Atlantic utilizes a distance-based award chart for flights operated by ANA to Japan, which creates a highly unusual pricing anomaly when combined with a 30% transfer bonus.


Regularly priced ANA Business Class tickets from the U.S. East Coast to Tokyo require 60,000 Virgin Points one-way. However, if you book a one-way ANA First Class ticket from the U.S. West Coast, the standard rate is 72,500 Virgin Points.


When you apply the 30% Amex transfer bonus, the actual credit card point requirement shifts dramatically:


- **ANA Business Class (East Coast to Tokyo):**


47,000 Amex points


- **ANA First Class (West Coast to Tokyo):**


56,000 Amex points


- **ANA First Class (East Coast to Tokyo):**


66,000 Amex points


Because of the bonus math, booking a flagship ANA First Class suite from the West Coast requires fewer transferable points than booking a regularly priced ANA Business Class flight from the East Coast. Getting a top-tier First Class cabin featuring gourmet dining and absolute privacy for just 56,000 points is an unmatched deal, especially considering that many competing airlines charge well over 100,000 points for a basic business class seat on the same route.


## Evaluating the Transfer


As a rule of thumb, you should avoid transferring flexible points speculatively without an immediate flight to book. Once your Amex points are converted into Virgin Points, they cannot be moved back to your credit card account. However, if you have been actively looking to book premium travel to Japan or Europe over the coming year, this 30% bonus provides a perfect opportunity to maximize your balance before the promotion concludes at the end of July.
