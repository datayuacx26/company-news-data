What if I told you that all you have to do to get the hardware of your dreams- totally for free and yours to keep- is to come and work for Pocket Worlds?! It’s a pretty sweet deal, and one we’re proud of bringing to life. Nedko and Jacob from our dev teams describe their experiences.

### **NEED FOR SPEED… AND MEMORY**


I’m not sure about you, but I definitely have a love-hate relationship with my hardy 2015 Macbook Air. It does a decent job of managing my 30 open tabs and a few background applications, only occasionally stuttering through a Zoom meeting before freezing up entirely.


Of course, simple word processing with dozens of open tabs is probably the peak strain my little fella has ever faced. Our devs, on the other hand, have requirements that are just a touch more demanding.


Jacob’s on our client team and started with the team last July with a faithful 2017 Macbook Pro in tow. Though decked out with the latest and greatest at the time of its release, it only featured a paltry 16 gigs of memory, and immediately began to hamper Jacob’s workflow. “Generally it was not good enough for developing the projects. It took around 10 minutes to build the project each time. For our job you have to do it over and over again so it was super annoying.”


Has a friend ever shown you a ten-minute song that you really weren’t into, but which you politely sat through anyway? Imagine that, but a dozen times a day.


If this sounds like an unusually long build time, even for a slow computer, you’re not wrong. Our app Highrise is more complex than your typical run-of-the-mill project. “The way to think of it is this: it’s composed of three different coding languages at the same time. The codebase I’ve worked with before was only one part of that, and if we used a different language, it would be a separate project. In Highrise, it’s all crammed into one, that’s why it takes so long,” Jacob explains.


So, our client team obviously needs some heavy-duty equipment to keep up with the hefty load of our triple-layered code. However, our server team isn’t without high-powered computing demands of its own. “We don’t have as high of requirements as the client team, but it still affects your workflow to wait a little more if your CPU is of an older generation,” says Nedko, who joined the team this January.


“My laptop was five years old, not cutting edge hardware,” he continued. “I was managing for the first two months, but did feel the need to upgrade, and with the upgrade, the experience changed significantly.”


### **UPGRADE TIME, BABY**


We’ve got our CTO Jimmy to thank for our groovy equipment sponsorship policy. Five months into his Pocket career, Jacob realized he’d need to improve his computer ASAP. “I’d been kinda talking about it, and been pushing toward making that happen,” he recounts. Within weeks, Pocket Worlds adopted the equipment benefit we all know and love: full equipment sponsorship, with the added perk of complete ownership after two years.


“I really went for that in my previous company, and I’ve seen how much of an improvement it was for me… It’s great that they could organize this in a short time for me when I needed it. It’s a huge bonus.”


By November, Jacob was a man reborn: armed with a brand spanking new iMac with maxed-out specs, the difference was “like day and night. It improved workflow since it allowed me to cut the time of the build by 60% to about four minutes. *That’s* the difference.”


Best of all, you really don’t have to worry about limitations on what kind of hardware you choose. It’s going to be yours forever, after all! Nedko, for instance, doesn’t use iOS, which is the standard for most of our devs: “I’ve been using Linux for the last ten years, so I feel it would significantly hamper my productivity \[to switch\]- I was anxious I would be forced to use Mac. They were ok with me using Linux, but we used some workarounds, so I’m still using the same tools I used before.
“I have a newer model Dell laptop, and a 27-inch monitor- which definitely helps my eyes!”


Let’s face it: when you’re shopping around for your next job, you want to interview your potential employer just as much as they want to interview you. A great benefits package can make or break your next opportunity, and that’s why we want to share the full story behind our rad equipment policy. Cue the sales pitch jingle, but we’re just not like the other fully remote tech companies.


Fellow remote workplaces often do provide equipment to new hires, and many throw in an office stipend to boot. Some include[similar perks](https://www.invisionapp.com/careers) like wellness and education budgets, or sweet[vacation policies](https://www.knack.com/careers) including company retreats (all of which we offer too, by the way). But do you get to choose your computer? Or keep it until we pry its cold, metal corpse from your hands to replace with the next best top-of-the-line hardware? Didn’t think so.


So, once you do find your next shiny new baby, go ahead and adopt it. You’re both totally worth it.
