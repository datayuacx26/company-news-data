# California Certified Payroll Requirements: Wage Determinations, Apprenticeship Ratios, and Contractor Compliance Before You File

California's prevailing wage and certified payroll system is among the most complex in the nation. The Department of Industrial Relations (DIR) administers the framework, which layers mandatory electronic filing, apprenticeship utilization requirements, and craft-specific wage determinations on top of the standard certified payroll obligations most contractors are familiar with from federal Davis-Bacon work. Contractors who approach California public works compliance with the assumption that federal experience is sufficient. The state's enforcement apparatus, including project-level labor compliance programs, electronic filing mandates, and apprenticeship ratio tracking, creates compliance obligations that demand dedicated systems and processes. often discover that the state's rules are broader in scope, stricter in enforcement, and more administratively demanding than anything at the federal level.


## **When California prevailing wage applies**


California Labor Code Section 1771 establishes the baseline: all workers employed on public works must be paid not less than the applicable prevailing wage rate when the project value exceeds $1,000. The threshold is low enough that virtually every public works contract triggers the obligation.


### **What qualifies as public works**


Labor Code Section 1720 defines public works broadly. The definition covers construction, alteration, demolition, installation, and repair work performed under contract and paid for in whole or in part with public funds. Certain privately funded projects that receive public subsidies or are developed under specific tax incentive programs also trigger prevailing wage requirements.


The breadth of California's definition means contractors encounter prevailing wage obligations on a wider range of projects than in most other states. School maintenance, water system upgrades, transit infrastructure, affordable housing with public subsidies, and charter school construction have all triggered coverage under various California court interpretations and DIR guidance.


Contractors should verify prevailing wage coverage with the awarding agency before bidding, because the obligation can attach to projects that do not appear publicly funded on the surface. Failure to verify before bid submission is a risk that experienced California contractors take seriously.


### **DIR registration**


All contractors and subcontractors performing public works in California must register with the DIR before bidding on or beginning covered work. Registration is a prerequisite for contract award, and performing work without valid registration can result in penalties and contract rescission.


## **DIR wage determinations**


The DIR publishes prevailing wage determinations by trade classification and county. Each determination specifies the basic hourly rate, fringe benefits (employer payments for health, pension, vacation, and training), and the total hourly rate. Rates are updated periodically, and contractors must apply the rate in effect at the time the project was advertised for bid.


### **Apprenticeable versus non-apprenticeable trades**


California distinguishes between trades that are apprenticeable and those that are not. Apprenticeable trades are marked with a "#" tag on the wage determination. For apprenticeable trades, the employer must pay a training fund contribution to either a DIR-approved apprenticeship program or the California Apprenticeship Council (CAC). The training contribution cannot be paid directly to the worker as cash.


For non-apprenticeable trades, the total hourly rate must be paid entirely to the worker through a combination of wages and benefits. No average costing of benefits across employees is permitted; the obligation is calculated per worker.


## **Apprenticeship ratio requirements**


California's apprenticeship requirements go beyond what most states and the federal government require. Contractors on public works projects valued at $30,000 or more must employ apprentices in applicable trades.


### **The ratio and the process**


For applicable classifications, apprentices must be employed at a ratio of not less than one apprentice for every five journeyworkers (20%) for the entirety of the project, though the specific ratio can vary by trade. Apprentices must be registered with a program approved by the Division of Apprenticeship Standards (DAS), not merely registered with the federal Office of Apprenticeship. Federal-only registration does not satisfy California's requirement.


Contractors must complete and submit several forms:


-


DAS 140 (Public Works Contract Award Information), notifying apprenticeship committees of the project


-


DAS 142 (Request for Dispatch of an Apprentice), requesting apprentices for each applicable trade


Failure to request apprentices through the proper channels, even if the contractor intends to meet the ratio, is itself a compliance violation.


## **Certified payroll filing requirements**


California requires electronic certified payroll reporting through the DIR's eCPR (Electronic Certified Payroll Reporting) system. Paper filing is not accepted for most projects.


### **Filing form and content**


The primary certified payroll form in California is the A-1-131 (Public Works Payroll Reporting Form). Each filing must document worker name, trade classification, hours worked, wages paid, and benefits provided. Training contributions must be reported separately.


Contractors must also maintain Statements of Compliance and Fringe Benefit Statements, and submit them alongside certified payroll records as required by the awarding agency.


### **Filing schedule**


Certified payroll reports must be available to the awarding agency and the DIR within specific timeframes established in the contract. Most contracts require monthly submission, though the underlying records must be maintained on a weekly basis. Contractors should confirm the specific filing schedule with the awarding agency, as deadlines can vary.


### **Recordkeeping**


Contractors must maintain certified payroll records for the duration of the project and for a period after completion as required by the awarding body and California law. Records must be available for inspection by the DIR, the awarding agency, and labor compliance programs at any time.


## **Penalties and enforcement**


California's enforcement structure uses both state-level DIR enforcement and project-specific labor compliance programs (LCPs). Many awarding agencies are required to operate or contract for an LCP, which monitors compliance on individual projects.


### **Penalty structure**


Penalties for prevailing wage violations under California law include:


-


Back wages to affected workers for the full amount underpaid


-


A penalty of not less than $200 per worker per calendar day for each violation of prevailing wage requirements under Labor Code Section 1775


-


Forfeiture of $25 per worker per calendar day for overtime violations under Labor Code Section 1813


-


Debarment from public works for three years for contractors found to have willfully violated prevailing wage requirements


### **The role of labor compliance programs**


LCPs review certified payroll records, conduct worker interviews, and investigate complaints on projects where they are assigned. Contractors working on projects with an active LCP should expect regular audits and documentation requests throughout the project, not just at closeout.

## **Skilled and trained workforce requirements**


Certain California projects carry an additional requirement beyond prevailing wage and apprenticeship: the Skilled and Trained Workforce (STW) mandate. Under Labor Code Sections 2600-2603, designated projects require that a minimum percentage of the workforce hold journey-level status through a state-approved apprenticeship program. The required percentage ramps up over time.


Contractors on projects with STW requirements must track and document the qualification status of each worker, in addition to prevailing wage and apprenticeship compliance. The administrative overhead is significant, but failure to meet STW requirements can result in contract penalties and project shutdown orders.

## **Frequently Asked Questions**


The questions below address what contractors ask most about California certified payroll and prevailing wage compliance.


### What is the project threshold for California prevailing wage?


Labor Code Section 1771 requires prevailing wages on public works projects exceeding $1,000, which covers virtually every public contract.


### Must California apprentices be registered with DAS, not just the federal program?


Yes. Only apprentices registered with a DAS-approved program satisfy California's apprenticeship requirements. Federal-only registration is insufficient.


### What form is used for California certified payroll?


The A-1-131 (Public Works Payroll Reporting Form) is the primary form, submitted electronically through DIR's eCPR system.


### What are the penalties for California prevailing wage violations?


Penalties include back wages, at least $200 per worker per day of violation, overtime forfeitures, and debarment from public works for up to three years.


### How do I request apprentices for a California public works project?


File DAS 140 to notify apprenticeship committees of the contract award, then file DAS 142 to request dispatch of apprentices for each applicable trade.


### Does California require electronic certified payroll filing?


Yes. Most projects require electronic filing through the DIR's eCPR system. Paper submissions are generally not accepted.
