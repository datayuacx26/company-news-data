# Receiving SAR from Saudi Arabian Clients: A Complete Guide for Indian Exporters and Service Firms

The deal with your Saudi client is closed. The purchase order is signed. Now comes the part nobody warned you about: getting the money into your Indian bank account cleanly. Not shipping, not SABER, not customs – the payment.


Your bank is asking for purpose codes you have never heard of. Your FIRC is missing. Your GST refund is stuck. And you are still not sure whether you should have invoiced in SAR or USD in the first place.


Here is the correction most exporters miss. Under FEMA and RBI rules, every inward remittance from a Saudi client is a regulated foreign exchange transaction with documentation, timelines, and compliance obligations that sit squarely on you, the Indian exporter.


For scale, India exported approximately USD 11.76 billion to Saudi Arabia in FY2024-25, making this one of the busiest payment corridors for Indian exporters. This guide walks you through the full payment receipt process so your Saudi revenue lands cleanly every time.


### Key Takeaways


- Receiving SAR from Saudi clients is governed by FEMA and RBI export proceeds realisation rules.
- You can invoice in SAR or USD. The choice affects forex conversion timing and bank documentation.
- A SWIFT wire transfer (TT) via a correspondent bank is the most common channel.
- Every inward remittance needs the correct RBI purpose code (typically P0101 or P0102 for goods, P0802 for IT services).
- RBI now mandates realisation of export proceeds within 15 months from the date of export for goods, software, and services.
- A FIRC or eBRC links your payment to your GST zero-rating claim.
- Non-compliance with Saudi e-invoicing rules can trigger fines starting at **SAR 5,000 on the seller side** , while the **buyer faces blocked VAT deductions** , which can delay your payment.

### The SAR Peg and What It Means for Your Forex Exposure


Because SAR is pegged to USD, your real forex risk sits between USD and INR, not between SAR and INR directly. Invoicing in SAR versus USD does not materially change your currency risk, but it changes how your Indian bank processes and documents the remittance.

## How to Receive Payment from Saudi Arabia in India – The Step-by-Step Process


### Step 1 – Set Up Your Bank Account Correctly


Use a current account designated for foreign inward remittances. Inform your bank that you will receive SAR or USD wire transfers, and confirm your bank’s SWIFT BIC code and correspondent bank details. Incorrect intermediary details are a leading cause of payment delays.


### Step 2 – Issue a Compliant Export Invoice


Include exporter name and GSTIN, buyer name and Saudi address, IEC number, description of goods or services, invoice currency and amount, SWIFT payment instructions, and a purpose code reference. Note that Saudi authorities require the country of origin to be shown on imported goods in Arabic or English. For service exports, reference the contract number to support eBRC matching later.


### Step 3 – The Saudi Client Initiates the SWIFT Transfer


The Saudi client’s bank sends a SWIFT MT103 message to your Indian bank, routed through correspondent banks. Typical transit time is 2 to 5 business days. Ask your Saudi client to use the “OUR” charging option so the full invoice amount lands.


### Step 4 – Your Indian Bank Processes the Inward Remittance


The bank issues an Inward Remittance Advice and credits your account in INR at the day’s rate. It will ask for an export invoice copy, a shipping bill or service contract, and a purpose code declaration. Once submitted, the bank processes the FIRC or reports the remittance for eBRC compliance.

## Can Indian Exporters Invoice in SAR – and Should They?


### The Three Invoice Currency Options for Saudi Clients


- **Option 1 – Invoice in SAR:** Fully permissible under FEMA. Your bank converts SAR to INR on the credit date using the prevailing TT buying rate.
- **Option 2 – Invoice in USD:** The most common practice. Since SAR is pegged at 3.75 per USD, the equivalent amount is predictable.
- **Option 3 – Invoice in INR:** Technically permissible in limited cases under RBI’s Rupee trade settlement framework, but not widely practised in 2026.


### What Happens When SAR Hits Your Indian Bank Account


The bank applies the day’s TT buying rate. The difference between your invoice value and the INR credited is your realised forex gain or loss. For GST, the export invoice value is recorded in foreign currency at the invoice date.


> **Pro-Tip:** If your Saudi client insists on paying in SAR, ask your bank in advance for their SAR TT buying rate and the exact documentation they need.


## RBI Purpose Codes for Saudi Client Payments – How to Get This Right


### What Is an RBI Purpose Code and Why Does It Matter


Every inward remittance into India must be classified under an RBI purpose code, a 4-character alphanumeric code that tells the RBI the nature of the transaction. A wrong code means the bank may reject the remittance advice, your eBRC may not generate correctly, and your GST zero-rating claim may be delayed.


### The Most Relevant Purpose Codes for Indian Exporters Receiving SAR

Always confirm the applicable code with your bank’s trade finance desk. The RBI Master Direction on reporting of foreign exchange transactions is the authoritative source.


### Common Purpose Code Errors and How to Avoid Them


Using P0101 for a services contract triggers a bank query asking for a shipping bill that does not exist. Leaving the code blank means the bank defaults to a catch-all code that may not match your GST filing.


> **Pro-Tip:** Create an internal SOP that specifies the exact purpose code for each type of Saudi contract, and paste it into every invoice template.


## RBI Time Limits for Export Proceeds Realisation – What You Must Know


### The 15-Month Rule for Goods Exporters


Under FEMA and RBI’s Master Direction, exporters must realise and repatriate export proceeds within 15 months from the date of export, a relaxation from the earlier 9-month limit. This applies to all goods exports, including those to Saudi Arabia. If proceeds are late, apply to your Authorized Dealer bank for an extension.


### The 15-Month Rule for Service Exporters


For service exports, the same 15-month realisation period now applies uniformly to goods, software, and services. If you take an advance, exporters now have up to 3 years from receiving an advance payment to complete the shipment.


### What Happens If You Miss the Deadline


Your bank reports unrealised proceeds to the RBI via the XOS return. Beyond the allowed period, a formal compounding application may be required, and incentive claims such as RoDTEP may be blocked until realisation is confirmed.

## FIRC and eBRC – The Two Documents That Protect Your GST and Income Tax Position


### What Is a FIRC (Foreign Inward Remittance Certificate)


A FIRC is a certificate from your Indian bank confirming that a specific amount of foreign currency was received from a specific overseas party. It evidences:


- GST zero-rating of export services
- Income tax documentation of a foreign income source
- DGFT claims and export benefit schemes


### What Is an eBRC (Electronic Bank Realisation Certificate)


The eBRC is the digital counterpart of the FIRC, recorded on the DGFT portal. For goods exporters, it links your shipping bill to your export proceeds. Under the current model, exporters self-certify and generate the eBRC on the DGFT portal against one or multiple inward remittances, and service exporters must complete the “Mode of Export of Services” field.


For service exporters, this self-certification is what stands in for the shipping bill you don’t have — the eBRC is generated against the inward remittance and your service agreement rather than a customs record. Complete the “Mode of Export of Services” field so the entry closes correctly. The FIRC remains your primary evidence that consideration was received in convertible foreign exchange — the single document a GST officer will ask for when your zero-rating is questioned.


### How to Obtain Your FIRC After a SAR Payment


- Step 1: Submit your inward remittance advice with the export invoice and purpose code declaration.
- Step 2: The bank credits INR.
- Step 3: Request the FIRC. Some banks issue it automatically; others need a written request.
- Step 4: For eBRC, confirm the remittance data and self-certify on the DGFT portal.


Keep every FIRC and eBRC filed by financial year, since audits can go back 3 to 5 years.


## How to Convert SAR to INR – Understanding Forex Rates on Inward Remittances


### How Indian Banks Apply Exchange Rates


Banks use the TT Buying Rate for crediting inward remittances. It is set daily and is lower than the interbank mid-rate. The SAR to INR digital TT spread from the mid-market rate is typically 0.2% to 0.8%, driven down by highly competitive digital remittance corridors.


### SAR to INR – How the Conversion Actually Works


Since SAR is pegged to USD at 3.75, the effective SAR to INR rate is approximately (1 divided by 3.75) multiplied by the USD/INR TT Buying Rate. Always confirm the exact applied rate on your remittance advice. SAR volatility against INR mirrors USD volatility, so your real hedging instrument is a USD/INR forward contract.


### Reducing Forex Leakage on SAR Inward Remittances


Negotiate preferential TT Buying Rates if your volumes are high. Ask your Saudi client to specify the “OUR” charge option. For recurring contracts, consider an international payment platform offering better forex rates for export receipts.


> **Pro-Tip:** On a USD 50,000 equivalent payment, a 0.5% rate improvement saves roughly INR 20,000 to INR 25,000.

## Common Mistakes Indian Exporters Make When Receiving SAR Payments (And How to Avoid Them)


### Mistake 1 – Using a Personal or Savings Account


Personal accounts are not authorised for foreign exchange transactions under FEMA. The bank may hold the funds or refuse a FIRC. Always use a business current account.


### Mistake 2 – Wrong Purpose Code


If the bank assigns a default code, the eBRC is generated wrong, creating a GST mismatch. Confirm the code with your trade finance desk before submitting.


### Mistake 3 – Invoice and SWIFT Details Mismatch


Funds may arrive at the correspondent bank but not be credited, causing a 7 to 10 day delay. Lock your SWIFT BIC, account number, and bank details as a template on every invoice.


### Mistake 4 – Missing the RBI Realisation Deadline


Set a calendar reminder for 14 months after every export invoice date. If payment has not arrived, contact your bank to document the delay and apply for an extension.


### Mistake 5 – Not Requesting the FIRC


At audit time a missing FIRC lets zero-rating be challenged and GST become payable. Build an SOP that makes the FIRC request mandatory within 48 hours of each credit.


## Payment Terms to Negotiate with Saudi Clients Before You Start Invoicing


### Standard Payment Terms Used in Saudi B2B Trade


- **Advance Payment (30 to 100% upfront):** Common for first-time exporters. Eliminates payment risk.
- **Letter of Credit (LC):** Standard for large goods shipments. Secure but documentation-intensive.
- **Open Account (30, 60, or 90 days net):** Common for established relationships. Carries payment risk. Consider ECGC insurance.
- **Milestone-Based Payments:** Common in IT. Each milestone is a separate remittance requiring its own purpose code and FIRC.


### What to Include in Your Payment Clause


Specify the invoicing currency and which party bears the forex risk. Specify the “OUR” charging option. State the SWIFT bank details in the contract body, and include a clause confirming the client will initiate payment within a set number of days to protect your realisation timeline.


## Saudi Arabia-Specific Considerations That Affect Your Payment Timing


### Saudi Weekends, Public Holidays, and Banking Hours


Saudi banks operate Sunday to Thursday. If a due date falls on a Friday or Saturday, adjust it to the following Sunday. Public holidays such as Eid al-Fitr, Eid al-Adha, and Saudi National Day typically add 3 to 10 banking days. Build this buffer into cash flow forecasting.


### How Saudi SABER and Customs Compliance Connects to Your Payment


SABER is the official online conformity platform Saudi Arabia uses for regulated imports. If your shipment is held at customs due to a missing SABER certificate, your payment is delayed. The SABER Shipment Certificate of Conformity is required for each individual shipment, not just the first. Do not initiate the payment request until the shipment certificate is in place.


### Saudi e-Invoicing Rules and Your Invoice Format


Saudi Arabia’s e-invoicing mandate (ZATCA) applies to Saudi-registered businesses. Ask your Saudi client what format their system requires before you issue the first invoice, since a mismatch can delay their approval, and pressure is higher now that the Wave 24 integration deadline passed on 30 June 2026. Non-compliance can trigger fines of SAR 5,000 for the buyer. Solve it upfront.

## Frequently Asked Questions


### How do I receive payment from Saudi Arabia in India?


The standard mechanism is a SWIFT wire transfer from the Saudi client’s bank to your Indian current account. You provide your SWIFT BIC, account number, and bank details. The payment credits in 2 to 5 business days at your bank’s TT Buying Rate. After the credit, submit your invoice and purpose code declaration for the FIRC and eBRC.


### Can Indian exporters invoice in SAR?


Yes. It is fully permissible under FEMA. Your bank converts SAR to INR at the day’s TT Buying Rate. Since SAR is pegged to USD at 3.75, the conversion is predictable, though many exporters still prefer USD for streamlined workflows.


### What is the best way to receive SAR payments in India?


For ad-hoc payments, a SWIFT wire is standard. For recurring receipts, an international payment platform like Razorpay allows automated reconciliation and competitive forex rates.


### Is a FIRC required for export payments from Saudi Arabia?


Yes, especially for service exports. The FIRC is the primary evidence that consideration was received in convertible foreign exchange, mandatory for GST zero-rating. For goods, the eBRC serves a similar function.


### What is the RBI time limit for realising export proceeds?


Under current guidelines,[exporters must realise proceeds within 15 months from the date of export](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/rbi-extends-the-time-period-for-realisation-of-full-export-from-9-to-15-months) for goods, software, and services. If delayed, apply for an extension from your Authorized Dealer bank.


### Which purpose code should I use when a Saudi client pays me?


For goods use P0101 or P0102. For IT services use P0802. For consulting use P0899. Always verify with your trade finance desk.


### What documents does my bank need for a SAR inward remittance?


Typically the inward remittance advice, export invoice copy, purpose code declaration, and for goods the shipping bill number. For services, a service agreement usually suffices.


### How do I convert SAR to INR – who controls the exchange rate?


Your bank applies the TT Buying Rate on the credit date. Since SAR is pegged to USD at 3.75, the effective SAR/INR rate equals the USD/INR TT Buying Rate divided by 3.75. You can negotiate a better rate at high volumes.


### **Do I need a shipping bill to receive payment for services exported to a Saudi client?**


No. Service exports have no shipping bill because nothing physically crosses a border. Your proof of export is your service invoice, your contract or SOW, and your FIRC/eBRC confirming foreign-exchange receipt. Software and IT exporters additionally file a SOFTEX declaration (an EDF from 1 October 2026) so the RBI can record the export value.


### **My Saudi client wants to pay in INR – is it still a zero-rated export of services?**


No. Under Section 2(6) of the IGST Act, payment must be received in convertible foreign exchange for the supply to qualify as an export of services. If you are paid in INR through a domestic transfer, the export condition fails, and 18% IGST applies. Insist on a SAR or USD wire, or a forex payout, and make sure your FIRC records the receipt in foreign currency.
