# Why I’m Starting A Construction Technology Company

# Why I’m Starting A Construction Technology Company


### A Personal Story


[Alexander Seutin](https://substack.com/@teamdrillbit)


Oct 28, 2023


Hi everyone,


Over the past few years, I’ve found it harder and harder to pretend that I enjoy something that I don’t. I think a lot of people have felt that way about their jobs. Unfortunately, we all need money to take care of ourselves and our loved ones, and it’s really hard to carve out the room to do anything about it. Rising costs of living and stagnating wages are only making the situation worse. I recognize that it’s a luxury to be able to dwell on this when so many people are just trying to make it to next month, but going on without acknowledging something that feels so obviously broken feels wrong.


Like many others, I grew up tight on money. I was an only child to a single mom living in San Francisco. We did our best to make ends meet on an entry-level salary. While we weren’t homeless, paying rent and keeping the lights on was a real struggle. I dreamed of one day owning a house and building a world for my future family where we didn’t have to be so worried about the basics. That always seemed like the obvious step. After all, the American Dream is anchored by this once-basic now-out-of-reach goal.


In my teenage years, I buckled down and focused on my North Star. I went on to study at Stanford, and I was lucky enough to work as an engineer at some of the big-name companies like SpaceX, Apple, Adobe, and Rivian. I learned a lot throughout the process and scrooged every penny along the way. I struggled a lot in those years because as much as I tried, I just couldn't find any attachment or deeper meaning in my work - and work and external validation is all I had learned to value up to that point. I think this was rooted in my misalignment: the carefree and creative boy and the rigid and anxious man within me could not coexist. Neither respected the other or shared a value system. I wasn’t able to trust myself to take risks because I felt trapped by that North Star.


After several years in industry and a global pandemic, I finally felt ready to do something about all of this. I met my partner and (definitely not in an effort to look cool in front of her) took a leap of faith. I quit my job, traveled around with a truck and trailer, moved across the country, got a dog, started side businesses, fixed the trailer, bought others, fixed those up, and built confidence in my ability to make things happen outside of a cubicle. This all culminated in buying an old ugly house in Austin, TX, and finally living out my HGTV renovation dreams. The actual renovation story is for another day, but fast forward a couple of years, and now we finally have a stable roof overhead.


As grateful as I am to be here, the journey was not without its struggles (and cost!). We did almost all of the work ourselves and got to know at least a little about every trade - we learned a ton! But I also noticed that there were a lot of things that could have - and should have - been easier and cheaper. It’s no secret that the construction industry hasn’t benefited as heavily as others from modern technology. I think part of the reason is that there aren’t enough people who know both of these worlds well enough to do something about it.


So, over the course of this process, I slowly started thinking like an engineer again. Rather than treating that part of myself as something like I had outgrown or left behind, I accepted that software development was a skill that I had developed over 15 years - and, outside of all the company politics, it’s something that I really love. If directed towards something more meaningful, it could genuinely help other people.


That’s where Drillbit comes in. It’s a set of tools aimed at helping construction companies run more smoothly - throwing some grease on the wheel rather than reinventing it - and to start making housing cheaper. It’s an effort to align my priorities and my interests in a way that feels genuinely worthwhile. And it’s the first of many steps I’m taking to do my part to allow others to lift their heads up and be a little bit more human again.


This is going to be a long road, but it feels right, and I’m excited to share the journey with you all. If there’s one thing I’ve learned in all of this so far, it’s how to be persistent and not give up on my dreams. It’s intimidating, it’s a little crazy, it’s uncharted territory, but it feels like I finally found some alignment. If any of this resonates with you, I’d love it if you followed along.


Alex
