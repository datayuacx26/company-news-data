# 10 Secrets for Booking Cheap, Last-Minute Flights


November 1, 2024


8 min read


Last October, my sister-in-law and I flew to Connecticut for the weekend to celebrate her birthday, and I generously offered to pay for her flight. This gesture wasn’t as ostentatious as it seems … our round-trip flights cost just $38 each.


While this was the best deal I’ve found on flights, I’m no stranger to the world of cheap airfare. I am the queen of last-minute travel deals and discount flights. In the last six months, I’ve flown to Austin, Texas, for $66 and to Provo, Utah, for $59.


What’s my secret? How do I find these awesome deals? And, more importantly, how can you get in on the action?


Here are my 10 secrets to booking cheap, last-minute flights.


1. Don’t be afraid of discount airlines and regional carriers
2. Be flexible
3. Don’t play the airline’s games
4. Look at one-way flights
5. Time your purchase right
6. Use major booking sites … or don’t
7. Search for last-minute deals
8. Pay with points or miles
9. Check other airports
10. Say “no” to cookies


## 1. Don’t Be Afraid of Discount Airlines and Regional Carriers


Those super-cheap flights to Connecticut? I booked those on Avelo Airlines. A low-cost carrier that started operations in 2021, Avelo offers routes to smaller regional airports and secondary markets. Don’t let the affordable plane tickets fool you; Avelo is rated #1 for on-time performance. I’ve had positive experiences on every Avelo flight I’ve taken.


Avelo is just one of several discount airlines I use on a regular basis. Allegiant Air and Southwest Airlines are my other two go-to airlines. Add Breeze Airways, Frontier Airlines, JetBlue, and Spirit to the mix, as well. I have flown all of these, and, in general, I can report minimal issues and most of those were weather-related.


As with anything, your attitude is a major factor in the outcome. If you go into your travels on a discount airline with your expectations low and view the flight as simply a way to your destination, you may be pleasantly surprised. You may not be sipping champagne or stretching out in one of those bougie first-class pods, but you’ll get to your destination with extra spending money in your pocket.


## 2. Be Flexible


Most airlines know that vacationers work during the week and want to fly to their destination on Friday or Saturday. They also know that business travelers typically fly on Sundays, so they make it to their Monday morning meetings. Flights on these days generally cost more. Tuesday and Wednesday flights, however, can be as much as 25% less. I avoid flying on weekends whenever possible. Instead, I book mid-week flights. Last summer, I saved roughly $1200 total on flights for four people to Calgary by planning a Wednesday-to-Wednesday vacation.


Being flexible also means being willing to take early-morning or late-night flights. Since my travel philosophy is to get to my destination as cheaply as possible so I have more money to spend on enjoyable experiences, I’m okay with sleeping on a plane.


Being flexible also means being willing to take a flight with a layover. Of course, I prefer a direct flight, but if the price is right, I will book a flight with a layover.


## 3. Don’t Play the Airline’s Games


It is so frustrating to find an awesome deal on a flight only to see the airline charging additional fees for every little thing. Sometimes, paying for a checked bag is just as much as the flight itself. I refuse to play the airline’s games.


I do not pay for luggage. Instead, I carefully check the airline’s website for rules around luggage size and follow those rules. I prefer to travel light anyway.


I never pay to select my own seat. A friend recently confessed that she thought you HAD to pick your plane seat and pay the fee, or you wouldn’t have a place to sit on the flight. Seriously. When you purchase your airline ticket, you are reserving a seat. It just might not be the seat you want. Middle seats are not my favorite, but I will sit in one for a few hours if it saves me some money.


Even when I am vacationing with my husband, sister-in-law, or a friend, I don’t pay extra to sit next to my traveling companion. In my experience, about 75% of the time, the airlines put people who buy their tickets on the same transaction in adjacent seats whether they pay to select their seats or not. And if they don’t, I’m okay with that.


Some airlines charge a fee to allow passengers to board early, require a credit card number to access their Wi-Fi, charge for in-flight movies or headphones, and ask passengers to pay for snacks and beverages, even water. All of these services are overpriced, so it is a hard pass for me. Instead, I come with snacks, headphones, water bottle and anything else I might need for my flight.


## 4. Look at One-Way Flights


Have you ever found a great deal on a flight to your vacation destination, only to see that the return flight is six times higher? That happens to me all the time. It is so frustrating. I assume this is by design … the airlines know you have to get back home after your trip so they can get away with bumping up the price. What many people fail to realize is that they have options.


Airlines like to make you think that you get the best deals when you book round-trip tickets, but this isn’t always the case. If you find lopsided flights – one way is significantly higher in cost than the other – book the cheap one and look for an alternative flight for the other leg of your trip. Perhaps a different airline has a more affordable choice. You don’t have to book both legs of your trip with the same carrier, especially if you can find a better deal elsewhere.


## 5. Time Your Purchase Right


Whenever possible, I purchase my plane tickets on Tuesdays. Many times, airlines run sales on Mondays and Tuesdays if algorithm data indicates there will be seats to fill. Travel experts contend that this frugal hack no longer works like it did a decade or so ago, thanks in part to the increase in third-party booking websites. I maintain that it still has some traction. I rarely buy flights on a whim. Instead, a destination will catch my eye, and I’ll watch the flights for a while before I pull the trigger. That gives me time to see if the flights dip down at certain times … like on Tuesdays.


I’m not the only one who still follows this “book on Tuesday” hack. FareCompare.com conducted an extensive study of airfares on various days and times. They published their findings on their website in February 2024, concluding that the optimal time to buy a cheap flight is … you got it! … on Tuesdays, at 3 p.m. Eastern time.


Those same travel experts advise us to book our flights between 30 and 45 days before our trip, but cheap last-minute flights can be had much closer to your departure day. Airlines want all their flights to be full at take-off. An empty seat represents lost revenue. Unsold or canceled seats need to be filled, and the airlines are willing to cut huge deals to move them.


## 6. Using Major Booking Sites … Or Don’t


Google Flights, Booking.com, Kayak, Orbitz, Expedia, and all the other major travel booking sites are convenient because they allow you to search flights on multiple airlines at once and compare costs. Some booking sites operate like online travel agencies; they negotiate with airlines to get volume rates. Others simply scan various airline websites and show you those airfares. Sometimes, you can find great deals using a third-party booking site, but you may get better rates booking directly through the airlines. There are pros and cons to using major online booking sites.


**Pros:**


- You can compare several airlines at once
- You can filter by price to find the lowest fare
- You can find competitive prices and amazing deals


**Cons:**


- Some discount airlines are not included (like Southwest and Allegiant)
- There may be hidden fees
- You may have trouble resolving an issue because you’ve booked via a third-party site


Often, I begin my flight search with Booking.com, Skyscanner, or Google Flights to get a feel for average fares, but my research doesn’t end there. I move on to look at discount airlines and other sites, like Airfarewatchdog, CheapOair, Hopper, and more.


## 7. Search Last-Minute Deals Sites


In addition to Booking.com, Hopper, Skyscanner, and Airfarewatchdog, there are several other apps and websites that allow you to search for last minute cheap flights. I’ve visited GoLastMinute, asapTickets, CheapFlightFares, and more. Websites and apps like these work as clearinghouses for airlines that have empty seats to fill.


## 8. Pay with Points or Miles


Airlines frequently have programs for frequent travelers that reward their passengers. Most of these programs tally the miles of your flight and concert these into discounts off future flights. You can bank your frequent flyer miles and redeem them for cheap or free flights. These sound like a great deal, and I have heard of people who regularly fly for free, thanks to their accumulated airline miles, but personally, I’ve never been loyal enough to one airline over another to build up frequent flyer miles.


Some airlines offer their own credit card to their passengers. In addition to earning airline miles, these cards offer other perks, such as companion flights. When you purchase one flight, your spouse, friend, or companion gets their flight for free. I’ve looked into these and, if you are considering it, I would recommend that you look at the fine print, so you know exactly what you need to do to redeem the perks. One companion pass deal, for example, requires travelers to book “vacation packages.” That means you must book flights and hotels together to qualify for a companion flight. But I wanted the companion flights because my husband and I frequently fly to Portland, Oregon, to visit his brother and sister-in-law. When we do, we stay in their guest room, so we have no need for a hotel. No hotel, no companion flights.


Instead, I signed up for a MasterCard that has a travel reward program. When I buy gas, groceries, or even pay my utility bills, I earn travel points and airline miles that I can use to get discounted flights. For my situation, this was the better option, but you should do your own research to find the option that will give you the best travel discounts.


## 9. Check Other Airports


Many large cities have more than one airport. Chicago, for example, is home to O’Hare International Airport, one of the nation’s largest and busiest airports, and Midway International Airport, a much smaller, less congested airport. Travelers going to Orlando can choose between the larger Orlando International Airport and the much more manageable Sanford International Airport, located nearby. Do your research to see if your destination is serviced by multiple airports.


Low-cost airlines typically fly into these secondary airports. Not only can you find better deals by flying in and out of these smaller airports, but you often experience shorter lines at security, a shorter walk to your terminal, and easier parking options.


## 10. Say “No” to Cookies


It sure is convenient that websites store our login information, order histories, and payment methods, but there is a price for this convenience. When we visit websites, these sites store tiny bits of data on our computers, called cookies, that remember our info and report our browsing history back to them. When you visit an airline’s website and look at a specific flight or destination, the airlines know that you are interested in traveling to this place. Armed with your browsing history, the airlines often hike up their prices. You can take some steps to say “no” to cookies and keep costs down.


When you are doing your flight research, search in incognito mode. Incognito mode allows you to search the web without allowing your computer to store your internet search history or giving permission for your online activity to be shared with companies. Your browsing history stays private, even from the cookies that are spying on you.


You can also clear your computer’s cache of cookies. This process takes a few steps to do, but it is worth it to keep your browser history from falling into the hands of your airline of choice, where an algorithm can use your data against you.


## Bonus Tip: Ask for a Discount


[Before you book flights](https://wanderlog.com/blog/2024/11/01/10-secrets-for-booking-cheap-last-minute-flights/) , check with the airlines you plan to use to see if you can get a discount. Some airlines offer special rates for members of the military, veterans, first responders, teachers, students, and senior citizens. If you fall into one of these categories, don’t be afraid to ask if there is a discount program you can take advantage of. My husband is a firefighter. We’ve received discounts on luggage fees, hotel stays, and in-flight meals because the airlines want to show their appreciation for first responders. My college student daughter has asked for and received airfare discounts simply by showing her student ID. Many times, the airlines keep quiet about these discounts. But all you have to do is ask.


When you arrive at the airport for your departure, stop at the ticket desk. Ask the airline employee if you can get a free upgrade, free checked luggage, or a free in-flight meal. You never know. The answer may be ‘yes!’


Lastly, if you are taking a last-minute flight because you have had a death or terminal illness in your family, talk to the airline about a bereavement discount. In the past, it was more common for all airlines to offer a bereavement discount. Although many airlines have done away with this discount, some still do. Here’s what you need to do:


- Find the flight you want, but be willing to be flexible
- Gather documents and information – death certificate, funeral arrangements, name and contact information of the funeral director, hospital, attending physician’s name and contact information, name of a hospice organization, name, and contact information for your loved one’s hospice caregiver.
- Call the airline’s customer service number and talk to a representative about your situation.
- Be prepared to book over the phone.
- Expect a refund rather than a discount – you may have to pay full price for the flight and submit documentation to get a refund.
