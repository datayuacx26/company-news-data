# Wanderlog vs TripIt


November 26, 2024


13 min read


Trip planning can be stressful if you don’t have the right tools. Trip planning apps like TripIt and Wanderlog give travelers the tools they need to easily plan a vacation.


TripIt and Wanderlog are probably the two most well-known travel organizer apps out there, with them turning up on many best-of lists. TripIt has been around since 2012, and Wanderlog (our own app) since 2019! Both have millions of users and lots of fans.


We compared TripIt and Wanderlog on a variety of dimensions, including:


- How well do they keep track of your flights? (Answer: TripIt)
- How easy is it to import your reservations? (Answer: both are pretty good for different reasons)
- Which has a better interface for organizing your trip, both in a map and itinerary/list view? (Answer: Wanderlog, by a wide margin)
- How well do they keep track of your budget? (Answer: Wanderlog)
- Which app is better for group travel? (Answer: Wanderlog.)
- Which is the better value for money? (Answer: Wanderlog, with the most free features)


TripIt has great features when it comes to organization, like flight details and reservation import and organization, but Wanderlog offers more features, the majority of which you get with the free version of the app.


**Summary:** if you just want to keep track of your own flights and other reservations; TripIt is a solid choice (and in fact has more flight-related features than Wanderlog), but if you’d like to plan your whole trip (including which restaurants to visit, attractions, and share it with friends), Wanderlog is a better bet.


Here’s a breakdown of what we found when we tested the apps head-to-head:


## Wanderlog: Pros and cons


Wanderlog is an all-in-one travel planning app, making it easy to not only plan the details of your trip, but keep track of your vacation while you travel.


**Pros**


- Color-coded map filtered by day or by category
- Budgeting tools
- Robust AI capabilities


**Cons**


- Limited flight details
- No central email to send confirmations and reservations


## TripIt: Pros and cons


TripIt is great for keeping all your travel information, reservations, and confirmations organized in one place.


**Pros**


- Robust flight details, including airport maps and baggage claim info
- Central email to send all your reservations and confirmations to be added to your trip plan


**Cons**


- Limited mapping features
- No budgeting tool
- No real AI capabilities

## Flight details


Flying can be the most stressful part of a trip thanks to delays, long check-in lines, and security snafus, so you should have a trip planner that makes navigating the airport easier. Here are some features that can help with an easier flight.


**Real-time flight alerts:** Both Wanderlog and TripIt give you real-time flight alerts if your flight is delayed or canceled, or if there have been any changes to your gate or terminal. Both apps send alerts through push notifications to your phone. Flight alerts, however, will cost you, since it’s a Pro feature for both Wanderlog and TripIt.


**Flight information:** TripIt also includes a ton of extra information about your flight, including:


- Airport maps to help you find your way around
- Baggage claim information
- Check-in reminder
- Help finding alternative flights if your flight is delayed/canceled
- Tracking your carbon footprint for the flight


Wanderlog doesn’t include any of this information, so TripIt is the better option here.


**Fare tracker:** TripIt also offers Fare Tracker for its Pro members, which will alert you if the price of your flight dips below what you paid for it. This can allow you to rebook the flight at the cheaper price. Wanderlog offers a similar tool for hotel bookings, but not for flights.


**Winner, TripIt:** TripIt particularly shines with flight details, and offers way more features and options than Wanderlog.


## Reservation import and organization


One of the main reasons you’d want a travel organizer app is that they keep all of your reservation information (e.g., flights, hotels, etc.) in one place, with your confirmation numbers. Ever had to dig through your email at the airport to find your confirmation? This helps save you time in stressful moments.


In general, both apps let you:


- Forward a confirmation email, or
- Connect your Gmail inbox


And they’ll scan the emails for reservation information and automatically add them to the app to a “trip” (e.g., your “trip to San Francisco” might have your flight to San Francisco, your rental car, a hotel reservation, and your flight back).


Sometimes, though, the apps won’t be able to read the reservation, and you’ll have to manually enter the details into the app yourself.


Regardless, at the end of this, you should have all your reservations in one place in either the TripIt or Wanderlog app. Here’s how easy these tasks are with each app:


- **Forward email experience:** TripIt is the better experience here, as you can forward all your emails to one address,plans@trip.com . Wanderlog randomly generates email addresses for forwarded emails — like trips+153334@wanderlog.com — and gives you a different email address for each trip you plan.
- **Scan emails experience:** If you have a Gmail account, both apps can scan your account for confirmation emails without you having to forward anything. Wanderlog only offers this to Pro members; TripIt does it for everyone in the free version.
- **Manual entry experience:** Sometimes the apps won’t be able to scan and parse an email, so you’ll have to enter the information manually. With Wanderlog, you can add a flight with just the name of the airline and flight number, since you’ve already determined the dates in your trip plan. TripIt requires date, airline, and flight number, but also requests the confirmation number. It’s the same for manually adding reservations from hotels to tour tickets; Wanderlog’s process is slightly more streamlined than TripIt.


**Winner, TripIt:** TripIt has more streamlined experience, where you can forward all your emails to one simple address, while Wanderlog gives you different email addresses for each trip you plan.


## Itinerary builder


Both apps were built to keep a detailed itinerary of all your travel plans. We looked at how each app allows you to add events and how it displays those events in your itinerary.


### What is an itinerary?


For TripIt and Wanderlog, an itinerary refers to the day-by-day list of places you plan on visiting, including your hotel, airports, restaurants, and sightseeing spots. Here’s what your itinerary might look like on both TripIt and Wanderlog:


**Layout:** Both apps have a day-by-day layout that starts with the first day at the top, and then you can scroll through the days of your trip. Stops are also in chronological order, but each app allows you to change the order to your preference.


**Adding places:** Want to add a popular restaurant or TikTok-ready historical site to your plans? Both apps make it easy to add everything from museums to beaches to favorite food spots.


For Wanderlog, the easiest way to add a place is the Add a Place button, where you can search a specific name. For example, if you click on the Add a Place button in a specific day (say, January 23, 2025), you can search Golden Gate Park, and from the results, you just click the + button and Wanderlog adds it to your itinerary for that day.


Things you can add to your itinerary:


- Places to stay (hotels, Airbnbs, etc.)
- Attractions (museums, tours, historic buildings, etc.)
- Restaurants
- Transit information (flights, buses, trains, ferries, etc.)
- Notes, which allows you to add personalized notes for any item on your itinerary.


You can also add places from Wanderlog’s Explore tab, which gives recommendations of places to visit, best restaurants, etc. But more about that in a bit.


It’s a little more difficult on TripIt. If you want to add a place, you need to press the blue + button at the bottom, and it will give you a menu of options including flights, hotels, concerts, ferries, restaurants, etc. Once you pick a category, you have to manually add all the information about the place.


**Editing your plans:** With Wanderlog, you click on the three dots at the top of the day and click on reorder section just by dragging and dropping. For Tripit, you need to go into the event/stop and change the start time and end time to change the order.


**Winner, Wanderlog:** While both Wanderlog and TripIt have easy-to-follow timeline-type layouts for your travel plans, Wanderlog makes it easier to add and edit those plans.


## Mapping and directions


Getting around on your vacation is key. You need an app that allows you to navigate from your hotel to sightseeing spots, restaurants, and other activities and back without any issues.


TripIt isn’t really designed to map things (the itinerary view is its *only* view), while for Wanderlog, the map is front and center. If you’re mostly going to business meetings by Uber or taxi and taking flights, you probably don’t really need a map, but on a vacation, Wanderlog’s map is very helpful for seeing what attractions/restaurants are close to you.


**Mapping:** Wanderlog lays out your entire trip itinerary on a map, including airports, hotels, restaurants and sightseeing stops. Each stop has a pin and you can click on pins to get more information about each stop.


TripIt, on the other hand, has limited mapping capabilities; you can click on the Map View for your itinerary, and it will show you all of your places laid out on a map, but there is no filtering or additional details besides the pins in the map.


If you click on an address for a stop on your itinerary, it will take you to Google Maps, outside of the TripIt app.


**Map filters:** With Wanderlog, you can see your entire trip plan laid out on a map, color-coded by:


- Day, allowing you to focus on one day at a time, or
- Category, allowing you to filter by restaurants, museums, hotels, attractions, etc.


Since TripIt doesn’t really have a mapping tool, it doesn’t offer map filters like this.


**Directions:** TripIt allows you to see your itinerary stops on Google Maps, which can then give you turn-by-turn directions, but this all happens outside the TripIt app. Wanderlog has a directions button between stops that will take you to your choice of Google Maps, Apple Maps, or Waze, but Pro users can also export their daily itinerary to Google Maps, giving them even more detailed turn-by-turn directions.


**Optimize route:** Wanderlog has a Pro feature that allows you to optimize your route for the day. You pick the starting point (maybe a breakfast restaurant) and your ending point (most likely your hotel), and Wanderlog will find the quickest route between all your stops for the day, grouping stops that are close together.


**Winner: Wanderlog:** While TripIt does allow you to use Google Maps with its itinerary builder, constantly switching between the apps is a hassle. Wanderlog’s integrated mapping and filters make the app stand out, and the optimize route feature puts it even farther ahead of TripIt.


## Budget tools


For many, a vacation can be one of the biggest expenses of the year, so it helps to keep track of budgets and spending so we don’t blow past our estimates. It also helps a ton for group trips, where we often have to split hotels and restaurant bills.


If you want to make tracking your budget easier, try an app. I previously used Google Sheets and Splitwise, but Wanderlog and TripIt also both have budgeting and expense tracking features of their own too.


**Set and track budget:** TripIt has limited capabilities when it comes to budgeting. You can manually add costs to each event/place on your itinerary, but there is no central location for all your costs.


For that, you want Wanderlog; it lets you set a budget for yourself and then track all of your expenses, broken down into categories like flights, food, etc. You do have to manually add in your costs, but it’s an easier process than TripIt, letting you add expenses with just a few taps on your phone.


**Split bills:** Traveling in a group? Wanderlog will let you split expenses with everyone, and even let you customize splitting per expense. Did your friend Sara not go to brunch? Leave her out of the splitting. TripIt will allow you add costs to, say, a restaurant in your itinerary, but doesn’t have a good way to layout who paid what and how to split the bill.


**Budget data:** Wanderlog can also categorize your expenses and give you report of how much you’ve spent for food, flights, hotels, entertainment, etc.


**Winner, Wanderlog:** Wanderlog offers a comprehensive budgeting tool that not only lets you set and track you budget, but will also allow you to split bills among tripmates.


## Explore places to go


The best trip planning apps will give you recommendations on what to see and where to go on your trip.


**Nearby places:** TripIt will show you nearby places of interest that you can add to your itinerary, like entertainment, pharmacies, grocery stores, parks, etc. It is limited to the immediate area around where you are, so the recommendations can be limited.


Wanderlog can also recommend nearby places if you are viewing your itinerary in map form. The map will include smaller pins of interesting places nearby, which you can click on and then add to your itinerary with the Save button.


**Recommendations:** Not sure where to go on your trip? Wanderlog’s Explore tab has recommendations aggregated from the top review sites (TripAdvisor, Google) to help you find the coolest things to do on your trip. You can filter recommendations down to the most specific categories, like:


- Kid-friendly attractions
- Beaches
- Museums
- Cafes
- Photo spots
- Cheap eats
- Romantic places
- Nature


Look through the recommendations and add the places you want to your itinerary with the Add button in the right corner.


**Winner, Wanderlog:** Wanderlog offers an extensive, curated selection of activities, restaurants, tours, etc. that you can add to your trip with a tap on your phone.


## Group travel tools


If you are traveling with a group, keeping everyone on the same page is essential. Features like itineraries that the group can edit and share, budgeting tools that help you split bills, and shared maps to keep everyone on the same route can really take the stress out of a group trip.


**Share your trip plans:** Tripit allows you to share your travel itinerary with your friends and family. You can either invite others to track updates on your itinerary (more useful for group trips) or share a read-only copy of your plans so people will know where you’ll be at any given time on your trip.


Wanderlog also lets you share a view-only itinerary with friends and family, either via email, Twitter, Facebook or other social networks. You can also decide if you want your reservations or notes and checklists to be visible to your friends or not.


But Wanderlog also has great tools for planning a …


**Group trip:** If you are traveling with a group, this app can be a huge asset. You can invite all your travelers to your trip plan, and they will be able to edit and add things to the itinerary, so you can plan the trip together. You can also set a group budget, and split bills any way you need to.


Invite groupmates when you start planning a trip in Wanderlog (you just need their email addresses), or you can add them later on through the Share arrow at the top of your trip.


**Winner, Wanderlog:** Both Wanderlog and TripIt allow you to share your trip plan with friends and family, but only Wanderlog let’s you plan a group trip where your groupmates can edit and add to the trip plan.


## Costs


Most popular travel planning apps have a free version that works for your basic travel needs, including Wanderlog and TripIt. Cost becomes a factor when you want to use some of the premium features of the apps. Both apps have a Pro version that has a yearly cost attached to it.


### Wanderlog


Wanderlog has two plans, a free version and the Pro version, which costs $39.99/year. Here’s what you get with each version of the app:


**Free version features**


- Itinerary builder
- Color-coded map
- Budgeting tool
- Trip sharing/group travel
- Explore and recommendations
- Document organization
- Flight tracker
- Checklists
- Import hotel and flight information


**What’s free?** The main aspects of Wanderlog are available in the free version. If you are heading to a urban area (like New York City) and don’t need tools like route optimization, the free version will provide everything you need to keep your trip fully organized.


**Pro version features ($39.99/year)**


- Offline access
- AI assistant (You get 5 messages for free, then have to upgrade to Pro)
- Export to Google Maps
- Route optimizer
- Automatic Gmail scanner


**How necessary is the Pro version?** The most essential parts of Wanderlog are offered in the free version, including the itinerary builder, the color-coded maps, the budgeting tools, the curated recommendations, and the document organization. That said, the Pro version does offer some great features (offline access, unlimited AI assistance, route optimizer), but isn’t absolutely necessary for you to enjoy the planning aspects of the app.


### TripIt


TripIt also has a free version and a Pro version, which costs $49.99/year. Here are the features you get with each version:


**Free version features**


- Import hotel and flight information
- Syncs plans with your calendar
- Stores important travel information
- Helps you share plans with others
- Shows transportation options
- Finds places near your hotel
- Shows neighborhood safety scores
- Includes airports and terminals maps


**What’s free?** Most of what you need to organize your travel details is available with the free version of TripIt, including unlimited email scanning for reservations and confirmations, the itinerary builder, sharing plans with others, and syncing with your Google calendar.


**Pro version features ($49/year)**


- Real-time flight alerts
- Monitors for fare refunds
- Finds alternate flights
- Displays baggage claim info


**How necessary is the Pro version?** There are some basic features that TripIt only includes with a Pro version, like real-time flight alerts and baggage claim information, which you can do without, but are important features to have. Other Pro tools like airfare price drop alerts and finding alternative flights are nice to have, but are not essential.


**Winner, Wanderlog:** Wanderlog offers a more diverse group of features in its free version (budget tool, explore recommendations, mapping, etc.), and also has a lower price for Pro at $39.99/year, compared to TripIt’s $49/year for Pro.

## What is the difference between Wanderlog and TripIt?


The big difference between Wanderlog and TripIt is the features. TripIt is a streamlined way for you to organize your trip documents and itinerary, but Wanderlog gives you more planning capabilities, including a budget tool, an explore section that gives recommendations for your trip, and a color-coded map that displays your entire itinerary in a visual way.


If all you’re looking for is an organizer, TripIt is your app. If you want a more robust offering of features and tools for trip planning, Wanderlog is the right choice.


## What are the cons of Wanderlog?


User reviews frequently talk about being overwhelmed with all the features. The app does have a learning curve to it since there are so many optional features. Some users also complain about the speed of the app, but that really depends on your mobile service.


## Is Wanderlog still free?


Yes, Wanderlog is still free. You get most of the features for free, including:


- Itinerary builder
- Color-coded map
- Budgeting tool
- Trip sharing/group travel
- Explore and recommendations
- Document organization
- Flight tracker
- Checklists
- Import hotel and flight information


The Pro version ($39.99/year) offers some useful tools, like offline access to your trip plan, a route optimization tool, and unlimited access to Wanderlog’s AI assistant, but the most common features are all in the free version.


## What is the best travel app like TripIt?


I’m obviously partial to Wanderlog; it really does have everything you need for trip planning. You can keep all your documents and itinerary in one place, just like TripIt, but you also get a lot of other features, including recommendations, a budgeting tool, color-coded maps, flight alerts, AI assistant, and more.


Other apps like TripIt include Tripsy and Roadtrippers, although both have their limitations. Roadtrippers is specifically for road trips, so it’s difficult to use for any other trips. Tripsy is not as integrated as Wanderlog but does offer more features than TripIt


## Is it worth paying for Wanderlog?


It depends. If you are going to a remote location and need offline access to your maps, that feature is only offered with the Pro version. If you want to get the most done with your vacation days, Pro also allows you to optimize your route to get more fun stuff packed in. You can also export your itinerary to Google Maps with Pro. There are other features, but the bottomline is, if you really want any of these features, Pro is worth it.

## Overall winner in TripIt vs. Wanderlog


The more all-inclusive option: Wanderlog


Wanderlog stands out as the more comprehensive travel planning app, with essential features that TripIt doesn’t offer, including a budgeting tool, color-coded maps, curated recommendations, and group travel planning. Wanderlog is also $10 cheaper a year, making it the clear winner.


TripIt does a good job of organizing all of your trip reservations and details, and offers some perks that Wanderlog doesn’t (ie, more information about your flight, airfare price drop alerts), but these features are not enough compared to the toolbox that Wanderlog offers from planning trips.
