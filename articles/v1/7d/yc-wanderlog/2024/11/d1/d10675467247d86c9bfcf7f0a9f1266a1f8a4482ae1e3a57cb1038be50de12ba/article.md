# Tips for Backpacking through Europe


November 7, 2024


6 min read


Backpacking through Europe can be quite an exciting adventure, as you can create countless memories that last a lifetime. The continent’s rich landscape blend, vibrant cities, and natural wonders galore make it an exciting option for travelers of every age, and[its low-cost opportunities](https://www.statista.com/topics/11264/backpacking-in-europe/) make it even more convenient.


I personally love the intricacies of European architecture and how so many cities have that “Old Town” vibe that virtually takes you back in time.


That said, while backpacking through Europe is a great way to travel, careful planning is essential to avoid stress. Budgeting is important to consider, and suitable accommodation can make a difference between a good trip and a bad one. For the most part, here are some things to keep in mind while planning your camping trips:


**Things to Do** **Things to Avoid**


Prepare your accommodation spots beforehand. Packing more than you can comfortably carry in a backpack.


Plan your route around festivals and other events. Not creating a backup plan for missed transportation or unsuitable accommodation.


Pick the season based on your preferred activities. Not setting up a route for countries, cities, and attractions to visit.


Look for freebies and free tours. Overcrowding your itinerary with too many activities per day.


Book your means of transportation ahead of time. Not packing the right gear based on the activities or season.


Creating the right backpacking strategy can help you make the most of your trip. In this article, you will learn how to do that.


## Top Things to Consider Doing


Nowadays, travelers are less focused on saving time and more on enjoying the moment. Still, carefully planning your trip as you backpack through Europe can save you the trouble. Here are some tips to keep in mind:


### Prepare Accommodation Beforehand


You may be backpacking through Europe, but you’ll still have to sleep at some point. Europe has[occupancy rates of about 50%](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/statistics-explained/index.php?title=Tourism_statistics_-_annual_results_for_the_accommodation_sector) depending on where you are going, and the chances of finding a place get significantly lower if you book the accommodation on the day you get there.


#### Book at the Right Moment ******


Prices always get higher for last-minute bookings because that’s when people are most desperate – and obviously, hotel owners know that. The earlier you book your accommodation, the higher your chances of getting a good price will be.


Keep in mind that the opposite might happen, too. For instance, if a place has too many empty spots, hostels might lower the price to get them sold. It’s riskier, but you can get a reasonable price if you’re lucky. Just look at this hostel in Athens, for example, with one night set in November and the other in March 2025.

#### **Consider Camping**


Camping is also an option for more adventurous travelers. The average price of campsites in Europe is around 30 EUR per night, with the prices getting even lower if you’re not traveling in the peak season. Some ideas for campsite prices include:


- Albania: ~$13
- Romania: ~$16
- Belgium: ~$23
- Iceland: ~$24
- Croatia: ~$38
- Italy: ~$40


I would often choose to stay at a campsite for 2-3 days while backpacking, then stay at a hostel for a day or two just to relish the feeling of a real bed. Since I’m switching accommodations quite often, I use[Wanderlog](https://wanderlog.com/) to keep track of all the places where I plan my stay.


Booking packages can also be helpful, as they include everything you need, from guided tours to hotel stays and meals. Most of them run around $750, meaning you’ll be paying around $100 daily.


[Tours4fun](https://www.tours4fun.com/popular-p/pre-planned-backpacking-trips-europe/multiday-tours/)


### Plan the Trips Around Events


Many of us backpack through Europe to enjoy authentic cultural experiences, and I found there’s no better way to do that than joining a festival. This gives you a front-row seat to unique opportunities you would not have been able to catch at other times. It maximizes the excitement, as you have multiple activities to enjoy.


Most festivals occur in summer, especially if you plan to attend a music festival. Some good ideas are the following:


- Sziget, Budapest
- Roskilde, Denmark
- Tomorrowland, Belgium
- Wacken Open Air, Germany


Chances are, you can also secure a camping place there, so that’s one less issue to worry about. That said, you can attend off-summer festivals such as the popular Oktoberfest in Munich or the Christmas Markets. The latter[stretch all over Europe](https://wanderlog.com/blog/2024/10/21/the-cheapest-european-countries-to-travel-to/) , and those in Vienna, Strasbourg or Colmar are always a delight.


### Pick the Season Wisely


Now, choosing the right season for backpacking is also quite important. Most first-time backpackers often believe that summer is the best season to travel, but that mainly depends on your goals.


- If you want to travel mostly by foot, go to the beach, and stay at the camping sites, then indeed, the perfect season is between April and September (with June to August being the most crowded season)
- December to February is the perfect season if you want to go skiing and visit the Christmas markets. Remember that the temperatures can get cold, so you might want to pack warm clothes and book your stays at hostels or hotels. The good news is that it’ll likely be less crowded, as around[52% of people](https://www.bankrate.com/personal-finance/home-for-the-holidays/) choose to stay home for the holidays.


### Look for Free Opportunities


Who doesn’t love[a good freebie](https://wanderlog.com/blog/2024/10/14/is-there-a-free-trip-planner/) ? I know I do. Fortunately, backpacking through Europe **** brings many of these opportunities, and you can enjoy traveling without breaking the bank. You must catch these opportunities at the right moment because they are limited by a timeline or the spots get filled out quickly.


[Free Walking Tour Prague](https://freewalkingtourprague.eu/)


Here are some freebies you can take advantage of:


- Prague, for instance, gives you plenty of opportunities for free walking tours, provided you sign up on time.
- Certain museums also offer free passes during certain days of the week or month, so you may take advantage of that.
- Another good idea would be to use the accommodation’s kitchen (if there is one) to cook your own meals, which can save precious money in the long run.


### Arrange for Transportation Beforehand


You may be backpacking, but chances are, you won’t be walking the entire distance. At some point, you’ll have to take some form of transportation.


- If you are traveling long-distance, flying is generally the best choice.
- Trains and buses are excellent for city-to-city transit, and you can easily use apps such as Wanderlog to keep track of your transportation.
- If you are planning to go through rural areas or places where public transit is less frequent, I suggest you rent a car instead. For example, renting a car in Prague is around $60-$70 per day in November, and you can either bring it back where you got it from or choose a different drop-off location.

### Top Mistakes to Avoid


While there are some tips you should follow, there are also some things you should *not* do. When thinking how to backpack through Europe smoothly, you should avoid the following pitfalls:


### Overpacking


Overpacking can lead to great inconveniences, and it’s not just about the weight of the luggage. Aside from the difficulty in hauling the baggage with you, it could also lead to extra costs such as airport fees and more.


Plus, it can lead to plenty of missed opportunities, like hiking, because, let’s face it, who wants to walk so much with tons of clothes and items weighing them down?


Some good tips here would be the following:


- Go for clothes that can be layered, so you’ll never be too hot or too cold.
- Shoes should also be kept at a minimum; a pair of walking shoes and a dressier one should be just enough.
- Use Wanderlog to create a packing checklist and pin the areas with laundry facilities to make things easier.


This way, it’s easier to stay organized.


### Not Having a Backup Method


You may be planning everything to the last detail, but occasionally, things might go wrong. Your plane may be late, causing you to mess up your schedule.


Trains in Europe are also[notorious for being late](https://www.express.co.uk/news/world/1900680/travel-train-delays-europe-worst-countries-list) , and I even missed my connection once despite leaving (supposedly) enough time between them. This is why I recommend always having a backup plan.


While you don’t need to book the backup tickets, you should know how to get them. Pin the bus stations in your[travel app](https://wanderlog.com/blog/2024/09/02/best-travel-planner-apps-for-each-type-of-traveler/) , putting down potential hours in case you miss your original transportation.


If there are any issues with the accommodation, you should research some hostels in the area that frequently have empty rooms so that you are not left stranded.


### Not Creating a Route


Some backpackers like to travel without a destination in mind, but when you have a set route, it’s easier to make the most of all the notable sights to see.


I once went backpacking from Prague to Vienna without a route in mind, and half of the time was spent figuring out where to go and where to stop. I spent minutes browsing Google Maps in the middle of the street, only to discover that the best places were too far for our comfort.


Creating a route saves time and allows you to book ahead. For instance, if you have specific places where you want to eat or enjoy a drink, you can call ahead and book a table.


### Overcrowding Your Itinerary


When backpacking through Europe, you should see everything out there. However, this can easily lead to burnout, making you feel way too tired to enjoy the place you are visiting. Not to mention that you may need more time than you expected, causing you to miss out on other activities you probably looked forward to.


Here is my advice:


- Limit yourself to two major activities per day and keep the rest for spontaneity.
- I always list a few “maybes” in my Wanderlog itinerary, which I check out once I finish my main must-sees.
- If you really want to cover multiple sites, I recommend going for guided tours.


### Not Packing the Right Gear


Climate change has[created a lot of havoc](https://eu.usatoday.com/story/news/nation/2023/12/12/climate-change-effects-in-winter/71670349007/) over the past couple of years, and Europe is no exception. Plus, the temperatures are different, depending on the region.


Take Romania, for example: winter in Constanța can reach as low as 42 degrees Fahrenheit, whereas Miercurea Ciuc’s temperature hits around 32 in January.


Thus, it’s essential to pack with adaptability and your route in mind.


A good idea is to consider your activities when packing:


- Bring your swim gear if you are hitting beach areas
- Go for thermal layers if you love winter sports
- Don’t forget to bring your waterproof jacket, as the weather is unpredictable
- Comfortable shoes are also a must since you’ll likely walk a lot


## Conclusion


Backpacking through Europe can prove to be an unforgettable experience, and if you plan right, you can make the most of it. By creating your travel plan ahead, you should be able to see everything on the list while creating beautiful memories and staying away from unpleasant surprises.
