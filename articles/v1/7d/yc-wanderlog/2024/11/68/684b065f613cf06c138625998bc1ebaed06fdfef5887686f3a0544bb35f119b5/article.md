# When Is the Best Time to Cruise to Alaska?


November 13, 2024


7 min read


In September, I checked off a bucket list item when I went on an Alaskan Cruise with my husband and two other couples. In all, it was a fabulous trip that included breathtaking views, breaching whales, scenic hikes, and plenty of laughs with good friends. We planned the trip about a year and a half in advance. Early in our planning stage, all six of us debated about the best time of year to take our cruise.


We settled on September for a few reasons. First, it didn’t interfere with move-in time for our college student children. Second, since schools were back in session at that time, we hoped there would be fewer kids on our cruise. Lastly, we hoped to maximize our chance of seeing the Northern Lights. While we were all able to get our students moved back to campus before our cruise, there were still quite a few kids on our cruise, and we didn’t see the Northern Lights … but more on that later.


### Best time to cruise to Alaska


**Summer (high prices, more to do):** If you don’t mind paying a little more, summer offers a wide variety of things to do and see in Alaska, from bigger waterfalls to whales and bears to the beautiful flora of Alaska. It’s also the dry season, so the chance of rain ruining your trip is minimal.


**Fall (lower prices, fewer crowds):** If you are looking for a deal on your trip with fewer crowds, fall is the off-season for Alaskan cruises, which means you can take advantage of cheaper prices and fewer crowds. You also have a better chance of seeing the Northern Lights.


## The Alaskan Cruise Season


Before we can get into the best time to take your Alaskan cruise, we need to understand how cruising to Alaska differs from cruising to a Caribbean island. In Alaska, there is a “Cruise Ship Season,” which runs from mid-May through mid-September. If you are envisioning a Christmastime cruise to Alaska, you are out of luck. The weather conditions for half of the year make it a safety issue for cruise ships to sail to Alaska. I’m sure you are picturing massive snowfall and frozen harbors, but cruisers should worry more about rough seas and huge waves than ice and blizzards …but more on that later, as well.


Peak season for an Alaskan cruise is relatively short. June and July are the most sought-after months for many travelers because they are the warmest months and have optimal daylight.


May, August, and September are considered shoulder seasons for Alaskan cruisers. During these times, cruisers may be able to find better cruise ship deals, more cabins available, and more shore excursion openings. As the crowd thins out, tourists can get discounts.


## Factors to Consider


Picking the best time to embark on your Alaskan cruise really depends on what you hope to do and see. You need to take the weather, wildlife, and nature’s oddities into consideration, balancing your expectations with the realities of the Last Frontier. Here are some factors you may want to consider:


- Weather
- Wildlife
- Crowds
- Costs and Availability
- Northern Lights
- Flowers and Foliage
- Daylight Hours
- Availability of Attractions


## Weather


When I told friends and family that I had booked a cruise to Alaska, many of them questioned why I would want to vacation in the cold and snow. They had images of the snowy tundra of the North Pole or scenes from Disney’s *Frozen* in their heads. What they didn’t understand was that the Alaskan Current brings warm ocean water to coastal areas of Alaska. It is not as frigid and brutal as one might think.


### What’s the Weather Like in Juneau?


Let’s look at Juneau, Alaska’s capital city, for example. Juneau was a port of call on my cruise ship, so I spent a day there in mid-September. I wore a sweatshirt and was fine. According to the National Weather Service, Juneau typically sees its first snowfall in mid-December, although in recent years, that has been pushed back to January.


January is the coldest and snowiest month of the year. Temperatures average in the upper 20s and 30s, and the average snowfall is about 95 inches per year. For reference, I live in Michigan. The average annual snowfall is between 100 and 125 inches, and the typical temperature in January is around 19 degrees Fahrenheit.


### What Was My Experience?


When I was in Juneau, it was 59 degrees. We definitely didn’t need thick, heavy winter coats. We did, however, need raincoats and umbrellas.


It rained on and off every day of my Alaskan cruise. Sometimes, it was just a constant drizzle, but at other times, it poured. Every Alaskan resident I spoke to joked, “That’s typical Alaskan weather.” That wasn’t in the brochure! I packed rain gear, so I was prepared. And I wasn’t going to let a little rain get in the way of my full Alaskan experience.


I learned that late summer, fall, and early winter are the rainy seasons in Alaska, while late spring and summer are the dry seasons. If you want to avoid the rain, I suggest you take your cruise earlier in the summer.


### Average Temperature and Precipitation in Juneau


- May 49ºF/3.40-inches
- June 55ºF/3.24-inches
- July 57ºF/4.60-inches
- August 56ºF/5.76-inches
- September 50ºF/8.65-inches


## Wildlife


The wildlife in Alaska is one of the state’s biggest attractions. On your cruise ship, you can see:


- Whales
- Dolphins
- Sea otters
- Sharks
- Eagles
- Puffins


Onshore – or when your cruise ship is close to shore – you may see:


- Bears
- Moose
- Deer
- Caribou
- Dall Sheep
- Wolves
- Mountain Goats


Lucky for you whale season in Alaska coincides with cruise ship season. Your chances of seeing whales are rather high. From the balcony of our cruise ship, I saw numerous whales, along with a few sharks, and tons of dolphins.


Our shore excursion in Sitka was billed as a “Sea Otter Harbor Boat Tour,” but we saw more whales than sea otters. A couple of humpback whales put on quite a show for us, breeching and rolling and slapping their fins on the water.


### Watching Whales and Rocking the Boat


I promised to circle back to the weather and rough seas, so now is the time. There was constant rain during the harbor tour. The boat had an enclosed lower deck for people who wanted to stay dry, but I chose to spend the two-hour tour on the upper deck, where the views were better, albeit wetter.


Those frolicking humpbacks I mentioned were, unfortunately, playing just outside the relative safety of the harbor, but our captain took us to see them anyway. Not only did we have the steady rain to deal with, but the swells were much larger. The boat rocked on the waves so violently that people were literally thrown about if they weren’t hanging onto something. It was rough! I got off the boat at the end of the tour soaking wet (not cold, though), slightly seasick, and with a hyper-extended knee, but with a camera roll filled with incredible photos and plenty of memories of those majestic humpbacks.


### Bear Season in Alaska


If you are hoping to see bears during your Alaskan adventure, plan to visit during the summer months … during peak tourist season. That’s when the bears are most active. June and July are the best time to fish for red salmon, too. You’ll find salmon even if you plan your cruise for August or September. During those months, silver salmon run.


## Crowds


June, July, and early August are the busiest tourist times in Alaska. If you are cruising during this time, expect lots of people. This is especially true at the ports of call. My cruise ship stopped in:


- Sitka
- Skagway
- Juneau


Both Sitka and Skagway are small towns, and even though Juneau is the capital of Alaska, it is not a large city. When a couple of thousand people step off a cruise ship, these places are inundated with tourists. I got to give them props … They handle it well.


Here’s what you should realistically expect:


- Every gift shop and museum to be packed with people.
- Lines at every attraction.
- Tourists in all your photos.
- Waits at every bar, restaurant, café, and brewery


I went on my trip during shoulder season, and I found the crowds to be overwhelming at times. In Juneau, for example, there were five cruise ships in port at the same time. It was impossible to walk through a souvenir shop, and we were disappointed that the wait at the famous Tracy’s King Crab Shack was too long.


While shoulder season is definitely less crowded than peak season, my best advice would be to temper your expectations. There will be lots of people everywhere you go. Just accept it.


## Cost and Cabin Availability


The cost for an Alaskan cruise is highest during June and July. You may be able to save as much as 20% by booking a cruise during shoulder season. We booked our cruise about 18 months ahead of time, so we had plenty of cabins to pick from. If you wait until closer to your desired departure, especially in mid-summer, you may not find an open cabin, or you may have to settle for an interior room.


I discovered that the cruise line was more willing to offer discounts on “extras” during off-peak cruise times. We got a percentage off the drink package, two of our shore excursions, and the cool souvenir fleece blanket.


At some of the shops at our ports of call, most items – sweatshirts, souvenir mugs, trinkets, and tchotchkes – were discounted. Since these stores are so closely tied to the cruise ship industry, their sales drastically drop when the cruise ships stop coming. I was there about two weeks before the end of the year’s cruise ship season, and shop owners were motivated to move their merchandise.


## Northern Lights


One thing we all were looking forward to on our Alaskan cruise was the opportunity to see the Northern Lights. In fact, that is one of the reasons we chose to go in mid-September. We had read that the Auroras are most prevalent from mid-September to early May. If the Northern Lights made an appearance during my cruise, we couldn’t see them through all the clouds and rain. Ironically, about two weeks after I got home from Alaska, I saw a spectacular display of the Northern Lights in my own backyard.


## Flowers and Foliage


Prior to my cruise, I imagined we would see brilliant fall foliage in Alaska. What I didn’t realize is that most of the trees in Alaska – at least, where I was – are evergreen trees. There are a few deciduous trees, but there are far more pines. Rather than vibrant oranges, yellows, and reds, I saw fifty shades of green.


In the spring and early summer, the wildflowers in Alaska can transform the scenery. The alpine forget-me-knot, Alaska’s state flower, blooms in June.


- Purple Mountain Saxifrage – Early to Mid-June
- Forget-Me-Knot – Early to Mid-June
- Mountain Avens – Early to Mid-June
- Wild Geranium – Mid- to Late-June
- Wild Roses – Mid- to Late-June
- Western Columbine – Mid- to Late-June
- Coastal Paintbrush – Mid- to Late-June
- Dwarf River Beauty – July
- Fireweed – Late July through mid-August
- Wild Larkspur – July to August
- Wild Goldenrod – August to early September


## Daylight Hours


Alaska is ruled by the Midnight Sun and Polar Night, especially the further north you go. Thanks to its proximity to the North Pole and the tilt of the Earth’s axis, the sun does not fully set on some parts of Alaska during the Midnight Sun season, from April 22 to August 20. It hovers just above the horizon, extending daylight hours.


During the Summer Solstice, which typically falls on June 20, places like Juneau and Skagway can experience 19 hours of daylight and just five hours of night. If you plan your Alaskan cruise to align with the Midnight Sun, you can keep the party going all night long!


## Availability of Attractions


Before you book your Alaskan cruise during shoulder season, do your homework. It is possible that some of the attractions on your Alaskan bucket list will be closed for the season.


- Denali – Mid-May to Mid-September
- White Pass & Yukon Scenic Railway – Mid-May to Mid-September
- Dog Sledding – Offered in the summer months, but typically on rollers rather than snow
- Fortress of the Bears Kayak Adventure – Mid-June to the end of August
- Fortress of the Bear in Sitka –Open only on weekends at the beginning and end of cruise ship season


Do your research to find out what seasonal attractions and activities will be open during your visit to avoid being disappointed.
