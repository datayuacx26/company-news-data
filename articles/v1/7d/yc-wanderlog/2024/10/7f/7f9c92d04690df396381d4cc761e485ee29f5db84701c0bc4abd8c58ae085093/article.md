# 7 Tips on How to Find Cheap Flights


October 16, 2024


6 min read


With airline ticket prices increasing by 25 percent in the past few years,[finding tickets for cheap flights](https://www.cnbc.com/select/airline-ticket-prices-are-up-25-percent-why-and-how-to-save/) is not only a matter of comfort but a necessity. I’ve gone on trips where I spent more on the flight itself than on everything else, causing me to drastically cut my budget. Things got even more frustrating when I checked the same route a few days later, only to find the ticket price dramatically lowered.


Airlines can be rather unpredictable regarding ticket prices, so you need to develop a good eye for browsing through them. Overall, here’s a checklist that I found can help you find lower prices:


- Consider taking an early flight
- Fly on Tuesday, Wednesday, or Saturday
- Consider being flexible with your time and destination
- Decide on how flexible your travel plans are
- Book ahead for fixed flights. Book last minute for flexible options
- Be careful about the hidden fees of discount airlines
- Use travel credit cards and airline miles to save money


Research is key when it comes to finding an affordable ticket. For this reason, I put together a guide with some tips and tricks on how to find cheap flights.


### 1. What Is the Best Time to Book a Flight?


There’s a myth going around that says booking a flight for 6 a.m. can get you a[lower plane ticket price](https://www.cntraveler.com/stories/2015-03-10/the-best-time-to-buy-an-airplane-ticket-and-when-we-actually-buy) . Sometimes, that’s true because no one really wants to get up so early to catch their flight. After all, you must commute and go through security, which is why most people prefer the night flight. The night flight also gets them to their destination in the morning for overnight[flights](https://wanderlog.com/blog/2024/09/26/10-tips-for-traveling-internationally/) , making it the most travel-friendly — and more expensive — choice.


The price during the time of day usually changes based on demand or whether or not there’s an incoming big event somewhere. The less interest someone shows in a flight, the lower the prices. This is why, rather than just “going with the flow,” you should look for times when people don’t travel that much.


### 2. What Is the Cheapest Day to Fly?


You might think it’s a good idea to fly out on Friday afternoon to enjoy a nice city break, but here’s the problem — other people think the same. Most people have Monday through Friday work schedules, so they only book their trips on Thursday and Friday to return on Sunday or Monday. Because of that, those days are likely the most expensive ones to travel.


You can find cheaper flights on Tuesdays and Wednesdays because that’s when most people are knee-deep at work. For instance, a flight from Rochester, NY, to Toronto can be $403 on a Tuesday and $501 on a Sunday.

Similarly, Saturday is just as affordable because let’s face it – who wants to leave on the first day of the weekend? You miss out on all the fun.


However, this pattern can change during the holidays as people have more time on their hands.[Ticket flight prices](https://www.foxbusiness.com/lifestyle/holiday-flight-prices-how-much-it-will-cost-you-book-ticket) can get higher by 9 percent around the holidays, and even more, if you choose a rush-hour time. If you have no option but to travel during that time, set your flight at least two weeks before the holiday rush kicks in. Unless you are traveling to a warm destination during Christmas (when most people want snow), you should[plan for tickets](https://www.washingtonpost.com/travel/tips/cheap-airfare-christmas-flights-google/) ahead.


### 3. Does Being Flexible Lower Airfares?


Airlines charge their prices based on how desperate individuals are to get from one point to another. The more they see people wanting to go somewhere, the more they hike up the prices. For example, I saw many passengers traveling to Greece last year, and airline companies obviously caught on to that. In the end,[flight fees increased by 30 percent](https://www.theguardian.com/money/2023/jan/19/package-holidays-flights-30-per-cent-more-expensive-last-year) simply because everyone wanted to get there.

The sudden popularity of Greece meant that other countries were ignored, so to entice travelers, airlines offered lower prices to those destinations. As a tip, you might want to learn how to use Google Flights to find cheap flights. For instance, the Google Flights map shows that getting from New York to Athens costs around $558, whereas flying to Budapest costs $395. The distance is similar, but since Budapest is less traveled, the ticket price is much cheaper.


Flexibility can also help you save some cash if you are not picky about the time of day or week. If you don’t have any specific appointments, consider booking your trip on a less traveled day. Look at the airlines’ offerings, see which day is cheaper, and book based on that. You might also want to compare prices between airports, as flying from a more distant airport could sometimes be more affordable.


### 4. Is It Better to Book Flights Early or At the Last Minute?


When booking their flight, I’ve seen many people go through the following debate: should I book early or leave it to the last minute? Both options have their advantages, depending on your plans for the trip.


For example, if you have a specific place in mind, then you might want to book as early as possible. The timing usually differs depending on the type of flight, along with the season. Here are some guidelines that could help you:


- **Domestic flights and low season:** 1-3 months in advance
- **International flights:** 2-8 months in advance
- **High (peak) season:** 6-12 months in advance


My advice is that you shouldn’t try to book *too* early, especially when it comes to domestic flights. I once made the mistake of booking six months ahead, before they released the discounted seats, and realized I could have gotten a much better price if I waited. My luck was that I booked through a site that allowed for rebooking, so I was eventually successful in matching the lowest price.


If you aren’t picky about your destination or travel dates, booking last minute could also bring cheaper ticket prices. Airlines will always try their best to ensure a plane is as full as possible, so if a certain flight has few passengers, they’ll try to entice air travelers with cheaper tickets. That’s when you might be able to score a better price.


### 5. Are Discount Airlines Cheaper?


Discount airlines such as Ryanair and EasyJet are often the first options we consider when trying to book a cheap ticket, but there are a few catches. With this type of service, you only pay for the flight, so it might work well for you if you only need a small bag and don’t need food or drinks. However, depending on the airline, you might end up with other hidden fees that boost the price.


For instance, Spirit Airlines might let you carry a small handbag for free, but if you have a bigger carry-on, the[airline charges a fee](https://www.forbes.com/advisor/credit-cards/travel-rewards/spirit-airlines-baggage-fees/) . You could end up paying $200 for a ticket to Europe, only to realize that you also have to pay extra for:


- **Carry-on:** An average of $55
- **Checked bags:** $50 or more
- **Seat selection:** An average of $10 or more, depending on the seat
- **Food and drinks:** An average of $15 for a snack box and a bottle of water


Depending on the company you are traveling with, you could end up paying more on a discount than you’d pay for a full-service carrier. It’s always a good idea to[make a checklist](https://wanderlog.com/blog/2024/10/03/the-ultimate-packing-list-for-any-type-of-trip/) of what you’ll have to bring with you to determine how much you’ll be charged. Wanderlog also helped me in that regard, as it allowed me to add notes to the trip and create a packing list to keep track of every item. This way, I didn’t accidentally pack too much.


### 6. Does a Package Trip Lower the Cost?


Package trips can also be a good alternative if you want to save money on your whole trip. With these offers, travel agencies bundle different travel aspects (i.e., your flight, accommodations, rental car, activities, etc.) under one ticket. These trips often have discounted rates compared to direct booking, so even if you pay more upfront, you can return with more money in your pocket.


It’s always a good idea to create a draft itinerary and compare prices, determining whether or not it’s a good deal. Use apps like Wanderlog to compare hotel prices, linking them to the travel organizer within the tool. Usually, package trips offer you a better price, but it’s never a bad idea to compare first.


For example, a one-week trip from New York to Budapest between November 11-17 would cost you:


- **Flight (round trip):** $525
- **Accommodation close to the city center:** $554
- **Car Rental:** ~ $260 for a small manual car
- **Total:** ~$1339
- **Package:** $1,000. (The same trip and time in a package through Expedia)

### 7. Can Travel Credit Cards Help Save Money on Flights?


Another tip for getting cheap flights is using the same airline and racking up miles. For example, you can get an airline-branded card if you always fly with a certain company. This might be convenient for you if you live in a hub such as Atlanta, where you may easily get a Delta card since Delta is the biggest airline there.


You can also get a non-branded travel credit card like American Express (Amex) or Capital One Venture. These are usually much more convenient when you’re not near an airline hub, as they don’t tie you to any one company. Mile redemption rates are often lower than airline-branded credit cards, but if you fly a lot, you can still earn enough points to lower ticket prices.
