# Top Winter Travel Ideas for the Ultimate Vacation


October 25, 2024


7 min read


Winter is one of the best times to go on a vacation, with travel trends[increasing by 15 percent](https://traveldataanalytics.de/en/growth-of-winter-season-is-obvious/) once the cold season hits. I find that winter is great for trips, mainly because I get to experience each place’s customs in full season. I also love that you can choose the climate based on what you feel like doing, whether you are a snow or sunshine type of person.


The right place for a winter vacation mainly depends on your goal for that trip. Some like being surrounded by snow and opportunities for winter sports, especially if their city doesn’t offer these activities. Some go on winter vacations to see the aurora. Others just want to relax on a warm beach and forget that their hometown is in the middle of a frost spell. There are so many places to visit that I decided to make things easier for you and create a list with some winter travel ideas.

## Travel Ideas for Winter in the US


Some people, especially those in the US, like to keep their travels close to home. You don’t need a visa, it can be cheap, and you spend less time on the road. Here are my top ideas:


1. **Best for Winter Sports: Lake Placid, NY**


Lake Placid is a great place for winter sports enthusiasts, and considering that it hosted the Winter Olympics twice, it’s no wonder. The area is perfect for ice skating, skiing, and sledding. I find Whiteface Mountain a particularly good place since it has beginner and advanced slopes. You also have the holiday markets, art galleries, and horse-drawn sleighs to enjoy.


Many accommodation options, including Whiteface Lodge and the Mirror Lake Inn, put you near the slopes. Keep in mind that the places get booked quickly in winter, so you may want to plan early. I found suitable accommodation and created an itinerary through the Wanderlog app, which ensured I didn’t miss anything.


1. **Best for Ski Trips: Big Sky, MT**


Montana is clad with snow during winter, and if you are a fan of skiing, Big Sky is where everything’s at. You have 5,850 acres of skiable terrain, with sections for beginners and advanced skiers. I loved that you had access to a modern lift, which brought you quickly to the top of the slope.


You can try staying at the Big Sky Resort, but if you want something more affordable, the Whitewater Inn or The Lodge are some of the best options. You can enjoy various activities there, whether it’s snowshoeing, going on a snowmobile tour, or relaxing at the spa.


1. **Best for Aurora Chasing: Fairbanks, Alaska**


Auroras are majestic, but there is only a[5 percent chance](https://www.washingtonpost.com/weather/2024/05/14/northern-lights-aurora-next-extreme/) you will see one in any given year. This is why so many people go to Fairbanks, as its location underneath the Auroral Oval makes it a prime spot. Skies are generally clear between late August and April, so there’s a good chance you’ll catch one in the evening.


Aside from chasing auroras, there are plenty of other things to enjoy there, such as:


- Dog sledding
- Wildlife viewing (I saw plenty of reindeer and moose at the Large Animal Research Station)
- Museum hopping


Keep in mind that it can be easy to lose track of time, so if you want to catch the aurora in time, you should log everything in your Wanderlog app.


1. **Best for a European-like Getaway: Vail, CO**


I love European-inspired architecture, but I don’t always feel like going all the way to Europe for that. Cumulated with its friendly atmosphere, this is why Vail made one of the best travel ideas for families in winter. **** The entire town was made after a European ski village, with the Tyrolean-style architecture making it a picturesque place to stay. You also have plenty of charming boutiques and cozy pubs to visit, making it quite relaxing.


The massive skiable terrain spans over 5,317 acres, giving you opportunities for numerous winter sports such as snowshoeing, skiing, or riding a snowmobile. Since it gets some 254 inches of snow per season, you won’t have to worry about slopes being closed. Even if you are not in the mood for winter sports, you can easily hit the film festivals or spas.


1. **Best for Holiday Vibes: New York City, NY**


No other city offers the Christmas vibe like New York does; *Home Alone* ingrained that in our brains. The city is, without a doubt, great for winter escapes due to its festive decorations and Christmas events. Rockefeller Center always has a giant Christmas tree put up, the ice rink is magical, and the holiday markets are packed with unique gifts.


It gets enough snow, but compared to other cities, it’s still a couple of degrees warmer, with the average going between 27 and 40 degrees Fahrenheit. You’ll still want to be stylish there, and some travel outfit ideas for winter **** could include knitwear, a chic scarf, and a long, fashionable coat.


## Travel Ideas Outside the US


Now and again, I may feel like leaving the US just for a change of scenery. Currency conversion could also help me save a couple of bucks, and these are some places that are perfect for traveling.


1. **Best for Historical Sights: Krakow, Poland**


Krakow dates back thousands of years, and its medieval charm makes it obvious why it was once the royal capital of Poland. From the Wawel Castle to the Jewish Quarter and Auschwitz-Birkenau, you have plenty of stunning sights to see.


Accommodation, dining, and entry fees are relatively cheap, and you can easily find a place to stay under $100. The Krakow Christmas markets, holiday parades, and cultural events are also a sight to see, keeping you entertained at all times.


Via Booking.com


1. **Best for Christmas Culture: Rovaniemi, Finland**


Known as the home of Santa Claus, this is where all the holiday cheer is. The best part of Rovaniemi, Finland, is that you can visit Santa at any time of the year at Santa Village. If you’re there during Christmas, you can also enjoy the festive meals and fabulous holiday markets.


Aside from the Santa stuff, you can shop for crafts, go on snowmobile tours, or perhaps visit the Arktikum Museum for cultural insights. There’s a good chance you’ll be able to admire the Northern Lights if you go there during winter.


1. **Best for Winter Kayaking: Tromsø, Norway**


Many people go to Tromsø for an opportunity to kayak on the icy waters among the Norwegian Fjords. The breathtaking scenery also makes it perfect for wildlife viewing and dog sledding. If you are in the mood for warmth, you could also hop through the local museums and art galleries.


Via Tripadvisor


There are plenty of guided tours, most of which cost around $200 and are led by experienced locals. Since the town is well-connected through a public transport system, you can easily get from one place to another by bus or even walking.


1. **Best for Northern Lights: Akureyri, Iceland**


While Norway and Finland are fantastic places to see the Northern Lights, there’s no place like Akureyri, Iceland, where you can see them in full force. The city features optimal viewing conditions and clear skies, giving you some stunning views if you are in a good spot. For the best experience, I recommend you visit between September and April when the nights get longer.


Aside from that, you can spend your time visiting the botanical garden or soaking in the geothermal baths. The Myvatn Nature Baths is a good idea, as the sights there are breathtaking. Temperatures are often around 14 and 28 degrees Fahrenheit, so[pack thermal clothing](https://wanderlog.com/blog/2024/10/24/what-is-the-best-app-that-helps-you-pack-for-a-trip/) .


## Travel Ideas for a Sunny Winter


When winters get cold, I find it appealing to go to a warmer place and catch some sun. I don’t have to pack as many thick clothes, and the change of scenery gives me a break from the Christmas craze. Below are some travel ideas for families in winter **** (and even couples or singles) that can help you truly relax.


1. **Best for Adventures: Cape Town, South Africa**


If you are feeling adventurous, Cape Town is the perfect place for you. Its dynamic landscape is packed with coastlines, beautiful markets, and vineyards, which you can explore to your heart’s content. Whether you want to hike the Table Mountain trails or cage dive with the sharks, there are plenty of ways to stay entertained.


Via Tripadvisor


Temperatures can go as high as 65 degrees Fahrenheit during winter, so while it may be warm, you should still have some sweaters or light jackets for the evening. You have public taxis and buses to get around, but to save time, you can opt for guided tours for around $100.


1. **Best for Beach Relaxation: Maui, Hawaii**


Those seeking beach time should consider Maui for their winter vacation, as temperatures can go as high as 80 degrees Fahrenheit. Its sandy beaches and crystal-clear waters make it the perfect spot for swimming or whale-watching. It does get a[high influx of tourists in winter](https://mauinow.com/2024/02/01/maui-visitor-arrivals-up-to-highest-levels-in-five-months-december-spending-down/) , but the beaches are big enough, so you won’t be bothered.


Maui is easy to get around, but the transportation system is limited. The good news is that there are plenty of opportunities to rent a car, which can cost around $112 daily.


Via Sixt


1. **Best for Exploring Culture: Cartagena, Colombia**


With its warm Caribbean climate, Cartagena is a wonderful place to go if you are looking for travel ideas for winter. I particularly enjoyed strolling through the city and looking at the colonial architecture, which has great historical significance. The Old Town and historic forts, such as Castillo San Felipe de Barajas, were also impressive.


There are plenty of beaches like Bocagrande and Playa Blanca to go to, but if you want a more secluded experience, I recommend Isla de Rosario. There’s a boatload (pun intended) of boats that you can go there with, and you can easily find affordable accommodation at all locations.


1. **Best for Coastal Retreats: Malibu, California**


Malibu has a mild temperature during winter that can go as high as 65 degrees Fahrenheit, making it the perfect choice for outdoor activities. There are plenty of opportunities for surfing, visiting local vineyards, and even hiking the Santa Monica Mountains. Public transport is limited, so I do recommend you rent a car.


While there’s no need for extensive packing, you need to bring layers, as evenings can get cold. I suggest you make a list of the activities you plan to engage in and then make a checklist of clothes you need, both of which can be done with the[Wanderlog app](https://wanderlog.com/home) .


1. **Best for Budget Trips: Yucatan Peninsula, Mexico**


When you’re tight on the budget but still want a warm winter vacation, you don’t have to go to any fancy exotic locations. The Yucatan Peninsula has everything from warm beaches to intriguing archeological sites to keep you entertained. You can spend your time exploring wildlife at the Sian Ka’an Biosphere Reserve, visit the Mayan ruins, swim with the turtles, or snorkel in the cenotes.


Temperatures can go as high as 80 degrees Fahrenheit in winter, making it perfect for some swimming. It’s also very affordable, with a week-long vacation going between $500 and $1,500.


Via Expedia

Winter is an excellent time for traveling, as you can enjoy plenty of unique experiences. Whether you are looking for a warmer or cooler climate, the world is yours to explore. By using tools such as the Wanderlog app, you can stay organized and enjoy your vacation to your heart’s content.
