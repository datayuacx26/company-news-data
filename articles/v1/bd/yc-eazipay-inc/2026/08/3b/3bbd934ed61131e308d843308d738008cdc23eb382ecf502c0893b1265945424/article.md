When setting up a clear payroll policy in Nigeria, you need clear rules on everything from tax laws to staff complaints before anyone gets paid. Relying on inconsistent payroll procedures causes problems quickly:


1. You calculate allowances differently every month, and employees notice.
2. You guess your payment timelines, miss tax deadlines, and get hit with heavy fines.
3. You don’t lock down your data, private salaries leak, and team trust is ruined.


This guide changes that. It is a simple map to help you build an accurate system that keeps your HR and finance teams on the exact same page.


##


**What Is a Payroll Policy?**


A company payroll policy is your business playbook for employee pay. It sets the rules for when people get paid, how bonuses are paid, which taxes you must legally deduct, and how you protect private staff data.


Do not confuse a policy with a procedure. Your payroll management policy sets the big ground rules, while your monthly procedures are the specific technical steps your team takes to run the numbers.


Your HR and finance leaders must build this together. HR makes sure the rules are fair and match local labor habits, while finance ensures the final bill fits your budget and banking limits.


##


**What Should a Payroll Policy Include?**


To build a practical payroll policy template that Nigerian business managers can actually use every month, your document must clearly cover these seven areas:


1. **Payday schedule:** Pick the exact day your team gets paid (like the 25th of the month).
2. **Payroll cut-off dates:** Set a hard monthly deadline (like the 18th) to stop taking new overtime logs or expense claims for that cycle.
3. **New hire data rules:** List what documents a new hire must submit (like their bank details and TIN) before they can join the pay run.
4. **Allowances and bonuses:** Be clear about how you handle transport, housing, or 13th-month bonuses.
5. **Overtime guidelines:** Define who qualifies for extra hours and exactly how those rates are calculated.
6. **Deductions:** List everything that comes out of a paycheck, from government taxes to staff loan repayments.
7. **Error fixes:** Give your employees a clear, calm way to flag and fix salary mistakes, just in case it happens.


##


**Setting Payroll Roles and Approval Controls**


Depending on your business size, one person should not handle the entire pay run, while a shared password can compromise your payroll system. If the same person types the numbers and sends the payment, you risk mistakes, and when several people have access, it’s difficult to know who changed a salary, who added an employee, or who approved a payment. Your policy must divide this work into three steps:


**Step 1: Preparation (HR Admin)**


Gathers the raw data, logs the overtime hours, and updates new onboarding profiles.


**Step 2: Review (Finance Manager)**


Double-checks the calculations, confirms tax tiers, and cross-references the master budget.


**Step 3: Approval (CFO/Founder):**


Approves and locks down the monthly run.


Protect your business by using strict role-based access in your software to build solid payroll controls. HR staff should only edit basic employee details and involve the right HR associates, while accountants handle financial fields and involve the right financial associates when required. ##


**Managing Statutory and Other Payroll Deductions**


Mistakes in remittances lead to penalties in Nigeria. Your policy must outline exact calculation rules and monthly deadlines to follow:


1. **Pay-As-You-Earn (PAYE) Tax:** You must deduct personal income tax from your staff and pay it to the State Internal Revenue Service where they live. These tax rates must follow the official rules set by the[Nigeria Revenue Service (NRS)](https://www.nrs.gov.ng/) .
2. **Pension Contributions:** Under the Pension Reform Act, you must run a mandatory monthly split, usually 10% from the employer and 8% from the employee. These funds must go to registered Pension Fund Administrators following the rules managed by the National Pension Commission (PenCom).
3. **National Housing Fund (NHF):** If your team qualifies, a specific percentage of their basic salary must be deducted and paid monthly toward federal housing plans, following the framework run by the Federal Mortgage Bank of Nigeria (FMBN).


Beyond these laws, your policy should set clear caps on other deductions like salary advances so employees always take home a fair wage.


##


**Handling Payroll Errors, Corrections and Employee Disputes**


Even with good software, mistakes happen. An allowance may be missed or a tax rate miscalculated if not properly documented. When an employee spots an issue on their payslip, they need to know how to get it fixed calmly.


Your salary payment policy should follow a simple, fast timeline:


1. **Flag it:** The employee emails HR within 5 days of payday.
2. **Check it:** The payroll admin checks the records and confirms the error.
3. **Fix it:** If they were underpaid, you send a supplementary transfer within 48 hours, or add it to the next month if they prefer.


Every single fix must create a clear digital audit trail so your books stay totally clean.


##


**Payroll Data, Records and Confidentiality**


Your payroll records hold sensitive information; bank accounts, home addresses, and base salaries. If this data leaks, it harms office morale and violates privacy standards.


Include these rules in your policy:


1. Only designated HR and finance leaders get access to master payroll files.
2. Make strong passwords and multi-factor authentication (MFA) mandatory on all accounting software.
3. Keep historical tax receipts and payroll records secure for at least six years so you are always ready for an audit.


##


**How Often Should a Payroll Policy Be Reviewed?**


Your team should review your payroll procedures in Nigeria guide at least once a year to keep up with changes:


1. New laws: Keep your system updated when national tax bands or labor laws shift.
2. Company growth: Upgrade your approval steps as your team grows.
3. New comp structures: Update your rules if you introduce hybrid work allowances or new sales commissions.
4. Tech upgrades: Change your team workflows when you move away from messy spreadsheets and adopt software.

**FAQ Section**


**What is a payroll policy?**


It is an internal company playbook that outlines the exact rules, timelines, approval chains, and tax compliance steps a business uses to pay its team.


**What should be included in a payroll policy?**


A complete policy must cover salary payment dates, monthly data cut-offs, statutory tax deductions, overtime rates, approval workflows, and data security rules.


**Why does a company need a payroll policy?**


It prevents calculation errors, protects you from costly tax penalties, reduces internal fraud risks, and ensures your team is always paid accurately and on time.


**Who should approve payroll?**


HR manages the initial preparation, your finance manager reviews the numbers against the company budget, and your CFO or founder gives the final sign-off for the bank release.


**How often should a payroll policy be reviewed?**


You should review it at least once a year to ensure your business stays aligned with evolving local tax laws, internal promotions, and company growth.
