## Evotec’s collaboration with Esperion leads to nomination of preclinical development candidate for treatment of Primary Sclerosing Cholangitis


- Evotec receives undisclosed payment
- Evotec to continue supporting the collaboration through its unique integrated, accelerated IND-enabling platform to progress the program toward IND


**Hamburg, Germany, 16 October 2025:**
Evotec SE (Frankfurt Stock Exchange: EVT, SDAX/TecDAX, ISIN: DE0005664809; NASDAQ: EVO) today announced that Esperion Therapeutics (“Esperion”) (NASDAQ: ESPR) has nominated an oral preclinical development candidate for the treatment of primary sclerosing cholangitis (“PSC”), a major milestone stemming from the productive collaboration between Evotec and Esperion.


The collaboration between Evotec and Esperion explores new therapeutic opportunities in ATP citrate lyase (“ACLY”) biology, with the goal to develop next-generation inhibitors optimized to address multiple life-threatening diseases. This PDC was discovered leveraging Evotec’s fully integrated small molecule drug discovery and development platform together with Esperion’s deep expertise in ACLY biology. Evotec will continue to support the project with its unique integrated, accelerated IND-enabling platform (Evotec INDiGO) to progress the program toward IND. The PDC nomination triggered an undisclosed payment to Evotec.


**Dr Cord Dohrmann, Chief Scientific Officer of Evotec, commented:** “The nomination of this preclinical development candidate marks a major milestone in our joint approach, combining deep scientific expertise with seamless operational execution. It is a key step toward clinical development and highlights the strength and efficiency of our collaboration. We are proud to contribute to the advancement of a program that has the potential to address a critical gap in current treatment options.”


**About Primary Sclerosing Cholangitis**
Primary Sclerosing Cholangitis (“PSC”) is a rare, progressive, cholestatic, immune‐mediated disease of the bile ducts. Bile ducts carry the digestive liquid bile from the liver to the small intestine. In PSC, inflammation and injury cause scarring, stricturing, and concentric, obliterative fibrosis within the bile ducts. These processes make the bile ducts hard and narrow leading to biliary cirrhosis, portal hypertension, and eventually hepatic failure in a majority of patients.


The pathogenesis of PSC remains unknown and there is no proven medical or interventional therapy to halt the progression of disease. A liver transplant is the only known long-term treatment option for PSC, however up to 30% of patients who receive a liver transplant for PSC experience recurrence.
