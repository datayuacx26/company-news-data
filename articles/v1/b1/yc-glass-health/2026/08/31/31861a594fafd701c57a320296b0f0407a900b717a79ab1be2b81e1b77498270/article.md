### How clinician review works in practice


Glass should be assessed as a product that can work from chart context while clinicians retain final control over documentation. The practical question is whether that boundary delivers enough value in everyday use. Can clinicians review the right context at the right moment? Does the workflow support efficient documentation review? Does the output fit the way your team already finalizes records? Those are the questions that matter more than a generic integration label.


For IT and security teams, clinician review creates a narrower implementation frame. The core review centers on access governance, audit expectations, data-access controls, and how final documentation is approved. That can make an early technical review more disciplined because the team is not mixing patient-context needs and final-charting requirements into one undifferentiated conversation.


For operational leaders, this boundary helps define a realistic pilot. You can decide in advance whether success means faster chart review, better note preparation, stronger context gathering, cleaner clinical review, or a more consistent workflow for specific specialties. You can also decide which encounter types belong in scope. A narrowly defined pilot is usually more useful than a vague promise of broad transformation, especially when the workflow model is intentionally bounded.
