# Cogent & Regional Broadband Providers

*August 13, 2026 byHayley Levine*


[Regional and rural broadband providers are experts at serving their local community. They build fiber to farmhouses, wire main streets, and connect the schools, municipal buildings and healthcare locations that anchor a town. When a family turns on Netflix from their home in Fargo, North Dakota, they trust their local provider to bring high quality bandwidth right to the house. Or a local store making sure they have access to their inventory system and website. What they don’t see is the greater network underlying it. To deliver that Netflix stream or website access, the local operator reaches beyond its own footprint and turns to an upstream provider like Cogent to bring traffic to or carry traffic from the rest of the internet.](https://www.cogentco.com/cogent-blog/5108-cogent-regional-broadband-providers)


[Read on for more](https://www.cogentco.com/cogent-blog/5108-cogent-regional-broadband-providers)


That handoff comes down to where these companies sit in the internet's structure. Most regional and rural operators, whether electric or telephone co-ops, municipal networks, fixed-wireless operators, or competitive fiber builders, focus on local business and consumer markets, and[purchase IP Transit](https://cogentco.com/en/products-and-services/internet/ip-transit) or Transport service from an upstream provider to increase their reach to content providers that their customers access.


Regional providers want reliable upstream service, not just available upstream. If the upstream service a regional provider buys has long outages, jitter, or significant latency, their end users will blame them – no matter how high quality the last mile is.


The value of an upstream partner is not only in its quality, but also in how connected it is. Every “handoff” between networks adds potential latency and increases the possibility of packet loss. For a customer streaming, gaming, or on a video call, the latency or packet loss degrade their experience. Look for an upstream provider that has direct connectivity to content delivery networks, key application platforms, cloud providers, and other ISPs regionally, nationally, and globally. With over 7500 unique ASNs connect to Cogent’s network, local and regional operators can easily reach the applications and content providers, as well as the government and health care services their communities use.


Resiliency is another piece of the puzzle. Even the most reliable network has occasional outages – such as for maintenance or capacity upgrades. An upstream provider’s resilience is part of the local / regional provider’s resilience. Look for upstream providers with multiple routing paths who can re-route traffic onto backup paths to minimize the impact of outages. You could also consider multi-homing in more than one Carrier Neutral Data Center (CNDC) or with more than one transit provider to improve resilience. Having a resilient network is nothing without having enough capacity. Cogent can provide the capacity that meets your needs, and makes sure that your end users don’t have any lag accessing the things they need most.


For Rural Networks far from large CNDCs,[Optical Wavelengths](https://cogentco.com/en/products-and-services/transport/optical-wavelengths) can be an ideal solution to handle the middle mile. By purchasing with Cogent, the time and money that would’ve been spent on acquiring and installing the fiber can be used towards other network needs. These high-capacity point-to-point circuits haul traffic from major interconnection points like large CNDCs or Carrier Hotels in major markets to the more rural market where the operator can more easily connect.[Cogent's Optical Wavelength network](https://cogentco.com/files/docs/network/maps/map_wave.pdf) spans the US, Canada, and Mexico across over[1,100 data centers](https://cogentco.com/en/wavemap) – many in smaller, more rural communities – such as Grand Island, NE; Fargo, ND; Las Cruces, NM; or Van Buren, AR. Waves move traffic at low latency and high speed, and can connect your regional network to major hubs, at capacities up to 400G.


Rural and Regional ISPs are leveraging Federal and State programs such as the Broadband Equity, Access, and Deployment (BEAD) program to build last mile infrastructure to reach unserved and underserved areas. However, it’s worthwhile to remember that this infrastructure – even fixed wireless and low earth orbit satellite – rely on fiber for upstream connectivity. The National Telecommunications and Information Administration’s (NTIA) Middle Mile Program put nearly a billion dollars toward middle-mile infrastructure – largely fiber - to help reach these unserved and underserved areas.


Consortiums like the[National Content & Technology Cooperative (NCTC)](https://www.nctconline.org/about/) , can help local and regional providers put the whole picture together for their end users. From content packages and network infrastructure, to equipment for the end user’s premises, these consortiums make it easier for local and regional providers to find the right solutions.


A regional ISP with strong local support, uncongested infrastructure, and solid upstream provider can outperform a larger ISP’s residential product in its own market. That is the point. The operator owns the last mile and the customer relationship. And with local technical support and installation, they can provide the personal touch a large ISP can’t. What they need from an upstream partner is a solid network, reach, and reliability worthy of the fiber they have already put in the ground.


Regional providers want what is best for their business and their community. They see the world going online, and they want their end users to have connectivity as strong as anyone in a major metro. Leveraging a Tier 1 provider like Cogent for upstream service is how they deliver it.
