# What is Data Security Posture Management (DSPM)?

The rate at which the digital landscape is evolving and technology is advancing is no secret — it's all happening rapidly. What organizations often don’t adequately plan for, however, is that the vulnerability of their data also increases at the same pace. In fact, business leaders report that[cybersecurity is the top business risk for organizations.](https://www.pwc.com/us/en/library/pulse-survey/managing-business-risks.html)


To keep up with[increasingly sophisticated cyberthreats](https://www.avepoint.com/blog/protect/ai-enhanced-cyberattacks) and the possible reputational and financial repercussions from such, regulatory requirements are becoming more stringent, with tightening legislation and steep fines rolling out in droves. Beyond that, human error and IT failures now account for[nearly a quarter of all data breaches](https://www.ibm.com/think/topics/data-breach) . What does this mean at an organizational level? The increasing vulnerability of sensitive data demands a paradigm shift in the way companies view data security. Simply taking a reactive stance to guarding data is no longer enough.


Enter the concept of data security posture management, also known as DSPM. As organizations shift to a more proactive stance on mitigating risk, DSPM embodies the strategic approach to ensuring critical information remains protected when – not if – a threat arises.


## What is DSPM?


While the concept of DSPM is not new, the term has emerged in recent years as the need for a more proactive approach to data security became vital. IBM[defines DSPM](https://www.ibm.com/think/topics/data-security-posture-management) as “a cybersecurity technology that identifies sensitive data across multiple cloud environments and services, assessing its vulnerability to security threats and risk of regulatory non-compliance.”


However, effective DSPM is not merely a technology, but a practice that empowers organizations with critical insights and automation to help security teams swiftly resolve data security and compliance issues — as well as prevent their recurrence.


DSPM enables organizations to effectively analyze the following:


- Where sensitive data is being stored
- How data is being moved and accessed
- Who has access to the data and whether their permissions are appropriate
- What security risks are at play within their current data posture


By implementing DSPM, organizations can not only gain a comprehensive understanding of their current security posture but also preemptively address vulnerabilities before they can be exploited.


## Why DSPM is Important


Modern collaborative cloud environments like Microsoft 365 have transformed organizational workflows, necessitating a new approach to data security for unstructured content.[89% of organizations](https://info.flexera.com/CM-REPORT-State-of-the-Cloud?_gl=1*15wyp96*_gcl_au*NzQ5OTY2ODcyLjE3MzU4MzYyNTI.) are now leveraging multiple cloud environments. As cloud adoption increases, so do risks such as unauthorized access and compliance issues. These risks necessitate comprehensive security measures and more proactive data management strategies to protect sensitive information. This is where DSPM comes into play.


Incorporating DSPM into an organization's security framework enables:


-


### Improved data protection


DSPM provides a robust shield against unauthorized access and potential data breaches by continuously monitoring and analyzing data flows and access patterns. It ensures that sensitive information is safeguarded, reducing the risk of data loss or theft.


-


### Compliance with regulations


While regulatory requirements become increasingly stringent, DSPM helps organizations stay compliant with various laws and standards, such as GDPR, HIPAA, CCPA, NIS2, and DORA. By identifying and mitigating risks, DSPM ensures that organizations meet regulatory requirements, avoiding hefty fines and reputational damage.


-


### Enhanced threat protection


DSPM offers real-time insights into potential threats and vulnerabilities, allowing security teams to respond promptly and effectively. When combined with technologies such as[browser isolation](https://nordlayer.com/learn/browser-security/what-is-browser-isolation/) , which sandboxes web traffic to neutralize malware, security teams can create a multi-layered defense. This proactive approach minimizes the impact of security incidents and helps prevent future occurrences.


With these benefits in mind, it’s clear that DSPM is a foundational component in a modern data security strategy. However, it's also essential to understand how DSPM complements and extends beyond traditional compliance measures.


## Compliance vs. DSPM


Compliance is a fundamental aspect of data security, but it is not sufficient on its own. This is because compliance frameworks, although crucial, often prioritize meeting regulatory benchmarks over proactive risk mitigation. They guide organizations to adhere to prescribed standards, but they may not always address the dynamic, evolving nature of data security threats.


This is where DSPM excels. It not only ensures regulatory adherence but also provides a continuous, vigilant safeguard against emerging risks. Unlike traditional compliance measures that might only offer periodic assessments, DSPM delivers real-time visibility and automated responses to security incidents. It enables organizations to take a more proactive stance by continuously monitoring and analyzing data flows and patterns within the organization, adapting to new threats and vulnerabilities as they emerge.


Here is an overview of some of the major cybersecurity regulations shaping data protection globally this year. These are also the regulatory requirements that compliance would typically cover.


-


### [General Data Protection Regulation](https://gdpr-info.eu/) (GDPR)


GDPR continues to be a cornerstone of data protection in Europe, with ongoing updates and tightening enforcement. Fines for noncompliance are steep and can reach 4% of a company’s global annual turnover.


-


### [California Privacy Rights Act](https://www.consumerprivacyact.com/california-privacy-act-2020-cpra/) (CPRA)


Expanding on the[California Consumer Privacy Act](https://www.oag.ca.gov/privacy/ccpa) (CCPA), CPRA introduces additional rights for consumers and imposes more stringent requirements on businesses, including enhanced data protection measures and the creation of the California Privacy Protection Agency (CPPA).


-


### [Personal Information Protection Law](https://www2.deloitte.com/cn/en/pages/risk/articles/personal-information-protection-law.html) (PIPL)


PIPL, China’s comprehensive data protection law, imposes strict requirements on how personal data is collected, stored, and processed, with severe penalties imposed for non-compliance.


-


### [Digital Personal Data Protection](https://pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2090271) (DPDP)


DPDP is in the process of being implemented in India as of early 2025; this legislation aims to establish a comprehensive data protection framework with provisions for data localization, consent management, and stringent penalties for data breaches.


While compliance provides the foundation for data security, DSPM practices build upon it, offering an advanced, proactive layer of protection that evolves with the threat landscape.


## Key Components of an Effective DSPM Strategy


To effectively leverage DSPM, organizations should integrate several critical elements into their strategy, such as robust data visibility, thorough risk management, and compliance assurance. The synergy of these components creates a comprehensive posture management framework that not only responds to regulatory demands but also proactively mitigates risk.


Let’s take a closer look at these three core pillars that make up a robust DSPM strategy:


-


### Data visibility


Organizations need a clear view of their cloud data, including where sensitive data is, how it's used, and who accesses it. This is crucial for effective security.


-


### Risk management


DSPM focuses on preemptive actions like detecting misconfigurations, enforcing access controls, and handling data to prevent accidental exposure or insider threats.


-


### Compliance assurance


Automated tools are essential to stay compliant with regulations, monitor standards, generate reports, and enforce policies consistently.


Traditional DSPM approaches, however, often stop at providing visibility and issuing reactive alerts. To meet the demands of modern cloud security, it's crucial to adopt a more holistic and integrated approach that goes beyond mere detection. This ensures that your organization’s security and governance protocols are seamlessly embedded into daily operations, enhancing overall data protection.
