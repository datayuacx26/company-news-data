## From Collaboration to Chaos: How Bidding Got Messy


There was a time when construction bidding was all about relationships. GCs and trade contractors would meet face-to-face, share plans, and collaborate on projects in a straightforward, respectful way.


But as technology took over, the process became less personal and more chaotic. Platforms promised efficiency but instead delivered a flood of irrelevant invites, spammy ITBs, and mismanaged workflows.


Contractors now ignore most bid invites, overwhelmed by the sheer volume, while GCs lose valuable time chasing responses and managing outdated contact lists. This frustration has led many in the industry to rethink how bidding should work. Platforms like Downtobid and BuildingConnected each offer a way forward—but they take very different approaches to solving the problem.

## What is BuildingConnected?


BuildingConnected has been a staple in the construction industry for years, offering a broad, all-in-one platform for managing bids. Its massive network of trade contractors, prequalification tools, and integrations with platforms like Autodesk make it a popular choice for large-scale projects.


BuildingConnected is often used by builders seeking a broader subcontractor network. It’s known for:


- A massive database of contractors in diverse locations, ideal if you frequently expand or handle multi-state projects.
- Deep integrations with Autodesk and other collaboration tools, letting bigger teams unify data, scheduling, and cost analysis in one system.
- Prequalification modules that can help with risk oversight, plus optional takeoff tools for early estimates.


The best features of BuildingConnected appear when you run huge coverage blasts or rely on synergy between many stakeholders. However, the sheer scale can lead to clutter. GCs often must verify whether a sub’s details are still current—some subs remain in the system after completing a job, even if they’ve moved on. For many, it’s still worth it: big GCs appreciate how BuildingConnected can get an entire office on the same page to collaborate on every job.

- **BuildingConnected:** Huge network, great if you need to cast a wide net. But GCs must often verify who’s active or not, especially if user-generated profiles are outdated.
- **Downtobid:** Maintains a smaller yet more vetted subcontractor network, so GCs rarely waste time chasing defunct contacts.


**Winner:** Tie—BuildingConnected for maximum scale, Downtobid for minimal “verification successful waiting.”

- **BuildingConnected:** Mass invites can lead to many “maybes” or no-shows, requiring GCs to do more follow-up.

**Winner:** Downtobid, for a more direct connection that fosters actual engagement and fewer lost bids.

**Winner: Downtobid**

Downtobid prioritizes better bid coverage by ensuring that every ITB is relevant, responses are tracked, and follow-ups happen automatically. By the time bids are due, GCs have a clear, accurate view of who’s bidding and where coverage gaps exist, reducing last-minute stress.

- **BuildingConnected:** Enterprise design; robust but can feel impersonal, especially for smaller GCs or single-trade invites.
- **Downtobid:** Maintains a simpler workflow, championing the human element. GCs see who’s reading the invite and get notifications if a sub doesn’t follow up, removing guesswork.


**Winner:** Downtobid, especially for GCs who want a frictionless approach that respects each sub’s time.

- **BuildingConnected:** Often described as higher cost for smaller teams. Larger builders might find its range of features worth it.

- **Choose Downtobid** if you value a human approach to coverage, prefer curated sub data, and want to reduce the complexity of chasing replies. It’s best for GCs who want to seamlessly share project details, confirm availability, and finalize bids without wrestling with massive extra modules.
