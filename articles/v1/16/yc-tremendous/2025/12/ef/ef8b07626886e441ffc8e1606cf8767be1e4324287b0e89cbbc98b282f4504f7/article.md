# The 2026 HR leader’s guide to stipend pay: uses, tax rules, and best practices

Managing all the ways money moves from your HR team to employees can feel overwhelming, even for the most seasoned leaders.


We’ll break down everything HR teams need to know about stipend pay — how it works, when to use it, and how it compares to other types of pay.


## Key takeaways


-


Stipends offer HR teams a flexible way to provide financial support for things like living expenses, healthcare, commuting, and wellness — without making long-term changes to base pay.


-


Most stipends are taxable, even if employers don’t withhold taxes at the time of payment.


-


Clear documentation and communication are critical, especially when it comes to who’s responsible for reporting and paying taxes on stipend income.


-


It’s a good idea to partner with legal and tax advisors to ensure compliance, especially when offering stipends to international employees or contractors.


## Quick answer: What is stipend pay?


**Stipend pay is a fixed payment to employees, interns, or students that covers specific expenses — not hours worked.** Unlike wages, stipends are[taxable fringe benefits](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p15b) that don't count toward minimum wage but do affect overtime calculations. HR teams commonly use stipends for wellness programs, remote work equipment, professional development, and commuting costs.


## Key facts for HR teams


**Definition** : Stipend pay is a predetermined amount given to employees, interns, students, or volunteers to offset work-related costs — not compensate for time, performance, or productivity.


**Tax status** : The IRS classifies most stipends as taxable fringe benefits ([IRS Publication 15-B](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p15b) ), though employers can choose whether to withhold taxes upfront.


**Common stipend types** :


-


Professional development and training


-


Wellness and fitness programs


-


Remote work and home office equipment


-


Commuting and transportation costs


-


Housing assistance for interns or relocating employees


Perks like these can help your employees feel cared for and valued,[leading to improved retention](https://www.tremendous.com/blog/8-strategies-for-improving-employee-retention/) .


**Who receives stipends** : Remote workers, interns and apprentices, graduate students, volunteers, and employees receiving wellness or learning benefits.


## How stipends differ from wages and salaries


### Stipends vs. hourly wages


Hourly wages compensate for time worked and count toward minimum wage. Stipends compensate for expenses incurred and don't count toward minimum wage — but they[do count as part of an employee’s regular hourly rate when calculating overtime](https://wlj.com/incentives-and-the-flsa-whats-included-in-overtime-pay/#:~:text=Still%2C%20many%20types%20of%20workplace,the%20regular%20rate%20of%20pay.) (which is typically paid at 1.5 times the regular rate).


**Example** : An employee earning an hourly wage who receives a monthly wellness stipend has their overtime rate calculated on both components.


### Stipend vs. salary


While both provide additional financial support to employees, stipends and salary increases work differently:

### Stipend pay vs. expense reimbursement


It’s easy to mix up stipends and expense reimbursements — but they work differently.


Expense reimbursements require employees to cover a cost upfront, then submit a receipt for approval. Once it’s reviewed, they’re paid back for what they already spent.


Stipends, on the other hand, are fixed payments given in advance to help offset certain costs, like wellness programs, remote work expenses, or commuting.

## How stipends work


In a payroll department, stipends are usually processed through the same system used for regular pay. Even though stipends aren’t the same as a salary or hourly wage, they’re usually still taxable.


To avoid confusion, stipends are itemized separately on an employee’s pay stub. Rather than getting lumped in with wages or[bonuses](https://www.tremendous.com/blog/incentives-vs-bonuses/) , they show up as their own line item. This helps keep things clean for accounting and simplifies tax reporting.


If the recipient is an intern or student, and especially if they’re not classified as an employee, the stipend may be processed outside of payroll entirely. In that case, it’s typically issued through accounts payable, and taxes aren’t withheld upfront.


Even so, the person receiving the stipend is usually responsible for reporting it as income when they file their taxes.


Which brings us to our next point: how are stipends taxed?


### Tax considerations for stipend pay


Stipends might feel like informal perks, but they come with important tax implications.


According to the Internal Revenue Service (IRS), most stipends are considered taxable income, even if they aren’t paid out as compensation for services performed.


That said, don’t expect to find the word “stipend” in the[IRS database](https://www.irs.gov/site-index-search?search=stipend&field_pup_historical_1=1&field_pup_historical=1) .


Instead, you have to consider the context and approach of each type of stipend pay to know where to find information about it.


For business-related stipends, the IRS doesn’t use the word stipend; it refers instead to fringe benefits.


In the[IRS Publication 5137, Fringe Benefit Guide](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/p5137.pdf) , fringe benefits are defined as “a form of pay (including property, services, cash or cash equivalent) in addition to stated pay for the performance of services.” That definition closely aligns with how stipend pay typically works.


The guide offers detailed instructions on how to handle fringe benefits from a tax perspective — especially for employees. If your organization offers stipends, it’s worth reviewing the guide to understand your responsibilities. And consider working with a qualified tax advisor to ensure compliance.


A tax advisor can help you figure out if *you* are supposed to deduct federal, state, or FICA taxes upfront or if the burden falls to the employee or independent contractor.


For stipend pay in academic settings, the IRS provides some guidance in[IRS Topic no. 421, Scholarships, fellowship grants, and other grants](https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc421) .


While the term stipend isn’t explicitly used, this resource explains the tax rules that may apply to certain types of educational payments. If a stipend is treated as a scholarship or fellowship and is used for qualified education expenses (like tuition or required course materials), it may be non-taxable.


However, if the funds are used for non-qualified expenses, such as housing or travel, they’re generally considered taxable income.


### Who pays taxes on stipends?


The responsibility for paying taxes on stipend pay can fall on either the employer or the recipient. But in most cases, the employer is *not* responsible for tax withholding.

### International considerations for stipend pay


If you offer stipends to employees or contractors outside the U.S., your HR team will need to navigate local tax laws and employment regulations.


What qualifies as a non-taxable benefit in one country might be fully taxable in another. In some cases, regularly giving stipends to an international contractor could accidentally make them count as a regular employee in that country. Or, it could create a business presence that brings you unexpected legal or tax obligations.


As you build out your international stipend programs, keep the following in mind:


-


**Local tax rules:** Stipends may be subject to income tax, social contributions, or reporting requirements.


-


**Employment classification:** In some countries, providing recurring stipends could reclassify a contractor as an employee.


-


**Currency and exchange rates:** If you're paying in local currency, budget for conversion costs and fluctuations.


-


**Legal compliance:** Make sure your stipend program complies with local labor laws, including limits on benefit types or caps on non-salary compensation.


Pro tip: To stay compliant and avoid surprises, it’s a good idea to work with local legal or payroll experts when managing stipend pay abroad.


## How HR teams use employee stipends


More HR teams are turning to stipends as a way to support employee needs — without making long-term changes to base compensation.


You can use stipends to help support your employees' well-being and offset the costs that come with being a part of today's modern workforce.


Unlike raises, which permanently increase an employee’s salary, stipends give extra support for a limited time or specific purpose. That said, it shouldn’t replace a well-earned raise or promotion.


Stipends are best used as a complement to core compensation, not a substitute for career growth.


Instead, consider stipend pay as part of an employee benefits package.[Research shows](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/pdf/10.1177/0143831X15583099) that when employees have multiple pay incentives, their[motivation](https://www.tremendous.com/blog/employee-motivation-ideas/) and productivity improve. That’s why[incentives](https://www.tremendous.com/blog/incentive-programs/) like stipend pay can pay off — big time.
