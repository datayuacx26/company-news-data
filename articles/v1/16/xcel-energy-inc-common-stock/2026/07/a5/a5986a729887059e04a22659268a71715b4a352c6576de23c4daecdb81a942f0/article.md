# Investing in the next generation of energy innovators

On a crisp morning in late April, students from across the Upper Midwest stepped foot into Xcel Energy service centers with one mission in mind: making energy work better.


Their visits were part of Make48’s nationwide competition, where more than 2,500 students face off in an innovation-based tournament that challenges teams to conceptualize, prototype and present solutions to unique, real-world problems — all within a 48-hour window.


The preliminary “classroom challenge” stage of the competition tasked students with developing a tool that would make a meaningful impact on lineworkers’ ability to serve their communities — working face-to-face with crews to better understand the real struggles they encounter every day.


One challenge identified by White Bear Lake High School’s team, The Bears, is how to maintain grip on small nuts and bolts while wearing bulky, shock-resistant gloves. But as teammates Logan Blalock, Ben Bower and Jack Firkus would soon discover, identifying the problem was only one piece of the puzzle.


When working in a demanding, high-voltage environment, safety will always take precedence over efficiency. If the team wanted to progress to the next stage of the competition, they would need to invent something that doesn’t just enhance dexterity but complements the safety equipment required in the field. To solve for this challenge, the team carefully considered factors like material and cost. They learned how to adapt, for instance, when they learned an early idea involving magnets would not meet safety standards. At every turn, they innovated to ensure their creation fit seamlessly — and safely — into the day-to-day work of a lineworker.


Their winning invention? A fresh take on a handheld wrench, with a simple yet exceedingly effective design. They had succeeded in creating a real, applicable and affordable solution to an inefficiency many lineworkers grapple with daily.


Reflecting on their visit with Xcel Energy crews, Blalock said, “It allowed us to get really personal with it and see what they truly need … we got down with them and saw what it’s actually like.” This is the kind of innovation made possible only through real, in-person connection between problem solvers and the people they seek to help.


“It was nice for them to come here and actually go through the trucks.” said Xcel Energy foreman Eric Nolan, who helped advise the second-place team in Hudson, Wisconsin. “Without \[the students\] ever doing it or knowing what you’re talking about, it’s kind of hard to explain.”


Xcel Energy is committed to supporting STEM education initiatives in our communities, and moments like this give kids the chance to get hands on experience in the workplace — building a strong bridge between industry experts and the leaders of tomorrow.


"Make48 gave my students an opportunity to think beyond the classroom and see themselves as innovators and problem-solvers” remarked The Bears’ teacher, Eric Olson. “The energy, teamwork and creativity throughout the experience exceeded my expectations, and students stayed engaged from start to finish.”


Looking to the future, their team is prepared to carry the lessons they learned in the field forward — both to the next stage of competition and into their future careers. They'll be participating in the Make48 Final Playoff Event at UW-Stout July 31 through August 2. The first-place team will win $2,000 and the top two teams will advance on to Season 10 Nationals.


“Going out and actually having to view something in person and try to solve a problem is very valuable across almost every single field of work,” said Bower.
