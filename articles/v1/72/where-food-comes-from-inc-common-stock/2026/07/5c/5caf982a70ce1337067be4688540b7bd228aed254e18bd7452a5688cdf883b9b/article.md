# Animal Welfare Certification vs. On-Farm Audits: Clearing Up the Confusion

When it comes to animal welfare, consumers are increasingly looking for assurance that the products they purchase come from farms that prioritize humane treatment of animals. However, confusion often arises between the concepts of animal welfare certification and the actual on-farm audits. While both are essential components of ensuring animal welfare, they are distinct processes that serve different purposes.


Animal welfare labels can be confusing—especially when terms like


*audit* and


*certification* are used interchangeably.


They’re related, but not the same.


##


**What Is an Animal Welfare Audit?**


An audit is the


**evaluation process** .


It assesses:


-


Animal health


-


Living conditions


-


Feeding practices


-


Handling methods


-


Caretaker training


-


Documentation and Record-keeping


-


Etc.


Audits are conducted by trained third-party auditors.


The purpose of an animal welfare audit is to identify areas for improvement and ensure compliance with an established welfare standard.


##


**What Is Animal Welfare Certification?**


Certification is the


**outcome** .


It confirms that a farm has met defined standards and allows them to use a verified label.


Animal welfare certification is a formal recognition that a farm meets specific standards related to the humane treatment of animals. When a certification is earned that means it is following guidelines and regulations set forth by that specific certification standard.


It typically will result in a status or label that can be displayed on productsand by the farm itself. Certification signifies that a farm has undergone a thorough evaluation process and has demonstrated adherence to established animal welfare criteria.


###


Key Characteristics of Animal Welfare Certification


-


Standards-Based: Certification programs are based on specific, predefined standards that must be met to obtain certification.


-


Program-Driven: Various organizations and initiatives create certification programs tailored to different livestock species and production practices.


-


Results in Label or Status: Once certified, farms can use the certification label on their products, providing consumers with assurance about their animal welfare practices.


###

-

No audit → no certification


###


How Audits Support Certification


Audits are the actual mechanism through which animal welfare certification is achieved. An audit evaluates a farm’s practices against the established standards set by the certification program. Here’s how the two processes interact:


-


Audits = Mechanism: Audits provide the necessary assessment to determine whether a farm meets the required standards for certification. Typically, in many instances and in many animal welfare standards, this requires as onsite audit, which for animal welfare is very important since the welfare of the animal should be observed in person to truly assess whether a farm deserves to receive that certification.


Consumers need to do their research of the animal welfare label they are seeking trust out of to ensure it aligns with their values – and if an onsite evaluation is something they value.


-


Certification = Outcome: Once a farm successfully completes an audit and meets the criteria, it is awarded certification, allowing the use of the certification label.


**In essence, audits serve as the foundation for certification, ensuring that only farms that adhere to high animal welfare standards earn the right to display the certification label.**


##


What Happens During an On-Farm Audit?


During an on-farm audit, you can expect auditors to conduct a thorough inspection of the farm's operations. The process typically includes:


1.


Pre-Audit Preparation: Farms prepare by reviewing their records and practices in advance of the audit.


2.


Onsite Walk-Through: Auditors perform a walk-through of the facilities, observing animal living conditions and handling practices.


3.


Record Review: Auditors examine records related to animal health, nutrition, and care practices to ensure compliance with standards.


4.


Interviews: Auditors may interview farm personnel to assess their understanding of animal welfare practices and any procedures the farm claims to have in place.


5.


Reporting: After the audit, auditors compile a report detailing their findings and any necessary corrective actions.


The goal of an on-farm audit is to ensure that all aspects of animal welfare are being addressed and to provide feedback for improvement.


###


Why Both Matter for Animal Welfare Claims


Both animal welfare audits and certifications are crucial for substantiating claims related to animal welfare. Here’s why:


-


Consumer Confidence: Certification allows brands to communicate their commitment to animal welfare, building trust with consumers who prioritize humane treatment.


-


Continuous Improvement: Regular audits help farms identify areas for improvement, ensuring that animal welfare standards are continually met and enhanced. This is crucial when looking to improve both the agriculture and food industry landscapes.


-


Transparency: Audits provide an objective assessment of animal welfare practices, offering transparency to consumers and stakeholders.


In summary, both audits and certifications play vital roles in promoting animal welfare and ensuring that claims made by producers are backed by rigorous evaluation and standards.


It is up to consumers to determine if the labels they are actively supporting and trusting are backed by a third-party verified standard.


##


Common Misconceptions


Despite the clarity provided here, several misconceptions persist regarding animal welfare audits and certification:


**“Audit = Certified”** False. Audits assess. Certification approves.


**“One audit is enough”** False. Ongoing audits are required to maintain certification.


##


**Bottom Line**


Audits and certification are two parts of the same system.


Together, they ensure that animal welfare claims are not just stated but verified.
