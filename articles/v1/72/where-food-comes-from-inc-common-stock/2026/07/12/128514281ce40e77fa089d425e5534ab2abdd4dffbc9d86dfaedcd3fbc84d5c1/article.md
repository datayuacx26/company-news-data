# How Animal Welfare Claims Are Verified in Beef and Livestock

Animal welfare has moved from a niche concern to a defining factor in how consumers evaluate food.


According to the Power of Meat 2025 study, 39% of consumers say label claims mentioning “humanely raised” influences their purchasing decisions.


But as demand grows, so does the question:


**How do you know those claims are real?**


Verifying animal welfare practices ensure that claims about humane treatment are not just stated—but proven—through standards, audits, and additional oversight.


##


What Are Animal Welfare Claims in Beef and Livestock?


Animal welfare claims refer to verified declarations made by beef and livestock producers regarding the treatment of animals in their care.


These claims typically


encompass a variety of practices widely based on animal welfare frameworks, such as the Five Domains, used to assess, measure, and facilitate overall quality of life for animals. The Five Domains was the foundational standard that brings attention to animal welfare and management that is a subjective focus to an animal’s overall welfare state.


**The Five Domains:**


1.


Nutrition


2.


Environment


3.


Health


4.


Behavior


5.


Mental State

##


The Role of Low-Stress Handling


Coinciding with the


Five Domains,


**low-stress management practices** play a critical role in animal welfare. They focus on minimizing fear and anxiety during an animal’s life from birth to harvest.


Promoting the positive not only improves animal well-being but also greatly contributes to higher quality meat products and enhanced food safety, making it both an ethical and economic priority.


It is imperative that producers take care of their animals, not only because they care about them, but because it impacts the quality of the product they are working so hard to produce.


##


Why Animal Welfare Verification Matters


**Consumer Trust**


Consumers increasingly expect proof of ethical claims. Animal welfare verification becomes essential for building trust. Verification provides confidence that products meet standards backed by science.


**Retailer Requirements**


Many retailers are adopting stringent animal welfare policies, requiring suppliers to verify their claims. This demand for verified animal welfare practices helps ensure that products on store shelves meet standards that align with the values of socially conscious consumers.


**Risk Mitigation**


Verification of animal welfare claims helps mitigate risks associated with non-compliance. Producers who adhere to established standards reduce risks tied to:


-


Mislabeling


-


Greenwashing


-


Negative publicity


-


Regulatory exposure


**Differentiation in Market**


Verified animal welfare claims help brands stand out in crowded markets and can serve as a unique selling proposition for producers and brands. Consumers are willing to pay a premium for ethically produced protein products.

###


Step-by-Step Verification Process


The verification of animal welfare claims involves a systematic process that includes:


1.


**Selecting a Standard**


A clear, measurable standard defines expectations around:


-


Animal care


-


Nutrition


-


Health management


-


Handling practices


-


Traceability


1.


**Desk or On-Farm Audits**


Auditors conduct


**on-site evaluations** —often annually or unannounced—to assess real-world conditions.


For animal welfare, physical observation is essential. You can’t verify animal care from paperwork alone.


1.


**Documentation Review**


Producers must maintain records that demonstrate compliance, including:


-


Health and treatment logs


-


Feed and nutrition records


-


Training documentation


1.


**Corrective Actions**


If deficiencies are identified during audits, producers must implement corrective actions to address issues and improve animal welfare practices.


1.


**Ongoing Monitoring**


Verification is continuous, not one time. Periodic reassessments, often annually, are crucial to maintaining compliance and ensuring that animal welfare practices evolve with industry standards and consumer expectations.


###


What Happens During an Animal Welfare Audit?


During an animal welfare audit, trained auditors assess various aspects of the livestock operation. Auditors evaluate:


-


Animal caretaker training


-


Animal behavior and health


-


Facility conditions (space, shelter, access to food and water)


-


Handling and transportation practices


-


Veterinary care records


-


Feed and water


-


Etc.


Auditors may also interview staff to ensure that welfare practices are understood and implemented consistently.


##


Who Verifies Animal Welfare Claims?


**Government vs Third-Party**


-


**Government agencies** establish regulatory baselines


-


**Third-party organizations** provide independent verification through audits


Animal welfare claims can be verified by government agencies or independent third-party organizations. Government oversight typically involves regulations that producers must follow, while third-party verifiers provide an additional layer of credibility through external audits.


**Role of Independent Verifiers,** **** **Reviewers, and Auditors**


Independent verifiers play a crucial role in ensuring the integrity of animal welfare claims.


They conduct audits, assess compliance with established standards, and issue certificates that validate adherence to humane practices. Working with a third-party verification organization can enhance a producer's credibility and build consumer trust.


Organizations like Where Food Comes From conduct audits, assess compliance, and validate claims through structured verification programs.


This third-party layer adds credibility and transparency beyond internal or regulatory checks.


##


Common Misconceptions


Despite growing awareness, several misconceptions persist regarding animal welfare claims:


**“All Labels Are Trustworthy”**


Not all animal welfare labels are created equal. Consumers should look for labels that are third-party certified from recognized organizations to ensure claims are verified and authentic.


**“Animal Welfare Is Just About Food”**


While nutrition is crucial, animal welfare encompasses a broader range of practices, including housing, treatment, and handling, as we mentioned above when we discussed The Five Freedoms. As well as promoting practices that align with sustainability goals that run up and down the supply chain.


**“Audits Happen Once”**


Understanding that animal welfare verification is an


**ongoing process is essential** . Regular audits and continuous improvement are vital for maintaining compliance.

##


FAQ Section


**What does an animal welfare certification mean?**


An animal welfare certification indicates that a livestock operation has met specific standards for humane treatment and care, verified through a third-party verification organization that works alongside independent auditors.


**Who verifies animal welfare in beef?**


Both government agencies and independent third-party organizations verify animal welfare claims in beef production, ensuring compliance with established standards. Consumers likely need to do some research to determine if the labels they see on packaging are a third-party verified claim.


**Are animal welfare claims regulated?**


Yes, animal welfare claims are regulated, but the level of regulation can vary by country and certification program. It's essential to look for recognized certifications for assurance.


**What is the difference between audited and certified?**


Audited refers to the producer’s practices having been evaluated against a set of standards, while certified means that a producer has successfully met those standards and received official recognition.
