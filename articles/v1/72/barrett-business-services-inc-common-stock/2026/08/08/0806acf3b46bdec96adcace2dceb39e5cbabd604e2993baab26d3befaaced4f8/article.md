# Closing the Gap: Protection for Life's Unexpected Events

## What Are Supplemental Benefits, and Why Should Employers Offer Them?


Supplemental benefits help employees cover out-of-pocket costs and income disruptions caused by accidents, illnesses, and hospital stays. For employers, these benefits can improve retention, morale, and financial wellness without increasing benefit costs.


Imagine one of your employees is injured in an accident outside of work.


Their health insurance helps cover treatment, but the financial impact rarely stops there. Time away from work, travel, childcare, and everyday household expenses can quickly add up. Deductibles, coinsurance, and lost income can create real financial strain even for those with good medical coverage.


You’ve likely seen how situations like this affect more than just the individual. Financial stress shows up in employee morale, productivity, and retention, and over time, it can impact the stability of your


*entire* workforce.


Supporting employees through life’s unexpected challenges isn’t just the right thing to do; it’s a practical way to build a stronger, more resilient business.


## **The Growing Financial Gap**


### **Why Are Employees Struggling to Cover Unexpected Medical Costs?**


For many employees, covering unexpected expenses is becoming more difficult.


According to the


[Kaiser Family Foundation](https://www.kff.org/health-costs/2025-employer-health-benefits-survey/) , the average annual deductible for single coverage has climbed to $1,886 - a 43% increase over the last decade. For smaller employers with 10 to 199 employees, that number grows to $2,631.


At the same time, household financial readiness remains a concern.


[Federal Reserve data](https://www.federalreserve.gov/consumerscommunities/shed.htm) shows that 63% of adults can cover a $400 unexpected expense using cash or savings. More than one-third need to borrow, sell something, or cannot cover it at all.


When you look at these trends together, it’s clear: even a relatively small disruption can create significant financial pressure for employees. Without an added layer of protection, many are left to bridge the gap on their own.


## **Support When It Matters Most**


###


**What Are Supplemental Benefits?**


Voluntary supplemental health benefits can help close this gap by providing employees with direct cash support when they need it most. These plans are designed to complement


*existing* health coverage, not replace it, and give employees more flexibility to manage unexpected costs.


Here is how that support shows up in practice:


- **Accident Insurance :** Provides benefits for covered accidental injuries and related care, helping employees manage out-of-pocket costs after an unexpected event.


- **Critical Illness Insurance :** Offers a lump-sum payment following a covered diagnosis, such as cancer, heart attack, or stroke, so employees can focus on recovery instead of finances.


- **Hospital Indemnity Insurance :** Provides cash benefits for hospital admissions and stays, helping offset the added costs that come with hospitalization.


## **Flexibility That Makes a Difference**


### **How Can Employees Use Supplemental Benefit Payments?**


One of the biggest advantages of supplemental benefits is how flexible they are. Employees pay premiums through post-tax payroll deductions, and eligible benefits are typically paid directly to them tax-free. That means they can use the funds however they need, including:


- Deductibles and coinsurance


- Medical and prescription expenses


- Rent or mortgage payments


- Utilities and everyday bills


- Transportation costs


- Childcare


- Replacing lost income during recovery


Flexibility can make a meaningful difference for many employees, allowing them to focus on getting better instead of worrying about medical bills.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Supplemental Benefits


##### What are supplemental benefits?


Supplemental benefits are voluntary insurance plans that provide cash benefits to employees for covered accidents, illnesses, or hospital stays.


##### Are supplemental benefits a replacement for health insurance?


No. Supplemental benefits are designed to complement major medical coverage by helping employees manage out-of-pocket expenses and income disruptions.


##### Who pays for supplemental benefits?


Employees typically pay for coverage through post-tax payroll deductions, often at no direct cost to the employer.


##### How can employees use benefit payments?


Payments can be used for medical expenses, deductibles, rent, mortgage payments, childcare, transportation, utilities, or other financial needs.


##### Why should employers offer supplemental benefits?


Offering supplemental benefits can improve employee financial wellness, retention, productivity, and overall workforce stability.
