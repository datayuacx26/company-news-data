# Amazon Just Changed 4 Rules. Is Your Brand Ready?

> This blog was written by[Chris Baird, CMO at Pattern](https://www.linkedin.com/in/cbbaird/) , leading marketing and go-to-market strategy for ecommerce brands worldwide.


Amazon just changed four rules in the space of a few weeks. Is your brand ready for all four?


None of them arrived as a splashy keynote. They landed as a Seller Central notice, forum posts, a press release, and a quiet product rename. Taken separately, each looks like housekeeping. Taken together, they move the same four levers every ecommerce brand lives and dies on: traffic, conversion, price, and availability. Put simply, the ecommerce equation.


Here is what actually changed, straight from Amazon, and the questions your team should be asking this week. I’ll share it straight, because I think most brands are going to feel this before they understand it.


## 1. The Buy Box just got more honest.


[Amazon confirmed it is removing seller performance as a standalone eligibility requirement for the Featured Offer](https://sellercentral.amazon.com/seller-forums/discussions/t/7336583f-09ac-41de-99d1-667017a88db2) , the placement most people still call the Buy Box. Rollout started in the EU and UK on July 20, and reaches the rest of the world by year end.


Performance is not disappearing. Order defect rate, late shipment rate, and valid tracking still matter. They stop being a pass or fail gate at the door and become weighted inputs inside one ranking contest. The practical effect is simple and immediate: sellers who were suppressed on performance grounds rejoin the pool, and any brand that priced defensively against a thinned field now competes against more live offers for the same button.


If part of your Buy Box win rate came from competitors being locked out on performance, that cushion is gone. Everyone's back in the pool now.


## 2. The Prime badge just got harder to keep.


Effective July 6,[Amazon raised the minimum delivery speed requirements for Seller Fulfilled Prime](https://sellercentral.amazon.com/seller-forums/discussions/t/b170390de2c22599b8fa34150e104cc5) . For standard size items, 40% of Prime page views now have to show a one day delivery date, up from 30%, and the two day threshold moved to 75% from 70%. The five day bar stays at 90%.


Two details matter more than the headline percentages:


1. Amazon is excluding weekends from speed scoring until October 17, so the real compliance deadline is mid October, not July.
2. A new delivery promise tool arriving in September lets sellers set shipping times, cutoffs, and weekend availability at the individual zip code level. That is a gift and a liability at once. The promise shown to the customer gets more accurate, and the accuracy is now on you. Miss it and you lose the badge, which pushes your offer below the Featured Offer threshold and out of Prime filtered search.


## 3. Shipping got a new option.


[Amazon opened its full logistics network to any business through Amazon Supply Chain Services,](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/retail/amazon-supply-chain-services-for-business) extending freight, distribution, fulfillment, and parcel shipping well beyond its own marketplace. Logistics platform Loop reports clients saving up to $6 per package by shifting eligible residential volume to Amazon Shipping instead of comparable FedEx and UPS services, with one large retailer cutting annual shipping spend by more than a third.


This is the availability lever and the margin lever moving together. Faster, cheaper residential coverage makes the new SFP speed bars reachable and protects the P&L at the same time. The brands that treat carrier mix as a strategic decision, not a back office one, will fund their own speed upgrades.


## 4. Rufus is gone. Alexa for Shopping just took its search bar.


On May 13,[Amazon retired Rufus and folded it into Alexa for Shopping](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/retail/alexa-for-shopping-ai-assistant) , an AI assistant that now sits inside the default Amazon search bar. Shoppers no longer type "men's running shoes" and scroll a grid. They ask a question, and a language model interprets intent, builds its own search, and returns a conversational answer with comparison cards.


For your listings, the winning inputs shifted. Attribute completeness, benefit led bullet structure, content that reads as informational rather than promotional, and review quality now decide whether an AI surfaces you at all. This is the same generative search shift reshaping the open web, now running inside the marketplace. Keyword stuffing quietly stopped working. Answering the shopper's real question started working.


## One equation ties all four together.


Every one of these changes[hits the same formula:](https://www.pattern.com/blog/the-ecommerce-equation-4-levers-ceos-use-to-drive-marketplace-revenue) revenue equals traffic times conversion times price times availability. Alexa for Shopping moves traffic. The Featured Offer change moves conversion and price. SFP and Amazon Shipping move availability and margin. Pull one lever in isolation and you leave the other three exposed.


This is exactly why point tools struggle. An ad platform optimizes traffic and ignores your Buy Box. A repricer defends price but never touches your delivery promise. Amazon just made it clear that the levers are connected, so the operating model has to be too.
