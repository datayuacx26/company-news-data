# Should you list it or fix it?

#### KEY POINTS


- Many homeowners with already-low mortgage rates are opting to remodel their homes, “fixing it” rather than “listing it.”
- For those looking to remodel on consolidate higher-interest debt, a HELOC offers flexible access to home equity without replacing a low-rate mortgage, while a cash-out refinance trades your rate for a new loan.
- The right decision depends on your goals, current mortgage rate and how you plan to use your home equity.


Rising home values have given many homeowners a valuable asset: equity. However, when interest rates are higher than your current mortgage rate, deciding what to do with that equity—whether to sell and move or improve what you already have—has become more complicated.


For many, the answer is increasingly clear: stay put.


“A lot of homeowners are sitting on mortgage rates in the 3% range,” said Bill Ott, a Fort Worth-based mortgage banker with BOK Financial Mortgage. “If they don’t have to move, they don’t. Moving today often means giving that up for a much higher rate.”


That dynamic is reshaping the housing market. Inventory remains tight because many homeowners are choosing not to sell unless they truly need to. Instead, they are asking a different question: *How can I make my current home work better for me?*


**When staying makes more sense than moving**


When interest rates were ultra-low, homeowners often sold simply to upgrade or change locations. Today, those “nice-to-have” moves are less common.


“Right now, people are really only moving when there’s a necessity,” Ott said. “Relocation for work, changes in school districts, or downsizing—those are the big drivers.”


If you’re happy with your location but not necessarily your house, staying can offer several advantages, such as:


- You keep your existing mortgage rate.
- You avoid the cost and stress of moving.
- You can tailor your home improvements to your exact needs.


That’s why many homeowners are investing in upgrades like kitchens, bathrooms, outdoor living spaces and even full renovations, rather than selling their homes.


**How to tap your home equity**


To fund those improvements—or consolidate higher-interest debt—homeowners typically turn to two options—a home equity line of credit (HELOC) or cash-out refinance. Each works differently, and the right choice depends on your goals.


**Why choose a HELOC:**


A HELOC acts like a revolving line of credit secured by your home. “Think of it like a large credit line,” Ott said. “Your payment is based on what you actually use—not the total amount available.”


That flexibility makes HELOCs especially useful for:


- Ongoing or phased renovations
- Emergency expenses
- Debt consolidation


For example, if you open a $100,000 HELOC but only use $40,000, your payments are based on that $40,000—not the full line, Ott explained.


HELOCs also allow borrowers to preserve their existing low-rate mortgage, something many homeowners are reluctant to give up.


**Why choose a cash-out refinance:**


With a cash-out refinance, you replace your current mortgage with a new, larger loan and receive the difference in cash. When you do so, you’re also moving your entire loan to a new interest rate based on current rates, Ott said. That move can gain you a lower interest rate when interest rates are low, but when interest rates are high, you’re swapping a lower-rate mortgage for a higher-rate one.


Because of this, cash-out refinances are typically used in more specific situations, like consolidating large, high-interest debt balances to reduce a customer's monthly debt obligation. They could also use the money for home remodeling or a combination of both.


Unlike a HELOC, where payments adjust based on usage, a cash-out refinance requires payments on the full loan amount from the start.


**What to consider before using your equity**


Before tapping into your home’s equity, it’s important to understand the tradeoffs and how much equity you have available. This[online calculator](https://www.bokfinancial.com/resources/calculators/home-loans/how-much-can-i-borrow-from-my-home-equity) can help you see how much you can borrow. Here are some factors that you should consider, according to Ott:


**1. Your existing mortgage**
If you have a low-rate mortgage, preserving it can be a major advantage.


**2. Your financial goals**
Your goals should lead the decision—and the discussion with your mortgage banker. “Most homeowners already know whether they want to stay or move,” Ott said. “What they need help with is how to finance it.”


**3. The costs and structure of each financing option**
HELOCs typically have lower upfront costs and simpler appraisals, while cash-out refinances often involve full appraisals and higher closing costs, Ott said. Keeping these differences in mind can help you choose whether a HELOC or a cash-out refinance is better for you, and it also can help you know what to expect from the process.


**4. The time horizon**
Even with those differences in cost and structure, both options usually take about 30 to 45 days from application to closing, depending on the documentation available and the complexity, Ott said.


**Questions to ask your lender**


If you’re weighing your options, come prepared with questions such as:


- What will my rate be—and can it change?
- How much equity can I access?
- What are the upfront costs and fees?
- When do payments start, and how are they calculated?
- What flexibility do I have if my needs change?


**The bottom line**


For many homeowners, the “list-it-or-fix-it” decision isn’t just about lifestyle; it’s about math.


However, the answer to that question—and, if you choose to fix it, the financing option that’s right for you—is a personal decision.


“There’s no one-size-fits-all answer,” Ott said. “It comes down to what you need—and what makes the most sense for your situation.”
