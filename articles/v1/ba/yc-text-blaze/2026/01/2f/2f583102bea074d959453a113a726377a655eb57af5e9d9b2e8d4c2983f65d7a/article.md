## GPA FAQ


### How to Calculate GPA


Calculating your GPA is straightforward once you break it into steps. These steps work for both semester GPAs and cumulative GPAs.


Here’s how to calculate GPA:


1.


**List each class** – Write down every course, the grade you earned, and the number of credits it’s worth.


2.


**Convert grades to numbers** – Change each letter grade into its numerical value using your school’s GPA scale.


3.


**Multiply grades by credits** – Multiply each numerical grade by the number of credits for that class to get your grade points.


4.


**Add all grade points** – Combine the grade points from all your classes.


5.


**Total your credits** – Add the number of credits you attempted during the grading period.


6.


**Divide to find your GPA** – Divide your total grade points by your total credits to calculate your GPA.


##### Example:


If you took three classes worth 3 credits each and earned an A (4.0), B (3.0), and A (4.0), you would multiply each grade by 3 credits, add the totals, and then divide by 9 credits. Your GPA would be **3.67** .


### What is a Good GPA?


Truthfully, a good GPA depends on your goals, your school, and the requirements for your major. Many colleges view anything around a 3.0 as solid, but competitive programs often expect higher.


> Though, in general, a 3.0 GPA is universally considered "good."


Some careers and scholarships also have GPA minimums you need to meet. Think about what you want after graduation to understand the GPA you should aim for. Tracking your grades each term helps you maintain the target you set.


### How Do Weighted Grades Impact GPA?


Weighted grades give extra value to advanced courses such as honors or AP classes. When a grade is weighted, an A might count for more than 4.0, which can raise your overall GPA.


This system rewards students for taking more difficult coursework. Schools use different weighting scales, so check how yours calculates them. Understanding the weighting helps you see how each class affects your total GPA.
