## What Makes a Good Resume?


A good resume needs to be clear, concise, and optimized for employers and applicant tracking systems. It should highlight key information in an easy-to-read format while emphasizing relevant skills and achievements.


Here are a few things to focus on:


-


**Clarity and Conciseness** - Keep information clear and to the point, avoiding unnecessary details.


-


**Formatting** - Use consistent fonts, proper spacing, and clear section headings for readability.


-


**Action-Oriented Language** - Focus on achievements with strong action verbs rather than listing job duties.


-


**Keyword Optimization** - Include relevant keywords from job descriptions to pass applicant tracking systems (ATS).


-


**Professional Summary** - Add a brief summary at the top highlighting key skills and experience.


-


**Relevance** - Avoid unnecessary details and keep content focused on the job being applied for.


A well-crafted resume increases the chances of grabbing an employer’s attention quickly. Ensuring proper structure, language, and optimization makes a resume more effective in job applications.

## How to Make a Resume: 6 Things to Make Sure You Include


A well-structured resume makes it easier for recruiters to see qualifications at a glance. Each section should highlight relevant information in a clear and organized manner.


Including the right details increases the chances of passing applicant tracking systems (ATS) and grabbing the attention of hiring managers.


Here are the key sections every resume should have, along with tips for making each one effective.


#### Contact Information


The contact section should be easy to find at the top of the resume. Include a full name, phone number, professional email address, and LinkedIn profile.


Ensure all information is up to date and avoid using unprofessional email addresses. Some job seekers also add a portfolio link if applying for roles in design, writing, or development.


A physical address is usually not necessary unless specifically requested by the employer.


#### Professional Summary


A professional summary provides a quick overview of key skills, experience, and career goals. It should be 2–3 sentences long and placed at the top of the resume.


This section helps recruiters quickly understand what a candidate brings to the table.


> Use strong action words and focus on measurable achievements rather than vague descriptions. Keep it concise and tailored to the job to make it more impactful.


#### Work Experience


The work experience section is one of the most important parts of a resume. List job titles, company names, locations, and employment dates in reverse chronological order.


Use bullet points to describe responsibilities and achievements, starting each point with a strong action verb. Whenever possible, include measurable results to demonstrate impact (e.g., “Increased sales by 20% in six months”).


Avoid generic descriptions and focus on relevant experiences that align with the job being applied for.


#### Skills Section


The skills section should highlight both technical and soft skills relevant to the position. Organize skills into categories if possible, such as “Technical Skills” and “Soft Skills.”


> Using industry-specific keywords increases the chances of passing ATS filters.


Keep the list well-structured and avoid including skills that do not add value to the application. Focus on skills that align with the job description and demonstrate expertise.


#### Education


Education details should include degrees earned, universities attended, and graduation dates. If relevant, mention coursework, honors, or certifications that add value to the job application.


Recent graduates with little work experience can expand this section to highlight academic achievements.


If work experience is strong, keep this section brief and to the point. Only include GPA if it is high and relevant to the job.


#### Additional Sections


Depending on the industry, adding sections such as certifications, volunteer work, and languages can strengthen a resume.


Certifications demonstrate expertise in a field and can make a candidate more competitive. Volunteer work can be valuable if it showcases leadership, teamwork, or industry-related experience.


Listing languages is beneficial, especially for roles that require bilingual or multilingual skills. Only include additional sections if they add meaningful value to the application.
