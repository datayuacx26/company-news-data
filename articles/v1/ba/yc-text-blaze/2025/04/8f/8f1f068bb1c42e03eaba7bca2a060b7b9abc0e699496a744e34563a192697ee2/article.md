## What Are APA Citations?


APA citation is a method of referencing sources in academic writing, commonly used in the social sciences. When you use another person's work in your own, be it a direct quote or an idea, you need to give credit to the original author.


This recognition is the 'citation' - a breadcrumb trail that allows your readers to trace the origins of your argument or data.


The APA style provides a uniform framework for consistency in presenting the list of works cited, the bibliography, or the references.


Its guidelines standardize the format, punctuation, and order of information presented, offering an organized and streamlined look to your academic paper.


## When Do You Use APA Citations?


APA citations are typically employed in papers related to psychology, education, and other social sciences. Yet, their use extends beyond these subjects due to their comprehensive structure and ease of use. It's critical to use APA citations whenever you:


- Quote directly from a source.
- Paraphrase another person's ideas or work.
- Refer to a theory, data, or research conducted by someone else.
- Use images, diagrams, or graphs from another source.


By doing so, you not only give the credit where it's due, but also protect yourself from allegations of[plagiarism](https://www.ox.ac.uk/students/academic/guidance/skills/plagiarism) - a serious offense in the academic world.

## What Is the Format for an APA Citation 7th Edition?


A[general APA citation format](https://owl.purdue.edu/owl/research_and_citation/apa_style/apa_formatting_and_style_guide/general_format.html) follows the author-date method. This means that the author's last name and the year of publication for the source should appear in the text. For example, (Smith, 2020).


In the reference list at the end of your document, the citation should include:


- Author's Last name, Initial(s). (Year of Publication). *Title of the work* . Source.


Here's an example of a book citation:


> Smith, J. (2020). *The Title of the Book* . Publishing Company.


For an article from a periodical or journal, the citation will look like this:


> Smith, J. (2020). Title of the article. *Title of Periodical, volume number* (issue number), pages. DOI or URL (if online).
