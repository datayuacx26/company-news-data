# 3 Best Recommendation Letters For Employee From Manager

Recommendation letters can have a significant impact on hiring. Having someone in your corner to back up your abilities and skills is invaluable.


> In fact,[according to Goshen College](https://www.goshen.edu/careers/preparation/letters-of-recommendation/) , references are often the determining factor when it comes to the final phase of the hiring process.


However, writing a compelling recommendation letter can be difficult. What should you say? How should you say it without sounding too biased?


Finding a balance between showing why the candidate is great and illustrating the work they have done is crucial for a quality recommendation letter.


In this article, we'll give you some tips on how to write a great recommendation letter, and even provide some templates to help you get started.


## How To Write A Good Recommendation Letter For Your Employees


Writing a good recommendation letter is about finding a balance between your relationship with them, what they did at work, and how it impacted your company.


What you should include in a recommendation letter ultimately depends on:


- who you're writing it for
- the company you work for
- the type of industry you work in


Despite this, there are some general things all recommendation letters should include. Read on to learn about these and some other tips you should keep in mind.


#### What to Include


To write a good recommendation letter for your employee, make sure you add the following information within your letter:


- The **role** the employee held.
- The **duration of employment** (how long they worked for you).
- **Key responsibilities** within their role.
- **Specific attributes** or **notable skills** .
- Your **contact information** , in case a follow-up is required.


#### Tips to Keep in Mind


Finally, if you are writing a recommendation letter to an employee from a manager, here a few tips to keep in mind:


-


Make sure to **personalize the recommendation letter** . Stories of your work with the employee work well.


-


**Find out about the role** your employee is applying for. That information may help you to write relevant points about their suitability for that role.


-


**Keep it concise** . One page should usually be sufficient while sharing necessary and relevant information about the employee.


-


**Include any noteworthy successes** or key performance indicators (KPIs) the employee achieved.


-


**Be fair and balanced** in your recommendation. Mention how the employee may have worked to address any areas identified for improvement.


-


The salutation **“[To whom it may concern](https://blaze.today/blog/to-whom-it-may-concern/) ”** is acceptable if no specific recipient name is known.
