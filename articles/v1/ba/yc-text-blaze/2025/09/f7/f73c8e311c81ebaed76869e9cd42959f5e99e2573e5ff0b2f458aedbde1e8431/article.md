## How Can AI Tools Help With Research?


AI summarizers and research assistants can make reading and writing tasks less stressful. Here are a few ways they actually help during the research process:


#### Summarize Articles Quickly


[AI research assistants](https://blaze.today/blog/best-ai-research-assistants/) can scan lengthy research articles and pull out the most important points. This saves time when you’re reviewing multiple sources.


> By summarizing articles with AI, you get a snapshot of the paper without needing to read everything word-for-word.


#### Extract Key Concepts


Instead of hunting for main ideas, AI can[proofread](https://blaze.today/blog/ai-proofreaders/) and highlight them for you. These tools identify key arguments, findings, and relevant quotes.


This can be useful when you're outlining a paper or preparing for a discussion.


#### Save Time on Note-Taking


Many[AI research tools](https://blaze.today/blog/ai-tools-for-research) can turn full articles into simple outlines or lists of takeaways.


This reduces how much manual typing and organizing you have to do. It keeps your research more structured from the start.


#### Discover Related Sources


Some AI tools suggest additional articles, studies, or authors connected to your topic.


This can help you build a stronger bibliography or deepen your understanding of a subject. It’s especially helpful during the early stages of writing.

### 2. TLDR This


[TLDR This](https://www.tldrthis.com/) is designed for fast summarization of online articles and web content. You enter a URL or paste the text, and the tool returns a clean summary with main ideas highlighted.


[Tools like TLDR This](https://blaze.today/blog/tldr-this-alternatives) are simple to use and works well for students skimming through reading material. While some don’t support PDFs, they're good at handling short-to-medium-length research online.


### 3. SciSpace


[SciSpace AI](https://scispace.com/) focuses on making academic papers easier to read by simplifying technical language and offering explanations. You upload a[research PDF](https://blaze.today/blog/ai-pdf-summarizer) and can ask follow-up questions on any paragraph.


It’s built to support understanding rather than just shorten the content. Students often use it to read journal articles and prepare for[academic writing like essays](https://blaze.today/blog/best-essay-writing-ai-tools/) .


### 4. Consensus AI


[Consensus AI](https://consensus.app/) answers research questions by summarizing findings from multiple academic papers. You ask a question, and the tool shows what the existing research says, pulled from real published studies.


This approach is helpful when you're trying to find evidence or define your stance on a topic. It speeds up early-stage research and helps identify gaps in the literature.


### 5. Unriddle AI (Anara)


[Unriddle AI](https://anara.so/) simplifies dense academic material by providing structured summaries and key highlights. It allows you to upload papers and extract the main points without reading every section.


The tool also identifies complex terms and offers context to help with comprehension.[Sites like Unriddle](https://blaze.today/blog/unriddle-ai-alternatives/) are useful when you’re reviewing new topics or handling unfamiliar subjects.

### 6. Scite AI


[Scite AI](https://scite.ai/) tracks how research papers are cited and whether they’re supported or challenged by others. It’s less about summarizing content and more about assessing credibility and influence.


This helps students choose sources that are reliable and relevant to their work. It’s especially helpful for building citations and understanding scholarly impact.


### 7. Elicit AI


[Elicit AI](https://elicit.com/) helps streamline research by finding relevant studies and summarizing them around a specific question. It breaks down each paper into its methods, findings, and takeaways.


You can compare multiple sources at once to build your argument or understand trends. It's useful for literature reviews or when you're looking for specific data from academic research.
