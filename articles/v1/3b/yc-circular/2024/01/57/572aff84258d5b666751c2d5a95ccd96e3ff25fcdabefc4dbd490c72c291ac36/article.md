# From outback landscapes to urban jungles: How your tech choices impact Aussie nature

In Australia, our love for technology is as vast as our diverse landscapes. While we enjoy the latest gadgets, it's crucial to understand how our tech choices shape not only our lives but also the environment around us; from outback landscapes like Uluru, to the Great Barrier Reef all the way to our bustling urban centers.


### The tech-environment connection


Though Australia may not be a primary site for resource extraction for electronics, the global nature of supply chains means our consumption choices have far-reaching effects. These processes can lead to habitat destruction and pollution in distant lands, subsequently impacting global biodiversity and climate - issues that know no borders.


### Tackling E-waste: A local and global challenge


With e-waste being one of Australia's fastest-growing waste categories, the urgency to address this issue is clear. The[National Waste Report 2020](https://www.dcceew.gov.au/environment/protection/waste/national-waste-reports/2020?ref=blog.nowcircular.com.au) revealed that Australians generate approximately 539,000 tonnes of e-waste annually, with a minimal percentage being recycled. This not only poses a local environmental challenge but also contributes to a larger global crisis.


> ...In 2021 only[17.4 percent of e-waste was effectively recycled](https://www.abc.net.au/news/science/2021-10-14/e-waste-electronics-landfill-gold-landfill-recycling/100524744?ref=blog.nowcircular.com.au#:~:text=E%2Dwaste%20is%20expected%20to,net%2Dzero%20emissions%2C%20experts%20say) in Australia, despite it containing gold, silver, valuable glass and rare earth elements...
