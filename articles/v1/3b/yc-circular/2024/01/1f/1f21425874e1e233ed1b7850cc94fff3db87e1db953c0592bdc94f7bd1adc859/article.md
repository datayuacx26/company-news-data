# How to: Make more sustainable holiday choices and travel responsibly

From over-tourism and carbon emissions to cultural exploitation and animal cruelty, traditional tourism practices can lead to negative social and environmental consequences. Fortunately, there is a growing movement towards sustainable tourism that seeks to minimize these impacts while maximizing the benefits for all involved. In this blog post, we will explore what sustainable tourism is all about, why it matters, and how you can embrace it on your next adventure.


**What is sustainable tourism?**


[Sustainable tourism](https://sdgs.un.org/topics/sustainable-tourism?ref=blog.nowcircular.com.au) or eco-tourism is a form of travel that prioritizes responsible and ethical practices to minimize negative impacts on the environment, cultural heritage, and local communities, while maximizing economic and social benefits. It involves planning and managing trips in ways that conserve natural resources, protect biodiversity, respect local cultures, promote social equity, and support sustainable livelihoods. Sustainable tourism can take many forms, from staying at eco-lodges and eating local cuisine to engaging in volunteer work and learning about conservation initiatives.


**Why does sustainable tourism matter?**


Sustainable tourism matters for many[reasons](https://www.unwto.org/tourism4sdgs?ref=blog.nowcircular.com.au) . Firstly, it helps to protect the natural beauty and biodiversity of our planet by reducing pollution, waste, and habitat destruction. It also conserves cultural heritage and respects the rights and dignity of local communities. Secondly, it can provide economic benefits to those in the tourism industry, promote local entrepreneurship, and help create sustainable jobs and livelihoods. Finally, it can enhance the personal satisfaction and well-being of travelers by creating authentic, meaningful, and transformative experiences.


**How can you embrace sustainable tourism?**


There are many ways to embrace sustainable tourism, regardless of your budget, destination, or style of travel. Here are some simple to get you started:


♻️


****Do your research:**** Before choosing a destination or a tour operator, do your homework to find out about the local environmental, social, and cultural issues, and how tourism can impact them. Look for eco-certifications, sustainability policies, and reviews from other travelers who share your values.


♻️


****Choose sustainable accommodations:**** Look for eco-lodges, guesthouses, and hotels that have a proven track record of sustainability practices, such as renewable energy, water conservation, recycling, and local sourcing. Consider staying at home-stays or family-owned properties to support the local economy.


♻️


****Reduce your carbon footprint:**** Opt for low-carbon transportation options, such as public transit, biking, or walking. If you have to fly, choose airlines that have a carbon offset program, or offset your own carbon emissions through reputable organizations.


♻️


****Respect the local culture and environment:**** Learn about the local customs, traditions, and beliefs before you arrive, and behave respectfully and responsibly. Respect wildlife, avoid animal entertainment or exploitation, and don't litter or vandalize natural sites.


♻️


****Support sustainable tourism initiatives:**** Look for opportunities to engage in sustainable tourism activities, such as volunteering, learning about conservation projects, or supporting local artisans and farmers. Avoid mass tourism attractions that exploit animals or promote cultural stereotypes.


By embracing sustainable tourism practices, we can make a positive impact on our planet and its people while creating unforgettable and meaningful experiences. While it might require some research, planning, and flexibility, the joys and rewards of sustainable travel are well worth the effort. So, the next time you plan your adventure, think beyond the usual tourist traps and consider a more sustainable and responsible way of exploring the world. After all, sustainable tourism is not only good for the planet, but also good for the soul.
