# When Do Sales Tax Exemption Certificates Expire? State-by-State Guide

In the United States, sellers are often obligated to collect sales tax on taxable items. In some cases, though, a buyer will be exempt from paying sales tax, either because the buyer is a qualifying nonprofit or government agency, or because the buyer is a reseller or manufacturer.


If your company sells to exempt buyers, you can't simply stop charging tax without proper documentation. **You must have a sales tax exemption certificate on file that certifies the transaction qualifies for an exemption from the normal sales tax due.**


Unfortunately, some companies accept an exemption certificate once, keep it on file, and assume they've fulfilled their obligations, but that's not necessarily the case.


Exemption certificates can become invalid or no longer apply. They can also expire, although the **rules for when this happens vary by state** . If you have certificates on file that are no longer valid and you're audited, you are responsible for uncollected tax, interest, and penalties.


This guide provides a state-by-state overview of when sales tax exemption certificates expire. It covers all types of certificates, including resale certificates which are often subject to different rules. You'll also learn what could invalidate a certificate and how to handle invalid certificates.

## When do sales tax exemption certificates expire? State-by-state breakdown


While each state establishes its own rules for[sales tax exemption certificates](https://www.numeral.com/blog/sales-tax-exemption-certificates) , most states fall into one of three broad categories regarding certificate expiration.


- **No expiration:** An exemption certificate remains valid indefinitely as long as the buyer's situation doesn't change and the information on the certificate remains the same.
- **Fixed expiration:** The certificate expires after a set period of time, such as 12 months.
- **Activity-based expiration:** The certificate remains valid as long as the buyer makes purchases within a certain period of time (such as 12 months).

### Florida: the annual renewal state to know


Ecommerce sellers must be aware of the state-specific rules for when certificates expire. Florida, in particular, can create complications because it requires sellers to collect an updated exemption certificate from buyers every single year.


As the[Florida Department of Revenue](https://floridarevenue.com/taxes/taxesfees/Pages/annual_resale_certificate_sut.aspx) explains, annual resale certificates expire on December 31 each year. While Florida automatically issues a new Annual Resale Certificate for Sales Tax and the certificate number remains the same, the expiration date is updated each year.


If sellers don't collect updated certificates from Florida buyers each year, they could be exposed to liability for uncollected sales tax in the event of an audit. Florida law makes dealers liable for all uncollected tax owed on transactions if there is no proper exemption documentation on file.

## What can make an exemption certificate invalid before it expires?


Regardless of whether a state sets an expiration date for an exemption certificate or not, a certificate can become invalid in certain circumstances. This can occur if the buyer:


- Changes their business name
- Changes their address
- Has their sales tax registration or permit number revoked
- Is no longer in a qualifying business
- Begins using purchased items in a manner inconsistent with the exemption claimed


Tracking changes that buyers experience can be very difficult for ecommerce sellers, especially those with a large number of exempt customers. Many businesses rely on their buyers to alert them to these changes, but buyers don't always follow through on this task.


Ultimately, it remains the seller's responsibility to ensure certificates are valid, so your company needs a process for re-collecting certificates any time a buyer's business information or purpose changes. You can't assume you're in compliance just because a certificate hasn't expired.


If an exemption certificate becomes invalid and the seller cannot demonstrate good-faith acceptance of the certificate, the seller may be held liable for uncollected sales tax.

## What happens if you accept an expired certificate?


State laws put the burden on the seller to ensure exemption certificates are valid before allowing a buyer to make a purchase without paying sales tax. This means confirming the information is complete and accurate, that the buyer is exempt, and that the certificate isn't expired.


A seller *can* fulfill this obligation even if the seller accepts an invalid certificate *if* the seller acts in good faith. But there are specific requirements to fulfill to do that. For example, as the Georgia Department of Revenue explains in[O.C.G.A. § 48-8-38(a)](https://dor.georgia.gov/taxes/business-taxes/sales-use-tax/nontaxable-sales) , you act in good faith *only if:*


- The certificate is fully completed with all required information from the taxpayer, including their signature
- The certificate is in a form that is appropriate based on the type of exemption claimed
- The exemption claimed was allowed by law at the time of the transaction in the location where the transaction was sourced
- The exemption applies to the item being purchased
- The exemption is reasonable given the nature of the buyer's business.


If you don't have a valid certificate on file and can't prove you acted in good faith, you become liable for uncollected sales tax. For example, the[New York Department of Taxation and Finance](https://www.tax.ny.gov/pubs_and_bulls/tg_bulletins/st/exemption_certificates_for_sales_tax.htm) explains:


> "You must exercise ordinary care when accepting a certificate. You could be held liable for the sales tax you didn’t collect if you knew that the purchase was not for an exempt purpose, or you knew that the certificate was false or fraudulent."


You could also owe interest and penalties on the uncollected tax. Since you generally cannot go back and collect the uncollected tax, this entire bill is your company's responsibility.


This can also happen if you have an expired certificate on file, even if it was otherwise valid, as an expired certificate provides no protection. To avoid significant legal liability, if a buyer presents an invalid or expired certificate, you should either:


- Charge sales tax on the transaction until a valid certificate has been provided.
- Request an updated certificate before fulfilling the order.

## Sales tax exemption certificate expiration FAQs


Still need to know more? Here are the answers to some frequently asked questions about expiring exemption certificates.


### When do sales tax exemption certificates expire?


Different states set different rules for when sales tax exemption certificates expire. There are three broad categories:


- States with no fixed expiration, like Minnesota and Texas
- States with a fixed expiration date, including Alabama and Florida
- States where expirations are activity-based, such as Kansas


### When do resale certificates expire?


Resale certificates generally expire at the same time as other sales tax exemption certificates, although there are a few states that set different rules for when resale vs. nonprofit certificates expire.


In some cases, resale certificates do not expire at all, but in other states, they may be valid only for a limited period, such as four or five years.


You can learn more about[verifying resale certificates here](https://www.numeral.com/blog/how-to-verify-resale-certificates-state-by-state) .


### What happens if I accept an expired exemption certificate from a buyer?


If you accept an expired exemption certificate and you cannot prove you acted in good faith when doing so, your company can become liable for the uncollected tax plus penalties and interest.


You should not accept an expired exemption certificate. Instead, pause the order until a valid certificate is on file or charge sales tax on the transaction.


### Does a resale certificate expire in California?


In California, resale certificates do not have a fixed expiration date. A blanket exemption can be good indefinitely as long as the business information does not change. California requires sellers to keep exemption certificates on file for four years from the time of the sale.


### Can an exemption certificate be invalid even if it hasn't expired?


An exemption certificate can be invalid even if it does not expire. It could be invalid from the start if it didn't contain all of the required details or was not valid for the type of purchase.


It could also become invalid if the buyer no longer qualifies for the exemption or if the buyer's details change, such as their business name, address, or permit number.


### How long should businesses keep exemption certificate records?


The rules for how long a business should keep exemption certificate records can vary by state. In most states, you should keep these records for three to four years. However, the exact timeline depends on the typical sales tax audit statute of limitations.
