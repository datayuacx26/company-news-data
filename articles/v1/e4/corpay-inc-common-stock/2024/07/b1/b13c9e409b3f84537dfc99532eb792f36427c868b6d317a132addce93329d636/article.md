The demand for **cross border payments** has expanded rapidly. Digital-first providers, banks, fintechs, neobanks, and non-bank financial institutions now compete to deliver scalable **international payments solutions** . Arguably, more change has occurred in global payments—and in global business processes—in the past five years than in the preceding fifteen.


For finance and treasury teams, this is no longer just about understanding how global payments work. It’s about choosing the right provider, one that aligns with your compliance requirements, growth plans, risk tolerance, and operational complexity.


Cross-border payments are no longer just an operational task; they are a strategic lever. The right provider acts as core infrastructure, enabling businesses to expand into new markets, work with a broader network of suppliers and partners, reduce friction, and gain greater visibility into payment flows and costs — all of which contribute to a stronger competitive edge.


Selecting the wrong partner can expose your business to hidden costs, operational delays, compliance failures, and unnecessary **cross border payment risks** .


And selecting the right one can strengthen control, visibility, and long-term scalability.


This article is designed to be your practical guide – checklists, questions to ask vendors, evaluation criteria, and red flags.


Due Diligence: ‘Know Your Provider’


Just as you would vet a potential supplier or vendor, and as service providers conduct KYC checks on clients, you should conduct a ‘KYP’ review when evaluating **cross border payment solutions** .


## Quick checklist: Licensing & regulatory coverage


-


Is the provider appropriately licensed in relevant jurisdictions?


-


Do they have a robust payments infrastructure and operate directly, or rely heavily on intermediaries?


-


Are they regulated as a bank, NBFI, or Money Services Business?


-


What is their track record with regulators?


Providers operating through multiple intermediaries may introduce:


-


Additional transaction fees


-


Slower settlement times


-


Reduced transparency


**Tip** : If your business operates in emerging or high-risk markets, verify the provider’s local expertise and licensing footprint.

## International Payment Compliance & Risk Management


**International payment compliance** is not static. Regulations evolve, enforcement tightens, and cross-border scrutiny increases.


A credible provider should demonstrate:


-


Robust Anti-Money Laundering (AML) and Counter-Terrorism Financing (CTF) frameworks


-


Thorough sanctions screening capabilities


-


Clear handling protocols for restricted or high-risk industries


-


Ongoing monitoring and reporting infrastructure


Questions to Ask Potential Providers


-


How do you manage regulatory changes across jurisdictions?


-


What happens if a payment is flagged or frozen?


-


What percentage of transactions typically require manual intervention?


-


Have you faced regulatory penalties in the past five years?


Cross-border transactions inherently carry compliance exposure. Your chosen provider should reduce that risk, not amplify it.


## Transparency in Pricing and FX Markups


When comparing **cross-border payments companies** , pricing transparency is critical and understanding pricing structures is paramount.


The FX markets move constantly, around the clock. Exchange rates displayed publicly are interbank rates, and they are typically delayed by a few minutes.


Most providers apply a ‘spread’ or a ‘margin’ to those rates, which can vary depending on the volume and frequency of the business’ payments. Some charge:


-


FX conversion margins


-


Transaction fees


-


Intermediary bank (lifting) fees


-


Account maintenance charges


-


Minimum balance requirements


-


Minimum trading requirements


**Evaluation criteria**


-


Is the FX spread clearly disclosed?


-


Are wire and settlement fees itemized?


-


Can you conduct a formal FX performance review?


-


Is pricing scalable as volume increases?


Hidden pricing structures often become apparent only after onboarding. Demand clarity upfront.


## Technology & Global Payment Platform Capabilities


A competitive **global payment platform** should support:


-


Multi-currency wallets


-


Batch payments


-


API integrations


-


ERP connectivity


-


Real-time reporting


-


Role-based permissions


At the same time, avoid overpaying for complexity you do not need.


**Ask:**


-


Is the platform scalable as we expand into new markets?


-


What integrations are native vs customizable?


-


What is the average onboarding timeline?


-


How frequently are system upgrades deployed?


Technology should enable operational efficiency - not create dependency risk. That’s the goal, isn’t it?


## Security & Client Fund Protection


Cyber risk and operational resilience are central when assessing **cross border payment risks** .


Key considerations:


-


How are client funds safeguarded?


-


Are client funds segregated?


-


Are data centres independently audited?


-


Are failover and disaster recovery systems in place?


-


What fraud detection controls exist?


A provider’s internal controls can directly affect your exposure.


## Market Stability & Risk Profile


Recent years have seen bank collapses, fintech shutdowns, regulatory fines, and volatility in adjacent sectors such as cryptocurrency.


When evaluating international payments solutions:


-


Review financial stability and parent company backing


-


Understand exposure to high-risk sectors


-


Ask about capital reserves and liquidity management


-


Assess operational continuity planning


If a provider’s business model depends heavily on volatile sectors, your risk profile may increase accordingly.


## Red Flags When Choosing Cross Border Payment Solutions Provider


Be cautious if you encounter:


-


Pricing that seems materially better than competitors’ pricing


-


Lack of clarity around licensing


-


Heavy reliance on third-party intermediaries


-


No clear escalation process for payment delays and errors


-


Vague answers about compliance frameworks


-


Inability or reluctance to provide client references


If it sounds too good to be true, it often is.


## Shortlisting Cross Border Payments Companies: A Final Decision Checklist


Research is first. Once you’ve done that, here is a quick reference guide to help you choose. Before signing, confirm:


-


Clear and competitive pricing structure


-


Strong international payment compliance controls


-


Scalable global payment platform


-


Transparent FX policy


-


Robust fraud prevention and security frameworks


-


Direct licensing where needed


-


Demonstrated operational resilience


-


Cultural and service alignment with your team


If you don’t understand a given product’s pros and cons, or the risks it might entail, it would probably be wise to take a beat before diving in head-first. Ask your questions and do your due diligence.


In sum, choosing a provider can be a bit like dating in the digital age. Take some time for a chemistry check, to ensure you and your potential partner understand each other and can exchange ideas for your business growth. Sharing your hopes and dreams for your business can signal whether you have a future together. It is your business—and your money after all. Trust is key.


If everything checks out, then, you may be ready to go ahead and swipe right.
