# Will CLO performance and leveraged finance trends diverge or align in 2026?

**Exploring how the resilience of collateralized loan obligations amid shifting leveraged finance dynamics set the stage for the coming year. [Leveraged Finance Outlook 2026](https://www.moodys.com/research/Leveraged-Finance-Global-2026-Outlook-Issuance-competition-to-grow-Outlook--PBC_1459974?cid=web-wbssts-19483)**


US and EMEA leveraged finance markets are set for a strong 2026 rebound, driven by rising M&A, LBO activity, and elevated refinancing. Competition between BSL lenders and private credit will intensify as borrowers seek flexible options, while private equity deploys record dry powder, fueling deal flow in tech and healthcare. Mega deals will favor hybrid financing, and CLO demand will support opportunistic refinancings.


Risks remain involving tariff shifts, inflation, geopolitics, hidden leverage, and weak documentation that could undermine stability. Emerging AI-related credit concerns add to long-term vulnerabilities, keeping investor sentiment fragile despite improving issuance momentum.


Key highlights:


- **Deal Activity Will Increase as Companies Shift Between BSL and Private Credit.** Borrowers are increasingly shopping between BSL and private credit markets, leveraging competitive pricing and flexible terms. Prospective rate cuts could accelerate BSL takeouts of private credit, while refinancings remain strong, supported by CLO demand and opportunistic issuance. Expect heightened risk as hidden leverage becomes more prevalent through off-balance sheet structures and NAV lending.
- **Private Equity Poised to Reignite LBO & M&A Activity.** Private equity sponsors face mounting pressure to deploy record dry powder as investment periods mature and valuation gaps narrow. Lower financing costs and stabilizing rates will unlock new deal flow, particularly in noncyclical sectors like technology, healthcare, and business services. Mega deals will increasingly rely on hybrid financing, combining syndicated loans and private credit. Even if traditional LBO volumes remain subdued, sponsor-to-sponsor transactions, dividend recapitalizations, and strategic add-ons will sustain overall activity.
- **Tariff Shifts, Inflation, and Geopolitics Remain the Main Downside Risks** . Despite constructive credit conditions, global trade policy uncertainty, inflationary pressures, and geopolitical tensions could disrupt base-case projections. Low-rated issuers remain most vulnerable to refinancing challenges and sector-specific shocks.

[CLO 2026 Outlook](https://www.moodys.com/research/CLOs-Global-2026-Outlook-Low-rates-buttress-performance-amid-Outlook--PBS_1460853?cid=web-wbssts-19483)


Declining interest rates will strengthen CLO performance in 2026, as speculative-grade defaults fall sharply in the US and EMEA. US defaults are projected to drop to 3.0% by October 2026 from 5.3% a year earlier, while Europe improves to 2.4% from 3.8%. Lower funding costs and active refinancing will support this trend, alongside rising LBO activity that could spur new CLO issuance. However, competition for quality assets between private credit and syndicated lenders will intensify, heightening structural risks such as looser covenants and PIK features. Tight spreads and limited rating changes will restrict managers’ trading flexibility, leaving little room for collateral quality adjustments.


Key Highlights:


-


**US CLOs: Defaults will decline along with funding costs.** US CLO collateral defaults will decline in 2026 as lower interest rates and extended maturities improve borrower liquidity. Fed rate cuts will reduce funding costs, supporting leveraged issuers and middle-market borrowers, though these remain more vulnerable than broadly syndicated loans. Competition with private credit will weaken covenants and add leverage risks, but CLO structures will help absorb defaults. Still, tight loan spreads will limit managers’ ability to optimize portfolios, making credit monitoring essential.


-


**EMEA CLOs: High liquidity will keep defaults down.** Strong liquidity and investor demand will keep defaults low and issuance high, with 2026 volumes near 2025’s €55 billion record. Syndicated and direct lenders will offer ample refinancing options, but at the cost of looser terms and weaker collateral. Private credit CLOs will remain niche, while broadly syndicated structures dominate. Robust refinancing and repricing will persist, though tight spreads will limit managers’ flexibility. The key challenge: balancing issuance growth with credit discipline amid rising structural risks.
