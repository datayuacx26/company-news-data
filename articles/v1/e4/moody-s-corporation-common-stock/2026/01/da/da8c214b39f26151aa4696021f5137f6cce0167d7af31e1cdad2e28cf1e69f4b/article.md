# How will the 2026 digital economy be impacted by changes in artificial intelligence, digital finance, cyber risk, and data centers?

###


**Look at how the rapid developments in AI, the expansion of digital finance, escalating cyber threats, and surging demands for data center capacity are set to impact 2026 credit conditions.**


[Artificial Intelligence Outlook 2026](https://www.moodys.com/research/doc--PBC_1463363?cid=web-wbssts-19483) ****


Artificial intelligence surged in 2025, with major breakthroughs in model capability, rapid expansion of global AI infrastructure, and increasing enterprise adoption. But this progress comes with drawbacks including infrastructure shortages, regulatory fragmentation, geopolitical pressure, and uneven productivity gains are creating a landscape where AI’s benefits are significant, but unevenly distributed.


Key Highlights:


- **Rapid innovation intensifying competition across models:** Leading models in the US (GPT‑5, Claude 4.5, Gemini 3) set new standards in reasoning, multimodality, and autonomous task execution. Open source and Chinese models (Qwen 3, ERNIE 5, Hunyuan) narrowed the performance gap, raising questions about long‑term monetization for proprietary players. We see that providers are shifting from standalone models to integrated enterprise AI tool suites.
- **Uneven gains in productivity:** AI adoption is accelerating, especially in customer-facing and analytical workflows. The largest increases occur in routine, document heavy processes but workflows with more nuances are facing friction. “Winner‑take‑most” dynamics favor big, well‑capitalized firms in industries like consumer goods and financial services.
- **Infrastructure constraints and rising costs:** Explosive demand for compute is outpacing supply, driving massive multi‑billion‑dollar data‑center expansion, straining global power grids, and inflating costs due to GPU shortages. These pressures risk slowing AI adoption for smaller firms and amplifying the resource advantage of hyperscalers and sovereign-backed players.
- **Escalating geopolitical, regulatory, and cyber risks:** Fragmented global regulations, from the EU’s AI Act to China’s tight licensing system, are forcing multinationals to maintain region‑specific AI stacks. At the same time, cyber threats like prompt injection and model poisoning are expanding as AI becomes more embedded in workflows, raising governance, security, and compliance demands.

[Digital Finance Outlook 2026](https://www.moodys.com/research/Digital-Economy-Global-2026-Outlook-Digital-finance-links-diverse-Outlook--PBC_1463256?cid=web-wbssts-19483)


Digital finance in 2026 will be powered by maturing tokenization frameworks, rising institutional use of stablecoins, and increasing convergence across once‑separate financial segments. As blockchain‑based infrastructure becomes more deeply embedded into settlement, collateral, and liquidity management, digital rails are emerging as a foundational layer of global finance. This is set to boost efficiency while introducing new operational, regulatory, and cybersecurity risks that will shape market behavior in the years ahead.


Key Highlights:


- **Shared digital technology is integrating previously distinct areas of finance** : Blockchain rails and shared digital infrastructure are dissolving boundaries between private credit, sustainable finance, and emerging‑market flows. Tokenized instruments are gaining traction, enabling automated servicing and faster settlement. Stablecoins and DeFi platforms are becoming connective tissue across segments, supporting real‑time liquidity movement.
- **Infrastructure development set to determine the transition speed:** Major institutions are continuing to pilot tokenization platforms, blockchain settlement networks, and digital custody systems to streamline issuance and operations. Stablecoins backed by cash and Treasurys are increasingly used in cross‑border payments and repo markets, but the broader shift relies on faster, more scalable blockchain rails, stronger custody layers, and resilient cyber protections.
- **Digital rails may potentially transform cost structures:** Tokenized issuance and programmable settlement reduce manual processing, accelerate liquidity turnover, and lower operational burdens. Real‑time digital cash instruments cut funding and counterparty costs, while coordinated regulatory progress is helping clarify rules around custody, redemption, and disclosures, all crucial for scaling digital finance.
- **Markets will consolidate around efficient, secure, and interoperable infrastructure:** Regulated stablecoins are poised to gain prominence. Platforms that provide strong interoperability with legacy systems will attract deeper liquidity. As institutions embrace smart‑contract‑driven workflows, the line between traditional finance and DeFi will continue to fade, favoring architectures that combine innovation with compliance, scalability, and resilience.

[Cyber Risk Outlook 2026](https://www.moodys.com/research/Cyber-Risk-Global-2026-Outlook-Cyber-threats-will-intensify-Outlook--PBC_1463266?cid=web-wbssts-19483)


AI is making traditional attacks even more sophisticated. As organizations continue embedding AI into operations, agentic systems capable of autonomous multi‑step actions introduce new unpredictability and compound risks. This will require faster detection, stronger governance, and more automated defenses to keep pace with attackers.


- **Ransomware’s grip is loosening for smaller firms but remains severe for large organizations:** Mid‑sized companies have improved detection, backup strategies, and recovery planning, reducing successful encryption rates and limiting operational disruption. Larger enterprises, despite greater investment, remain attractive and vulnerable because of their complex networks and higher ransom‑payment capacity.
- **Cryptocurrency theft highlights major security gaps amid rising institutional adoption:** DeFi platforms and exchanges suffered significant breaches in 2025, driven by coding flaws, manipulated interfaces, and off‑chain security weaknesses. As institutional participation grows, cybersecurity shifts from a differentiator to a minimum requirement for market access, liquidity, and long‑term viability.
- **Cloud outages underscore systemic operational risks:** Recent non-malicious outages at AWS, Azure, and Cloudflare revealed the fragility and interconnectedness of global cloud dependencies. Multi-region deployments and hybrid architectures offer more practical resilience than costly full multi‑cloud strategies but require investment and ongoing management as regulators increasingly scrutinize cloud‑concentration risk.
- **Global efforts to harmonize cybersecurity rules face slow progress:** Companies face an expanding patchwork of overlapping regulations across the EU, U.S., and Asia, creating costly compliance burdens that siphon resources from actual defense. Without stronger coordination, firms will continue to navigate conflicting requirements while attackers exploit gaps faster than governments can close them.

[Data Centers Outlook 2026](https://www.moodys.com/research/Data-Centers-Global-2026-Outlook-Capacity-growth-remains-robust-Outlook--PBC_1460236?cid=web-wbssts-19483)


Data center demand will surge in 2026 as AI and cloud workloads expand, with most new capacity pre‑leased by hyperscalers. This ensures rapid monetization but concentrates risk while development is increasingly shaped by power constraints, regulatory hurdles, rising construction costs, shifting financing structures, and the escalating expense of GPUs.


- **Double‑digit capacity growth continues with rapid monetization:** Global data center capacity is projected to grow sharply—reaching about 600 TWh in 2026—as hyperscalers rapidly energize and absorb new supply. Nearly all major new builds are pre‑leased, driving immediate revenue generation while expanding the AI and cloud infrastructure base. Massive hyperscale sites coming online will accelerate AI model development even as questions about the sustainability of capital intensity grow.
- **Regulatory and power constraints limit development:** Local resistance and strained power grids are slowing new builds, while regions with supportive policies move faster. Reliable power access remains the critical bottleneck, often requiring dedicated or temporary generation.
- **Tenants absorb more risk to speed construction:** To shorten build timelines, tenants are accepting risks they once avoided, even as labor shortages and rising equipment costs drive construction and lease rates higher across key markets.
- **Capital markets evolve as scale increases** : Financing needs are ballooning, drawing in banks, private capital, and institutional lenders. Start‑up AI firms rely heavily on big‑tech credit support, and new financing structures aim to reduce lease‑renewal risk and broaden investor participation.
- **High GPU costs drive demand for alternative financing options:** GPUs—now the largest cost in AI‑focused data centers—require frequent refresh cycles, pushing developers toward nontraditional financing models and GPU‑as‑a‑service options to support escalating compute needs.
