# Artificial intelligence reshapes labor, growth, social risks

Moody’s Ratings recently published a collection of research that examines how artificial intelligence is no longer a distant productivity story, but a force actively reshaping labor markets, economic growth, and social outcomes across the globe. As AI-driven automation and augmentation accelerate, the potential for productivity gains is growing—alongside meaningful labor disruption, fiscal pressures, and social risks that vary widely across economies.


Through data-driven research and comprehensive analysis, the campaign explores how these dynamics will influence productivity, fiscal resilience, and sovereign credit quality, highlighting the decisive role governments’ policy choices will play in shaping outcomes. The analysis underscores why AI’s impact will be profound but uneven across advanced and emerging economies, and why managing labor transitions, social strains, and policy responses will be critical as adoption continues to expand.

##### **AI productivity gains to hinge on demographics and occupational structures**


Recent advances in artificial intelligence are set to reshape global labor markets through the automation and augmentation of tasks, delivering overall productivity gains while displacing some workers. Moody’s estimates average productivity gains from generative AI at around 1.5% annually across a broad set of rated sovereigns, though outcomes will vary widely across and within advanced and emerging economies. The scale of gains will depend on factors including sectoral and occupational workforce composition, technological readiness, demographics, unemployment levels, and labor costs.


Advanced economies are likely to benefit more due to greater exposure to AI, particularly as many face aging populations and weak productivity growth. Emerging markets may experience less labor displacement but still stand to gain as digital infrastructure improves and enabling policies take hold. Ultimately, sovereign credit implications will reflect the balance between productivity gains and the social and fiscal costs associated with labor disruption and workforce transitions.

##### **AI will reshape the nature of labor, with varying social risks across economies**


Artificial intelligence will have profound but uneven effects on labor markets, shaping productivity, growth prospects, and social dynamics across economies. While AI adoption can support economic resilience, it also risks creating or intensifying social strains that influence political stability and long-term fiscal strength. Governments’ ability to manage labor transitions and social protection will be central to determining both the pace of AI adoption and its credit implications.


AI diffusion is likely to alter the role and cost of labor as automation reduces production costs and encourages greater investment in capital and AI-enabling infrastructure. Aging economies may benefit as productivity gains help offset shrinking workforces, but high inequality, limited labor mobility, and gaps in social safety nets could heighten transition risks. Governments with stronger fiscal capacity will be better positioned to mitigate these risks, including through social protection measures and the potential taxation of AI-driven gains.

##### **Vodcast: AI, the future of work, and the risks for governments**


In this vodcast, Moody’s Ratings analysts examine how artificial intelligence is beginning to automate and augment the cognitive tasks at the core of many professions — a shift with far‑reaching implications for workers, companies, and governments alike. While AI has the potential to significantly boost productivity, it also risks disrupting labor markets and widening inequality both across and within economies.


As companies reorganize and investors pour billions into AI models, chips, and data centers, governments face critical decisions on how to minimize social disruption while maximizing economic gains. The discussion explores which jobs are most exposed, what factors could amplify productivity gains, and the policy tools — from reskilling and social protection to fiscal strategies — that will shape how economies navigate the transition and capture AI’s benefits.


##### **AI's fiscal impact will weigh productivity and revenue gains against spending demands**


The adoption of artificial intelligence has the potential to strengthen government finances by supporting productivity-led growth, improving public sector efficiency, and boosting tax revenues. AI-driven digitalization may enhance tax administration and compliance while increasing corporate and wealth tax receipts as economic activity expands. These dynamics create opportunities for governments to improve both revenue generation and spending effectiveness over the medium to long term.


At the same time, AI deployment will bring significant upfront fiscal costs. Governments may face higher public investment needs to upgrade digital and energy infrastructure, alongside increased social spending to support labor reskilling and social protection for displaced workers. The net fiscal impact of AI will vary widely across advanced and emerging economies, depending on productivity outcomes, infrastructure readiness, and policy capacity. Economies with strong public finance management, agile policy frameworks, and a supportive investment climate will be best positioned to maximize AI’s fiscal benefits while managing new spending and contingent liability risks.
