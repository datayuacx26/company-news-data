# AML for Solicitors: A Compliance Guide

Financial crime is on the rise, as a result solicitors are under pressure to identify and prevent money laundering whilst maintaining a smooth client experience.
Combatting financial crime is certainly high on the agenda of the[Solicitors Regulation Authority (SRA)](https://www.sra.org.uk/) . It is increasingly investigating and penalising firms that fail to meet[Anti-Money Laundering (AML)](https://www.identitysolutions.ncino.com/success-hub/glossary/anti-money-laundering-checks) and financial crime regulations.


In its latest report the[SRA details that it took action against 78 cases in 2023/2024](https://www.sra.org.uk/globalassets/documents/sra/research/solicitors-regulation-authority-limited-financial-statements-2024.pdf) . It’s important to note that in more than half of these cases, the most common breaches related to firms having inadequate risk and AML processes with a total of 44 fines totalling almost £600,000. This is a significant increase from the 47 investigations and 23 fines totalling £60,000 in 2022/2023, demonstrating the SRA’s proactive approach and increased fining powers.


### Why are solicitors struggling?


Regulatory complexity is obviously a huge challenge, but so too is keeping pace with evolving client expectations, especially at onboarding stage. It’s harder than ever for firms – both small boutique practices and large firms - to balance compliance and experience whilst preventing money laundering and mitigating wider financial crime exposure.


Just like the SRA, it’s time for the legal services industry to take a more proactive approach to AML compliance.


## Why AML checks are critical for solicitors


In the legal world AML duties stem primarily from:


- [The UK Money Laundering, Terrorist Financing and Transfer of Funds Regulations 2017](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2017/692/contents)
- [Proceeds of Crime Act 2002 (POCA)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2002/29/contents)
- [SRA Standards and Regulations](https://www.sra.org.uk/solicitors/standards-regulations/)
- For firms operating across the EU, the[6th Anti-Money Laundering Directive](https://www.identitysolutions.ncino.com/success-hub/glossary/sixth-eu-money-laundering-directive)
- For solicitors conducting multi-jurisdictional work in the US, they must comply with the[Money Laundering Act of 2020](https://www.fincen.gov/anti-money-laundering-act-2020) .


‍
All of these frameworks require solicitors and[TCSPs](https://www.identitysolutions.ncino.com/success-hub/glossary/trust-or-company-service-provider) to exercise a “risk-based” approach to[AML compliance](https://www.identitysolutions.ncino.com/blog/the-ultimate-guide-to-anti-money-laundering-aml-checks) , i.e. the level of[customer due diligence](https://www.identitysolutions.ncino.com/success-hub/glossary/customer-due-diligence) should be matched to the client’s risk profile.


For small clients, this might involve straightforward ID checks and basic screening. Whilst for larger more complex cases it could[beneficial ownership](https://www.identitysolutions.ncino.com/success-hub/glossary/beneficial-owner-ultimate-beneficial-owner) (UBO),[PEP’s, sanctions](https://www.identitysolutions.ncino.com/blog/the-complete-guide-to-peps-and-sanctions) and adverse media screening, and[enhanced due diligence (EDD)](https://www.identitysolutions.ncino.com/success-hub/glossary/enhanced-due-diligence) ongoing monitoring processes.


In additional to meeting compliance obligations, the benefits of strong AML measures include:


- **Preventing financial crime:** legal services are high-risk money laundering routes.
- **Protecting the firm’s reputation:** failures lead to significant fines and reputational damage.
- **Safeguarding the legal system’s integrity:** upholding the highest ethical and professional standards. Important for winning and retaining business.


### What does a risk-based approach to AML look like in practice?


There are essentially three types of AML compliance firms need to be conducting:


**1. Simplified Due Diligence (SDD):** For low-risk client’s firms must conduct basic identity verification activities
**2. Standard Client Due Diligence (CDD):** This involves the standard range of[ID&V](https://www.identitysolutions.ncino.com/success-hub/glossary/identity-verification) and[KYC verification](https://www.identitysolutions.ncino.com/products/kyc-software) checks
**3. Enhanced due diligence (EDD):** For high-risk clients, and complex multi-jurisdictional cases its vital firms also conduct advanced checks including corporate structure and UBO, PEP’s and sanctions, adverse media monitoring, financial and credit data,[CCJ](https://www.identitysolutions.ncino.com/success-hub/glossary/county-court-judgement) checks, and fraud prevention processes.


Solicitors must perform AML risk assessments at three levels – firm wide, to evaluate total client portfolio exposure to financial crime; at client level, to identify those that present elevated risks; and at case level, to assess each case against the firm’s individual risk tolerance.


[The SRA has a range of information and guidance](https://www.sra.org.uk/solicitors/resources/money-laundering/guidance-support/) to help law firms and solicitors to understand their obligations, stay compliant and prevent money laundering. But, knowing what you need to do and having the capability to implement AML processes in a way that balances client expectations, limits compliance costs, and mitigates risk is very different. For smaller, boutique law firms without huge budgets these requirements can seem overwhelming.
