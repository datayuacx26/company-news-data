# Redefining personalization in the customer experience

# **Redefining personalization in the customer experience**

*Using UX copy to improve experiences and offer more pathways to guidance.*


*By Darren White, Tripadvisor*


In a world increasingly lived through screens, it takes a lot for any digital experience to feel new or special. For brands to break through the monotony of automation, their users need to feel truly seen, heard, and engaged through personalization. Over the last year at Tripadvisor, we’ve discovered that some of the most impactful ways to foster that type of engagement come from thoughtful words placed at just the right moment. Through these data-driven, copy-focused interventions, we’ve introduced new ways for users to personalize their experiences with our products.


## Getting to the heart of personalization


Personalization in e-commerce is, unsurprisingly, table stakes.[A recent report from McKinsey](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/growth-marketing-and-sales/our-insights/the-value-of-getting-personalization-right-or-wrong-is-multiplying) revealed that about 71% of customers expect personalized experiences when shopping online, and 76% get frustrated when they don’t see them.


But in that same McKinsey study, when consumers were asked to define what personalization meant for them, **they “associate \[personalization\] with positive experiences of being made to feel special…when brands demonstrate their investment in the relationship, not just the transaction.”**


At its core, personalization isn’t just about displaying a name or remembering a previous purchase. It’s about understanding where the user is in their journey and meeting them there, with exactly what they need to move forward confidently. The emotional, qualitative parts of personalization — checking in on someone’s experience, offering up guidance, building trust through action and language — are what stand out most.


We’ve been exploring ways to lean into this more emotional side of personalization, using existing research and encouraging UX copy to offer more pathways to guidance for our travelers. What we’ve learned is that sometimes the smallest interventions, like a simple question or a reassuring piece of information, can heavily impact users’ sentiment and motivation.


## AI anchor points: show me around the neighborhood


Travel planning can be overwhelming when you’re trying to maximize time in a new place.


We built our current AI trip builder with that experience in mind: to bring some ease and expertise to this period of discovery. But even still, our research with users indicated that they wanted more customized guidance — 57% of them wanted to center recommendations around a specific place, like a hotel, an attraction, or a specific neighborhood. We hypothesized that by adding a more contextual entry point into the AI trip builder flow on attraction pages, we could drive trip creation, saves, and bookings.


The AI anchor point feature addresses this need by providing a pathway for personalized recommendations around a selected point of interest directly from the attraction detail page. Planning a trip to Florence and exploring Ponte Vecchio? This feature can recommend nearby stays, restaurants, and other sights and sounds to make the most of your trip.

The results validated our approach: with a nearly 4% increase in trip creation and a 2% boost in member registration, we are effectively helping travelers discover the relevant activities to create a trip designed just for them, at their own pace.


Users are engaged with a simple prompt to get them started, powered by millions of insights in the background. Like a friend offering up their own lived experience, this simple intervention in the user’s journey transforms a moment of inspiration into actionable, personalized planning.


## Explicit intent: *following up and following through*


Personalization tools, like the AI anchor points feature, are driven by the instinct to provide what a user needs at every point. But what happens when, instead of making an assumption, you just ask?


We knew from existing internal research that many visitors to hotel detail pages had already booked their stay at that hotel. We wanted to offer these repeat visitors a more relevant experience by guiding them to other things to explore during their visit. We started with a small question, held within a gray module under the hotel’s price display:


“Did you already book this hotel?”


This piece of copy opens up entirely different pathways for our users, allowing us to serve them more relevant experiences based on where they are in their planning process. When users answer yes, we offer a direct path to our AI trip builder to get more personalized recommendations for what to do, see, and eat in the area. When they answer no, we lead them to compare deals on that specific hotel, read reviews, or save that hotel to a trip so they can come back to it later.

These changes have resulted in a 10% increase in saved restaurants, tours, and attractions, suggesting that we’re more effectively helping travelers find relevant recommendations for things to do and places to eat on their trip.


For users, providing a simple response opens the door to personalized guidance and more relevant paths throughout the product. For us, it leads to a significant boost in engagement while reinforcing our brand promise to make everyone a better traveler.


## Top reason to book: doubling down on trust


Tripadvisor has developed a strong reputation for accuracy, transparency, and guidance, but building trust in any new digital experiences is always a challenge. We knew all of this would be put to the test as we introduced in-app hotel bookings.


Much of the messaging around this new feature was value-driven, emphasizing the potential for lower prices, rewards, and other voucher-based discounts for booking with Tripadvisor. But in leaning into those financial aspects, we underutilized important drivers such as flexibility, price confidence, and customer service — pillars of building trust with a new product. To address this gap, we promoted additional top reasons to book with Tripadvisor, including features like “cancel for any reason” and “price match guarantee.”

These additions drove higher engagement and interest in in-app bookings, emphasizing the importance of cancellation flexibility, especially for first-time bookers. We saw a significant 5% increase in room selection click-through and 5% uplift in in-app bookings per shopper.


This demonstrates how a small copy intervention can build the foundation of trust and care that enables future actions. We believe that users feel more confident about booking with Tripadvisor over other online travel sites when we clearly communicate the safety nets we provide.


## Small changes, big impact


As we work, we’re reminded by our users that the most powerful interventions for change often feel invisible. When users feel heard and supported, they don’t necessarily notice the complex systems working behind the scenes — they just feel like the experience was designed for them.


Altogether, these initiatives represent a shift in how we can offer the kind of “personalization” that users crave: experiences and copy that demonstrate care, guidance, and understanding. These small nudges for users open up a world of personalization and guidance as our travelers explore everything the world has to offer. And in turn, we create a braid of trust and confidence between them and Tripadvisor.
