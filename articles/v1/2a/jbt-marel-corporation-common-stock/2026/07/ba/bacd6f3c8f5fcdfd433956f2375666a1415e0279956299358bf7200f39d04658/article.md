# Quality control as a single, connected system?

Walk the floor of many food processing facilities today, and you can still find quality managers doing things the hard way. Clipboards in hand, they move from station to station completing paper forms, then spend hours at their desks entering the same numbers into spreadsheets. The repetitive nature of the task makes it ripe for human errors. Add to this, data that sits in disconnected silos — one system for receiving, one for production, another for environmental, a fourth for final goods inspections — with no easy way to see the full picture; the true value of your data is being wasted.


In a competitive industry when a single quality failure can trigger a product recall, damage a brand, and expose a business to regulatory risks, this fragmented approach is a liability to profits as well as food safety.


##### The daily reality of a QC manager


Consider a quality manager in any food processing plant; poultry, fish, meat, dairy, or pet food. They are responsible for inspections at every stage, primary, secondary, chilling, inventory, final packing, environmental etc. They must ensure reports satisfy any number of international regulations from HACCP, ISO 2200, and BRC Global Standards, to IFS audits, GFSi, and more; and respond to production deviations that put product specs and standards at risk.


Under a paper-based system, the gap between a problem occurring on the line and a manager becoming aware of it can be hours. By then, products have moved downstream or been dispatched to consumers. Disconnected data silos can make tracking of problem products for recall complex and slow.


[AXIN Quality Control](https://jbtmarel.com/en/products/innova-quality-control/) closes that gap. Quality data is captured digitally at the point of inspection and fed into a centralized dashboard in real time. Whether it is a core temperature reading, a pathogen intervention record, a defect score, or a raw material check for supplier benchmarking, every data point is logged and instantly available to the quality team. Each data entry is linked to incoming lots, animals, and batches, making it simple to track the route of end products back to their origins.
