# Economic Update Q3 2025

Consumers held fast in Q3, despite a softening job market and sticky inflation.[Gross Domestic Product](https://www.fanniemae.com/data-and-insights/forecast) (GDP) growth according to Fannie Mae was almost at its historical average of 2% but final revisions may bump it up. Momentum in falling mortgage rates, more inventory and slightly improved affordability pushed home sales up a bit, while builders remained cautious. The Commercial Real Estate (CRE) market continued its turnaround, albeit at a slower pace. With a less restrictive path for the Federal Reserve’s (the Fed) rate finally in sight, prospects brightened for the end of the year.


**Residential Real Estate**


While heatwaves hit the country this summer, the housing market remained lukewarm, despite some favorable factors. The[30-year fixed rate mortgage](https://www.freddiemac.com/pmms) posted an 11-month low during September, swaying to 6.3% by the end of the quarter. Moreover, the[FHFA House Price index](https://www.mba.org/news-and-research/forecasts-and-commentary/download-mortgage-finance-forecast) for Q3 edged up 1.8% and is now below inflation, further lessening the sting for buyers. For almost the past two years,[the inventory of homes for sale](https://www.realtor.com/research/august-2025-data-2/) 1


rose monthly. With over 1 million[active listings](https://www.realtor.com/research/august-2025-data-2/) 1 in August, engaged buyers had more choice and negotiating leverage. Thus, both[Existing and New Home Sales](https://www.mba.org/news-and-research/forecasts-and-commentary/download-mortgage-finance-forecast) rebounded somewhat in Q3, up 4.2% and 1.7% respectively, per the Mortgage Bankers Association (MBA).


However, the housing inventory situation had a cloudy side. It indicated that many buyers are still sitting on the sidelines amid job security concerns and elevated interest rates.[Housing Starts](https://www.mba.org/news-and-research/forecasts-and-commentary/download-mortgage-finance-forecast) were flat from Q2 to Q3. Builders felt price pressure from the slow traffic and overbuilding in some areas, particularly in the South and West.


[Median New Home prices](https://www.mba.org/news-and-research/forecasts-and-commentary/download-mortgage-finance-forecast) were below Existing ones[again](https://www.oldrepublictitle.com/blog/economic-update-q2-2025/) .


Worsening affordability has chilled the housing market for the last few years, as steady income growth for American families was outweighed by rising mortgage rates and home prices. But with payment factors trending down as of late, and[median earnings](https://www.mba.org/news-and-research/newsroom/news/2025/09/25/mortgage-application-payments-decreased-in-august) robust (up 3.2% annually in August), affordability may be turning a corner, as the chart below illustrates.


The MBA's Purchase Applications Payment Index ([PAPI](https://www.mba.org/news-and-research/newsroom/news/2025/09/25/mortgage-application-payments-decreased-in-august) ) declined for most of the months this year, indicating improved affordability. In Q2, the National Association of Home Builder’s[Cost of Housing Index](https://www.nahb.org/news-and-economics/housing-economics/indices/cost-of-housing-index) 2


revealed that new homes drove most of the progress, with builders reacting to market forces by offering smaller homes, built on smaller lots, with fewer feature options. As the[MBA](https://www.mba.org/news-and-research/newsroom/news/2025/08/28/mortgage-application-payments-decreased-in-july) noted, “While still elevated, continued income growth and softening home-price gains should boost prospective buyers’ purchasing power in the months ahead.”


**Commercial Real Estate**


CRE transactions for the major asset groups were $71 billion in Q3 – a cooler quarter compared to the last, but one that reflected 17% improvement in 12-month growth, according to CoStar. The industry’s outlook stayed positive while it kept an eye on inflation and nascent tariff effects *.*


Investors favored the Multifamily sector, which posted its second consecutive quarterly rise in deal volume. The cap rate for Office ticked up for the first time this year and all other sectors held steady. Prices, however, continued to rise. CoStar’s Commercial Repeat Sale Indices ([CCRSI](https://www.costargroup.com/press-room/2025/third-quarter-2025-supply-deliveries-were-down-sharply) ) picked up in both July and August.[Delinquencies](https://www.mba.org/news-and-research/newsroom/news/2025/09/11/commercial-and-multifamily-mortgage-delinquency-rates-increased-in-second-quarter-2025) rose in Q2, mostly for Commercial Mortgage-Backed Securities (CMBS) issuers of Office and Multifamily bonds, but[distressed deals](https://www.costargroup.com/press-room/2025/third-quarter-2025-supply-deliveries-were-down-sharply) had little impact on transaction activity. The prominence of private equity investors has fostered greater flexibility toward adverse conditions, accordingto a recent Bisnow[podcast](https://www.bisnow.com/national/news/capital-markets/first-draft-live-jim-costello-executive-director-at-msci-research-on-why-interest-rates-arent-everything-131045) 3 .


The Retail sector’s fundamentals held firm; the[July vacancy rate](https://www.nar.realtor/research-and-statistics/research-reports/august-2025-commercial-real-estate-market-insights) 4


was 4.3%, the lowest of the major sectors, and[rent growth](https://www.nar.realtor/research-and-statistics/research-reports/august-2025-commercial-real-estate-market-insights) 4 was the highest at 1.8%. For the first time, service establishments (whose costs are less impacted by tariffs) occupied more leased space than goods providers. Both Multifamily and Office landlords focused on tenant retention, which curbed[rental growth](https://www.nar.realtor/research-and-statistics/research-reports/august-2025-commercial-real-estate-market-insights) 4


to 1% and 0.7%, respectively. According to CoStar, slowing deliveries may lower Multifamily’s rate to 8% by year-end, while Office[vacancy rates](https://www.nar.realtor/research-and-statistics/research-reports/august-2025-commercial-real-estate-market-insights) 4 continue to hover around 14%. The Industrial sector’s overall demand was dampened by finalized tariff agreements;[vacancies](https://www.nar.realtor/research-and-statistics/research-reports/august-2025-commercial-real-estate-market-insights) 4 rose to 7.5% in July and[rents](https://www.nar.realtor/research-and-statistics/research-reports/august-2025-commercial-real-estate-market-insights) 4 advanced just 1.7%. But manufacturing reshoring and expansion beat leasing projections in Q2.


Looking beyond the major asset classes, interest in Data Center space continued to soar. The potential for power constraints to curb future construction propelled the sector’s vacancy rate in Q2, with the majority of facilities under construction already pre-leased.


**A Glance Forward**


Economically, Q4 is expected to prolong Q3 trends.[The Fed](https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/files/fomcprojtabl20250917.pdf) projects a 0.5% rate reduction by year-end to bolster the declining[employment situation](https://www.fanniemae.com/data-and-insights/forecast) and boost[GDP](https://www.fanniemae.com/data-and-insights/forecast) growth. The downside of the cuts is the perpetuation of[inflation’s](https://www.fanniemae.com/data-and-insights/forecast) upward course.[Federal Reserve Chair Jerome Powell](https://www.federalreserve.gov/mediacenter/files/FOMCpresconf20250917.pdf) thinks tariffs will have a “one time” impact, but one that could linger over several quarters.


For CRE, the Fed’s more predictable path comes as welcome news. Reacting to the Fed’s latest projections,[CBRE](https://www.bisnow.com/national/news/capital-markets/cre-reacts-to-the-feds-first-rate-cut-of-2025-131003) bumped up its[investment volume forecasts](https://www.bisnow.com/national/news/capital-markets/cre-reacts-to-the-feds-first-rate-cut-of-2025-131003) 5 for 2025 by 5%. Higher property valuations, more refinancings and more CMBS issuance are expected to follow. “The Office sector, particularly Class A- and B- properties that have experienced significant value adjustments, stand to benefit most,” expressed[Avison Young CEO Mark Rose5](https://www.bisnow.com/national/news/capital-markets/cre-reacts-to-the-feds-first-rate-cut-of-2025-131003) .


The[MBA](https://www.mba.org/news-and-research/forecasts-and-commentary/download-mortgage-finance-forecast) foresees mortgage rates barely moving next quarter, reflecting a small moderation in the 10-year treasury yield. Concerns over inflation and government spending may impede further declines.[New builds](https://www.mba.org/news-and-research/forecasts-and-commentary/download-mortgage-finance-forecast) will likely remain slow, as significant inventory is still sitting on the market across much of the country. The good news is that[home price growth](https://www.mba.org/news-and-research/forecasts-and-commentary/download-mortgage-finance-forecast) of just 1% is expected ** to push the turnaround in affordability. MBA economists think[New Home Sales](https://www.mba.org/news-and-research/forecasts-and-commentary/download-mortgage-finance-forecast) could increase 15% more than a year ago, while[Existing Home Sales](https://www.mba.org/news-and-research/forecasts-and-commentary/download-mortgage-finance-forecast) and[refinancings](https://www.mba.org/news-and-research/forecasts-and-commentary/download-mortgage-finance-forecast) continue to improve.


Despite recent projections of a snowy winter ahead, the fourth quarter could usher in a much warmer-than-usual real estate season.
