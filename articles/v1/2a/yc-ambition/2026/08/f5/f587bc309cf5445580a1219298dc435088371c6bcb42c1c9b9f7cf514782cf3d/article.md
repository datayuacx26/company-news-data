# 9 Criteria for Sales Coaching Software in 2026

Sales coaching software has changed. It is no longer just a place for managers to take notes or review sales calls.


For mid-market and enterprise revenue teams, the bigger challenge is making sure good coaching happens consistently across teams, managers, and locations. Leaders need to know whether managers are coaching, what they are coaching on, and whether those conversations are helping reps improve.


That means choosing **sales coaching software** is about more than comparing features. You need a platform that makes coaching easier for managers and gives leaders a clear view of what is happening across the team.


If you are evaluating a new platform, replacing an existing tool, or deciding whether to build your own coaching system, these nine criteria can help.


## What should you look for in sales coaching software?


The best sales coaching software helps teams make coaching consistent, connect conversations to performance data, track whether coaching is happening, and make the process simple enough that managers and reps actually use it.


For larger or distributed teams, there are nine important things to consider:


1. Coaching session tracking
2. Consistency across managers
3. Salesforce and performance data integration
4. AI-powered preparation and follow-up
5. Support for distributed teams
6. Manager and rep adoption
7. Coaching effectiveness
8. Connections to your other sales tools
9. Whether to buy software or build your own system


Here is what to look for in each area.


## 1. Can you track coaching sessions across your team?


Start with a simple question: **Can you tell who is actually being coached?**


For a small sales team, a leader might know this without looking at a report. At a larger company, it becomes much harder.


Coaching can happen in calendar meetings, video calls, spreadsheets, documents, messaging tools, or dedicated software. Without one place to track it, leaders may struggle to answer basic questions:


- Which managers are holding regular 1:1s?
- Which reps are receiving consistent coaching?
- Are scheduled coaching sessions being completed?
- Where are reps missing coaching?
- What topics and skills are managers coaching most often?


Look for software that gives leaders visibility into **coaching session tracking** without asking managers to manually report every conversation.


The goal is not to track meetings for the sake of tracking them. It is to understand whether your team is getting the coaching it needs.


## 2. Can you create consistency without making every 1:1 the same?


Consistency is one of the hardest parts of **sales manager coaching** .


Ask ten managers to coach their reps every week, and you may get ten very different approaches. One manager reviews pipeline. Another talks through deals. Another focuses on skills. Another cancels the meeting when the week gets busy.


Good sales coaching software should provide enough structure to create consistency while giving managers room to focus on what each rep needs.


Look for features such as coaching templates, recurring 1:1s, structured questions, skill areas, action items, and follow-up.


A good system should help your company set clear expectations for coaching without turning every conversation into a script.


## 3. Does it connect coaching to Salesforce and performance data?


Coaching is more useful when managers can connect the conversation to what is actually happening with a rep.


That makes **Salesforce integration** and access to performance data important when comparing sales coaching software.


Managers should not have to jump between Salesforce, dashboards, spreadsheets, call recordings, and coaching notes just to prepare for a 1:1.


Instead, the information they need should be part of the coaching process.


Depending on the role, that might include:


- Pipeline created
- Meetings booked
- Opportunities created
- Conversion rates
- Revenue attainment
- Sales activity
- Individual or team goals
- Performance changes over time


Every company measures performance differently. The important question is whether managers can easily use the data your company already cares about during coaching.


Instead of asking, "How do you think things are going?" a manager can start with the data and have a more useful conversation about what is working and where a rep needs help.


## 4. Does AI make coaching easier for managers?


AI should save managers time, not give them another tool to manage.


One of the most useful ways to use AI in sales coaching is to reduce the work managers have to do before and after a 1:1.


For example, AI can create a pre-read before a meeting that summarizes recent performance and highlights areas worth discussing. After the meeting, it can help capture notes, summarize the conversation, and keep track of next steps.


When evaluating AI features, pay attention to what the manager actually has to do.


Do they need to open another tool and ask a chatbot the right questions? Or does the software automatically bring useful information into the coaching process?


Managers already have busy calendars and plenty of software to manage. AI is most valuable when it removes work rather than adding another step.


## 5. Can it support distributed teams?


**Distributed team coaching** can make consistency even harder.


Your sales team may work across different offices, time zones, regions, roles, and business units. The same coaching approach may not work for everyone.


Your software needs to support that complexity without becoming difficult to manage.


Ask whether you can:


- Create different coaching programs for different teams or roles
- Set different coaching schedules based on team needs
- Create shared standards across regions
- Give senior leaders visibility into coaching
- Adjust coaching as teams and reporting structures change


The larger your company becomes, the more important this flexibility is.


You do not need every manager to coach exactly the same way. You do need a clear standard for what good coaching looks like across the company.


## 6. Will managers and reps actually use it?


A coaching platform cannot help your team if managers do not use it.


That makes **sales tool adoption** one of the most important parts of the buying process.


During a demo, it is easy to focus on everything a platform *can* do. You should spend just as much time looking at how much work those features require from managers and reps.


Ask:


- How long does it take a manager to prepare for a 1:1?
- How much information has to be entered manually?
- Does the software automatically surface useful information?
- Can managers use it alongside the tools they already work in?
- Can reps easily find feedback and next steps?
- Does the software save managers time?


The best software should fit naturally into a manager's workday.


For larger companies, ease of use should be part of the buying decision from the beginning. It should not become a problem you try to solve after launch.


## 7. Can you tell whether coaching is helping?


Knowing that coaching happened is useful. Knowing whether it helped is more important.


Revenue leaders should be able to compare coaching activity with changes in rep performance.


For example, imagine a manager spends six weeks helping a rep improve discovery skills. Can you see whether the rep's performance changes during or after that coaching?


Or maybe your enablement team launches training on a new sales methodology. Can you tell whether managers are reinforcing those skills during coaching and whether performance is improving?


Look for software that helps you connect:


**What the data shows → What the manager coaches → What happens next**


Not every performance change can be tied directly to a single coaching conversation. Sales performance has too many variables for that.


But leaders should be able to see patterns and understand whether their coaching programs are helping reps improve.


## 8. Does it work with the sales tools you already have?


Most enterprise sales teams already have plenty of software.


You may have a CRM, conversation intelligence platform, sales engagement tool, enablement software, reporting tools, and communication platforms.


Your sales coaching software should make those tools more useful, not become another place managers have to check.


For example, Salesforce can show a manager what is happening with a rep's pipeline. Conversation intelligence software can show what happened on a call. Enablement software can show which training a rep completed.


Coaching is where the manager can bring that information together and decide what the rep should work on next.


When evaluating integrations, do not just ask whether two platforms connect.


Ask a more practical question: **Does this connection make coaching easier or better for the manager?**


That is the difference between simply sharing data and actually helping someone do their job.


## 9. Should you buy sales coaching software or build your own?


Finally, consider the **buy versus build** decision.


Building an internal coaching system can sound simple at first. Your company may already have Salesforce, calendar tools, call recordings, AI, spreadsheets, and internal dashboards.


The challenge is keeping all of those pieces working together as your team grows.


Over time, an internal system may need to handle:


- Manager permissions and reporting structures
- Recurring coaching schedules
- Coaching templates
- Session tracking
- Performance data
- AI summaries and pre-reads
- Next steps and follow-up
- Notifications
- Reporting
- Integrations
- Team changes
- Security


Building your own system may make sense if your coaching needs are simple or very specific.


Buying software may make more sense when you need to provide consistent coaching across a large team and building it yourself would require ongoing work from engineering, operations, or enablement.


Do not compare the price of software with the initial cost of building something internally. Compare the total time, cost, and work required to maintain both options over time.


## Sales coaching software evaluation checklist


Before choosing a platform, your team should be able to answer nine questions:


1. Can we see whether coaching is happening consistently?
2. Can we create coaching standards while giving managers flexibility?
3. Can managers easily use Salesforce and performance data during coaching?
4. Does AI reduce preparation, note-taking, and follow-up work?
5. Can the platform support different teams, roles, and locations?
6. Is it simple enough that managers and reps will actually use it?
7. Can we see whether coaching is helping reps improve?
8. Does it work well with the sales tools we already use?
9. Is buying the software more efficient than building and maintaining it ourselves?


These questions can tell you much more than a long feature comparison.


## Choose software that makes better coaching easier


Sales coaching software should do more than give managers another place to take notes.


It should make it easier for managers to understand where reps need help, have better conversations, follow up on what they discussed, and see whether performance improves.


For revenue leaders, it should also answer a bigger question: **Are our reps getting consistent, useful coaching across the company?**


As you compare sales coaching software in 2026, focus less on which platform has the longest feature list.


Instead, ask:


**Will this make it easier for our managers to coach their people well?**


That is ultimately what sales coaching software should help you do.
