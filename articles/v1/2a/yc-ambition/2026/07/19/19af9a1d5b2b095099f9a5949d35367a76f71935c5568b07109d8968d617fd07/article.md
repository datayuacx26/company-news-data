# The Coaching Black Box: The Biggest Blind Spot in Revenue Leadership

Ask a CRO how pipeline is trending, and they'll probably have an answer.


Ask them how many of their frontline managers coached their reps this week—and whether those coaching conversations were any good—and the answer is usually much less certain.


That's what we call the coaching black box, and it’s one of the biggest blind spots for revenue organizations. Leaders know coaching is happening (or at least hope it is!) but they have little visibility into its consistency, quality, or impact. The very activity responsible for translating strategy into execution often happens behind closed doors, with no way to inspect, measure, or improve it.


Spoiler alert: That’s a problem.


## **Coaching Is One of Your Biggest Performance Levers. Why Can't You See It?**


No one really questions the importance of coaching anymore.


Sales organizations make huge investments in manager development, enablement programs, sales methodologies, and AI tools designed to improve rep performance. They expect frontline managers to reinforce those investments through regular coaching.


But expectations and reality are often very different.


In Ambition's recent industry survey:


- Only **30% of organizations** coach on a weekly or bi-weekly cadence.
- **43%** rely on quarterly, ad hoc, or unknown coaching cadences.
- **35%** describe coaching quality as **highly variable** across managers.
- Another **12%** say they have **no way to measure coaching quality at all.**


That means nearly half of revenue organizations either coach inconsistently or don't know if coaching is being optimized for effectiveness. If coaching is one of your most important drivers of performance, that's a risky place to be.


## **You Can't Improve What You Can't See**


That may sound cliche, but that doesn’t mean it’s not true. Revenue leaders have spent years figuring out how to make pipeline more visible and predictable through weekly forecast inspections, real-time activity dashboards, and carefully monitoring conversion rates, attainment, and deal progression.


Coaching, meanwhile, often remains invisible. (Even though it’s just as important as all those other predictors of success!)


A manager may hold one-on-ones every week while another skips them for a month. One manager may coach against conversion rates and opportunity quality, while another focuses entirely on deal updates. Some conversations end with documented action plans, and others disappear the moment the meeting ends.


From an executive's perspective, it all looks the same.


The result is that coaching gets stuck in a cycle of ineffectiveness where it’s difficult to scale, standardize, and improve.


## **The Hidden Cost of the Coaching Black Box**


When leaders can't see how managers are coaching, they also can't answer critical questions:


- Are managers reinforcing the behaviors we trained at SKO?
- Are reps receiving coaching on the metrics that matter most?
- Which managers consistently develop top performers?
- Where is execution beginning to drift before it shows up in the forecast?


When these questions go unanswered, coaching is just reactive problem management. Instead of proactive performance enhancement and coaching quality improvement, no one pays attention until deals start to slide. The coaching black box can hide execution risk until it’s too late.


## **Making Coaching Visible**


Solving this problem doesn’t require execs to be in the weeds of every 1:1 and coaching program. It does require the implementation of a system where coaching can become structured, measurable, and consistent both in cadence and quality.


The highest-performing organizations establish a coaching rhythm that leaders can actually inspect. That rhythm might include identifying common priorities for managers to coach against, following a consistent structure across coaching sessions, documenting action items, and tracking progress over time.


Visibility recenters the conversation. Instead of wondering whether coaching happened, leaders can begin tracking whether coaching is improving performance.
