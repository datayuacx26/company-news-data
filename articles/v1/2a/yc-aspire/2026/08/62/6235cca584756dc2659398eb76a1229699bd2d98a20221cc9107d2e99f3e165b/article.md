# Business valuation in Singapore: Methods, industry multiples and how to calculate

## What is business valuation in Singapore and when do you actually need one


Business valuation is the process of determining your company's economic worth at a specific point in time, using a defined method, for a defined purpose. *The purpose of the valuation determines the method, and the method determines the number.*


There are four situations where a business valuation moves from useful to non-negotiable:


- **Fundraising:** Any institutional investor, be it angel, VC, or private equity, will form a view on your pre-money valuation before the first term sheet. If you have formed one first, you're set to have the upper hand during negotiations


- **Mergers and acquisitions:** Whether you're acquiring or being acquired, the valuation is the foundation of every deal term that follows: price, earnout structure, working capital adjustment, representations and warranties


- **ESOP issuance:** In Singapore, issuing equity to employees requires a defensible valuation of the underlying shares. A well-documented valuation prevents both regulatory exposure and employee relations risk


- **Shareholder exits and disputes:** An independent valuation is the most reliable mechanism for resolving shareholder disagreements about price and for structuring clean exits without litigation risk


***There is a fifth trigger most CFOs underuse*** **.**


Internal strategic benchmarking. Using a valuation as a periodic health check against competitors, your own prior round, or sector multiples is what separates finance functions that inform strategy from those that report history.


For[Singapore-registered businesses](https://aspireapp.com/blog/a-guide-to-singapore-financial-reporting-standards?) , the Accounting and Corporate Regulatory Authority (ACRA) and the Institute of Valuers and Appraisers Singapore (IVAS) provide the professional framework. IRAS guidelines for asset transfers and certain corporate restructuring events mean valuation isn't always discretionary.


***Best practice:*** If your business is 18 months from a potential exit or next raise, the time to establish a baseline valuation is now, not when the counterparty asks for one. Valuations prepared under transaction pressure are rarely as defensible as those built without it.


## Business valuation methods and which one applies to your company


There is no universally correct method. The right approach depends on your company's stage, the predictability of your revenue, and what the valuation will be used for. Most professional valuers use more than one method and triangulate to a range. In fact, a single-method valuation is a red flag in any investor or acquirer diligence process.


### Income approach: Discounted cash flow (DCF)


The DCF method values your business by estimating its future free cash flows and discounting them back to their present value using a risk-adjusted rate (the Weighted Average Cost of Capital, or WACC).


**Best for:** Growth-stage companies with two to three years of predictable revenue history and a credible forward forecast. This is the most commonly used method in fundraising because it forces both parties to agree on growth assumptions, not just historical earnings.


**How it works:**


- Project annual free cash flows over a five to ten-year period.


- Apply a terminal value to capture value beyond the forecast window.


- Discount the total to present value using your WACC (which typically ranges between 15% and 25% for Singapore-based SMEs), depending on sector risk and capital structure.


**Example:** A B2B SaaS company generating SGD $2 million in annual recurring revenue, growing at 30% year-on-year with 70% gross margins and a 20% WACC, might produce a DCF valuation in the range of SGD $8 million to SGD $12 million, depending entirely on the terminal growth rate assumption. Move that assumption from 3% to 5% and the valuation shifts by 25%.


### Market approach: Comparable companies and transaction multiples


The market approach values your business by comparing it to similar companies that have been sold or funded recently, applying a multiple to your earnings or revenue.


**Best for:** Businesses in sectors with active M&A activity or where comparable funded companies exist — eCommerce, professional services, technology. This is the most commonly used method for mainstream SME transactions in Singapore.


**How it works:**


- Identify comparable companies or recent transactions.


- Apply the relevant multiple (EV/Revenue, EV/EBITDA, or SDE multiple) to your own financial metric.


- Adjust for company-specific risk factors like customer concentration, revenue quality, and founder dependency.


**What Singapore-based businesses should consider:**


In Singapore, 2×–5×[EBITDA](https://aspireapp.com/blog/ebita-explained-formula-and-calculation-methods) is the standard range for most mainstream SME transactions, meaningfully lower than Malaysia (3×–6×) or Thailand (7×–10×). The multiple you receive sits within that range based on how well your business performs against the risk factors investors and acquirers apply.

### Asset-based approach: Net asset value


The asset-based approach values your business by calculating the net value of its assets, that is, total assets minus total liabilities. It comes in two variants: going concern (adjusted book value) and liquidation value.


**Best for:** Asset-heavy businesses, property holding companies, or businesses in financial distress. It is also relevant for multi-entity structures in Singapore where a property-holding entity and an operating entity are linked; each needs to be valued on appropriate terms.


**What it misses:**


For eCommerce, SaaS, and professional services businesses, the primary audience for this guide. Asset-based valuation almost always understates economic value. It captures what you own, not what you generate or what the market will pay for your earnings stream.


### Startup valuation: When there are no earnings to model


For pre-revenue or early-revenue companies, traditional financial methods produce numbers that are either unreliable or meaningless. Startup valuation methods substitute financial history with team quality, market size, traction signals, and investor risk tolerance.


**Key methods:**


- **Berkus method:** Assigns a value (typically up to SGD $2.5 million) to five non-financial factors: viable idea, prototype, quality management team, strategic relationships, and product rollout or revenue. Simple and transparent, it is useful for very early-stage conversations


- **Scorecard method:** Benchmarks the startup against regional comparable funded companies, then adjusts the average pre-money valuation based on weighted factors including team strength, market opportunity, competitive positioning, and technology moat


- **Venture Capital method:** Works backwards from the investor's required return. If the investor needs 10× return and expects to exit at SGD $50 million in five years, the pre-money valuation is implied by the ownership percentage required to deliver that return


- **Entry cost method:** Values the startup by estimating what it would cost to rebuild the business from scratch — technology, IP, hiring, and customer acquisition included. Useful for hardware and deep tech startups, but limited as a standalone method because it captures current asset value, not future earnings potential


- **Future valuation multiple method:** Projects revenue or EBITDA at a defined future point, applies a sector-appropriate multiple to arrive at a future enterprise value, then discounts back to today. Most applicable at Series A or traction stage, when forward projections are credible enough to hold up to investor scrutiny


- **Risk factor summation method:** Starts with the average pre-money valuation of comparable funded startups in the same region and sector, then adjusts that baseline up or down based on 12 standard risk factors (including management risk, market competition risk, technology risk, legislative risk, and capital raising risk)


**Singapore context:**


Enterprise Singapore grants and SGInnovate co-investment schemes are material inputs to startup valuation. Non-dilutive capital reduces the risk premium that investors apply. A company with SGD $500,000 in government grants already deployed has de-risked its early milestones in a way that moves a pre-money negotiation.


**Watch out for:** DCF is highly sensitive to discount rate and terminal value inputs. A 1% change in either can move the total valuation by 20–30%. Any investor will run their own model, which is why your assumptions need to be documented and coherent, not optimistic.


## How to calculate business valuation: A step-by-step guide


Before engaging a valuation advisor, a CFO can complete an internal baseline in six steps:


### Step 1 — Define the purpose


[Fundraising](https://aspireapp.com/blog/fundraising-for-start-ups-guide) , M&A, ESOP, or internal benchmark. The purpose determines the method, which determines the inputs you need to prepare.


### Step 2 — Normalise your finances.


Calculate adjusted EBITDA by removing owner-specific expenses that will not continue post-transaction, one-off items (litigation costs, restructuring), and non-cash charges. This is your true earnings baseline.


### Step 3 — Choose your method


Apply DCF if you have a credible multi-year forecast. Apply market multiples if comparable transactions exist in your sector. Use both where possible and triangulate.


### Step 4 — Build a range, not a single number


A valuation presented as a single figure invites a counterparty to attack the assumptions. A range, supported by methodology, gives you room to negotiate without appearing defensive.


### Step 5 — Stress-test against comparables


Run your number against publicly available transaction data for your sector. If you are significantly above or below the range, understand why and be able to articulate that difference confidently.


### Step 6 — Document every assumption


The model is not the valuation. The assumptions behind the model are what get interrogated in a diligence process. Revenue growth rate, churn, margin expansion, and discount rate need a source and a rationale.


## Business valuation in mergers and acquisitions: What changes


M&A valuation is not the same exercise as fundraising valuation. The lens shifts from growth potential to synergy value, integration risk, and what the business is worth to a specific buyer, not to the market in general.


For CFOs at multi-entity companies, several additional factors come into play:


- **Consolidation and intercompany adjustments:** An acquirer will look at group-level EBITDA after eliminating intercompany transactions. If your Singapore entity bills a regional subsidiary at an above-market rate, that revenue disappears in consolidation, and so does the multiple applied to it


- **Transfer pricing:** Cross-border intercompany pricing must be documented and defensible under IRAS guidelines. In an M&A context, an acquirer's tax advisor will review transfer pricing as part of diligence. Misaligned pricing creates both valuation risk and post-close tax exposure


- [Net working capital](https://aspireapp.com/blog/what-is-working-capital-and-how-to-improve-it) **:** Most M&A transactions include a net working capital adjustment at close. A business with volatile debtor days or poorly managed inventory will have a larger working capital peg, which reduces the cash consideration the seller ultimately receives


- **Earnout structures:** Buyers use earnout provisions to bridge valuation gaps. Understanding how earnouts interact with your post-close operating model (and whether you retain enough control to hit the targets) is as important as the headline valuation number


- **Choosing between business valuation advisors:** For straightforward SME transactions in Singapore, an IVAS-accredited business valuation firm (SGD $3,000–$10,000) provides the methodology and report. For complex cross-border or multi-entity deals, an investment bank or Big 4 M&A advisory practice provides the full sell-side or buy-side mandate at a substantially higher cost. Know which engagement you are in before you sign an advisory mandate


## What actually moves your valuation and what your finance workflow controls


Four levers sit directly within the finance function's control:


**Revenue quality:** Recurring versus transactional revenue, customer concentration, and churn rate are the three variables that move your multiple most significantly. A business with 80% recurring revenue and net revenue retention above 100% commands a meaningfully higher multiple than one at identical EBITDA with lumpy, project-based income. This is a structuring decision, not an accounting one and it should be made years before a transaction.


**Financial visibility:** Investors and acquirers apply a risk discount when they cannot trust the numbers. Financials that are reconciled monthly, with clear cost allocation across entities and transparent intercompany flows, reduce the information asymmetry that drives discounts. Clean, current data is a negotiating asset.


**Working capital efficiency:** Buyers model working capital at close. A business with predictable cash conversion cycles, low days sales outstanding, and disciplined payables management presents lower operational risk and the market prices that accordingly.


**Operational independence:** Founder or key-person dependency is one of the largest single valuation discounts applied in SME and mid-market deals. Finance teams that can demonstrate process-driven operations, where outputs are not dependent on any individual's institutional knowledge, reduce the risk premium the acquirer applies.


For CFOs building towards an exit or next raise, the finance function's infrastructure is directly linked to the valuation outcome. When financial data is always current, assumptions are always auditable, and processes are built to run without individual intervention, the risk discount applied to your business narrows. That gap between a defended valuation and a discounted one, often lives inside the finance function itself, not in the market.


## Common business valuation mistakes that reduce credibility


Common mistakes include:


- Using revenue instead of quality revenue


- Applying industry multiples without matching company profile


- Ignoring debt, cash, and working capital


- Overstating recurring revenue


- Ignoring customer concentration


- Treating forecast as fact


- Using one method only


- Not separating entity-level[financial performance](https://aspireapp.com/blog/financial-performance)


- Ignoring FX and cross-border tax exposure


- Not documenting assumptions


A valuation is only as credible as the evidence behind it.

## FAQs


### What is business valuation and why does it matter?


Business valuation is the process of determining your company's economic worth at a specific point in time using a defined methodology. It matters because it determines your negotiating position in fundraising, M&A, ESOP issuance, and shareholder exits, and because the method used can produce materially different numbers from the same financial data.


### What are the main business valuation methods?


Three approaches cover the vast majority of valuations: the income approach (DCF), the market approach (comparable company multiples and precedent transactions), and the asset-based approach (net asset value). Most professional valuers apply more than one and triangulate to a range.


### How do I value a startup with no revenue in Singapore?


Pre-revenue startups are typically valued using the Berkus Method, Scorecard Method, or Venture Capital Method, each of which substitutes financial history with team quality, market size, and traction signals. Enterprise Singapore and SGInnovate co-investment schemes can positively influence pre-money positioning by reducing perceived risk.


### What are typical business valuation multiples by industry in Singapore?


For mainstream Singapore SMEs, 2×–5× EBITDA is the standard range. Sector-specific multiples vary significantly: SaaS businesses command 4.8×–7.3× ARR; eCommerce trades at 2.8×–5.0× profit; professional services typically sit at 2.0×–3.5× EBITDA. Company-specific factors like revenue quality, concentration, and founder dependency move the final number within those ranges.


### When do I need a business valuation in Singapore?


Fundraising, M&A (buy or sell side), ESOP issuance, and shareholder exits or disputes all require a defensible valuation. IRAS guidelines also make professional valuation necessary for certain asset transfers and corporate restructuring events. Proactive internal benchmarking is a practice the most well-prepared finance functions maintain annually.


### What is the difference between valuation for fundraising versus M&A?


Fundraising valuation focuses on growth potential and future earnings; the investor is buying into what the business can become. M&A valuation focuses on what the business is worth to a specific acquirer, accounting for synergies, integration risk, and working capital. The buyer is pricing what they can extract. The same business can produce meaningfully different numbers under each lens.


### How much does a business valuation cost in Singapore?


Professional SME valuations typically range from SGD $3,000 to SGD $10,000, depending on complexity and the scope of the engagement. Multi-entity or cross-border valuations for M&A purposes cost more, particularly when conducted by Big 4 or investment banking advisors who charge a retainer plus a success fee.
