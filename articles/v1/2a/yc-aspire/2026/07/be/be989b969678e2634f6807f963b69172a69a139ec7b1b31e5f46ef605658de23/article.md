# How to automate expense policy enforcement in Singapore

## **What is an expense policy?**


An expense policy defines how business spending is governed across an organisation. It sets the rules for eligible expenses, approval authority, spending limits, and paperwork required. It helps businesses govern company spending, avoid policy exceptions, and keep financial controls consistent as the business grows.


You may see words such as business expense policy, company expense policy,[corporate expense policy](https://aspireapp.com/blog/everything-you-need-to-know-about-building-your-corporate-card-expense-policy) , and employee expense policy. Don’t jumble the words up. They're all the same document from different perspectives. The business establishes the rules, the company owns the rules, and the employees follow the rules day to day.


### **Who should an expense policy apply to?**


A corporate expense policy should apply to anyone who spends business money, whether they are an employee, manager, executive, contractor or corporate cardholder. However, if you make exceptions for senior executives or trusted staff, it can weaken the expense policy enforcement and lead to inconsistent approval decisions across your organisation. This means that applying the same governance standard to each team results in more consistent approvals, finance has better visibility into company spend, and you retain controls in place as your business grows.


### **Expense policy vs expense reimbursement policy**


An expense policy dictates the rules for your business spending, including spending limits, approval protocols, and permitted items. An expense reimbursement policy is implemented when an employee spends their own money and needs to get it back. Many organisations combine the two into a single corporate expense policy, but decreasing out-of-pocket costs with[corporate cards](https://aspireapp.com/corporate-card) keeps reimbursements cheaper and offers finance teams better control over company spend.

## **Why expense policies matter for organisations**


Four business outcomes emerge once finance teams can enforce an expense policy consistently as the organisation grows.


### **Create consistent spending decisions**


A conference ticket at SGD $2,000 should get the same approval logic regardless of which department head, manager, or legal entity submits it. This ensures inconsistency disappears, and employees stop feeling like approval is a matter of luck.


### **Reduce policy violations before they become reimbursement disputes**


Catching a duplicate SGD $180 taxi claim at the point of submission is a different experience from catching it three weeks later, once the employee has already been paid. The first is a system doing its job. The second is an argument about whether the money can be clawed back, and it usually can't be, cleanly.


### **Improve audit readiness and expense compliance**


IRAS requires businesses to maintain accounting records and supporting papers for a minimum period of five years. A clear expense policy, supported by receipts, approvals, and audit trails, helps meet these record-keeping standards and allows for a timely response when supporting paperwork is sought.


Strong documentation also protects your GST input tax claims. Under IRAS requirements, inadequate records or missing supporting documents may result in input GST claims being disallowed, forcing your business to absorb GST costs that could otherwise have been recovered. This creates a direct financial impact alongside potential penalties and increased audit scrutiny.


An expense compliance gap in March that was overlooked becomes a rush in November when an auditor requests backup on an SGD $30,000 travel queue.


### **Scale finance operations without increasing manual reviews**


As transaction volume increases across teams, entities, and markets, manual reviews become difficult to sustain. A scalable expense policy embeds approval rules and spending controls into day-to-day operations. This allows the finance team to maintain consistent governance without expanding the manual review process.

## **How to build an expense policy that scales**


A growing business needs more than controls on spending. It requires an expense policy that offers clear guidance to employees, facilitates quicker approvals, and allows finance teams to maintain consistent controls as the volume of transactions increases.


1. **Assess where your current policy breaks down**


First, evaluate how your current policy is working in practice. Review recent spend across departments and entities to find common policy exceptions, approval bottlenecks, duplicate claims, delayed reimbursements, or manual workarounds. The patterns show where existing controls are no longer keeping up with the business and where governance needs to be enhanced.


1. **Set spending categories and limits**


Review your present spending categories and restrictions to ensure they still match how the business functions today. As businesses grow in departments, legal entities and markets, so should their expenditure controls. Establish restrictions per cost centre, employee role, company division or transaction amount to ensure consistent policies with no unnecessary exclusions.


1. **Establish approval workflows**


Build approval workflows around delegated authority, not organisational hierarchy. Route approvals by transaction value, department, business unit, budget owner, cost centre, project, or vendor type so routine purchases move fast while higher-risk expenditure gets extra attention. Risk-based approval rules allow you to enforce expense policy compliance without adding manual reviews.


1. **Define reporting and reimbursement requirements**


Define standard procedures for expense reporting, including receipt requirements, submission deadlines, approval timings, reimbursement timing, and required documentation. Consistent reporting enhances audit readiness, reduces follow-up effort and supports expense reimbursement policy best practices.


1. **Embed your policy into day-to-day spending**


Policy only works if it is applied consistently. Incorporate spend restrictions, approval criteria and documentation requirements into the tools employees currently use – corporate cards, expense management procedures and more. This lowers the need for human reviews and helps to ensure policies are enforced before transactions hit finance.


1. **Review and update your expense policy regularly**


Review your expense policy if the company enters new entities, new markets, or new payment methods or finds recurring exceptions to the policy. Regular evaluations assist in ensuring that approval systems, controls over spend and documentation requirements still represent the way the business operates.


## **Expense policy best practices**


An effective expense policy balances financial control with employee experience. Use these expense policy best practices to write clear policies, define receipt thresholds, establish timely expense submission deadlines (such as a 30-day window where appropriate), and apply consistent approval rules across the organization.


### **Write policies that employees can understand**


Avoid legal or accounting jargon where possible. If employees interpret the same policy differently across teams or business entities, finance teams end up relying on manual judgement instead of consistent policy enforcement. A policy that defines expectations in straightforward language with practical examples is much more likely to be followed by employees.


### **Use category-specific spending limits**


Finance teams can manage the higher risk spend without slowing down routine purchases by having different spending limitations for areas such as travel, meals, software and office supplies. Review category limits periodically to ensure they are consistent with true business costs.


### **Build approval workflows based on spending thresholds and risk**


An SGD $50 stationery order and an SGD $15,000 vendor deposit should not share the same approver. Set up approval guidelines based on transaction value, department, budget owner, cost centre, project, or business entity so routine purchases move quickly while higher-risk expenditure receives additional oversight.


### **Reduce reimbursements by using corporate cards where appropriate**


Each item the company pays for with a corporate card rather than the employee’s own money is one less reimbursement claim to complete. Corporate cards also give finance teams greater control over business spend by applying spending limits and policy rules before purchases are completed.


### **Automate receipt collection**


Chasing a missing receipt over Slack costs more time than the receipt is worth. Automated receipt collection simplifies follow-up, enhances the quality of paperwork, and speeds up cost review and month-end reconciliation.


### **Review recurring policy violations**


If the same manager keeps approving dinners over the limit, the problem is a system gap, not a reason to send another reminder email. Review policy exception reports regularly to identify recurring concerns and change the spending limits or approval procedures if needed.


### **Update the policy as the business grows**


Following these best practices for spend policy setup is what separates a policy on paper from one that holds. Review the policy whenever the business expands into new markets, adds legal entities, or changes approval authority to ensure governance evolves alongside the organisation.

An expense policy should evolve alongside the organisation. As transaction volumes increase, operations expand across entities, and approval structures become more complex, spending controls and governance frameworks need to scale as well. - Spending limits by category


- Approval workflows and thresholds


- Corporate card usage rules

**Single-entity business example**


A business operating from a single legal entity may rely on a simpler approval structure, with finance leaders overseeing most spending decisions. Even at this stage, defining clear spending limits, approval rules, and documentation requirements creates a stronger foundation for future growth.


**Scaling business example**


As a business expands across departments, markets, or legal entities, approval authority typically becomes more distributed. Finance teams often introduce department-level approvers, corporate cards, and approval rules based on transaction value or budget ownership. Well-defined governance helps routine spending move efficiently while maintaining appropriate financial controls.


**Multi-entity business example**


A business operating in Singapore, Indonesia and the UAE needs entity-specific regulations, multi-currency spending restrictions, regional approvers, and standardised documentation. While local tax regulations and approval standards may vary from country to country, a centralised strategy can help to provide consistent financial controls across the business.


### **When should your business update its expense policy?**


Review your expense policy whenever your business changes how it operates. Common triggers include:


- Headcount grows significantly.


- Corporate cards or new payment methods are introduced.


- The business expands into new countries or legal entities.


- New spending categories or departments are created.


- Tax or regulatory requirements change.


- Finance teams see recurring policy exceptions or approval bottlenecks.


An expense policy should evolve with your business. Reviewing it regularly helps ensure spending controls, approval workflows, and documentation requirements continue to reflect how the business actually operates.


## **Common expense policy mistakes to avoid**


### **Writing vague policies**


"Reasonable business expenses" tells employees and approvers little about whether an SGD $600 client dinner complies with company policy.


### **Reviewing expenses only after employees spend**


By the time finance flags a duplicate claim, the employee's already been reimbursed twice, and the second payment rarely gets clawed back.


### **Using one approval workflow for every expense**


An SGD $30 taxi claim routed through the same sign-off as an SGD $20,000 vendor contract slows the small claims without scrutinising the large ones.


### **Relying on spreadsheets and email approvals**


A shared spreadsheet spanning multiple departments or business entities isn't an audit trail; it's a search problem that undermines expense compliance the moment an auditor requests supporting documentation.


### **Ignoring recurring policy exceptions**


An emergency vendor payment approved outside policy every month isn't an emergency. It's a gap in your expense policy that needs a permanent rule.

## **FAQs**


### **What is expense policy compliance?**


Expense policy compliance means employees follow your company's approved spending rules, including eligible expenses, approval workflows, spending limits, and documentation requirements. Consistent compliance helps control costs, simplify audits, and reduce policy exceptions.


### **What expenses should a company include in its policy?**


Most expense policies should cover travel, meals, accommodation, software, office supplies, client entertainment, training, mileage, and other common business expenses. Each category should include spending limits, approval requirements, and documentation rules.


### **How often should an expense policy be reviewed?**


Review your expense policy at least once a year or whenever your business changes significantly. New spending patterns, company growth, regulatory updates, or recurring policy exceptions are all good reasons to update it.


### **What's the difference between a reimbursement policy and an expense policy?**


An expense policy defines how company money can be spent, while a reimbursement policy explains how employees claim back approved business expenses paid from their own pocket. Many businesses combine both into one corporate expense policy.


### **Can expense policy enforcement be automated?**


Yes. Modern[expense management platforms](https://aspireapp.com/expense-management) automate spending controls, approval workflows, receipt collection, and policy checks, helping finance teams enforce expense policies consistently while reducing manual reviews and administrative work.


### **What should expense report guidelines include?**


Expense report guidelines should explain submission deadlines, required receipts, eligible expenses, approval workflows, reimbursement timelines, and any supporting information needed to review claims accurately and consistently.


### **What are 5 examples of expenses?**


Common business expenses include employee travel, client meals, accommodation, office supplies, and software subscriptions. Your expense policy should define eligibility, spending limits, and approval requirements for each category to ensure consistent spending.
