# International business payments: Methods, costs, and how to send money overseas

## What are international business payments


International business payments are transfers a business makes to a recipient in another country: paying a supplier, a freelancer, a software vendor, or a subsidiary abroad.


Unlike a local transfer, these payments usually involve currency conversion, cross-border banking networks, and compliance checks. That's what makes them slower and more expensive than moving money at home.


## How international business payments work


Most cross-border payments follow four steps.


### Payment initiation


You enter the beneficiary's details, amount, currency, and purpose. Get the account number,[SWIFT code](https://aspireapp.com/blog/what-is-a-swift-code-and-how-does-it-work) , or recipient name wrong, and the payment gets delayed or rejected.


### Payment processing


The provider validates the transaction and runs it against compliance checks. With a traditional bank wire, the payment often passes through one or more correspondent banks before it reaches the recipient.


### Currency conversion


If sender and recipient use different currencies, the funds convert at the provider's[exchange rate](https://aspireapp.com/blog/decoding-mid-market-rates-in-foreign-currency-exchange) . This is where hidden cost usually shows up, since many providers add a spread on top of the mid-market rate.


### Settlement and delivery


The recipient gets the payment in their account or wallet. Some local rails settle instantly. SWIFT-based transfers can take several business days.


## Types of international payment methods


### SWIFT wire transfers


SWIFT reaches a broad range of countries and works well for large payments or destinations with no local rail available. It's also often the slowest and most expensive option, since intermediary banks can each take a cut along the way.


### International ACH transfers


Cheaper than a bank wire and well suited to recurring payments where you can accept a longer settlement time. Coverage is narrower than SWIFT, but the economics are better for predictable flows.


### Digital payment platforms


These combine transfer, FX, and account management in one place, with faster onboarding, clearer pricing, and better visibility into payment status. For growing businesses, this can simplify supplier payments and recurring cross-border expenses without the manual back and forth.


### Multi-currency business accounts


Hold, receive, and send funds in multiple currencies without converting everything immediately. This reduces unnecessary FX conversions and gives you more control over when you convert and at what rate.


### Cards and digital wallets


Useful for smaller international expenses like SaaS tools, ads, or subscriptions. Foreign transaction fees and FX markups make them a poor default for regular business payments.


### SEPA transfers


If you're paying suppliers or partners in Europe, SEPA (Single Euro Payments Area) transfers can be a more efficient option for EUR-denominated payments. They typically settle faster and cost less than a SWIFT transfer because they move through Europe's domestic payment network instead of the correspondent banking system. For Singapore businesses making regular euro payments, SEPA can help reduce transfer costs and improve settlement times where it's supported.


### Regional payment rails


Regional payment rails connect domestic payment systems across countries, making transfers faster and cheaper than traditional bank wires. Singapore's PayNow is linked with Malaysia's DuitNow, Thailand's PromptPay, and India's UPI, giving businesses a more efficient way to send payments across these supported corridors.


## What international business payments actually cost


The transfer fee is only part of the bill. ### Transaction fees


A flat fee, a percentage fee, or both. A low headline rate can still get expensive once FX conversion and receiving-bank deductions are added on top.


### FX costs


Often the biggest hidden expense. Even a **1% difference in the exchange rate means paying SGD $100 more on every SGD $10,000 converted.** For businesses making regular **cross-border business payments** , those costs compound quickly over the course of a year.


### Intermediary bank charges


In a SWIFT transfer, each[correspondent bank](https://aspireapp.com/blog/dbs-swift-code-the-essential-guide-for-overseas-transfers) in the chain can deduct its own fee, which shrinks what the recipient actually receives.


### Compliance and operational cost


Screening, documentation, and reconciliation all take time. It's easy to underestimate until payment volume grows and that overhead starts showing up in your team's workload.


## Where founders run into friction


### FX rates move


Between the moment you send a payment and the moment it settles, the rate can shift, which creates real risk on thin margins or frequent overseas invoices.


### Fees stack


Multiple banks and conversions in one payment chain can push the effective cost well above the fee shown upfront.


### Settlement drags


Depending on the rail and destination, a transfer can take hours or several days, which disrupts cash flow planning and supplier relationships.


### Compliance varies by country


Businesses making overseas payments may need additional verification under Singapore's AML and KYC requirements, which can slow payments if documentation is incomplete.


### Fraud risk is real


[Invoice fraud](https://aspireapp.com/blog/how-to-prevent-bec-email-fraud) and spoofed beneficiary details target cross-border payments specifically. Strong approval controls and verification aren't optional.


### Local payment rails aren't always available


Not every provider supports local payment rails like SEPA or regional payment networks. When they don't, payments often default to international wires, increasing both costs and settlement times.

## Best practices for managing overseas payments


- **Double-check beneficiary details before you send.** A single incorrect account number or SWIFT code can delay a payment by days or require manual recovery.


- **Standardise your payment approval workflow.** Clear approval rules reduce fraud risk and help prevent duplicate or unauthorised transfers.


- **Match currencies between income and expenses where possible.** If you're collecting USD from customers and paying suppliers in USD, you avoid converting the same money multiple times.


- **Hold balances in a multi-currency business account.** Keeping frequently used currencies on hand gives you more flexibility over when to convert funds.


- **Reconcile payments as they happen.** Regular **payment reconciliation** makes it easier to identify missing invoices, unexpected deductions, or failed transfers before month-end.


- **Know your total payment cost before you send.** Compare transfer fees, FX spreads, and any receiving or intermediary bank charges before choosing a provider.


- **Review provider performance by payment corridor.** One provider may be cost-effective for **Singapore business payments** to Malaysia but less competitive for transfers to Europe or North America.

## FAQs


### What's the best method for international business payments?


It depends on the payment. SWIFT covers the most destinations, ACH is cheaper for recurring transfers, and a multi-currency account or digital platform usually wins on speed and FX control.


### How long do international business payments take?


It varies by method and corridor. Digital payment platforms and local rails can settle same-day or within a few hours. SWIFT transfers typically take 1 to 5 business days, depending on how many intermediary banks the payment passes through and the destination country's banking infrastructure. Payments to countries with well-established local rails tend to move faster than those made via SWIFT alone.


### How much do international business payments cost?


Total cost usually includes four components: the transfer fee charged by your provider, the FX spread applied during currency conversion, any intermediary bank charges deducted along the way (common with SWIFT transfers), and the operational cost of manually reconciling and tracking payments. The visible transfer fee is often the smallest part of the total cost, so it's worth calculating the all-in cost rather than comparing headline fees alone.


### Do I need a local bank account in the recipient's country to send international payments?


No. Most providers, including digital payment platforms and multi-currency accounts, let you send payments without a local account on either end. This differs from older banking setups, where cross-border payments sometimes required a correspondent relationship or local presence to settle efficiently.


### Can I lock in an exchange rate before sending an international payment?


Some providers offer forward contracts or rate locks for larger or recurring payments, letting you fix the rate before settlement to avoid market-rate fluctuations. This is usually available through digital payment platforms or dedicated FX providers rather than standard bank wires, and it's worth asking about if you regularly pay the same overseas supplier or subsidiary.
