Many organizations approach AI as a technology rollout, where leaders expect results to follow once the tools are in place. But AI adoption usually stalls for a different reason: **the people** . A 2026 survey of senior data and AI leaders found that[93% of organizations](https://www.labmanager.com/human-factors-remain-the-biggest-barrier-to-ai-adoption-survey-finds-35039) cite culture and change management as the greatest impediment to AI adoption. This is why AI change management—the discipline of guiding people through the transition—has become a defining leadership responsibility.


Leaders who view AI as a change to navigate will help their teams move from early hesitation to lasting adoption. In times of rapid disruption, organizations need more than available technology. They need leaders who can guide the human side of change.


### Key Takeaways:


- Leaders set the pace of AI change management by modeling new behaviors and effectively guiding their teams through the transition.
- The human side of adoption decides the outcome; resistance and uncertainty stall AI efforts long before the technology does.
- Clear governance and human oversight build the trust that gives employees the confidence to adopt AI.


## What Is AI Change Management?


AI change management is the structured approach leaders use to prepare and support their people as the organization adopts artificial intelligence. Simply putting tools in place accounts only for implementation; adoption happens when people trust the technology and fold it into their daily work. Rather than focusing on acquiring AI tools and expecting usage rates to automatically follow, AI change management centers on the human side of the transition and helps employees build both the confidence and skills to use AI well.


AI change management differs from traditional change management. A typical change initiative introduces a new system and reinforces the new way of working until it becomes routine. But because AI keeps advancing, adoption never really ends. Instead, it becomes an ongoing practice. Employees keep learning as tools evolve, and it’s a leader’s responsibility to guide that learning over time. Unlike a routine software update, AI can also reshape how people see their own roles, which raises deeper questions about identity and value. Leaders who address those questions early and in an ongoing capacity give their teams a reason to engage.


## Why AI Change Management Is a Leadership Priority


As AI disruption accelerates, organizations are no longer asking if they should adapt but how quickly they can do it. Leaders who delay AI adoption risk falling behind their competition as organizations increasingly embed AI into everyday operations. And because managers directly create the conditions for innovation, collaboration, and growth, AI change management has become the new leadership imperative. When leaders use AI visibly, establish guardrails, build trust, and model use cases for their teams, they create productive change and help their teams navigate this new frontier.

## Leadership Behaviors That Drive AI Change Management


## We can only graduate to leading people through change if we know how to deal with change as humans first.


— Kory Kogon, Vice President of Content Development, FranklinCovey


At first, driving AI adoption may seem to be primarily a technical challenge. But it’s your people who make any kind of change happen. Leaders must help their people understand what AI means for their work, define how it should be used, and demonstrate the behaviors they expect to see. Organizations that sustain AI adoption consistently invest in these leadership practices.


### Build Team Confidence


FranklinCovey Institute data reveals that[70% of employees say AI](https://resources.franklincovey.com/c/tlg-ai-transformation-human-imperative?x=j4G37p&lb-mode=overlay#page=1) and technology are evolving faster than their cultures can adapt. What’s more, our research shows that nearly[one in four employees feels overwhelmed by AI](https://resources.franklincovey.com/c/tlg-great-leader-insight-report?x=j4G37p&lb-mode=overlay) . Teams are more likely to embrace AI when leaders replace uncertainty with confidence. Leaders build that confidence by encouraging thoughtful experimentation, establishing parameters, and making it clear that human judgment remains essential. As confidence grows, employees become more willing to use AI, explore new applications, and strengthen their capabilities.


### Communicate Clear Expectations


People adopt new technology with greater confidence when they understand how it supports business priorities and where human judgment remains essential. Leaders reduce uncertainty by setting clear expectations for when AI should be used, what quality standards apply, and who remains accountable for the final outcome.


Many organizations leave these questions unanswered. FranklinCovey research finds that only[9% of employees](https://resources.franklincovey.com/c/tlg-ai-transformation-human-imperative?x=kzdbGE) say their manager explains how AI will help the business grow, while 40% remain unclear about how AI will affect their jobs. Overall, our data shows that 80% of employees say their[manager’s approach to AI](https://resources.franklincovey.com/c/tlg-great-leader-insight-report?x=j4G37p&lb-mode=overlay) leadership is “hands-off.” When leaders fail to explain the purpose behind AI adoption and communicate expectations for its use, employees create their own assumptions, culminating in pockets of hesitation, fear, and uneven adoption. Clear communication gives teams the confidence to use AI with consistency and sound judgment.


### Reinforce Human Judgment


AI delivers its greatest value when employees treat it as a strategic thinking partner, rather than an answer engine. Leaders shape that mindset by reinforcing the idea that curiosity and critical thinking remain essential to high-quality work.


AI can remove routine work and create additional capacity, though that capacity only creates value when employees redirect it toward work that requires human judgment. The FranklinCovey Institute found that only[35% of employees](https://resources.franklincovey.com/c/tlg-ai-transformation-human-imperative?x=kzdbGE) who regularly use AI reinvest the time they save in innovation, strategic thinking, or deeper client engagement. Productivity gains alone do not improve performance. Leaders determine whether reclaimed time becomes higher-value work or simply more output.


### Model Adaptability


FranklinCovey research finds that, although 70% of employees say that uncertainty is a defining characteristic of their work environment, only[13% of leaders view disruption as a strategic opportunity](https://resources.franklincovey.com/c/tlg-great-leader-insight-report?x=j4G37p&lb-mode=overlay) to innovate and grow. Employees pay close attention to how leaders respond to[disruption](https://www.franklincovey.com/courses/disrupt-everything/) . When leaders remain open to learning and adjust as conditions change, they encourage others to do the same.


AI will continue to reshape how work gets done. Leaders who view change as an ongoing responsibility create teams that adapt more quickly as technology evolves. Their example reinforces the idea that learning is part of the job, that improvement never stops, and that AI adoption is a continuous process rather than a one-time initiative.

## Benefits of Effective AI Change Management


## When you take what AI can bring and combine it with what’s uniquely human, you get hybrid intelligence—a partnership that allows us to think better, make better decisions, and create greater things than either could alone.


— Kory Kogon


Organizations that manage AI change effectively do more than increase adoption. They create the conditions for stronger execution and sustained performance. Key benefits include:


- **Lasting productivity.** AI improves speed and efficiency, though lasting value comes from leaders who help teams apply those gains to better decisions and stronger business outcomes. Recent FranklinCovey research shows that[74% of employees](https://resources.franklincovey.com/c/tlg-ai-transformation-human-imperative?x=kzdbGE) say that AI improves their work, and 80% say they could clearly explain how it increases efficiency or output. Organizations that pair AI capability with strong leadership and other human strengths are better positioned to convert those efficiency gains into sustained performance.
- **Faster decision-making.** Clear priorities, decision rights, and expectations reduce uncertainty. Our research found that[36% of employees](https://resources.franklincovey.com/c/tlg-ai-transformation-human-imperative?x=kzdbGE) hesitate to make decisions without manager approval, while 92% spend multiple hours each week waiting for clarity or resolving misalignment. Reducing ambiguity enables teams to make decisions more confidently and sustain momentum as AI adoption expands.
- **Higher-value work.** AI creates capacity, but leaders determine whether that capacity becomes a competitive advantage. By redirecting employee effort toward work that depends on human judgment, creativity, and collaboration, leaders help teams generate more value instead of simply increasing output.
- **Greater trust.** Recent FranklinCovey Institute research finds that[two-thirds of employees have low trust](https://resources.franklincovey.com/c/tlg-great-leader-insight-report?x=j4G37p&lb-mode=overlay) overall in today’s leaders, and only 42% say they view their own leaders as trusted.[Trust in the workplace](https://www.franklincovey.com/courses/trust-and-inspire/) reduces friction, encourages responsible experimentation, and helps AI become part of everyday work. Employees are more willing to embrace AI when they understand how to use it responsibly and where human judgment remains essential. As confidence grows, AI adoption becomes a sustainable way of working and not a temporary initiative.
