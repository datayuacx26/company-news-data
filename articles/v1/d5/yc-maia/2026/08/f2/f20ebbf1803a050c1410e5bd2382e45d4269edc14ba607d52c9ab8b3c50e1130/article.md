# What AI hears in your arguments that you miss

You know the fight was bad. You probably could not tell anyone exactly how it started.


That is the strange part. An hour later you can quote the worst sentence your partner said, word for word, with the pause before it. What you said in the ninety seconds beforehand is mostly gone. Memory does not store arguments. It stores injuries.


So when the two of you try to sort it out the next day, you are not comparing notes. You are comparing two highlight reels, each edited by the person who got hurt in it.


## Neither of you is a reliable witness to your own fight


This is not a character flaw and it is not you being dramatic. When your body reads a conversation as a threat, attention narrows fast. You track tone, volume, face, the door. Content goes blurry. That is why you can remember precisely how someone looked at you and not remember the sentence that made them look that way.


Then the editing starts. Almost everyone ends up holding a version of the fight where they were basically reasonable and the other person was the one who escalated. Both of you are being honest. Both accounts are incomplete in the same predictable direction, which is[why you and your partner remember the same fight differently](https://www.ourmaia.com/post/the-power-of-three-how-this-ai-can-help-you-resolve-your-fights) and why the argument about what actually happened can go on longer than the original argument.


An outside read of the same twenty minutes has none of that. It was not hurt. It has the whole thing, in order, including the parts you were too flooded to hear.


## Five things that turn up on the read-back


- **The real escalation point, which is earlier than you think.** Couples almost always point to the loudest moment. The turn usually happened three or four minutes before that, in a small flat line like "okay, sure" or "that's not what I said." That is where it stopped being a conversation.
- **The question nobody answered.** One of you asked something direct. It got met with a different grievance, so the original question is still sitting there, unanswered, and it will be back next week wearing a different outfit. This is a big part of[why the same argument keeps coming back](https://www.ourmaia.com/post/we-keep-having-the-same-argument) .
- **The topic drift.** It began about the dishes. By minute six it was about respect. By minute eleven it was about his mother and a trip in 2023. Nobody chose that, and it is the single most common reason a fight ends with both people exhausted and nothing resolved.
- **The repair attempt that sailed past.** Somewhere in there, one of you softened. A hand on an arm, a bad joke, a quiet "I'm not trying to fight with you." The other person, still braced, did not register it. Missed repair attempts are usually invisible to the person who missed them and very obvious on the recording.
- **The gap between the words and the delivery.** "It's fine." "Do whatever you want." "I said I'm sorry." On paper those are agreement, permission and an apology. Heard back, they are none of those things, and the person who said them is often the most surprised.


## The thing couples are least prepared to hear


Here is what comes up over and over in Maia conversations, and it is rarely what people brace for.


Couples arrive convinced the fight was about the specific thing: the money, the plan, the tone. What surfaces in the read-back is that both of them said a version of the same sentence, usually within a minute or two of each other. *You act like I'm not even trying.* *Nothing I do counts.* Two people making the identical complaint, at the same volume, neither one hearing it, because each was too busy defending against the other's.


People go quiet when that gets pointed out. Not because it is a clever observation, but because it moves the question from who is right to what are we both so afraid of. That question has somewhere to go. The other one does not.


## Where this goes badly


The fastest way to ruin all of it is to treat the analysis as evidence. If you walk back in holding a summary and open with "see, it says right here that you interrupted me," you have not gained insight. You have hired a lawyer. Your partner will hear it exactly that way, and the next fight will be about the recording.


Two things keep it useful. Look at your own half first, honestly, before you look at theirs. And go back to your partner with a question rather than a verdict: *I think I shut down around the point where you asked about the weekend. Did it feel like that to you?*


It also matters whether they are willing to look at all. If your partner will not listen to a conversation you both had, will not discuss it, will not consider that there might be another version, then the problem is not that you lack a better tool. It is willingness, which is a different and harder conversation, and one worth having plainly. This is also true of[a partner who shuts down and stops talking during arguments](https://www.ourmaia.com/post/my-partner-shuts-down-during-arguments) .
