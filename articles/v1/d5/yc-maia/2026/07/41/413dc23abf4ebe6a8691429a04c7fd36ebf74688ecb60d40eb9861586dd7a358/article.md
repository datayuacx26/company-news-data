# I feel like I'm the only one trying in my relationship

You made the reservation. You started the hard conversation. You noticed they seemed off, asked about it, and then carried the answer around for the rest of the week.


And at some point a thought shows up and will not leave: *if I stopped doing all of this, would anything happen at all?*


The honest answer up front: that feeling points to two very different situations, and they usually get treated as one. Sometimes your partner is putting in real effort that you are not counting, because it does not look like effort to you. Sometimes you really are the only one holding the relationship up. Most advice on this picks a side. It is worth finding out which one you are in before you decide anything.


## Two situations that feel identical from the inside


The first is a mismatch of currency. One person shows love by planning, initiating, talking things through, repairing after a fight. The other shows it by fixing the car, taking the early shift, handling the thing nobody else wanted to handle. Both are trying. Neither feels chosen, because each is being paid in a currency they do not value, and effort that goes unnoticed quietly stops.


The second is a real imbalance. One person is running the relationship: the check-ins, the calendar, the repair work, the[invisible planning and remembering](https://www.ourmaia.com/post/the-default-parent-carrying-the-mental-load) . The other is a passenger. Not unkind, not hostile, just not steering. This one does not get solved by more appreciation. It gets solved by the passenger deciding to drive, or by you deciding what you can live with.


A quick way to feel the difference. In the first, you feel unseen. In the second, you feel alone.


## The part people leave out when they tell us this


In Maia conversations, this usually arrives as a list. The trip I planned. The counselor I found. The three separate times I brought it up. The list is almost always accurate.


What tends to surface a few minutes later is something the person did not plan to say: they stopped asking a while ago. Not out loud, anyway. They started running a quiet experiment instead. *I will stop reaching out and see if they notice. I will leave it and see how long the dishes sit there.*


It makes sense. Asking stopped working, so waiting to be chosen feels safer than asking again and getting the same nothing back. But the test has a flaw built into it. Your partner does not know they are being tested. They are failing a class nobody told them they were enrolled in, and every day they fail it, your certainty that they do not care gets a little more solid. By the time it finally comes out, it does not come out as a request. It comes out as a verdict.


## Why trying harder usually backfires


The instinct here is to increase your output. Plan the better weekend. Write the more careful message. Be so obviously good to them that they cannot help but match it.


It rarely works, for a plain reason. The more competently you run the relationship, the less any of it requires them. Effort that is never missed is never noticed. Meanwhile the resentment does not sit still while you wait. It leaks out sideways, usually as an edge in your voice over something small, which hands your partner a concrete thing to react to instead of the real problem. Now you are having a fight about your tone.


Scorekeeping is the other trap. Once you are counting, you are collecting evidence, and evidence is for a case. Partners can feel when they are being prosecuted, often before the charge is named, and[people who feel accused defend themselves](https://www.ourmaia.com/post/my-partner-gets-defensive-when-i-bring-up-a-problem) rather than step up.


## How to find out which one you are in


Stop testing and run something clearer instead. Two things, in this order.


**Say the whole thing once, plainly.** Not the list. The feeling underneath the list, plus one specific ask. Something like: "I feel like I am carrying us right now, and I am tired, and I do not want to be the only one who notices when we are drifting. Can you plan one thing for the two of us this month, start to finish, without me involved?" It is small on purpose. Small asks are legible. "Try harder" is not.


**Then leave real space, and do not fill it.** This is the hard half. If you ask them to handle something and then handle it yourself on day three because it was not moving fast enough, you have answered your own question and learned nothing. Let it be a little worse for a while. That gap is the only place their effort has room to show up.


What you are watching for is not a perfect performance. It is movement. Did they do it, or something in the neighborhood of it? Did they come back to the conversation on their own without you reopening it? When[they said sorry, did anything actually change](https://www.ourmaia.com/post/my-partner-apologizes-but-nothing-changes) after? One real attempt, even a clumsy one, tells you more than a month of good intentions.


## If nothing moves


Give it a few weeks, not a few days. But if you have said it clearly, made a specific and small ask, left the space, and the answer is still nothing, then you have your information. The problem at that point is not that they do not understand you. It is that they are not willing, and no better phrasing fixes willingness.


That is worth saying without softening it, because people in this spot tend to keep upgrading their technique for years. You can love someone who is not going to meet you. What you do with that is a separate question, and a fair one to sit with slowly: whether this is[a rough patch or something you should stop trying to fix](https://www.ourmaia.com/post/should-i-break-up-or-keep-trying) , and how much of yourself has[already gone into holding it together](https://www.ourmaia.com/post/ive-lost-myself-in-my-relationship) .


One thing worth separating out. If any of this involves fear, or being punished for asking, that is a different situation than an unequal relationship, and it is worth talking to someone you trust or the National Domestic Violence Hotline (1-800-799-7233, US).


If you are somewhere in the middle of this and genuinely cannot tell whether you are unseen or actually alone, that is a good thing to talk through out loud with the real details in it: what you have asked for, what you quietly stopped asking for, and what you would need to see to feel differently.
