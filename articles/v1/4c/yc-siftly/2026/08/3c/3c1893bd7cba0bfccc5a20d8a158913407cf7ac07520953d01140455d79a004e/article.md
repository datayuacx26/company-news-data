# 10 Best Semrush Alternatives to Try in 2026

### **TL;DR**


- **Best overall alternative:** Siftly
- **Best for budget-conscious teams:** Airefs
- **Best for content teams already writing at volume:** Surfer SEO
- **Best for HubSpot CRM users:** HubSpot
- **Best traditional SEO suite alternative:** Ahrefs


Semrush has spent years as one of the two default answers to "what SEO tool should we buy," and it earned that spot with a genuinely massive feature set: keyword research, competitor analysis, technical site audits, and a backlink index that rivals Ahrefs. In 2026, it folded its old separate AI Visibility Toolkit into unified "SEO & AI Search" plans, so every tier now comes with some level of AI-search tracking baked in rather than sold as an add-on.


That consolidation sounds convenient, but it hasn't fixed the complaints that have followed Semrush for years, and it's introduced a few new ones. Full coverage of Claude and Grok is still locked behind Enterprise pricing, the AI prompt allotment on the entry AI tier runs out fast for a brand that's actively being monitored, and the billing and cancellation issues that show up across review sites haven't gone anywhere. If you're evaluating whether to renew or switch, it's worth seeing what the rest of the category actually offers.


## Why You Might Be Looking For A Semrush Alternative


Semrush remains a genuinely capable platform, but a specific set of complaints keeps surfacing across review sites.


1. **Billing and cancellation issues are the single most common complaint:** Semrush sits around 1.9 to 2.8 out of 5 on Trustpilot across 900 to 1,200-plus reviews, and the BBB has a documented case of a customer charged $531.91 a month for 10 months after they'd already cancelled. There's no self-serve cancel button.
2. **Cost comes up constantly in reviews:** G2 tags "Expensive" in over 540 reviews and "High Pricing" in another 468, which is a lot of repetition for one complaint.
3. **The pricing structure itself confuses buyers:** One G2 reviewer put it plainly: "I never feel sure which capabilities I actually have access to."
4. **The entry AI tier runs out of room fast:** The Starter plan's AI-search allotment is 50 prompts a day, which a brand doing any real monitoring will burn through quickly.
5. **Claude and Grok are Enterprise-only:** Despite marketing AI visibility as a core feature now, two of the more widely used AI engines are gated behind a custom-priced tier most buyers never reach.

## How to Make the Right Choice


Choosing the right Semrush alternative depends on why you're actually looking to switch, not just which tool has the biggest feature comparison chart. Here's what's worth thinking through.


- **Are you replacing Semrush, or just its AI layer?** Some tools on this list, like Ahrefs and SE Ranking, are trying to be a full Semrush replacement. Others, like Siftly, Surfer SEO, and Writesonic, are narrower by design. Decide whether you actually need one platform doing everything, or whether you'd rather pick a specialist for AI visibility and keep your existing SEO tool for the rest.
- **Monitoring vs. execution:** A lot of tools in this category stop at telling you where you stand. Fewer close the loop by actually writing content or running outreach based on what they find. Decide how much of the follow-through work you want the tool to do for you.
- **Engine coverage that isn't gated behind Enterprise:** Check what you actually get at the tier you'll realistically pay for, not what the marketing page claims. Semrush, Writesonic, and Ahrefs all gate Claude and Grok behind pricier tiers, and it's worth knowing that before you commit.
- **Pricing transparency:** Semrush's billing complaints are the most consistent theme in its reviews. Before switching, check whether a tool has clear self-serve pricing and an easy cancellation path, rather than annual contracts and features that are hard to pin down.
- **Attribution to real traffic:** Visibility data on its own doesn't tell you whether any of it matters. Favor platforms that connect AI mentions and citations back to GA4 or Search Console, so you can see whether the work is actually moving the numbers.
