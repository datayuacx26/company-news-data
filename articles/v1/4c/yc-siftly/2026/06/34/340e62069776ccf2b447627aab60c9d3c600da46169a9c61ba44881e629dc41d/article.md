## Integration and Alert Systems: How Tools Enable Cross-Functional Workflows


### Read-Only Monitoring Vs. Write-Enabled Integrations


Enterprise AI search monitoring platforms diverge into two architectural camps: read-only monitoring (which tracks visibility without modifying external systems) and write-enabled integrations (which push data into CRM or marketing automation platforms for attribution). Siftly uses a read-only architecture, enabling monitoring without requiring API write access to downstream systems. Write-enabled integrations, by contrast, require CRM API trust and formal compliance review because they inject third-party visibility data into the system-of-record for revenue attribution. This is a deliberate trade-off, not a feature gap: read-only tools reduce attack surface and accelerate security sign-off, while write-enabled tools create the pipeline-attribution layer that finance and RevOps teams require to connect mentions to revenue outcomes.


### Alert Delivery Mechanisms for Team Coordination


Alert delivery infrastructure determines whether insights reach content teams, PR teams, and product marketing in real time or require manual dashboard checks. The spectrum runs from email alerts (asynchronous, no integration required) through dashboard-only visibility (manual pull, suitable for weekly reviews) to Slack/Teams notifications (synchronous, native to team workflows) and API webhooks (machine-to-machine, enabling custom automation). Free-tier constraints shape operational viability: tools offering 1 free check per day across 150 AI queries force teams into dashboard-only workflows that don't scale beyond solo practitioners. Paid tiers unlock higher query volumes and multi-surface delivery, email, Slack, Teams, and web, enabling the cross-functional alert distribution that enterprise teams need to act on competitive shifts as they happen.


### Attribution Gap: Visibility Monitoring Vs. Revenue Tracking


No platform offers a defensible citations-to-revenue calculation model. Visibility monitoring tracks whether AI engines cite a brand; attribution integration connects those mentions to pipeline impact. The integration gap exists because no source documents how these tools integrate with CRM or marketing automation platforms for attribution. Teams seeking revenue-attribution must treat vendor integration claims as directional guidance requiring direct verification during the sales cycle, not as documented feature guarantees. Visibility monitoring remains a top-of-funnel awareness layer; connecting AI mentions to revenue outcomes demands separate attribution infrastructure, analytics platforms, CRM tagging, and marketing automation rules, that sits outside the monitoring tool's architectural scope.
