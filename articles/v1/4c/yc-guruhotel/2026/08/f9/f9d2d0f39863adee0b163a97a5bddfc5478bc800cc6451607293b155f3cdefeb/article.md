# The biggest hotel chains in the world — and how independents compete

## The giants at a glance


Marriott International leads global room count (30+ brands from Ritz-Carlton to Courtyard). Hilton and IHG follow with broad midscale-to-luxury portfolios. Wyndham operates the most properties, concentrated in economy and midscale franchising. Accor dominates in Europe, Jin Jiang and H World in China, and Choice Hotels rounds out the US franchise heavyweights.


## Chains rarely own hotels


The modern chain is asset-light: most branded hotels are owned by investors and franchised or managed by the chain. The chain sells distribution (loyalty program, brand.com, GDS presence), standards and purchasing power — and charges franchise, marketing and loyalty fees that commonly add up to a low-double-digit share of room revenue.


## Soft brands: the chains courting independents


Collections like Marriott's Autograph, Hilton's Curio and IHG's Vignette let independent hotels keep their name while plugging into chain distribution and loyalty — at chain-fee prices. It's a real option for large independents, and clear evidence that distribution, not the building, is what chains actually sell.


## The independent playbook


You can replicate the chain's core advantage — a strong direct channel — without the fees: a fast official website with a real booking engine, presence in Google's hotel results, guest data you own for repeat marketing, and OTA listings used for discovery rather than dependency. That's the stack chains charge double-digit fees to provide.
