# What is a GDS? Global distribution systems in hotel distribution, explained

A GDS (global distribution system) is a network that connects hotels, airlines and car rentals to travel agents and corporate booking tools. The big three are Amadeus, Sabre and Travelport. For hotels, a GDS mainly brings corporate and travel-agent business — a different channel from OTAs and your own website.


## How a GDS works


Travel agents and corporate travel platforms search the GDS for inventory. Hotels publish rates and availability into the system — usually through a CRS or channel-management connection — and pay per-booking fees plus, often, a commission to the booking agent. Bookings flow back into the hotel's systems like any other channel.


## GDS vs OTA vs direct: three different channels


The GDS reaches professional intermediaries (agents, corporate programs). OTAs like Booking.com and Expedia reach consumers directly. Your own website is the direct channel — the only one with no commission and full ownership of the guest relationship. A healthy strategy prices and allocates inventory deliberately across all three.


## Does an independent hotel need a GDS?


It depends on your demand mix. Properties near business districts, airports or convention centers can win meaningful corporate volume from the GDS. Leisure-driven independents often get better returns from OTA visibility plus a strong direct channel, adding GDS later through a representation company if corporate demand appears.


## Where the direct channel fits


Whatever your GDS/OTA mix, every channel you add makes the commission-free one more valuable. Travelers who discover you anywhere often verify on your official website; if it loads fast, shows live rates and books in a few taps, you capture that demand at zero acquisition cost.
