# Late checkout: what it is, what it costs and how hotels should price it

## How guests get late checkout


Ask the front desk the night before or at breakfast — availability depends on that day's arrivals. Loyalty tiers and suites often include it. Some hotels sell it at booking or pre-arrival as a fixed-price extra, which guarantees it instead of leaving it to luck. Checkout at 14:00–15:00 is the typical paid window; beyond that, expect a half-day or full-day charge.


## Designing the policy as a hotel


Tie it to operations: grant freely on low-occupancy days (goodwill costs nothing), price it on high-turnover days when housekeeping is tight. A simple grid works: free until 13:00 when available, fixed fee until 15:00, half-day rate after. Publish the policy — ambiguity creates front-desk friction and inconsistent revenue.


## Late checkout as an upsell product


Sold pre-arrival at a fixed price, late checkout is one of the highest-acceptance extras: it needs no inventory beyond a clean schedule and monetizes hours the room would sit idle anyway. Pair it with early check-in for a 'flexible stay' bundle — travelers with awkward flight times buy it happily.


## Keep it synced with housekeeping


Every late checkout shifts a room's cleaning window and can collide with an early arrival. The PMS should be the single source: register the new departure time on the folio so housekeeping boards, arrivals planning and any connected systems reflect reality. Verbal agreements at the desk are how overlaps happen.
