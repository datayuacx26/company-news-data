# The hotel front desk: functions, shifts and the tech that runs it

## Core functions of the front desk


Check-in (identity, registration card, payment guarantee, key), check-out (folio review, payment, feedback), reservations management for walk-ins and phone bookings, guest services (requests, complaints, local guidance), cash and card handling, and constant coordination: housekeeping statuses, maintenance tickets, early arrivals and late departures.


## Shifts and roles


Morning (7–15h): check-outs, billing, departures rush. Afternoon (15–23h): check-ins, guest requests peak. Night (23–7h): night audit, security, late arrivals. Small properties cross-train one person per shift; larger hotels split roles into receptionist, concierge, bell staff and a front office manager who owns scheduling and standards.


## The tech stack behind the desk


The PMS is the operating system: room assignments, folios, housekeeping boards. Around it: a payment terminal or gateway, a channel manager feeding OTA reservations, keycard systems and increasingly guest-messaging tools. When the booking engine and payments integrate with the PMS, the desk stops re-typing reservations and starts hosting.


## The front desk as a revenue center


Reception touches every guest at the two highest-intent moments — arrival and departure. Trained teams monetize both: room upgrades and early check-in at arrival, late checkout and rebooking at departure. A direct-booking culture starts here too: invite guests to book their next stay on the official website before they leave.
