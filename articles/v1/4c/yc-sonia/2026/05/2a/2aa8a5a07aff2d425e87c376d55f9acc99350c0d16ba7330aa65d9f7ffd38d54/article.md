# Building a Nightly Wind-Down Routine


Sonia Team · May 2, 2026 · 2 min read


A consistent wind-down routine signals to your body that the day is over. Here’s a simple framework for building one that actually sticks.


Sleep quality has less to do with what happens at midnight and more to do with what happens in the hour before it. A predictable wind-down routine helps cue the body that it’s time to shift out of "go" mode, which can make falling asleep feel less like a struggle.


The idea is repetition, not perfection. A routine works because your body starts to associate the sequence of steps with rest — the same way a commute can put you in "work mode" before you even reach the office. It doesn’t need to be elaborate to be effective.


> A routine works because your body starts to associate the sequence of steps with rest.


A simple framework to start with:


- Pick a consistent start time, even if it shifts by 30 minutes some nights. Consistency matters more than precision.
- Dim the lights and step away from screens where you can. Bright light and fast-moving content both signal alertness, which works against the goal.
- Do one calming, low-stakes activity — reading, stretching, a warm shower, or simply talking through the day to clear your head before bed.
- Keep the bedroom for sleep. If your mind is still racing, it can help to get thoughts out of your head and onto paper (or into a conversation) rather than turning them over while lying in bed.


If racing thoughts are the main obstacle, that’s worth addressing directly rather than working around. Some people find it helpful to talk through what’s on their mind earlier in the evening, so bedtime isn’t the first quiet moment their brain has had all day to process things.


A wind-down routine won’t fix every sleep issue, and persistent insomnia is worth bringing to a doctor. But for the ordinary nights when your mind just won’t quiet down, a consistent routine — including a few minutes to talk through the day — is one of the most reliable tools available.
