###


**My Time at Portia**


#


**Platform:** iOS, Android | **Price:** $7.99 one-time purchase


**[My Time at Portia](https://portia.pathea.net/)** puts you in a post-apocalyptic world that’s quietly rebuilding itself. You inherit your father’s run-down workshop in a small town. The game is partly about restoring it through crafting and commissions. Mostly, though, it’s about the community: a large NPC cast with distinct personalities and schedules, each with backstories that deepen the longer you stay.


What it nails is relationship depth. For example, friendships develop through shared events, commissions, and gifts that reflect each character’s actual interests rather than generic item preferences. For players who lived for Stardew’s heart events and romance options, My Time at Portia runs on the same slow-burn rhythm with more cinematic delivery, like jealousy penalties and rivals.


However, the iOS version hasn’t been updated since January 2022 and may have compatibility issues on recent devices. The crafting layer is also denser upfront than most NPC-focused players will expect. In contrast to the relaxing, play-at-your-own-pace rhythm in *Stardew* , *Portia* has timed main quests you need to complete with other builders, which bring a faster pace and a bit of competition.

**Wylde Flowers**


#


**Platform:** iOS only (Apple Arcade) | **Price:** Included with Apple Arcade (~$6.99/month)


**[Wylde Flowers](https://wyldeflowersgame.com/)** is a farming and magic sim where the story carries more weight than the harvests. You play as Tara, who travels to the coastal town of Fairhaven to help her grandmother with the family farm, in a complicated community and a secret witches’ coven. The mystery unfolds through the relationships: a factional rivalry, romance options with genuine depth, and characters who reveal new layers the closer you get.


Among mobile games like Stardew Valley here, Wylde Flowers is the one that nails the most in the emotional story and relatable characters: the island mystery gives each NPC arc a through-line that Stardew Valley’s character stories sometimes lack. It’s built for slow discovery, the story progresses at your own pace and the cozy life sim feel holds even when the narrative gets complicated.


The limitation is firm: The game falls short in character creation and open-world experience like Stardew. Wylde Flowers is an Apple Arcade exclusive with no Android version. Still, if you’re on iPhone and already subscribe, it’s a solid option for mobile RPG games like Stardew Valley.


###


**Potion Permit**


#


**Platform:** iOS, Android | **Price:** $9.99


**[Potion Permit](https://playdigious.com/games/potion-permit/)** starts with the town not wanting you there. You arrive in Moonbury as a chemist hired to treat the villagers, and the community is openly skeptical. Progress means earning trust through daily interactions, completed requests, and gifts that reflect what you’ve actually learned about each resident.


Relationship-building feels like something that happened rather than something you unlocked. The potion-crafting loop (gathering ingredients, diagnosing ailments, brewing the right formula) is distinct from Stardew’s farming, and as a result it reads as a genuine alternative for players looking for games like Stardew Valley for Android or iOS with a different core loop entirely.


Where it falls short: the cast is smaller than Pelican Town’s, and the main story arc resolves faster than most Stardew players expect. However, it works well as a focused weekend playthrough or a regular check-in game. Don’t go in expecting 100+ hours of NPC content.
