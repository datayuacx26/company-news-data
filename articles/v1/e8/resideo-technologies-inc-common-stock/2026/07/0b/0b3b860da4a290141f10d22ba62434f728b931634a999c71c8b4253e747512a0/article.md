## Frequently Asked Questions:


**What is the Fortune 500?**


The Fortune 500 is an annual ranking published by Fortune that lists the 500 largest U.S. companies based on total annual revenue. The list is widely regarded as one of the most recognized benchmarks of corporate size and business performance in the United States.


**How are companies selected for the Fortune 500?**


Companies included in the Fortune 500 must be incorporated in the United States, operate in the U.S. and file financial statements with a government agency. Eligible organizations include public companies, certain private companies, cooperatives and mutual insurance companies that meet Fortune's reporting requirements.


**How are the Fortune 500 companies ranked?**


Companies are ranked by total revenues for their respective fiscal years.


**What companies are excluded from the Fortune 500?**


The Fortune 500 excludes private companies that do not file financial statements with a government agency, companies incorporated outside the United States and U.S. companies that are consolidated into the financial statements of another domestic or foreign company. Companies that fail to report complete financial statements for at least three quarters of the fiscal year are also excluded.


**What financial period does the 2026 Fortune 500 ranking cover?**


The 2026 Fortune 500 is based on financial results for fiscal years ending on or before January 31, 2026. Unless otherwise noted, figures reflect the year ended December 31, 2025.


**Why is the Fortune 500 important?**


The Fortune 500 serves as a benchmark for the scale, financial performance and economic impact of major U.S. companies. Inclusion on the list reflects significant business growth and positions companies among the largest organizations operating in the United States.


**What is the U.S News & World Report Best Companies to Work For list?**


The U.S News & World Report Best Companies to Work For list evaluates a combined 3,900 publicly and privately owned companies to help employees and job seekers make decisions about workplaces that may be a good fit for them. They offer an analysis of organizations that best meet consumer preferences across factors including quality of pay and benefits, work-life balance and flexibility, job and company stability, physical and psychological comfort, belongingness and esteem, and career opportunities and professional development.
