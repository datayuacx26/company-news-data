# What does per diem mean? How to create a per diem expense policy

Definition


**Per diem meaning:** A fixed daily allowance paid to employees to cover business travel expenses such as lodging, meals, and incidental costs. The term comes from Latin, where *diem* means “day,” making the full phrase translate to “for each day.”


The world has become increasingly globalized, so it's no surprise that business travel has become an even more essential component for many organizations that want to expand their reach and foster relationships. However, managing the financial aspect of these trips can get a little... *complicated* .


One way companies often use to streamline[travel expenses](https://use.expensify.com/resource-center/guides/guide-to-travel-expenses) is the "per diem system," which offers employees a predetermined daily allowance for expenses incurred while traveling. Understanding how per diem works and establishing a clear policy can help businesses control costs while providing their employees with flexibility.


In this article, we'll answer “What does per diem mean?”, compare it with other expense reimbursement methods, and guide you through creating a comprehensive per diem[travel expense policy](https://use.expensify.com/resource-center/guides/travel-and-expense-policy-best-practices-with-expensify) tailored to your organization's needs.


## Key takeaways


- **Per diem is a fixed daily allowance that covers business travel essentials:** Typically lodging, meals, and incidentals, so teams can travel without collecting itemized receipts for every purchase.
- **Rates are location-based and updated annually in the U.S.:** The General Services Administration publishes federal per diem rates each fiscal year, starting October 1.
- **For international trips, per diem rates are set by the U.S. Department of State and can change frequently:** Verify the destination's rate before travel at the State Department per diem directory.
- **Tax treatment depends on compliance:** Under an accountable plan, per diem up to federal rates is generally not taxable, while amounts above those rates are taxable. Employees must substantiate the business purpose, dates, and destination.

## What does per diem mean?


What is per diem? The **per diem** definition (sometimes spelled as one word: “perdiem”) starts with its Latin roots: "for each day." In the business world, it's a daily allowance that companies give employees to cover travel expenses including lodging, meals, and incidentals.


**Incidentals** are minor expenses that come up during[business travel](https://use.expensify.com/travel) , such as tips for hotel staff, baggage handling fees, or dry cleaning services.


The main advantage of per diem is simplicity. Instead of requiring employees to save every receipt (which usually includes[itemized receipts](https://use.expensify.com/resource-center/guides/what-are-itemized-receipts) ) from a business trip and submit detailed[expense reports](https://use.expensify.com/expense-reports) , companies provide a flat daily rate.


Employees receive their allowance and can spend it as needed, without the administrative burden of itemizing every meal or taxi ride.


## How per diem works


Companies typically determine per diem rates based on travel destination and the expected cost of living in that area. For example, an employee traveling to New York City might receive a higher daily allowance than someone heading to a smaller city because lodging and meals cost more in high-cost cities.


The same principle applies globally. Companies headquartered in London, Berlin, or Singapore set location-based rates that reflect local costs, whether employees are traveling domestically or internationally.


Business travel continues to represent a significant portion of corporate spending. According to the U.S. Travel Association,[business travel generated $312 billion](https://www.ustravel.org/issues/reignite-person-meetings-and-events) in spending in 2024, with individual work-related trips accounting for roughly $186 billion of that total.


So, as organizations manage large travel budgets, standardized reimbursement approaches like per diem help control costs and simplify[expense management](https://use.expensify.com/expense-management) .


### Who receives per diem?


Per diem allowances are typically provided for:


**Employees traveling for business purposes.** This includes sales representatives visiting clients, consultants working at client sites, executives attending conferences, or anyone whose job requires overnight business travel.


**Government workers and contractors.** Federal, state, and local government employees often receive per diem for official travel, following established government rates.


**Temporary workers on assignment.** Professionals working on short-term projects away from their home base may qualify for per diem rather than relocation assistance.


### When employees receive per diem payments


Companies handle per diem payments in different ways, depending on their cash flow policies and administrative preferences:


-


**Before travel:** Some companies provide per diem as an advance payment, giving employees cash or a deposit before the trip begins. This approach helps employees avoid using personal funds for business expenses.


-


**After travel:** Other organizations[reimburse employees](https://use.expensify.com/travel-expense-reimbursement) for per diem after the trip is complete. The traveler submits documentation showing the business purpose, destination, and dates of travel, then receives payment.


-


**With payroll:** Some companies add per diem to regular paychecks during the travel period. This method integrates per diem payments into existing payroll systems rather than processing separate reimbursements.


### Partial days and unused per diem


Most companies prorate per diem for partial travel days, particularly the first and last day of a trip. A **partial day** occurs when an employee begins travel partway through the day or returns home before the day ends. Rather than paying a full day's allowance when someone leaves at 3 PM or arrives back at 10 AM, companies typically provide 75% or 50% of the standard rate.


What happens to leftover per diem funds varies by company policy. In following federal guidelines, many organizations allow employees to keep any unspent allowance. This approach can actually encourage cost-conscious behavior since employees have a financial incentive to spend wisely.


**An important note** : employees may be subject to reporting this unspent allowance on their tax return, so they should check with their tax preparer if they’re unsure what’s required for their personal situation.


Other companies require employees to return unused funds or provide receipts showing how the allowance was spent.

## What expenses does per diem cover?


Per diem typically covers three main expense categories during business travel, though the specific structure can vary by organization.


### Lodging


**Lodging** allowances cover hotel or accommodation costs during business trips. Many per diem systems, particularly federal rates, separate lodging from other expenses because hotel costs can vary dramatically by location.


Some companies include lodging in a single, combined per diem rate, while others provide a separate lodging reimbursement alongside meals and incidentals.


### Meals and incidental expenses


**M&IE (meals and incidental expenses)** combines daily food costs with small miscellaneous expenses. Under a per diem policy, meals typically include breakfast, lunch, and dinner eaten during business travel.


Incidentals bundled with meals include things like: tips for service staff, porter and baggage fees, dry cleaning or laundry during extended trips, and other minor personal expenses related to travel.


Federal per diem rates published by the General Services Administration separate lodging allowances from meals and incidental expenses (M&IE), with rates varying by location and updated each fiscal year.


### Transportation


**Transportation** expenses such as flights, rental cars, and mileage are usually reimbursed separately and are not part of the standard per diem allowance.


### What does per diem not cover?


A per diem allowance is designed to cover specific business travel expenses, not every cost incurred during a trip. In most cases, it does not include:


-


**Transportation costs** , such as airfare, train tickets, rental cars, ride-share services, or mileage driven in a personal vehicle.


-


**Entertainment expenses** , including concerts, sporting events, or other recreational activities.


-


**Personal purchases** , such as alcoholic beverages, laundry services, dry cleaning, or gifts, unless your company's travel policy specifically allows them.


-


**Daily commuting costs** between an employee's home and their usual workplace.


-


**Expenses from personal travel days** , side trips, or other activities that are unrelated to business purposes.


For these types of expenses, ensure you're[tracking and submitting receipts](https://use.expensify.com/receipt-scanning-app) to get paid back. If employees use their personal vehicles for work travel, employers may reimburse mileage separately using the IRS standard mileage rate.

## Types of per diem


Per diem rates and structures vary, depending on whether you're dealing with government agencies, private companies, or international travel.


### Government per diem


The General Services Administration (GSA) sets **federal per diem rates** for U.S. government employees traveling domestically. The GSA is the federal agency responsible for managing government property and establishing standard reimbursement rates. These rates serve as benchmarks that many private companies use when creating their own per diem policies.


Federal rates distinguish between standard locations and high-cost areas. Travel to major cities like San Francisco, New York, or Washington, D.C., qualifies for higher per diem amounts than smaller markets with lower costs of living.


Many countries outside the United States maintain similar government-established per diem systems.[European Union institutions publish per diem rates](https://www.eeas.europa.eu/sites/default/files/documents/2024/Annexe%20M%20Current%20per%20diem%20rates.pdf) for staff travel, while individual member states like Germany, France, and the Netherlands establish their own official rates for public sector employees.


Companies headquartered in these regions often reference government rates as starting points for their corporate policies, just as U.S. companies reference GSA rates.


### Corporate per diem


**Private companies** can establish their own per diem rates based on business needs and budget considerations. Some organizations adopt GSA rates as a starting point, then customize them based on where employees typically travel and how much the company wants to spend on travel allowances.


Companies headquartered outside the United States follow similar approaches, often referencing their home country's government rates or industry standards. A company based in Amsterdam might use Dutch government rates for European travel while establishing separate rates for trips to Asia or North America.


The core principle remains consistent across regions: set location-based allowances that balance cost control with employee support.


Companies might set flat rates for simplicity, tiered rates based on destination cost levels, or role-based rates that provide different allowances for executives versus individual contributors.


### International per diem


**International business travel** follows a different per diem structure than domestic trips in the United States. While[federal domestic rates](https://www.gsa.gov/travel/plan-book/per-diem-rates) are published by the General Services Administration (GSA),[international per diem rates](https://allowances.state.gov/web920/per_diem.asp) are established by the U.S. Department of State.


These rates vary significantly, depending on the destination and are updated regularly based on local lodging costs, currency fluctuations, and economic conditions. Companies that rely on standardized travel allowances often reference the official State Department per diem tables when creating[international travel expense policies](https://use.expensify.com/resource-center/guides/global-expense-policy) .


Employers and travelers can verify the current rates for specific destinations using the U.S. Department of State's international per diem directory.


## What is a per diem job?


Beyond travel allowances, you might wonder, “What is per diem work?” **Per diem work** is working on an as-needed basis without guaranteed hours or a set schedule. Think of it as on-call employment where workers fill in when regular staff are unavailable.


### Per diem employee meaning


A **per diem employee** is someone hired to work irregular schedules, typically filling shifts as needed rather than following a consistent schedule. This employment structure is common in industries with fluctuating staffing needs, such as:


-


**Healthcare:** Nurses, therapists, and medical technicians who cover shifts at hospitals or clinics when regular staff call out or during busy periods.


-


**Hospitality:** Hotel workers and event staff brought in during peak seasons or special events.


-


**Education:** Substitute teachers who fill in for absent regular teachers.


Per diem employees are different from part-time workers who have regular scheduled hours, and from full-time employees with consistent schedules and comprehensive benefits.


### Per diem pay and work scheduling


What is per diem pay? Per diem employees typically receive higher hourly rates than their full-time counterparts to compensate for the lack of guaranteed hours and reduced benefits. The pay premium offsets the uncertainty of irregular work schedules.


Scheduling works on short notice. Per diem workers might get called the day before a shift or even the morning of, depending on immediate staffing needs. This flexibility benefits employers who need to cover gaps quickly, while giving workers the freedom to accept or decline shifts based on their availability.


## Benefits of per diem


Per diem offers several advantages for both companies and employees managing business travel expenses.


### Simplified administration


Per diem eliminates the tedious process of collecting, organizing, and reviewing countless receipts. Finance teams spend less time scrutinizing expense reports, and employees avoid the hassle of documenting every meal and taxi tip. This reduction in paperwork frees up resources for more strategic work.


### Improved budget predictability


Companies can forecast travel costs more accurately when using fixed daily rates. Instead of wondering whether a team member will spend $40 or $140 on dinner in a particular city, finance teams know exactly what the daily cost will be. This predictability makes budget planning and expense forecasting significantly more reliable.


### Increased policy compliance


Clear, daily limits reduce disputes over appropriate spending and make policy violations easier to identify. When the daily allowance is transparent and predetermined, there's less room for interpretation about what constitutes reasonable expenses.


### Potential cost savings


Employees who know they can keep unused funds may choose less expensive options, benefiting both themselves and the company. This natural incentive to spend wisely can lead to meaningful savings over time without requiring micromanagement of individual spending decisions.


## Challenges and downsides of per diem


Per diem policies also present some practical challenges that organizations should consider:


-


**Geographic variations:** Standard per diem rates may not adequately cover actual costs in unusually expensive cities or during peak travel seasons, leaving employees to cover the difference from their own pockets.


-


**Perceived unfairness:** Employees traveling to high-cost areas may feel shortchanged if the per diem doesn't match their actual expenses, while those in lower-cost locations might receive more than they need.


-


**Tracking complexity:** Managing different rates for different locations, adjusting for partial days, and handling exceptions requires administrative systems that can handle geographic variations.


-


**Policy abuse:** Without proper oversight, some employees might claim per diem for trips that didn't occur or extend business travel unnecessarily to collect additional allowances.

### 1. Research GSA rates and industry standards


Start by reviewing current federal per diem rates as a benchmark. The GSA publishes updated rates each October covering hundreds of locations across the United States. These rates reflect government research into actual lodging and meal costs in each area.


For companies headquartered outside the United States, research your home country's government-established rates or industry benchmarks. Many European countries publish official per diem rates that serve the same benchmarking function as GSA rates in the U.S.


Next, consider industry standards and what competitors typically provide. Some sectors have established norms for travel allowances.


Finally, analyze your company's historical travel data to understand where employees travel most frequently and what they typically spend.


### 2. Define covered expenses and limits


Specify exactly which expenses the per diem covers and any daily caps or restrictions. Clear documentation prevents confusion and disputes later.


Decide whether lodging is included in the per diem or reimbursed separately. Clarify which meals are covered and whether there are different rates for different meal times. List which incidentals are included and which require separate approval.


Document any expenses explicitly excluded from per diem coverage, such as personal entertainment or alcohol beyond reasonable business meals.


### 3. Establish approval and documentation processes


Outline who needs to approve per diem requests before travel occurs. Specify what minimal documentation employees must provide, such as proof of business purpose, travel dates, and destination. Create a clear exception handling process for unusual circumstances that fall outside standard policy.


Even though per diem reduces receipt collection, employees should still document their travel itinerary and business justification to maintain IRS compliance under accountable plan rules.


### 4. Communicate the policy to employees


Make the policy easily accessible through your employee handbook, company intranet, or expense management platform. Provide training sessions for frequent travelers so they understand how to calculate their allowances and what documentation they need to submit.


Create quick reference guides or FAQs addressing common questions about partial days, weekend travel, and how to handle expenses that exceed the allowance.

## FAQs about per diem


-


###


Per diem payments are generally not taxable if they do not exceed federal rates and the employee provides basic documentation of business travel purpose, dates, and destination. Amounts exceeding federal limits are typically taxable and must be reported as income.


-


###


Per diem typically covers an employee's lodging, meals, and incidental expenses (M&IE) incurred while traveling for business. Incidental expenses may include tips and other small travel-related costs.


Some employers provide a full per diem that includes both lodging and M&IE, while others reimburse lodging separately and pay a per diem only for meals and incidental expenses.


-


###


GSA per diem rates vary by location and are updated annually each October. Employers should check the GSA website for current rates specific to each travel destination. Rates range from standard amounts for most locations to higher rates for expensive cities.


-


###


Many companies allow employees to keep unused per diem funds, which can encourage cost-conscious travel spending. Federal per diem policies permit this, though individual company policies may differ. Some organizations require employees to return unspent allowances.


-


###


Same-day travel typically qualifies for reduced per diem or meals-only allowances, depending on company policy and whether the trip includes an overnight stay. Many companies do not provide lodging per diem for same-day trips but may offer a reduced meal allowance.


-


###


When expenses exceed the per diem allowance, employees typically cover the difference out of pocket unless company policy allows exceptions with manager approval. Some companies permit employees to submit receipts for actual expenses instead of taking per diem if they know costs will be higher than the standard rate.
