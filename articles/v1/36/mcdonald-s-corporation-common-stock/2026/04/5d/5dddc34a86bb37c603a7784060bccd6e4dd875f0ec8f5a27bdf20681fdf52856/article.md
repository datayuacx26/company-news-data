# A Collaborative Tech Innovation Day: Where Big Ideas Begin

Behind-the-scenes of a day built for bold thinking, big questions, and breakthrough ideas — and what it revealed about innovating at scale through partnership.


*by: Henry Lyles, Director, Operations Excellence & Kris Heller, Manager, Operations Excellence*


Quick Bytes:


- McDonald’s Enterprise Products and Platforms (EPP) hosted a Tech Innovation Day that brought together Business and Tech teams to surface 35+ actionable ideas
- Teams found opportunities for AI and automation to make a big impact across enterprise systems
- When given the space, intentional collaboration models can shape the future of innovation


When McDonald’s signed a strategic partnership to accelerate the transformation of our global enterprise systems through AI and cloud solutions, the goal wasn’t just to deploy new technology. It was to rethink how we solve problems together.


For the McDonald’s Domain Automation team, that partnership represented a meaningful inflection point in a journey more than seven years in the making: using automation to simplify complexity, improve processes, and rethink how work gets done across the enterprise.


That partnership came to life this year when the Enterprise Products and Platforms (EPP) team hosted a Tech Innovation Day, a collaborative event designed to spark creativity, empower our employees, challenge assumptions, and explore how new technology can add value to the systems we rely on every day.


**Thinking beyond the status quo**
The day was built around a simple but powerful set of questions:
*What if? Imagine the possibilities. How might we do this differently together?*


Across focused sessions within key EPP Domains — Supply Chain, Finance, People, and Corporate Systems — participants explored how AI, automation, and intelligent solutions could be applied to real challenges they face today. These sessions were structured like a job fair or convention floor, with booths dedicated to major business categories such as People, Finance, Corporate, Supply Chain, Operations Excellence, and Tech Deployment.


Each booth showcased multiple business case examples demonstrating how AI and technology could be applied, supported by live demos and subject matter experts to answer questions and discuss concepts. Participants could also scan QR codes or submit digital intake forms to request deeper dives or follow‑up conversations.


These weren’t theoretical conversations about future-state technology. They were grounded discussions rooted in lived experience: how work actually happens, where friction exists, and where emerging tools might meaningfully improve outcomes. For example, teams explored Supply Chain technologies for Supply Risk Management and smart data extraction, while People teams reviewed demonstrations of Recruit IQ and interviewing agents to understand how AI could streamline high‑volume talent processes.


**Collaboration that sparks innovation**
What made this event special wasn’t just the tech; it was the people. Business and technology leaders — including teams from Global Technology Operations Excellence, internal business customers, and supporting consultants — gathered in one space, sharing ideas and challenging each other to think differently.


Innovation isn’t a solo act; it’s a team sport fueled by curiosity, connection, and courage. That spirit was everywhere during Tech Innovation Day, and it’s what will keep us moving forward.


That collaboration surfaced several meaningful insights about where the biggest opportunities for AI and automation exist across our enterprise.


**What we learned when we stepped outside the day-to-day**
One clear pattern emerged: the biggest opportunities for AI and automation are not isolated within a single function. They live in the handoffs, the shared data, and the repetitive decisions that span teams and systems. When those connections were discussed openly, ideas moved quickly from “interesting” to “actionable.”


Another surprise was how different teams approach innovation depending on their operating context. Some domains naturally gravitate toward experimentation; others focus first on risk, scale, or stability. Seeing those perspectives side by side created productive tension and better ideas. It challenged assumptions about what innovation “should” look like and reinforced the value of learning across domains.


Perhaps most importantly, the day reinforced that people don’t need more tools to innovate; they need shared context and permission to ask better questions. Once those conditions were in place, ideas surfaced that likely wouldn’t have emerged through traditional intake or planning processes.


Taken together, these insights gave teams a clearer picture of where progress is possible — and set the stage for what should come next.


**Building momentum for what’s next**
The event didn’t end when the sessions wrapped up. More than 35 ideas generated during the event are now being evaluated and funneled into our innovation pipeline, where they can be prioritized, tested, and refined. Just as importantly, the event created momentum, a shared understanding of where opportunity exists, and a stronger network of people ready to move ideas forward together.


It also established a repeatable model: create space, bring the right mix of perspectives into the room, and focus on real problems. That model will continue to shape how we approach innovation going forward.


**What others can take away**
For organizations navigating innovation at scale, the lesson isn’t to host an event; it’s to design intentional moments that connect people, partners, and purpose.


True innovation doesn’t come from isolated brainstorming or technology for its own sake. It comes from collaboration, from seeing problems through multiple lenses, and from building partnerships that challenge how teams think and work.


Our EPP Tech Innovation Day was one step in that journey for McDonald’s Technology team, but it reinforced a broader truth: when you create the conditions for bold thinking, progress follows. This is only the beginning.
