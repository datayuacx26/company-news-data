# The Truth About What’s Lurking in Your Tap (and how to make it safer)

###### *By Scott Nielsen, Director of Filtration, Zurn Elkay*


You might not think twice about filling a glass from your kitchen tap, but sometimes what you can’t taste or see is what matters most. While your drinking water might look crystal clear and taste crisp and fresh, that doesn’t always mean it’s clean or safe.


As someone who’s spent years focused on filtration and water safety, I’ve seen firsthand that many families have misconceptions about the quality of their home water. Here are a few important facts that might make you reconsider what’s coming from your faucet and what you can do to make your family’s water safer.


**The Biggest Myth: “If It Looks Clean, It Is Clean”**
One of the biggest misconceptions people have is that you can tell if water is safe just by tasting it or looking at it. The reality is that the most concerning contaminants, like lead and forever chemicals (PFAS), don’t change the look, taste or smell of your water. And boiling it doesn’t help either. While boiling can kill bacteria, it actually concentrates many chemical contaminants, including lead and PFAS, making things worse instead of better.


**Hidden Threats in the Water**
Three contaminants concern me the most: lead, forever chemicals and microplastics.


Lead remains widespread due to aging infrastructure. Even when cities replace old lead service lines, lead can still leach into water from older pipes or faucets inside your home.


PFAS, or “forever chemicals,” are found almost everywhere: food packaging, textiles, cookware and yes, drinking water. These chemicals are nearly indestructible, persisting in the environment for hundreds of years. Even in extremely low concentrations, they’ve been linked to cancers, reproductive issues and developmental delays.


Microplastics, tiny fragments of plastic, are now showing up in water systems across the globe. We’re still learning about their long-term effects, but early findings suggest they accumulate in the body, and that’s unlikely to end well.


**Widespread, Not Isolated**
Unfortunately, these threats aren’t confined to a few communities. PFAS and microplastics have been found in drinking water sources across the country, and lead remains a problem wherever outdated infrastructure exists.


The EPA estimates there are still 9.2 million lead service lines in use in the U.S. today. That means millions of families are still at risk from lead exposure every time they turn on the tap. The “Good Taste” Trap Many people assume their water is safe because it tastes fine or because it comes from a trusted municipal source. But our senses can’t detect the contaminants that matter most. And while refrigerator or pitcher filters are convenient, most are only certified to address taste and odor, not harmful contaminants.


**If you want real protection, look for filters certified to NSF/ANSI standards 42, 53 and 401.**


- Standard 42 covers taste, odor and appearance.
- Standard 53 covers contaminants like lead, PFAS and microplastics.
- Standard 401 covers emerging contaminants like microplastics.


If a filter isn’t certified to all three, it’s probably not doing enough.


**The Reality: Bottled Water Isn’t Safer**
Studies have found forever chemicals and microplastics in bottled water, too. And the process of producing and recycling a single-use plastic water bottle actually requires about nine times more water than the bottle itself holds. So, for every 20-ounce bottle you drink, you’ve effectively used more than a gallon and a half of water.
