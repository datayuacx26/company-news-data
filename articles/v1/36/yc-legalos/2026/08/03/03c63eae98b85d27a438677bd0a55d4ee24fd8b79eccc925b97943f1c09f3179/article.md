# Robinhood Ventures Fund II invests in LegalOS

## LegalOS joins the Robinhood Ventures Fund II portfolio


Robinhood Ventures Fund II (NYSE: RVII) has invested in LegalOS. We are now part of the RVII portfolio, and we are glad to share the news with the founders, researchers, engineers, and talent teams who trust us with their immigration work.


The pairing makes sense once you look at what each side is trying to do. Our work starts from a simple belief: the U.S. immigration system should not be navigable only by people who can afford $30,000 in legal fees and a partner's cell phone number. RVII operates from a parallel conviction in a different arena — that access to venture capital and the wealth it creates should not be reserved for a narrow few.


Both missions come down to the same thing. They widen the door for people who have historically been kept outside it.


For us, that means giving an extraordinary researcher, a first-time founder, or a growing company a path through O-1A, EB-1A, H-1B, or E-3 that is transparent, fast, and priced in public. For RVII, it means opening private-market investing to a far broader base. The investment brings those two efforts into the same room.

## What Robinhood Ventures Fund II is


Robinhood Ventures Fund II is a closed-end Business Development Company, or BDC, regulated under the Investment Company Act of 1940. It is managed by Robinhood Ventures, an SEC-registered investment adviser and a subsidiary of Robinhood Markets ([Robinhood](https://robinhood.com/us/en/support/articles/about-robinhood-ventures-fund-ii/) ).


The structure is what makes it interesting for everyday investors. Instead of waiting for a startup to go public, retail investors can buy shares in a diversified portfolio of private companies through a single NYSE listing.

## Why immigration is the right place for AI


Consider a founder on an O-1A extension who is asked for evidence of "original contributions of major significance." The clock is running on their status. Their attorney is three time zones away and bills for the email. The evidence exists — press, funding, patents, letters — but nobody has assembled it into the argument USCIS is actually looking for. The RFE lands. The case slips a quarter. Sometimes the hire walks.


That gap is not an edge case. It is the default experience of the U.S. immigration system: opaque pricing, slow document collection, inconsistent quality, and legal expertise that does not scale.


LegalOS was built for that gap. We are an AI-native immigration firm, not a document-generation tool bolted onto a law practice. Attorney-led work product, AI-driven evidence assembly and petition drafting, and a client experience where you can see status, pricing, and next steps without sending an email.


‍


## What changes for LegalOS clients


The short answer is nothing. LegalOS remains an independent company, your case team stays the same, and every commitment in your engagement letter carries on unchanged. Attorney-client privilege, confidentiality, and how your documents are handled are unaffected. This investment does not change who runs LegalOS or who is responsible for your petition.


What it does is add a backer. Robinhood Ventures now joins our existing investors, including Y Combinator, which backed us in the W26 batch.


The practical benefit shows up in our roadmap. With more capital behind us, we can move faster on the work clients ask for most:


- Expanding coverage to more visa and green-card categories
- Deeper integrations with HR and onboarding systems so companies can start a case from an existing hiring workflow
- Faster evidence assembly and RFE response, with more of the drafting cycle handled before a human ever waits on it
- Scaling to more companies and firms that need reliable, high-volume immigration support


There is also a public dimension to this. As of August 13, 2026, anyone who believes the immigration system should be navigable by the people going through it can own a piece of that mission through RVII.
