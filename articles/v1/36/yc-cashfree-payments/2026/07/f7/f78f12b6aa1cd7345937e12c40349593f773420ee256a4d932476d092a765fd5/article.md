# Debit Note and Credit Note: Meaning, Key Differences and Examples

Table of Contents


Toggle


***TL;DR:** A debit note is usually issued by the buyer to request an invoice adjustment for damaged goods, short supply, pricing errors, or overcharges. A credit note is generally issued by the seller to confirm the adjustment. Together, they help businesses maintain accurate accounting records, simplify GST compliance, and streamline invoice reconciliation.*


---


Businesses often need to correct invoices because of damaged goods, pricing errors, short deliveries, or product returns. Instead of cancelling the original invoice, companies use debit notes and credit notes to record these adjustments while keeping their accounting records accurate.


Although these documents are commonly used in business transactions, many people confuse their purpose and when each should be issued. Understanding the difference is especially important for businesses in India, where debit notes and credit notes also play a key role in GST compliance and invoice reconciliation.


##


What is a Debit Note?


A debit note is a business document issued by the buyer (purchaser) to inform the seller that the invoice amount should be adjusted due to an issue with the supplied goods or services. It acts as a formal request asking the seller to reduce the payable amount or review the invoice because something doesn’t match the original agreement.


A debit note is commonly issued when:


- Goods are damaged during delivery
- Fewer products are delivered than ordered
- The seller charges more than the agreed price
- Incorrect products are supplied
- Additional charges are applied by mistake


Instead of immediately requesting a refund, the buyer first sends a debit note explaining the issue. The seller then reviews the claim and, if accepted, issues a credit note to complete the adjustment.


### Information Included in a Debit Note


A debit note typically contains:


- Debit note number
- Date of issue
- Buyer and seller details
- Original invoice number
- Description of the issue
- Quantity affected
- Amount to be adjusted
- GST details (if applicable)
- Authorized signature


#### Example of a Debit Note


Imagine a retailer purchases 50 laptops from a supplier for ₹500,000. After delivery, the retailer discovers that five laptops are damaged.


Instead of paying the full invoice amount, the retailer issues a debit note requesting an adjustment for the damaged units. The supplier then verifies the claim before issuing a credit note.

##


What is a Credit Note?


A credit note is a document issued by the seller (vendor) to reduce the amount payable by the buyer after accepting a claim related to damaged goods, sales returns, invoice corrections, pricing mistakes, or billing errors. Think of it as the seller’s confirmation that the invoice has been corrected.


Rather than issuing a completely new invoice, businesses use credit notes to maintain a proper audit trail while updating the customer’s outstanding balance.


Common reasons for issuing a credit note include:


- Customer returns goods
- Invoice amount was higher than agreed
- Wrong GST amount was charged
- Pricing errors
- Discounts offered after invoicing
- Seller accepts a buyer’s debit note


Once the credit note is issued, the buyer’s outstanding amount is reduced accordingly.


### Information Included in a Credit Note


A standard credit note generally includes:


- Credit note number
- Date
- Seller and buyer details
- Original invoice number
- Reason for issuing the credit note
- Amount credited
- Applicable GST adjustment
- Signature or authorization


#### Example of a Credit Note


Continuing the previous example, after inspecting the returned goods, the supplier confirms that five laptops were damaged during transportation.


The supplier issues a credit note for the value of those laptops, reducing the buyer’s payable amount and updating both parties’ accounting records.


##


Debit Note and Credit Note: What’s the Difference?


The main difference between a debit note and a credit note lies in who issues the document and why it is issued.


A debit note is generally raised by the buyer to notify the seller about an issue with an invoice or delivered goods and request an[adjustment](https://www.cashfree.com/blog/what-is-adjustment-types-examples/) . A credit note, on the other hand, is issued by the seller after accepting the buyer’s claim to confirm that the invoice has been corrected.


Although these documents serve different purposes, they often work together. In many business transactions, a debit note initiates the correction process, while the credit note finalizes it.


### Debit Note vs Credit Note Comparison


**Basis** **Debit Note** **Credit Note**


**Issued By** Buyer (Purchaser) Seller (Vendor)


**Purpose** Requests an adjustment to the invoice Confirms the adjustment and reduces the invoice amount


**Raised When** Goods are damaged, short supplied, incorrectly priced, or overcharged Seller accepts the buyer’s claim, goods are returned, or invoice corrections are required


**Impact on Buyer** Requests a reduction in the payable amount Reduces the buyer’s outstanding balance


**Impact on Seller** Indicates a claim has been raised Acknowledges and accepts the claim


**Relation to Invoice** References the original invoice to request correction References the original invoice to confirm correction


**GST Usage** Used to initiate GST-related invoice adjustments Used to complete GST adjustments after acceptance


**Accounting Effect** Purchase-related adjustment Sales-related adjustment


**Business Purpose** Notify the seller of discrepancies Officially record the approved adjustment


**Typical Sequence** Issued first Usually issued after the debit note is accepted


### How Debit Notes and Credit Notes Work Together


These documents are not competitors – they are complementary.

Which Document Should You Use?


**Situation** **Debit Note** **Credit Note**


**Goods damaged** Buyer issues Seller issues after approval


**Short supply** Buyer issues Seller confirms adjustment


**Invoice overcharged** Buyer issues Seller corrects invoice


**Sales return** Buyer requests adjustment Seller issues credit note


**Discount after invoicing** — Seller issues credit note


##


Debit Note and Credit Note in India


Debit notes and credit notes are widely used by Indian businesses to correct invoices without cancelling the original transaction. They help businesses maintain accurate accounting records,[resolve billing disputes](https://www.cashfree.com/blog/payment-disputes/) , process sales returns, and comply with[GST requirements](https://www.cashfree.com/blog/what-is-gst-goods-and-service-tax/) .


These documents are commonly used across industries such as manufacturing, wholesale, retail, eCommerce, logistics, and B2B services, where invoice adjustments are a regular part of business operations.


Some of the most common situations include:


- Sales returns
- Damaged or defective goods
- Short deliveries
- Incorrect pricing
- Excess or duplicate billing
- Discounts offered after invoicing
- GST adjustments


By properly issuing debit notes and credit notes, businesses create a clear audit trail that simplifies reconciliation and reduces accounting errors.


##


GST Perspective: Why Debit Notes and Credit Notes Matter


Under India’s GST system, debit notes and credit notes play an important role in recording invoice adjustments while maintaining transparency in tax reporting.


These documents help businesses:


- Correct taxable value after an invoice has been issued
- Record sales returns
- Adjust GST payable or receivable
- Maintain accurate books of accounts
- Simplify GST reconciliation during return filing


Instead of cancelling and reissuing invoices, businesses can use debit notes and credit notes to document legitimate changes while preserving the original transaction history.


> **Note:** Businesses should ensure that every debit note or credit note clearly references the original invoice and complies with applicable GST documentation requirements.


##


Accounting Entries for Debit Notes and Credit Notes


The accounting treatment depends on whether you are recording the transaction from the buyer’s or seller’s perspective.


### Debit Note Entry (Buyer’s Books)


When goods are returned or the invoice value is reduced:


```text
Supplier A/c Dr.        To Purchase Return A/c
```


This entry records the reduction in the amount payable to the supplier.


### Credit Note Entry (Seller’s Books)


When the seller accepts the claim:


```text
Sales Return A/c Dr.         To Customer A/c
```


This entry reduces the customer’s outstanding balance and records the sales adjustment.


> **Note:** The exact accounting treatment may vary depending on the organization’s accounting policies and ERP system.

##


Common Mistakes to Avoid


Even though debit notes and credit notes are straightforward documents, mistakes can create accounting discrepancies, GST reconciliation issues, or audit challenges.


Here are some common errors businesses should avoid:


**Mistake** **Why It Matters**


Not referencing the original invoice Makes reconciliation difficult


Issuing the wrong document Can lead to incorrect accounting entries


Missing GST details May create compliance issues


Incorrect adjustment amount Results in inaccurate financial records


Poor document numbering Makes audit tracking difficult


Delayed issuance Can complicate month-end closing and reconciliation


Following a standardized process for issuing debit notes and credit notes helps maintain accurate records and reduces the risk of disputes.


##


Best Practices for Businesses


To improve accounting accuracy and maintain proper documentation, businesses should follow these best practices:


- Always reference the original invoice number.
- Clearly state the reason for the adjustment.
- Include supporting documents where applicable (such as delivery reports or return requests).
- Ensure GST details are correctly captured.
- Issue the document as soon as the discrepancy is identified or approved.
- Keep debit notes and credit notes securely stored for future reference and audits.
- Regularly reconcile adjustments with accounting records and[GST returns](https://www.cashfree.com/blog/types-of-gst-returns-in-india/) .


Implementing these practices can help businesses streamline invoice management and reduce reconciliation errors.


##


Real-World Example


**Scenario:** ABC Traders purchases **100 office chairs** from XYZ Furniture for **₹500,000** . Upon delivery, 10 chairs are found to be damaged.


**Step 1:** ABC Traders issues a **debit note** requesting an adjustment for the damaged chairs.


**Step 2:** XYZ Furniture verifies the claim and issues a **credit note** for the approved amount.


**Result:** The invoice is adjusted, the buyer pays only for the undamaged goods, and both parties update their accounting records and GST documentation.


##


Conclusion


Debit notes and credit notes are essential documents for correcting invoice-related issues without cancelling the original transaction. A debit note is generally issued by the buyer to report discrepancies, while a credit note is issued by the seller to confirm and record the approved adjustment.


Using these documents correctly helps businesses maintain accurate financial records, simplify GST compliance, and ensure smooth reconciliation between buyers and sellers.

Frequently Asked Questions


#### What is the difference between a debit note and a credit note?


A debit note is usually issued by the buyer to request an invoice adjustment, whereas a credit note is issued by the seller to confirm that the adjustment has been made.


#### Who issues a debit note?


A debit note is generally issued by the **buyer** when goods are damaged, short supplied, incorrectly priced, or overcharged.


#### Who issues a credit note?


A credit note is generally issued by the **seller** after accepting a buyer’s claim or processing a sales return or invoice correction.


#### Is a debit note mandatory before issuing a credit note?


Not always. While a credit note often follows a buyer’s debit note, sellers can also issue credit notes directly for sales returns, post-sale discounts, or invoice corrections.


#### Are debit notes and credit notes used under GST?


Yes. They are commonly used to record invoice adjustments, sales returns, and tax corrections while maintaining accurate GST records.


#### Can a buyer issue a credit note?


In standard business practice, **no** . A credit note is generally issued by the seller, while the buyer typically raises a debit note.


#### Why are debit notes and credit notes important?


They help businesses correct invoices, maintain accurate accounting records, support GST compliance, and simplify reconciliation between buyers and sellers.
