# How Credit Card Processing System Works: A Beginner’s Guide

Table of Contents


Toggle


Every time a customer pays with a credit card, several systems work together behind the scenes to authorize, verify, and settle the payment, often within a few seconds. While the process may seem complex, understanding how credit card processing works can help businesses choose the right payment solution and deliver a smoother checkout experience.


In this guide, we’ll explain how a credit card processing system works, the key stakeholders involved, the payment flow, transaction fees, and what to consider when choosing a payment gateway.


##


What is a Credit Card Processing System?


A credit card processing system is the technology and financial infrastructure that enables businesses to securely accept credit card payments online or in-store.


When a customer makes a payment using a credit card, multiple parties including the[payment gateway](https://www.cashfree.com/payment-gateway-india/) , payment processor, card network, and banks – work together to verify the transaction, authorize the payment, and transfer funds to the merchant’s account.


Today, credit card processing has become an essential part of digital commerce. As online shopping,[subscriptions, and recurring billing](https://www.cashfree.com/recurring-payment/) continue to grow, businesses need a secure and reliable payment system that offers fast authorization, high success rates, and seamless settlements.


##


Stakeholders/Players in Credit Card Processing System


First, we need to define the important players in the credit card payment ecosystem. These are all parties involved when the customer clicks the “pay” button on a site or mobile application and when the merchant receives their payment.


**Here is an example to understand the process.** Priya purchases a pair of wireless earbuds (₹1,499) using her visa credit card from an online retailer.


**Priya (Cardholder):** She is the customer initiating the purchase by using her credit card.


**Online Store (Merchant):** This is the merchant selling the earbuds to Priya and accepting payment via credit card through a credit card payment gateway.


**Visa (Credit Card Network):** Credit card network in this example. Networks like Visa, RuPay, and MasterCard are trade associations of many large branded cards. They establish pricing, provide standards for security, assist with dispute resolution and much more.


**Acquiring Bank (Where Online Store Receives Payment):** Where the acquiring bank receives funds for each settled transaction made by the merchant.


**Issuing Bank (From Which Funds Are Withdrawn):** Priya’s issuing bank – the bank withdrawing the ₹1,499 from Priya’s account.


**Credit Card Payment Gateway (Between Merchant Website & Acquiring Bank):** Validates Priya’s credit card credentials and facilitates the final settlement process. Encrypts Priya’s sensitive credit card numbers as they pass from Priya to the Acquiring Bank; neither party can see Priya’s full credit card number.


**Processor (Process Payment Information Between Banks and Credit Card Network):** Communicates payment information from Visa to the two banks that are part of this transaction.


Using a credit card payment gateway protects Priya’s sensitive credit card details by allowing them to be transmitted securely from Priya to the acquiring bank (with no one able to view Priya’s actual credit card number).

Factors Affecting Credit Card Processing


A smooth credit card payment experience depends on more than just accepting card payments. Factors such as transaction speed, system reliability, pricing transparency, and customer support all play an important role in ensuring successful payment processing.


### 1. Transaction Speed


Customers prefer to pay with debit and credit cards as it is a quick mode of transacting with your business. They want the process to be fast and secure. Ideally, you should choose a credit card processing system that can handle large volumes of transactions safely and accurately. Friction-less merchant credit card processing can efficiently complete a transaction in less than two seconds.


### 2. High Uptime and Reliability


Credit card payment system declines can prove detrimental to your business. As a result, customers could deem your brand as inconvenient and might move to a different online merchant. Reliable card processors will have a robust uptime history and backup measures to minimize service interruptions.

Credit card processing rates should be transparent. You must ask the credit card payment processing system provider for complete details of their rate structure. Troubleshooting and maintenance of your credit card payment gateway is an essential part of dealing with a complex system. Ensure your credit card processor provides 24/7 customer support.

Credit card payments involve sensitive customer information, making security one of the most important aspects of payment processing.


A secure payment gateway should offer:


- [PCI DSS compliance](https://www.cashfree.com/blog/pci-dss-compliance-requirements-checklist/)
- End-to-end encryption
- Card tokenization
- [Fraud detection and risk management](https://www.cashfree.com/blog/payment-fraud-types-detection-prevention-guide/)

##


How Credit Card Processing Works: Step-by-Step


Every credit card transaction follows two major stages:


1. **Authorization** – The payment is verified and approved (or declined).
2. **Settlement** – The approved funds are transferred to the merchant’s bank account.


Let’s look at each stage in detail.


### Step 1: Authorization Process


The first part of the credit card processing cycle is an authorization process; verification of a customer’s identity to help prevent fraudulent transactions as well as Anti-Money Laundering (AML) violations.


Authorization consists of four parts. Below are those four parts applied to Priya making a purchase with her credit card of ₹1499 for ear phones.


- Upon completing her purchase, Priya inputs her card information at checkout. The store’s payment gateway tokenizes and encrypts the entered data and then transmits the encrypted data to the Acquiring Bank.
- The encrypted data flows to the Payment Processor. The Payment Processor utilizes Visa’s Network to forward the Data to Priya’s Bank (the Issuing Bank).
- The Issuing Bank verifies Priya’s identity and confirms if there is sufficient funds in Priya’s account to complete the ₹1499 purchase.
- In accordance with that review the Issuing Bank will either authorize or decline the purchase. The results of the bank’s decision will be reflected in real time to the store’s payment gateway. If the bank authorizes the purchase Priya will see an order confirmation. If the bank denies the purchase Priya will be redirected to attempt another purchase.


### Step 2: Settlement and Funding Process


Once the bank authorizes the transaction, settlement occurs. After authorization the store may now process Priya’s order but will not receive the funds until the settlement is completed. Below are the four parts of the settlement as they apply to Priya’s ₹1499 purchase.


- Once the store receives authorization its payment gateway will transmit the transaction for reconciliations to the Payment Processor.
- The Payment Processor subtracts all applicable fees from the ₹1499 prior to depositing the remaining amount into the store’s Acquiring Bank Account. Typically settlements occur within one or two business days. However, many merchants settle their transactions daily or weekly via batch processing, so that each day they have fewer transactional costs associated with processing a large number of sales during a single day.
- Visa deducts ₹1499 from Priya’s Issuing Bank and directs it to the Payment Processor who subsequently transfers it into the store’s Acquiring Bank Account.
- Priya’s Issuing Bank subtracts ₹1499 from Priya’s account and displays it on her next month Credit Card Statement. By her next payment due date, she will need to pay off this amount, otherwise, the bank will charge her interest on any outstanding balances.


##


Transaction Fees in Credit Card Processing


Every successful credit card transaction involves multiple stakeholders, including banks, card networks, payment processors, and payment gateways. Each participant may charge a fee for the services they provide.


Depending on the payment provider, these charges may be deducted from each transaction or settled separately based on the agreed pricing model.


**Annual/one-time start-up fee:** To establish a merchant account. If you use a payment gateway you may avoid paying this cost altogether.


M **onthly Statement Fee:** Covers the maintenance and reporting of accounts. Make sure to find out from your provider if the Monthly Statement Fee is included in their normal plans before you decide to go with them.


**Discount Rate:** The percentage of every transaction charged by the processor. Discount rates vary depending upon the type of card used, your merchant category and your average monthly transaction amount.


**Transaction Fee:** Flat fee charged on each individual transaction, usually in addition to the discount rate.


**Minimum Monthly Charge:** Fixed amount charged per month if your total transaction fees fall below a minimum threshold established by your provider for that month.


**Lease Charges for Equipment:** Only applies if you’re renting a physical terminal or card reader. Internet-based merchants never pay this charge.


**[Payment Gateway Fees](https://www.cashfree.com/payment-gateway-charges/) :** Applied whenever you’re using an internet merchant account to accept payments over the web.


**Charge Back Fees:** Charged to you when a buyer contests a transaction. Check with your provider to see if they will absorb the charge back costs as part of their services, prior to committing to them.


**Verification of Billing Address Fee:** Charged for the cost of validating a buyer’s billing address during the course of a transaction.


**Termination Fees:** Charged by your provider if you cancel your merchant account prior to completion of your contract. Review the Termination Fee clause very closely before signing up with any provider.


> Here’s how this looks in practice. On Priya’s ₹1,499 purchase, a 2% discount rate works out to roughly ₹30. Add a flat transaction fee of a few rupees, and the store nets close to ₹1,465 after processing costs, before any gateway or monthly fees. These figures are illustrative. Actual rates vary by provider and business category.

Frequently Asked Questions


**What is a credit card processing system?**


Credit card processing systems validate, authorize and settle all credit card transactions between the card holder, the merchant, and their respective banks.


**How soon does credit card payment settlement occur?**


Fund transfer generally takes place within one to two business days after authorization of a credit card transaction.


**Which fees apply to credit card processing?**


Most common fees include a discount rate, per transaction fees, gateway fees and charge-back fees. The specific fees you pay will depend on your merchant account provider and the type of business you operate.


**Who are the primary parties in a credit card transaction?**


These parties include the card holder, the merchant, the card association, the issuer’s bank, the acquirer’s bank, the payment gateway and the payment processor.


**Do I require a payment gateway to process credit card transactions via my website?**


Yes. A payment gateway is used to safely pass the card holder’s financial information from your website to the participating banks. As such, you cannot accept credit card payments online unless you utilize a payment gateway.
