# Fraudulent Transactions: How to Identify and Prevent Them

Table of Contents


Toggle


Every day, thousands of digital payment fraud attempts target consumers and businesses. From phishing scams and fake UPI requests to account takeovers and card-not-present fraud, online payment fraud continues to evolve. Understanding how fraudulent transactions happen – and how to identify them early – can help prevent financial loss and protect customer trust.


##


What is a Fraudulent Transaction?


A fraudulent transaction is any payment or account activity carried out without the cardholder’s or account owner’s authorization. It usually occurs when fraudsters steal or misuse sensitive information such as card details, banking credentials, passwords, or OTPs to make purchases, transfer funds, or access accounts without the user’s knowledge.


##


Types of Fraudulent Transactions


Online[payment fraud](https://www.cashfree.com/blog/payment-fraud-types-detection-prevention-guide/) can take many forms. Here are some of the most common types businesses and consumers encounter.


### Card-Not-Present (CNP) Fraud


This occurs when a card is used for online or phone purchases without being physically presented. Since merchants cannot verify the physical card, fraudsters often exploit stolen card details to complete unauthorized transactions.


### Account Takeover (ATO) Fraud


Fraudsters gain access to a customer’s online banking or payment account using stolen credentials. Once inside, they can transfer money, change account details, or make unauthorized purchases.


### Phishing Scams


Scammers impersonate banks or trusted organizations through emails, SMS, or calls to trick users into revealing passwords, OTPs, or banking information.


### Fake UPI Payment Requests


Fraudsters send fake payment or collect requests disguised as legitimate transactions. Approving these requests can result in unauthorized money transfers from the victim’s account.

### Merchant Fraud


Fraudsters create fake online stores or merchant identities to collect payments without delivering genuine products or services.


##


How to Identify a Fraudulent Transaction


Fraudulent transactions are easier to stop when detected early. Watch for these warning signs:


- Payments you don’t recognize
- Multiple small transactions within a short period
- Transactions from unusual locations or at odd hours
- International payments you didn’t authorize
- Duplicate charges for the same purchase
- Charges from unfamiliar merchants
- OTP requests you didn’t initiate


Not every unusual transaction is fraudulent. It could be a recurring subscription, a family member’s purchase, or a merchant using a different billing name. If anything seems suspicious, verify it immediately with your bank or payment provider.

##


How Fraudulent Transactions Are Detected


Banks and[payment providers](https://www.cashfree.com/blog/payment-service-provider/) use multiple security measures to detect suspicious activity before it causes financial loss. Common fraud detection methods include:


- Transaction pattern analysis
- Velocity checks for repeated transactions
- Device and IP monitoring
- OTP and multi-factor authentication verification
- Risk scoring and blacklist checks
- Behavioral analysis to identify unusual user activity


Businesses also monitor unusually large orders, repeated payment failures, billing and shipping mismatches, and abnormal customer behavior to identify potential fraud early.


##


What to Do If You Discover a Fraudulent Transaction


If you discover that a payment transaction was made without your knowledge or consent, take action now.


- Check your banking institution or mobile banking application to verify the exact dollar amount, date/time and[transaction ID](https://www.cashfree.com/blog/transaction-id/) associated with the disputed transaction(s).
- Block your credit/debit card, digital wallet or account if necessary.
- Contact your banking institution/[payment processor](https://www.cashfree.com/blog/difference-between-payment-gateway-and-payment-processor/) immediately.
- Report the disputed transaction as unauthorized to your banking institution/payment processor.
- Save copies/screenshots of SMS/Email notifications confirming the dispute along with any documentation supporting your claim.
- Change passwords/PIN numbers on accounts that may have been compromised by an unauthorized party.
- File a[Cyber Crime Complaint](https://cybercrime.gov.in/home) via your Country’s Official Reporting Process (if required).


Prompt Action is Important Because Banking Institutions and Payment Networks May Be Able to Freeze or Track a Disputed Transaction Based Upon Timely Notification From the Account Holder.


##


Suspicious Transaction vs. Fraudulent Transaction


Although the terms are often used interchangeably, they are different.


**Suspicious Transaction** **Fraudulent Transaction**


Appears unusual or unexpected Confirmed unauthorized activity


Requires verification Requires immediate action


May be legitimate Involves deception or unauthorized access


For example, a forgotten subscription renewal is suspicious until verified, whereas a payment made using stolen credentials is fraudulent.


##


Why Do Fraudulent Transactions Occur?


Most payment fraud happens because fraudsters gain access to sensitive information through:


- Phishing emails/fake support calls designed to obtain sensitive account information.
- Stolen account numbers/card details.
- Weak passwords/password reuse.
- Malicious QR code or fake payment pages used to steal sensitive account information.
- Malware installed on devices (phones/computers).
- Social Engineering tactics where victims are tricked into providing their sensitive information to scammers posing as representatives of banks/institutions.


Most fraud cases do not require sophisticated hacking techniques. They rely on unsuspecting victims being hurried/distracted/convinced to provide sensitive information.


##


How Businesses Can Prevent Transaction Fraud


Since businesses suffer losses related to revenue,[chargebacks](https://www.cashfree.com/blog/chargebacks-meaning-how-they-works-types-causes-prevention/) , and customer distrust as a result of fraudulent transactions, they can implement various measures to mitigate these risks. These include:


- Setting transaction limits
- Using fraud detection and risk-scoring tools
- Reviewing high-value or high-risk orders
- Monitoring repeated payment failures
- Securing checkout with two-factor authentication
- Tracking refund and chargeback patterns


Preventing fraud does not solely involve preventing malicious activities. It is equally important to minimize false positives and allow legitimate customers to continue processing transactions successfully.


##


How Individuals Can Reduce Fraud Risk


Individuals can lower their exposure to fraudulent transactions by implementing a few basic habits. This includes:


- Never share OTPs, UPI PINs, or passwords.
- Use strong, unique passwords.
- Enable transaction alerts.
- Review bank statements regularly.
- Avoid clicking suspicious links.
- Verify website URLs before making payments.
- Lock cards when they are not in use, if supported by your bank.


Staying alert and following basic security practices can prevent many common fraud attempts.

FAQs


#### What is a fraudulent transaction?


A fraudulent transaction is any payment or account activity made without the cardholder’s or account owner’s permission. It usually involves stolen payment information, compromised accounts, or deceptive tactics such as phishing and fake payment requests.


#### **How do I detect fraudulent transactions?**


Fraudulent transaction detection refers to the identification of questionable or unauthorized payments prior to them causing further damage.


#### **How can I tell if a transaction was fraudulent?**


Use your judgment! If you don’t recognize the payment, did not authorize it or are seeing unusual activity in your account, consider it suspect and contact your bank immediately.


#### **Can a fraudulent transaction be reversed?**


Possibly yes, depending on when you report it and the payment method used. The more quickly you act, the better.


#### **Is a suspicious transaction the same as fraud?**


No. A suspicious transaction is one that is unusual; however, a fraudulent transaction represents proven unauthorized activity.


#### **What should I do first if I discover a fraudulent transaction?**


Block the payment method from further use if possible. Call your bank, credit union or payment provider immediately.


#### **Can businesses automatically detect fraudulent transactions?**


Yes. Most businesses utilize some form of fraud scoring, behavior analysis, velocity checks and other tools to identify potential fraud immediately.


#### **Why are there so many fraudulent transactions?**


Because thieves have stolen information using phishing methods, account takeovers and social engineering tactics to trick people or bypass security measures.


#### **How do I prevent fraudulent transactions?**


Follow safe practices: keep passwords secure, never give out one-time passcodes (OTPs), and check your statements regularly.
