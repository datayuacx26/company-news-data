# Payment Terms: Meaning, Types, Examples & Best Practices

Table of Contents


Toggle


Every invoice has a due date, but getting paid on time depends on much more than that. Payment terms help businesses clearly define when customers should pay, which payment methods are accepted, whether early payment discounts apply, and what happens if payments are delayed. For MSMEs, freelancers, SaaS businesses, exporters, and enterprises, well-defined payment terms improve cash flow, reduce payment disputes, and create better customer relationships.


### 📌 Key Takeaways


- **Payment terms** define when, how, and under what conditions customers should pay.
- Common payment terms include **Due on Receipt, Net 15, Net 30, Net 60, Advance Payment, Cash on Delivery (COD), and Milestone Payments.**
- Clear payment terms help businesses improve cash flow, reduce payment disputes, and set clear payment expectations.
- Offering digital payment methods like **UPI, Cards, Payment Links, and Net Banking** makes it easier for customers to pay on time.
- Export businesses commonly use payment terms such as **Advance Payment, Letter of Credit (LC), Documents Against Payment (D/P), Documents Against Acceptance (D/A), and Open Account.**


##


What Are Payment Terms?


Payment terms are the agreed conditions that define when and how a customer should pay for goods or services. They specify payment deadlines, accepted payment methods, discounts for early payment, penalties for late payment, and any other conditions agreed upon between the buyer and seller.


You’ll typically find payment terms on invoices, purchase orders, quotations, and contracts.


In simple terms, payment terms answer three important questions:


- **When** should the customer pay?
- **How** should they pay?
- **What happens if they pay early or late?**


#### Example


A business issues an invoice worth **₹100,000** with **Net 30** payment terms.


This means the customer must pay the full invoice amount within **30 days from the invoice date** .


##


Payment Terms vs Payment Conditions


While these terms are often used together, they cover different aspects of a transaction.


**Component** **What It Covers**


**Payment Terms** Due date, payment schedule, credit period


**Payment Conditions** Accepted payment methods, discounts, penalties, taxes, currency, milestone payments, and special requirements


Together, they define the complete payment agreement between buyer and seller.


##


What Do Payment Terms and Conditions Include?


Payment terms often include several important elements, such as:


- Payment due date (Due on Receipt, Net 15, Net 30, etc.)
- Accepted payment methods ([UPI](https://www.cashfree.com/upi-payment-gateway/) , Cards, Net Banking, Wallets, Bank Transfer)
- Early payment discounts (for example, **2/10 Net 30** )
- Late payment charges or interest
- Partial payment or milestone schedules
- Currency (especially for international invoices)
- Applicable taxes and GST
- Invoice reference or purchase order details


Clearly written payment terms help businesses reduce[payment disputes](https://www.cashfree.com/blog/payment-disputes/) , accelerate collections, and improve cash flow predictability.


##


Common Types of Payment Terms


Different businesses use different payment terms depending on their industry, customer relationships, and cash flow requirements.


**Payment Term** **Meaning** **Best For**


**Due on Receipt** Payment is due immediately after receiving the invoice Retail, Freelancers, D2C


**Net 15** Payment due within 15 days MSMEs


**Net 30** Payment due within 30 days B2B Businesses


**Net 60** Payment due within 60 days Large Enterprises


**Advance Payment** Customer pays partially or fully before work begins Manufacturing, Custom Orders


**Cash on Delivery (COD)** Payment collected when goods are delivered Ecommerce


**Milestone Payment** Payments released after completing project phases Agencies, Contractors


**2/10 Net 30** 2% discount if paid within 10 days; otherwise due in 30 days B2B Sales


Businesses choose payment terms based on customer creditworthiness, negotiation power, project size, and working capital needs.


##


Payment Terms for MSMEs in India


For[MSMEs](https://www.cashfree.com/blog/msme-registration-india-process-certificate-eligibility/) , payment terms have a direct impact on[working capital](https://www.cashfree.com/blog/what-is-working-capital/) . Since many small businesses purchase raw materials or pay employees before receiving customer payments, delayed collections can create serious cash flow challenges.


Some commonly used payment terms for MSMEs include:


- 50% advance and 50% on delivery
- Net 7, Net 15, or Net 21 instead of longer credit periods
- Milestone-based payments for projects and services
- Early payment discounts to encourage faster collections


Under the[MSMED Act ,](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/2013/3/A2006-27.pdf) eligible MSMEs are protected against delayed payments, and buyers may be liable to pay interest on overdue invoices in certain situations. Clearly mentioning payment terms on invoices helps businesses strengthen payment discipline and reduce disputes.


Offering customers convenient payment options like UPI, cards,[payment links](https://www.cashfree.com/payment-links/) , and bank transfers further improves the chances of receiving payments on time.


#### Example: MSME Invoice Payment Terms


A small manufacturing MSME supplies machine components to a larger company.


The invoice includes:


- **Payment Terms:** 30% advance, remaining 70% payable within 15 days of delivery (Net 15)
- **Payment Methods:** UPI, Cards, Net Banking, Payment Link
- **Late Fee:** 1.5% per month on overdue payments


The advance payment covers raw material costs, while the shorter credit period improves working capital and reduces outstanding receivables.


##


Export Payment Terms


International transactions involve additional risks such as currency fluctuations, longer shipping timelines, and cross-border compliance. As a result, exporters and importers commonly use standardized payment terms that balance risk for both parties.


Common export payment terms include:


- **Advance Payment** – Buyer pays before shipment.
- **Letter of Credit (LC)** – A bank guarantees payment once agreed documents are presented.
- **Documents Against Payment (D/P)** – Buyer pays before receiving shipping documents.
- **Documents Against Acceptance (D/A)** – Buyer accepts a time draft and pays later.
- **Open Account** – Goods are shipped first, with payment made on a future agreed date.


The appropriate payment term depends on the level of trust between trading partners, transaction value, and country-specific risks.


#### Example: Export Payment Terms


An Indian apparel exporter ships garments to a buyer in Europe.


The agreed payment terms are:


- **20% Advance Payment**
- **80% payable through a Letter of Credit at Sight**
- **Currency:** EUR
- **Payment released after submitting shipping documents such as the commercial invoice, bill of lading, and packing list**


This structure provides upfront working capital while reducing payment risk through bank-backed assurance.

##


Best Practices for Setting Payment Terms


Well-designed payment terms improve collections while maintaining positive customer relationships.


**Follow these best practices:**


- Clearly mention payment due dates.
- Offer[multiple payment methods](https://www.cashfree.com/blog/payment-mode-types/) .
- Keep payment instructions simple and easy to understand.
- Include late payment charges where applicable.
- Offer early payment discounts to encourage faster payments.
- Use milestone payments for long-term projects.
- Send automated payment reminders before due dates.
- Keep payment terms consistent across quotations, contracts, and invoices.
