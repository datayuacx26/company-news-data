*by*[Martin Rosenfeld](https://www.linkedin.com/in/marrosenfeld/)


It’s been almost 2 years since I decided to make a move and join[Emi](https://www.emilabs.ai/) . After working for 7 years in a big-sized company, I felt it was time to break out of my comfort zone and switch to a **startup** . Many things have changed for me, I’ve had ups and downs, but I am sure it was the right decision for me.


My career mainly developed in the same company. I started as a Semi Sr Software Developer and made my path towards becoming a Software Engineering Manager. I learned a lot from smart colleagues, in a company where the IT area had ~800 people. However, after so many years, I felt I was lacking motivation. I wanted something different.

When I opened the door to listening to other possible companies, I found so many alternatives that I felt a bit overwhelmed 🤯. But one thing started rolling in my mind. Working in a startup was something I had always felt attracted to. I like my job having a great impact. I like fast-moving environments. At the same time, I want stability. And I am conservative by nature.


## Why Emi?


I started interviewing at Emi. The first thing I found was that Emi was focused on adding value by solving a real problem. Emi is built over this solid foundation. The founders showed me the company’s potential to expand in many business directions. If I wanted to have an impact on making a company grow, Emi was the right choice. Tech challenges would have to go along with this growth.


I felt *comfortable* during my interviewing process. I felt they listened to every one of my concerns, my questions. They offered me to talk with investors, current employees, stakeholders. Emi is actually people-driven. Everyone in Emi takes care of the culture as an asset to build a great company. And I started perceiving this since the first interview.


I mentioned I wanted stability, as well. However, startups are unstable by definition. What I found instead was *confidence* . Confidence in the business and the founders. And in the fact that the company will succeed.


## Working in a startup


I started working at Emi with many fears. I must admit all of them have become true 😆


## Be careful about what you suggest


Almost everything in a startup is yet to be built. This is valid for processes, cultural initiatives, technical stuff. I remember one of my first meetings with my manager. I suggested we needed to trace requests across the services. His response was something like “I agree, do it, you are now the owner”. This is not something that doesn’t happen in a big company. But on big companies, movements are slower, there are more stakeholders and it is more difficult to carry out cross-team initiatives. In a startup, you are the one who pushes initiatives and make them happen. It is all about *ownership* .


## The right decision for the moment/context


In a startup nearly everything is to be done, and hands are always fewer than needed. When making a decision you have to be careful. There is always a tradeoff between robustness, scalability of the solution, and the time to have it done. In Emi we are always challenged to ask ourselves *why.* What is the *value* we are giving? With that in mind, it is easier to choose the right alternative.


## It’s all about people


In a big company, culture is a force with inertia, it’s difficult and slow to transform. In a startup, culture has to be cared for every day, in every action. And culture is driven by people. In Emi the individual is in the center of attention. Since I joined Emi, I have had my 1:1 meeting with my manager every week. It has never been postponed or canceled.


I was recently shared a phrase from Vinod Khosla, one of Emi´s investors: *“The company becomes the people it hires”.* This is why hiring is so important. Not only do we need more hands. We need *better* hands, as well. It is known that tech hiring is very difficult. There is a lack of candidates for the number of jobs needed. In big companies, it sometimes ends up in relaxing the recruiting process requirements to prioritize its speed. In a startup, this could be dangerous. That’s why in Emi we take the time to be sure the person to hire is the best fit, both technical and cultural.


## The whole picture


One of the first company-wide talks I had in Emi was one in which the CEO showed the company status. In my previous company, they did a real effort to continuously improve communication. However, I always felt distant from decision-makers and it was unclear for me the rationale behind their decisions. In a startup, you have the whole picture in your hand.


## You will be disappointed


For sure you will be disappointed 😔. The code will not have the quality you expected. Big companies have years of maturity, and much work done to ensure quality. As a software developer, you have many tools that help you do your job. Libraries and frameworks which you can reuse. Teams that are specialized in specific technologies.


In a startup, the context is less mature. You will find yourself suffering reading unintelligible code. Struggling to understand why things are done the way they are. You will always have more things to do for the available time you have.


## There is no right/wrong decision


Working in startups and big-sized companies is undoubtedly different. But none is better than the other. It is all about what you want and need in a specific moment. Make your own way.
