A couple of weeks ago, we finally decided the name we’d use for the Engineering and Data Science team at[Emi Labs](https://www.emilabs.ai/) . As we grow as a company and as a team, we decided we wanted to frame our own team identity within the strong culture we have at Emi as a company, so we started by thinking about a name. We came to this idea loosely inspired by the[PayPal Mafia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PayPal_Mafia) because we are about the same size (15 in the team by Aug 2021) as the group in their very[famous photo](https://i.insider.com/541d7b4069bedd480815d44c?width=1000&format=jpeg&auto=webp) .


## What’s in a name


The first part of a series of exercises we did to come up with our name was, naturally, brainstorming a list of options :). We were trying to find a name that we would feel identified with, and that could be related to our cultural values in some way. We finally came up with a shortlist of:


- **Squirrels** -> cute, fast learners and can[learn from their peers](https://phys.org/news/2015-07-grey-squirrels-quick-learners.html) . Did I mention they are cute :)
- **Ravens** -> capable of feeling empathy,[extremely smart](https://elkodaily.com/outdoors/nature-notes-how-intelligent-is-a-raven/article_95b12c31-52f9-5715-8105-24ee25a83a10.html) , love to play, and are adaptable to different environments.
- **Elephants** -> very social beings, they spend most of their time interacting with other elephants, and are known to have a[high level of emotional intelligence](https://www.pbs.org/wnet/nature/echo-an-elephant-to-remember-elephant-emotions/4489) .
- **Ninjas** -> silent yet very effective, they are laser focused.
- **Octopuses** -> they have eight arms,[each of them can “think” independently](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/science-nature/ten-curious-facts-about-octopuses-7625828/) . They also have three hearts, which could be a metaphor for our “People Driven” cultural value. They are quite intelligent and have been observed using tools.
- **Crafters** -> we can relate our job to that of a craftsman/crafstwoman. We are constantly creating new things, trying to delight our customers and stakeholders.
- **Sailors** -> they have to master storms and make lots of small but tough decisions taking context into account and still deliver results.


After a few async discussions via Slack, we created a poll and each member of the Engineering and Data Science team voted:

This wasn’t the *final* vote, but it does show that the name “ **Ravens”** was the most popular, right from the start.


When we finally agreed on the name, we did some *more* reading about the animal itself, and we learnt details of fantastic characteristics that related pretty well to our cultural values. These are some quotes from[this article](https://www.mentalfloss.com/article/53295/10-fascinating-facts-about-ravens) we read, and how they relate to our values:


***People Driven***


> when a raven’s friend loses in a fight, they will seem to console the losing bird. They also remember birds they like and will respond in a friendly way to certain birds for at least three years after seeing them.


Well, it’s not really *people* but it’s their ability to feel empathy that’s amazing.


***Humble Learners***


> Ravens have been known to imitate wolves or foxes to attract them to carcasses that the raven isn’t capable of breaking open.


Identifying what we don’t know how to do well and asking for help from the right people is a key to our success. Again, OK, a wolf is not *people* but it works :)
Also, its *really smart* for a bird to do this!


***Proactive Ownership***


> If a raven knows another raven is watching it hide its food, it will pretend to put the food in one place while really hiding it in another. Since the other ravens are smart too, this only works sometimes. In the wild, ravens have pushed rocks on people to keep them from climbing to their nests, played dead beside a beaver carcass to scare other ravens away from the feast, and stolen Costco customers’ packaged meats[right out of their carts](https://www.mentalfloss.com/article/645515/ravens-stealing-groceries-alaska-costco) .


Not much to add here, all three examples are great evidence on how the raven will proactively and creatively (and again, what a smart bird!) achieve what it wants.


***Deliver with focus***


> In one logic test, a raven had to reach a hanging piece of food by pulling up a bit of the string, anchoring it with its talon, and repeating until the food was in reach. Many ravens got the food on the first try, some within 30 seconds.


This value is really hard to live by, especially for us humans living in this world full of distractions. Well, ravens seem to be very *to-the-point* and this example is just one of the many that show how they, pretty quickly, plan **and act** on a given situation.


## So, Ravens it is


Ok, so this is how we’ll call our team. Like everything at Emi, it may change in the future, but it looks like a great name, especially after reading some more about the crazy smart things this bird can do.
