# Tendon Repair: How The Integrity Implant Supports Your Return to Active Living

Few injuries strike more fear into athletes and active individuals than a torn tendon. In just a split second, everything can change, leaving you or your favorite athlete out of the game for nearly a year, especially when it comes to injuries like a ruptured Achilles tendon or a torn rotator cuff.


The recovery from a torn or ruptured tendon is often a long journey, and it’s no easy feat.

## Understanding Tendon Injuries and Why They’re So Difficult to Heal


Tendons are the strong, fibrous bands of tissue that connect your muscles to your bones. They play a critical role in virtually any motion that involves movement, such as pushing, pulling, running, or jumping. When a tendon tears, whether from sudden stress, overuse, or age-related degeneration, the impact on your daily life can be significant.


Common tendon injuries include rotator cuff tears in the shoulder and Achilles tendon ruptures in the ankle. These injuries can disrupt your life and prevent you from enjoying the things you love, from recreational sports to simple tasks like reaching overhead or simply walking.


The challenge with these injuries is that tendons naturally have limited blood supply compared to other tissues in the body. This limited blood flow means healing can be slow and, in some cases, incomplete. Traditional surgical repair of a partial, fully torn, or ruptured tendon often requires surgery that reattaches the torn tendon to the bone with sutures and anchors. However, even with successful surgery, there’s always concern about the strength of the repair and the risk of re-tearing.


As Dana Walach, M.D. notes, “The less blood delivered, the longer it takes tissue to heal. Add to that the sheer size of the Achilles tendon, and you’re in for a lengthy recovery for athletes; these are usually season-ending injuries.”1


The same principles apply to rotator cuff tears and other tendon injuries throughout the body which brings us to tendon augmentation


## What Is Tendon Augmentation?


Tendon augmentation is an approach that goes beyond simply reattaching the torn tendon. When surgery is required to repair a torn tendon, your surgeon may choose to augment the tendon repair, meaning they will use something in addition to the sutures and anchors that secure the tendon back into place. For example, they may use[Anika’s Integrity Implant](https://anika.com/products/regen/integrity/) , a biologic scaffold that is placed on top of the repair to create an optimized healing environment.


Think of augmentation as providing both protection for the injured tendon as well as a biologic environment that encourages healing. The scaffold serves as a framework that supports regenerative healing, in our case with hyaluronic acid, and can help thicken the tendon tissue during recovery.

## What to Expect: The Patient Journey with Augmented Tendon Repair


If you have a torn tendon and your orthopedic surgeon is suggesting surgery, understanding what to expect can help you feel more prepared and confident about your treatment.


## Is Tendon Augmentation Right for You?


Augmentation isn’t recommended for every tendon repair. Your surgeon will consider several factors when deciding whether to use a biologic scaffold:


- The size and severity of your tear


- Your age, activity level, and overall health


- The quality of your tendon tissue


- Your goals for recovery and return to activity


- Risk factors that might complicate healing (such as chronic conditions, previous injuries, or structural issues)


The decision to augment is personalized to your specific situation. If your surgeon recommends Integrity


, it’s because they believe the addition of Integrity


will improve your chances of a successful recovery.


## Life After Augmented Tendon Repair


Recovery from tendon repair surgery is a gradual process, like many other surgeries, which requires patience and commitment. While augmentation with Integrity


is designed to enhance healing, the healing process still takes time.


**After Surgery:** Most patients go home the same day


**Early Recovery:** You’ll likely wear a brace or boot to protect the repair. Depending on which tendon was repaired, limited weight-bearing or arm movement may be necessary to prevent stress on the healing tendon. Follow your surgeon’s post-operative instructions carefully during this critical phase.


**Rehabilitation:** Physical therapy (PT) is essential for regaining strength, movement, flexibility, and function. Your therapist will guide you through progressive exercises designed to rebuild your tendon and surrounding muscles safely. This is where the benefits of augmentation often become apparent as many patients find they’re able to progress through PT with greater confidence in their repair.


**Return to Activity:** Everyone heals at a different pace. Your surgeon and physical therapist will work with you to determine when it’s safe to gradually increase your activity level. Most people return to daily activities in a few weeks, but full recovery can take several months depending on your injury and repair.


**Recovery timeline will vary based on the individual patient.* *Always follow your surgeon’s instructions. They will provide you with a detailed post-op care plan for best results.*


## Success Factors for Long-Term Recovery


While the Integrity


implant provides protection and a biological environment that encourages healing, your commitment to the recovery process is equally important. Key factors for success include:


- **Following post-operative instructions** : Protecting the repair during early healing is critical
