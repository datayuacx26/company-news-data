Moving oversized or overweight freight takes more than a larger trailer. Heavy haul transportation utilizes specialized trailing equipment engineered to handle unusual dimensions, extreme weights and complex site requirements. For logistics leaders responsible for coordinating large-scale projects, understanding the equipment options available and when each is used can support better planning, reduce risk and help keep projects on schedule.


In this guide, we break down the trailer types most commonly used in heavy haul transportation, the freight they typically move and the planning considerations logistics teams should keep in mind when evaluating equipment needs.


## What Is Heavy Haul Transportation?


Heavy haul transportation involves moving cargo that exceeds standard legal limits for weight, height, width or length. These shipments typically require permitting, specialized trailing equipment and, in many cases, route engineering and escort coordination to move safely and in compliance with state and federal regulations.


Heavy haul freight spans a wide range of industries, including:


- Energy & utilities — wind turbine components, transformers, generators
- Industrial manufacturing — machinery and industrial equipment
- Construction & infrastructure —heavy equipment, building materials, prefabricated components
- Aerospace & defense — aircraft components and military equipment
- [Data infrastructure](https://www.landstar.com/services/industries/data-centers/) — generators, transformers and cooling equipment


## Why Trailer Selection Matters


Trailer type affects overall height, weight distribution, axle configuration and ground clearance, all factors that influence permitting requirements, routing options and site access. Equipment that doesn't align with a shipment's dimensions or weight can contribute to delays, added costs or safety concerns. Matching freight to the right trailer configuration from the outset supports a more efficient move and helps manage risk throughout the process.


For complex heavy haul moves, equipment planning should begin as early as possible. Capacity sourcing, equipment positioning, permitting and route planning can be lengthy processes, particularly when specialized trailers are required. In some cases, trailers may be so large or complex that permits are needed even when moving empty, making equipment positioning a planning consideration of its own.


## Common Heavy Haul Trailer Types and Their Applications


### Flatbeds


Flatbed trailers feature an open, level deck with no sides or roof, making them well-suited for freight that doesn't require a lower deck height.


- **Commonly used for:** construction materials, machinery, equipment
- **Benefit:** can be loaded and unloaded from the side or top


### Step Decks


Step deck trailers include a standard flatbed section at the front and a dropped section toward the rear, creating additional vertical clearance.


- **Commonly used for:** taller industrial equipment
- **Benefit:** helps shipments stay within legal height limits in many cases


### RGNs (Removable Gooseneck Trailers)


RGNs feature a detachable gooseneck that allows equipment to be driven directly onto the trailer rather than lifted, simplifying loading for tracked or wheeled machinery.


- **Commonly used for:** construction equipment, industrial machinery
- **Benefit:** low deck height reduces ground clearance needs


### Stretch Trailers


Stretch trailers, also called extendable trailers, can be lengthened to accommodate freight that exceeds standard trailer dimensions.


- **Commonly used for:** pipe, beams, wind energy components like turbine blades
- **Benefit:** extends to fit long, narrow loads


### Lowboys


Lowboys are designed with a low deck height, typically positioned close to the ground between the axles.


- **Commonly used for:** cranes, bulldozers, other construction machinery
- **Benefit:** additional clearance for tall, heavy equipment


### Double Drop Trailers


Double drop trailers include a dropped center section positioned between the front and rear axles, providing additional height clearance for tall cargo.


- **Commonly used for:** large industrial equipment and machinery
- **Benefit:** accommodates height requirements that exceed what a step deck can handle


### Multi-Axle and Steerable Trailers


Multi-axle trailers distribute weight across additional axles, while steerable trailers add axles that can turn independently.


- **Multi-axle benefit:** helps meet bridge and roadway weight restrictions
- **Steerable benefit:** improves maneuverability for tight turns or congested routes
- **Commonly used for:** superloads and large industrial components


### Perimeter Trailers


Perimeter trailers, sometimes called perimeter-frame trailers, use a frame that runs along the outer edges of the deck rather than through the center, allowing freight to sit lower to the ground.


- **Commonly used for:** transformers, industrial tanks
- **Benefit:** minimizes overall height for tall or heavy loads


### Schnabel Trailers


Schnabel trailers are engineered to cradle a specific piece of freight within the trailer structure, rather than resting cargo on a deck.


- **Commonly used for:** pressure vessels, reactor parts
- **Benefit:** highly customized transport solution for extremely large or heavy components

## FAQs


### 1. What is heavy haul transportation?


Heavy haul transportation refers to moving cargo that exceeds standard legal weight and size limits, typically requiring permits and specialized trailing equipment.


### 2. What is an RGN trailer used for?


RGN trailers feature a detachable gooseneck that allows equipment to be driven directly onto the trailer, making them useful for construction and industrial machinery.


### 3. What is the difference between a lowboy and a double drop trailer?


Both trailers offer a lower deck height for tall freight. Lowboys sit close to the ground between the axles, while double drop trailers include a dropped section positioned between the front and rear axles.


### 4. What trailer is used for wind energy components?


Wind turbine blades and towers are commonly transported using stretch trailers, which can extend to accommodate long, narrow freight.


### 5. What trailer is best for oversized loads?


Trailer choice depends on the freight being moved. Common options for oversized loads include RGNs, lowboys and double drop trailers, selected based on weight, height and site requirements.


### 6. What industries use heavy haul trailers?


Industries such as energy and utilities, industrial manufacturing, construction, aerospace and data infrastructure frequently rely on heavy haul trailers to move oversized equipment.


### 7. How is the trailer type determined for a heavy haul shipment?


Trailer selection depends on factors such as freight dimensions, weight, ground clearance requirements and site access, along with permitting and routing considerations.


### 8. Why does trailer selection matter for heavy haul shipments?


Choosing an appropriate trailer configuration supports safer, more efficient moves by aligning equipment with a shipment's weight, height and routing requirements.


### 9. When should I start planning a heavy haul shipment?


Heavy haul shipments should be planned as early as possible, especially when oversized freight requires specialized trailers, permits, route planning or equipment positioning. For some trailer types, the process of positioning equipment can require added lead time before the loaded move begins.
