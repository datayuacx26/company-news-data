## What is the Definition of Trigonometry?


Let’s keep it simple. Trigonometry is “a branch of mathematics that deals with the relationships between the sides and angles of triangles.”


In simpler terms, trigonometry is a type of math that helps us understand how the sides and angles of triangles are connected. It’s all about triangles—specifically, right triangles. Trigonometry introduces new tools called **sine** , **cosine** , and **tangent** , which are essential for solving problems with angles and distances.


## Key Elements of Trigonometry: Sine, Cosine, and Tangent


Trigonometry is built on three major functions that help us calculate unknown sides and angles of triangles. Here’s a breakdown of each one.


### What is Sine?


The **sine** function (abbreviated as **sin** ) compares the length of the opposite side to the hypotenuse of a right triangle.


-


**Formula:** Sin(θ) = Opposite / Hypotenuse


-


**Example Use:** If you know an angle and the hypotenuse, you can use sine to find the length of the opposite side.


### What is Cosine?


The **cosine** function (abbreviated as **cos** ) compares the length of the adjacent side to the hypotenuse of a right triangle.


-


**Formula:** Cos(θ) = Adjacent / Hypotenuse


-


**Example Use:** If you know an angle and the hypotenuse, you can use cosine to find the length of the adjacent side.


### What is Tangent?


The **tangent** function (abbreviated as **tan** ) compares the length of the opposite side to the adjacent side of a right triangle.


-


**Formula:** Tan(θ) = Opposite / Adjacent


-


**Example Use:** If you know an angle and one side of a triangle, tangent can help you find the other side.


### Study Tip! Remember SOH-CAH-TOA


A popular study method for easily remembering your trigonometric ratios is called SOH-CAH-TOA.


-


SOH: Sine is Opposite over Hypotenuse


-


CAH: Cosine is Adjacent over Hypotenuse


-


TOA: Tangent is Opposite over Adjacent


"Opposite" refers to the side length the angle faces, "adjacent" refers to the side length next to the angle, and "hypotenuse" refers to the longest side length, opposite to the right angle. These three functions—sine, cosine, and tangent—are the building blocks of trigonometry. Want to learn more about how they work? ## Why Do We Need Trigonometry?


Trigonometry isn’t just about triangles in a textbook—it’s all around you in the real world. Here are some career paths that utilize trigonometry on a daily basis:


1.


**Engineering and Construction** : Building bridges, skyscrapers, and roads all require trigonometry to calculate angles and measurements.


2.


**Astronomy and Space Exploration** : Measuring the distance between planets and stars wouldn’t be possible without trigonometry.


3.


**Navigation and GPS** : Modern GPS systems rely on trigonometry to determine locations and distances.


4.


**Video Game Design** : Creating 3D graphics and animations in video games requires the use of trigonometry to make characters and objects move in realistic ways.
