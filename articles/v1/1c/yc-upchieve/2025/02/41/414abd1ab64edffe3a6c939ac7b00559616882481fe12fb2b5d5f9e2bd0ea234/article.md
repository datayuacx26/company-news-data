# What is Algebra?

Algebra is like solving a puzzle where you need to solve for the missing pieces. It’s all about using numbers, symbols, and operations to find answers to problems—even when you don’t have all the information. If you’ve ever wondered how to figure out the total cost of something on sale, split a bill evenly with friends, or predict how much time you’ll need to finish a homework assignment, you’ve already used algebra without realizing it!


##
What is the Definition of Algebra


Algebra is a branch of math that uses symbols (like letters) to represent numbers. These symbols, called variables, stand in for values that might change or aren’t known yet. Instead of solving math problems with just numbers, algebra teaches you how to work with these variables to find answers.


### Key Parts of Algebra


-


**Variables:** Letters like *x* or *y* that represent unknown numbers. For example, in 2 *x* + 5 = 15, *x* is the variable you need to figure out.


-


**Expressions:** Expressions are mathematical phrases made up of some combination of numbers, variables, and operations (like addition or multiplication), such as 4x + 2.


-


**Equations:** They are like math sentences where two things are equal, such as 3x = 12. Solving equations means finding the value of the variable.


-


**Functions:** Rules that show the relationship between two sets of numbers. For example, a function could show you how many miles you can drive based on how much gas you have.


## **What is the equals sign?**


The equals sign indicates balance, which *must* be maintained no matter what manipulation is performed on the equation. You may square an equation, subtract 10,000, add 0.00001, and perform almost any other mathematical operation you can think of—so long as it’s done to *both* sides of the equals sign. Just please do not divide by zero! This is not allowed as the result is undefined.

## What Will You Learn in Algebra?


Algebra covers a wide range of topics, but each topic builds upon each other. Here are some main topics you’ll likely study?


### **Linear Equations:**


These are equations like y = 2x + 3 that describe straight lines when you graph them. You’ll learn how to solve these equations and see how changing one number affects the whole equation.


### **Inequalities:**


Sometimes, instead of finding an exact answer, you need to figure out a range of possibilities. Inequalities, like x > 3, teach you how to do this.


### **Quadratic Equations:**


These equations involve variables squared (like x2). They might look complicated at first, but you’ll learn tools like factoring and the quadratic formula to make them manageable.


### **Exponents and Polynomials**


[Exponents](https://upchieve.org/blog/what-are-exponents) and Polynomials introduce powers (like x3) and multi-term expressions, helping you tackle bigger and more complex problems.
