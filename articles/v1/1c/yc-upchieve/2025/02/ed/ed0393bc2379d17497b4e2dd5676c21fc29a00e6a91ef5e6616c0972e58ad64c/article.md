## What is the Definition of Calculus?


Calculus is defined as **“a branch of mathematics that studies continuous change using concepts like limits, derivatives, and integrals.”**


In simpler terms, calculus helps us understand how things change over time or how quantities accumulate. It’s like tracking how fast a car is going at one specific moment (not over time, but *right now* ) or figuring out how much space is under a curved graph.


There are two main branches of calculus:


-


**Differential Calculus** : Focuses on how things change, like how fast something is moving or growing.


-


**Integral Calculus** : Focuses on total accumulation, like finding the total area under a curve.


If you think of calculus as a video game, differential calculus is like figuring out how fast your character is moving, while integral calculus is like tracking how far you’ve gone in total.

Integral calculus is the opposite of differential calculus. It’s about understanding *total accumulation* —like figuring out how much water has filled up a glass over time. It helps you find the total area under a curve, which comes in handy for everything from physics to economics.
