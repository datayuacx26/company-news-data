# What Can You Do With a Chemistry Degree?

Are you captivated by the wonders of atoms, molecules, and chemical reactions? If so, a chemistry degree could be an excellent match for your[interests and career goals](https://upchieve.org/blog/career-exploration-for-high-school-students) . But, you might be asking, what can you do with a chemistry degree?


A chemistry degree strengthens your scientific knowledge and analytical skills. You'll dive into biochemistry, organic chemistry, physical chemistry, and more. You will develop problem-solving skills, analyze data, and understand scientific methods. Joining the chemistry club is a great way to meet friends and learn more about chemistry careers. In-person or online, a chemistry degree opens the door to a world of career opportunities.


## **What Can You Do With a Chemistry Degree?**


Now, let's dive into the wide array of[career paths](https://upchieve.org/blog/best-tools-for-career-exploration-for-high-schoolers) and answer the crucial question, What can I do with a chemistry degree?


### Research Scientist


A Research Scientist is an explorer seeking new knowledge, diving into mysteries from atoms to space and the human body. Their discoveries can lead to new technologies, medicines, or a deeper understanding of our universe. If you're curious, love problem-solving, and enjoy discovery, this career might be for you!


### Pharmaceutical Chemist:


Pharmaceutical Chemists are experts in medicine creation, combining creativity with scientific analysis to design safe, effective drugs. They blend chemistry, biology, and pharmacology to create life-changing treatments that impact health worldwide. If you're passionate about chemistry and healthcare, this could be the career for you!


### Forensic Scientist


If solving mysteries with science sounds exciting, forensic science might be for you. Forensic scientists analyze crime scene evidence, using chemistry to identify substances, trace drugs or toxins, and decode DNA to help solve cases. It's a rewarding role with a meaningful impact.


### Chemical Engineer


Chemical Engineers use chemistry, biology, physics, and math to address production challenges across industries like fuel, food, and pharmaceuticals. They design and test manufacturing processes and manage operations—a versatile career with diverse applications.


### Environmental Chemist


Environmental Chemists use chemistry to tackle environmental issues, analyzing pollutants and natural substances to understand their impact on nature. One day, they might be in a lab; the next, collecting field samples. It’s an ideal role for those passionate about chemistry and environmental health.


### Food and Flavor Chemist


Food chemists use chemistry to enhance flavors, create new foods, and ensure safety and quality. They impact everything from taste and texture to shelf-life. If you love food and science, this field could be perfect for you!


### Science Writer or Journalist


Science Writers translate complex research into accessible stories, bringing science to the public through articles, books, and exhibits. If you love writing and science and want to share knowledge, this career could be a fit.


### Toxicologist


Toxicologists study how chemicals, drugs, and pollutants can harm us. They determine safe exposure levels and help protect health and wildlife. If you’re interested in science, health, and the environment, consider toxicology.


### Patent Attorney


Patent Attorneys protect inventors’ ideas, combining law with a deep understanding of science. They secure patents to safeguard new inventions, making this a great role for those who love science and legal strategy.


### Quality Control Analyst


Quality Control Analysts ensure products like food, medicine, and cosmetics meet safety standards. Using chemistry and advanced tools, they test and maintain quality, so we can trust the products we use every day.


### Materials Scientist


Materials Scientists investigate and enhance materials like metals and polymers to create products that are durable, safe, and eco-friendly. Their innovations span technology, manufacturing, and beyond.


### Clinical Laboratory Technician


Lab Techs work behind the scenes in healthcare, running tests on samples to assist diagnoses. Their careful, precise work helps identify viruses, monitor conditions, and keep patients safe.


### Nanotechnologist


Nanotechnologists work with materials at a microscopic scale, creating new tech for medicine, electronics, and energy. Imagine tiny robots fighting diseases—nanotechnology offers groundbreaking possibilities.


### Teacher or Professor


Chemistry Teachers inspire curiosity, guiding students through everything from basic reactions to advanced concepts. If you love chemistry and teaching, this might be the role for you.


### Cosmetic Chemist


Cosmetic Chemists develop personal care products like lip balm and shampoo, blending science with creativity to meet beauty trends safely and effectively. If you’re passionate about beauty and science, this could be your calling.


### Astrochemist


Astrochemists use chemistry to study space, analyzing stars, planets, and galaxies to understand the universe's composition. If you love both chemistry and astronomy, this career is cosmic!


### Agricultural Chemist


Agricultural Chemists enhance crop growth and soil quality, supporting safe, sustainable food production. From labs to fields, they work to improve food security and fight climate challenges.


### Hazardous Waste Chemist


Hazardous Waste Chemists manage dangerous substances to protect health and the environment, finding ways to handle and reduce toxic waste safely.


### Health and Safety Officer


Health and Safety Officers ensure workplace safety by identifying hazards, training staff, and investigating incidents. It's a role for those who prioritize health, problem-solving, and detail.


### Using Chemistry as a Pre-Professional Step


A chemistry degree is a strong foundation for fields like medicine, pharmacy, and veterinary science. Courses in organic and biochemistry prepare students for competitive medical-related programs.


### Final thoughts


Chemistry, known as the "central science," bridges biology, physics, and geology, offering a variety of rewarding careers across traditional and innovative fields.
