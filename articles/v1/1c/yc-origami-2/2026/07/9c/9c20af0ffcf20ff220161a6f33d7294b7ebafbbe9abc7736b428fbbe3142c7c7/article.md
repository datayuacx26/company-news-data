### Where Do Medical Practices Post RCM Job Openings?


**Answer: The highest-intent hiring signals often appear on state medical boards, professional association sites (MGMA, HFMA, AAHAM), niche healthcare job boards, and the practice's own careers page—not on general-purpose platforms that sales intelligence tools typically scan.**


**State medical licensing boards** maintain public directories and sometimes job boards. A small practice advertising for a "billing specialist" might post only on the state board's classifieds section. Because these pages aren't structured like a corporate career site, static databases skip them entirely.


**Regional and national associations** (MGMA, HFMA, state medical societies) run job boards frequented by RCM professionals. A practice manager who posts a "Revenue Cycle Manager" role on the local MGMA chapter's site is demonstrating clear intent to invest in revenue cycle leadership. If you can find that post, you have a warm lead with a verifiable pain point.


**Niche healthcare job boards** like Health eCareers, RCM-specific forums, and even LinkedIn—when filtered precisely—can surface roles that don't make the front page of Indeed. The key is knowing how to search for "RCM supervisor," "billing compliance officer," or "denials management specialist" across dozens of these niche destinations simultaneously. A practitioner who has done this work explained the frustration: "I could tell you half of them are relevant or half of them are no longer active. And so I don't know what to do from there to make my list smarter." That's the reality of manual cross-referencing.


**Practice career pages** themselves are gold mines. A large multispecialty group might list five open billing positions on its own website—positions that never appear on an aggregator. A live web search that can crawl these pages in real time turns those hidden openings into actionable leads.

### What Data Points Should You Look For in RCM Hiring Signals?


**Answer: Beyond the job title, the strongest signals are recency of the posting, the specific RCM function (coding, billing, denials, compliance), the practice size, and whether the role replaces an existing position or reflects a new expansion.**


A practice that posts for a "denials management specialist" likely has a specific revenue leakage problem. If it's a "billing compliance officer," there may be an audit or regulatory change driving the hire. These nuances tell you exactly what pain point to address in your outreach.


Also pay attention to practice structure: is it a solo practice hiring a first-ever biller? That's a completely different conversation than a multispecialty group building a business office. A sales leader in healthcare once said, "the data is just so shitty" when referring to inaccurate contact information for small clinics. Having reliable contact data—especially verified emails for the practice manager or director of revenue cycle—is the difference between a conversation and a dead lead.


### How to Approach Practices That Are Hiring for RCM Roles


**Answer: Reference the specific role in your outreach, tie it to a common RCM challenge, and offer a solution that aligns with why they're hiring.**


If they're looking for a coder, mention the upcoming ICD-11 transition or recent payer-specific coding changes. If they need a billing supervisor, acknowledge the strain of managing both insurance and patient billing. This signals that you understand their world.

Channels that work well for RCM prospecting include email, phone, and direct mail—especially for older practice owners who may not be active on LinkedIn.
