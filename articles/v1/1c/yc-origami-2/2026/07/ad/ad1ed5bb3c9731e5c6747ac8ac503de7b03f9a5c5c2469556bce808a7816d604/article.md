# Origami vs Lead411: Which B2B Data Platform Wins in 2026?

You describe your ideal customer in plain English, and Origami’s agent crawls the live web, not a static database. Lead411 is a solid, traditional sales intelligence platform with good intent data and Boolean filtering, but it can’t match the flexibility or freshness of a live web search for niche, SMB, or newly founded companies. For teams tired of manual filtering and stale contacts, Origami wins; for those who prefer a classic database with firmographic filters and don't mind periodic refreshes, Lead411 remains a reliable workhorse.

## How We Compared Origami and Lead411


I’ve spent weeks talking to sales leaders, founders, and RevOps teams who are actively deciding between these two tools. Most of them fall into one of two camps: “I need a list right now and I don’t want to learn filters” or “I want a database I can query with Boolean logic for my established ICP.” Both tools can deliver contacts, but they do it in fundamentally opposite ways.


Origami is an AI agent that searches the live web. You tell it “find HR directors at mid-size construction companies in Texas who recently changed software vendors,” and it goes out to LinkedIn, Google Maps, company websites, and directories—then returns a structured list. Lead411 is a curated database of companies and contacts, refreshed periodically, that you filter using fields like industry, employee count, revenue, and intent signals.


This difference in architecture matters more than pricing or features. It dictates whether you’ll find that family-owned HVAC company with no LinkedIn presence, or whether you’ll only see companies already in a database. Let’s dig into the details.


---


## Which Platform Gets You a List Faster?


For pure speed from thought to export, Origami is vastly faster. You type a prompt and often have a first draft of contacts in under a minute. No need to learn a filtering interface, understand which fields map to what, or figure out how to combine advanced Boolean. I’ve watched new users go from prompt to a structured table of 50 contacts in less than two minutes.


Lead411 requires a learning curve. You have to know what filters are available and how to combine them, similar to an Apollo or ZoomInfo. If you’re experienced, you can build complex searches, but it still takes more clicks. For a team without a dedicated sales ops person, that friction adds up. If your ICP is already well-defined and fits neatly into Lead411’s firmographic categories, the extra 5–10 minutes per list is fine. But if you’re iterating on your ICP or testing new segments, the prompt-based model wins hands down.


**Winner:** Origami for speed of list creation; Lead411 for precision within known database fields.


---


## Does Lead411 Have Fresher Data Than Origami?


Lead411’s data is maintained and refreshed periodically—but it’s still a database. That means if a company was founded last month, or a key contact changed roles two weeks ago, it might not be there. Origami’s crawler searches what’s live on the web right now. That doesn’t mean every phone number is 100% accurate (no tool can guarantee that), but it does mean you can find companies that aren’t indexed in a static database yet.


For example, a private equity firm looking for newly formed HVAC roll-ups would be blind using a traditional database. Those companies don’t exist in Lead411 yet. Origami can find them via a fresh web crawl, pulling data from Google Maps, state business registries, and even recent news articles. Similarly, if you need to reach decision-makers at local service businesses—like a roofer who just got their license—Origami’s live search pulls that in, while Lead411 likely won’t have them.


Lead411 excels for established mid-market and enterprise companies that have been around long enough to be indexed. Its data is clean, verified, and comes with useful extras like intent signals (which topics a company is researching). For a traditional B2B SaaS GTM motion targeting companies with 100+ employees, Lead411’s data quality is comparable to the big players.


**Winner:** Origami for freshness and niche/off-the-beaten-path targets; Lead401 for reliable, long-term B2B company data.

## Pricing and Value: How Do They Stack Up?


Origami’s pricing starts with a generous free plan: 1,000 credits, no credit card required. Paid plans begin at $29/month, and even the top-end Scale plan is $499/month for 40,000 credits. You pay for what you use—each credit covers an AI action, not just a contact returned. This makes it flexible for teams that prospect in bursts.


Lead411 starts at $49/month for 1,000 exports per month (Spark plan) and goes up to $150/month for more exports, with an enterprise plan for unlimited exports. It’s a traditional licensing model: you pay for a set number of exports and access to their database and intent data.


If you’re a small team or solo founder who only needs a few hundred contacts a month, Origami’s free plan gives you enough to get started without spending a dime. Lead411 requires a paid plan after the 7-day trial. However, if you’re a large team that needs unlimited exports and wants built-in intent data, Lead411’s enterprise tier could be more cost-effective than Origami’s credit usage at scale (depending on what “unlimited” actually means).


**Winner:** Origami for low-budget or sporadic prospecting; Lead411 for predictable, high-volume, database-style exports.


---


## Which Tool Is Better for Niche or Local ICPs?


This is where the architectural gap widens. Origami was built for the “I can’t find these people in any database” problem. Sales leaders from home services, medical aesthetics, construction, and industrial supply have told us that ZoomInfo, Apollo, and similar tools “miss the paving contractors we’re going after” or “don’t have SMB contacts.” Lead411 faces the same inherent limitation—if a business doesn’t have a polished online presence or isn’t already in the database, it’s invisible.


Origami’s agent can search beyond the usual suspects. It can look at local business directories, review sites, state license databases, and even social media to build a list. I’ve seen a user find owners of small pest control companies that didn’t appear in any other tool simply by prompting “find pest control business owners in Florida with revenue over $500k who are running Google Ads.”


Lead411 still works if your ICP fits the mold: U.S. companies, tech-friendly verticals, 50+ employees. For those, its filters and intent data save time. But if you’re targeting a non-standard industry where the best contacts aren’t on LinkedIn, Origami is the only choice that’ll give you a list instead of a blank screen.


**Winner:** Origami for niche, local, and hard-to-find prospects; Lead411 for standard B2B segments.


---


## Where Lead411 Still Beats Origami (Honestly)


Let’s be fair: Lead411 has some genuine strengths that Origami doesn’t yet match.


1. **Intent data.** Lead411 tracks what topics companies are researching, which can warm up a cold list. Origami doesn’t offer intent signals (yet). If you want to know which companies are currently researching your competitor, Lead411 gives you that signal out of the box.
2. **Boolean filtering precision.** If you know exactly which firmographic criteria matter and you want to combine them in complex ways (e.g., “company size 100-500, in manufacturing, with a head of IT AND a VP of procurement”), Lead411’s interface lets you build that query systematically. Origami’s AI agent can do it too, but you need to write a clear prompt, and sometimes it requires back-and-forth.
3. **Unlimited exports on enterprise plans.** For a massive outbound engine that needs thousands of contacts daily, an unlimited plan can be simpler than managing credits.
4. **Salesforce integration.** Lead411 offers native integration, while Origami’s is still maturing. If you live in Salesforce and need a two-way sync, Lead401 might feel more turnkey.


So, if you’re a traditional sales team at a mid-market company, you value intent data, and you already have a well-defined ICP that fits a database, Lead411 will absolutely get the job done. It’s been around for years and has a loyal following for a reason.

## Which Type of Team or Company Should Choose Each Tool?


**Choose Origami if:**


- You sell to local businesses, tradespeople, SMBs, or any niche where the decision-makers aren’t on LinkedIn.
- You want to experiment with different ICPs quickly without learning complex filters.
- Your team is lean, without a RevOps person, and you need a tool that works from a plain-English description.
- You need fresh data on newly founded companies or recently changed roles.
- You want to start for free and only pay when you scale.


**Choose Lead411 if:**


- You target established mid-market or enterprise companies and want solid intent data and company news.
- You prefer a traditional filter-based interface and are already comfortable with Boolean logic.
- You need unlimited exports for a high-volume outbound team and prefer a flat-fee model.
- Your workflow relies heavily on Salesforce native integration.

## The Verdict: Origami or Lead411?


I don’t say this lightly: most sales teams I talk to are switching because they’re tired of the same stale contacts from the same databases. If your ICP is “the usual” B2B SaaS buyer—say, VP of Sales at a 100+ person tech company—Lead411 will serve you well and you’ll appreciate its intent signals.


But if you’re targeting local businesses, blue-collar decision-makers, newly funded startups, or any niche that doesn’t neatly fit a database category, Origami is the clear winner. Its ability to crawl the live web and turn a single prompt into a structured list of fresh contacts is something no database can replicate. Add the free plan and the fact that it’s built for speed, not filters, and you have a tool that gets results when others come up empty.


My recommendation? Start with Origami’s free plan. Run a few searches for your hardest-to-find ICP. If you consistently get lists you can act on, you’ll know you don’t need to pay for a database you’ll outgrow. If you find that Origami’s agent isn’t precise enough for your firmographic-heavy targeting, then Lead411 is a solid, reasonably priced alternative.
