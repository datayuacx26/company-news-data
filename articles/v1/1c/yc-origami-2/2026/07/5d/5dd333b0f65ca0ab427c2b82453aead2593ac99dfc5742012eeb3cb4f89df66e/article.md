## What’s the fundamental difference between Origami and Lusha?


Origami is an AI‑powered agent that searches the live web. You describe your ideal customer in plain English — “find me owners of HVAC companies in Dallas who are not running Google Ads” — and the agent crawls the internet, pulls data from multiple sources, and returns a list. It’s a natural‑language Clay competitor that works for any ICP, not just the ones that show up in a static B2B database.


Lusha is a contact database. It stores phone numbers and email addresses, and it refreshes them on a periodic cycle. The core workflow is looking up a person you already know about, typically via its Chrome extension on LinkedIn or a CRM integration. It’s fast for that one task, but it doesn’t build lists for you, and it can’t find companies that aren’t already in its index.


The difference matters. If your ideal customer is a general contractor in Oklahoma, a med spa owner who lives on Instagram, or a startup founder who just raised but isn’t on Crunchbase, Lusha’s database won’t have them. Origami will find them because it doesn’t rely on a pre‑built index — it goes out and looks.

Origami takes a different approach. For targeting larger, LinkedIn‑active organizations, both tools can work. But if your ICP includes any kind of “hard to find” business, Origami is the tool built for that challenge.

## When is Lusha genuinely the better choice?


Let’s be fair. Lusha remains a solid tool for specific workflows, and there are scenarios where it genuinely makes more sense than Origami:


1.


**You only do ad‑hoc lookups.** If your entire outbound motion consists of browsing LinkedIn profiles and occasionally pulling a direct dial or email, Lusha’s Chrome extension is purpose‑built for that. Origami’s agent is overpowered for a one‑at‑a‑time lookup flow.


2.


**Your CRM already does heavy lifting.** Lusha integrates directly into Salesforce and HubSpot, and many sales teams use it as a lightweight enrichment layer inside those systems. If you already have a clean account list and just need a phone number, Lusha may be all you need.


3.


**You only sell to large, well‑known companies.** If every one of your prospects appears on LinkedIn, has a corporate email, and fits neatly into standard firmographic categories, Lusha’s indexed database will likely have them.


In short: Lusha wins at the “enrich this one person” use case. Origami wins at everything before and after that moment — finding who to contact in the first place, building targeted lists, and scaling to accounts that don’t live in a traditional B2B database.
