## How does LeadIQ and Origami source contact data differently?


**LeadIQ** is a Chrome extension that sits on top of LinkedIn (and other sites) and lets you capture a prospect’s name, title, company, and then enriches that record with contact details from its database. It is, at its core, a database-backed enrichment engine with a workflow heavily optimized for Salesforce users. When a rep lands on a LinkedIn profile, LeadIQ can scrape the public fields and attempt to find that person’s email, phone, and sometimes other employer history in its own stored index.


The catch: if the contact isn’t in LeadIQ’s database, you won’t get enrichment beyond what LinkedIn already shows. And because the database is refreshed on a periodic cycle, the data can be days or weeks old. For standard SaaS buyer personas — VP of Sales, Head of Marketing at a tech company — this is often sufficient. For industries where people don’t update LinkedIn religiously (home services, construction, agriculture), or for contacts who aren’t on LinkedIn at all, LeadIQ hits a wall.


**Origami** works on a fundamentally different model. You describe your ideal customer in plain English: “find me roofing company owners in Florida who have at least 5 employees and a Google Business Profile.” The AI agent then crawls the live web — Google Maps, business directories, company websites, social media, job boards, news mentions, even government databases — and assembles a list in real time. There is no static database to fall out of date. Origami also enriches each lead with verified emails and phone numbers drawn from multiple waterfall sources, with your prompt driving the entire search.


The result: Origami can surface companies that LeadIQ’s database has never seen, simply because the business isn’t on LinkedIn or hasn’t been indexed by a traditional B2B data vendor. For teams that need to expand their total addressable market beyond the standard SaaS buyer, this fresh, prompt-driven approach ends the frustration of stale database coverage.


## Which tool gives you better data for niche industries and local businesses?


If your ICP includes people who don’t live on LinkedIn — paving contractors, church leaders, medical spa owners, regional logistics providers — **Origami wins by a large margin** . A user trying to sell to athletic directors summed it up perfectly: “Athletic directors aren’t really liking posts on LinkedIn.” Many small business owners, home services operators, and niche B2B buyers have barely maintained LinkedIn profiles, if they exist at all. LeadIQ’s enrichment engine is tied to LinkedIn profile data; without a robust LinkedIn presence, the tool has nothing to scrape and nothing to match against its database.


Origami’s live web crawling bypasses that constraint entirely. When a private equity professional needed a list of family-owned paving companies, he described the frustration: “I had them build a list of paving companies, and it was totally not a list of paving companies, like it was landscape, total junk.” Standard B2B databases — the kind LeadIQ relies on — often misclassify local trades or simply lack the granular data to distinguish a residential landscaper from a commercial paving operation. Origami’s agent can be told, “Find commercial paving contractors in Florida, privately held, not already PE-backed, with a valid phone number,” and it will comb through DOT registries, local directories, and company websites to build the list.


For enterprise SaaS buyers in standard verticals, LeadIQ holds its own. If your team is prospecting into tech companies, financial services, or large healthcare organizations, the contacts are typically well-represented on LinkedIn, and LeadIQ’s database will often have the email and phone data you need. The difference emerges when you move to mid-market or unknown accounts, or when you want to find brand-new roles (e.g., a CISO hired last week). Origami’s live search finds what’s on the web today; LeadIQ’s database may take weeks to reflect that change.


## Ease of use: prompt vs. browser extension


LeadIQ’s Chrome extension is purpose-built for a specific workflow: a rep is browsing LinkedIn Sales Navigator, finds a prospect, and wants to immediately capture that contact into Salesforce (or HubSpot) with enriched details. The learning curve is minimal — click the extension, select which fields to capture, and the record is created. For high-volume SDR teams who spend hours daily in LinkedIn, this can feel seamless.


The friction starts when you need to build a list from scratch, rather than capture one contact at a time. LeadIQ doesn’t have a “find me 200 roofing company owners” prompt. You would need to manually search LinkedIn, open each profile, and click capture for every single lead. One sales rep described a similar multi-tool process as “copy paste, copy paste like function away.” That grinds prospecting to a halt when you need volumes in the hundreds or thousands.


Origami’s interface is a single chat bar. You type “Find enterprise CSOs at US logistics firms that use SAP and have a sustainability report on their website.” The agent interprets the intent, runs the search, and populates a table with columns like name, title, company, email, phone, LinkedIn URL, and even custom reasoning notes. There is no browsing profiles, no clicking through pages. As one user described it: “I just have to type, I don’t have to find my Marcel with the filters.” This makes Origami dramatically faster for building bulk lists, and it opens up prospecting to anyone on the team — not just the people comfortable navigating complex filter UIs.


LeadIQ’s workflow is natural if your outbound volume is low (5-15 contacts per day) and your entire motion starts inside LinkedIn. For scaling teams that need to generate hundreds of fresh leads per week across multiple ICPs, Origami’s prompt-driven approach saves hours each week.

## When is LeadIQ the better choice in 2026?


Being fair: LeadIQ is a strong tool for a particular workflow, and in some environments it’s the natural fit.


**1. Heavy Salesforce-centric teams that prospect one contact at a time.** If your reps are already spending hours in LinkedIn and your primary goal is to speed up the “profile → Salesforce record” flow, LeadIQ’s extension is purpose-built. It supports custom field mapping, automatic deduplication, and logging of touchpoints. For organizations with deep Salesforce investment and a refinement of an existing ABM list, LeadIQ eliminates dozens of manual copy-paste steps per day.


**2. You’re not building net-new lists; you’re enriching accounts you already know.** If your outbound motion is based on a defined target account list (TAL) and you just need to find the right contacts and emails for companies already in your CRM, LeadIQ’s database alignment with LinkedIn works. However, if you discover your TAL is missing companies or you need to expand into an adjacent vertical, you’ll quickly need a separate list-building tool — which is where Origami complements LeadIQ nicely.


**3. Your ICP is a classic SaaS buyer persona at a company with 50+ employees and robust LinkedIn presence.** For VPs of Engineering at fintech companies, Heads of Product at mid-market SaaS, or Marketing Directors at e-commerce brands, LeadIQ’s data is generally reliable. The LinkedIn profiles are maintained, and the database coverage is decent.


LeadIQ is not the right tool if you need to prospect offline business owners, local service providers, or niche industries where LinkedIn is weak. It also doesn’t help with bulk list generation, intent signals, or AI-driven personalized messaging at scale — all areas where Origami’s prompt-to-outreach loop shines.
