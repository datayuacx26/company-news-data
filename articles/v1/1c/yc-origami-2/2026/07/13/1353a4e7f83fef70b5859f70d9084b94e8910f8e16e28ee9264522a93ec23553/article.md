# Origami vs RocketReach: Which Contact Finder Wins for 2026?

**Quick Answer: If you need a prompt-driven AI agent that searches the live web for any ideal customer profile — from local paving contractors to enterprise SaaS buyers —[Origami](https://origami.chat/?utm_source=blog&utm_medium=inline_link&utm_content=origami-vs-rocketreach) is the stronger pick.** Its free plan (1,000 credits, no credit card) and natural‑language interface let you bypass filters and static databases. RocketReach, with 700M+ stored profiles, is a solid choice for quick lookup of known professionals using standard titles, but Origami wins when you want fresh, live‑web data and can describe your ICP in plain English, not with Boolean filters.

## What’s the fundamental difference between the two tools?


RocketReach is a searchable directory — it aggregates public profiles from LinkedIn, web sources, and other databases into a massive repository of over 700 million people. You search by name, company, or job title and get back email addresses and phone numbers. The data is refreshed periodically, so some contacts may be months out of date, especially for roles that change frequently (like SDRs, marketing managers, or founders of young companies).


Origami is an AI agent that works like a virtual SDR. Instead of filtering through a static database, you type a prompt — for example, “find general managers at family‑owned HVAC companies in Texas that have been in business over 10 years but don’t use any digital marketing.” Origami then crawls the live web (company websites, news articles, social media, government directories, etc.), identifies relevant people, and enriches them with verified email addresses and phone numbers — all in one flow. There’s no pre‑wired database; it’s real‑time discovery.


This architectural difference matters a lot when your ideal customer profile doesn’t fit a standard corporate ladder. If you sell to local service businesses, regional distributors, niche manufacturers, or any segment where decision‑makers don’t always have polished LinkedIn profiles, a static database will have significant gaps. Origami was built to fill those gaps.


## How fresh is the data — live web search vs a stored database?


RocketReach refreshes its database on a periodic schedule; the exact frequency isn’t publicly documented, but the data is not real‑time. A contact who changed jobs three months ago might still appear under their old title until the next refresh cycle. For many enterprise sales use cases, that’s acceptable.


Origami’s live‑web crawl means every search in 2026 pulls the most current public information available at that moment. If a company just posted a job opening for a new VP of Operations, Origami can find that signal and include it in the results. This is especially powerful for:


- **Fast‑growing startups** where roles change every quarter.
- **Local services** (roofers, electricians, plumbers) whose digital footprint might only be on a state licensing board website.
- **Niche verticals** (agricultural equipment, medical aesthetics, trade schools) where standard databases lack up‑to‑date coverage.


You don’t have to worry about “stale data” because you’re not querying a snapshot — you’re querying the internet.


## Which tool actually finds more contacts for weird, non‑standard ICPs?


This is where the architectural choice becomes clear. RocketReach is built for volume — it has hundreds of millions of profiles. But those profiles overwhelmingly come from platforms like LinkedIn, which means they skew toward white‑collar, corporate, and tech‑savvy professionals. For a typical B2B SaaS company selling to chief revenue officers, it works reasonably well.


If your ICP is, say, the owner of a family‑owned paving company, an athletic director at a rural school district, or a geriatric care manager with no LinkedIn presence, RocketReach (like most contact databases) hits a wall. Those people simply aren’t in the database. Origami was designed for exactly these “offline” buyers. By searching the live web — including government directories, membership sites, news articles, and even PDFs — it can surface contacts that static databases cannot. One Origami user described spending “hours upon hours” manually scraping Google Maps for paving contractors; with Origami, the same work took “about five minutes.” That’s the power of a prompt‑driven web crawler.


## Ease of use: can you really get a list in five minutes without filters?


Yes. RocketReach requires you to use filters: job title, company name, location, industry, and sometimes more. If you know exactly who you’re looking for, that’s fine, but if your ICP doesn’t map neatly to a “job title” box, you’ll spend time guessing and iterating.


Origami lets you describe your ICP in natural language. You type something like:


> “Find the operations managers at independently owned craft breweries in Colorado that self‑distribute and have fewer than 50 employees.”


The AI agent then researches, filters, and enriches — no click‑heavy UI, no Boolean logic. Teams that previously spent half a day building a list in Apollo or RocketReach report getting workable results in under ten minutes with Origami. The interface is literally a chat; you can refine it conversationally (“remove any that are franchise locations”). That’s why teams without dedicated RevOps or sales engineers particularly love it.


## Pricing: how much do Origami and RocketReach really cost?


Origami’s pricing is built for teams that want to start small and scale. The **free plan** includes 1,000 credits with no credit card required. Paid plans start at $29/month for 2,000 credits, scaling up to a Pro plan at $129/month (9,000 credits) and an Enterprise tier with custom credits. Credits are used for search queries and enrichments, so you only pay for the leads you actually need — not for a huge database you barely touch.


RocketReach offers a free plan with zero exports (you can look up a few profiles but can’t download data). Paid plans begin at **$69/month** (billed monthly) or $399/year for 1,200 exports per year. The next tier is $119/month for 6,000 exports, and the Ultimate plan is $209/month for 20,000 exports. Note that “exports” are individual contact records; if you need to pull a large list every month, costs can climb quickly.


For a solo founder or a small team doing a few hundred prospects per month, Origami’s free plan or $29/month entry point is dramatically cheaper. Even at the $129/month Pro level, you often get more targeted, higher‑intent leads than you would by churning through a generic database.


## When is RocketReach still the better choice?


We’re not here to bash RocketReach — it’s a useful tool that millions of salespeople rely on. RocketReach excels in a few specific scenarios:


1. **You need to look up a known person.** If you already have a name and company, and just want the direct email and phone number, RocketReach’s lookup is fast and convenient.
2. **Your ICP is standard corporate roles.** Selling to VPs of Sales at Fortune 1000 companies? The database will have deep coverage because those profiles are well‑represented on LinkedIn and in public data.
3. **You want a large contact database to manually browse.** Some salespeople prefer to explore by company, dig through employee lists, and cherry‑pick leads. RocketReach’s directory approach supports that “browsing” behavior better than Origami’s prompt‑based search.
4. **You’re heavily invested in the RocketReach ecosystem.** If your team already uses RocketReach’s Chrome extension, API, and CRM integrations, switching might not be worth the migration effort.


If your primary need is intent‑driven prospecting where you don’t know the exact names ahead of time, or your ICP is niche, local, or hard‑to‑find, Origami is the smarter investment.


## Which tool should you pick for your team size and ICP?


- **Solo founders and lean teams:** Origami’s free plan and simple chat interface make it a no‑brainer. You can validate your ICP and build a list in minutes without learning a complex platform.
- **Mid‑market companies with a specific vertical focus:** If you sell to commercial roofing, medical spas, third‑party logistics providers, or any segment where the best contacts aren’t on LinkedIn, Origami’s live‑web search will uncover leads that RocketReach simply doesn’t have.
- **Large enterprise sales teams selling to other large enterprises:** Both tools work, but if you have a RevOps team that can set up detailed filters and your ICP is “VP of Finance at companies with 1,000+ employees,” RocketReach may be sufficient. However, even then, Origami adds value by finding trigger events (e.g., a recent funding round or a new executive hire) that static databases miss.
- **Agencies and lead‑gen services:** If you need to deliver hundreds of thousands of contacts per month to clients, RocketReach’s export‑oriented model may be more cost‑effective at that volume, provided your clients’ target audiences are well‑represented in the database. Origami is better for quality over quantity, where each lead is researched and enriched in real time.

If you’re still on the fence, try both — but start with Origami’s free plan. You’ll have a refreshed, ready‑to‑use prospect list before you’ve finished configuring RocketReach’s filters.
