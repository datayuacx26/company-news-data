## How Does Origami’s Live Web Search Compare to Clearbit’s Database?


Origami operates as a prompt-driven AI agent that crawls the live web in real time—company websites, Google Maps, social profiles, job postings, and public directories. That means when you type “find me family-owned HVAC companies in Texas with fewer than 50 employees and a Facebook page,” the agent assembles a fresh list, not a snapshot from weeks ago. The system searches across dozens of sources simultaneously, including places traditional databases ignore: state licensing registries, local chamber of commerce directories, and social media signals that indicate a business is active but unlisted on aggregated databases.


Clearbit’s model is fundamentally different. It maintains a curated database of companies and contacts, refreshed on a periodic cycle. That works well for well-known mid-market and enterprise organizations with established digital footprints, but it struggles when your ICP lives offline or in vertical databases (roofing contractors, private dental practices, independent consultants). If a business recently changed ownership, moved locations, or rebranded—Clearbit may not yet reflect that, whereas Origami’s live search sees the current state.


Consider a real scenario: a private equity firm we spoke with targets family-owned paving companies that do $5–$30 million in revenue. Their frustration was palpable: “I had them build a list of paving companies, and it was totally not a list of paving companies, like it was landscape, I mean total junk.” That’s the architectural gap—Clearbit’s database enrichment model works best when the company has a clear, structured web presence. Origami’s agent can follow scent trails that a curated database simply can’t: a job posting for a crew foreman, a local news piece about a road project, a Facebook page showing completed driveways. Those signals don’t exist in Clearbit’s field mapping, but they’re gold for a salesperson.


For teams who sell into “dark” segments—blue-collar owners, restaurant groups, niche law firms—the architectural difference matters. Clearbit’s database was built to enrich accounts you already know; Origami was built to discover the accounts you can’t find anywhere else.


## Which Tool Is Better for Building a Prospecting List from Scratch?


**Origami wins for net-new list creation.** You describe your ideal customer in plain English—“I want coffee shop franchisees in the Pacific Northwest who’ve posted a job for a store manager in the last 30 days”—and the agent returns a spreadsheet with contacts, emails, and phone numbers, often in under two minutes. No filters, no CSV uploads, no boolean logic. This is especially powerful for reps who don’t have a pre-defined account list and need to generate target accounts from a set of characteristics.


Clearbit does not offer a search-by-prompt interface. Its primary value is enrichment: you supply a company domain or a list of domains, and Clearbit appends firmographics, technographics, and contact data. That’s powerful if you already have a target account list, but it won’t help you discover which companies match a nuanced ICP you just defined. If your sales motion starts with “find me companies that look like this,” Clearbit isn’t built to answer that question; Origami answers it in seconds.


Multiple sales leaders in our research described spending hours manually building lists in Apollo or scraping Google Maps with Clay, only to get “total junk” for SMB segments. One sales ops leader explained they spent hours upon hours doing Google Maps scrapes for their ICP, and with Origami the same work was done in about five minutes. That’s the time-to-value gap: Clearbit would require you to first compile a list of domains (somehow), then enrich them. Origami does discovery and enrichment in one step.


For resource-strapped teams without a RevOps engineer, that time savings alone justifies the switch. You don’t need to learn a workflow builder or maintain data pipelines—you just describe who you want and get a list.


## Does Clearbit Have Better Data for Enterprise Companies?


Clearbit’s firmographic data (industry, employee count, revenue, tech stack) is often more structured for large, publicly traded companies. If your entire ICP is Fortune 500 or well-funded SaaS startups, Clearbit’s enrichment of existing account records is a solid choice—especially if you’re already on HubSpot, since Clearbit is now owned by HubSpot and deeply embedded in their CRM. That integration automatically enriches incoming leads with firmographics, so your SDRs never see a blank company field.


However, even within enterprise accounts, buyer roles shift frequently. A VP of sales who left three months ago still populates many static databases. Origami’s live web search catches recent updates—LinkedIn profile changes, news announcements, new job postings—that a batch-refreshed database can miss. One prospect told us they had been building lists from Sales Navigator, only to find that “half of them are no longer active.” That’s the cost of relying on periodic refreshes.


Moreover, many enterprises have complex corporate structures with hundreds of subsidiaries and locations. A database like Clearbit may show the parent company HQ but miss the specific warehouse or distribution center where the decision-maker actually works. Origami’s agent can crawl Google Maps to find those individual locations and surface contacts there—a feature one logistics software buyer called out as crucial: “Something like Zoom Info isn’t going to have all the different warehouses of Albertsons, for example, listed on their website or listed in Zoom Info.” The same limitation applies to Clearbit.


For RevOps teams that already invest in a data orchestration platform, Clearbit’s firmographic enrichment can be a reliable complement. But if your goal is to find net-new logos and reach the person who currently holds the role, a live web search gives you a meaningful freshness advantage.

Clearbit’s pricing is not publicly listed; you must contact sales. Historically, Clearbit has been positioned for mid-market and enterprise companies with a budget that can accommodate annual contracts and minimum commitments. For a startup with two founders and no RevOps team, that’s often a non-starter. Even for larger teams, the opaque pricing model creates friction: you can’t simply start a free trial, see if it works, and scale up.


One prospect in the healthcare space told us they were about to pay $25,000 for a vertical-specific data tool. Origami gave them comparable results for a fraction of the cost. While Clearbit may come with richer analytics and CRM integration out of the box, you pay for that complexity—whether your team uses it or not. If you’re a lean team doing outbound prospecting, Origami’s consumption-based model aligns better with variable usage patterns: you pay for what you use, not for a seat license you might not need every month.


## Can You Use Clearbit for Outbound Without a CRM?


Clearbit is primarily an enrichment API. It’s designed to plug into your CRM (Salesforce, HubSpot) or CDP to append data automatically. If you’re not already using a CRM, Clearbit’s standalone value is limited—you’d need to build a workflow to push lists into a sequencer or engagement tool. That means more tools, more integrations, more maintenance.


Origami, in contrast, is a self-contained outbound prospecting platform. From the chat interface, you can build a list, enrich contacts (email, phone, LinkedIn), and immediately launch multi-channel sequences (email + LinkedIn) without leaving the tool. For solo operators or teams that want “one screen to rule them all,” Origami’s consolidated workflow removes the friction of exporting CSVs and uploading them elsewhere. One founding AE told us: “I don’t want to be using and creating all these different kind of tools between CRM to cross-referencing everything.” That’s the consolidation value Origami provides and Clearbit does not.

## Real‑World Use Cases: Where Each Tool Shines


Let’s make this concrete with real scenarios:


**Use case 1: A PE firm sourcing SMB acquisition targets.** The partner wants “privately held commercial security companies in the Midwest with $3M–$10M revenue, not already PE-owned.” Clearbit’s database wouldn’t surface many of these because they lack corporate websites or sell through word-of-mouth. Origami’s agent can crawl industry association directories, state registrations, and review sites to find owners and their contact information—companies that are often invisible to structured databases.


**Use case 2: A SaaS company targeting HubSpot users at mid-market tech firms.** Clearbit wins here. It already has rich technographic data on software usage, and its HubSpot integration means you can automatically enrich inbound leads with that signal. If you already know your target companies, Clearbit tells you which of them use your competitor and who the likely buyers are.


**Use case 3: A healthcare recruiter needs nursing home administrators in rural Texas.** Clearbit might return generic facility data. Origami’s web search can find the specific administrator name from a recent state inspection report, a LinkedIn post, or a job listing—and verify the email before delivering it. That’s the difference between “I think this person works there” and “this person definitely works there and here’s how to reach them.”


**Use case 4: A RevOps leader wants to score and route website visitors automatically.** Clearbit Reveal is purpose-built for this. Origami doesn’t offer website de-anonymization, so if that’s a core part of your workflow, Clearbit is the clear choice.


## Where Is Clearbit Genuinely the Better Choice?


Context matters. Clearbit excels in a few specific scenarios:


- **Website visitor intelligence:** Clearbit Reveal identifies companies visiting your website, which Origami does not do natively. If you rely on de-anonymizing website traffic to trigger sales outreach, Clearbit is the right tool.
- **HubSpot-centric stacks:** Since HubSpot acquired Clearbit, the integration is native and powerful. If your entire GTM motion lives inside HubSpot and you just need company enrichment on inbound leads, Clearbit will feel seamless.
- **Programmatic enrichment at scale:** If your product needs to enrich millions of records via API into an existing data pipeline, Clearbit’s enrichment API is purpose-built for that—though you’ll pay enterprise rates.


If you’re doing outbound prospecting for new accounts—especially in SMB, local services, or niche industries—Origami is the better fit. Its prompt-driven model and live web search deliver results that a batch-curated database wasn’t designed to generate.

**Choose Origami if:**


- You need to build prospecting lists from scratch based on a detailed ICP description.

- You have an enterprise budget and a dedicated RevOps team to manage data pipelines.


Both tools solve real problems. But if your team’s mantra is “I need to find the right people and reach them now,” Origami’s AI agent, fresh web data, and zero-friction onboarding make it the faster path from idea to booked meeting.
