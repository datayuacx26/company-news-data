## The Challenge: High-Voltage Isolation in Electric Vehicles


Electric vehicles contain multiple high-voltage subsystems that must remain electrically isolated from the chassis, including:


- High-voltage battery packs
- DC-DC converters
- Traction inverters
- Onboard chargers connected to the power grid


Without reliable isolation measurement, leakage currents can create **safety risks, false faults, or system shutdowns** .


#### Why Isolation Measurement Matters


Over time, isolation resistance can degrade due to:


- Moisture ingress
- Dirt and contaminants
- Thermal cycling
- Mechanical stress


Without continuous monitoring, isolation failure can lead to unsafe touch voltages, system faults, or vehicle shutdowns. This makes reliable, low-leakage switching components essential for isolation measurement circuits.

## Why Reed Relays Are Used for EV Isolation Measurement


[Reed relays](https://standexdetect.com/products/reed-relays/) combine high isolation voltage, ultra-low leakage current, and long operational life, making them well suited for electric vehicle isolation measurement circuits.


### Key Electrical Advantages


- Isolation voltages up to **10 kVDC**
- Breakdown voltage up to **4.5 kV**
- Leakage current in the **picoampere range**
- Contact resistance in the **milliohm range**


### How Reed Relays Work (and Why It Matters)


A[reed relay](https://standexdetect.com/products/reed-relays/) consists of:


- A hermetically sealed reed switch with magnetically soft alloy contacts
- An inert gas or vacuum-filled glass capsule to prevent oxidation
- A copper coil that magnetically actuates the contacts
- A sealed housing for mechanical and environmental protection


### Engineering Benefits of This Construction


- No exposed contacts → no contamination or corrosion
- No mechanical wear mechanisms → long service life
- Stable contact resistance → consistent measurement accuracy


Compared to traditional electromechanical relays, reed relays eliminate many common failure modes caused by debris, oxidation, and mechanical fatigue.
