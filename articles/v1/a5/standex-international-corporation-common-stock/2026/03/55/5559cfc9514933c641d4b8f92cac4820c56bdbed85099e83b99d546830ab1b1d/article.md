# An Essential Design Decision: Reed Switches Benefits

### Introduction


Reed switches are electromechanical devices actuated by a magnetic field. Two ferromagnetic blades sealed in glass either close (NO) or open (NC) when a magnet approaches, providing galvanic isolation and hermetic protection against corrosion and arcing. They’re ideal for harsh, dusty, or wet environments across a wide temperature range.

### Key Electrical Parameters


- **Pull-In (PO):** Field/current/voltage (or AT) where contacts close; typically specified as a maximum.
- **Drop-Out (DO):** Point where contacts open (release/reset).
- **Hysteresis:** Often expressed as DO/PI (%); useful to avoid chatter.
- **Contact resistance:** Typically, contact resistance for a reed switch is ~ 70 mW, (10 to 25 mW of which is the actual resistance across the contacts).
- **Dynamic contact resistance:** Measured during operation to assess true contact condition.


For a more in-depth explanation of these and other key parameters jump to the blog:[Basic Electrical Parameters of Reed Switch Products](https://standexdetect.com/resources/reed-technology-academy/reed-switch-characteristics/basic-electrical-parameters-of-reed-switch-products/) .


> **Pro tip:** Saturate the reed with a strong magnetic field before tests for consistent PI/DO readings.


### Benefits of Reed Switches


#### Hermetically sealed, galvanically isolated contacts


Operate in liquids, gases, dust, and radiation.


#### Zero power


Zero power to hold its off state; low control power (~50–200 mW) for relays.


#### Fast switching


(~0.5–1.5 ms) with low resistance (~0.05–0.15 Ω) and high insulation (10¹⁰–10¹² Ω).


#### Durability


10⁶–10⁸ cycles; shock tolerance to ~500 g; vibration to ~3 kHz at 15–20 g.


#### Temperature range


About −60 °C to +155 °C.


#### Load flexibility


from μW to ~120 W.


#### Tighter sensitivity bands


(e.g., 18–22 AT) than typical Hall sensors, plus inherent hysteresis useful in metering/positioning.
