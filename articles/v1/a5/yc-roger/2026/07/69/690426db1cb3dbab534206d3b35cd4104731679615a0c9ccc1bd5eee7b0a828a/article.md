# Understanding 'Under Contract' vs. 'Pending' in Real Estate

## ❓What does 'under contract' mean in real estate?


In[real estate](https://www.thanksroger.com/usecases/real-estate) , the term "under contract" signifies that a seller has accepted a buyer's offer, and both parties have signed a purchase agreement. However, this agreement is typically contingent upon certain conditions being met before the sale is finalized. These conditions, known as contingencies, can include home inspections, financing approvals, appraisals, or the sale of the buyer's current home.


During this phase, the property is still technically on the market, and the seller may accept backup offers. This status indicates that while the initial steps toward a sale have been taken, there are still hurdles to clear. The buyer and seller must work through these contingencies, and if any are not satisfied, the deal can fall through.


It's important for both buyers and sellers to understand that being "under contract" does not guarantee a sale. The process requires careful navigation of the agreed-upon terms, and until all contingencies are resolved, the property remains in a state of limbo. This stage is crucial for due diligence, allowing both parties to ensure that the transaction proceeds smoothly and in accordance with the agreed terms.


## ❓What does 'pending' mean in real estate?


The term "pending" in real estate indicates that all contingencies outlined in the purchase agreement have been met, and the transaction is in its final stages before closing. At this point, the buyer and seller have fulfilled their respective obligations, such as completing inspections, securing financing, and agreeing on any necessary repairs.


Once a property is marked as pending, it is typically removed from the market, and no further offers are entertained. This status reflects a high level of commitment from both parties, signaling that the sale is imminent. However, it's worth noting that, although rare, deals can still fall through during this phase due to unforeseen circumstances, such as last-minute financing issues or changes in buyer or seller circumstances.


For prospective buyers, a pending status usually means the opportunity to purchase that particular property has passed. For sellers, reaching the pending stage is a positive sign that the transaction is nearing completion. It's a period of anticipation and preparation for the final steps of the real estate transaction, culminating in the transfer of ownership.


## ❓How do 'under contract' and 'pending' statuses differ?


While both "under contract" and "pending" indicate that a property is in the process of being sold, they represent different stages within that process. Understanding the distinction between these terms is essential for buyers and sellers navigating the real estate market.


"Under contract" refers to the initial phase after a seller has accepted a buyer's offer, but before all contingencies have been satisfied. During this time, the sale is not guaranteed, as various conditions must be met. The property may still be shown to other potential buyers, and backup offers can be submitted.


In contrast, "pending" signifies that all contingencies have been addressed, and the sale is moving toward closing. At this stage, the property is typically taken off the market, and no additional offers are considered. The likelihood of the sale falling through is significantly lower compared to the "under contract" phase.


In summary, "under contract" is an earlier stage where the sale is conditional, while "pending" indicates that the transaction is nearing completion, with all conditions met. Recognizing these differences helps manage expectations and informs decision-making for all parties involved.


## ❓Can a deal fall through when a property is under contract or pending?


Yes, real estate deals can fall through during both the "under contract" and "pending" stages, although the likelihood varies between the two. Understanding the potential pitfalls at each stage is crucial for buyers and sellers.


During the "under contract" phase, deals are more susceptible to falling apart due to unmet contingencies. Common reasons include:


-


**Financing Issues** : The buyer may fail to secure a mortgage.


-


**Inspection Problems** : Unforeseen issues discovered during the home inspection can lead to renegotiations or cancellations.


-


**Appraisal Discrepancies** : If the property's appraised value is lower than the agreed-upon price, financing may be affected.


-


**Title Complications** : Legal issues with the property's title can delay or derail the sale.


In the "pending" stage, most contingencies have been resolved, reducing the risk of the deal collapsing. However, unexpected events can still occur, such as:


-


**Job Loss** : The buyer loses employment, affecting their ability to secure financing.


-


**Personal Circumstances** : Changes in personal situations may lead to withdrawal from the deal.


-


While the "pending" status offers more assurance, it's not an absolute guarantee of sale completion. Both buyers and sellers should remain vigilant and prepared for potential challenges until the transaction is officially closed.


## ❓Can buyers submit offers on properties that are under contract or pending?


Yes, buyers can submit offers on properties that are under contract, and in some cases, even on those that are pending. However, the likelihood of the seller considering these offers varies depending on the property's status.


For properties "under contract," sellers may accept backup offers. These are secondary offers that the seller can consider if the primary deal falls through due to unmet contingencies or other issues. Submitting a backup offer can position a buyer favorably should the initial transaction not proceed to closing.


When a property is "pending," it typically means all contingencies have been satisfied, and the sale is nearing completion. In this stage, sellers are less likely to entertain new offers. However, it's not entirely out of the question. Some sellers may accept backup offers as a precaution against unforeseen complications that could jeopardize the pending sale.


Buyers interested in such properties should communicate with their real estate agents to understand the specific circumstances and determine the best course of action. While the chances may be slim, being proactive can sometimes lead to unexpected opportunities in the dynamic real estate market.


## ❓How can buyers and sellers navigate the 'under contract' and 'pending' stages effectively?


Navigating the "under contract" and "pending" stages requires diligence, communication, and preparation from both buyers and sellers to ensure a smooth transaction.


**For Buyers:**


-


**Stay Informed** : Regularly communicate with your real estate agent to stay updated on the progress of contingencies and any potential issues.


-


**Fulfill Obligations Promptly** : Complete necessary inspections, secure financing, and provide required documentation within agreed timelines.


-


**Be Prepared for Contingencies** : Understand the terms of your contingencies and be ready to negotiate or address any findings from inspections or appraisals.


**For Sellers:**


-


**Maintain Transparency** : Provide accurate information about the property's condition and any known issues to prevent surprises during inspections.


-


**Stay Engaged** : Keep in touch with your agent and be responsive to requests for information or access to the property.


-


**Consider Backup Offers** : Evaluate the benefits of accepting backup offers, especially during the "under contract" phase, to mitigate risks if the primary deal falls through.


Both parties should work closely with their real estate professionals to navigate these stages effectively. Clear communication, adherence to timelines, and proactive problem-solving are key to transitioning from "under contract" to a successful closing.
