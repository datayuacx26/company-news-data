# How Affidavits Work and When to Use One | ROGER Blog

## ❓What is an affidavit?


An affidavit is a written statement of facts that you swear to be true, usually in front of a notary or authorized witness. It’s used in legal settings where someone needs to formally confirm the truthfulness of information, like in court cases, estate matters, or official declarations.


Once signed and witnessed, it becomes a legally binding document. Lying in an affidavit can lead to penalties, including perjury charges. If you’re signing one, make sure everything in the document is accurate and honest.


## ❓Do affidavits need to be notarized?


Yes, in most cases affidavits must be notarized or signed in front of someone authorized to witness legal documents. This adds a layer of authenticity and ensures that the statement was made voluntarily and with an understanding of the legal consequences. Depending on where you are, this witness could be a notary public, lawyer, or court official. Online notarization is becoming more common, making the process even more accessible.


## ❓Is an affidavit the same as a sworn statement?


They’re similar, but not always the same. Both involve stating facts under oath, but an affidavit is typically a more formal document that’s signed and witnessed as part of a legal process. A sworn statement may or may not require a notary, depending on the jurisdiction or the purpose.


If you're not sure which one applies to your situation, it's best to check with your lawyer or the institution requesting the document.


## ❓When would you use an affidavit?


You might need an affidavit in a variety of situations. These can include confirming your identity, verifying residency, stating income or assets, or providing testimony in a legal dispute. They're commonly used in family law, probate matters, immigration cases, and even for routine paperwork like name changes.


Whether you're in court or simply completing a legal process, affidavits allow you to present verified facts without needing to appear in person.

## ❓What should be included in an affidavit?


An affidavit should clearly include your full name, address, and a statement that you are swearing the contents are true. Then comes the main body, where you list the facts you’re declaring. Finally, it should include your signature, the date, and space for notarization or witnessing.


Keep the language factual and straightforward. Avoid speculation or opinions. If you're unsure how to format one, there are templates available, or you can consult a legal professional.


## ❓Is an affidavit legally binding?


Yes, once properly signed and witnessed, an affidavit is a legally binding document. It’s considered evidence in legal proceedings and holds the same weight as in-person testimony. If it’s proven that you knowingly lied in an affidavit, you could face perjury charges, fines, or even jail time.


Because of its legal weight, you should never sign an affidavit unless you're confident everything in it is true to the best of your knowledge.


## ❓How long is an affidavit valid?


Affidavits do not typically expire, but their relevance can depend on the context. For instance, if you swore an affidavit to confirm your income or residency, it might only be considered accurate for a limited time, such as 30 or 60 days, depending on who’s requesting it.


Courts and agencies usually assess affidavits based on when they were signed and whether the information remains current. So even though your affidavit is legally valid, make sure it's still factually accurate if you're reusing it.
