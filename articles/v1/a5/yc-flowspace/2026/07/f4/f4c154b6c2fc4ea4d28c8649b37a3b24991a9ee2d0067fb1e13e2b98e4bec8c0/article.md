# What is the best fulfillment provider for B2B companies?

Choosing the best fulfillment provider for B2B companies is a high-stakes decision—one that directly impacts margins, retail relationships, freight costs, and long-term scalability.


B2B fulfillment is structurally different from DTC ecommerce fulfillment. It often involves:


- Palletized and freight-based shipments
- Purchase order–driven workflows
- EDI integrations
- Retailer routing guide compliance
- Recurring wholesale replenishment
- Multi-node inventory coordination


These requirements demand operational discipline, freight expertise, and system integration depth that many ecommerce-first 3PLs are not designed to handle at scale. This guide evaluates leading fulfillment providers through a B2B lens—and explains why Flowspace is often the strongest fit for mid-market brands managing freight-heavy, compliance-driven operations.


## What is B2B fulfillment?


B2B (business-to-business) fulfillment refers to the storage, picking, packing, and shipping of products from one business to another, including:


- Wholesale accounts
- Retail distribution centers
- Distributors
- Marketplace retail programs
- Enterprise buyers


Unlike DTC fulfillment, B2B operations are structured around purchase orders, compliance documentation, and freight coordination.


Common B2B fulfillment requirements include:


- Case-pack and palletized shipments
- LTL and FTL freight management
- EDI transactions (e.g., 850, 856, 810)
- Advanced Shipping Notices (ASNs)
- Retailer routing guide compliance
- Carton and pallet labeling (UCC-128)
- Appointment scheduling with retailer DCs
- Lot tracking and recall readiness


While some B2B orders ship parcel, such as retail drop-ship programs, wholesale replenishment is frequently freight-driven and operationally complex. Not every 3PL is built for this level of coordination.


## What differentiates a true B2B fulfillment provider?


Many 3PLs advertise B2B capabilities. Fewer are structurally optimized for it.


Here are the capabilities that matter most:


### 1. PO-driven workflows and retail compliance


B2B fulfillment requires:


- Accurate purchase order processing
- Partial shipments and backorder management
- EDI integrations with retail partners
- ASN accuracy and timeliness
- Routing guide adherence to prevent chargebacks
- Lot-level traceability when required


Compliance failures don’t just create operational friction—they reduce margins.


### 2. Freight-first infrastructure


Freight management is central to most B2B programs.


Look for:


- LTL and FTL coordination
- Pallet configuration and documentation standards
- Retailer DC appointment scheduling
- Carrier network access and rate optimization
- Cross-dock and bulk handling capabilities


Providers built primarily around small-parcel ecommerce may support freight—but often as a secondary capability rather than a core operating model.


### 3. Distributed inventory strategy


As wholesale volume increases, distributed inventory becomes a strategic lever:


- Reduced transit times to retail DCs
- Lower zone-based parcel costs for hybrid programs
- Improved OTIF performance
- Risk mitigation across regions


A scalable B2B partner should support multi-node flexibility and network orchestration—not just single-warehouse execution.


### 4. Systems integration and visibility


B2B fulfillment requires reliable integration across:


- ERP systems
- EDI providers
- Order management systems
- Inventory planning platforms


Without integration depth and real-time inventory visibility, operations become manual and error-prone.

### 2. ShipBob (best for DTC brands expanding into retail)


**Best for:** Ecommerce brands adding wholesale or retail distribution to an established DTC business.


ShipBob is widely recognized for DTC fulfillment and supports omnichannel operations, including EDI-enabled retail distribution.


**Pros:**


- Strong ecommerce platform integrations
- Nationwide warehouse presence
- Unified DTC and retail order management
- Retail-compliant shipping workflows

- Operational model is rooted in parcel-first ecommerce
- Freight-heavy wholesale programs may require additional coordination
- Deep customization for complex B2B workflows may be more limited than freight-focused providers

### 3. Red Stag Fulfillment (best for heavy, bulky, or high-value products)


**Best for:** Manufacturers and distributors shipping large, heavy, or fragile products.


Red Stag specializes in big and bulky fulfillment and emphasizes shipment accuracy and damage prevention. It supports B2B freight programs and retail compliance workflows.


**Pros:**

- Secure handling of high-value goods
- LTL and freight experience
- High-touch operational support

- Not designed for high-SKU ecommerce scale
- Multi-node inventory orchestration may be more limited than broader network platforms


Red Stag is particularly well suited for product-specific freight needs rather than nationwide distributed B2B scale.


### 4. DHL Supply Chain (best for global enterprise operations)


**Best for:** Large multinational enterprises with complex contract logistics requirements.


DHL Supply Chain operates at global scale and offers integrated warehousing and transportation solutions under long-term contract logistics models.


**Pros:**

- End-to-end contract logistics solutions

### 5. Flexport (best for international freight-led logistics)


**Best for:** Companies where global trade and international freight visibility are primary operational priorities.


Flexport is best known for freight forwarding, customs brokerage, and global logistics visibility. It also offers warehousing and fulfillment capabilities within its logistics network.


**Pros:**


- Strong ocean and air freight expertise
- Centralized global shipment visibility
- Integrated customs brokerage
- Warehousing and fulfillment within its platform


**Cons:**


- Core strength is international freight rather than domestic wholesale fulfillment
- Domestic B2B retail compliance capabilities should be validated based on program requirements

### 6. Regional or niche 3PLs (best for specialized B2B requirements)


**Best for:** Industry-specific needs such as medical, industrial, manufacturing, or regulated products.


Regional 3PLs often provide high-touch service and tailored operational models.


**Pros:**


- Industry expertise
- Dedicated account management
- Flexible customization
- Strong local carrier relationships


**Cons:**


- Limited geographic coverage
- Scalability constraints as volume increases
- Technology and integration depth may vary


These providers can be strong tactical fits but may require reevaluation as distribution complexity grows.

## How to choose the right B2B fulfillment partner


Before signing, ask:


- Is freight coordination a core competency or an add-on service?
- How do they manage EDI and retailer compliance workflows?
- What are their OTIF and inventory accuracy benchmarks?
- Can they support distributed inventory strategy?
- Will scaling require a full network migration?
- How transparent are reporting and pricing?


The right B2B partner should strengthen retail relationships, protect margin, and scale with your growth.


## Final thoughts


B2B fulfillment demands more than storage and shipping. It requires freight expertise, compliance discipline, inventory orchestration, and integration depth.


For brands navigating wholesale expansion, retail compliance, and distributed freight operations, selecting a provider purpose-built for complex B2B workflows can materially reduce operational risk and support long-term growth.


When evaluating providers, look beyond feature lists and assess whether B2B fulfillment is truly core to the operating model.
