# How to choose a 3PL: Why onboarding matters

TL;DR


- **3PL onboarding should factor into your decision.** It shows how well a provider can get your operation live.
- A strong onboarding process covers systems, inventory, workflows, testing, and go-live.
- Evaluate 3PL integrations for both your current channels and future growth.
- Ask who owns implementation, how transitions are managed, and what support looks like after launch.
- The right 3PL should make onboarding clear, structured, and scalable.


Choosing a 3PL often comes down to cost, warehouse locations, shipping speed, technology, and fulfillment capabilities.


But there’s another important factor: how the provider gets you up and running.


A strong 3PL onboarding process should make it easier to connect systems, transfer inventory, establish workflows, and launch without disrupting operations. It can also reveal what working with a provider will be like long term.


If you’re figuring out how to choose a 3PL, onboarding should be part of the evaluation process.


## What to consider when choosing a 3PL


The right[third-party logistics partner](https://flow.space/blog/third-party-logistics) (3PL) should fit your current operation and support where your business is headed. Key considerations include:


What to consider What to look for


Fulfillment capabilities Support for your products, order profiles, shipping requirements, returns, kitting, and special handling needs.


Warehouse network Locations that support your customer base, delivery goals, and shipping-cost targets.


Technology and visibility Clear visibility into orders, inventory, shipping, and fulfillment performance.


Channel support Ability to support ecommerce, marketplaces, wholesale, retail, and future channel expansion.


Scalability Capacity to handle seasonal peaks, higher order volume, new products, and geographic growth.


Customer support Clear account ownership, escalation paths, and ongoing communication.


Pricing and contracts Transparent costs across receiving, storage, fulfillment, shipping, special projects, minimums, and contract terms.


These factors tell you what a provider can offer. Onboarding shows you how effectively they can put those capabilities into practice.


## Why 3PL onboarding should factor into your decision


Your relationship with a[3PL](https://flow.space/blog/differences-between-3pl-4pl) starts before the first order ships.


During onboarding, the provider needs to understand your operation, connect systems, receive inventory, configure fulfillment rules, document workflows, and prepare for launch.


A clear 3PL onboarding process can reduce issues like inaccurate inventory, incorrect shipping rules, integration failures, and unclear responsibilities.


It can also be an early indicator of the overall partnership.


When choosing a 3PL, ask not only what the provider can do once you’re live, but how they plan to get you there.


## What does the 3PL onboarding process include?


While every provider works differently, onboarding typically includes a few key stages.


### 1. Discovery and planning


The 3PL should understand your products, order volume, sales channels, shipping requirements, packaging, returns, and other operational needs.


Both teams should also align on responsibilities, dependencies, and launch timing.


### 2. Product and SKU setup


Product details such as SKUs, dimensions, weights, barcodes, bundles, and handling requirements need to be configured accurately.


### 3. Systems integration


Your commerce and operational systems need to connect with the 3PL so orders, inventory, shipment status, and tracking data can move reliably.


### 4. Inventory inbounding


The provider should establish when and where inventory will arrive, how it should be prepared, and how receiving will be handled.


### 5. Fulfillment workflows


Shipping methods, routing rules, packaging, kitting, inserts, retail requirements, and exception workflows should be documented and configured.


### 6. Returns


Define how returned products will be received, inspected, restocked, or otherwise handled.


### 7. Testing


Test orders should confirm that integrations, inventory data, fulfillment workflows, shipping rules, and tracking are working correctly.


### 8. Go-live


Before launch, inventory should be available, integrations tested, workflows documented, and ownership clear.


## Where 3PL integrations fit in


At a minimum, orders need to reach the fulfillment provider, and shipment information needs to flow back. But modern fulfillment operations often involve much more than a single ecommerce platform and warehouse.


Your 3PL integration requirements might include connections to:


- Ecommerce platforms
- Marketplaces
- ERP systems
- [Order management systems](https://flow.space/blog/best-order-management-software)
- Retail and EDI connections
- Inventory management systems
- Other logistics and technology partners


When evaluating integrations, don't focus solely on your current tech stack.


For example, a DTC brand that currently generates most of its orders through its ecommerce website may plan to expand into Amazon, wholesale, or major retail partners. Those channels introduce new fulfillment and inventory requirements.


A provider that can support those channels within the same broader fulfillment operation can make expansion easier than one that requires new systems, workflows, or partners every time the business evolves.


Inventory visibility is another consideration. As orders come from multiple channels and inventory is distributed across locations, your team needs to know what inventory is available and where. Look for technology that provides centralized inventory visibility and helps your business make decisions around allocation, replenishment, and potential stockouts.


### Questions to ask about 3PL integrations


- Which integrations are native, and which require custom development?
- Who owns the technical implementation?
- How is order and inventory data synchronized?
- How are integration errors surfaced and resolved?
- How will your team know if an order fails to flow correctly?
- Can the technology support additional sales channels as the business grows?
- Can you view inventory across multiple fulfillment locations and channels?
- What inventory management or planning capabilities are available?


*The goal isn't just to connect two systems, but to create a reliable flow of information across your fulfillment operation.*


## What to ask about onboarding before choosing a 3PL


Before signing a contract, ask:


- What does your 3PL onboarding process look like?
- How long does onboarding typically take?
- Who owns implementation?
- What does your team need from ours?
- What does the 3PL integration process involve?
- How will inventory be transferred or received?
- How are SOPs documented?
- How do you test workflows before launch?
- What happens if something delays go-live?
- What support is available after launch?
- How would you support a new channel or fulfillment location?


The answers should give you a clear sense of timing, ownership, responsibilities, and dependencies.

## Frequently asked questions about 3PL onboarding


### What is 3PL onboarding?


3PL onboarding is the process of setting up a brand to begin fulfilling orders with a third-party logistics provider. It typically includes operational planning, product and SKU setup, system integrations, inventory receiving, fulfillment workflow configuration, testing, and preparation for go-live.


### Why is onboarding important when choosing a partner?


Onboarding can provide an early indication of how a 3PL operates. When[switching 3PLs](https://flow.space/blog/switch-3pl-after-prime-day) , a clear process with defined responsibilities, reliable integrations, thorough testing, and strong communication can help reduce disruption.


### How long does onboarding take?


The timeline depends on the complexity of the operation. Factors such as SKU count, integrations, inventory transfers, sales channels, fulfillment requirements, and custom workflows can all affect how long onboarding takes. Ask prospective providers for a timeline based on your specific operation rather than relying on a standard estimate.


### What should I look for in a 3PL onboarding process?


Look for clear ownership, defined milestones, support for system integrations, a detailed inventory transition plan, documented fulfillment requirements, pre-launch testing, and a clear process for resolving issues before and after go-live.


### What is a 3PL integration?


A 3PL integration connects a fulfillment provider with the systems a brand uses to manage commerce and operations. These may include ecommerce platforms, marketplaces, ERPs, order management systems, retail and EDI connections, and inventory management tools.


### What integrations should I consider when choosing a 3PL?


Consider both the systems you use today and the channels you may add in the future. If you plan to expand from DTC into marketplaces, wholesale, or retail, for example, make sure your 3PL can support those channels and provide visibility into inventory and orders across your operation.


### What questions should I ask before onboarding?


Ask who will own implementation, what information your team needs to provide, how integrations are set up and tested, how inventory will be transferred or received, how fulfillment workflows are documented, and what support is available after launch.
