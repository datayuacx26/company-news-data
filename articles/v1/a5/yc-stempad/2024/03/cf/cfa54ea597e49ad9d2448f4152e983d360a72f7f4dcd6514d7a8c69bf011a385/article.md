# A Brief History of Notetaking

# A Brief History of Notetaking


By Jordan Nguyen


Created on May 5, 2025


Notetaking has been an essential part of human knowledge for centuries. From ancient clay tablets to digital apps, the way we capture and organize information has constantly evolved. Let's take a look at the main eras of notetaking.


## Early Notetaking: Clay, Papyrus, and Paper


The earliest records of notetaking date back to Mesopotamia, where scribes marked stones with notches to record useful things like trade records and grain storage. In Egypt, scholars used papyrus scrolls to document everything from medical texts to philosophy. But it was the invention of paper in China around 100 CE that truly revolutionized notetaking, making writing easier and more accessible.


## The Rise of Personal Notes


During the Renaissance, scholars like Leonardo da Vinci kept detailed notebooks filled with sketches, observations, and ideas. This was the beginning of a new era where people could write more personal ideas, instead of just writing when 'necessary'. This was also an era where more people began to write, instead of it being limited to a small selection of literate people.


The printing press also helped spread literacy, making it easier to share documents on a wider scale. By the 18th and 19th centuries, notebooks became a staple for scientists, writers, and students, and writing out of interest and passion became more normalized.


## The Digital Shift


The 20th century brought another leap forward—digital notetaking. With the rise of computers, people began using word processors to replace handwritten notes. It took a while for typing to catch on, but eventually typing became just as critical a part of education as learning to handwrite or understand cursive. This is especially helpful for people who don't have good handwriting.


The internet further expanded possibilities, allowing users to store, search, and share their notes effortlessly. Today, cloud-based apps make notetaking more dynamic, integrating multimedia, collaboration, and advanced organization features. People no longer have to worry about storing their own notes as paper copies, or as files that take up space on their computers.
