# Welcome to Stempad: Scientific Writing at the Speed of Thought


By Alastar Kerpel


Created on January 20, 2025


Welcome to the Stempad blog! If you're frustrated with taking scientific notes or just discovered Stempad, this article is for you. With scientific research, education, and collaboration becoming increasingly digital, what about the tools that support them?


The need for a scientific writing tool and scientific visualization platform has never been greater. But until now, the only choices were taking notes by hand or creating a patchwork of complex tools. Introducing Stempad, scientific writing at the speed of thought, purposefully built for STEM teachers, students, and scientists.


## Necessity is the Mother of Invention


Ralph Rouhana created Stempad as a personal project while studying computer and electrical electronics engineering at the University of Waterloo. Like a lot of people in the STEM field, he struggled with handwriting notes yet existing tools like LaTeX, while powerful, were too complex for the quick learning curve needed while studying. So, Rouhana created what he needed for scientific notetaking.


> I always struggled with handwritten notes in class," Rouhana confesses. "Doing illustrative scientific writing digitally was incredibly clunky. There was no tool that made it fast, intuitive, or offered an all-in-one platform.


What started as a personal project quickly evolved into a full-fledged business concept that earned a spot in Y Combinator's Summer 2024 (S24) batch. Rouhana's vision for Stempad was simple: to create an all-encompassing digital platform where users could take scientific notes, draft papers, conduct exams, and collaborate on research. By doing so, Stempad solves a common problem in the scientific fields, where fragmented and costly tools have been standard.


## The Problem: Traditional Scientific Tools are Outdated


Most scientific tools today aren't made for the needs of students, scientists, and educators. These solutions fall short in several ways.


- Handwriting challenges: Many students and scientists rely on handwriting, which can be slow and messy. Paper notes, tablets, and whiteboards, don't produce very organized digital documents.
- Not purpose-built: Text editors like Microsoft Word and Notion are popular but lack the functionality of scientific writing tools such as quick equation input, chemical and molecular diagrams, or scientific visualization tools.
- High costs and learning curves: Tools like LaTeX, while powerful require a significant amount of time learning them to become proficient. Similarly, digital whiteboards require expensive tablets, styluses, and software, making them out of reach for many people.


Fragmented systems really don't align with modern scientists and educators, especially when you factor in licensing fees and other hurdles. For teachers and students who want to take notes quickly, troubleshoot experiments, and visualize data tools, there isn't another solution that's lightweight yet efficient.


## Stempad: The Ideal Scientific Writing Tool


Stempad preserves the ease of pen-and-paper but also offers speed and precision. With an integrated platform, users can switch between text, code, equations, and scientific visualizations—all from their keyboards.


- **Real-Time Collaboration:** Collaborate with team members or classmates, which can be useful in research, study groups, or tutoring settings.
- **All-in-One Platform:** Take notes, write research papers, create exams, and grade assignments in one place.
- **Save and Export:** Easily share research with colleagues or submit assignments to professors through built-in save and export functionality.
- **Ease of Use:** Jump in and get started right away using Stempad's intuitive platform, regardless of technical skills.


Stempad enables virtually anyone who works in the sciences to increase productivity and experience a better quality of life. Rouhana notes "The ability to quickly and collaboratively document scientific ideas with a keyboard is a huge quality-of-life and productivity boost for people and institutions doing science."
