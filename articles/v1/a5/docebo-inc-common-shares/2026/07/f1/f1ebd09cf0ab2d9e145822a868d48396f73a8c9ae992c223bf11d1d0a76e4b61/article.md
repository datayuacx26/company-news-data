# From fragmented training to unified impact: How Insurity built an AI-ready learning ecosystem

Quick recap because we know you’ve got a day job: last time, we talked about[skills intelligence](https://www.docebo.com/learning-network/blog/skills-intelligence-system-workforce-readiness/) , and how SNCF turned a 155,000-person workforce into a real-time skills engine instead of a spreadsheet nobody updates. The headline was €100 million in savings. The real story was what happens when talent and learning finally look at the same data.


This time, we’re widening the lens. Because the AI readiness gap doesn’t stop at your org chart. It follows your product out the door, into your customers’ hands and your partners’ workflows too.


Meet Insurity.


## Breaking free from training in silos


Insurity builds software for the insurance industry, which means their success depends on three different audiences actually knowing how to use what they built: employees who support it, customers who buy it, and partners who implement and extend it.


For a while, each of those audiences got a different version of training. Employees had one system. Customers got another, often delivered in person, which is a nice way of saying expensive, slow to scale, and hard to personalize. Partners were mostly figuring it out on their own.


Siloes are challenging because other than creating redundant work, they actively work against personalization. You can’t tailor a learning path to what someone actually needs if you don’t have a unified view of what they’ve already learned, what they’re stuck on, or what they’re trying to do next. Three disconnected systems means three disconnected pictures of readiness, and none of them are complete.


To break free from this, Insurity consolidated employee, customer, and partner education onto a single Docebo platform. The end result was that learning came from the same infrastructure, same personalization engine, same data model, with each different audience pulling what they needed from it.


One platform, three audiences, one growth engine


When Insurity unified their platform, they weren’t just cutting costs on venue rentals and travel. They gave their AI-driven learning engine a full picture across every audience it serves. A customer struggling with a specific feature and a new partner ramping up on implementation can both get development paths that reflect where they actually are, not a generic onboarding module built for an average that doesn’t describe anyone.


This shift moved them from in-person, cost-heavy training to scalable digital-first delivery. But the more interesting part is that this consolidation led to:


- **A 50+ point increase in employee NPS**
- **30X user growth**
- **New revenue streams through certification**


Internal readiness and external adoption stopped being separate initiatives and started reinforcing each other, the same cycle we described with skills intelligence, just running across a wider set of learners.


## Lessons from Insurity: What you can apply to your org


Insurity didn’t get here by flipping a switch. VP of Learning Development and Documentation Julie McGoldrick built this over years, one deliberate decision at a time. Pull it apart and you get a blueprint any L&D leader can adapt, regardless of industry:


### Fund the transformation before you ask for a bigger budget


Julie moved customer training online first, which unlocked new revenue that helped fund the rest of the platform from day one. If you’re trying to get executive buy-in for a broader shift, look for the piece of your program that can pay for itself first.


### Use branching to stop treating every learner like an average


Insurity built role-based learning paths so people find the right content faster, which also cuts the admin time your team used to spend on manual assignments. Personalization isn’t just a nicer learner experience. It’s a tax cut on your own team’s time.


### Automate compliance instead of chasing it


By triggering compliance training automatically through the SSO process, Insurity was able to get to 100% enrollment with zero manual tracking. If your team is still manually confirming who completed what, that’s hours you could get back.


### **Build for a distributed, multi-region workforce from the start**


Certification tracking by region let Insurity handle recertification across different states and countries without it turning into a spreadsheet nightmare. Compliance at scale means designing for your most complicated jurisdiction, not your simplest one.


### Don’t build everything yourself


Insurity integrated third-party content from providers like LinkedIn Learning, Cloud Guru, Pluralsight, and Kaplan, which expanded what employees could access without expanding the content development budget. A unified platform doesn’t mean a platform where you author everything.


### Let the same infrastructure serve learners you don’t employ


Onboarding and technical certification for customers, mandatory product certification for partners: none of it needed a separate system. It needed the same one, opened up to different audiences.


### Treat certification as a product, not a formality


Insurity is rolling out a self-serve paid certification program for partners. That’s the natural endpoint of unification: once your learning data and delivery engine are solid, certification becomes something you can package and sell, not just something you require.


### Bring AI in as a coach, not just a content engine


Insurity adopted AI-focused office hours and Docebo’s AI Coach for personalized coaching, which is a good reminder that AI readiness isn’t only about generating content faster. It’s about giving learners a way to ask questions and get unstuck in the moment.


None of these moves depend on being an insurance company. They depend on treating your learning platform as infrastructure for growth, not a line item for compliance.


## Bottom line


If you’re evaluating your own learning infrastructure, ask yourself the blunt version of the question: how many separate systems are your employees, customers, and partners learning on right now, and what would it take to put them on one?


Because the pattern is consistent across every example in this series so far. Fragmentation is inefficient and expensive.
