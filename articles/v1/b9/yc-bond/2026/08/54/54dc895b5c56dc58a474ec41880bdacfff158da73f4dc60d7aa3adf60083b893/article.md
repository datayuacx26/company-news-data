# Chief of Staff vs. Executive Assistant: Which One Do You Actually Need?

### The Core Difference, in One Paragraph


An Executive Assistant manages your time, your inbox, and the logistics of your day: the calendar, the travel, the "did that follow-up actually go out" of it all. A Chief of Staff extends your judgment across the company: driving cross-functional priorities to completion, with real authority to make calls on your behalf. One protects your day. The other extends your reach. Eventually, plenty of founders end up needing both. When deciding between the two, however, a few differences are important to note.

### Do You Need an EA, a Chief of Staff, Both, or Neither Yet?


#### Hire an EA first if…


Your bottleneck is time and logistics, full stop. Your calendar looks like a losing game of Tetris, your inbox is a crime scene, follow-ups get promised into the void, and nobody owns travel, scheduling, or the fifty small admin tasks quietly eating your week alive. This is almost always the first hire, at almost any stage, because it fixes the most immediate leak in your day before anything else has a chance to matter.


#### Hire a Chief of Staff if…


Everything downstream of your calendar is falling apart. Initiatives stall because nobody owns pushing them forward. Board and investor prep eats a disproportionate chunk of your week. Multiple senior leaders all need coordination that isn't naturally anyone's job, and things quietly fall through the cracks between teams until they're already on fire.


#### Hire both if…


Your logistics and your cross-functional execution are both collapsing at the same time. The fix is two different hires, as a single hire stretched across this usually ends up mediocre at logistics and underpowered on judgment. Not a great combo.


#### Hire neither, yet, if…


You're pre-product-market-fit, your team's still small enough to run through you directly, or your real problem is something no amount of hiring fixes: missing functional leader, or priorities so undefined there's nothing to actually delegate. Hiring either here just adds a layer of coordination you don't need.


## Frequently Asked Questions


##### **Q: Can one person do both jobs?**


**A:** At a small enough company, sure. Some early hires blend calendar work with lighter project ownership. But the judgment and cross-functional trust real Chief of Staff work requires gets hard to sustain alongside full logistics ownership, which is why the two usually split as things grow.


##### **Q: Does a Chief of Staff replace the need for an EA?**


**A:** No, and this is a common mix-up. Most CEOs with a Chief of Staff still need someone dedicated to time and logistics. The two coexist far more often than one replaces the other.


##### **Q: Can an Executive Assistant become a Chief of Staff?**


**A:** Yes, and it's a pretty natural path for an EA who's shown real judgment and started taking on genuine project ownership beyond the calendar.


##### **Q: Which role should report to the other?**


**A:** Both usually report straight to you, with a dotted line between them. The EA takes day-to-day sequencing direction from the Chief of Staff so your time reflects real priorities, without ever losing their direct line to you.
