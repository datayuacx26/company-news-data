# What Does a Chief of Staff Actually Do? (2026 Guide)

### What is a Chief of Staff, in plain terms?


If you're a founder or CEO trying to decide whether to hire one, the job is easiest to understand this way: a Chief of Staff extends your judgment and your capacity. They sit close enough to your priorities to translate them into execution without you having to spell out every step, they protect your time and attention from the dozens of things competing for it, and they act as a trusted right hand who can operate across the whole company rather than owning one function.


Most explanations of this role are written for the person doing the job: what skills to build, what it's like as a career step. But for those of you who are CEOs trying to decide whether hiring a Chief of Staff would actually help you, this guide is written from your side of the desk.


### What does a Chief of Staff actually do for a CEO, day to day?


The scope of the role shifts with company stage and the CEO's own gaps, but in practice it breaks down into six recurring categories of work:


1.


**Making sure the CEO’s priorities don’t get lost in the day-to-day.**


A Chief of Staff briefs the CEO before meetings so they always walk in with the context they need. After meetings, they ensure decisions made or follow-ups promised actually get done afterward. They also constantly re-prioritize the CEO's calendar against what matters most that week, not just what's on the schedule, so they don’t need to worry about whether they are moving the needle forward and can simply execute.


2.


**Acting as connective tissue across teams.**


A Chief of Staff is often the only person besides the CEO with visibility into what every function is working on. They catch the things that fall between teams before they become fires.


3.


**Driving strategic projects to completion.**


CEOs generate more priorities than the org can execute on. A Chief of Staff picks up the ones that don't have a natural owner, such as a new pricing model, an operating model redesign, or an acquisition integration, and drives them from idea to done, reporting back to the CEO along the way.


4.


**Serving as a communication filter and honest broker.**


They triage what actually needs the CEO's attention versus what can be handled or delegated, and they're often the person who can tell the CEO an uncomfortable truth: that a plan isn't landing, or that a leader is struggling.


5.


**Preparing board and investor materials.**


Board decks and investor updates take real time to get right, and someone has to pull together the numbers, the narrative, and the story of how the company's actually doing. A Chief of Staff usually owns that process end to end, even if the CEO still writes the final version in their own words.


6.


**Owning the projects that don't have a home.** Every company ends up with important work that doesn't belong to any one team: a crisis that needs managing, a new market worth a look, a reorg that has to happen. A Chief of Staff often ends up owning these simply because their job is to work on whatever the CEO needs most right now, not to stay inside a fixed lane.


### What a Chief of Staff is not


The role gets confused with three adjacent roles founders often already have or are considering. The clearest way to separate them is by authority and scope.


**Role**


**Primary focus**


**How it differs from a Chief of Staff**


Executive Assistant


Calendar, travel, logistics, correspondence


An EA manages the CEO's time and administrative load. A Chief of Staff does that plus drives strategic projects and represents the CEO's priorities across the organization


COO


A specific function, usually operations, with direct authority


A COO owns a function and the people in it, with the authority to make and enforce decisions inside that function. A Chief of Staff has no direct authority anywhere and operates through influence and the CEO's mandate, working across every function rather than owning one.


VP of Operations


A fixed operating function (systems, processes, cross-team logistics)


A VP of Operations owns a defined function with a consistent scope quarter to quarter. A Chief of Staff's scope is set by whatever the CEO's priorities are in a given season.


### When does a founder or CEO actually need a Chief of Staff?


#### Signs you need one now:


-


You're spending most of the week in reactive mode, putting out fires instead of working on what actually matters most.


-


Strategic initiatives you've championed stall out because no one is dedicated to driving them forward.


-


Board and investor prep eats a disproportionate amount of your time every cycle.


-


No single person has visibility into cross-team commitments, so things fall through the cracks between functions.


-


You find yourself re-explaining the same context and priorities to different people because there's no one translating them consistently.


### Skills to look for in a great Chief of Staff


Hiring for this role is less about a checklist of hard skills and more about evaluating judgment under ambiguity. The strongest candidates tend to show:


-


**Judgment.** Can they tell what actually matters to the business without being told explicitly every time?


-


**Discretion.** Will they handle sensitive information, such as compensation, board dynamics, and personnel issues, with the right restraint?


-


**Strategic thinking.** Can they take a loosely defined problem and turn it into a plan, rather than needing the plan handed to them?


-


**Communication.** Can they represent the CEO's thinking accurately to other people, and tell the CEO things they might not want to hear?


-


**Follow-through.** Do things actually get done once they own them, without needing to be chased?


-


**Low ego about authority.** Are they comfortable getting things done through influence rather than title, since this role rarely comes with direct reports or formal power?


### Frequently asked questions


##### **Q: Does a Chief of Staff have direct reports?**


**A:** Usually not, at least not as a core part of the role. A Chief of Staff typically operates without direct authority over other teams, getting things done through the CEO's mandate and their own influence rather than a reporting line. Some Chiefs of Staff do pick up a small team over time if they take on a specific function, but that's an expansion of the role, not its default shape.


##### **Q: Is being a Chief of Staff a stepping stone to becoming a CEO or COO?**


**A:** It can be, and many Chiefs of Staff move on to senior operating or executive roles afterward, since the job exposes them to how a company is run end to end. That said, it's not automatic or guaranteed, and plenty of people stay in Chief of Staff roles for years because the breadth and proximity to decision-making is exactly what they want.


##### **Q: Can one person be both an Executive Assistant and a Chief of Staff?**


**A:** At a small enough company, yes. Some early hires blend both, handling calendar and logistics alongside strategic project work. As the company and the CEO's needs grow, though, these tend to split into two roles, since the depth of strategic work a true Chief of Staff takes on becomes hard to sustain alongside full administrative ownership.


##### **Q: How is the Chief of Staff role changing today?**


**A:** AI tools are increasingly absorbing the more mechanical parts of the job , which frees Chiefs of Staff to spend more of their time on judgment-heavy work like strategy and cross-functional problem-solving. Some founders are also using AI tools to cover parts of this function directly, particularly at a stage where a full Chief of Staff hire isn't yet justified.
