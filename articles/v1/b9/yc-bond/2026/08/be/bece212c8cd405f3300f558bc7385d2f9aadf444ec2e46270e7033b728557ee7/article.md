# Bond vs. Macro

Macro is an open-source workspace marketed as a single replacement for Notion, Slack, Superhuman, Linear, and many other staple tools.


Instead of working across your current stack, Macro rebuilds their core functions inside their own system, allowing them to create shared context across conversations, projects, customers, and tasks.


While the thesis is interesting, eliminating fragmentation by pulling all work onto one platform, the tradeoff is extreme.


To receive full benefits from Macro, an entire company would have to abandon their current tools, migrate existing information, and retrain themselves to understand a whole new system. Macro offers integrations and migration paths for external platforms, but the most amount of value can be extracted from significant behavior change and unwavering desire across an entire team or company to completely transition workspaces.


## Onboarding and Time to Value


The real onboarding question is not just how long it takes to create an account. Onboarding evaluations should also include the time it takes before a product becomes genuinely useful.


#### Macro’s approach


Macro’s vision is to become the central workspace where your company communicates, writes, manages projects, handles email, and coordinates work.


That means onboarding is not simply a matter of connecting a few integrations and exploring a new interface.


**To extract Macro’s full value, your entire team will eventually have to move the work, data, and habits built across existing tools into an entirely new workspace.**


Sure, every new platform comes with a learning curve that can oftentimes be tedious. But Macro’s goal is to completely replace the platforms you are currently used to. And until enough of a company’s work lives inside Macro, the platform cannot fully deliver on its promise of providing one unified source of context.


Macro does offer integrations, which can make the transition easier. But integrations are not the end state of its product. The greatest value is meant to come after your organization has consolidated its work inside Macro.


For teams already committed to replacing their software stack, that may be a worthwhile investment. For everyone else, it creates a long path between signing up and experiencing the product’s full value.


#### Bond’s approach


Bond takes a completely opposite approach.


Instead of asking your company to replace the tools, workflows, and habits it has already spent years building, Bond connects to the systems where your work already happens.


During onboarding, you connect tools such as your email, calendar, Slack, and meeting notetakers. Bond then uses the context already inside those systems to build an immediate understanding of your role, responsibilities, priorities, and outstanding commitments.


By the time onboarding is complete:


-


Your self-updating, prioritized to-do list is already populated.


-


Commitments from across your tools have been brought into one place.


-


Personalized routines and workflows have been built.


-


Bond has begun building context around how you work.


-


You are connected with the Bond team for ongoing Slack support.


You do not need to convince your company to migrate, your coworkers do not need to change their behavior, and you do not need to manually rebuild the systems you already use before Bond can become useful.


You can begin extracting value immediately.


From there, Bond becomes increasingly personalized. As it observes more of your work, feedback, priorities, and decisions, it develops a deeper understanding of what matters to you and how you operate. The product is useful on day one, but its value compounds even more over time.


## AI Chief of Staff Capabilities


While Chiefs of Staff are well-known for doing—well—a bit of everything, their most common responsibilities are often related to context management, prioritization, and making sure nothing important falls through the cracks.


A lot of products can answer questions, draft documents, and create tasks when prompted.


But truly strong ******[AI Chiefs of Staff](https://www.bondapp.io/insights/8-best-ai-executive-assistants-for-busy-leaders-in-2026) are able to proactively recognize what needs attention, surface it at the right moment, and take action without needing to be prompted.


#### Macro’s Approach


Macro has built a powerful AI agent that can read across email, messages, tasks, documents, and relevant data inside its workspace. Users can ask it questions, have it create documents or tasks, draft emails, update records, and run scheduled automations.


But Macro’s agent is useful when you actively engage it.


During our testing, we asked Macro whether it could proactively remind us to do something.


Its answer was direct:


**“I can’t proactively reach out to you on my own—I only respond when you message me.”**


We then asked whether it could autonomously send emails or briefings. Again, the answer was no.


Macro does have native notifications for assigned tasks, due dates, messages, mentions, and other activity inside its unified inbox. But that still requires the user to create the task, assign the deadline, and regularly check Macro for updates.


#### Bond’s Approach


Bond’s philosophy is grounded in action. It continuously monitors the tools where your work already happens, identifies new commitments, and updates your priorities as context changes.


Bond can proactively:


-


Remind you about commitments and overdue follow-ups.


-


Prepare briefings before upcoming meetings.


-


Surface tasks buried across email, Slack, calendars, and meeting notes.


-


Update your prioritized to-do list without waiting for you to maintain it.


-


Alert you when something important requires your attention.


You can also configure personalized routines around the way you work.


Bond can remind you to follow up after a meeting if you haven’t, send a weekly summary of team activity, and more depending on what your specific needs are.


And because Bond already understands the context across your existing tools, those reminders are not limited to tasks you manually entered. It can catch work you agreed to in an email, a Slack message, or the final thirty seconds of a meeting.


## Task Management


#### Macro’s Approach


Macro includes a full task-management system built directly beside its email, messaging, and documents.


Users can assign statuses, priorities, and owners, turn an email or message into a task, connect tasks to GitHub activity, and delegate certain tasks to AI agents. For engineering teams, Macro can automatically move a task into review when a pull request opens and close it when that pull request merges.


But again, the value depends heavily on the work happening inside Macro.


In other words, Macro gives your team a highly capable place to manage work, but your team is still responsible for making sure the work gets there.


For technical teams already committed to making Macro their central workspace, this can be incredibly useful. But for executives and Chiefs of Staff whose responsibilities appear unpredictably across inboxes, meetings, messages, and documents, it still leaves much to be desired.


#### Bond’s Approach


Bond takes a much more personal and proactive approach to task management.


Rather than giving you another empty task database to maintain, Bond builds your to-do list for you based on specific context it knows.


It pulls commitments from the places where they naturally appear: an email you promised to answer, an action item assigned during a meeting, a Slack message you need to follow up on, or a deadline buried inside a document.


Bond then brings those commitments into one master list, attaches the original context for you to scan through, and helps prioritize what deserves your attention first. The list continues updating as your work changes, so you do not have to spend every morning manually reconstructing your priorities.


For those who enjoy their to-do list in a specific format, Bond also offers multiple views, including Eisenhower, “get things done”, and Kanban, along with labels that automatically sort tasks by project, team, goal, or whatever else is currently taking over your life.


So while Bond can help decide what belongs on your list and keeps it updated for you, you still control how that work is organized and how you want to tackle it.


### Security


Both Bond and Macro take security seriously, so this is not a category where either product should be dismissed.


Bond is[SOC 2 Type II](https://www.probo.com/hub/soc2) certified. Customer data is encrypted in transit and at rest, is never sold, and is not used to train AI models. Users can disconnect integrations or delete their accounts, and associated data is removed from active systems within 30 days. Enterprise plans also offer SSO, SCIM, audit logs, and custom integrations.


Macro’s hosted product also maintains a SOC 2 Type II audit, and the company also states that it is ISO 27001 certified, enforces zero data retention with its model providers, and does not train on customer data. Because Macro is open source, technical teams can inspect its code or self-host it under its AGPL license.


#### Bottom Line


Macro may appeal to very technical teams that want to merge their entire workspace and are willing to spend time learning and configuring it on their own. But for fast-moving executives and busy Chiefs of Staff who simply want an accurate system that is easy to adopt and can immediately move the needle forward, Bond has the edge.
