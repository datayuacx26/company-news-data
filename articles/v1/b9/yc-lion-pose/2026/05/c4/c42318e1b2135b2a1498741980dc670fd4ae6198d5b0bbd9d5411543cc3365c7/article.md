## What is Eczema-Prone, Dry Skin?


Eczema-prone skin, also called[atopic dermatitis](https://www.niams.nih.gov/health-topics/atopic-dermatitis) in clinical settings, is a chronic inflammatory skin condition characterized by a compromised skin barrier, increased transepidermal water loss, and heightened sensitivity to environmental triggers. Dry skin, or xerosis, refers to insufficient moisture retention in the epidermis, often resulting in rough texture, flakiness, tightness, and visible irritation. ## Dermatologist Tips for Managing Dry, Eczema-Prone Skin


Dermatologists recommend a multi-pronged approach to managing eczema-prone, dry skin that combines effective moisturization with lifestyle modifications and barrier-supportive skincare routines.


**Apply Moisturizer to Damp Skin** : After cleansing, apply your barrier-repair moisturizer within three minutes to lock in moisture while the skin is still hydrated, maximizing absorption and efficacy.


**Use Gentle, Non-Foaming Cleansers** : Harsh surfactants strip the skin of natural lipids, exacerbating dryness and barrier dysfunction. Opt for creamy, hydrating cleansers that maintain the skin's pH balance.


**Avoid Hot Water and Long Showers** : Excessive heat and prolonged water exposure deplete the skin's natural oils, worsening dryness. Use lukewarm water and limit showers to 10 minutes or less.


**Reapply Moisturizer Throughout the Day** : For optimal hydration, reapply your barrier-repair cream after washing hands or whenever skin feels tight, particularly in low-humidity environments.


**Incorporate Barrier-Supportive Ingredients Nightly** : Nighttime is when skin undergoes the most repair, making it the ideal time to apply lipid-rich moisturizers with bio-peptides and ceramides.


**Patch Test New Products** : Even hypoallergenic products can trigger sensitivity in eczema-prone skin. Patch test new moisturizers on the inner forearm for 48 hours before full-face application.
