## What Is Azelaic Acid and How Does It Work?


Azelaic acid is a naturally occurring dicarboxylic acid with antibacterial, anti-inflammatory, and keratolytic properties that make it effective for treating acne, rosacea, and hyperpigmentation. Understanding the azelaic acid moa (mechanism of action) helps explain why this single ingredient addresses such diverse skin concerns.


At its core, azelaic acid works by inhibiting the growth of acne-causing bacteria on the skin's surface. This antibacterial action reduces the formation of new breakouts while its anti-inflammatory properties calm existing blemishes and redness. For those dealing with rosacea, this dual action makes azelaic acid particularly valuable—it addresses both the visible redness and the underlying inflammation driving flare-ups.


The ingredient also normalizes keratinization, meaning it helps prevent the buildup of dead skin cells that can clog pores and lead to comedones. This keratolytic effect keeps pores clear and contributes to a smoother skin texture over time.


Perhaps most notably for those concerned with uneven skin tone, azelaic acid inhibits tyrosinase—the enzyme responsible for melanin production. By interrupting this process, it gradually fades dark spots, post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation from acne, and melasma patches. This makes it a compelling alternative for anyone researching[how azelaic acid compares to hydroquinone](https://lionpose.com/blogs/news/ingredient-showdown-azelaic-acid-vs-hydroquinone) for brightening concerns.


In terms of azelaic acid drug class, it's classified as a topical antimicrobial and keratolytic agent. Unlike some prescription acne treatments, it doesn't increase sun sensitivity, making it suitable for year-round use without the photosensitivity concerns associated with retinoids or certain acids.


## Azelaic Acid Concentrations: 10% vs 15% vs 20%


The concentration of azelaic acid in a product determines both its potency and its accessibility, with azelaic acid 10 available over the counter while azelaic acid 15 and azelaic acid 20 typically require a prescription.


**Azelaic acid 10%** represents the highest concentration available without a prescription in most markets. Products at this level, like The Ordinary's Azelaic Acid Suspension, offer an accessible entry point for those new to the ingredient or with sensitive skin. This concentration effectively addresses mild to moderate acne, early hyperpigmentation, and general skin texture concerns. Most users tolerate daily application well, making it ideal for building a consistent routine.


**Azelaic acid 15%** is the standard prescription strength for rosacea treatment. Finacea azelaic acid gel and Skinoren azelaic acid cream both deliver this concentration and are frequently prescribed by dermatologists for managing papulopustular rosacea and moderate acne. The increased potency means faster results for stubborn concerns, though some users experience initial tingling or mild irritation as their skin adjusts. If you're considering prescription options, understanding the differences between finacea azelaic acid and skinoren azelaic acid formulations—Finacea is a gel while Skinoren is a cream—can help you choose based on your texture preferences and skin type.


**Azelaic acid 20%** represents the highest available concentration and is reserved for more severe cases of acne or hyperpigmentation that haven't responded adequately to lower strengths. This prescription-only option delivers the most aggressive treatment but also carries the highest potential for irritation. Dermatologists typically recommend this concentration only after patients have demonstrated tolerance to lower percentages.


For those seeking prescription-strength results without a doctor's visit, generic azelaic acid options have become more accessible through telehealth platforms, often at lower price points than brand-name prescriptions.

## How to Use Azelaic Acid in Your Skincare Routine


Azelaic acid integrates easily into most skincare routines and can be applied once or twice daily depending on your skin's tolerance and the product's concentration.


**Basic Application Guidelines**


Start by cleansing your skin thoroughly. Apply azelaic acid to dry skin—applying to damp skin can increase penetration and potentially cause irritation, especially when you're first introducing the ingredient. A pea-sized amount covers the entire face; there's no need to apply heavily.


For the recommended azelaic acid dose in your routine, begin with once-daily application in the evening for the first two weeks. If your skin tolerates this well without excessive dryness, redness, or irritation, you can increase to twice-daily use. Those using prescription-strength formulas should follow their dermatologist's specific guidance.


**Layering Order**


In a multi-step routine, azelaic acid typically goes after water-based serums but before heavier creams and oils. If you're using a serum-textured azelaic acid product, apply it after any hydrating essences or toners. Cream or gel formulas can serve as your moisturizer in simpler routines or go underneath a separate moisturizer if you need additional hydration.


**Morning vs. Evening Use**


Unlike retinoids, azelaic acid doesn't increase photosensitivity, making it suitable for morning application. Many users prefer applying it in the morning for its anti-inflammatory benefits throughout the day, followed by retinol or other actives in the evening. However, using it at night works equally well if that fits your routine better.


**Special Considerations**


One of azelaic acid's notable advantages is its safety profile during pregnancy. It's considered one of the few active ingredients safe for use while pregnant or breastfeeding, making it valuable for those who need to modify their skincare routine during this time. ## Frequently Asked Questions


### What does azelaic acid actually do for your skin?


Azelaic acid delivers antibacterial, anti-inflammatory, and brightening benefits that address acne, rosacea, and hyperpigmentation simultaneously. It kills acne-causing bacteria, reduces inflammation and redness, normalizes skin cell turnover to prevent clogged pores, and inhibits excess melanin production to fade dark spots. This multi-mechanism action makes it uniquely versatile among skincare actives.


### What percentage of azelaic acid is most effective — 10%, 15%, or 20%?


The most effective percentage depends on your skin concern and tolerance. Azelaic acid 10% works well for mild acne and maintenance, azelaic acid 15% (found in Finacea and Skinoren) is optimal for rosacea and moderate concerns, while azelaic acid 20% addresses severe or resistant conditions. Higher isn't always better—consistent use of a well-tolerated concentration produces better long-term results than sporadic use of a stronger formula that irritates your skin.


### Can you use azelaic acid every day?


Yes, azelaic acid can be used daily and even twice daily once your skin has adjusted to it. Start with once-daily application for the first two weeks to assess tolerance, then increase frequency if desired. Unlike many active ingredients, azelaic acid is gentle enough for daily use without causing significant irritation for most skin types.


### Is azelaic acid safe to use during pregnancy?


Azelaic acid is considered safe for use during pregnancy and breastfeeding, making it one of the few active ingredients that doesn't need to be discontinued during this time. It's classified as pregnancy category B, meaning animal studies have shown no risk and it's generally accepted as safe for topical use. However, always confirm with your healthcare provider before continuing or starting any skincare active during pregnancy.


### How does azelaic acid compare to vitamin C for dark spots and hyperpigmentation?


Both ingredients fade dark spots but work through different mechanisms. Azelaic acid inhibits tyrosinase to reduce melanin production and offers additional anti-inflammatory benefits, while vitamin C provides antioxidant protection and brightening through a different pathway. For stubborn hyperpigmentation, using both ingredients—vitamin C in the morning for antioxidant protection and azelaic acid in the evening—often produces superior results compared to using either alone.


### What should you not pair with azelaic acid?


Azelaic acid is compatible with most skincare ingredients, including retinoids, vitamin C, niacinamide, and hyaluronic acid. The main caution is avoiding layering it with other potentially irritating actives like strong chemical exfoliants (high-percentage glycolic or salicylic acid) in the same routine, especially when first introducing azelaic acid. If using multiple actives, alternate them between morning and evening applications or on different days until your skin demonstrates tolerance.


### What is the difference between prescription azelaic acid (Finacea, Skinoren) and generic or OTC versions?


Prescription azelaic acid products like Finacea and Skinoren contain 15-20% concentrations compared to the maximum 10% in OTC products. Finacea is a gel formula while Skinoren is a cream, offering different texture options. Generic azelaic acid prescriptions contain the same active ingredient at prescription strengths but typically cost less than brand-name versions. OTC products at 10% can be effective for milder concerns and maintenance but may not provide sufficient strength for diagnosed rosacea or stubborn hyperpigmentation.
