# Best Night Serums for Dark Spots and Hyperpigmentation (2026)

Dark spots fade fastest when your most active ingredients go on at night, while your skin is repairing and there's no sun to undo the work. Below is what actually fades pigment after dark, and the five night serums worth your bathroom shelf in 2026.

## Why should you treat dark spots at night?


Nighttime is the best window for dark-spot actives because the ingredients that fade pigment most effectively are either weakened by sunlight or make skin more sun-sensitive. Retinoids and alpha hydroxy acids (glycolic, lactic) both increase photosensitivity, so applying them after dark avoids daytime UV exposure that would irritate skin or degrade the ingredient. Skin also shifts into repair mode overnight — cell turnover and barrier recovery are more active during sleep — which helps push pigmented cells toward the surface where they can shed.


Layering pigment-fading actives at night also keeps your morning routine simple and protective: antioxidants and sunscreen by day, correction by night. This "treat at night, protect by day" split is the structure most dermatologists recommend for hyperpigmentation.


## What ingredients actually fade dark spots?


The ingredients proven to fade dark spots work by slowing melanin production and speeding up cell turnover. The American Academy of Dermatology names five topicals that can fade existing dark spots and, in some cases, slow melanin production: **azelaic acid, glycolic acid, kojic acid, a retinoid (retinol, tretinoin, adapalene, or tazarotene), and vitamin C** ([AAD](https://www.aad.org/public/everyday-care/skin-care-secrets/routine/fade-dark-spots) ).


Two more actives round out most modern dark-spot serums. **Tranexamic acid** interrupts the signaling pathway between UV exposure, inflammation, and pigment production, which makes it especially useful for melasma and stubborn brown patches. **Niacinamide** reduces the transfer of pigment to skin-surface cells and calms the inflammation that drives post-acne marks. The strongest formulas combine a melanin-blocker (tranexamic, azelaic, or kojic) with a resurfacing agent (an AHA or retinoid) so you're fading existing spots and preventing new ones at once.


## How long do night serums take to fade dark spots?


Most people see visible fading in about 8 to 12 weeks, but full clearing takes longer and depends on how deep the pigment sits. The AAD notes that "a spot that is a few shades darker than your natural skin color will usually fade within 6 to 12 months," while pigment that lies deeper in the skin "can take years" ([AAD](https://www.aad.org/public/everyday-care/skin-care-secrets/routine/fade-dark-spots) ). Consistency matters more than strength — nightly use over months beats an aggressive routine you abandon after a week of irritation.


Post-acne marks and recent sun spots tend to respond faster than melasma, which is hormonally driven and prone to returning. If a product burns or stings, the AAD advises stopping use, since irritation can actually trigger more pigment in the skin.

## Do you still need sunscreen if you treat dark spots at night?


Yes — daily sunscreen is non-negotiable, even if all your active ingredients go on at night. UV exposure is the single biggest driver of new dark spots and of existing ones darkening again, so without daytime protection you'll spend every night undoing the day's damage. The AAD recommends a broad-spectrum SPF 30 or higher, and specifically suggests a **tinted sunscreen with iron oxide** for hyperpigmentation, because iron oxides also block the visible light that can worsen melasma ([AAD](https://www.aad.org/public/everyday-care/skin-care-secrets/routine/fade-dark-spots) ). Think of your night serum as the treatment and your morning sunscreen as the thing that lets it work.


## Frequently Asked Questions


### Can I use a vitamin C serum and a dark-spot night serum together?


Yes, but most people get the best results using vitamin C in the morning and a pigment-correcting serum at night. Vitamin C is an antioxidant that pairs naturally with daytime sunscreen, while acids and retinoids belong in your evening routine. Splitting them across AM and PM reduces irritation and covers more of the pigment pathway.


### Are night serums safe for dark or deeper skin tones?


Night serums are safe for deeper skin tones, but gentle, consistent use matters more because irritation can trigger post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation. Choose formulas with tranexamic acid, azelaic acid, or niacinamide, introduce acids and retinoids slowly, and stop if a product stings. Daily sunscreen is essential to prevent spots from returning.


### Can I use a retinol and an acid serum on the same night?


Layering a retinoid and strong acids on the same night can over-exfoliate and irritate skin, which may worsen dark spots. It's safer to alternate nights, or choose one multi-active serum formulated to combine them at balanced levels. If you do combine, watch for redness, peeling, or stinging and scale back.


### How do I know if my dark spots are melasma or just sun spots?


Melasma usually appears as larger, symmetrical brown or gray-brown patches on the cheeks, forehead, or upper lip, often triggered by hormones and sun. Sun spots are smaller, more defined, and appear on the most sun-exposed areas. Melasma is more stubborn and prone to returning, so a dermatologist's input helps confirm the diagnosis.
