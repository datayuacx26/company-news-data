# SEO for a new website: A sequencing guide for your first 90 days

Starting SEO for a new website feels like being handed a 40-item to-do list and told not to break anything.


Luckily, you only have to do a few of the right things, in the right order. We'll walk you through exactly that, even if you're a complete beginner.


This guide treats SEO for a new website as a sequence: what to decide before you publish a single page, what to do in your first few weeks, and what can safely wait.


You'll also learn how to earn visibility inside AI answers like ChatGPT and Google's AI Overviews, where a fast-growing share of people now discover brands they've never heard of. ChatGPT alone reached 900 million weekly active users ([OpenAI](https://techcrunch.com/2026/02/27/chatgpt-reaches-900m-weekly-active-users) ) in early 2026.


## What SEO does for a new website


For a new website, SEO is less about how much work you do and more about the order you do it in, because early structural decisions get expensive to reverse once content and links pile on top of them.


What separates a new site that compounds from one that stalls is sequence. For example:


- Choosing your URL structure before you publish costs nothing. Changing it after 80 pages are indexed and linked means redirects, lost equity, and a week you'll never get back.
- Picking keywords you can realistically win before you write saves you from a library of well-crafted pages that never rank. The order is the strategy.


This matters more for a brand-new site than for an established one because you have no authority buffer to absorb mistakes. An established site can publish in the wrong order and still rank on domain strength. A site launched last month cannot.


SEO and AI search expert[Bill Widmer](https://www.linkedin.com/in/billwidmer/) often does SEO for new websites, and he puts it like this:


> “Doing SEO in the right order from the start can be the difference between an afternoon of work and months of headache. I’ve seen it firsthand. Follow the sequence and you avoid that.”


## Why SEO is important for a new website


SEO is how a brand-new website gets found by the people who don't yet know it exists, and starting early turns search into a compounding asset instead of a rescue project.


Search sends strangers who are actively looking for what you offer. And unlike paid ads, that traffic doesn't stop when you stop paying. A page that ranks keeps working for months or years after you publish it. That compounding result is why SEO is worth doing sooner rather than later.


For a new website specifically, two things make early SEO pay off.


1. It builds equity you can't buy overnight, since authority accrues slowly and the sites that start sooner are further ahead when a buyer finally searches.
2. It positions you for AI-driven discovery, because the same signals that help you rank also help AI systems find and cite you.


If you already have a few pages live, a quick[SEO audit](https://www.semrush.com/blog/seo-audit/) will tell you what to fix first.


## How search engines (and AI answers) work


Search engines find your pages by crawling, store them by indexing, and order them by ranking.


- [Crawling](https://www.semrush.com/blog/website-crawler/) is when a bot like Googlebot follows links to discover your pages
- [Indexing](https://www.semrush.com/blog/what-are-crawlability-and-indexability-of-a-website/#what-is-indexability) is when the search engine stores and understands those pages so they can be served later.
- [Ranking](https://www.semrush.com/blog/seo-ranking/) is when it decides, for a given search, which indexed pages appear and in what order


If a page isn't crawled, it can't be indexed. If it isn't indexed, it can't rank. This is why the very first thing to confirm on a new site is that search engines can reach and[index your pages](https://www.semrush.com/blog/google-index/) , not whether you've optimized a title tag.


AI systems now add a fourth step: generating a response.


Tools like ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Google's AI Overviews pull passages from sources they trust and synthesize an answer, sometimes citing you by name. Showing up in these answers is an extension of SEO, called[generative engine optimization (GEO)](https://www.semrush.com/blog/generative-engine-optimization/) or[answer engine optimization (AEO)](https://www.semrush.com/blog/answer-engine-optimization/) .


These AI tools draw on two sources of information, often blending them in a single answer:


1. **Their training data** , the large snapshot of text they learned from when they were built. This is their internal memory. It's powerful, but frozen in time: it doesn't include anything published after the model's training cutoff, and it doesn't reliably attribute where a fact came from.
2. **Live web results** , which they retrieve in real time to answer questions their training data can't. When a tool does this, it pulls passages from current pages and often cites the sources it used by name, which is where a new site can realistically show up.

### 1. Set up the technical essentials


Before anything else, make sure search engines can crawl and index your site, because no amount of keyword or content work matters if your pages can't be found.


It's what experienced practitioners check first, too. As[Isaac Church](https://www.linkedin.com/in/ibullen/) , Director at 3WH, puts it:


> "When our team kicks off SEO for a brand-new website, the first thing we check is indexing."


Start with the four accounts and files that give you eyes on your site.


1. Create a[robots.txt file](https://www.semrush.com/blog/beginners-guide-robots-txt/) to tell crawlers which pages to access and which to skip
2. Set up[Google Search Console (GSC)](https://www.semrush.com/blog/google-search-console/) to see how Google crawls, indexes, and ranks your pages
3. Submit an[XML sitemap](https://www.semrush.com/blog/xml-sitemap/) through GSC so search engines have a complete list of the pages you want indexed
4. Connect[Google Analytics 4 (GA4)](https://www.semrush.com/blog/google-analytics/) to track how visitors behave once they arrive


On a new site, GSC won't show much at first, and that's normal: it takes time for Google to gather data, so set it up early and let it fill in.


Once your accounts are live, run a technical crawl with[Site Audit](https://www.semrush.com/siteaudit/) , Semrush's technical SEO tool.


When the crawl finishes, head to the " **Issues** " tab, click the " **Category** " drop-down, and select " **Indexability** " to surface the technical problems that stop pages from being indexed, like noindex tags or blocked resources. Fix those first, since they gate everything else.


While you're here, confirm the site itself is[technically sound](https://www.semrush.com/blog/technical-seo/) :


- Serve every page over[HTTPS](https://www.semrush.com/blog/what-is-https/)
- Make sure the design is[mobile-friendly](https://www.semrush.com/blog/mobile-seo/) (Google indexes the mobile version of your site first)
- Ensure your site[loads fast](https://www.semrush.com/blog/page-speed/) (a Largest Contentful Paint under 2.5 seconds)


Site Audit flags these performance and page-experience issues alongside the indexability checks. See our[SEO audit guide](https://www.semrush.com/blog/seo-audit/) for the full step-by-step walkthrough.


### 2. Plan your site architecture and URLs


Decide how your pages are organized and how your URLs are structured before you publish. This is a decision that's easy today but difficult and time-consuming to reverse once content and links stack up.


[Site architecture](https://www.semrush.com/blog/website-structure/) is the map of how your pages relate: which pages are top-level, which nest beneath them, and how a visitor (or a crawler) moves between them.


Sketch the skeleton before you build. Most new sites need a homepage, an about page, core product or service pages, a contact page, and a blog, with room to expand each into subpages. That skeleton tells both users and search engines what your site is about and how its parts connect.


Decide your[URL slugs](https://www.semrush.com/blog/what-is-a-url-slug/) now, too. Will blog posts live at /blog/post-name or /articles/post-name? Will products sit under /category/product-name/ or at the root? Keep slugs short, descriptive, and keyword-focused. Avoid numbers and special characters.


Once a URL is indexed and other pages link to it, changing it means setting up redirects, and every redirect is a small tax on equity and a new chance to introduce errors. Get it right the first time and you never pay that tax.


### 3. Find keywords and prompts you can win


[Keyword research](https://www.semrush.com/blog/keyword-research/) is how you learn what your audience types into search, so you can build pages around terms worth ranking for. For a new site, the goal isn't the highest-volume keywords, it's the ones you can realistically rank for and that lead to revenue.


Use[Keyword Magic Tool](https://www.semrush.com/analytics/keywordmagic/) to find keyword ideas. Enter a seed keyword, your domain, and your target location. For each keyword you'll see its **search volume** (how often it's searched each month), **search intent** (informational, commercial, transactional, or navigational), and **Personal Keyword Difficulty** (PKD%), which estimates how hard it is for your specific site to rank.


On a brand-new domain, a good rule of thumb is to filter for a PKD% under 20 and look for terms with 100 or more monthly searches. Open the " **Questions** " filter to find long-tail queries, which are usually easier to win because they're more specific and less contested.


Use[Keyword Gap](https://www.semrush.com/analytics/keywordgap/) to compare your domain against your competitors and see the keywords they rank for but you don't. This is a fast way to find winnable targets you'd otherwise miss.


After you have your target keywords, find the prompts your buyers type into AI tools. Prompts are longer and more conversational than keywords (e.g., "what's the best CRM for a two-person real estate team?" rather than "best CRM").


Use the[Prompt Research](https://www.semrush.com/ai-seo/prompt-research/) tool to find potential prompts. Enter a topic, then click the “ **Prompts** ” tab to see real prompts and AI responses related to that topic.


Review all the prompts and keywords you’ve found, and use that data to[map your keywords to their target pages](https://www.semrush.com/blog/keyword-mapping/) . Choose commercial keywords for your key pages, and plan what content you need to create for any informational queries.


### 4. Create content people and AI systems want to cite


Content earns rankings and AI citations the same way: by being useful, organized into topics you cover in depth, and structured so a specific passage can be lifted out and cited.


Start with[topic clusters](https://www.semrush.com/blog/topic-clusters/) rather than one-off posts. A cluster is a pillar page on a broad topic plus supporting pages on its subtopics, all internally linked.


Topic clusters signal depth to search engines and give AI systems a coherent body of work to draw from, which is better for a new site than a scattering of unrelated articles. Pick one topic you can own and build it out before you move to the next.


Then, make each page worth citing. The single biggest differentiator for a new site is original input: your own data, tests, or first-hand experience. As[Carlos Silva](https://www.linkedin.com/in/carlos-silva-maldonado/) , Senior Content Writer at Semrush, puts it:


> "Conducting even small-scale experiments or surveys gives search engines a compelling reason to rank you above established competitors."


Real-world proof gives both Google and AI systems a reason to recommend you over a bigger competitor who only summarizes.


Next, tighten your[on-page SEO](https://www.semrush.com/blog/on-page-seo/) : the elements on each page that help search engines and AI systems understand it.


Make sure the page matches the search intent behind its target query, then handle the basics:


- **Title tags** : write a clear, specific title under about 550 pixels that includes the page's target keyword
- **Meta descriptions** : summarize the page in a sentence or two that earns the click (it won't directly move rankings, but it lifts click-through)
- **Headings** : use descriptive H2s and H3s that answer the real questions from your keyword and prompt research
- **Alt text** : describe each image in plain language for accessibility and context
- **Internal links** : link related pages to each other so readers and crawlers can move through your cluster


### 5. Build authority with backlinks and brand mentions


[Backlinks](https://www.semrush.com/blog/what-are-backlinks/) (links from other sites to yours) and[brand mentions](https://www.semrush.com/blog/brand-mentions/) (references to your business, linked or not) both tell search engines and AI systems that other people take you seriously.


You need to publish citable content before you worry about links. Once your core pages exist, here are the[best ways to build links](https://www.semrush.com/blog/link-building/) to a new site:


- Publish original research that others want to reference (the surveys and experiments we mentioned in the last section)
- Write[guest posts](https://www.semrush.com/blog/guest-posts/) for relevant blogs in your niche
- Claim your business in directories and citation sources like[Google Business Profile](https://www.semrush.com/blog/google-my-business/) (keeping your name, address, and phone number identical everywhere)
- Participate authentically in communities like Reddit and other niche forums
- Implement[digital PR](https://www.semrush.com/blog/digital-pr/) , earning mentions on trusted publications
- Add relevant internal links between your pages


Use the[Backlink Gap](https://www.semrush.com/analytics/gap/backlinks/) tool to compare your backlink profile against competitors and find backlink opportunities. Enter your domain and up to four competitors to see which sites link to them but not to you.


### 6. Show up in ChatGPT, Perplexity, and AI Overviews


A new site earns[AI visibility](https://www.semrush.com/blog/ai-visibility/) by being unmistakably clear about who you are and what you do, structuring content so passages are easy to extract, and showing up in the sources AI systems already trust.


This is a different job from ranking, even though the foundations overlap. Ranking is about pages; AI citation is about passages and entities.


Here are four things that help a new site show up in AI systems:


1. **Entity clarity** : state plainly what your brand is, what it does, and who it's for
2. **Extractable structure** : use short, self-contained sections with descriptive headings that answer one question each
3. **Consistent naming** : use the same brand name, product names, and terminology everywhere
4. **Presence in sources AI trusts** : get mentioned on the review sites, directories, and community threads that models pull from when they answer questions in your category


It’s important to measure your visibility to understand whether what you’re doing is working. Use[AI Visibility Toolkit](https://www.semrush.com/ai-seo/overview/) to see if and how you appear across ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, and Google AI Overviews, how that compares to competitors, and the sentiment attached to your brand.

## Common beginner mistakes to avoid


The most common SEO mistakes on a new site come from skipping the sequence, so the fixes below are less about effort and more about doing things in the right order.


The most common ones to watch for:


- **Publishing before confirming indexing** : If Google can't index your pages, nothing else matters. Check first.
- **Choosing URLs and structure after launch** : The single most expensive reversal on a new site. Decide before you publish.
- **Chasing high-volume keywords you can't win** : Volume feels like progress; winnable, revenue-relevant terms deliver it.
- **Building links before you have citable content** : Authority-building works only when there's something worth linking to.
- **Ignoring search intent** : A page that doesn't match what the searcher wants won't rank no matter how polished it is.
- **Over-optimizing** : Forcing keywords into every line hurts more than it helps today.
- **Skipping measurement** : Without Search Console and analytics set up early, you're guessing about what's working.


For more on the fundamentals behind these fixes, see our[beginner SEO tutorial](https://www.semrush.com/blog/seo-tutorial/) .


## What to expect: timeline and early signals


Set expectations by separating two clocks: indexing is fast (days to a few weeks), but ranking is slow (usually four to 12 months before meaningful movement), so early wins show up as visibility signals long before traffic does.


Indexing happens first. Once you submit your[sitemap](https://www.semrush.com/blog/website-sitemap/) and Google crawls your site, pages typically start appearing in the index within days to a few weeks. You can confirm this in Google Search Console by going to the “ **Pages** ” report under Indexing.


Ranking is slower. For a new site with little authority, it commonly takes[four to 12 months to see meaningful ranking movement](https://www.semrush.com/blog/how-long-does-seo-take/) , and competitive terms can take longer.


Watch the early signals so you know you're on track before traffic arrives. Across your first 90 days, they tend to show up in this order:


- Pages moving from "discovered" to "indexed" in Search Console
- First impressions on long-tail queries
- A handful of low-competition keywords entering the top 50
- Your first AI mentions


Use the[Position Tracking](https://www.semrush.com/position-tracking/) tool to measure your rankings for target keywords daily. It also tracks ChatGPT Search and Google AI Mode, so you can watch both surfaces with one tool.


## Frequently asked questions


### Is SEO dead, or just evolving?


SEO isn't dead, it's expanding. The mechanics of being found are shifting from ten blue links toward AI answers, but the core job (being crawlable, relevant, and trustworthy) is exactly what earns visibility in both places. For a new site, the fundamentals matter more than ever, because they're now the price of entry to AI answers as well as search results.


### How much does SEO cost for a new website?


SEO cost for a new website ranges widely based on which route you take. Doing it yourself costs little more than an SEO tool subscription plus your time. Hiring a freelancer or agency costs more and buys speed and expertise, with prices driven by scope, competitiveness of your niche, and how much content you need. A brand-new site can start effectively on the DIY end and add help as it grows.


### Can you teach yourself SEO, and is SEO hard to learn?


Yes, you can[teach yourself SEO](https://www.semrush.com/blog/can-you-teach-yourself-seo/) , and no, SEO is not hard to learn at the basic level. The core concepts (crawling, indexing, keywords, content, links) are approachable, and a motivated beginner can run competent SEO on a new site within a few weeks. Mastery takes longer and comes from doing, but you do not need it to start.


### Do small business owners need an agency, or can they DIY?


Most small business owners can[DIY SEO](https://www.semrush.com/blog/diy-seo/) for a new site, at least at first. The early work (technical setup, keyword research, a content cluster, basic authority-building) is very learnable, and tools handle much of the heavy lifting. Consider an agency when SEO becomes a meaningful revenue channel and your time is better spent elsewhere, or when you're in a highly competitive niche.


### How do startups approach SEO when launching a new SaaS product site?


Startups launching a SaaS site should sequence SEO around their product from day one: lock architecture and URLs before launch, target bottom-of-funnel product and comparison keywords first (where intent and revenue are highest), then expand into top-of-funnel education through a blog. The advantage of[SEO for startups](https://www.semrush.com/blog/seo-for-startups/) is speed of iteration; the risk is publishing fast in a structure they'll have to unwind later.


### Why does SEO matter from day one?


SEO matters from day one because the cheapest, highest-impact decisions all happen before you have traffic. Architecture, URLs, and a clear content plan cost nothing to get right early and a great deal to fix later, and authority compounds, so the site that starts sooner is always ahead. Waiting doesn't make SEO easier, it makes the same work more expensive.
