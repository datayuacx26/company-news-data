# Traffic Is Down—Now What? Marketing Success In the Age of AI

For decades, the SEO playbook was simple: create great content, optimize for search, and watch the traffic roll in. A lot of successful businesses were built on that playbook — Semrush included.


But creating great content is no longer enough, and with fewer clicks coming from search, focusing on SEO alone doesn’t deliver the same predictable return on investment (ROI) it once did.


Google now answers a growing share of queries directly inside AI Overviews, and platforms like ChatGPT and Claude field the questions people used to type into a search bar.


Neither sends much traffic back to your site — and both tend to cite big, established brands over smaller ones, making visibility harder to earn no matter which platform someone searches on.


So, how do you build a successful online business when traffic sources are drying up?


Before we share our advice, let's revisit the pre-AI world of SEO and explore why we should do marketing with more intention.


## SEO was a surefire marketing activity


For nearly two decades, you could build a thriving online business by making Google's algorithm happy. If you did technical optimizations, targeted the right keywords, and built[backlinks](https://www.semrush.com/blog/what-are-backlinks/) , traffic would flow to your site.


Classic marketing skills like brand building, promotion, and creativity weren't necessary for success in practice.


But now, growing visibility across the web requires exactly those skills:


- **Brand building** : Your brand needs to be popular enough that it naturally gets mentioned across the web when conversations about your industry are happening
- **Promotion** : You need to actively distribute and amplify your content through email, social media, and influencer partnerships (rather than just relying on Google traffic)
- **Creativity** : You need to create content and campaigns that stand out and get people talking — the kind of work that's genuinely interesting or valuable, not just optimized
- **Willingness to try new ideas** : You need to be open to testing new strategies (even if they’re not yet proven). For example, we recently[tested query fan-out optimization](https://www.semrush.com/blog/query-fan-out-experiment/) to see if it helps improve our AI visibility.


If you embrace these foundational marketing skills, your business can thrive.

You’ll see that these are mostly big brands. For smaller businesses, this creates a chicken-and-egg problem. You need authority to get cited in AI answers, but you need to be more visible and recognized in general to convince AI you’re an authority.

## How to build a brand & grow your business


To build a successful brand and grow your business, you need to show up where your audience already spends their time and add value to their lives. Here are seven tactics that you can use to start brand building:


### 1. Create sticky content


Creating sticky content keeps visitors engaged and makes them more likely to share, which builds your visibility over time.


To make your content sticky:


- Lead with a strong point of view or original data so readers remember your company, not just the topic
- Publish a recurring series or format (a weekly roundup, an annual report) that gives people a reason to return
- Show the people behind your brand — bylines, behind-the-scenes context, real voice — so readers feel connected to a team, not a faceless site
- Build in a genuinely useful or surprising element (a free tool, a bold take, a counterintuitive stat) that people want to share unprompted


Take Growth.Design’s content as an example. They create interactive stories that teach design and growth principles through engaging visual narratives.


Their content combines beautiful illustrations, storytelling, and practical lessons in a format you won't find anywhere else. Here's one of their[interactive case](https://growth.design/case-studies/tiktok-feed-psychology) studies you can check out.


Growth.Design gets a lot of praise for their unique approach. Here's what Peep Laja, founder of CXL,[says](https://growth.design/courses/product-psychology) :


“Many sites are in a sea of sameness. But Growth.Design’s content is just so different. Serious effort goes in there. It’s clearly hard to produce. Most people aren’t ready to put in that kind of work and resources.”


This approach has helped Growth.Design establish their brand presence in the design education space.


### 2. Launch PR campaigns


Regularly launching PR campaigns builds your brand's credibility by getting it covered in outlets your audience already trusts.


That coverage does double duty: It puts your brand in front of a publication's readers, and it creates the kind of authoritative, third-party mentions that search engines and AI platforms use to judge whether your brand is worth citing.


In early 2025, Duolingo announced on social media that their mascot Duo had died. While obviously a stunt, the announcement went viral on TikTok and generated millions of views across social platforms.


Following the viral social media buzz, media outlets like CBS News, NPR, and USA Today also started covering the news.


This campaign reinforced Duolingo's playful brand personality and kept them top of mind for language learners. It worked because it was unexpected yet perfectly aligned with their brand voice.


But you don't need elaborate stunts to make PR work. You can also get press coverage through brand collaborations.


In 2025, Perplexity partnered with Airtel to give its users in India a free one-year Perplexity Pro subscription. This single move generated coverage across media publications like[The Economic Times](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/industry/telecom/telecom-news/airtel-offers-perplexity-pro-subscription-worth-17000-/-year-for-free-to-all-users-how-to-avail-it/articleshow/122613306.cms?from=mdr) .


### 3. Invest in digital ads


Investing in digital advertising (running ads on platforms like Google, Meta, and ChatGPT) helps you reach more people in your audience.


Here's what makes digital advertising so effective:


- **Instant exposure** : Digital ads appear prominently across advertising platforms
- **Laser-focused targeting** : Digital allows precise audience targeting based on demographics, interests, and behaviors
- **Real-time insights** : You can see exactly how your campaigns are doing and tweak them to boost performance


Many successful companies find their best-performing content and promote it through paid ads.


For example, Grammarly promoted its workplace productivity guide through LinkedIn ads. This positions them as experts in the business productivity tools space, while also putting their brand in front of a highly relevant audience.


Notion does something similar. When creators post videos about how they organize their work in Notion, the company amplifies those videos through paid promotion.

### 4. Post consistently on social media


Posting consistently on social media strengthens your brand because a steady presence builds familiarity, and familiarity is what earns trust over time.


And that familiarity extends beyond your followers — social conversations, shares, and mentions all contribute to the broader signals that search engines and AI platforms reference when evaluating who to trust and surface.


Here's what works based on our experience:


- **Support your colleagues’ posts** : Like, comment on, and reshare posts from your teammates about the company and industry. It’s one of the easiest ways to increase reach for your brand.
- **Engage in conversations** : Don’t just post; interact. Leaving thoughtful comments on relevant discussions draws people back to your profile and, by extension, your brand.
- **Use visual content** : Text posts can work, but experiment with image and video posts. Create branded visuals, short explainer clips, or even memes (when relevant) to capture attention.
- **Use hashtags strategically** : Adding two to three highly relevant hashtags helps more people discover your posts
- **Collaborate with industry voices** : Work with people who already have the audience you want to reach. This could be industry experts, popular influencers, or even customers who love your product.

### 5. Create merchandise


Creating merchandise gets your brand noticed in the real world in ways your website and social posts can't.


And that real-world exposure often loops back online — someone who spots your logo on a hoodie in public may later search your brand by name.


Merchandise works especially well for growing businesses because it can help:


- **Boost brand visibility** : When your logo is on an item people actually use — like hoodies, water bottles, or tote bags — it turns them into brand ambassadors everywhere they go
- **Build positive associations** : Giving away quality merchandise feels like a gift, and customers often share that experience with friends or on social media. That extends your brand’s reach far beyond the original recipient.
- **Bring your digital brand into the physical world** : For SaaS companies and online-first businesses, merch makes the brand feel more real


The goal is to create something that’s useful, stylish, or fun enough that people want to keep it.


For example, if you have software engineers on your team, you've probably seen GitHub's Copilot stickers on their laptops and water bottles. Developers love these.


GitHub also creates hoodies and t-shirts that developers actually wear with pride. It helps them signal that they belong to the coding community.


If you're ready to create branded merchandise, companies like[Printful](https://www.printful.com/) and[CustomInk](https://www.customink.com/) make it easy to design and distribute quality products without managing inventory yourself.


Both platforms handle production and shipping, letting you focus on building your brand instead of managing logistics.


### 6. Sponsor events (or organize your own)


Sponsoring events — or running your own — boosts brand visibility by putting your company in the room with people who already care about your industry.


Event sponsorships also tend to generate backlinks, press mentions, and social posts from attendees and organizers — the kind of third-party signals that strengthen your visibility well beyond the event itself.


Sponsorship delivers on two fronts:


- **Borrow authority from the event** : When you sponsor a well-established conference or trade show, your brand benefits from the credibility of that event. Attendees naturally associate your company with the event’s reputation.
- **Reach a highly targeted audience** : Conferences and niche gatherings bring together people who are already invested in your industry, making it much easier to connect with high-intent prospects


Through sponsorship arrangements, companies typically secure brand placement on event materials like signage, promotional items, digital displays, or stage backdrops. Top sponsors might also receive exhibition spaces in high-traffic areas or speaking opportunities.


But you don't always have to rely on other people's events. Running your own events can be just as powerful.


For instance, we host an annual conference called[Spotlight](https://www.spotlightconf.com/) , where digital marketing professionals share insights about search, content marketing, and more. This in-person event allows us to deeply engage with our audience and reinforce our position as a leader in the digital marketing space.


### 7. Develop and share your brand story


Developing and sharing your brand story — the "why" behind what you do — helps people connect with your business on a human level.


But having a compelling story isn't enough. You need to actively share it across multiple touchpoints to build brand recognition. That consistent story, repeated across every touchpoint, is what helps users and search systems recognize who you are and what you do.


Here's how you can do this:


- **On your website** : Create an "About Us" page that explains your mission, values, the problem you’re solving, and what makes your approach different — written in a way that feels authentic rather than corporate
- **Through founder visibility** : Get founders to share their journeys on LinkedIn, podcasts, or at industry events
- **In content marketing** : Weave elements of your story into blogs, newsletters, videos, and case studies so it's reinforced over time
- **Inside your product experience** : Reflect your brand personality in onboarding flows, UX copy, micro-interactions, and feature announcements
- **During customer interactions** : Train your team to communicate your brand values in support conversations and sales calls


For example, Notion’s team consistently shares their story of building a tool that adapts to how people actually think and work — not forcing them into rigid structures.


This narrative appears everywhere: in their marketing copy, product demos,[founder interviews](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=q3cBTR4gKVc) , and community discussions. It reinforces why Notion feels different from traditional project management tools.


The lesson isn't just to have a good story — it's to make that story a consistent part of how you communicate your brand.


## Get started with brand building


Building a brand is the foundation for driving sustainable growth and visibility online.


This list of brand-building techniques is by no means exhaustive. There are dozens of other tactics we didn’t cover here, but we wanted to share some ideas you can get started with today.


So, pick a few tactics that best align with your resources and implement them right away. Later, scale your efforts and layer in other ideas as you gain momentum.


You’ll find that as your brand becomes stronger, traffic follows. That’s the real power of having a brand.
