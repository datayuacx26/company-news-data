## **Key questions about procurement software**


### **What is procurement software?**


Procurement software helps businesses manage the process of purchasing goods and services—from the initial request through approval, ordering, receiving, and payment. Instead of handling purchase requests, approvals, and vendor communications across email and spreadsheets, procurement software brings these workflows into a single platform with controls and visibility.


Most procurement platforms include some combination of purchase request management, purchase order creation, approval routing, vendor management, invoice matching, and spend reporting. Some platforms also integrate with accounts payable to connect the procurement process to payment execution.


### **How does procurement software work?**


1. **Purchase request.** An employee submits a purchase request through the platform, specifying what they need, the estimated cost, and the vendor. Some platforms auto-populate request fields using AI.
2. **Approval routing.** The request is routed to the appropriate approvers based on configurable rules—amount thresholds, department, budget, or vendor type. Approvers can review and approve from desktop or mobile.
3. **Purchase order.** Once approved, the request converts into a purchase order that is sent directly to the vendor. The PO captures agreed pricing, quantities, and delivery terms.
4. **Matching and payment.** When the invoice arrives, the platform matches it against the original PO (and receiving records, if applicable) to verify accuracy before routing for payment.


### **What features should you look for in procurement software?**


- **Purchase request and PO management.** The ability to create, route, and track purchase requests and purchase orders with automated approval workflows.
- **Invoice matching.** 2-way matching (PO to invoice) or 3-way matching (PO, invoice, and receiving record) to catch discrepancies before payment.
- **Approval workflows.** Configurable routing based on amount, department, vendor, or budget, with mobile access for approvers.
- **Vendor management.** A centralized vendor directory, onboarding workflows, and the ability to track vendor performance and compliance.
- **Budget controls.** Real-time visibility into committed spend versus available budget, with alerts when purchases approach budget limits.
- **Accounting integrations.** Native sync with your accounting software (QuickBooks, NetSuite, Sage Intacct, Xero) to automate GL coding and reduce manual reconciliation.

## **Benefits of procurement software**


Procurement software delivers value beyond basic purchasing in several key areas:


**Spending control before commitment.** Pre-approval workflows ensure purchases are reviewed and approved before financial obligations are made—shifting spend control from reactive (catching overages after the fact) to proactive (preventing them before they happen). \[1\]\[21\]


**Reduced manual processing.** Automated approval routing, PO generation, and invoice matching eliminate manual handoffs between teams. Platforms with AI-powered features can further reduce data entry—BILL's Invoice Coding Agent, for example, reduces manual time by 20%. \[2\]\[4\]


**Fewer payment errors.** Matching purchase orders to invoices catches pricing discrepancies, quantity mismatches, and duplicate invoices before payment is sent. Configurable tolerance thresholds let you flag only the mismatches that matter. \[1\]\[2\]


**Vendor relationship visibility.** A centralized vendor directory with order history, payment status, and compliance data gives procurement teams a complete picture of each supplier relationship. Some platforms connect to vendor networks that let suppliers track POs and payments in real time. \[1\]\[21\]


## How to choose the right procurement software for your business


Factor How it affects your decision


**Purchase volume** Teams processing fewer than 50 POs per month may need only basic procurement controls, while higher-volume teams benefit from automation, AI matching, and batch processing.


**AP integration** If you want procurement connected to invoice processing and payment, look for platforms that include both—or verify that a separate AP tool integrates cleanly.


**Number of requestors** Compare how platforms charge for purchase requestors. Some include unlimited requestors; others charge per user or seat.


**Existing financial systems** Prioritize platforms that integrate with your accounting software or ERP. Native two-way sync avoids manual data re-entry at month-end.


**Budget and pricing model** Per-user pricing scales well for small teams; flat monthly plans may be more economical for larger groups. Factor in add-on costs for modules like AP, cards, or advanced features.


**Implementation timeline** Some platforms deliver value within weeks; enterprise solutions may require months of configuration and consulting. Match the timeline to your team's urgency.
