## Key questions about expense management software


These sections provide more information about what expense management software is, how it works, what to look for, and what it typically costs.


### What is expense management software?


Expense management software helps businesses track, approve, and reimburse employee-initiated expenses through a centralized platform. Instead of managing receipts, approvals, and reimbursements across spreadsheets, email, and paper forms,[expense management software](https://www.bill.com/product/spend-and-expense) brings these workflows into a single system that connects to your accounting software.


Most[expense management platforms](https://www.bill.com/learning/expense-management) combine some or all of the following: corporate cards with spend controls, receipt capture and categorization, approval workflows, reimbursement processing, and integrations with accounting or ERP software. The scope varies—some platforms focus primarily on expense tracking and reporting, while others bundle corporate cards, budgets, and broader spend management into a single product.


### How does expense management software work?


1. **Expense capture:** Employees incur expenses and the platform captures transaction data automatically through corporate card feeds, receipt scanning (photo, email, or text), or manual entry. Some platforms match receipts to card transactions without manual intervention.
2. **Categorization and coding:** The software[categorizes each expense by typ](https://www.bill.com/blog/categorize-business-expenses) e, department, or project—often using AI to auto-populate fields based on merchant, amount, and transaction history.
3. **Approval and compliance:** Expense reports are routed to the appropriate approvers based on configurable rules. The platform flags out-of-policy items for review and maintains an audit trail for each transaction.
4. **Reconciliation and sync:** Approved expenses sync to the company's accounting software or ERP, with transactions coded to the correct general ledger accounts, cost centers, or departments.


### What features should you look for in expense management software?


- **Receipt capture and matching:**[Automated receipt scanning (OCR)](https://www.bill.com/blog/best-receipt-scanning-app) , email-forwarded receipt capture, and smart matching of receipts to card transactions to reduce manual expense reporting.
- **Corporate card options:** Whether the platform offers its own corporate cards, supports bring-your-own-card programs, or both—and what spend controls are available at the card level.
- **Policy enforcement:** The ability to set expense policies that are applied before or during a purchase—not just flagged after the fact. Look for controls by category, merchant, department, or role.
- **Approval workflows:** Configurable approval routing based on amount, department, or policy exception, with mobile access for managers who need to approve on the go.
- **Budget management:** Tools for setting and tracking budgets by department, project, or cost center, with real-time visibility into spend against those budgets.
- **Accounting integrations:**[Native sync with your accounting software](https://www.bill.com/integrations/explore) (QuickBooks, NetSuite, Sage Intacct, Xero) to automate GL coding and reduce manual reconciliation at month-end.


### How much does expense management software cost?


Expense management software pricing varies widely depending on the platform and what's included:

- **Enterprise and quote-based pricing** is common for larger organizations. SAP Concur does not publish pricing \[6\], and most providers offer custom enterprise plans with dedicated support and implementation services.


### Benefits of expense management software


Expense management software delivers value beyond basic receipt tracking in several key areas:


**Reduced manual work.** Automated receipt capture, transaction matching, and expense categorization can eliminate hours of manual data entry each month. Platforms that connect corporate card transactions directly to expense reports remove the need for employees to manually compile and submit expenses after the fact. \[1\]\[9\]


**Policy compliance without policing.** Configurable expense policies that are enforced at the point of purchase—or flagged automatically when expenses are submitted—reduce the need for admins to manually review every transaction. This shifts compliance from reactive (chasing violations after the fact) to proactive (preventing them before they happen). \[1\]\[3\]\[9\]


**Real-time visibility into spend.** Instead of waiting for expense reports to trickle in weeks after purchases are made,[expense management platforms give finance teams a live view](https://www.bill.com/blog/automate-expense-management) of card transactions and budget consumption as they happen. This supports faster decision-making and more accurate forecasting. \[1\]\[9\]\[13\]


**Faster reconciliation and close.** When corporate card charges and receipts are automatically matched and coded to the correct GL accounts, month-end close becomes significantly faster. Several platforms in this list integrate directly with accounting software to sync approved expenses without manual export. \[1\]\[7\]\[9\]


## How to choose the right expense management software for your business


Expense Platform Decision Factors Factor How it affects your decision


Expense volume Companies with high transaction volumes benefit more from platforms with AI-powered categorization, automated receipt matching, and real-time reporting. Teams with lower volumes may prioritize simplicity over automation.


Policy complexity If your expense policies vary by department, role, or spending category, look for platforms that enforce rules at the point of purchase rather than flagging violations after the fact.


Number of employees Compare per-user pricing carefully—costs range from $0 to $15/user/month and add up across a team. Some platforms charge only for users who submit expenses, not everyone on the account.


Existing tools If your team already uses corporate cards, an accounting system, or an HRIS platform, prioritize expense software that integrates with what you have rather than requiring you to switch.


Card needs Some platforms include their own corporate cards with built-in spend controls, while others support bring-your-own-card or rely on bank card feeds. Choose based on whether you want to consolidate or keep your current cards.


Admin resources Teams without a dedicated finance operations role should look for platforms that automate policy enforcement and reconciliation—so the system does the oversight work instead of adding headcount.
