# 10 Best Quote and Proposal Software Platforms for 2026 (Buyer's Guide)

Quote and proposal software is where deal velocity now lives. The average B2B buyer expects a personalized, e-signable, price-configured proposal delivered within hours of a discovery call not days. Teams that still send static Word documents attached to email lose deals to teams that send interactive proposals with live pricing, embedded video, and one-click signature.


The proposal-software market is projected to grow at roughly **11% CAGR through 2030** , according to industry analyst reports ([Gartner CPQ Software market reviews](https://www.gartner.com/reviews/market/cpq-software) ), driven by AI-generated content, deeper CPQ integrations, and collapse of boundary between proposal, contract, and e-signature. That growth has produced a crowded market dozens of platforms, overlapping feature sets, and pricing that ranges from $19/user/month at low end to enterprise contracts north of $100,000/year.


This guide covers ten platforms that consistently rank at top of buyer shortlists in 2026, how they differ, who each is best for, and a decision framework at end for narrowing down. It is written for sales, revenue-ops,[professional-services firm](https://www.finlens.app/accountants) , and agency buyers who actually have to pick one.


## What is quote and proposal software


Quote and proposal software is a category of sales-enablement platform that lets teams create, send, track, and close business proposals including quotes, statements of work, engagement letters, and contracts from a single interface. It replaces older workflow of drafting proposals in Word, converting to PDF, emailing an attachment, and chasing signatures manually.


The category overlaps with **CPQ (Configure-Price-Quote)** at enterprise end and with **contract lifecycle management (CLM)** at post-signature end, but core value delivered by every tool in this list is same: shorten time between "we'd like to see a proposal" and a signed agreement.


### Key features to look for


- **Templates and content library.** Reusable blocks pricing tables, scope-of-work sections, case studies so reps do not rewrite same proposal twice.
- **Configure-Price-Quote (CPQ).** Rule-based product configuration, discount approvals, and quote pricing that matches your actual price book.
- **E-signature.** Legally-binding signature capture, ideally without a separate DocuSign contract.
- **Real-time tracking and analytics.** Notification when prospect opens proposal, which section they spend time in, and whether they forwarded it internally.
- **CRM integration.** Bidirectional sync with Salesforce, HubSpot, or Pipedrive so quote data flows both ways without duplicate entry.
- **Interactive elements.** Embedded video, live pricing widgets, ROI calculators proposals that behave more like landing pages than PDFs.
- **Payment collection.** Take a deposit or first invoice at moment of signature, especially important for[Client Accounting Services](https://www.finlens.app/blogs/client-accounting-services) firms and agencies on retainer who then sync those invoices back into[QuickBooks](https://www.finlens.app/resources/quickbooks-automation) or Xero.


### Benefits for business


Teams using proposal software typically see **30–40% faster proposal turnaround** , higher close rates on shorter cycles, and materially better visibility into where deals stall. For services firms sending recurring engagement letters where[pricing tiers](https://www.finlens.app/blogs/bookkeeping-services-fees) and scope are updated annually recurring-proposal automation alone often pays for tool. Firms that pair proposal automation with clean monthly[reconciliation](https://www.finlens.app/blogs/reconciliation-in-accounting) recover margin end-to-end rather than losing it to manual bookkeeping downstream.


## How we evaluated platforms


The ten platforms below were selected on four dimensions: (1) breadth of features across templates, CPQ, e-sign, and analytics; (2) integration ecosystem, especially CRM and payment; (3) pricing transparency and value at small-to-mid segment; (4) verified user review volume on[G2's proposal software category](https://www.g2.com/categories/proposal) and Capterra with a 4.0+ average.


Enterprise-only platforms with custom-quoted pricing appear where they are meaningfully dominant in their segment. AI-generation features were considered but not required every platform in this list now offers some form of AI drafting.

## The 10 best quote and proposal software in 2026


### 1. PandaDoc


**Best for:** Sales, revenue ops, and services teams that want a single platform for proposals, contracts, and e-signature without stitching three vendors together.


PandaDoc is most broadly deployed platform in category and shows up on nearly every buyer shortlist for a reason it does templates, content library, CPQ, e-signature, payments, and analytics in one interface, and its Salesforce and HubSpot integrations are among most mature in market. The template library is deep enough that most teams do not need to build from scratch on day one.


Recent releases have leaned heavily into AI proposal drafting from a discovery-call transcript or from CRM opportunity data. Payment collection at signature is native (Stripe integration), which matters for agencies and CAS firms that want deposits on spot.


Where PandaDoc gets criticism: pricing tiers gate a lot of good features (approval workflows, advanced analytics, unlimited templates), and mid-tier plan is where most teams end up rather than entry plan. Occasional PDF rendering quirks on complex layouts.


**Pricing:** $35/user/mo Essentials, $65 Business, custom Enterprise · **Free trial:** 14 days.


### 2. Proposify


**Best for:** Agencies, consultancies, and creative shops where proposal itself is a design artifact and needs to look every bit as good as client's brand guidelines.


Proposify was built by an agency and it shows. The editor is closer to a design tool than a document editor precise control over typography, spacing, and image placement and template library is stronger on visual polish than on operational scale. For agencies that pitch on positioning and creative, Proposify frequently wins over PandaDoc for exactly that reason.


Content library, CPQ (called "Pricing tables"), e-signature, and CRM integrations are all present. Analytics are more focused on section-level engagement than on cross-team pipeline reporting.


Where Proposify is weaker: less depth on complex enterprise CPQ (multi-tier discount workflows, product bundling), and pricing has crept up in recent years, making it noticeably more expensive than Better Proposals for similar-size teams.


**Pricing:** $49/user/mo Team, $65 Business · **Free trial:** 14 days.


### 3. Better Proposals


**Best for:** Small agencies, freelancers, and consultants who want a low-friction proposal tool that is affordable and fast to learn.


Better Proposals is cost-effective option in category and is platform most new-to-proposal-software teams start with. The template library is large, editor is straightforward, and pricing tier is among lowest of any serious proposal tool at $19/user/mo. E-sign, live-chat inside proposal, and payment collection are included on lower tiers than at competitors.


Where Better Proposals gets thinner: CPQ is basic (fine for simple retainers or one-line-item pricing, less capable for multi-tier configuration), analytics are lighter than PandaDoc or GetAccept, and integrations are shallower than enterprise-oriented tools.


For a two-to-ten-person agency, Better Proposals is often right answer for years before outgrowing it.


**Pricing:** $19/user/mo Starter, $29 Premium, $49 Enterprise · **Free trial:** 14 days.


### 4. Qwilr


**Best for:** Modern sales teams that want their proposals to feel like interactive web pages, not documents.


Qwilr's differentiator is format. Proposals are rendered as responsive web pages embedded video, live pricing widgets, ROI calculators, interactive tables buyer can toggle rather than PDF-style documents. For technology, SaaS, and services companies selling to buyers who expect a modern digital experience, Qwilr materially raises perceived polish.


CPQ is capable (rule-based pricing, tiered options), e-sign is native, and analytics show which sections of page buyer spent time on. Salesforce and HubSpot integrations are solid.


The trade-off: some buyers still want a PDF they can print or email internally, and Qwilr's PDF export is functional but not always as clean as interactive version. For heavily-regulated industries (financial services, healthcare) some legal teams still push back on non-document proposals.


**Pricing:** $35/user/mo Business, $59 Enterprise · **Free trial:** 14 days.


### 5. GetAccept


**Best for:** Sales teams whose revenue-ops workflow lives inside Salesforce or HubSpot and who want proposal-to-signature velocity as their primary metric.


GetAccept is CRM-native option in category. The Salesforce and HubSpot integrations are among deepest available quote data, opportunity stages, and buyer-engagement signals flow both ways and platform treats "digital sales rooms" as a first-class concept, meaning proposals live inside a shared buyer-and-seller workspace rather than as static documents.


Features cover CPQ, e-signature, video personalization, and live chat inside proposal. Analytics on buyer engagement are among strongest in category, which is what CRM-heavy teams tend to care about most.


Weaker areas: pricing sits at higher end of mid-market, and platform is less compelling for teams that are not already deep in Salesforce or HubSpot.


**Pricing:** $49/user/mo Essentials, custom higher tiers · **Free trial:** 14 days.


### 6. Oneflow


**Best for:** Teams that want contract lifecycle management folded into proposal dynamic documents editable in real time after send.


Oneflow's unique angle is "dynamic contract." A proposal sent through Oneflow can be edited by either party after send counterparty can propose changes inline, negotiations happen in document itself, and final signed version is generated from current state rather than a PDF re-send. That workflow is meaningfully different from every other tool on this list.


Contract lifecycle features (post-signature amendments, renewals, storage) are stronger than at pure proposal tools. Integrations to Salesforce, HubSpot, and other CRMs are solid, and pricing tier is one of more approachable in category including a free tier that is genuinely usable for small teams.


Where Oneflow is thinner: "dynamic contract" model is powerful but new to many buyers and takes some internal education. Some CPQ workflows are less mature than at DealHub or Conga.


**Pricing:** Free (limited); paid from $19/user/mo Essentials, custom Business/Enterprise · **Free trial:** Yes.


### 7. DealHub


**Best for:** Revenue-ops teams that need full CPQ, deal desk, and contract management as one integrated stack.


DealHub is where category shades into full CPQ. It handles product configuration, pricing rules, discount approvals, deal desk workflows, contract management, and e-signature all with heavy Salesforce native integration. Teams selecting DealHub typically have complex pricing (usage-based, tiered, bundled) that lighter tools cannot model cleanly.


Reporting and analytics on deal velocity, discount leakage, and quote-to-cash cycle time are enterprise-grade. The trade-off is complexity: DealHub is not a tool a two-person sales team should buy, and implementation typically runs 6–12 weeks with a professional-services engagement.


Pricing is entirely custom, and buyers should expect DealHub to sit at higher end of platform stack cost.


**Pricing:** Custom (typically $50K+ ARR) · **Free trial:** Demo only.


### 8. QuoteWerks


**Best for:** SMB technology resellers, MSPs, IT distributors, and value-added resellers who need product-catalog-driven quoting.


QuoteWerks has been in category for longer than most tools on this list and dominates IT reseller and MSP segment specifically. Its integrations with distributor catalogs (Ingram Micro, Tech Data, D&H) mean a rep can pull live pricing and availability into a quote without leaving tool a workflow no other proposal tool replicates as cleanly.


CRM integrations, e-signature, and CPQ are all present, and platform runs both on-premises and in cloud, which some IT resellers still prefer for data-residency reasons. Interface is more utilitarian than Qwilr or Proposify this is a tool for operators, not designers.


Best fit: teams selling hardware, software licenses, or bundled IT services where product catalog volume matters more than proposal aesthetics.


**Pricing:** ~$199/user/mo (perpetual + subscription hybrid); volume discounts · **Free trial:** 30 days.


### 9. Salesforce Revenue Cloud


**Best for:** Enterprise Salesforce shops that want CPQ, billing, and revenue management as a native Salesforce application.


Salesforce Revenue Cloud (which absorbed former Salesforce CPQ product) is incumbent enterprise choice for organizations already committed to Salesforce platform. Native integration is total quote objects, price books, opportunity data, and forecasts share one data model and platform scales to genuinely large product catalogs and multi-tier discount structures.


E-signature is available through Salesforce Sign or integration to DocuSign. AI-assisted quote generation and revenue forecasting have expanded significantly with Einstein platform.


The trade-off: implementation is complex, per-user costs are among highest in category, and non-Salesforce customers see essentially no reason to pick it over PandaDoc or DealHub. This is a decision made by Salesforce shops, not by tools shopping category cold.


**Pricing:** Custom, typically from ~$75/user/mo for CPQ · **Free trial:** Demo only.


### 10. Conga CPQ


**Best for:** Large enterprises with complex product configuration, multi-entity pricing, and heavy compliance requirements.


Conga is other enterprise CPQ heavyweight alongside Salesforce Revenue Cloud. It handles same problem set complex product configuration, guided selling, deal desk, contract management with strong support for manufacturing, life sciences, and financial services verticals. Its document generation (Conga Composer) is a category unto itself for template-based document assembly at scale.


Where Conga wins over Salesforce Revenue Cloud: platform-agnostic CRM support (works with Salesforce, Microsoft Dynamics, and others equally well) and stronger document assembly features. Where it loses: it does not have native tightness of a same-vendor stack that Revenue Cloud gets inside Salesforce.


**Pricing:** Custom · **Free trial:** Demo only.


## Key features to weigh in your decision


Every platform in this list handles core "send a proposal and collect a signature" workflow. The differentiators sit in five areas:


- **CPQ depth.** Rule-based product configuration, discount approval workflows, and price-book integration are where enterprise tools separate from mid-market tools. If your product catalog has more than 200 SKUs or your pricing has multiple discount tiers, mid-market tools will hit their limits.
- **CRM integration depth.** "Integrates with Salesforce" spans a wide range. GetAccept, DealHub, and Salesforce Revenue Cloud have deep bidirectional sync; some others are effectively one-way at quote-created event.
- **Design flexibility.** Agency and services buyers weight this heavily; enterprise CPQ buyers rarely do.
- **Contract lifecycle features.** If you need to manage renewals, amendments, and post-signature revisions, Oneflow, DealHub, and Conga are stronger than pure proposal tools.
- **Payment capture at signature.** Native Stripe or ACH support at signature is a meaningful workflow reduction for services firms that use[QuickBooks](https://www.finlens.app/resources/quickbooks-automation) for invoicing and want deposits collected upfront.


## How to choose right platform


The right tool depends on team size, deal complexity, existing tech stack, and how much design polish proposal itself needs to carry.


Five questions to run through before shortlisting:


### **1. How complex is your pricing?**


If pricing is a single line item or a tiered retainer, any of mid-market tools will do. If you have multi-tier discounting, product bundles, and approval workflows, only CPQ-heavy platforms (DealHub, Salesforce Revenue Cloud, Conga) will not hit walls.


### **2. Where does your CRM live?**


Salesforce shops with big Salesforce spend should evaluate Revenue Cloud first. HubSpot shops should look at PandaDoc, GetAccept, and Proposify. Non-CRM teams have wider optionality.


### **3. Who at your company actually opens proposal?**


If answer is "a designer or a marketer approves layout before send," Proposify or Qwilr are worth premium. If answer is "rep sends what CRM generates," design flexibility matters less.


### **4. Do you need post-signature workflow?**


Renewals, amendments, quote-to-cash automation, and contract storage. If yes, Oneflow, DealHub, or Conga are stronger picks than pure proposal tools.


### **5. What is realistic budget per user per month?**


For a two-to-ten-person team, budget $19–$65 per user. For mid-market (10–50 users) plan on $35–$100 per user with negotiation on enterprise tier. For 50+ users with complex CPQ, expect custom pricing that starts around $75/user/mo and rises with implementation.


The wrong reason to pick a tool is because a specific competitor uses it. The right reason is that tool handles your top three workflow bottlenecks better than alternatives.

## FAQ


### **What is difference between proposal software and CPQ software?**


Proposal software is broader it covers document creation, e-signature, and buyer engagement. CPQ (Configure-Price-Quote) is a subset focused on rule-based product configuration and pricing. Enterprise tools like DealHub, Salesforce Revenue Cloud, and Conga combine both. Mid-market tools like PandaDoc, Proposify, and Better Proposals offer lighter CPQ inside a broader proposal platform.


### **Can proposal software collect payment at signature?**


Yes. PandaDoc, Proposify, Better Proposals, and GetAccept all support payment collection at signature through Stripe or similar processors. This is especially valuable for services firms wanting deposits or first-month retainers on spot.


### **How much does quote and proposal software cost?**


Entry pricing starts at ~$19/user/mo (Better Proposals) and rises to $35–$65/user/mo for mid-market (PandaDoc, Proposify, Qwilr, GetAccept). Enterprise CPQ (DealHub, Salesforce Revenue Cloud, Conga) is custom and typically starts around $75/user/mo with meaningful implementation costs on top.


### **Is proposal software worth it for a small team?**


Yes for any team sending more than 5–10 proposals per month. Even at entry tier of ~$19–$35/user/mo, time saved on template reuse, e-signature, and follow-up tracking pays back tool within first month for most teams.


### **What is ROI timeline for implementing proposal software?**


Most teams report proposal-turnaround time drops 30–40% in first 60 days, with measurable close-rate improvements in first 90 days. Total-cost payback typically lands under 90 days at mid-market tiers, longer for enterprise CPQ where implementation itself takes months.
