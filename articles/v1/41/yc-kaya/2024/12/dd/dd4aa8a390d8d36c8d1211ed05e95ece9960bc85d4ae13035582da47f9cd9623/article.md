## What are Google Ads, and why do companies need them?


Google Ads, formerly known as Google AdWords, is Google's pay-per-click (PPC) platform that allows businesses of all sizes to gain visibility across Google's properties.


There are a number of reasons why people choose Google Ads as their primary paid marketing channel. But the biggest reason for them is that this is the only platform where search intent matches a conversion opportunity.


Here’s an example to make the analogy easier: When someone searches for “best startup marketing agency in the US” or “Ad management services near me,” they’re not just browsing for leisure, they are actively looking to hire an agency. Google Ads increases the likelihood that your business shows up at decision-making moments like this, and connects you with customers ready to convert.


Here are some more benefits of using Google Ads as your performance marketing channel:


### **1. Precision targeting and control**


Google Ads allows you to tailor campaigns to reach specific audiences based on location, demographics, interests, and search behavior. As a result, you can target with more versatility. For example, you can target local customers with Google Local Service Ads or run Google Shopping Ads for an eCommerce-lookalike audience. Furthermore, Google Ads allow you full control over budgets, bids, and targeting, enabling you to optimize campaigns.


Smart campaigns rarely target an entire country. Instead, they zero in on top-performing cities, zip codes, or even a 5-mile radius around high-value stores. Then layer device, demographic, and in-market audiences on top. Tight geo and audience filters raise relevance scores, improve CTR, and stop budget from leaking on low-intent regions.


### **2. Scalable and measurable results**


You can easily scale Google Ads. It is understandable if you don’t want to invest much in your first paid campaign. Google Ads allows you to start with a minimal budget and go up from there as required. Besides this, the platform comes with analytics, which allows you to track every aspect of your performance. This simplifies justifying ad spend and optimizing campaigns with a data-backed approach.


### **3. Multi-format reach across platforms**


Google Ads isn’t limited to search results. The platform allows you to engage users across multiple touch points through its Display Network, YouTube Ads, and Shopping Ads. So whether you’re generating brand awareness with Video Ads or driving direct sales with Search and Shopping Ads, Google Ads makes sure you’re visible throughout the customer’s buying journey.

### How to make ads more effective with extensions


Google rewards ads that give searchers more choices. Sitelinks, callouts, call extensions, location pins, and structured snippets expand your real estate, often doubling clickable space without extra cost per click. Effective agencies launch with at least four sitelinks and two callouts per ad group, then rotate fresh variants every month to keep Quality Score high.


Yet, companies often struggle to maximize all these potentials. Without the right expertise, campaigns can turn into a cesspool of wasted ad spend, irrelevant clicks, and missed opportunities. Therefore, understanding the nuances of Google Ads campaigns is essential.


## DIY Google Ads management: pros and cons


At first glance, managing your own Google Ads seems straightforward. You create an account, pick some keywords, write an ad copy, and watch the leads roll in — right? Not quite.


The in-house ads management has its perks. To start with, you have total control over your campaigns and the satisfaction of seeing your efforts pay off (if you get it right). It’s also a way to save on agency fees, which can be tempting for companies with lean budgets.


### Some other benefits of managing Google ads in-house


- **Complete visibility:** You have full visibility into every campaign detail.
- **Product knowledge:** In-house marketers have better knowledge about the product which is later reflected in the copy.
- **Hands-on learning:** Managing campaigns gives you insights into audience behavior and Google Ads mechanics.


But in reality, managing Google Ads is a full-time job disguised as a part-time task. From selecting keywords to analyzing performance metrics, it can be a tenuous process requiring technical knowledge, creative expertise, and domain experience. Any lack can cause the campaigns to be a major failure and end up wasting money.


### Pitfalls of DIT Google Ads management


- **Keyword mismatches:** Choosing keywords that drive irrelevant traffic wastes both time and budget.
- **Poor ad targeting:** Failing to define the right audience means your ads land in front of the wrong people.
- **Neglecting conversion tracking:** Without proper tracking, you’re essentially flying blind.
- **Inefficient bidding strategies:** Overspending on clicks or underbidding on crucial keywords can topple a campaign.
- **Negative keyword hygiene:** One of the biggest money-pits in self-managed accounts is failing to add negative keywords. Every day your ads can trigger for near-match queries that look relevant but never convert. Think “free,” “jobs,” or competitor names.


For businesses attempting the DIY route, tools like Google Keyword Planner and Google Analytics are a must. While certifications like the Google Ads Search Certification can provide foundational knowledge, managing campaigns at scale is entirely different.

Agencies use advanced keyword tools to find high-converting search terms, long-tail variations, and competitor gaps. They also define a strategy for match types, keyword grouping, and negative keyword lists to filter out irrelevant traffic.

Experienced copywriters create compelling headlines and descriptions designed to grab attention and generate clicks. A/B testing is used to compare ad variants and identify top performers across campaigns.


### **Campaign structure**


A clean structure of campaigns and ad groups ensures better performance, easier optimization, and higher Quality Scores. Agencies align structure with business goals, funnel stages, and audience types.


### **Landing page optimization**


To boost conversions, agencies evaluate landing pages for design, message match, speed, and mobile responsiveness. Many also run A/B tests to optimize layout, CTA placement, and user experience.


### **Bid and budget management**


Experts monitor and adjust bids across campaigns to strike a balance between volume and efficiency. Whether it’s manual CPC or automated bidding like Target CPA, budget allocation is tuned for best ROI.


### **Conversion tracking and attribution**


Before a single dollar is spent, agencies wire up GA4, Google Tag Manager, and (for service businesses) call-tracking numbers. This lets the algorithm learn which clicks become leads, demos, or purchases—fuel for smart bidding strategies that lower cost per acquisition over time.


### **Analytics and reporting**


Performance marketing agencies provide clear, actionable reports that highlight spend, ROAS, CTR, and conversion trends. Many also offer custom dashboards and insights to guide decision-making.


### **Retargeting**


Agencies set up remarketing campaigns to re-engage users who visited your site but didn’t convert. These ads often drive some of the highest ROI by targeting warm audiences ready to take action.

## FAQ


### What are Google Search Ads?


Google Search Ads are text ads on search results that let you reach people while they’re searching on Google for the products and services you offer.


Google Search Ads work on a bidding system. When users enter a search query, Google runs an auction, where your ad competes with others for a spot on the search results page. The position of your ad depends on factors like your bid amount, the relevance of your ad to the search term, and your ad's quality score. You only pay when a user clicks on your ad, hence the term 'pay-per-click' (PPC). Popular paid search platforms include Google Ads and Microsoft (Bing) Ads.


### Where can I see Google Ads performance?


Kaya's Competitor Ad Intelligence Tool allows you to see competitors' ads on Google. You can also use the Google search engine, keyword planner, and Google's Ad Transparency Center.


### What are the three basic components of a Google Search Ad?


A Google Search Ad usually has three parts in the search results. They are a display URL, headline text, and description text.


### How much does it cost to have someone manage your Google Ads?


The cost of professional Google Ads management varies depending on the agency, scope of services, and your monthly ad spend. Most agencies charge either a flat monthly fee (e.g. $500–$2,000) or a percentage of your ad spend (typically 10%–20%). Some also offer performance-based or tiered pricing models. While it may seem like an added expense, a good agency often pays for itself by improving campaign efficiency, reducing wasted spend, and driving better ROI through expert optimization.


### How do I advertise my services on Google?


To advertise your services on Google, you’ll need to set up a Google Ads account, choose your campaign type (Search, Local Services, Display, etc.), and define your targeting criteria such as location, keywords, and audience. You’ll then create compelling ad copy, set your budget, and monitor performance using Google’s built-in analytics. Many businesses also create a dedicated landing page for better conversion rates. If you're unsure where to start, working with a Google Ads agency can help streamline this process.


### Do Google Ads work for service businesses?


Yes, Google Ads can be incredibly effective for service-based businesses. Whether you're a local plumber, SaaS provider, or B2B consulting firm, Google Ads helps you appear at the top of search results when potential customers are actively looking for your services. Formats like Search Ads and Local Service Ads are designed specifically to capture high-intent traffic and convert leads into bookings, calls, or consultations.
