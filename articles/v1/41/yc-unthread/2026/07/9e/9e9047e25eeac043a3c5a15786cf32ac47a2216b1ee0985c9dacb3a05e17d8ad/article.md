# Console vs Unthread: Choosing the Right AI Service Desk for Internal Support

When IT, HR, and operations teams evaluate service management platforms, the decision often comes down to two distinct philosophies: traditional ITSM automation versus Slack-native conversational AI. While Console focuses on IT-specific automation with native identity and access management,[Unthread](https://unthread.io/products/slack-support?ref=unthread.io) delivers a broader internal support solution that transforms Slack channels into structured help desks for IT, HR, finance, legal, and workplace operations. The platform choice significantly impacts not just IT workflows but how every department handles employee requests, from confidential HR matters requiring private ticketing to finance approvals needing cross-functional visibility. Understanding these fundamental differences, along with how pricing scales, where automation excels, and which teams each platform truly serves, helps growing organizations select the platform that matches their multi-department support needs and long-term operational strategy.


## **Key Takeaways**


- Unthread offers transparent per-agent pricing at[$50-$75 per agent/month](https://unthread.io/pricing?ref=unthread.io) , while Console uses custom quote-based pricing typically based on agent count and feature tier
- Unthread's Slack-native architecture means employees never leave Slack to submit or track requests, while Console uses bot-based intake that routes to a separate platform
- Unthread's purpose-built AI agents achieve[40% automatic ticket resolution](https://unthread.io/blog/case-study-how-lemonade-boosts-efficiency-with-unthread-and-gen-ai/) across IT, HR, Legal, Procurement, and Finance teams at Lemonade
- Unthread supports multi-department internal operations (IT, HR, finance, legal, RevOps) with private DM flows for sensitive HR requests, while Console focuses primarily on IT workflows
- Unthread's[self-learning knowledge base](https://unthread.io/products/knowledge-base?ref=unthread.io) automatically generates documentation from resolved tickets, reducing manual KB maintenance
- Unthread provides bring-your-own-LLM flexibility via MCP integration, reducing AI vendor lock-in


Modern internal support teams face a critical choice: adopt an IT-specific automation platform or implement a Slack-native solution that handles requests across every department. Console and Unthread represent these two approaches. Console positions itself as an AI-native ITSM platform with deep identity and access management capabilities. Unthread operates as a purpose-built Slack helpdesk that converts conversations into structured tickets while automating workflows across IT, HR, finance, legal, and operations. This comparison shows why Unthread is a strong fit for organizations seeking flexible, multi-department internal support.

The fundamental difference: **Console automates IT workflows through a separate platform, while Unthread transforms Slack itself into your internal help desk** for every department.

## **Slack Integration Depth Defines User Experience**


The integration philosophy creates dramatically different user experiences.


**Console's Slack integration** operates through bot-based intake. Employees interact with a Console bot in Slack or Teams, which routes requests to Console's separate platform for processing. Support agents work in Console's interface, returning to Slack only to communicate resolutions.


**Unthread's Slack-native design** keeps everything in Slack. The platform automatically converts Slack threads and DMs into tickets without disrupting natural conversation flow. Features include:


- Any Slack channel becomes a full help desk (like #it-help or #hr-requests)
- Tickets created automatically from messages, no bot commands required
- Status updates, assignments, and priorities managed from Slack threads
- Agents to triage and route based on content analysis
- Private DM flows for sensitive requests that need confidentiality


This Slack-native approach reduces context-switching by keeping more work inside Slack. Employees submit requests naturally, and support teams manage everything from the Slack inbox. Some ticket types remain in-channel for visibility while others move to DMs for privacy, all within the same platform.
