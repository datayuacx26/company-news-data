# How to Implement Full Cycle Recruiting in July 2026

Selecting candidates is the fourth step in the process where your team conducts interviews and determines who will move forward in the process. It’s a tricky step because this is a team effort. Everyone on your hiring team plays a part in assessing candidates and alignment here can make or break the hiring process.


### Step 1: Define what selection criteria you're looking for


The core team (hiring manager, recruiter and leadership) should align on candidate qualities before interviews begin.


### Step 2: Design an interview plan


It's important to set up your interview process including how many stages of interviews you want candidates to go through. Your interview process should include enough stages to find the best candidate for your company while providing a positive candidate experience throughout the interview process. You can build your interview plan in your applicant tracking system.


### Step 3: Create interview questions and scorecards


Based on the qualities you’re looking for, the next step is to **create an interview rubric and keep it somewhere where it can be easily accessed.**


If you've never hired for a role or hired in general before, the best way to develop a rubric is to ask folks at other companies what they've found to be critical in assessing different personas and helping them make a great hire. You can check out Dover's[interview process best practices](https://help.dover.com/en/articles/6229487-interview-process-best-practices) or our guides to hiring[account executives and sales representatives](https://help.dover.com/en/articles/8184223-guide-to-interviewing-account-executives-and-sales-representatives) ,[CSMs](https://help.dover.com/en/articles/8184662-guide-to-interviewing-customer-success-managers) , designers,[engineers](https://help.dover.com/en/articles/8054720-guide-to-interviewing-engineers) ,[marketers](https://help.dover.com/en/articles/8184464-guide-to-interviewing-marketers) , and product managers.


*Dover's ATS lets you add feedback forms / scorecards for each interview stage.*


### Step 4: Make scorecards accessible to all interviewers


Once you have a rubric, make it accessible to everyone involved in hiring. A **centralized ATS** lets interviewers submit structured feedback so every candidate gets graded against the same criteria.


You don’t have to buy an expensive ATS to do this. You can easily set up a table in a tool like Notion, Google Sheets or Airtable.


### Step 5: Interview candidates and conduct a debrief


After the final interview with each candidate, conducting a debrief with all of the interviewers is a great way to align on the candidate to determine whether or not you should make them an offer.
