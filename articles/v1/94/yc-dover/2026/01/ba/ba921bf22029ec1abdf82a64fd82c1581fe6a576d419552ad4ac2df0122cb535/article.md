# LinkedIn Recruiter Lite vs. Professional: Is the $170/mo Worth It? (August 2026)

Dover's[Recruiter Marketplace](https://www.dover.com/blog/what-is-recruiter-marketplace) connects you with[fractional recruiters](https://www.dover.com/blog/what-is-a-fractional-recruiter) who handle end-to-end sourcing for[$2,000 to $7,000 per hire](https://www.dover.com/blog/fractional-recruiter-costs) . You pay for candidates in your pipeline, not monthly database access. The[hourly recruiting model](https://www.dover.com/blog/contingency-vs-hourly-recruiting-costs) scales with hiring demand, and because Dover pairs its free ATS with on-demand recruiters in one shared system, both you and the recruiter see the same pipeline in real time.


What separates Dover from a typical recruiter directory is that fractional recruiting agencies build their delivery workflows on top of Dover's infrastructure. The pipeline visibility, shared ATS, and recruiter coordination tools that agencies rely on internally are the same ones you access directly as a Dover client. That means you get the structured process of an agency engagement without paying the agency's management margin or losing ownership of your candidate data when the search closes. Dover operates as the underlying layer those agencies run on, so working through the marketplace puts you closer to that infrastructure than going through an agency that adds its own overhead on top of it.


Choosing a recruiter also carries less guesswork than a traditional agency arrangement. Dover's marketplace surfaces real, verified reviews from companies that have worked with each recruiter, covering the types of roles they've filled, the stage of company they've worked with, and what clients say about sourcing quality and communication. Before a search starts, you can read actual client accounts of how a recruiter performs on searches like yours. That level of transparency is not common in traditional agency relationships, where recruiter performance data typically stays internal and clients have little visibility into how a specific person has performed before the engagement begins.


The decision comes down to hiring volume and role complexity. LinkedIn Recruiter can be enough for familiar roles at low volume. When a search is senior, specialized, or competing for time against product and sales work, a fractional recruiter can take sourcing off your plate. If you're still unsure, this guide on[what type of recruiter is best](https://www.dover.com/blog/what-type-of-recruiter-is-best) can help you decide.
