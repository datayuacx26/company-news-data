## What Is an Applicant Tracking System?


An applicant tracking system is software that centralizes the hiring process: job postings go out, applications come in, and instead of a scattered inbox, every candidate lands in one place where they can be sorted, scored, and moved through stages.


The core problem an ATS solves is volume. Once a role gets more than a handful of applicants, manual tracking breaks fast. Spreadsheets get stale, emails get missed, and qualified candidates slip through without a clear record of why they were passed over. An ATS handles the parsing and organizing so hiring teams spend time on decisions, not data entry.


HR teams at large companies have used these systems for decades. What changed is accessibility. Tools that once required enterprise contracts and IT support are now available to founders and small teams, often for free. If you want a deeper breakdown, see this guide on[what an applicant tracking system is](https://www.dover.com/blog/what-is-an-applicant-tracking-system) . Who uses an ATS today ranges from a solo recruiter managing a pipeline to a startup founder filling their first five roles without any dedicated recruiting staff.


## How an ATS Works: Step by Step


Most ATS workflows follow the same sequence regardless of which tool you use.


You write a role description, publish it, and the ATS distributes it to job boards automatically. One action covers LinkedIn, Indeed, and dozens of other sites, with every application routing back to a single pipeline. As resumes come in, the system parses structured data (work history, education, skills) and, in most tools, scores candidates against the criteria you set. A role with hundreds of applicants gets condensed into a shorter review list without manual sorting.


From there, candidates move through customizable stages: phone screen, technical interview, hiring manager review, offer. Moving someone forward or rejecting them triggers automated emails, and calendar integrations let candidates self-schedule interviews. At the close of a search, the ATS holds the full record of every applicant, every stage change, and every decision, which matters for compliance and for running better searches on the next similar role.

## Key ATS Features That Actually Matter


The differentiators worth weighing:


-


Multi-board job distribution. Posting once and having applications route to a single pipeline from 100-plus boards is a meaningful time saver. Manually reposting per site is the kind of friction that compounds across every search.


-


AI-assisted candidate scoring. According to[Select Software Reviews](https://www.selectsoftwarereviews.com/blog/applicant-tracking-system-statistics) , 79% of organizations have integrated AI or automation directly into their ATS. Used well, scoring tools filter high-volume inbound down to a reviewable list. The key word is “assistive.” These tools surface candidates worth reviewing; they don’t make the hire.


-


Interview scheduling automation. Calendar integrations that let candidates self-book eliminate the back-and-forth that eats coordinator time without adding any value.


-


Employee referral tracking. Most ATS tools treat referrals as an afterthought. If your referral pipeline matters, check whether the tool tracks referral source at the candidate level or just logs it as a note somewhere.


-


Careers page builder. A branded careers page that collects applications directly removes the dependency on a developer every time a role opens.


-


Funnel reporting. Knowing where candidates drop off, whether they were sourced but never screened or screened but rejected at the phone-screen stage, tells you where the process breaks, not merely how many people applied.

## ATS in HR: Compliance, DEI, and Data Integrity


For HR teams managing hiring across multiple roles or geographies, an ATS often serves as the primary compliance infrastructure instead of a convenience layer.


EEOC recordkeeping rules require covered employers to retain certain personnel and employment records, including application and hiring records, for specified periods. An ATS can make those records easier to retain and manage consistently. GDPR adds another layer for companies hiring across Europe: employers need an appropriate lawful basis for processing candidate data, along with processes for retention, transparency, and applicable data-subject rights such as erasure.
