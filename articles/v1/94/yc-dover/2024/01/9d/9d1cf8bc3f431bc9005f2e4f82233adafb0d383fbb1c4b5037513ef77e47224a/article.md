## Understanding the Role


An executive assistant plays a critical role in keeping the business running smoothly and can help maximize a founder’s effectiveness. Their responsibilities often extend beyond traditional administrative assistant tasks, given the ever-changing and fast-paced environment of a startup.


Here are some key responsibilities:


-


**Administrative support** — The primary role of an EA will be to handle scheduling and calendar management including coordinating meetings, events, and travel. Your EA can act as a consistent point of contact for your team, clients, partners and any other stakeholders that need to reach you. They can also filter your communications, sort them by importance and make sure urgent messages reach you right away. This helps you get more done and also makes sure nothing slips through the cracks.


-


**Strategic support & management** — At a startup, often an EA will help with market research, competitor analysis, event planning, and sometimes communication strategy. They can also help identify and solve day-to-day challenges, allowing founders to focus on high-level strategic decisions.


-


**Brand ambassador** — Your EA can help build your company culture, assisting in growing a great company work culture and making sure that the founder’s vision is communicated effectively.


## What to Look for in an Executive Assistant


When you’re looking for an EA for your company, you’ll likely need someone who has the following qualities:


Quality What to Look For Why It Matters at a Startup


**Communication Skills** Clear, concise written and verbal communication; comfortable representing you on calls and in email Your EA will often be the first point of contact for clients, partners, and stakeholders, so how they communicate reflects directly on you


**Organizational Skills** Proven ability to manage multiple calendars, coordinate events, and keep competing priorities in order At a startup, schedules shift constantly; a disorganized EA can cause missed meetings, dropped follow-ups, and wasted founder time


**Adaptability** Comfort with ambiguity, changing priorities, and picking up new tools quickly Startup EA roles shift faster than traditional assistant positions, and the scope of the job in month six may look very different from month one


## Planning and Preparation


Before you start, it’s good to outline what you’re looking for in an EA and your plan to hire them. While this can take some time out of your day, it will save you time in the long run. If you write down everything you’re looking for, you can make sure your job description attracts the right kind of candidates.


Part of your planning process should include all decision-makers along with people with whom the EA will be working with. Once you have your team together, you should meet with your team and ask some of the following questions to get on the same page:


-


What will the day to day responsibilities of the role include?


-


How much experience do they need to be successful in the role?


-


What team will they be joining?


-


What skills do they need?


-


What tools will they use for their job? Do they need to have prior experience using them?


-


What’s the market rate for this position? Review[BLS executive assistant wage data](https://www.bls.gov/oes/2023/may/oes436011.htm) to set a realistic budget range.


-


Is this a full-time or part-time position?


Once you have a clear understanding of your needs, wants and budget for the role, you and your team are ready to start putting together your job description.

## Interviewing Candidates


### Initial Screening


Once you’ve screened resumes to a manageable pool of candidates, it’s time to start scheduling your interviews. The EA interview process should start with a screening call handled by HR, so they can eliminate anyone unqualified or who isn’t a good fit for the role.


### Final-Round Interviews


Once you’ve narrowed things down to a few top candidates, executive assistants should be interviewed by their direct superiors. If you’re the founder, that means you’ll need to interview those who make it to the final stages. Since they’ll be working closely with you and other members of the C-suite daily, it’s important to get a chance to speak with each of them one-on-one.


Throughout the interview process, you should be vetting their skills, problem-solving and cultural fit for the organization.[Structured interview research](https://www.criteriacorp.com/resources/complete-guide-to-structured-interviews) shows this approach is roughly twice as predictive of job performance as informal conversation, making it worth adopting even for a single-role search. Many times, the executive assistant will be the first point of contact for those trying to reach you, so you’re sort of hiring the face of the company. Keep that in mind when making your decision.

## FAQs


### What qualities should I focus on when hiring an executive assistant for a startup?


Adaptability is the most important quality to screen for in a startup EA. The scope of the role in month six will often look very different from month one, and candidates who thrive in structured corporate environments may struggle with that pace of change. Beyond adaptability, look for strong written communication and proven organizational skills, since your EA will frequently be the first point of contact for clients and partners.


### How should I structure the interview process for an executive assistant role?


Start with an HR screening call to filter out candidates who are unqualified or a poor culture fit before involving founders or executives. Once you have a small group of finalists, the founder or direct supervisor should conduct one-on-one interviews, since the EA will work closely with leadership daily and cultural alignment matters as much as technical skill.


### Should I hire a full-time or part-time executive assistant as a startup founder?


The right answer depends on how fragmented your schedule is and how much communication volume you are managing, both of which you should clarify with your team before writing the job description. Startups with a founder handling multiple stakeholder relationships and a packed travel calendar typically benefit from full-time support, while earlier-stage companies with lighter administrative load may find part-time coverage sufficient.


### How do I write an executive assistant job description that filters out unqualified candidates?


Lead with the specific day-to-day responsibilities (calendar management, stakeholder communication, research) and be explicit about required skills so applicants can self-select out if they are not a fit. Include details about your startup’s mission, culture, and any tools the EA will use regularly, since candidates who align with the company’s stage and working style are more likely to stay and grow with the role.
