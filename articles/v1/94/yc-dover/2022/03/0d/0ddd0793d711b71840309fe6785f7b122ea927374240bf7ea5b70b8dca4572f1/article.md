# How to Pay Employees at Your Startup (August 2026)

Paying employees at a startup involves more than picking a number. You need to decide how to split cash and equity, how much runway to set aside for salaries, and how to stay consistent as the team grows. The piece that ties all of that together is structure. Figuring out[what to pay your first employees](https://www.dover.com/) is one of those decisions founders tend to defer until it becomes urgent. A candidate pushes back on an offer, two people in similar roles are making different amounts, or a new hire finds out their compensation is below market. At that point, you are reacting instead of operating from a structure. Salary bands are how you get ahead of that.


### **TLDR:**


-


Salary bands reduce one-off negotiation and give your team a shared reference point for raises and promotions.


-


Target the 50th percentile for base salary and 75th percentile for equity during early funding stages.


-


Start building bands around the 50-to-60-employee mark, before pay gaps become hard to explain or fix.

## Why Are Salary Bands Important?


Creating salary bands creates transparency within your organization, and helps employees to understand how and when they’ll receive raises, promotions, or adjustments**.** If expectations are set early on, the goalposts are clear and your team will be better equipped to meet them.


For an early-stage company, it’s important to balance the right amount of cash and equity in an offer so that:


-


Cash flow is not a stressor for employees and,


-


Equity is parsed out in a way that makes sure it’s still available for future hires and refresher grants.


When your departments are starting to take shape (around the[50 to 60 employee mark](https://www.dover.com/blog/when-startups-hire-fractional-recruiter) ) you’ll want to create a structured way to assess different bands across roles.


## How to Build Salary Bands in 5 Steps


The process does not require a compensation consultant or a dedicated HR team. What it does require is a clear picture of your current roles, some market data, and the discipline to build the structure before you need it. The five steps below cover that sequence from role inventory to ongoing review.


-


**Take stock of all the roles in each department.** When creating levels within a department, you’ll want to assess how your current team would fit into those bands. List out each employee and their title, as well as the requirements and expectations in order to be successful. You’ll also want to include any open roles you have, especially if they’re urgent and will join your team soon.


-


**Rank each role across departments.** This process might be based more on intuition and the logical ordering of your team. In fact, teams may structure themselves this way already. Titles are helpful here: a senior or director position is obviously higher in the hierarchy than a new grad. That being said, don’t be too precious about this, but try to see if there is a clear structure and pattern to seniority, responsibilities, and titles.


-


**Pull market rate data for each role.** Once you have a list of roles within your own company, you’ll want to assess how those line up with market rates. If you have unique job titles or positions, pick the closest or most popular title for the purposes of comparison. This will help you determine how much others are paying, and how competitive your offer is. Tools like[Pave](https://www.pave.com/) and[Carta Total Compensation](https://carta.com/learn/startups/compensation/benchmarking/) are well-regarded options for startup-specific benchmarking data.


-


**Create salary bands and make adjustments for current employees as needed.** Start by assessing your current funding and running a few cash/equity calculations; the[Homebrew offer slider](https://coda.io/@homebrew/compensation-at-startups/offer-package-5) is a useful reference. Bands let teammates grow in pay as they grow in skill, without requiring a title change. Before publishing, check whether any current employees are below market and make adjustments to close those gaps. For base salary, target the 50th percentile; for equity, target the 75th. Equity-heavy packages conserve runway and align employees with company outcomes, while cash-heavy packages are easier to recruit against but burn runway faster. The right split depends on your stage and who you are hiring. For a deeper look, Dover’s[startup compensation guide](https://www.dover.com/ebooks/startup-compensation-guide) covers each step in detail.


-


**Review salary bands regularly against the job market and cost of living (if you’re not following a remote/hybrid model).** Your salary bands should change as your business grows and in accordance with market fluctuation. A good starting point is to formally reevaluate your structure once a year.


## What 2026 Compensation Trends Mean for Your Salary Bands


The market heading into 2026 rewards founders who have already built structured bands.[Carta’s H2 2025 State of Startup Compensation](https://carta.com/data/startup-compensation-h2-2025/) (published May 2026) found that median salaries for individual contributors rose 6.4% over the past two years, with AI/ML engineers seeing a 9.1% salary gain over that same span.

## FAQs


### What tools should a company under 50 employees use for hiring in 2026?


An ATS handles[pipeline tracking](https://www.dover.com/blog/startup-ats-guide) , candidate communication, and job board distribution. For teams under 50, the priority is a system that works without a dedicated recruiting operations function behind it. Free tools covering basic pipeline management are a reasonable starting point. Fractional recruiting agencies typically work inside the client’s ATS so that candidate data, pipeline status, and hiring-team communication stay in one shared system instead of getting split between tools. When a role calls for active sourcing, that agency layer is a cost-effective path compared to full retained search. ### When should a startup start building salary bands?


Most early-stage teams can manage with informal offers through the first 10 to 20 hires. Once departments begin taking shape, typically around the 50 to 60 employee mark, pay gaps between people in similar roles become harder to explain and harder to fix on the fly. If two people doing comparable work are at different pay levels with no documented rationale, you will face friction when promoting, backfilling, or onboarding a senior hire who asks how ranges are set. Building the structure before those situations come up is the safer call.


### What percentile should an early-stage startup target for base salary and equity?


For base salary, the 50th percentile is a solid starting point during early funding stages, since it keeps cash flow manageable without pricing you out of good candidates. For equity, targeting the 75th percentile helps offset the cash restraint that comes with limited runway. Both targets change as you raise more capital. A later-stage company can move base pay toward the 65th or 75th percentile while pulling back on equity. Tools like Pave and Carta Total Compensation can help you benchmark where your bands stand relative to the market.


### Do I need a recruiter if I’m already using an ATS?


An ATS handles the mechanics: posting roles, tracking applicants, scheduling interviews, and managing candidate communication. It does not source candidates on your behalf. For roles where inbound volume is sufficient and the process is mostly coordination, an ATS alone can cover the workflow. When a role is senior, specialized, or inbound is thin, the ATS keeps your pipeline organized but can leave it empty. That is when a fractional recruiting agency becomes a practical addition. The agency sources and vets candidates while working directly inside your ATS pipeline, so nothing gets siloed. Dover operates as the shared system where both layers run, which means you keep full pipeline visibility whether sourcing support is active or not.
