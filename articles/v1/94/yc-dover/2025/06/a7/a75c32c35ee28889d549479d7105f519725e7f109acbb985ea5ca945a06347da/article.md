## What Are Startup Recruiters and Why Do Commission Structures Matter?


[Startup recruiters](https://www.dover.com/recruiters) specialize in the unique challenges of early-stage companies. They understand equity compensation, fast-paced environments, and the need to find the right talent to fit the startup culture.


**But here’s the thing** : how you pay your recruiter affects your hiring budget and outcomes.


The wrong commission structure can eat up 30% of your Series A funding on recruiting fees alone. We’ve seen startups burn through $200K in contingency fees for five hires when a fractional approach would have cost $50K.


Understanding these models is about hiring top talent efficiently within your budget.


As of August 2026, startup hiring has grown more deliberate. Venture funding remains selective, burn-rate scrutiny is high, and many founders are managing leaner headcount plans than they were two years ago. That context makes the cost gap between recruiting models more consequential. A $116K contingency bill on five hires can meaningfully compress your runway, while a $20K to $35K fractional approach for the same hires keeps that capital working in the business.


## The Four Main Startup Recruiter Commission Models


### Contingency Recruiting: Pay-for-Success Model


Contingency recruiting follows a simple “no hire, no fee” structure. You only pay when the recruiter successfully places a candidate who accepts your offer.


**How it works:**


-


Recruiter works on your role alongside other clients


-


Fee ranges from 15-25% of first-year salary


-


Payment due when candidate starts


-


No upfront costs. For a $120K engineering role, you’re looking at $18K-30K in fees


The appeal here is obvious, zero risk if they don’t deliver. However, since recruiters juggle multiple clients, your role might not get priority attention or will be managed by different team members. The quality can vary because speed often trumps fit in this situation.


Vetting external recruiters becomes important in contingency setups. You need recruiters who understand startup culture and won’t just throw resumes at you.


Contingency recruiters typically[fill 25% of assigned roles](https://retrainedsearch.com/blog/contingent-vs-retained-recruitment/#:~:text=contingently%2C%20you%20average-,25%25%20fill%20rates,-.%C2%A025%25%20is%20low) . The success rate varies a lot based on role difficulty and market conditions.


### Retained Search: Premium Executive Recruitment


Retained search represents the high end of recruiting services. Clients pay 30-50% of the projected first-year salary, typically split into installments regardless of outcome.


**The retained model breakdown:**


-


30-50% upfront retainer


-


Additional payments at 30 and 60 days


-


Dedicated focus on your search


-


[85-95% completion rate](https://www.jrgpartners.com/retained-search-success-rate-statistics-completion-stick-rates/) for retained executive searches


-


Complete candidate assessment and market mapping.


Retained search makes sense for C-level hires where the[cost of a bad hire](https://www.shrm.org/topics-tools/news/talent-acquisition/real-costs-recruitment) exceeds the search fee. For a $200K CEO role, a $60K-100K retained search fee might be worth it. Most startup roles don’t need retained search. You’re paying premium prices for a level of service that’s overkill for individual contributor roles.


### Full-Time In-House Recruiters: Building Internal Capacity


Full-time recruiters provide focused in-house resources. While[recruiter salaries](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/business-and-financial/human-resources-specialists.htm) vary by market, the total investment includes base compensation plus benefits and payroll taxes, making this a serious commitment for growing companies.


**When full-time makes sense:**


-


Hiring 20+ people annually


-


Multiple searches running at the same time


-


Need for company culture knowledge


-


Long-term recruiting strategy development. The math is straightforward: If you’re paying $25K per contingency hire and making 5+ hires annually, a full-time recruiter starts looking budget friendly


[Recruiting process bottlenecks](https://www.dover.com/blog/how-to-reduce-recruitment-process-bottlenecks-for-startups) aren’t solved by adding headcount. You need solid systems first. That’s where tools like Dover’s[free ATS](https://www.dover.com/ats) become important. Even with internal recruiters, you still need systems to track and organize candidates. The biggest mistake? Scaling up recruiting without setting up processes first. A full-time recruiter without proper tools is just an expensive coordinator.


### Fractional Recruiters: The Sweet Spot for Startups


Fractional recruiting offers the knowledge of senior recruiters on an hourly or project basis. Rates typically range from $75-$150 per hour, with most projects requiring 20-40 hours per successful hire.


**Why fractional works for startups:**


-


Pay only for time invested


-


Access to senior-level expertise


-


Flexibility to scale up or down


-


No long-term employment commitments


-


Typically costs 50% less than contingency for multiple hires.


A fractional recruiter might spend 30 hours on a $120K engineering role, costing $2,250-$4,500 vs. $18K-30K for contingency. The savings add up quickly.


What makes recruiting a perfect fit for[fractional work](https://www.dover.com/blog/five-reasons-why-recruiting-is-uniquely-suited-for-a-fractional-model) is that it’s project-based with clear goals. You’re buying expertise and execution, not ongoing management. The key is finding experienced fractional recruiters who understand startup dynamics.


The model works best when the recruiter and your internal team share the same system. Without that foundation, you’re coordinating over email, losing context at handoffs, and rebuilding candidate history from scratch with every new search. A modern ATS changes this: all sourcing notes, outreach history, and candidate data stay in your account, not the recruiter’s. When the engagement ends, the recruiting intelligence stays with you, and every future search starts from a stronger position.

## When to Choose Each Recruiting Model by Company Stage


### Pre-Seed to Seed Stage: Bootstrap Smart


Early-stage startups should focus on fractional recruiting. You’re typically hiring 1-3 people, making contingency fees overly expensive.


**Pre-seed priorities:**


-


Find PMF and not run out of money.


-


Focus on critical first hires.


-


Build recruiting foundation with free tools.


-


Use fractional expertise for key roles.[Top hiring practices for early-stage startups](https://www.dover.com/blog/top-hiring-practices-every-early-stage-startup-should-know) help you focus on getting the foundation right. ## FAQs


### What’s the real cost difference between contingency recruiting and fractional recruiting for a startup making 5 hires?


Contingency fees at 20% can run $116,000 for five typical startup hires (two engineers, one PM, one sales rep, one marketing manager), while fractional recruiting for the same five roles typically runs $15,000-$37,500 at 30 hours per role. The structural difference is that contingency fees scale with salary, while fractional costs scale with time invested, so the gap widens on higher-compensation roles.


### When does retained search actually make sense versus fractional recruiting?


Retained search is worth considering for C-level hires where the cost of a wrong decision outweighs the search fee: a $60,000-$100,000 retained engagement on a $200,000 CEO role can be defensible. For individual contributor and mid-level roles, the 30-50% upfront fee structure rarely matches the value delivered, and fractional recruiting covers the same ground at a fraction of the cost.


### Should I hire a full-time recruiter or use fractional recruiting at Series A?


Fractional recruiting tends to be the stronger fit at Series A unless you’re already running 20+ hires per year with multiple concurrent searches. A full-time recruiter costs $110,000-$125,000 annually once benefits, payroll taxes, and equipment are factored in, equivalent to 15-60 fractional hiring engagements. Series A companies with bursty, variable hiring volume get more control by paying for recruiter time only when searches are active.


### How do contingency recruiting and Dover’s fractional model compare for startup hiring?


Contingency models charge 15-25% of first-year salary and carry roughly a 25% fill rate on assigned roles, with recruiters splitting attention across multiple clients and no guaranteed focus on your search. Dover’s fractional model charges $75-$125 per hour with most hires running $2,000-$7,000, using a dedicated recruiter working within a shared ATS so all sourcing intelligence stays in your account after each engagement ends, not with the recruiter.


### How do I know when to switch from contingency recruiting to a fractional model?


The clearest signal is making three or more hires per year. At that volume, contingency fees compound faster than fractional costs, and the savings typically exceed $50,000 across five hires. A second signal is any role that stays open longer than expected: contingency recruiters have no financial incentive to put hard-to-fill searches first, while an hourly model keeps attention focused regardless of role complexity.
