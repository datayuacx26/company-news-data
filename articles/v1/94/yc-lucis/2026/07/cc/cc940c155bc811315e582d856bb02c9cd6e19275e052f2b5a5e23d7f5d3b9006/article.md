# The 6 biomarkers that show how your skin handles sun exposure

This listicle is inspired by our 2-part series on the sun:


-


[What the Sun Really Does to Your Body](https://www.lucis.life/blog/what-the-sun-really-does-to-your-body)


-


[Preparing Your Skin for the Sun: Nutrition, Hydration and Protection](https://www.lucis.life/blog/preparing-your-skin-for-the-sun)


Preparing your skin for the sun is not just about sunscreen. How your skin copes with UV rays also depends on its internal terrain: antioxidant reserves, nutritional status, inflammatory balance. Here are the 6 biomarkers that shed light on that terrain.


## 1. Vitamin D (25-OH): the reference marker for your sun status


The first marker to look at whenever the topic is sun and skin.


**Why it matters:** 25-OH vitamin D is the reference marker for assessing your status, whether the vitamin D comes from the sun, food or supplementation. Many people assume summer is enough to rebuild their reserves, which is not always true.


**Context:**


-


persistent fatigue


-


fragile immunity


-


low sun exposure


-


darker skin


-


pregnancy or perimenopause


**Key takeaway:** measure your 25-OH vitamin D with your doctor, especially if you recognize yourself in one of these contexts. Summer does not guarantee a sufficient level.


## 2. Ferritin: your iron stores for energy, skin and hair


A terrain marker that matters for energy, recovery, skin, hair and nails.


**Why it matters:** ferritin reflects your iron stores. It becomes relevant when fatigue, hair loss or slow recovery set in.


**Context:**


-


fatigue


-


hair loss


-


heavy periods


-


intense training


-


a diet low in iron


**Key takeaway:** if you combine fatigue and hair loss, have your ferritin checked with your doctor.


## 3. Zinc: the mineral for skin, wound healing and immunity


Useful when the skin becomes inflamed or heals poorly.


**Why it matters:** zinc is involved in the skin, wound healing and immunity. It can run low with inflammatory skin or insufficient dietary intake.


**Context:**


-


inflammatory skin


-


acne


-


slow wound healing


-


fragile immunity


-


brittle hair or nails


**Key takeaway:** do not supplement blindly over the long term, excess zinc can disrupt copper balance. Measure first, with your doctor.


## 4. Biotin (vitamin B8): a cofactor for hair and nails


A rare deficiency, but one that matters for hair and nails.


**Why it matters:** biotin is involved in hair loss and brittle nails. Deficiency remains rare.


**Context:**


-


hair loss


-


fragile or brittle nails


**Key takeaway:** for persistent hair loss or brittle nails, talk to your doctor rather than supplementing at random.


## 5. Omega-3 index: the quality of your membranes and your inflammatory balance


A marker of skin barrier quality and inflammatory balance.


**Why it matters:** the omega-3 index, or an erythrocyte fatty acid profile, assesses your EPA/DHA status and omega-3/omega-6 balance. It reflects cell membrane quality and inflammatory balance. Omega-3s support the skin barrier, hydration and recovery.


**Context:**


-


dry, inflammatory or reactive skin


-


tightness


-


dryness


-


sensitivity


-


slower recovery


**Key takeaway:** the omega-3 index or the AA/EPA ratio are more informative than the omega-6/omega-3 ratio alone. Measure with your doctor.


## 6. hs-CRP: the signal of your background inflammation


An indicator of systemic inflammation.


**Why it matters:** hs-CRP gives an indication of systemic inflammation and helps identify whether the terrain is inflammatory, which matters when the skin reacts strongly.


**Context:**


-


inflammatory skin


-


a terrain that seems inflammatory


**Key takeaway:** if your skin reacts strongly or the terrain seems inflammatory, hs-CRP helps make it objective. Interpret it with your doctor.


## In short


Together, these 6 markers tell the story of how your skin, hair and terrain handle the sun: your vitamin D status, your iron stores, your zinc, your biotin, the quality of your membranes and your background inflammation.
