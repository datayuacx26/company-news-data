# The 10 biomarkers that reveal your brain's terrain

This listicle is inspired by our two-part series on cognitive health:[Part 1: what cognitive health really means](https://www.lucis.life/blog/cognitive-health-preserving-your-brain-1) and[Part 2: the pillars that support your brain](https://www.lucis.life/blog/cognitive-health-preserving-your-brain-2) .


Faltering memory, fading focus, the end-of-day brain fog: these signals often reflect a shifting biological terrain. Here are 10 biomarkers that help you understand that terrain, and that you can act on.


## 1. Omega-3 index (EPA/DHA): the structure of your neurons


DHA contributes to the structure of neuronal membranes, EPA to inflammatory modulation. **Why it matters** : well-built neuronal membranes support communication between neurons and synaptic plasticity. The omega-3 index lets you objectify your status rather than assume it. **Context** :


-


A diet low in fatty fish


-


Cognitive fatigue and brain fog **Key takeaway** : aim for 2 portions of fatty fish per week, or measure your omega-3 index to know where you stand.


## 2. Homocysteine: the methylation and vascular marker


An amino acid regulated by vitamins B6, B9 and B12. **Why it matters** : elevated homocysteine may reflect a less favorable terrain for the brain, particularly in terms of vascular health and methylation. **Context** :


-


Insufficient B vitamin intake


-


A vascular terrain worth watching **Key takeaway** : it reads alongside B12 and folate. Discuss it with your doctor if elevated.


## 3. hs-CRP: the inflammation thermometer


A marker of low-grade inflammation. **Why it matters** : when chronic inflammation persists, it can affect the brain through immune activation and oxidative stress. In the short term, this can translate into brain fog and harder concentration. **Context** :


-


Brain fog, unusual fatigue


-


Harder concentration **Key takeaway** : a chronic inflammatory terrain is worth identifying early. Review it with your doctor.


## 4. Fasting insulin / HOMA-IR (and HbA1c): the brain's energy


The brain needs stable energy to think, remember and concentrate. **Why it matters** : when insulin resistance sets in, the brain's ability to use glucose efficiently can be impaired. HbA1c reflects blood sugar stability over time. **Context** :


-


Brain fog after meals


-


Cravings, need for sugar or caffeine


-


Difficulty concentrating **Key takeaway** : stable blood sugar = a more stable brain terrain. Fasting insulin, HOMA-IR and HbA1c read together.


## 5. ApoB (advanced lipid panel): cerebral perfusion


The brain depends on blood flow for its oxygen and nutrients. **Why it matters** : elevated ApoB and lipid metabolism disturbances can compromise cerebral perfusion over time. What protects the heart often protects the brain too. **Context** :


-


A cardiovascular terrain worth watching **Key takeaway** : ApoB refines vascular risk assessment beyond classic cholesterol.


## 6. 25-OH vitamin D: support for repair


A vitamin involved in repair and protection functions. **Why it matters** : vitamin D is among the nutrients that support the brain's repair, signaling and protection functions. **Context** :


-


Often low status, especially in winter **Key takeaway** : easy to measure and correct. Have your 25-OH vitamin D tested.


## 7. Vitamin B12 and folate (B9): neurotransmitters and myelin


Essential cofactors of brain function. **Why it matters** : B9 and B12 contribute to neurotransmitter synthesis, myelin and homocysteine regulation. **Context** :


-


Mental fatigue, difficulty finding words **Key takeaway** : they read alongside homocysteine to assess the methylation terrain.


## 8. Ferritin: your iron stores


Iron is a cofactor in the production of neurotransmitters. **Why it matters** : iron is among the cofactors needed to convert amino acids into neurotransmitters. Ferritin reflects your stores. **Context** :


-


Fatigue, unstable mental energy **Key takeaway** : a simple marker, best interpreted alongside the inflammatory context.


## 9. Thyroid panel: the brain's tempo


The thyroid influences brain energy and processing speed. **Why it matters** : a thyroid imbalance can translate into brain fog, lower concentration and cognitive fatigue. **Context** :


-


Mental fatigue, slowness, lower concentration **Key takeaway** : a thyroid panel helps explain persistent cognitive fatigue.


## 10. Cortisol and DHEA-S: the stress axis


Two key hormones of the stress response and recovery. **Why it matters** : cortisol modulates memory, stress and recovery. DHEA-S has documented neuroprotective effects and buffers some harmful effects of glucocorticoids. **Context** :


-


Stronger emotional reactivity, slower recovery


-


Unrefreshing sleep **Key takeaway** : cortisol and DHEA-S read together to assess stress load and resilience.


## Conclusion


None of these markers alone diagnoses cognitive decline. Taken together, they answer a more useful question: is your brain operating in a terrain that supports mental clarity, repair and adaptation?
