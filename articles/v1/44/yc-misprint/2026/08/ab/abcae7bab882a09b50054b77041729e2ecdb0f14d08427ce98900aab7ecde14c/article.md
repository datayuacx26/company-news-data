# How Misprint's Bid/Ask Pricing Works for One Piece Cards

# How Misprint's Bid/Ask Pricing Works for One Piece Cards


A card marketplace that works like a stock exchange, explained simply.


By Misprint Editorial | Published Aug 17, 2026 | 4 min read


*Most card marketplaces only let you do one thing: take the price a seller set, or walk away. Misprint adds a second option that changes everything, the ability to name your own price and wait for it.*


If you have used a stock brokerage, the model will feel familiar. If you have not, it is still simple once you see it. This article explains how Misprint's bid/ask system works for One Piece cards, why it usually gets buyers a better deal and sellers more liquidity, and how to actually use it.


## Asks and Bids: The Two Sides of the Market


Every card on Misprint has two prices that matter:


- **The ask** is the lowest price a seller is currently willing to accept. When you see a card "listed at $180," that is the lowest ask. Buy at the ask and you own the card immediately.
- **The bid** is the highest price a buyer is currently willing to pay. Buyers place bids to say "I want this card, but at my price, not the seller's." A seller can accept a bid at any time to sell instantly.


On a traditional marketplace, only asks exist. You either pay what the seller wants or you leave. On Misprint, both sides are visible, which means the market shows you not just what sellers hope to get, but what buyers are actually willing to pay.


## The Spread


The gap between the highest bid and the lowest ask is called the **spread.** Say the lowest ask on a One Piece alt-art is $180 and the highest bid is $160. The spread is $20, and that $20 is the negotiating room.


- A **buyer** who does not want to pay $180 can place a bid at, say, $168. If a seller decides that is good enough, the sale happens at $168 and the buyer saved $12.
- A **seller** who does not want to wait for someone to pay the full $180 ask can accept the $168 bid and sell right now.


The spread is where deals get made. A tight spread means the market strongly agrees on price. A wide spread means there is room to negotiate, in both directions.


## Why This Is Better for Buyers


On a fixed-price listing, your only move to save money is to hunt for a cheaper listing. On Misprint, you have another lever: place a bid below the ask and let sellers come to you.


This works especially well for One Piece cards because the market moves fast and sellers value liquidity. Plenty of sellers would rather take a fair bid today than wait an unknown number of weeks for full ask. Buyers who bid patiently regularly pay meaningfully less than the lowest listed price, without ever "overpaying to be first."


## Why This Is Better for Sellers


The bid system means your card is generating interest even when nobody is ready to pay your full ask. Instead of a listing that just sits there, you can see real offers accumulating and accept one whenever the price works for you.


For One Piece specifically, where a card's price can shift quickly around reprints and tournament results, having live bids gives you a way to sell into demand on your terms. You are never forced to drop your ask blindly to attract attention; you can watch the bids and take the one that makes sense.


## Price History and Population Data Make the Prices Honest


A bid/ask market only works if both sides can price intelligently, so every One Piece listing on Misprint shows two things that most marketplaces bury or omit:


- **Full price history,** so you can see whether a card is climbing, flat, or rolling over, and price your bid or ask against reality instead of hope.
- **Population data** for graded cards, so you understand how scarce a given grade actually is. A PSA 10 with a tiny population is a very different card than one with thousands graded, and the price should reflect that.


Combined with cert verification on every graded listing, this is what makes bidding safe: you are placing offers on cards whose authenticity is confirmed and whose fair value is visible.


## How to Actually Use It


**As a buyer:**


1. Find the card and look at the lowest ask and the highest bid.
2. Check the price history to understand fair value.
3. If the ask feels high, place a bid somewhere in the spread. A bid near the ask is likely to be accepted quickly; a lower bid may take longer but can save you more.
4. Wait. Sellers see your bid and can accept it any time.


**As a seller:**


1. List at a competitive ask based on the price history.
2. Watch the incoming bids.
3. Accept a bid whenever the price works for you, or hold out for someone to buy at your ask.


## Our Take


The bid/ask model is the core of what makes Misprint different, and it fits One Piece cards especially well. The game's fast-moving, reprint-driven market rewards a system where buyers can name their price and sellers can sell into demand on their terms, all backed by visible price history, population data, and cert verification. For buyers it usually means paying less than the sticker price. For sellers it means real liquidity instead of a listing that just waits. Once you have used it, going back to take-it-or-leave-it pricing feels like a step backward.
