# Best Commander Precon Cards Worth Money

# Best Commander Precon Cards Worth Money


The chase cards that hid inside $35 preconstructed decks.


By Misprint Editorial | Published Aug 15, 2026 | 7 min read


*The chase cards that hid inside $35 preconstructed decks.*


Commander preconstructed decks were never supposed to be where the money was. They are the ready-to-play, buy-it-off-the-shelf product line: a hundred cards, a couple of foils, a price tag that has hovered around $35 to $45 for most of the product's life. Wizards of the Coast uses them as a printing press for reprints, which usually keeps individual card values low.


Except that every so often a precon hides a genuinely powerful new card that never got a Standard printing, only exists in Commander products, and quietly becomes a format staple. Those are the cards that make an old sealed precon worth more than its sticker price, and they are the ones collectors and players actually hunt for singles.


A word of caution before the list. Commander singles are the most reprint-exposed corner of the entire game. A card can sit at $80 for three years and then lose 70 percent of its value in a week when it shows up in a bonus sheet or a new Commander deck. Prices below are pulled from public trackers (mtgstocks, mtgprice, tcgplayer, mtggoldfish) in mid-2026, and aggregator numbers often run ahead of what cards actually sell for. Treat them as a snapshot, not a guarantee. For a broader view, our guide to[the most valuable Commander cards worth money](https://www.misprint.com/posts/most-valuable-commander-cards) covers the whole format, and[which Magic sets hold their value best](https://www.misprint.com/posts/which-magic-sets-hold-value-best) is worth a read before you buy anything as a hold.


Here are ten of the best, counting down to the single most valuable card ever to debut in a Commander precon.


## 10. Toxic Deluge (Commander 2013)


Toxic Deluge, Commander 2013. Art by Svetlin Velinov. Card image via Scryfall. Magic: The Gathering is © Wizards of the Coast.


Around $7 to $10. Toxic Deluge is one of the best black board wipes ever printed, a sweeper that dodges indestructible and regeneration by lowering toughness instead of dealing damage. It debuted in the Commander 2013 "Eternal Bargain" deck and once traded north of $40. Its current price is a perfect illustration of reprint risk: it has since appeared in Eternal Masters, Commander Collection: Black, Commander Masters, and multiple Commander decks, and the flood pushed the original printing down to single digits.


## 9. Flusterstorm (Commander 2011)


Flusterstorm, Commander 2011. Art by Erica Yang. Card image via Scryfall. Magic: The Gathering is © Wizards of the Coast.


Around $10 to $12. A one-mana counterspell with storm, Flusterstorm is a premium answer in cutthroat Commander and older eternal formats, prized for winning counter wars and protecting a key spell. It was a Commander 2011 original and spent years as a $30-plus card before Iconic Masters and later reprints softened it. Collectors who want the first-run copy still pay a small premium over the newer printings.


## 8. Dockside Extortionist (Commander 2019)


Dockside Extortionist, Commander 2019. Art by Forrest Imel. Card image via Scryfall. Magic: The Gathering is © Wizards of the Coast.


Around $10 to $12. Few cards tell the reprint-and-ban story better. Dockside makes a small army of Treasure tokens based on your opponents' nonland permanents, a routinely explosive turn-one to turn-three play that made it a $70-plus staple. It was banned in Commander in September 2024, and the price collapsed from the $80s toward $20. In 2026 it spiked again, tripling to around $30 on speculation tied to an Avatar: The Last Airbender reprint and unban chatter, before easing back to roughly $10 to $12. If you want a lesson in how policy moves Commander prices, this is it. Our piece on whether[Magic cards are a good investment](https://www.misprint.com/posts/are-magic-the-gathering-cards-a-good-investment) digs into exactly this kind of volatility.


## 7. Tymna the Weaver (Commander 2016)


Tymna the Weaver, Commander 2016. Art by Winona Nelson. Card image via Scryfall. Magic: The Gathering is © Wizards of the Coast.


Around $23 to $28. Tymna came out of Commander 2016's "Open Hostility" deck with the partner mechanic, and she became half of the most-played commander pairing in competitive Commander, usually alongside Thrasios, Triton Hero. Card draw tied to combat damage, on a cheap body, in white, has kept demand steady for a decade.


## 6. Atraxa, Praetors' Voice (Commander 2016)


Atraxa, Praetors' Voice, Commander 2016. Art by Victor Adame Minguez. Card image via Scryfall. Magic: The Gathering is © Wizards of the Coast.


Around $28 to $32. The face of Commander 2016's "Breed Lethality" deck, Atraxa is one of the most popular commanders ever printed, a four-color flier with vigilance, deathtouch, lifelink, and proliferate. She anchors superfriends and +1/+1 counter decks and has been a top-ranked commander on EDHREC for years. Multiple reprints keep the original around $30 rather than higher.


## 5. The Ur-Dragon (Commander 2017)


The Ur-Dragon, Commander 2017. Art by Jaime Jones. Card image via Scryfall. Magic: The Gathering is © Wizards of the Coast.


Around $30 to $37. The five-color dragon boss from Commander 2017's "Draconic Domination" deck, The Ur-Dragon powers the single most popular tribal archetype in the format. Eminence, cost reduction, and free card advantage every time a dragon attacks make it an evergreen build-around, and dragon typal decks are perennially in fashion.


## 4. Edgar Markov (Commander 2017)


Edgar Markov, Commander 2017. Art by Volkan Baǵa. Card image via Scryfall. Magic: The Gathering is © Wizards of the Coast.


Around $45. For years Edgar was the poster child for precon-exclusive value, a vampire commander from Commander 2017's "Vampiric Bloodlust" deck that climbed past $100 while never seeing a wide reprint. That changed when he was reprinted in Innistrad Remastered, which knocked the price down before it recovered into the mid-$40s. He remains one of the most-played commanders in the game.


## 3. Teferi's Protection (Commander 2017)


Teferi's Protection, Commander 2017. Art by Chase Stone. Card image via Scryfall. Magic: The Gathering is © Wizards of the Coast.


Around $43 to $52. A three-mana white instant that phases you out entirely, protecting your life total, permanents, and board from everything until your next turn. It is the premier fog-plus-fizzle effect in the format and answers combo kills, alpha strikes, and mass removal in one card. First printed in Commander 2017's "Arcane Wizardry" deck, it has stayed expensive despite reprints thanks to universal white demand.


## 2. Fierce Guardianship (Commander 2020)


Fierce Guardianship, Commander 2020. Art by Randy Vargas. Card image via Scryfall. Magic: The Gathering is © Wizards of the Coast.


Around $52 to $60. Effectively a free counterspell for any noncreature spell as long as you control your commander (which, in Commander, you usually do), Fierce Guardianship is arguably the most impactful blue card the precon line has ever produced. It debuted in the Ikoria-era Commander 2020 "Timeless Wisdom" deck. Even after reprints in Commander Masters and elsewhere it holds strong, because it slots into nearly every blue deck at the table.


## 1. Deflecting Swat (Commander 2020)


Deflecting Swat, Commander 2020. Art by Izzy. Card image via Scryfall. Magic: The Gathering is © Wizards of the Coast.


Around $64 to $72. The most valuable card ever to debut in a Commander precon, Deflecting Swat is the red half of the "free spell" cycle from Commander 2020's "Symbiotic Swarm" deck. For zero mana with a commander in play, it redirects any spell or ability, which lets it steal counterspells, hijack removal, and flip targeted kill spells back at the caster. Its combination of raw utility, red's smaller pool of good interaction, and heavy competitive play has kept it at the top of the precon value chart even through reprints.

The through-line here is that precon value lives and dies by playability and reprint status. A card is worth money because Commander players want a physical copy, and it stays worth money only until Wizards decides to print a few hundred thousand more. If you are buying these as holds rather than to play, watch reprint announcements closely, keep condition high, and consider grading the standout copies.
