# The Best Magic Cards for New Collectors Under $50

# The Best Magic Cards for New Collectors Under $50


Iconic cardboard, real history, none of it five figures.


By Misprint Editorial | Published Jul 16, 2026 | 7 min read


*Iconic cardboard, real history, none of it five figures.*


Starting a Magic collection does not require a Black Lotus or a five-figure budget. Some of the most recognizable cards in the game's thirty-plus year history sit comfortably under $50, and a few cost less than a fast-food lunch. The trick for a new collector is knowing which cheap cards actually matter: which ones carry real history, show up in decks people still play, or simply look gorgeous in a binder.


This list counts down ten cards that give you the most character, playability, or nostalgia for the money. Every price below is a rough market figure from mid-2026 aggregators like TCGplayer, Card Kingdom, and mtgdecks. Treat them as a starting point rather than gospel. Prices move week to week, and aggregator "market" numbers can run a little ahead of what cards actually sell for, so always sanity-check a recent sold listing before you buy.


A quick note on printings: almost every card here has been reprinted many times, and the cheap modern reprint plays identically to the pricey original. Chase the old border only if you want the look. Otherwise, the budget printing is the smart buy.


## #10. Counterspell


Counterspell, Tempest. Art by Stephen Daniele. Card image via Scryfall. Magic: The Gathering is © Wizards of the Coast.


The most famous two-word answer in the game. Counterspell has been printed since Alpha in 1993 and remains the plainest, cleanest "no" in Magic. Modern reprints run about $1 to $4, so a playset costs less than a booster pack. Original Alpha and Beta copies climb into the hundreds, but for a starter collection a common reprint like this 1997 Tempest printing delivers all the history for pocket change.


## #9. Lightning Bolt


Lightning Bolt, Magic 2010. Art by Christopher Moeller. Card image via Scryfall. Magic: The Gathering is © Wizards of the Coast.


If Counterspell is the definitive answer, Lightning Bolt is the definitive threat. Three damage for one red mana has been a benchmark of card design since 1993, and the card is still legal and played in Pioneer, Modern, and Commander. Common reprints from Magic 2010 and 2011 sit around $1 to $6. Christopher Rush illustrated the original 1993 Bolt and Beta copies command real money, but the modern printings shown here carry newer art and the same three-for-one punch at a fraction of the cost. You are buying the effect, not the rarity.


## #8. Birds of Paradise


Birds of Paradise, Magic 2012. Art by Marcelo Vignali. Card image via Scryfall. Magic: The Gathering is © Wizards of the Coast.


One of the best mana accelerants ever printed and, thanks to Mark Poole's original art, one of the prettiest cards in the early game. A one-mana creature that taps for any color has defined green ramp for decades. Reprints are wonderfully accessible: an 8th Edition copy runs a dollar or two, while this Magic 2012 printing sits around $12. Note that old-border copies have been quietly climbing as players chase the vintage look, so the nicest versions are worth grabbing sooner rather than later.


## #7. Serra Angel


Serra Angel, Revised Edition. Art by Douglas Shuler. Card image via Scryfall. Magic: The Gathering is © Wizards of the Coast.


Serra Angel is Magic royalty. She was the face of the game in the early years, powerful enough that she anchored decks and cameos across the brand for a generation. Revised and later reprints run about $2 to $8, which makes owning a piece of Magic's founding iconography almost trivially cheap. The original Douglas Shuler art, seen on this Revised copy, is the one to look for.


## #6. Shivan Dragon


Shivan Dragon, Revised Edition. Art by Melissa A. Benson. Card image via Scryfall. Magic: The Gathering is © Wizards of the Coast.


The first great dragon in Magic, and the card that taught a generation of kids what a "bomb" felt like. A 5/5 flyer with a firebreathing ability was the dream rare to open back in 1993, and it still radiates that starter-set romance. Reprints land around $2 to $6. Pair it with Serra Angel and you have two of the most nostalgic rares in the game for the price of a single booster.


## #5. Craterhoof Behemoth


Craterhoof Behemoth, Avacyn Restored. Art by Chris Rahn. Card image via Scryfall. Magic: The Gathering is © Wizards of the Coast.


Where the earlier entries lean on nostalgia, Craterhoof earns its place through raw utility. It is the premier "I win now" finisher in green Commander decks, turning a board of small creatures into a lethal alpha strike. It sat well above $50 for years, but a 2025 reprint in Tarkir: Dragonstorm softened the price to about $20 to $24. If you play Commander at all, this is one of the highest-impact cards you can own for the money.


## #4. Emrakul, the Aeons Torn


Emrakul, the Aeons Torn, Rise of the Eldrazi. Art by Mark Tedin. Card image via Scryfall. Magic: The Gathering is © Wizards of the Coast.


The original Eldrazi titan and one of the most feared cards in casual Magic. A 15/15 with flying, protection from colored spells, annihilator 6, and a free extra turn when you cast it, Emrakul is the definition of a game-ending threat. Copies run about $25 to $32. For a new collector it doubles as a conversation piece: this is the card people point to when they talk about Magic going big.


## #3. Atraxa, Praetors' Voice


Atraxa, Praetors' Voice, Commander 2016. Art by Victor Adame Minguez. Card image via Scryfall. Magic: The Gathering is © Wizards of the Coast.


Few cards have launched more Commander decks than Atraxa. A four-color angel with flying, vigilance, deathtouch, lifelink, and a proliferate engine, she has been one of the most-built commanders in the format since her 2016 debut. Expect to pay about $35 to $40. If you are curious how far a single deck can carry a card's demand, Atraxa is a case study, and a natural next read is our roundup of[the most valuable Commander cards](https://www.misprint.com/posts/most-valuable-commander-cards) .


## #2. Sword of Fire and Ice


Sword of Fire and Ice, Darksteel. Art by Mark Zug. Card image via Scryfall. Magic: The Gathering is © Wizards of the Coast.


The best of the artifact "Swords" cycle and one of the most efficient equipment ever designed. It grants protection from two colors, deals two damage, and draws a card every time the equipped creature connects. At about $43 to $50 it is the priciest card on this list and sits right at the budget ceiling, so shop the cheaper Modern Masters and Double Masters printings if you want a little breathing room. It is worth stretching for: this card improves almost any deck it touches.


## #1. Sol Ring


Sol Ring, Commander Legends. Art by Mike Bierek. Card image via Scryfall. Magic: The Gathering is © Wizards of the Coast.


The single best two dollars a new Magic collector can spend. Sol Ring is a one-mana artifact that taps for two colorless, a rate so absurd it is banned across most competitive formats and auto-included in nearly every Commander deck. It has been reprinted so many times that a clean copy costs about $2 to $4. Nothing else on this list combines iconic status, universal playability, and a rock-bottom price so completely. Buy a few. You will use them.

You get the founding icons, the most feared casual threats, and the Commander staples that people actually play. That mix, rather than a single expensive card, is what makes a collection feel alive.
