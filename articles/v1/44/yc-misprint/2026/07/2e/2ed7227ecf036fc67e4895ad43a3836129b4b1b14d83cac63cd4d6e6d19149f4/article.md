# The Most Expensive Gyarados Cards

# The Most Expensive Gyarados Cards


From the Lake of Rage to the Gold Star tier, counting down what the big red sea serpent is actually worth.


By Misprint Editorial | Published Jul 19, 2026 | 6 min read


*The two most expensive Gyarados cards are both red. That's not a coincidence; the shiny red Gyarados is the original "shiny" in Pokemon lore, and the market has never forgotten it.*


Gyarados has one of the cleanest value stories in the hobby. The Lake of Rage red Gyarados from the Gen 2 games became the franchise's shiny mascot, and the two cards that depict it, the Gold Star and Shining Gyarados, tower over everything else in the catalog. Beneath them sits a deep vintage bench that the 2026 anniversary rally has been repricing month by month.


This countdown ranks the ten most expensive English Gyarados cards by raw near-mint value as of early July 2026, with graded prices in their own section. Full recap table at the bottom.


## 10. Dark Gyarados Holo, 1st Edition Team Rocket #8/82 (2000): ~$225


The villain-era classic. Around $225 raw for a clean 1st Edition, with PSA 10s near $3,300. One modern wrinkle to know: the 2021 Celebrations Classic Collection reprinted this exact card, and that reprint is everywhere (it's one of the most-graded Gyarados cards, period). The 2000 Wizards original is the one with the value.


[View on Misprint Dark Gyarados-Holo 1st Edition (8) 2000 Pokemon Rocket Market Price: $161.20](https://www.misprint.com/card/527166)


## 9. Gyarados Holo, Shadowless Base Set #6/102 (1999): ~$250


The middle rung of the Base Set edition ladder. Shadowless copies (no 1st Edition stamp, no drop shadow on the art frame) trade around $250 raw with recent sales up to $299, and the PSA 10 population is a startlingly low 61, which puts gems at $5,600. Watch for "1999-2000 copyright" copies sold as Shadowless; they aren't.


[View on Misprint Gyarados-Holo Shadowless (6) 1999 Pokemon Game Market Price: $121.04](https://www.misprint.com/card/702169)


## 8. Gyarados delta species, EX Holon Phantoms #8/110 (2006): ~$260


The affordable red one. This holo depicts the shiny red Gyarados and comes from the same set as the Gold Star, which makes it both a great budget pickup and a persistent scam vector: sellers list it as "the Gold Star" constantly. It's a lovely $260 card; it's just not a $2,500 one. The real Gold Star has a gold star symbol after the name and is numbered 102/110.


[View on Misprint Gyarados-Holo (8) 2006 Pokemon EX Holon Phantoms Market Price: $262.11](https://www.misprint.com/card/651550)


## 7. Misty's Gyarados Holo, 1st Edition Gym Challenge #13/132 (2000): ~$300


The Gym era's Gyarados, and a quiet mover: recent mid-grade raw sales at $275 to $350 suggest true near-mint is now $300-plus, and PSA 10s printed twice in June around $3,900 to $4,100. Character-owned Gym cards keep benefiting from the same nostalgia current lifting Sabrina's Gengar.


[View on Misprint Misty's Gyarados Holo-1st Edition (13) 2000 Pokemon Gym Challenge Market Price: $360.24](https://www.misprint.com/card/829872)


## 6. Gyarados Prerelease Promo, XY Black Star #XY60 (2015): ~$400


The sleeper promo. Handed out at Ancient Origins prerelease events, it sells around $400 to $430 raw with PSA 10s recently at $1,600-plus. Two versions exist: the regular stamp and a much scarcer STAFF stamp that carries a hefty premium. Contrary to what listings imply, it is not a shiny; the value is pure scarcity.


[View on Misprint Gyarados-Holo Ancient Origins Prerelease (XY60) 2015 Pokemon XY Black Star Promos Market Price: $404.37](https://www.misprint.com/card/2223789)


## 5. Shining Gyarados, Unlimited Neo Revelation #65/64 (2001): ~$650


The people's red Gyarados. The unstamped version of the famous secret rare trades around $590 to $680 raw, with PSA 10s near $9,200. And no, "65/64" is not a typo: Shining cards were numbered past the set size on purpose, the original secret rares.


[View on Misprint Shining Gyarados-Holo (65) 2001 Pokemon Neo Revelation Market Price: $95.91](https://www.misprint.com/card/863812)


## 4. Gyarados Holo, 1st Edition Base Set #6/102 (1999): ~$800


The original. First-print Base Set Gyarados runs $600 to $1,000 raw depending on condition, and its graded market is sprinting: the PSA 10 guide value jumped about $3,500 in the past 30 days to roughly $16,800 on a population of 89. The anniversary rally at work, in real time.


[View on Misprint Gyarados-Holo 1st Edition (6) 1999 Pokemon Game Market Price: $30.01](https://www.misprint.com/card/544032)


## 3. Gyarados Holo, Skyridge #H10/144 (2003): ~$1,200


The graded story of the Gyarados market this year. Raw copies trade around $1,100 to $1,300 (thin and volatile), but PSA 10s have sold three times since late April at $17,650, $18,100, and $20,446. Skyridge is the scarcest English set of its era, the H-series holos are its premium slots, and the number trap is vicious: the non-holo and reverse holo #11/144 are worth a tiny fraction and routinely get passed off as H10.


[View on Misprint Gyarados-Holo (H10) 2003 Pokemon Skyridge](https://www.misprint.com/card/847885)


## 2. Shining Gyarados, 1st Edition Neo Revelation #65/64 (2001): ~$2,300


The card that made "shiny" a collecting category. The 1st Edition Shining Gyarados carries the best verified raw datapoint on this whole list, an eBay sale at $2,318 in June, with PSA 10 estimates near $29,000. The 1st Edition stamp multiplies the unlimited version's price roughly four-fold, so check under the left corner of the art before you pay stamped money.


[View on Misprint Shining Gyarados-Holo (65) 2001 Pokemon Neo Revelation 1st Edition](https://www.misprint.com/card/850294)


## 1. Gyarados Gold Star delta species, EX Holon Phantoms #102/110 (2006): ~$2,500


The king of the red serpents. The Gold Star Gyarados combines the 2004-2007 era's rarest rarity with the franchise's most famous shiny, and its market has a peculiarity worth understanding: price guides quote raw copies around $1,100 to $1,600, yet a PSA 1 slab, the worst possible grade, sold for $2,400 in June, and PSA 5s bring over $4,000. The gap is counterfeit risk: raw copies trade at a fraud discount, while anything authenticated commands a premium. A verified near-mint raw copy realistically clears $2,500, PSA 8s have touched five figures, and the tiny-population PSA 10 last confirmed around $18,000. If you buy this card raw, authentication isn't optional; our guide to[spotting fake Pokemon cards](https://www.misprint.com/posts/how-to-spot-fake-pokemon-cards) covers the checks, and it sits alongside its peers in our[most expensive Gold Star cards](https://www.misprint.com/posts/most-expensive-gold-star-cards) roundup.

## The Graded Kings


Four Gyarados cards are trading in or near five figures slabbed, and three of them got there this spring.


The Skyridge H10 leads on verified 2026 sales: $20,446, $18,100, and $17,650 between late April and early June. The Shining Gyarados 1st Edition sits around $29,000 in PSA 10 by guide value. The Gold Star's ladder is the strangest and most telling: ~$18,000 for the PSA 10, $10,999 for a PSA 8 in June, and $10,000 for a CGC 9, five figures for grades that would be consolation prizes on most cards. And the Base Set 1st Edition holo crossed $16,800 with the fastest 30-day gain of the group.


A caution that applies doubly here: this corner of the market circulates some flashy fake datapoints (an "$85,000 PSA 8" among them) that don't survive verification. The numbers above come from confirmed sales; treat anything wilder with suspicion. And if you're sitting on a clean raw copy of any of these, the[grading math](https://www.misprint.com/posts/is-it-worth-grading-pokemon-cards) has rarely been this favorable.


## Buyer Traps to Avoid


- **Red doesn't mean Gold Star.** The $260 Gyarados delta species is red and holo too. The Gold Star is #102/110 with a gold star after the name, and it's one of the most counterfeited cards in the hobby. Graded or verified-seller only.
- **Japanese versions are separate markets.** The Japanese Gold Star (#008) and Japanese Neo 3 Shining Gyarados are worth a fraction of the English cards and frequently get listed without the language disclosed.
- **H10 or nothing on Skyridge.** The holo is H10/144; the #11/144 non-holo and reverse are cheap lookalikes.
- **The Celebrations Dark Gyarados is a 2021 reprint** , not the Team Rocket original, and it floods search results.
- **Learn the Base Set ladder.** 1st Edition stamp, then Shadowless, then Unlimited; a "1999-2000" copyright line means the late print, not Shadowless.

## The Bottom Line


The Gyarados market is a shiny-red duopoly with a vintage bench that woke up this spring: the Gold Star and Shining Gyarados own the top, Skyridge and Base Set are printing five-figure slabs beneath them, and one modern sleeper (the BREAKpoint gold Gyarados EX, $140 raw and suddenly $3,300 in PSA 10 this June) hints the repricing isn't done. The whole catalog rewards exactly one skill: reading the card in front of you, its number, its stamp, its language, its holo pattern, before believing its price tag.
