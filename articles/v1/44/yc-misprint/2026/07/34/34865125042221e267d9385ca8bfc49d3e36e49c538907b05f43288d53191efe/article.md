# Universes Beyond Sets, Ranked by Collector Value

# Universes Beyond Sets, Ranked by Collector Value


From Assassin's Creed to the $2 million One Ring, here is how the crossover sets stack up.


By Misprint Editorial | Published Jul 16, 2026 | 7 min read


*From Assassin's Creed to the $2 million One Ring, here is how the crossover sets stack up.*


Universes Beyond started as a curiosity and turned into the engine of Magic's collectible market. What began with a handful of Godzilla alternate arts in 2020 now includes full Standard-legal expansions, Commander-only releases, and the single most expensive Magic card ever pulled from a pack. Not every crossover lands the same way with collectors, though. Some minted instant grails, others crashed within weeks of release, and a couple are quietly appreciating because they went out of print.


Here is how the major Universes Beyond releases stack up by collector value, counting down to the set that rewrote the rules. Prices below track public aggregators like TCGplayer, mtgstocks, and pricecharting as of mid-2026. Treat them as a moving target: aggregator numbers can run ahead of what cards actually sell for, and Universes Beyond singles are especially volatile in the weeks around release.


## 7. Assassin's Creed


Released as a compact "Beyond" booster set on July 5, 2024, Assassin's Creed is the clearest example of a crossover that collectors mostly shrugged at. The chase cards never developed real ceilings. Sword of Feast and Famine, a reprint of an already-expensive Commander staple, sits around $35, and the set's marquee commander, Edward Kenway, spiked past $30 in the weeks after launch before settling back near $7 for the regular printing. Everything else falls off fast.


Ezio Auditore da Firenze, Assassin's Creed. Art by Fajareka Setiawan. Card image via Scryfall. Magic: The Gathering is © Wizards of the Coast.


The set does have serialized cards and a slick showcase frame, but the small print run of premium product was met with tepid demand. If you want a cheap way into a Universes Beyond set with recognizable art, this is it. As an investment, it has been flat to down since launch.


## 6. Warhammer 40,000


Magic's first big Universes Beyond Commander product, released in October 2022, gets more interesting with age. The four preconstructed decks (Tyranid Swarm, Necron Dynasties, Forces of the Imperium, and Tivadar's Crusade) went out of print, and sealed copies have crept back up. Sealed Tyranid Swarm decks trade in the $330 to $370 range in 2026, well above their original retail.


The Swarmlord, Warhammer 40,000 Commander. Art by Antonio José Manzanedo. Card image via Scryfall. Magic: The Gathering is © Wizards of the Coast.


The value here is concentrated in sealed product and in a few reprints that Commander players always want. As singles, the standout cards are role-players rather than trophies, so the collector story is really about the scarcity of the physical decks rather than any one chase card.


## 5. Fallout


The Fallout Commander decks (March 8, 2024) delivered one genuinely clever chase idea: serial-numbered Bobbleheads, seven of them, one for each S.P.E.C.I.A.L. stat, limited to 500 copies apiece in double-rainbow foil. Those serialized cards command real money, with Intelligence Bobblehead leading the pack around $590 and up.


The Wise Mothman, Fallout. Art by Sergei Leoluch Panin. Card image via Scryfall. Magic: The Gathering is © Wizards of the Coast.


Outside the serialized slots, prices deflated hard after launch. Ravages of War, a long-scarce reprint that players badly wanted (its only prior printing was in Portal Three Kingdoms), holds near $60 in its borderless version, and the Pip-Boy and Vault Boy treatments on cards like Arcane Signet stay popular. The lesson of Fallout is the lesson of most Commander-set crossovers: the numbered cards hold, the bulk mythics do not.


## 4. Doctor Who


The four Doctor Who Commander decks (October 2023) produced one of the more emotionally resonant chase structures in the whole line. The serialized double-rainbow-foil Doctors, with a TARDIS showcase treatment pulled only from Collector Boosters and limited to roughly 500 copies each, are the grails here.


The Tenth Doctor, Doctor Who. Art by Luisa J. Preissler. Card image via Scryfall. Magic: The Gathering is © Wizards of the Coast.


Early copies of the serialized Tenth Doctor were listed as high as $5,000 in the launch frenzy; the market has since settled it closer to $635, with the occasional optimistic listing far above that. Regular standouts like Cyber Conversion, a genuinely strong removal spell, keep the set relevant in Commander, but the serialized Doctors remain the sentimental blue chips of the set. Doctor Who rewards collectors who love the property.


## 3. Marvel's Spider-Man


Spider-Man (September 26, 2025) is the crossover that split the fanbase. Many competitive players consider it one of the weakest Standard sets in memory, yet it still became one of Magic's best-selling sets on the strength of collector and speculator demand for Collector Booster displays.


The Soul Stone, Marvel's Spider-Man. Art by Volkan Baǵa. Card image via Scryfall. Magic: The Gathering is © Wizards of the Coast.


The value lives in the Borderless Marvel Universe treatments and premium mythics. The Soul Stone is the anchor: its borderless foil has traded well into four figures, while even the standard version sits above $100. Best-selling chase cards like Venom, Evil Unleashed, Spider-Punk, and Spider-Rex, Daring Dino round out the demand list. The gap between gameplay reception and sales makes Spider-Man a fascinating case study in how[Universes Beyond changed Magic's collectible market](https://www.misprint.com/posts/how-universes-beyond-changed-magic-market) : the cardboard sells whether or not the deck wins.


## 2. Final Fantasy


Final Fantasy (June 13, 2025) is, by the numbers, the biggest Magic release ever. Hasbro's CEO confirmed it as the best-selling set of all time, noting it moved around $200 million of product in a single day, a milestone that took Lord of the Rings six months to reach. It was also the first Universes Beyond set to be Standard-legal, which meant tournament demand stacked on top of collector demand.


Sephiroth, Fabled SOLDIER // Sephiroth, One-Winged Angel, Final Fantasy. Art by Wisnu Tan. Card image via Scryfall. Magic: The Gathering is © Wizards of the Coast.


The chase pyramid is deep. At the very top sits the serialized golden Traveling Chocobo, seeded into just 77 English Collector Boosters worldwide, one of the scarcest cards Wizards has ever printed; individual copies have sold for tens of thousands of dollars. Below that, surge-foil borderless characters run hot: Sephiroth, Fabled SOLDIER surge foils have climbed toward $849 on scarcity, while the Lightning, Army of One borderless surge foil trades around $130 to $200. Because the set sold so heavily, most commons and uncommons are cheap, but the premium foils have proven resilient. This is the crossover to study if you want to know[which Magic sets hold their value best](https://www.misprint.com/posts/which-magic-sets-hold-value-best) .


## 1. The Lord of the Rings: Tales of Middle-earth


Nothing else is close on prestige. LOTR (June 2023) was Magic's first full Universes Beyond expansion, and it produced the most expensive Magic card in history: the 1-of-1 serialized The One Ring, pulled from a pack, graded PSA 9, and sold to Post Malone for a reported $2 million.


The One Ring, The Lord of the Rings: Tales of Middle-earth. Art by Veli Nyström. Card image via Scryfall. Magic: The Gathering is © Wizards of the Coast.


That single card defined the entire serialized-chase era, but the set's value goes deeper. The standard borderless The One Ring still trades around $100 to $180 depending on foiling, years after release. The serialized Sol Ring variants (Elven, Dwarven, and Human) were pulled from Collector Boosters, with print runs (300 Elven, 700 Dwarven, and 900 Human in foil) nodding to the Rings of Power poem, and the rarest Elven copies fetch four figures. LOTR was the best-selling set in Magic history until Final Fantasy took the crown, and it remains the benchmark. ## What is coming next


The 2026 calendar is stacked with crossovers that could reshuffle this list: a full Marvel Super Heroes set covering the Avengers roster in June, The Hobbit in August, Star Trek in November, and a Teenage Mutant Ninja Turtles release earlier in the year. Whether any of them produces a grail on the level of The One Ring is the open question. If you want the wider context before the next wave lands, start with[Universes Beyond, Explained](https://www.misprint.com/posts/universes-beyond-explained) .


A note on tracking all this: because so much Universes Beyond value now sits in graded and serialized copies, the useful signal is real sold data, not sticker prices. Tools like Misprint's graded-market tracking exist precisely because aggregator listings on these cards can drift far from where they actually change hands. When a serialized Doctor is listed at $1,500 and last sold at $635, the sold number is the one that matters.
