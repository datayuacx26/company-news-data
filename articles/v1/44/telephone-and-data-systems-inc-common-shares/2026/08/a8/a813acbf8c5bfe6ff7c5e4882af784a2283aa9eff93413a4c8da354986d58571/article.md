# Protect your home network from risky streaming devices

Everyone loves a great deal, especially when it comes to home entertainment. But if a streaming device promises unlimited TV channels and movies for a one-time fee, it may come with hidden risks that go far beyond copyright concerns and future product uncertainty.


[A recent investigation](https://www.plume.com/newsroom/plume-security-labs-exposes-hidden-proxy-network-inside-superbox-devices) from Plume found that some Android-based streaming devices marketed as “SuperBox” products may contain software that can silently turn a customer’s home internet connection into part of a residential proxy network. According to Plume, these devices can route unknown third-party internet traffic through a subscriber’s home network without the user’s knowledge or consent.


Researchers reported finding traffic that included login credentials, verification codes, and activity designed to bypass online security protections. They also found that some of these devices can install unsafe software, potentially exposing users to additional cybersecurity threats. The FBI also[recently warned consumers](https://www.fbi.gov/investigate/cyber/alerts/2025/home-internet-connected-devices-facilitate-criminal-activity) about similar compromised internet-connected devices that can become part of botnets or residential proxy services used for criminal activity.


**How to protect yourself**


When dealing with an internet-connected device, keep these tips in mind:


- Be cautious of devices that advertise “free” access to premium TV channels, movies, or subscription services.
- Purchase streaming devices from reputable manufacturers and retailers.
- Download apps only from official app stores.
- Keep device software and firmware up to date.
- Watch out for unusual internet activity or performance issues on your home network.
- Avoid devices that require you to disable security features.
