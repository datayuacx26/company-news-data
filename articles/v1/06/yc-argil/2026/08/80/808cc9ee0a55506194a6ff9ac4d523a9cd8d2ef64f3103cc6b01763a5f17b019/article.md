The best time to post on TikTok on a Friday is between 3 p.m. and 6 p.m. in your audience's local time. That band is the only unbroken stretch where every hour is backed by at least two of the five largest posting time studies. A second, weaker spike lands around 8 p.m.


## The best time to post on TikTok on a Friday


Two of the most widely cited datasets land inside that band.[Sprout Social](https://sproutsocial.com/insights/best-times-to-post-on-tiktok/) , which analyzed 2 billion engagements across 307,000 profiles between November 27, 2025 and February 27, 2026, puts Friday at 3 to 5 p.m. local time.[Hootsuite](https://blog.hootsuite.com/best-time-to-post-on-tiktok/) , working from over 1 million posts localized across 118 countries, lands on 4 to 6 p.m.


Treat it as a band. These samples pool every country and niche on the platform, so what they describe is the general shape of a Friday afternoon, not a single winning minute.


Every window on this page is set by your audience's clock. A London creator publishing at 4 p.m. GMT for a US audience reaches the East Coast at 11 a.m., which falls well outside the Friday band.


## What five TikTok posting studies actually say about Friday


Across the five most widely cited original datasets, Friday's recommended peak lands anywhere from 8 a.m. to 10 p.m. Each study below is listed with its Friday call and the sample it came from.


- **Sprout Social** puts Friday at 3 to 5 p.m. local time, from 2 billion engagements across 307,000 profiles belonging to 30,000 customers, measured November 2025 to February 2026
- [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/best-time-to-post-on-tiktok/) ** **puts Friday at 6 p.m., then 10 p.m. and 8 p.m., from 7.1 million posts with time zones mathematically normalized
- **Hootsuite** puts Friday at 4 to 6 p.m., from over 1 million posts localized across 118 countries, published November 2025
- [SocialPilot](https://www.socialpilot.co/blog/best-time-to-post-on-tiktok) puts Friday at 8 a.m. and 1 to 3 p.m., from 700,000 posts across more than 50,000 accounts during 2025
- [RecurPost](https://recurpost.com/tiktok-scheduler/best-time-to-post-tiktok/) puts Friday at 10 a.m., 5 p.m. and 8 p.m., from more than 2 million scheduled posts, reported in EST


[Influencer Marketing Hub](https://influencermarketinghub.com/best-times-to-post-on-tiktok/) is worth separating out. It publishes a Friday recommendation of 9 a.m., 1 to 3 p.m., and 6 to 8 p.m., but it compiles those figures from Buffer, Hootsuite, Sprout Social, and SocialPilot rather than collecting its own, and it discloses no sample size. Read it as a synthesis of the studies above.


Count how many of the five back each individual hour and a shape appears. 3 p.m. has two studies behind it, 4 p.m. has two, 5 p.m. has three, and 6 p.m. has two. That makes 3 p.m. to 6 p.m. the only unbroken run where no hour drops below two independent datasets. The one meaningful cluster outside it sits at 8 p.m., where Buffer and RecurPost agree.


Outside that band the support thins out fast. 8 a.m., 10 a.m., 1 p.m., and 2 p.m. each rest on a single study. 5 a.m. still circulates as a Friday slot and appears in none of these five datasets.


## Why the five studies disagree


Four differences in method explain most of the spread.


Time zone handling is the biggest. Sprout Social reports in local time, Buffer normalizes time zones mathematically before aggregating, RecurPost reports in EST, and SocialPilot describes its timings as timezone agnostic.


Then there is what each one counts. RecurPost's numbers come from scheduled posts, which captures when people find it convenient to queue content as much as when audiences are awake. Sprout Social counts engagements. Buffer counts posts.


Each dataset is also drawn from that company's own customer base, which quietly decides whose Friday you are reading. Sprout Social's 30,000 customers skew toward brands and agencies with dedicated social teams. A solo creator's audience does not behave like a mid market brand's audience, and neither behaves like TikTok as a whole.


The measurement windows differ too. SocialPilot covers most of 2025 while Sprout Social covers a three month slice ending in February 2026.


Our read is to trust the overlap. Five companies counting different things on different clocks each name at least one Friday window reaching into the 3 p.m. to 6 p.m. band, and that agreement carries more weight than any one company's precise number.

## How posting time actually works


Posting time works through exactly one mechanism, and understanding it changes how much effort the question deserves.


In[TikTok's own description of the For You ranking system](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/en-us/how-tiktok-recommends-videos-for-you) , videos are ranked by how likely each user is to find them interesting, using signals such as whether a video is watched through to the end, plus likes, comments, and shares, none of which is a clock. Posting time acts on those signals indirectly, by changing how many of your followers are awake and scrolling when the video first appears.


The consequence is that a video which fails to hold attention fails at 3 p.m. on a Friday exactly as it fails at 4 a.m. on a Tuesday, because the signals TikTok ranks on are the same either way. Timing multiplies a video that already earns attention. We have made the same point in our[breakdown of what actually moves TikTok views](https://www.argil.ai/blog/how-to-get-more-views-on-tiktok) , where early engagement quality does the heavy lifting.


The decision rule we would give a creator with limited time is an ordering of effort. Hook quality first, publishing volume second, posting time third. The opening three seconds decide whether anyone stays for the rest of the video. Once your videos clear that bar, moving into the Friday afternoon band is a free adjustment worth making.


## How to find your own best Friday posting time


Your own follower data settles this question for your account, and collecting enough of it takes about three weeks.


Start with the data TikTok already gives you. Switch to a creator or business account, open TikTok Analytics, go to the Followers tab, and find Most Active Times. That view shows when your specific followers are on the app, broken down by day.


Those figures carry one quirk worth knowing about. TikTok Analytics timestamps everything in UTC, so each peak needs converting before it means anything where you sit. Read a 10 p.m. peak from Los Angeles and the hour you are actually looking at is mid afternoon Pacific, a completely different recommendation.


Then run a real test, because follower activity and video performance are related without being identical. Hold everything else constant: same video style, same length, and same hook structure. Publish two consecutive Fridays at 3 p.m. and two at 6 p.m. Compare average watch time and completion rate, since a single breakout video distorts raw view counts at low follower numbers.


Four data points give you a starting slot built on your own followers instead of an average pooled from hundreds of thousands of accounts across every niche on the platform. ## The lever that beats Friday timing


Posting frequency has been measured across a dataset as large as any of the timing studies, and unlike the timing effect, it comes with a number attached.


[Buffer's frequency study](https://buffer.com/resources/how-often-should-you-post-on-tiktok/) analyzed 11.4 million posts from more than 150,000 accounts using a fixed effects model that compares each account against itself over time, which controls for the obvious objection that bigger accounts simply post more. Measured against posting once a week, accounts posting 2 to 5 times a week got up to 17% more views per post. At 6 to 10 times a week that rose to 29%, and past 11 posts a week to 34%.


Sit those numbers next to the timing question. No study on this page puts a figure on what a better posting hour is worth, so nobody can tell you the size of that gain. The frequency gain is on the record, and it applies to every post you publish.

## FAQ


### Is Friday a good day to post on TikTok?


Friday is the one weekday where Buffer and Hootsuite actually agree. Buffer's 6 p.m. call sits inside Hootsuite's 4 to 6 p.m. window, which is not the case from Monday through Thursday, where the two datasets land hours apart. Friday is not the strongest day in Buffer's breakdown of 7.1 million posts, and it is not the weakest either. What it does have is unusually stable guidance, so you can act on it without waiting for a tiebreaker.


### What is the worst time to post on TikTok on a Friday?


The thinnest support across the five studies sits in the early morning hours and late at night past roughly 10 p.m. Only Buffer lists a 10 p.m. Friday window at all, and no study in this set places a Friday recommendation between midnight and 6 a.m. Steer clear of those hours on a Friday unless your own TikTok Analytics shows your followers active then.


### How often should you post on TikTok?


Start from the number you can still hit in week four, not the number that looks best in a study. Buffer's analysis of 11.4 million posts found views per post rising at every step up in cadence, from 17% for 2 to 5 posts a week to 29% at 6 to 10 and 34% past 11. The increments shrink as you climb, from a 17 point gain at the first step to 5 more past 11 posts, which is why Buffer names 2 to 5 a week as the best return on the effort.


### Does posting time actually matter on TikTok?


It matters as an input you set once, not a weekly decision. Publishing while more of your followers are already scrolling puts a bigger pool of viewers in front of the video in its first hour, which is when TikTok collects the watch and completion data it ranks on. Precision is the limit here. Five studies covering more than 10 million posts between them cannot narrow Friday past a three hour band, so treat the best time to post on TikTok on a Friday as a range you schedule against and leave it alone after that.
