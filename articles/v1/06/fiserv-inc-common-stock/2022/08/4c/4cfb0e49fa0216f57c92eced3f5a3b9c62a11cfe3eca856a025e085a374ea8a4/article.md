### Introduction


As we progress further into the future, newer and newer technologies are being created and used in the financial technology sector. While the transfer of goods and services between merchants and customers has always existed, it’s the method of how things are exchanged that has always been changing. As more and more people are getting their hands on desktop and mobile devices, the number of transactions through digital platforms is set to increase substantially. To get ahead of the curve, the interns for the TOS team at Fiserv spent their summer developing a prototype for a new potential service, a pay-by-link solution.


### **What is Pay-By-Link?**


Pay-By-Link is a system to allow merchants to generate payments and easily send them out to a customer without a point of sale. The customer can then fulfill the payments from a personal device. This allows for a cheaper payment processing solution for the merchant. Customers will similarly appreciate the solution for its simplicity and straightforward nature.

### **Technical Experience**


While building project, we ran into several large learning curves but were able to get through them efficiently. A majority of us had little to no experience working with JavaScript and React and we encountered new technologies like Yugabyte DB, GraphQL, Next.js, etc. Learning all of the new tech did take a great effort for us to do but by the end of building the application, we were proficient. We found the curve to be fruitful and we gained a much larger understanding of what it’s like to build in a corporate setting.


We were also able to use Fiserv Developer Studio and integrate the test environment of Fiserv’s CommerceHub to build out our payment functionality. Using the company’s APIs to create something new fascinated us and showed us the value a resource like this had. Our project is a great example of how Fiserv products can be used to build applications even internally.


As it is with any feat, we all encountered many challenges. We found that Next.js has very specific mechanisms sometimes that are very different from React.js. We also struggled trying to set up and connect to Yugabyte DB. We found that the Yugabyte DB documentation is outdated which made setting up a little difficult. However, we were able to connect using the Docker image they provided on their website.


One of the greatest challenges was actually communication between team members and increasing modularity of the code. Keeping the code organized when you’re starting from absolutely no code base is no easy feat. We ended up mastering the art of communication and coordination by the end of the internship but it took a lot of diligent team work to make this happen.


### **Design Experience**


From a design perspective, we wanted to create a product that boosts convenience and efficiency. We conducted extensive research from competitive analysis, interviews, and multiple user testings to deliver an MVP that fulfills user’s priorities. Throughout our design process, we had to recognize what features and designs were MVP and filter out those that were not, while still ensuring we are meeting all the wants and needs of users. We took on the task of designing our project for two different platforms, mobile and desktop. As a design team of two, we designed one platform each so we had to make sure we were communicating all ideas to nail unification across all designs.


We started off the design process with multiple sketch iterations, that followed with multiple lo-fi and mid-fi iterations. Iterations were user tested to narrow down designs, filter features, and to establish a concrete user flow. We ensured user friendly designs with easy navigation and clear features, and a productive but simple user flow for an effortless user experience.


### **Overall Experience**


All the interns can wholehearted agree when we say that, initially, it was difficult to communicate, coordinate, and work together when all of us are in two different time zones and four different locations. We had to learn to communicate well and prioritize tasks while also learning to work in a corporate environment. Working together as a team of four engineers and two designers was also something we had to figure out but were able to perfect after a couple sprints.


Regardless of roadblocks during our project, we all supported one another through all our ideas and questions that were communicated. Our mentors were also there to support and guide us through all of our successes and bumps. This project not only prepared us for what real-life work has in store for us but also to value and respect each other’s different skill set.
