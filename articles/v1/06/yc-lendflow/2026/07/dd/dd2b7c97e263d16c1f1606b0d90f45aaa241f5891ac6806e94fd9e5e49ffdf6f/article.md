### What is embedded finance


The best embedded finance provider depends on what you're trying to build. If your priority is payment routing, Stripe and Adyen lead the pack. For card issuing, Marqeta dominates. Banking infrastructure points toward Unit or Treasury Prime. And for SMB lending specifically, platforms like Lendflow offer[multi-lender orchestration](https://www.lendflow.com/post/multi-lender-orchestration-guide) that general-purpose providers don't match.


Embedded finance means integrating financial services directly into non-financial apps and platforms. Rather than sending users to a separate bank or lender, brands offer payments, loans, or accounts right inside the product experience.


You've probably used embedded finance without thinking about it. Buy-now-pay-later at checkout, instant payouts for rideshare drivers, and working capital loans inside accounting software all qualify. The financial product feels like part of the app rather than a detour to somewhere else.

### Embedded finance vs banking as a service


Banking as a Service (BaaS) and embedded finance get used interchangeably, but they're different layers of the same stack. BaaS refers to the[fintech infrastructure](https://www.lendflow.com/post/fintech-infrastructure) —APIs, banking licenses, and sponsor bank relationships that make financial products legally and technically possible. Embedded finance is what the end user actually touches.


One way to think about it: BaaS is the plumbing, embedded finance is the faucet. A platform might pull BaaS infrastructure from one provider while building a custom embedded experience on top. Or it might choose an all-in-one solution that handles both layers together.

### Types of embedded finance


Most providers specialize in one or two categories rather than covering everything. Knowing the landscape helps narrow down which platforms fit your use case.


#### Embedded payments


Payment processing is the most mature category. Embedded payments let platforms accept cards, ACH transfers, and digital wallets without redirecting users elsewhere. E-commerce sites, marketplaces, and SaaS platforms commonly embed payment acceptance as a baseline feature.


#### Embedded lending


Embedded lending brings loans, lines of credit, or installment plans directly into non-financial platforms. SMB lending is growing particularly fast in this space —


[vertical software companies](https://www.lendflow.com/post/vertical-saas-embedded-lending-revenue-growth) , with platforms like Shopify Capital purchasing


[$4.2 billion in merchant financing](https://www.pymnts.com/loans/2026/platforms-expand-smb-lending-as-working-capital-demand-rises/) in 2025 alone.


[Vertical software companies](https://www.lendflow.com/post/vertical-saas-embedded-lending-revenue-growth) and B2B marketplaces can offer working capital to business customers at the exact moment they need it.


#### Embedded banking


Non-banks can offer branded deposit accounts, debit cards, and cash management through embedded banking. This approach typically requires a sponsor bank relationship and BaaS infrastructure underneath to handle the regulatory side.


#### Embedded insurance


Contextual insurance coverage at the point of need falls into this category. Examples include shipping insurance at checkout, travel coverage during booking, or equipment protection during a purchase.


#### Embedded wealth


Investment and savings products integrated into apps represent a newer segment. Some platforms now offer round-up investing or yield-bearing accounts alongside their core product, though adoption is still early compared to payments and lending.

## How to choose the best embedded finance platform


Picking a provider comes down to matching capabilities to your specific situation. Here's a framework that works across use cases.


#### 1. Define your embedded finance use case


Start by clarifying what you're actually embedding. Are you offering payments, lending, banking, or some combination? Is your audience consumers or SMBs? Domestic or global? Answering these questions narrows the field significantly before you evaluate any specific vendor.


#### 2. Evaluate API coverage and time to launch


Compare developer documentation, sandbox access, and typical implementation timelines across providers. Plug-and-play tools like widgets and landing pages can compress launch timelines dramatically—some platforms go live in days rather than months.


#### 3. Audit compliance and data security


Verify SOC 2 certification, licensing, and how each provider handles regulatory responsibility. For lending products especially, clarify who holds the compliance burden since state licensing requirements vary.


#### 4. Compare total cost of ownership


Transaction fees tell only part of the story. Integration costs, ongoing maintenance, and charges for data access or reporting can add up quickly. Request detailed pricing that reflects your expected volume and use patterns.


#### 5. Check partner network and lender coverage


For embedded lending specifically, the breadth of the lender network matters. Platforms like Lendflow offer access to 75+ lenders through a single integration, with[smart waterfall routing](https://www.lendflow.com/post/multi-lender-orchestration-core-lending-infrastructure) that matches deals to the right capital source automatically.

### Challenges and risks of embedded finance


Embedded finance comes with real obstacles worth understanding upfront.


#### Regulatory and compliance exposure


Platforms may inherit compliance obligations depending on how the embedded product is structured. Lending products often require state licenses, and the line between "technology provider" and "lender" can blur in ways that create unexpected regulatory exposure.


#### Fraud and credit risk


Embedded lending introduces default risk that platforms don't face with payments alone. Real-time data and[explainable risk scoring](https://www.lendflow.com/post/credit-decisioning-tools) help underwriters make consistent decisions, but credit losses remain a factor in any lending program.


#### Vendor lock-in and integration complexity


Switching costs and API dependencies can create problems down the road. Open architecture platforms that avoid proprietary lock-in offer more flexibility if your requirements change or you want to add new capabilities later.

Timeline varies by complexity. Widget-based implementations can launch in under two weeks, while full API integrations typically take 30–45 days depending on customization requirements and compliance review.
