The average cost to pressure wash a house is **$311** , but you could pay between **$100** and **$714** , depending on the size of your house, its location, and other factors. Small areas—like driveways and patios—will be on the lower end of that range, while pressure washing an entire home will cost much more.

Note that pressure washing a small area means that a technician is spending little time at your home, despite having to pay for gas, insurance, and all other overhead costs. For this reason, pressure washing costs less per square foot when you give a professional more to do.

### Driveway


Driveways are one of the most popular choices for pressure washing, as they tend to accumulate grime quickly due to car and foot traffic. Overall, the[cost to pressure wash a driveway](https://www.angi.com/articles/cost-to-pressure-wash-driveway.htm) falls between **$100 and $500** and sits at an average of **$100 to $250** , depending on the size and the amount of work it takes. This often breaks down to **$0.20 to $0.40 per square foot** . Driveways with lots of motor oil accumulation require[pressure washing with bleach or a less abrasive cleaning solution](https://www.angi.com/articles/bleach-alternative-for-pressure-washing.htm) , as well as more labor.


### Patio or Deck


To have a professional[pressure wash a deck](https://www.angi.com/articles/how-to-power-wash-deck.htm) or patio, you’ll pay **$100 to $200** . Costs depend on the size of the patio, the material, and the ease of access. If a technician has to deal with stairs or obstructions—or if they need to reduce pressure to avoid damaging the surface—you’ll pay more for the added time and effort.


### Fence


The cost to pressure wash a fence is **$150 to $300** . Your price will depend on the length of the fence and material type. Wood fences take longer than PVC or metal fences because pros must use less pressure and more care to avoid damaging the wood.


### Gutters


You’ll pay **$0.50 to $1.50 per foot** for a tech to[pressure wash your gutters](https://www.angi.com/articles/clean-gutters-with-pressure-washer.htm) , so a single-story house costs between **$50 and $150** . If you have a taller home, your technician will need a ladder or a bucket truck to reach and[clean the gutters](https://www.angi.com/articles/how-much-does-gutter-cleaning-cost.htm) , which will add to the cost of your project.

### Siding Only


Many homeowners find that simply pressure washing their siding can significantly affect the building’s overall look. For this simpler project, you’ll pay anywhere from **$150 to $1,400** on average, though this price can climb upwards of **$2,500** for large projects, depending on how much siding needs cleaning.

### Brick


Even though brick is a durable material, it requires careful washing. A pressure washing pro will follow certain practices to avoid damaging the mortar or discoloring the masonry. On average, it costs **$0.10 to $0.40 per square foot** to[pressure wash brick](https://www.angi.com/articles/how-to-pressure-wash-brick.htm) .


### Stucco


[Cleaning stucco](https://www.angi.com/articles/how-clean-stucco.htm) also requires a gentle approach. It takes time to remove dirt and grime from around the ridges. You’ll pay a pro between **$0.30 and $0.50 per square foot** to pressure wash stucco.


### Wood


Wood’s porous nature also makes it trickier to pressure wash. To prevent damage, professionals often need to[soft wash](https://www.angi.com/articles/soft-wash-better-regular-pressure-washing.htm) wooden siding, fences, and decks. You’ll pay between **$0.30 and $0.55 per square foot** to pressure wash wood.


### Vinyl


Vinyl can withstand more water pressure than many other materials, including stucco and wood. However, your pro will still use specific techniques to not crack the vinyl. Pressuring washing this type of material costs **$0.20 to $0.50 per square foot** .

## Pressure Washing vs. Power Washing Cost

## How Often Should You Pressure Wash Your House?


You should aim to pressure wash your home once per year, and the best time is between March and November in most areas. These months rarely have temperatures that dip too low, which risks freezing the water or damaging the machines. That said, demand is higher in the spring and summer, so you could experience longer wait times for a professional to come (and possibly higher costs).


Here’s how often you should pressure wash different areas of your home:


Area of Home Best Time to Pressure Wash Reason


Siding Spring or fall Mild temperatures, less risk of mold and mildew buildup


Driveway Spring or fall After winter snow or fall leaves to remove debris and grime


Deck or patio Late spring and end of summer Prepare outdoor spaces for summer use and clean up after peak season


Roof Late spring or early fall Prevents moss growth and removes debris before harsher weather

-


Pros can often point out issues with rotting wood, damaged fascia boards, and other materials that require repair that a DIYer might miss.

-


Professionals are more likely to get an even clean that maximizes curb appeal and home value.
