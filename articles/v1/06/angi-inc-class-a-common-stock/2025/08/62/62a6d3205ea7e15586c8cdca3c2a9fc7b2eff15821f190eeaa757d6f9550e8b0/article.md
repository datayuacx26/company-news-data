The cost to install exterior trim ranges from **$500** to **$9,000** , with an average project costing **$1,800** . Homeowners can expect to pay between $6 and $15 per linear foot, depending on the trim material and project size. Larger homes or custom trim profiles may increase the total price.


Exterior trim not only frames your home’s style but also shields it from the elements. This guide will walk you through the factors that affect the cost to install exterior trim, how to plan your project, and what to expect every step of the way.


## **Cost by Size of Exterior Trim Project**


The overall size and scope of your exterior trim project play a major role in determining your total cost. The more linear feet of trim you need, the higher your material and labor expenses will be. For smaller homes, trim installation might cover the basic perimeter and a few windows and doors, while larger or multi-story homes require significantly more footage.


Complex architectural features, such as bay windows, dormers, or wraparound porches, increase the amount of trim required. The number of windows, doors, and corners on your home also directly affects the amount of trim you’ll need. Choosing to install trim on the entire exterior versus just a section—like a front façade—will also change your project’s price.

## **Exterior Trim Cost by Type**


The type of exterior trim you select has a significant impact on cost, appearance, and durability. Common trim types include fascia boards (which cover the roof edge), soffits (under the eaves), window and door casings, corner boards, and decorative moldings. Each serves a different purpose and varies in installation complexity.


Standard trim profiles are more budget-friendly, while custom millwork or ornate details will increase both material and labor costs. The right trim type depends on your home’s style, climate, and your maintenance preferences.

## **Exterior Trim Cost by Location**


Where you live can affect the cost to install exterior trim, due to differences in labor rates, material availability, and local codes. Urban areas often have higher labor costs than rural locations. Climate also plays a role; homes in wet or coastal areas may require premium, weather-resistant materials.


Some cities or regions require permits for exterior trim work, which can add to your total cost.


## **Exterior Trim Installation Cost Factors**


Several factors influence the cost to install exterior trim. Understanding these can help you plan and budget effectively.


### **Labor**


The professionals who install exterior trim range from skilled carpenters and general contractors to siding specialists. Labor is charged by the hour, per linear foot, or by the entire project. Hourly rates often fall between $40 and $90, while per-foot rates align with the complexity of the trim.


Minimum service fees apply to small projects, making them relatively more expensive per foot. Labor costs increase for experienced specialists, multi-story homes, or hard-to-reach areas. Labor includes preparation, installation, caulking, and cleanup, but always confirm with your contractor.


### **Permits**


Permits may be required for exterior trim installation, especially in areas with strict building codes or for historic homes.[Permit costs](https://www.angi.com/articles/how-much-does-building-permit-cost.htm) vary by location, ranging from $50 to $300. Your contractor often handles permit applications, but it’s important to clarify responsibility up front. Permit requirements can impact your project’s timeline and budget, so plan accordingly.


### **Additional Features**


Upgrading your trim with decorative moldings, crown details, or custom millwork can add style and value, but it also increases costs. Weatherproofing details—such as flashing, premium caulking, or advanced sealants—help protect your home but add to your materials bill.


[Painting or staining the trim](https://www.angi.com/articles/best-exterior-and-interior-trim.htm) after installation is another expense to consider. Some homeowners also opt for insulated trim or trim that integrates with gutters or siding for improved energy efficiency and protection.

## **Additional Costs to Consider**


Beyond installation, there are ongoing and one-time costs to keep in mind for your exterior trim project.


### **Warranties**


Exterior trim may come with manufacturer or installer warranties. Terms vary, but most cover defects or premature failure for five to 25 years. Extended warranties or service plans are sometimes available for an additional fee. Warranties can reduce long-term repair or replacement expenses, but always review the fine print for coverage limits and exclusions.


### **Maintenance**


Maintenance requirements depend on your trim’s material. Wood trim needs more frequent painting and caulking, while PVC, fiber cement, and aluminum are lower-maintenance.


Annual maintenance costs can range from $50 to $300, depending on your home’s size and the trim material. Watch for signs of wear, such as peeling paint or soft spots, to address problems early and extend trim lifespan.


### **Taxes**


Sales tax on materials and labor varies by state and can add to your project total. If your trim installation is part of a major remodel, your property taxes could increase slightly due to a higher home value.


### **Insurance**


Homeowner’s insurance may cover trim damage caused by storms or accidents, but not routine wear or neglect. Installing new trim does not increase insurance premiums, but always check your policy. Contractors should carry liability and workers’ compensation insurance during installation to protect you from risk.

## **Exterior Trim Replacement vs. Repair**


Deciding whether to repair or replace exterior trim depends on the extent of damage and the age of your trim. Minor issues, like small cracks, loose sections, or isolated rot, can often be repaired at a lower cost. Repairs might run $100 to $500, depending on the size and location.


Full replacement is recommended when there’s widespread rot, water damage, or if your trim is outdated and no longer matches your home’s style. Replacing all exterior trim is costlier but provides a longer-term solution and can improve curb appeal.


A good rule of thumb: If the cost to repair exceeds **50% of**[trim replacement costs](https://www.angi.com/articles/cost-to-repair-replace-trim.htm) , it’s best to replace. Assess the age and warranty status of your trim to help make your decision.


## **Does Exterior Trim Add Value to Your Home?**


Installing new exterior trim offers a strong return on investment (ROI), often recouping **60% to 80%** of its cost at resale. Fresh trim enhances curb appeal, making your home more attractive to buyers and improving first impressions.


Compared to other exterior projects like siding or windows, trim is a relatively affordable upgrade with noticeable results. The quality of materials, design, color choice, and installation craftsmanship all influence your ROI. Besides value, new trim can improve weather resistance, energy efficiency, and overall safety while boosting your home’s visual character.


## **Tips to Reduce Costs While Installing Exterior Trim**


Here are some helpful tips to keep exterior trim installation costs within budget:


-


Get multiple quotes from licensed, insured contractors.


-


Choose budget-friendly materials that offer durability and low maintenance.


-


Limit custom profiles or ornate designs to reduce labor and material costs.


-


Schedule installation during off-peak seasons for potential discounts.


-


DIY prep or removal of old trim if safe and feasible.


-


Bundle exterior trim installation with other exterior projects (e.g., siding, painting) for package pricing.


-


Maintain trim regularly to avoid costly repairs or premature replacement.
