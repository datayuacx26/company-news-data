On average, flowerbed installation costs **$2,000** , with most homeowners paying between **$1,000** and **$3,000** for a new bed. Prices range from $10 to $20 per square foot, depending on materials, labor, and design. The final cost reflects the size of your flowerbed, plant selection, and any custom features you choose.


A well-planned flowerbed adds beauty and function to your landscape. This guide will walk you through what to expect for flowerbed installation costs, including what drives prices and how to get the most value from your project.


## **Flowerbed Size**


The size of your flowerbed is one of the strongest factors affecting installation cost. Larger beds require more plants, soil, edging, and labor, which increases the total price. Most residential flowerbeds fall into small (under 50 square feet), medium (50 to 150 square feet), or large (over 150 square feet) categories. While bigger beds sometimes benefit from bulk pricing on materials, the added labor and complexity often outweigh the savings.

## **Flowerbed Type**


Choosing the right type of flowerbed impacts both cost and long-term maintenance. Common options include in-ground, raised, container, and specialty beds. Each has its own material and labor requirements.


-


**In-ground beds:** Use existing soil and are cost-effective, but may require extra soil amendment.


-


**Raised beds:** Offer improved drainage and accessibility but cost more due to lumber or stone.


-


**Container beds** : Versatile and mobile but limited in size and can be pricier per square foot.


-


**Specialty beds:** Pollinator, shade, or xeriscape beds often require specific plants or features, raising both material and design costs.


Customization, such as curved shapes, multi-level beds, or built-in irrigation, will also increase the price.

## **Cost by Location**


Your geographic location and specific site conditions affect flowerbed installation cost. Labor rates, material prices, and plant availability vary by region. Urban areas often have higher labor costs than rural locations.


Site-specific factors such as installing a bed in the front yard (easy access) versus a backyard (limited access), or on sloped terrain, can affect both labor and material expenses. Local regulations or HOA rules may also require permits or specific materials, adding to the cost.


## **Flowerbed Installation Cost Factors**


Several factors directly impact your final flowerbed installation cost. Here’s a closer look at what impacts your total.


### **Labor**


Professional[landscapers](https://www.angi.com/articles/how-much-does-landscaping-cost.htm) , gardeners, and design consultants are often involved. Labor rates can range from $30 to $100 per hour or $5 to $15 per square foot, depending on experience and region. Some companies charge a minimum service fee, especially for smaller projects.


Complex designs, extensive soil work, or installing irrigation will raise labor costs. Labor rates also rise during busy seasons.


### **Permits**


Permits are usually only needed for major excavation, retaining walls, or permanent irrigation systems. Fees range from $50 to $400, depending on your municipality. Always check with your local building department or HOA to confirm what’s required.


### **Features**


Adding features will increase your flowerbed installation cost:

**Edging materials (stone, metal, plastic):** $1 to $25 per linear foot

-

-

-


**Lighting:** $100 to $500 per bed


-


**Decorative accents (boulders, statues):** varies by choice


Premium materials or custom shapes also add to the price.


### **Other Cost Factors**


Design complexity, such as curved or multi-level beds, increases both material waste and labor time. Accessibility—like beds far from your driveway, steep slopes, or tight spaces—can add to labor and delivery costs.


Site prep, including removing old landscaping or[soil testing](https://www.angi.com/articles/how-much-does-testing-soil-cost.htm) , will also raise your total. After the work is done, post-construction cleanup and debris removal may be an extra charge.


If you[hire a landscape designer](https://www.angi.com/articles/how-much-does-hiring-landscape-designer-cost.htm) , design fees range from $50 to $150 per hour. Some HOAs or cities require inspections, which can add $50 to $200.


## **Additional Costs to Consider**


Keep these extra costs in mind when planning your flowerbed installation project to avoid surprises.


### **Warranties**


Some landscapers offer warranties on plants, materials, or workmanship, often for one to three years. These may cost extra—5% to 10% of the project price—but provide peace of mind. Warranties often cover plant replacement if they fail within the covered period, but read the fine print for exclusions.


### **Operating Costs**


Ongoing costs include watering, fertilizing, seasonal planting, and pest control. Automated irrigation may raise your water bill by $10 to $30 monthly in peak season. Plant selection affects maintenance: native or drought-tolerant species often require less water and care, while annuals or specialty plants need more regular attention.


### **Maintenance**


Routine maintenance involves weeding, mulching, pruning, and replacing annuals. Expect to spend $500 to $1,200 per year if hiring a service for monthly visits, or less if you do it yourself or only have your beds professionally serviced in the spring and summer. Raised or specialty beds may require more frequent care.


### **Taxes**


Sales tax may apply to both materials and labor, depending on your state. Rates and exemptions vary, so check local regulations to avoid surprises on your final bill.


### **Insurance**


Always hire insured professionals to protect yourself from liability during installation. Insurance covers accidents or damage to your property. Large or complex projects may require the contractor to carry extra coverage—ask to see proof before work begins.

## **Flowerbed Replacement vs. Repair**


Sometimes, you can repair a flowerbed instead of replacing it. Repairs are best for issues like dead plants, damaged edging, or compacted soil. Full replacement is recommended for severe weed infestations, poor drainage, or an outdated or failing design.


If your repair costs exceed 50% of the replacement cost, it’s best to replace the bed. For example, repairing a medium bed (replacing plants, fixing edging, adding soil) may cost $400 to $800, while a full replacement could be $1,000 to $2,000. Factor in the age of the bed and any plant warranties—older or poorly performing beds are better candidates for replacement.
