Starting, buying or expanding an independent pharmacy comes with unique financial demands, from higher upfront costs to managing cash flow between inventory purchases and delayed reimbursements.


The good news: with the right lender and up-front planning and preparation, financing for your pharmacy is within reach.


**Start with the right financing strategy**


Not all loans are created equal. While conventional loans can work in some cases, many pharmacy owners turn to SBA-backed financing, particularly the SBA 7(a) program.


The SBA 7(a) Loan Program offers higher loan limits and longer repayment terms. It also offers flexible structures based on cash flow, rather than hard assets, which is important for pharmacy financing.


Equally important is choosing a lender who understands pharmacy economics and has deep SBA experience. A lender with Preferred Lender status can often move faster, make decisions in-house and provide more practical guidance based on similar transactions.


**Expect a comprehensive review**


Beyond standard criteria like credit score and liquidity, lenders will look at the full picture, including:


- Pharmacy or healthcare experience
- Business ownership background
- Strength of projected cash flow
- Viability of the specific opportunity


Some lenders take a more rigid, checklist-driven approach. Others, like Byline Bank, evaluate the broader story, including your experience, your plan and the business itself, which can make a meaningful difference in outcomes.


**Prepare early to improve your position**


Strong preparation speeds up the loan process and improves your chances of approval. Focus on:


- **Target opportunity:** Identify a specific pharmacy to acquire or a viable market and location
- **Financial readiness:** Review your credit and liquidity
- **Budget clarity:** Outline startup or acquisition costs, including inventory, buildout, staffing and operations
- **Business plan:** Clearly articulate your model, market strategy and financial projections


If you’re acquiring, financial performance and cash flow will be central. For startups, your personal financial position and experience may carry more weight.


**Key considerations**


- **Down payment:** Typically 10% or more, depending on structure
- **Loan terms:** SBA 7(a) loans can offer up to $5 million, with terms up to 10 years (or 25 for real estate)
- **Timing:** Identify your location first, but don’t sign a lease until financing is secured


**Why lender experience matters**


Pharmacy financing is specialized. A knowledgeable lender can help structure the deal appropriately, flag risks early and guide you through the SBA process efficiently.


The right partner doesn’t just evaluate your application, they help you understand what works, what doesn’t and how to position your business for long-term success.
