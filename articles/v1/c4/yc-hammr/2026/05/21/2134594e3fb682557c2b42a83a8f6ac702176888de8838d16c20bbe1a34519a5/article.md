#### **Meet Jodi, the Payroll Admin**


Jodi runs payroll and administration for Alvitre Civil, a growing civil contractor in California. Managing a workforce spread across multiple prevailing wage projects is no small task, especially when you’re the only one who knows all the in’s and out’s of compliance.


Alvitre operates south of Fresno where pistachios grow and business still runs on handshakes. The kind of place where your reputation is everything and everyone knows if you screw up.


As the main payroll admin, Jodi’s role is the kind that sounds manageable until you realize the work never actually ends…and there’s no backup when things pile up.


*“I’m my own little payroll island. And no one really cares what I do…until I don’t do it.”*


#### **The Problem: Prevailing Wage Payroll Was Eating Her Alive**


Here’s what it’s like being the only person who knows how to run payroll when your crews work across several prevailing wage projects: You can’t take a vacation without planning it around pay cycles. You work evenings because the work doesn’t care that you have a life outside the office. And when things get overwhelming? You cancel plans because there’s nobody else to pick up the slack.


Before Hammr, Alvitre ran payroll through QuickBooks - fine for a simple office business, but not built for construction payroll and prevailing wage complexity. Here’s what Jodi’s week looked like:


- **All Day Tuesday: 5-8 hours just processing payroll.** Review every paper timecard. Every rate manually entered into every employee profile. Multiple classifications per employee - operator two, operator three, operator four, and more. Then manually adjusting rates each time entry if they hit prevailing wage that week.
- **2.5 hours per certified payroll. Per project. Per week.** At the time Alvitre came to Hammr, they were running 10 active prevailing wage jobs simultaneously - that’s about 25 hours in certified payroll reporting alone, every week. Averaged across a typical week with 5-7 active prevailing wage jobs, they were spending around 15 hours on certified payroll reporting on top of 5 hours of payroll prep - roughly 20 hours a week total, just to get crews paid on Friday and report on it. Sometimes the reports piled up until end of month when Jodi would carve out entire days and weekends - just to get caught up.
- **Evenings always belonged to payroll.** Work until 4PM. Handle kids. Get dinner sorted. Log back on around 7:30PM to tackle the backlog. It was a game of late nights.
- **Vacations? Funny joke. Canceled.** Not because she didn’t want to go - because coming back to a nightmare of piled-up work wasn’t worth the stress and anxiety.


*“I’ve actually canceled many vacations in the past because things were just too overwhelming. I couldn’t handle it when stuff would just pile up. I can’t believe I’m saying this but I’m now taking the longest vacation in 8 years since I’ve been with Alvitre. I would’ve never guessed that after working with you guys (Hammr), that it would finally give me some time to take a vacation with my family.”* — Jodi
