Email invitations: best practices


Now that you’ve seen some great examples of email invitations, let’s cover some basics for when you’re ready to send one on your own.


### Write a clear and catchy subject line


Your email’s subject line is the first impression. It determines whether your invitation gets opened or ignored (or worse, marked as spam).


Crafting a subject that informs and intrigues can significantly increase your open rates. Think of phrases that spark curiosity, like “You’re Invited: \[Event Name\]” or “Unlock Exclusive Access at \[Event Name\]”.


These not only convey the event’s purpose but also evoke a sense of exclusivity and urgency.


Here are a few good examples from Warner Bros. Studio Tour London that goes along with the first example we shared earlier.


Screenshot by Brandon Lee


### Add a touch of personalization


A personal touch can make all the difference in your tone and messaging.


Addressing the recipient by their name and curating content relevant to them helps build a deeper connection and relationship. Plus, studies have shown that personalized emails can[boost engagement rates](https://www.kalungi.com/blog/email-personalization-best-practices) substantially.


So, when crafting your invite, ensure you’re speaking directly to the individual. Make them feel seen and valued.


### Include the essential details


While you want your invite to be engaging, it’s still important to prioritize clarity.


Why? The underlying purpose of the email is to share[important information about your event](https://blog.inevent.com/others/event-planning-checklist-for-any-kind-of-conference.html) .


So, keep the details such as date, time, venue, and event purpose apparent without much scrolling required.


An effective invitation respects the recipient’s time, allowing them to quickly decide their interest and availability without sifting through excessive text.


But you don’t have to throw creativity completely by the wayside. A brilliant example of this is incorporating the dress code theme into the invitation itself.


Imagine receiving an invitation encouraging guests to don their[stylish leather jackets](https://www.leathercult.com/leather-jackets) and pants to match the neutral color scheme of the event. This approach not only sets the tone for a unique and exciting event but also benefits both the host and the guests.


[Image Source](https://www.zazzle.com/minimalist_wedding_attire_dress_code_cards-256634834183102093)


For the host, specifying a dress code adds an element of fun and cohesion to the party’s ambiance. For the guests, it sparks anticipation and allows them to unleash their creativity in assembling the perfect ensemble, making it an event they won’t want to miss.


### Write compelling email copy


Compelling copy often evokes emotion. Whether it’s the event’s excitement, the urgency of a limited-time offer, or the warmth of a personal story, weaving emotion into your email can create a deeper connection with your audience.


But why should readers trust your message?


Using statistics, expert quotes, testimonials, or endorsements can give your email a foundation of trustworthiness (like ConvertKit in example four). Establishing credibility early in the email can increase the chances of your audience taking the desired action.

Today’s schedules are jam-packed. We’re all busy people juggling our personal and professional lives.


Providing an easy “Add to Calendar” link or button to your email innovations improves the chances that your event gets a reserved slot in your invitee’s busy schedule.

**Frequently Asked Questions**


**1. What makes a great event invitation email?**
The best event invitation emails do three things well — they grab attention with a strong subject line, make the event details impossible to miss, and give people a clear reason to say yes. Visuals help a lot too. A great photo or short video of your venue or past events can do more heavy lifting than three paragraphs of copy ever will.


**2. How important is branding in an event invitation email?**
Very. When someone opens your email, they should know instantly who it’s from. Consistent colors, fonts, and tone build recognition and trust — which directly affects whether people open your emails in the first place. BILL is a great example of this done right; every element of their invite matches their brand identity.


**3. Should I use images or keep my event invitation email text-only?**
Both can work — it just depends on your brand and your audience. Image-heavy emails like Warner Bros.’ Harry Potter invite create a wow factor and emotional pull. Text-based emails like Bass Rocks Golf Club’s can be just as effective if the copy is tight and the formatting is clean. The key is to match the format to the feeling you want to create.


**4. How do I use social proof in an event invitation email?**
Add testimonials, speaker names, or past attendee quotes directly in the email body. ConvertKit did this brilliantly by leading with creator testimonials before even getting to the event details. If people see that others have attended and loved it, they’re far more likely to sign up themselves.


**5. What’s the best way to create urgency in an event invitation email?**
Limited spots, early bird pricing, and countdown language all work well. The trick is to make sure the urgency is real — don’t say “limited seats” if you have 2,000 spots. ConvertKit’s early bird ticket visual is a good example: it shows a clear deadline and a specific dollar amount saved, which makes the decision feel easy.


**6. Do I need to include an “Add to Calendar” option in my event invitation?**
Yes, always. People are busy and even genuinely interested attendees forget. An Add to Calendar button removes all friction — one click and your event is locked into their schedule. Pair it with a reminder email closer to the date and you’ve significantly reduced your no-show rate.
