# Open AI GPT-6 Astra Release Date

OpenAI has not announced a GPT-6 release date, and it has not confirmed that GPT-6 and Astra are the same model. As of August 2026, the only official fact is Astra itself: on August 1, OpenAI named Astra as "our next major model" in a[research post](https://openai.com/index/ten-advances-in-mathematics/) , then said almost nothing else. There is no release date, no model card, no API, no price, and no statement tying the Astra name to the GPT-6 label that most people are searching for.


Here is what OpenAI has actually confirmed, why Astra and GPT-6 are not yet the same thing on paper, and what the timing signals point to.


## What OpenAI has confirmed about Astra


OpenAI confirmed[Astra](https://emergent.sh/news/openai-astra-launch) on August 1, 2026, through research rather than a product launch. The company published a post titled "Ten advances in mathematics and theoretical computer science," reporting that an internal version of Astra produced new results on ten long-standing open problems, each with a machine-checkable proof in the Lean theorem prover. OpenAI described Astra in that post as "our next major model."


The math results are the substance of the announcement. OpenAI said the problems had seen no progress on their central results for at least a decade, and in most cases far longer, spanning fields such as high-dimensional geometry, group theory, quantum complexity, and lattice cryptography. The company noted that the tokens needed to find all ten solutions would have[cost roughly $2,000](https://the-decoder.com/openai-announces-its-next-major-model-astra-by-dropping-ten-previously-unsolved-math-solutions/) at GPT-5.6 Sol API rates. That figure is a research compute estimate, not a price for the model, a distinction several early write-ups blurred.


What OpenAI did not publish is everything a release would include. No release date, model sizes, context window, API model ID, pricing, ChatGPT rollout plan, or safety card accompanied the post. The announcement named a model and demonstrated a capability. It did not ship a product.


## Why Astra is not officially GPT-6


Astra and GPT-6 are not confirmed to be the same product, and treating them as interchangeable is the most common error in coverage. OpenAI has used only the Astra name. According to[BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/artificial-intelligence/openai-teases-astra-its-next-major-ai-model-after-it-solves-10-long-standing-math-problems/) , the company has reportedly not decided whether the model will ship as GPT-5.7, GPT-6, or under another name entirely.


Recent history is the reason to be careful here. Earlier in 2026, a model codenamed "Spud" was widely rumored to be GPT-6. It[launched in April](https://evolink.ai/blog/gpt-6-release-date) as GPT-5.5. An internal codename or a community label is not a product name until OpenAI says so, and the company has a track record of releasing models under version numbers that undercut the pre-launch speculation.


This is why the two terms belong together in one question. Whether Astra eventually ships as GPT-6, as a point release, or as a separately named flagship, the practical status today is identical: a named next-generation model with no release date attached.


## Why there is still no GPT-6 release date


A named model is not a scheduled one, and Astra carries a specific reason for delay. On August 7, 2026, OpenAI[disclosed](https://openai.com/index/responding-next-frontier-critical-cyber-capabilities/) that it "cannot rule out" Astra reached the "Critical" cybersecurity capability threshold under its Preparedness Framework. This is the first OpenAI model to hit that classification, and a Critical designation triggers additional safety controls and testing before any wide release.


The rollout path adds further distance. OpenAI's recent pattern, seen with GPT-5.6, was a limited preview restricted to roughly 20 government-approved partners, followed weeks later by general availability. A frontier model flagged Critical for cyber risk is more likely to follow that gated path than to launch straight to the public. Sam Altman has also[demonstrated the model](https://www.axios.com/2026/08/07/openai-astra-model-delay-cybersecurity-risks) privately to US lawmakers, which points to a release treated as a policy event rather than a simple product drop.


"Release date" itself hides three separate milestones that rarely land on the same day: a product announcement naming the model, availability inside ChatGPT, and a callable API with a published model ID and price. Astra has cleared none of these. It has a name and a research post, and nothing more.


## What prediction markets expect


Prediction markets currently point to a late-2026 launch, with the odds thinning sharply the earlier you look. On[Polymarket's](https://polymarket.com/event/gpt-6-released-by) "GPT-6 released by" market, traders give roughly a 72% chance of a public release before December, about 66% by the end of 2026, and around 47% before November, while near-term August windows have collapsed to single digits. These are crowd forecasts tied to a defined resolution rule, not inside information, and the numbers move continuously, so treat them as a snapshot rather than a schedule. Figures are as of publication.


The market's own history is a caution. The same market repeatedly priced in earlier launch windows through the spring and summer that never resolved, because OpenAI kept shipping GPT-5.x point releases instead of a numbered successor. Crowd odds track sentiment and timing expectations, not OpenAI's calendar.


## Most GPT-6 Astra rumors are unconfirmed or false


Most of the specific "GPT-6 Astra" claims circulating are unconfirmed, and a few are already debunked. The widely shared figures, such as a 10 trillion parameter count and a 1.5 million token context window, trace to leak clusters on X, not to OpenAI. The "August launch" chatter has repeatedly failed to resolve. The "$2,000" number is a compute estimate from the research post, not a model price. And the earlier "GPT-6 launched on April 14" claim is false, since the model that followed was GPT-5.5.


The verified core is narrow: OpenAI named Astra, showed a real and independently discussed math result, disclosed a Critical cyber-risk classification, and briefed lawmakers. Everything past that, including the final name and any date, is speculation until OpenAI publishes product details.
