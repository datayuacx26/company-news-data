# OpenAI Solves 10 Major Math Problems with o3 Reasoning


OpenAI has published breakthrough results demonstrating its o3 reasoning model's ability to solve previously unsolved problems in pure mathematics and theoretical computer science. The announcement, shared on the official OpenAI blog, showcases solutions to long-standing open problems spanning geometry, cryptography, and computational complexity, marking a significant milestone in AI-assisted mathematical discovery.


The results represent months of collaborative work between OpenAI researchers and academic mathematicians, validating the model's capacity to tackle problems that have resisted human efforts for years or even decades.


## Breakthroughs Across Multiple Domains


The ten advances span diverse areas of mathematics. In geometry, o3 produced novel proofs for conjectures related to higher-dimensional polytopes and manifold classification. The model discovered efficient constructions that mathematicians had sought unsuccessfully since the early 2000s.


In cryptography, the system identified new approaches to lattice-based encryption schemes and quantum-resistant protocols. These findings have immediate implications for post-quantum cryptography standards currently under development by standards bodies worldwide.


Complexity theory saw perhaps the most dramatic results, with o3 establishing new separations between computational complexity classes. While not resolving P versus NP, the model proved several related conjectures about circuit lower bounds and derandomization that had appeared in problem lists for over 15 years.


## How o3 Achieved These Results


According to OpenAI, the breakthroughs rely on o3's extended reasoning capabilities, which allow the model to explore mathematical proof spaces far more thoroughly than previous systems. The model chains together intermediate lemmas, tests counterexamples, and refines approaches over thousands of reasoning steps.


Key technical innovations include:


- Multi-hour proof search with backtracking and refinement
- Formal verification integration to ensure correctness
- Domain-specific training on mathematical corpora and proof assistants
- Collaboration frameworks where human mathematicians guide exploration


Several solutions required compute budgets exceeding 100,000 reasoning tokens, demonstrating the necessity of deep, iterative thinking for frontier mathematical work.


## Academic Verification and Publication


OpenAI has submitted the results to peer-reviewed mathematics journals and made preprints available through arXiv. Independent mathematicians have begun verification, with early responses praising the rigor and novelty of the proofs.


The company is working with institutions including MIT, Stanford, and the Institute for Advanced Study to ensure the results withstand academic scrutiny. Several proofs have already been formalized in Lean and Coq theorem provers, providing machine-checkable verification.


## Implications for Mathematics Research


These advances signal a shift in how mathematicians might approach open problems. Rather than replacing human insight, o3 acts as a tireless collaborator capable of exploring vast proof spaces and identifying promising directions.


Mathematicians quoted in the announcement emphasized that human creativity remains essential for formulating conjectures and interpreting results, but AI can now handle extensive computational searches and routine verification steps. This division of labor could accelerate progress on problems requiring both deep intuition and exhaustive case analysis.


## What This Means


OpenAI's publication of ten solved mathematical problems demonstrates that frontier AI systems have reached a capability threshold for contributing to pure research. The results validate years of investment in reasoning models and formal verification infrastructure. For the mathematics community, these tools offer unprecedented support for tackling problems once considered computationally intractable. As verification continues and more researchers adopt these techniques, we may witness an acceleration in mathematical discovery comparable to previous revolutions brought by computer algebra systems and proof assistants. The boundary between human and machine mathematical work continues to blur in productive ways.
