# Why Interactive Experiences Accelerate CX Transformation Faster Than Training

**Modern video games don’t make you read a manual before you start to play.** They drop players into a simulated world, give them simple objectives, and let them learn by doing. Even board games take the same approach, with cut-down quick-start versions and even free downloadable content to hook you in.


That same principle explains why interactive experiences often drive better customer experience (CX) outcomes than traditional training.


## **From Slides To Simulations**


At our recent CX Summit and Forums, participants built strategies, negotiated budgets, responded to executive challenges, and adapted to unexpected changes — in 50 minutes, all simulated. One participant told us, “We made more progress in 50 minutes than in months of real-world discussions.”


The reason is simple: Simulations remove just enough real-world complexity to help people focus on decision-making. Rather than debating every organizational constraint, participants can experiment, collaborate, and see the consequences of their choices in real time. They build a mental model of success before returning to the realities of implementation.


## **Design Experiences, Not Presentations**


Game design shows us how to get started and how to hook “


players ”


:


- **Start simple.** Give participants clear goals before layering in complexity.


- **Hand over control.** Let teams make decisions rather than passively consume information.


- **Simulate change.** Create realistic scenarios that mirror the challenges of transformation.


Interactive experiences also create something that presentations rarely achieve: emotional engagement. People remember the debate, the negotiation, the surprise challenge, and the ownership they felt over the outcome. Those experiences build confidence and alignment far more effectively than another strategy deck.


If you want to accelerate CX transformation, stop asking how to improve the next presentation. Ask how you can help people explore, experiment, and learn together. The fastest path to change is often through play.
