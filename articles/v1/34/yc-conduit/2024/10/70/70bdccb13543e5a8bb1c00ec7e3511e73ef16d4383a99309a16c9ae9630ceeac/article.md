# How Conduit Helps Warehouses Meet WAIRE Compliance and Reduce Fees in Southern California

## Understanding the WAIRE Program and How Conduit Can Improve Compliance and Reduce Fees


The WAIRE program, officially known as Warehouse Actions and Investments to Reduce Emissions, was introduced to reduce pollution caused by warehouses and the goods movement industry in Southern California. Implemented by the South Coast Air Quality Management District (SCAQMD), the WAIRE program enforces a set of regulatory requirements for warehouses larger than 100,000 square feet. These rules aim to minimize the environmental impact by regulating operations and encouraging emission-reduction activities.


### What is the WAIRE Program?


WAIRE, established under Rule 2305, is a key regulation targeting air quality improvements in Southern California. It applies to large warehouse facilities that contribute to significant pollution, especially from diesel trucks and other sources linked to goods movement. The program enforces strict reporting and compliance standards, and warehouses must take active steps to reduce their emissions.


The WAIRE program operates in the following regions:


• Los Angeles County


• Riverside County


• Orange County


• Salton Sea Basin


• Mojave Desert Basin (partial)


### The Financial Impact of Non-Compliance


Warehouses that fail to comply with WAIRE regulations face significant financial penalties. The compliance deadlines are strict, and facilities must submit reports such as the **one-time** **Initial Site Information Report (ISIR)** by set dates to avoid fines. Penalties can reach up to $10,000 per day for non-compliance, making it essential for warehouses to stay on top of their obligations.


In addition to the one-time ISIR, warehouses must also submit **annual WAIRE reports** detailing their compliance efforts. The reporting requirements can be overwhelming without the right tools in place, and missing a deadline can lead to steep fines that accumulate rapidly.

### WAIRE’s Points-Based System: A Flexible Compliance Approach


The WAIRE program employs a points-based system, offering some flexibility for warehouses in how they meet their obligations. Facilities can earn WAIRE Points by selecting from a menu of emission-reducing actions, such as:


• Installing solar panels


• Transitioning to electric trucks


• Implementing energy-efficient lighting


Alternatively, warehouses can submit a Custom WAIRE Plan tailored to their specific operations, allowing them to choose strategies that best fit their capabilities. If a warehouse cannot meet the required points, they have the option to pay mitigation fees instead. However, relying on fees can quickly become expensive, so most warehouses prefer to find compliance actions that are both cost-effective and environmentally beneficial.


### Challenges in WAIRE Compliance for Warehouses


Complying with WAIRE regulations presents numerous challenges for warehouse operators. Tracking truck trips, emissions, and energy usage across large facilities is complex, especially when it involves integrating various systems. Manually managing these operations often leads to errors, missed deadlines, and non-compliance, which could result in significant financial penalties.


Additionally, warehouse operators need to ensure they are not only meeting the requirements but also optimizing their operations to reduce emissions in the most cost-efficient way. This is where technology plays a crucial role.

### Optimizing Yard and Dock Operations for WAIRE Compliance


In addition to the annual reports, one of the key areas where warehouses can improve compliance is by optimizing their yard and dock operations. Trucks sitting idle at loading docks are a major source of emissions. By implementing efficient traffic systems, warehouses can reduce the time trucks spend idling, which in turn reduces emissions and helps meet WAIRE requirements.


Automating gate check-ins, dock scheduling, and yard operations can also lead to better time management, reducing bottlenecks and ensuring a smoother flow of goods. This leads to lower operational costs and fewer emissions, both of which are critical for WAIRE compliance.
