# Crucial Components to Successful Yard Management

Efficient dock and yard management is paramount. Your yard plays a critical role in larger supply chains, facilitating the transportation of goods and resources. Your customers rely on fast and cost-effective shipments. Unfortunately, many challenges exist for companies like yours.


Countless assets move through your yard, and drivers constantly come in and out to meet shipping deadlines. Your dock and yard must operate like a well-oiled machine. Otherwise, backlogs and congestion can cause costly delays, rising costs and unhappy carriers and customers.


Improving operations can seem like an uphill battle, but it's easier than you think. Keep reading to learn more about the key components of successful yard management and how a platform like Conduit can lead to great improvements across the board.


## What is Yard Management?


First things first: What exactly is yard management?


At its core, yard management is a practice that revolves around the careful planning and execution of yard operations in a warehouse or distribution center. Your yard bridges the gap between transportation and warehousing. It likely has a never-ending line of inbound and outbound trucks. Drivers are there to pick up shipments, and it's up to dock and yard workers and managers to effectively load or unload them, handle documentation and send drivers on their way.


It sounds simple enough, but you know better than anyone else that yards can be chaotic. This initial stage of transportation has many moving parts. It's up to you to keep things moving efficiently to maintain and support the supply chain. Yard management involves dock scheduling, managing driver check in and out, managing drop trailers and containers, coordinating workers, managing documents, keeping track of inventory and more.


## Key Elements


Effective management can turn your yard into a well-oiled machine. However, getting to that point isn't easy. There are several key elements to successful yard management. Focusing on these areas can lead to substantial improvements.


### 1. Facility Efficiency


Contrary to popular belief, picking up a shipment isn't as simple as having a driver show up at the yard and leave with a full load. The docking process requires strategic direction and a team of trained dock workers. Depending on the goods moved, preparing a delivery may involve using forklifts and other heavy-duty equipment. Plus, there's careful documentation you must complete.


The biggest challenge with yard management is maintaining efficient workflows. More often than not, drivers spend hours waiting for facilities to do their part, resulting in huge delays, yard bottlenecks and other issues.


Effective yard management requires facility efficiency. Every task, including scheduling and forklift dispatching, must be rock-solid.


### 2. Visibility of Inventory


Another key component of yard management is inventory visibility. Your yard gets a constant influx of new assets while simultaneously sending other assets out. Failing to keep track of available inventory and its location can lead to a nightmare scenario. Not only does a lack of inventory transparency significantly reduce facility efficiency, but you could run into problems fulfilling orders and getting goods to customers.


Data plays a big role in inventory management. In the past, yard managers relied on paper documentation. Fortunately, there are better ways to do things today. However, failing to prioritize inventory visibility will only harm your business.


### 3. Transportation Management


Yard management benefits from seamless transportation management integration. While your yard is the link between transportation and warehousing, successful management needs the ability to collect and exchange information after deliveries leave your property.


Great transportation management facilitates the flow of information between inbound logistics and outbound deliveries, keeping all stakeholders in the loop to maximize supply chain efficiency.
