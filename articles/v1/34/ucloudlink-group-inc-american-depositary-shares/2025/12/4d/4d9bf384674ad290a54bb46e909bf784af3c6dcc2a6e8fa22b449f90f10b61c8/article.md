## Key Differences Between Portable WiFi and Mobile Hotspot


Before diving into the specific features, it’s important to understand how these two connection options differ in terms of their overall design and the way they operate.


### Device Type & Design


-


A Portable WiFi hotspot device is an independent, portable, compact, and lightweight device that does not rely on a mobile phone. By inserting a


[SIM card](https://eu.glocalme.com/collections/sim-card) or using CloudSIM technology, it can provide stable and reliable network connections for multiple devices simultaneously.


-


Mobile Hotspot is a feature that most smartphones come with and needs to be activated in the phone's settings. It uses the data package of the phone's SIM card. The strength of the WiFi signal, the amount of data, and other factors all depend on the data package of the phone's SIM card and performance.


### Network Connection Methods


-


A portable WiFi hotspot usually connects to the network through a dedicated SIM card or CloudSIM technology. Taking the


[MeowGo G40 Pro](https://eu.glocalme.com/products/meowgo-g40-pro) as an example, based on HyperConn™ technology, it supports intelligent switching between different networks, allowing you to connect to the local best network at any time.


-


Mobile Hotspot relies on the data package and network quality of the mobile phone. It has a simple connection method, but is also limited by the phone's network environment.


### Power Consumption & Battery Life


-


The portable WiFi hotspot device is equipped with a battery, so it won't consume the battery power of your phone and can provide longer battery life.


-


Although the Mobile Hotspot is convenient and requires no additional devices, it will continuously consume the battery power of your phone and cause it to overheat after long-term usage.


### Network Stability & Performance


-


A portable WiFi hotspot device is specifically designed for network sharing. It usually has stronger signal processing capabilities and anti-interference ability, providing a more stable and reliable network connection.


-


The network sharing capability of Mobile Hotspot depends on the performance of the mobile phone and the signal quality, and is easily affected by other nearby devices.


### Multi-Device Connectivity


-


A portable WiFi hotspot can support up to 10 or even more devices to connect simultaneously. For example, GlocalMe's MeowGo G40 Pro enables 10 devices to share a high-speed network simultaneously, providing a better experience for multi-device users.


-


The number of Mobile Hotspot connections is limited (generally between 3 and 5 devices). Connecting multiple devices will cause the overheating of the mobile phone and affect the signal strength and stability of the hotspot.


### Cost & Long-Term Practicality


-


Although portable WiFi hotspot devices require an additional purchase, they offer the flexibility to choose data plans and networks, making them a better option for long-term use.


-


Mobile Hotspot requires no additional device and has a lower startup cost, but it has obvious disadvantages in terms of stability, the number of device connections, battery life, etc.
