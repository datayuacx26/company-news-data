# Death by Login: How Manual Bank Workflows Sabotage Daily Cash Positioning

**The Workflow That Eats the Morning**


Every controller in a multi-bank environment knows the routine. Log into the first bank portal. Export the balance report. Open the next portal. Repeat. Paste everything into a spreadsheet. Reformat the columns so they align. Check for missing accounts. Flag anything that looks off. By the time daily cash positioning is complete, half the morning is gone and the first round of payment approvals is already waiting. This is not an efficiency problem in the traditional sense. It is a structural bottleneck that sits at the start of every downstream treasury decision. Our team estimates that finance teams managing 8 or more bank relationships spend 45 to 90 minutes each morning on manual aggregation before the cash position is even reviewable.


**Bank Portals Were Not Designed for Aggregation**


Each bank portal serves its own customers well enough in isolation. But no bank portal was built to participate in a consolidated workflow across institutions. Session timeouts interrupt exports midway. Report formats differ between portals, requiring manual adjustment before data can be combined. Some portals restrict export options to PDF, forcing teams to re-key figures. Controllers managing bank data aggregation across five or more institutions are not performing one task five times. They are performing five different tasks that happen to share the same goal.


Bank portals give you access to data. They do not give you a workflow.


**The Spreadsheet Becomes the System**


Once balances are exported, the spreadsheet takes over as the operating layer. Entity mappings, currency conversions, account groupings, and exception flags all live in a file that one person built and one person maintains. When that person is out, the cash position is late or incomplete. When a formula breaks, the error propagates silently into the numbers the CFO reviews. The cash forecasting process downstream inherits every limitation of the spreadsheet it depends on. We often see organizations running 3 to 5 separate workbooks that feed into a master file, each with its own logic and its own fragility.


The most critical financial view of the day lives in a file nobody else fully understands.


**Where the Delay Creates Real Cost**


The time cost is visible. The decision cost is not:


- Payment runs delayed because the cash position was not ready when AP needed to release the batch


- Short-term borrowing decisions made conservatively because the team could not confirm available balances across entities fast enough


- Intercompany funding requests approved based on yesterday's position because today's was still being assembled


Treasury operations slow down not at the point of decision but at the point of data assembly. The decisions themselves are straightforward once the numbers are trusted.


**Removing the Assembly Step Entirely**


Optimizing the spreadsheet is a dead end. The leverage is in eliminating the assembly step altogether. A platform like Arpari connects to banks directly, aggregates balances and transactions automatically, and delivers a consolidated cash position without requiring portal logins, file exports, or manual formatting. Controllers open one view instead of eight portals. The daily cash positioning workflow shifts from construction to review, which is where finance expertise actually belongs.


**Key Takeaways**


Daily cash positioning in multi-bank environments is slow not because of complexity in the analysis but because of friction in the data collection. Portal logins, format mismatches, and spreadsheet assembly consume the first hours of the day before any treasury decision can be made. Controllers and finance operations leaders should measure the cost of this workflow not in minutes but in the decisions that wait for it to finish. Centralizing bank data aggregation into a single platform converts the morning routine from a manual build into an automated refresh. The cash position should be ready when the team arrives, not when the team finishes building it.
