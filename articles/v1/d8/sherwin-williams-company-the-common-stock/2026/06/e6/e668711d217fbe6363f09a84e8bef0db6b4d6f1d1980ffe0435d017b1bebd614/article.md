# How to Love Your Loneliest Spaces

Every home has them. The lonely in-between spaces that leave no impression as we pass through, the nooks that never quite get styled, the rooms we know we need to deal with someday. Because they aren’t the star of the show, they rarely get attention. But these often-overlooked areas are some of the best spots to experiment with small moments of color.


Inspired by The Loneliest Color™ — Offbeat Green SW 6706 — and its coordinating palette, we’re looking at bringing personality and intentional (if not unconventional) style to the most neglected parts of your home without major renovations or pricey budgets. Just thoughtful color choices and a little creativity.


*Featured Color:[Offbeat Green SW 6706](https://www.sherwin-williams.com/en-us/color/color-family/green-paint-colors/SW6706-offbeat-green)*


**Closets and Dressing Areas: A Little Luxury Behind Closed Doors**


Closets might be the loneliest spaces of all, but they’re also surprisingly rewarding to paint. Color here feels personal. Private. Almost indulgent. Because they tend to be small paint projects, it’s the perfect opportunity to try something a little more daring without long-term commitment.


**Try this:**


- Let yourself go darker, bolder or richer than you would elsewhere
- Use contrast to define shelving or hanging areas


**Loneliest Color energy that works here:**


- Deep, cocooning tones
- Unexpected colors that feel expressive, not decorative


**DIY tip:**
Closets are one of the easiest spaces to experiment with paint because they’re lower stakes and quick to finish. Try something different: wide, statement stripes or color drenching for a richer, more elevated look with minimal effort.


*Featured Color:[Wild Poppy SW 9694](https://www.sherwin-williams.com/en-us/color/color-family/red-paint-colors/SW9694-wild-poppy)*


**Entryways, Mudrooms & Drop Zones: First Impressions That Feel Like Home**


Entryways are one of the hardest working spaces in a home. And they rarely get the design attention they deserve. Adding eye-catching color here sets the tone for everything that follows.


**Try This:**


- Choose a shade that feels like a mood boost in addition to a greeting
- Look for color with depth – something that reveals more the longer you live with it


**Loneliest Color energy that works here:**


- Warm, grounding neutrals with character
- Confident, energizing hues that feel uplifting


**DIY tip:**
Color becomes the backdrop for hooks, baskets and shoes so it should feel grounding but also elevated and welcoming.


*Featured Color:[Minimalist SW 9611](https://www.sherwin-williams.com/en-us/color/color-family/neutral-paint-colors/SW9611-minimalist)*


**Hallways: Turning Pass-Throughs Into Pauses**


Hallways are easy to overlook because we don’t tend to linger and spend time in them. This is exactly why color can make such a difference. It can slow things down and make the space feel more considered and intentional.


**Try this:**


- Use an airy, atmospheric tone that softens the pace and creates a sense of ease
- Choose a color slightly deeper or more complex than the rooms it connects


**Loneliest Color energy that works here:**


- Soft grays with warmth that feel steady but not sterile
- Quiet, moody neutrals that hold space without asking for attention


**DIY tip:**
Paint just the walls or go bolder with trim or the ceiling for a subtle surprise.
