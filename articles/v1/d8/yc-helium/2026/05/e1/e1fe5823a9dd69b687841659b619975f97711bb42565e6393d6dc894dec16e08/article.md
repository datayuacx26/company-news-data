# 100 Paywall Tests that Work

###


1.


When showing plans with different durations, normalize the prices to the same time period.


1.


For example, if you have a $13.99 monthly plan and a $99 yearly plan, show the yearly plan as $8.25 per month. Just write the full annual price the same size or larger to adhere to App Store guidelines.


2.


Add badge for higher-LTV plans designating the discount amount


3.


Consider only adding trials on higher-LTV plans to push users to higher-value plans.


4.


Price annual plans at slight premium on monthly LTV


1.


AKA if the monthly price is $3.99 and the retention is average retention is 4 mo, then the LTV is roughly $16. Consider pricing the annual plan at $19.99, even though it’s a 60% discount off the annual value it will likely increase revenue.


5.


Price should be one of the smallest components on the app


6.


Test how many plans to show — two often wins (monthly/annual, pro/premium) but for some apps adding a third can create the Goldilocks effect of pushing users to the middle


7.


Once someone is on lowest tier, upgrade them.


1.


Even when you’ve converted a user, your job isn’t done. Build additional surfaces to upsell them onto the next tier, whether it’s converting them to an annual plan or a more premium offering.


8.


Especially if your product is seasonal, push them from monthly to yearly at period of highest intent before the season is over.


9.


If your app is in the wellness or faith space, considering offering a plan to let premium users sponsor a plan for other users, thereby increasing overall subscriptions


10.


Consider which plan to default at which point in app, higher-converting entrypoints can generally benefit from defaulting to higher LTV plans (or only showing them)


11.


Allow to choose between a trial and discount offer


1.


E.g. 7 days free then $6.99 / mo, or a discounted $2.99 for the first month.
