# How to Build Individual Graduation Plans [+ Free Template]

Individual Graduation Plans (IGPs) connect students to their interests, strengths, and goals. When they're done well, they guide real planning conversations between counselors, students, and families.


A strong IGP shows students a concrete path toward graduation and beyond. It also evolves as students' circumstances change—new interests, improved grades, different college plans.


In this post, we'll walk through how to create a strong IGP. We'll also share a template you can customize for your state or district requirements.


## **What are Individual Graduation Plans?**


Many states require


[Individual Graduation Plans](https://www.panoramaed.com/blog/what-is-an-individual-graduation-plan) , though they go by different names. South Carolina calls them IGPs. Colorado calls them Individual Career and Academic Plans. Indiana calls them Graduation Pathways. Washington calls them High School and Beyond Plans. But the concept is the same: personalized planning that connects academic progress to career and post-secondary goals.


## **Why Comprehensive Graduation Planning Matters**


Strong IGPs bring together information that usually stays separate: academic progress,


[CTE alignment](https://www.panoramaed.com/blog/cte-student-engagement) , career exploration, post-secondary readiness. When these pieces are connected in one plan, counselors can have more meaningful conversations with students. Students see how their coursework connects to their goals. Families understand the full picture. The plan becomes a real tool, not just a compliance document.


## **How to Write an Individual Graduation Plan**


Comprehensive IGPs require pulling together information from multiple places. They require counselors to have time for students. And counselors must be able to make updates to IGPs as student needs, goals, and circumstances change.


[We've created a template](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1KMHGFJyy4E5bQYCKhqRF-QKr1doF3YdmgdsG374HIrA/edit?usp=sharing) that shows the information to include and discuss: career exploration, CTE alignment, academic progress, distinctions, post-secondary readiness, and action steps.
