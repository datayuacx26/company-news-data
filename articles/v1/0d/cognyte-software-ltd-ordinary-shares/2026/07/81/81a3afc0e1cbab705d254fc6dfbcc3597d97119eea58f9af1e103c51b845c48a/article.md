# The Growing Role of BizINT in Financial Crime Investigations

**TL;DR**


- **BizINT (Business Activity Intelligence)** is the process of collecting, correlating and analyzing corporate, commercial and organizational data to reveal the businesses, beneficial owners and relationships behind financial activity.
- **FININT** reveals where the money has moved. BizINT reveals who is behind it, and the two are most effective when used together.
- **Criminal networks** hide behind complex corporate structures, layered ownership and cross-border transactions so financial transactions alone rarely provide enough context to uncover the true masterminds.
- **Decision intelligence platforms** like NEXYTE combine BizINT and FININT into a single investigative view, helping analysts uncover hidden networks and accelerate evidence-based decisions.


For decades, financial crime investigations have been guided by a simple principle: follow the money.


Tracing the movement of funds has helped authorities uncover fraud, expose money laundering schemes and identify criminal activity. While that principle remains true, today’s financial crime landscape has become far more sophisticated.


Criminal organizations often operate through complex corporate structures, layered ownership, cross-border transactions, nominee directors and seemingly legitimate businesses designed to conceal illicit activity.


As these networks become more sophisticated, financial transactions alone rarely provide enough context to uncover the true masterminds.


While FININT helps investigators provide insight into financial transactions, BizINT combines corporate, commercial and organizational data to reveal the businesses, beneficial owners and relationships behind financial activity.


FININT reveals where the money has moved.


BizINT reveals who is behind it.


Together, BizINT and FININT provide investigators with a more complete understanding of the networks that enable financial crime.


## How BizINT Reveals Hidden Insights


A suspicious transaction rarely provides enough evidence on its own.


Two companies exchange funds. The payment appears legitimate. The transaction follows an expected pattern. Nothing immediately raises concern.


Only after investigators examine corporate records do they discover that the companies share the same beneficial owner, operate from the same address or are connected through a network of intermediary businesses across multiple jurisdictions.


Individually, these findings may seem insignificant.


Together, they expose a hidden financial network.


In practice, these connections rarely emerge from transaction data alone. Investigators must correlate corporate registrations, beneficial ownership records, commercial databases, sanctions information and other intelligence sources to understand how seemingly unrelated entities are connected.


This is where BizINT becomes essential.


[Business Activity Intelligence](https://www.moodys.com/web/en/us/kyc/resources/insights/business-activity-intelligence-bizint-powering-investigations-into-national-security-threats.html) (BizINT) is the process of collecting, correlating and analyzing corporate, commercial and organizational information to understand the businesses, ownership structures and relationships behind financial activity. It draws on data from corporate registries, beneficial ownership records, commercial data providers such as Moody’s, Dun & Bradstreet and Sayari, company filings, sanctions lists and other publicly available sources to provide investigators with a comprehensive view of legal entities and their connections.


By comparison, Financial Intelligence (FININT) is derived from financial activity, including bank transactions, wire transfers, cryptocurrency transactions, mobile money payments and other payment records. While FININT provides insight into how funds move between accounts and entities, it does not always explain the organizations, ownership structures or individuals behind those transactions.


Much of the information used in BizINT originates from[open-source intelligence](https://cognyte.com/blog/open-source-intelligence) (OSINT), where publicly available corporate and commercial information is collected, verified and correlated to support investigations. When combined with FININT, BizINT adds the business context needed to connect financial transactions with the companies, beneficial owners and organizational networks that enable financial crime.


Rather than analyzing financial transactions alone, BizINT helps investigators connect businesses, beneficial owners, corporate hierarchies and financial relationships into a single investigative picture. This broader context enables investigators to identify shell companies, uncover hidden ownership structures and reveal criminal networks that might otherwise remain concealed.


## Why BizINT and FININT Work Better Together


BizINT and FININT are often viewed as separate disciplines, yet they are most effective when used together.


FININT explains how money moves.


BizINT explains who’s moving it.


By combining transactional data with corporate intelligence, investigators can move beyond isolated events and begin to understand the broader networks behind financial activity.


Beneficial ownership information, corporate registrations, sanctions exposure and supply chain relationships all add valuable context that helps investigators identify hidden risks and uncover previously unseen connections.


Together, BIZINT and FININT transform fragmented information into actionable investigative intelligence.
