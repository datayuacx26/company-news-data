## **How to Write a Prompt That Gets Better Storyboards**


The quality of your storyboard depends almost entirely on how clearly you describe the sequence. After testing multiple prompts, these three techniques consistently produced more coherent and useful panels.


### **1. Start With the Setting**


Begin your prompt by describing **where** the scene takes place and what it looks like. Mention the location, time of day, lighting, or atmosphere before introducing the action.


For example, instead of writing:


*A woman drinks coffee.*


Try:


*A cozy neighborhood coffee shop on a rainy morning with warm sunlight coming through large windows. A woman orders coffee and sits by the window.*


Giving the AI a strong visual foundation helps every panel feel like part of the same scene.


### **2. Describe a Sequence, Not a Single Image**


Think of a storyboard as a series of moments rather than one snapshot.


Instead of:


*A man running.*


Write:


*A man checks his watch, starts running through a city park, slows down near a bridge, and finally catches his breath on a bench.*


Because the AI understands multiple actions, it can naturally spread them across several storyboard panels instead of generating nearly identical frames.


### **3. Include the Visual Style**


A few descriptive words can dramatically change the final result.


Consider adding styles such as:


- cinematic
- realistic photography
- anime
- watercolor illustration
- comic book
- sketch style
- bright and colorful
- dark and moody
- minimalist


For example:


*Cinematic, warm golden-hour lighting, realistic photography.*


or


*Hand-drawn pencil storyboard style.*


These style cues help every panel share the same visual identity.
