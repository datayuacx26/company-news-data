### **Step 3: Upload Your Video and Cartoon Reference**


Upload the real video you want to transform, then add your cartoon reference image. If you want to test the workflow quickly, choose one of the built-in presets instead of uploading a separate image.


For the source footage, avoid clips with heavy motion blur, very dark lighting, frequent cuts, or a subject who is heavily blocked by other objects. Magic Hour specifically notes that low light, motion blur, heavy occlusion, and fast cuts can contribute to flicker or drift.


### **Step 4: Generate**


Click **Generate** and let Magic Hour process the clip. For videos under 15 seconds, generation typically takes **less than 5 minutes** . The tool keeps the source video's timing, body movement, poses, and camera motion while replacing the primary subject.


### **Step 5: Review and Download**


When the result is ready, watch the full clip before downloading it. Check whether the cartoon character stays recognizable, whether the movement remains stable, and whether the original background still looks natural.


Magic Hour says Character Replace keeps the original scene and background while changing the primary subject. If the first result is not quite right, try another reference image or preset rather than rebuilding the entire source video.


## **Tips for the Most Natural Result**


- **Start with a simple cartoon design.** Flat colors and bold outlines generally give the AI clearer visual features to work with than highly detailed illustrations.
- **Use slower or medium-paced footage first.** Fast movement gives the model less visual information per frame and can make character details harder to maintain.
- **Keep the source background relatively simple.** A clean background makes the replacement character easier to distinguish from surrounding objects.
- **Use even lighting.** Bright, consistent lighting helps separate the original subject from the background and reduces the conditions that can contribute to flicker or drift.
- **Make the character's silhouette distinctive.** For cartoons, a strong outline, recognizable hairstyle, large accessories, or a simple color palette can make the replacement easier to read than a character with many tiny details.


The biggest takeaway is that the reference image matters just as much as the source video. If the character is difficult to identify in the still image, the same problem can become more noticeable once it is moving.


## **A Note on Copyright**


Technically, an AI tool can accept a reference image of a recognizable cartoon character, but that does not mean you have permission to use the character commercially. Using copyrighted characters from companies such as Disney or Nickelodeon in commercial content generally requires the appropriate rights or license. For commercial projects, the safer approach is to use an original character you created yourself or a character you have properly licensed.


For personal, clearly non-commercial fan content, the situation can vary depending on the country, platform, and specific use, so check the rules that apply to your project.
