## **The Legal Rules in Plain English**


The rules are simpler than most articles make them sound. They come down to three questions.


### **1. Is it for personal use or commercial use?**


Personal projects that you create for yourself, share privately, or publish without making money are generally considered the lowest-risk category in many countries. Once a celebrity voice appears in advertising, sponsored content, paid products, or anything suggesting the celebrity supports your brand, you are entering commercial territory where written permission is generally expected.


If you're unsure which category your project falls into, the safest approach is to treat it as commercial until you know otherwise.


### **2. Is it clearly labeled as parody or satire?**


Parody and satire receive legal protection in countries including the United States, the United Kingdom, and much of Europe, but that protection depends on the circumstances. The joke or commentary should be obvious, viewers should understand that the voice is fictional, and the content should not be used to promote or sell products.


For example, a meme featuring an AI-generated celebrity voice delivering an absurd joke is generally much safer than publishing a fake interview designed to convince people the celebrity actually said those words.


### **3. Could someone mistake it for the real person?**


This is often the clearest line. If a reasonable viewer could believe the celebrity genuinely recorded the audio, both legal and ethical concerns become much more significant regardless of your intent.


Beginning **August 2, 2026** , the **EU AI Act** introduces transparency obligations requiring machine-readable disclosure for AI-generated audio depicting real people. Major platforms such as YouTube, Instagram, and TikTok also require creators to disclose AI-generated content through labels, descriptions, or platform settings when appropriate.

### **Is it legal to use an AI voice of a celebrity?**


For personal, clearly labeled parody, memes, and fan projects, it is generally considered low risk in many countries. Commercial use or content that implies a celebrity endorsement typically requires written permission.
