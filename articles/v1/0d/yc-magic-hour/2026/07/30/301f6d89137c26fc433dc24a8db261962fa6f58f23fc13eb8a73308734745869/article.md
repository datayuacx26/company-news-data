## **Prompts That Didn't Work As Well**


Some prompts produced noticeably weaker results because they were too vague.


- **"A cool animation."**
The AI had almost no creative direction, so the output felt generic.
- **"Funny GIF."**
Humor is subjective. Without describing the characters or situation, the result lacked personality.
- **"Beautiful landscape."**
The scenery looked nice, but there wasn't enough motion to create an interesting loop.


A good rule of thumb is to always describe **who or what is moving, how it's moving, and what style you want.**


## **Tips for Better GIFs**


- **Describe the action, not just the subject.** "A golden retriever catching bubbles in slow motion" produces much more interesting results than simply "a dog."
- **Choose naturally repeating actions.** Water flowing, glowing lights, spinning objects, falling snow, and waving hands create smoother loops.
- **Include a visual style.** Terms like *anime* , *cinematic* , *minimalist* , *pixel art* , or *watercolor* noticeably change the final look.
- **Only change one part of your prompt when regenerating.** Adjusting the subject, style, and action all at once makes it difficult to understand what improved the result.
- **Keep your prompt focused.** During testing, prompts with one clear subject consistently produced cleaner animations than prompts trying to animate several unrelated objects at once.

## **GIF vs MP4: Which Format Should You Download?**


GIF files work almost everywhere and usually autoplay in messaging apps, emails, and websites, but they have larger file sizes and a more limited color range than MP4. MP4 files are much smaller and maintain higher visual quality, although not every platform treats them like traditional GIFs. For Discord, Slack, iMessage, and most email clients, downloading as a GIF is usually the better choice.
