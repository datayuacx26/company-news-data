# How Yelp Continues to Protect Your Content in the Age of AI

At Yelp, our mission has always been to connect people with great local businesses. Trustworthy reviews, ratings, photos and other consumer content help people make more informed decisions and provide invaluable feedback to local businesses.


Consumer trust is one of Yelp’s top priorities, which is why we use[advanced technology and human moderation](https://heyzine.com/flip-book/2025-yelp-trust-and-safety.html) to protect the integrity and quality of content on our platform. Building on more than two decades of rigorous moderation efforts, our cultivation of authentic, high-quality, human-generated content is what continues to set Yelp apart.


In the era of AI, protecting that content has never been more important. Today, we’re publishing our[Content Use Policy](https://www.yelp-support.com/article/Yelp-s-Content-Use-Policy?l=en_US) , a clear explanation of how content on Yelp can and can’t be used, and what we do to protect it.


## **Public Doesn’t Mean Free-for-All**


Yelp is a publicly accessible platform by design. That openness is what makes it useful. But increasingly, bad actors use scraping tools, data brokers, and other unauthorized means to harvest content in bulk — often to train AI models or sell data to those building competing products — with no regard for accuracy, consumer protection, or privacy. When someone uses outdated or cached content from Yelp, they strip away systems we’ve built to keep information reliable.


The risk? Misinformation. Stale content. Previously removed reviews surfacing in places they shouldn’t. That’s bad for consumers and bad for the business owners who work hard to earn their ratings and reviews.


## **What We Do and Why**


We do make Yelp content available to authorized third parties through formal licensing agreements. These are structured, enforceable contracts that govern exactly how content from Yelp can be accessed and used, allowing us to:


- **Know** who is accessing content from Yelp and for what purpose
- **Restrict** prohibited uses that could spread misinformation or harm consumers
- **Require** that deleted content is promptly removed from others’ systems.
- **Ensure** that update-to-date information is available for display and that stale, incorrect, or unreliable information is not.


The organizations we work with typically include businesses monitoring customer sentiment, AI and technology companies that agree to adhere to our data protection guidelines, automotive companies, and academic researchers.


Every partner is subject to strict restrictions. Licensees **may not:**


- Continue using or displaying deleted content (beyond a limited-time cache) or content that has been removed from the most recent daily feed
- Use content to spam, harass, or harm any Yelp user or business
- Modify or alter any part of content from Yelp
- Use content from Yelp for any purpose that may violate any law or regulation
- Exploit content from Yelp to promote any products, services or materials that constitute, promote or are used primarily for spyware, adware and/or other malicious programs or code.


Authorized access comes with accountability. Unauthorized access seeks to avoid it. That’s exactly the problem we’re solving.


## **The User is in Control**


Yelp is open by design, but you are in control of your own participation. You can remove your posted reviews, photos, or videos at any time. You can edit a review within 30 days of posting it, or post a review update to share a newer experience. And you can close your account entirely, removing the content you’ve posted as a consumer.


One important note: we cannot guarantee that third parties who scrape content from Yelp *without authorization* ever delete it. We can only enforce such requirements on authorized users of Yelp content. That’s another reason why the formal agreements we enter with licensees matter: they give us the contractual tools to require that when content is removed from Yelp, it’s removed from our partners’ systems too.


It is important to note, Yelp does not sell or license non-public information from your account or other non-public data. That includes:


- Account details like your email address, phone number, or IP address
- Direct messages you exchange with businesses or other users
- Previously-public content that was removed at the time of licensing
- Browsing or transaction history


You trust us with your data. We take that seriously.


## **Looking Ahead**


The rise of AI has made questions about content source, accuracy, and consent more urgent than ever. Our answer is to be transparent, protective, and consistent, defending the human-generated content that makes Yelp valuable to consumers and businesses alike.


Our[Content Use Policy](https://www.yelp-support.com/article/Yelp-s-Content-Use-Policy?l=en_US) is one part of that commitment. Additionally, we’ve already taken steps to prevent outside AI tools from diminishing the quality of the content we showcase on Yelp, such as adding AI-related policies to our[Content Guidelines](https://www.yelp.com/guidelines) . These policies make clear that AI-generated reviews and photos submitted by users violate Yelp’s content policies, and reviewers are prohibited from using third-party AI tools to write their reviews. While spell-check and grammar tools are permitted, reviews that appear to be drafted or revised using third-party AI tools are not allowed, as content must be authentically created by reviewers, reflecting their firsthand experiences as consumers, to keep Yelp helpful and reliable. We’ll continue updating these resources as the landscape evolves.


For full details, read the[Content Use Policy](https://www.yelp-support.com/article/Yelp-s-Content-Use-Policy?l=en_US) and our[Privacy Policy](https://www.yelp.com/tos/privacy_policy) . If you have questions, visit our[Support Center](https://www.yelp-support.com/) .


Contributions about real experiences are what make Yelp work. Protecting them isn’t just policy. It’s our responsibility.
