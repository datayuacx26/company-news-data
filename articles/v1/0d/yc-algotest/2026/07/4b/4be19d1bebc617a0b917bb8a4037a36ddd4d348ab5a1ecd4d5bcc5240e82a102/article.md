Nifty expiry day is one of the busiest sessions on the NSE, where option premiums swing fast, volumes spike, and the market can move in ways that catch even experienced traders off guard.


We'll cover seven practical rules experienced options traders follow on Nifty expiry day, why expiry feels different from a regular session, and why you should test your approach.


## What Is Nifty Expiry Day?


Every options contract has an expiry, the date it stops trading and settles. Nifty offers both weekly and monthly contracts: weekly ones open and close within days, while monthly ones run longer and carry more built-up[open interest](https://algotest.in/blog/how-to-read-nifty-oi-data) by the time expiry arrives.


Expiry drives the time left on an option, and as that number falls toward zero, the time value baked into the premium disappears with it. That's why the final session of an option's life behaves so differently from any other trading day.


## Nifty Weekly and Monthly Expiry Schedule (2026)


The expiry calendar changed after SEBI's November 2024 rule limiting each exchange to one weekly index-options expiry. NSE kept a weekly contract only on Nifty, so Bank Nifty,[FinNifty,](https://algotest.in/blog/guide-to-finnifty-financial-services-index-trading) and[Midcap Nifty](https://algotest.in/blog/nifty-midcap-150) now settle only monthly.


From September 2025, NSE also moved the Nifty weekly expiry from Thursday to Tuesday, and BSE shifted the Sensex weekly expiry to Thursday.

## Why Nifty Expiry Day Feels Different


On expiry day, time decay ([theta](https://docs.algotest.in/financial-education/options/greek-interactions/) ) accelerates fast, especially in the final two hours: an option worth ₹40 at 1 PM can be worth ₹5 by 3 PM if the market doesn't move in its favour.


Volumes and open interest concentrate around the nearest strikes as traders unwind or roll positions, which can trigger sharp, short bursts of movement.


Because premiums move so quickly, expiry day pulls emotional decisions out of traders who normally stick to a plan, and a trader watching a losing position often holds on, hoping for a reversal that decay is working against every minute.

## 7 Rules Every Options Trader Should Follow on Nifty Expiry Day


### 1. Know the Expiry Schedule Before You Place a Trade


Before entering any trade, confirm whether you're trading nifty expiry day itself or a regular session mid-cycle.


Check the schedule, note any holiday shift, and confirm which index you're trading:[Bank Nifty](https://algotest.in/blog/bank-nifty-expiry-day/) ,[FinNifty](https://algotest.in/blog/finnifty-expiry-dates-times-trade-tricks/) , and[Midcap Nifty](https://algotest.in/blog/maximizing-profits-midcap-nifty-expiry-proven-techniques/) no longer carry their own weekly expiry, only a monthly one. A five-minute check avoids position-sizing and timing mistakes later.


### 2. Respect Time Decay Instead of Chasing Cheap Options


Cheap options on expiry day are cheap for a reason: time value is running out fast. Buying an OTM option because it "only costs ₹10" ignores that theta can erode it to ₹0 within an hour.


Time decay favours option sellers over buyers on expiry day. Before entering, ask whether the setup still makes sense if the[premium](https://algotest.in/blog/black-scholes-option-pricing-model) halves in the next 30 minutes from decay alone, not a price move.


### 3. Choose a Strategy That Matches Market Conditions


Not every strategy fits expiry day. A Long Call or Long Put needs a genuine breakout to work once gamma and theta both work against a stationary price. Range-bound sessions tend to favour strategies like a[Short Straddle](https://docs.algotest.in/financial-education/options-strategies/short-straddle/) or[Iron Condor](https://algotest.in/blog/how-to-use-strategy-builder-iron-condor) , built to profit from decay rather than fight it. Match the strategy to what the market is doing that day, not what worked last week.


### 4. Don't Confuse High Volatility With Easy Profits


Bigger price swings on expiry day cut both ways. A move that helps a well-placed position can just as easily blow through a stop-loss on the other side.


High volatility means bigger opportunity and bigger risk in the same breath, not a free pass to size up. Traders who treat[volatility](https://algotest.in/blog/iv-vs-rv-and-trading-edge-with-vrp) as pure opportunity, without adjusting position size, often take the largest single-day losses of their month.


### 5. Plan Your Exits Before You Enter


Decide your[stop-loss](https://algotest.in/blog/trail-stop-loss-to-break-even-price-when-backtesting) and target before you place the trade, not while watching the position move, since expiry-day price action moves too fast for on-the-fly decisions to stay rational.


Write the exit levels down and treat them as rules, not suggestions. This single habit removes more emotional decision-making from expiry day than any indicator ever will, because the call was already made while you were calm.


### 6. Avoid Trading Every Expiry


Not every Nifty expiry day sets up a good trade. Some weeks are genuinely directionless and choppy, punishing every strategy equally.


Skipping a session isn't a missed opportunity: it's a risk-management decision, and often the right one.


Experienced traders track which setups they skipped, then review whether that preserved capital more often than it cost them.

A strategy that sounds logical on paper can behave differently once real data is involved.[Backtesting](https://algotest.in/blog/how-to-backtest-options-trading-strategies-with-examples/) it across multiple past expiry cycles, trending months and range-bound ones alike, shows how it actually performs, not how it's assumed to.

## Common Mistakes Traders Make on Nifty Expiry Day


→ Ignoring time decay and holding long options positions too late into the session.


→ Trading position sizes that are too large for how fast expiry-day moves can happen.


→ Chasing momentum after a big move has already happened instead of before it.


→ Holding a losing position and hoping for a reversal that decay is working against.


→ Entering a trade without deciding the exit levels in advance.


Most expiry-day losses trace back to one of these five habits, not to a flawed strategy.
