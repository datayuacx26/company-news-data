## What Is Chartink Used For?


Chartink is a stock screening and scanning platform that helps you find stocks based on technical and fundamental conditions.


For example, you can create a scan to find stocks where:


-


RSI is above 50


-


Price is above the 20 EMA


-


Volume is higher than the recent average


-


Price breaks above a previous high


-


Multiple technical conditions occur together


Instead of checking hundreds of stocks manually, you can define your conditions and let the screener find stocks that match them.


This makes Chartink particularly useful when you want to narrow down the market and find potential intraday or swing trading setups.


You can also create alerts for your screening conditions and receive notifications when stocks match them.


## How to Use a Chartink Screener for Intraday Trading


A typical intraday screening workflow looks like this:


1.


Define the setup you want to trade.


2.


Convert it into technical conditions.


3.


Create the conditions in a Chartink screener.


4.


Scan for stocks that match.


5.


Set an alert for matching setups.


6.


Decide whether to take the trade.


For example, you may want to find stocks where:


-


Price is above the 20 EMA


-


RSI is above 50


-


Volume is above its recent average


-


Price breaks the previous high


A Chartink screener can help you find stocks that meet these conditions. But screening only tells you that a stock matches your setup. It does not tell you whether the complete trading strategy works.


To answer that, you need to test the trading logic behind the setup.


## The Limitation of a Stock Screener


A screener can tell you that your conditions are currently being met.


It does not necessarily tell you whether the complete trading strategy works.


Once you have created your setup, you may still want answers to questions such as:


-


How often did this setup generate trades?


-


How many of those trades were profitable?


-


What was the average return?


-


What was the maximum drawdown?


-


What stop loss worked best?


-


What target worked best?


-


Did the strategy work in different market conditions?


-


What would have happened if you followed the setup on every signal?


To answer these questions, you need to test the trading logic itself.

## Backtest Your Trading Strategy


Once your signal logic looks correct, you can test it against historical market data.


This is an important step because a setup that looks good on a few recent charts may not work consistently over a larger period.


With AlgoTest, you can connect your signal to a trading strategy and backtest it.


You can evaluate metrics such as:


-


Cumulative P&L


-


Win rate


-


Maximum drawdown


-


Year wise performance


-


Number of trades


-


Individual trade results


For example, suppose your intraday signal generated 200 trades over your selected historical period.


You can use the[backtest results](https://algotest.in/blog/how-to-backtest-algo-trading-strategies) to understand how that strategy performed instead of relying only on a few successful examples.


A backtest does not guarantee future returns. Actual results can differ because of factors such as slippage, execution delays, liquidity, and live market conditions.
