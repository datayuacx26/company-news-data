If you trade options, knowing the Sensex expiry date and timing is essential. As expiry approaches, time decay and option sensitivity can increase, making price movements more significant for your position.


Sensex weekly options currently expire on Thursday. We'll cover the Sensex expiry schedule, timing, what happens to options on expiry, trading strategies, key risks, and how you can backtest an expiry strategy before trading it live.


## What Is Sensex Expiry Day?


Sensex expiry day is the day when a Sensex derivatives contract reaches the end of its trading life and is settled according to the applicable exchange and clearing rules.


For options traders, expiry matters because there is limited time left for the expected move to happen. This can make factors such as time decay, delta and gamma more important, particularly for options close to the money.


If you’re new to options trading, start with our[options education guide](https://docs.algotest.in/financial-education/options/) to understand the key concepts covered in this article.


## When Is Sensex Expiry Day?


The Sensex weekly expiry day is Thursday.


Under SEBI's current framework, each exchange can offer one weekly benchmark index options contract, with the expiry day set as either Tuesday or Thursday. BSE uses Thursday for its Sensex weekly options.


The regular schedule is:


-


Sensex weekly expiry, Thursday.


-


Nifty 50 weekly expiry, Tuesday.


The exact expiry date can change because of trading holidays or exchange announcements. Always check the latest Sensex contracts listed by BSE before trading.

## What Time Does Sensex Expire?


BSE's regular equity market trading session runs from 9:15 AM to 3:30 PM. Sensex derivatives follow the applicable exchange trading and settlement rules, so you should check the contract specifications before trading.


If you have an open position on expiry day, do not wait until the final few minutes to decide what to do. Set your exit rules in advance and account for the faster price changes that can occur near expiry.

## What Happens on Sensex Expiry Day?


As expiry approaches, an option has very little time left to gain or retain value. This can make option premiums more sensitive to movements in the Sensex.


Four factors become particularly important.


### Time Decay


Options lose time value as expiry approaches. The effect can become more noticeable when there is very little time left.


For an option buyer, the expected move needs to happen within a limited time. If the Sensex stays flat, the premium can decline even if your market view is still correct.


### Delta


Delta shows how much an option's price can change when the underlying moves.


Near expiry, options close to the money can become highly sensitive to movements in the Sensex. A small move in the index can therefore have a noticeable effect on your position.


### Gamma


Gamma measures how quickly an option's delta changes when the underlying moves.


Gamma can become particularly important near expiry, especially for at the money options. This means your position's sensitivity can change quickly during a fast market move.


### Implied Volatility


Implied volatility reflects the market's expectations of future price movement and affects option premiums.


A change in[implied volatility](https://algotest.in/blog/implied-volatility-and-how-to-read-and-compute-iv) can increase or reduce an option's premium even when the Sensex itself has not moved much.

## Why Is Sensex Expiry Day Important for Options Traders?


Sensex expiry day is different because your option has very little time left to work. So, getting the direction right is not enough. The timing and size of the move also matter.


Before entering a Sensex expiry trade, consider:


-


Direction, do you expect the Sensex to rise, fall or stay range bound?


-


Magnitude, how large does the move need to be?


-


Timing, how quickly do you expect it to happen?


-


[Strike selection](https://algotest.in/blog/how-to-choose-the-right-strike-price) , is the option suited to your market view?


-


Risk, how much are you willing to lose?


-


Exit, when will you close the trade if your view is wrong?


For example, if you buy a call expecting a breakout, a late move may not give the option enough time to gain value. This is why a cheap expiry option is not necessarily a good opportunity.


Define your entry, strike, stop loss, target and exit time before trading.


Then backtest the strategy across multiple Sensex expiry sessions to see how it performs in different market conditions.


## Sensex Expiry Day Trading Strategies


There is no single best Sensex expiry strategy. Your choice should depend on your market view, expected volatility and risk tolerance.


Here are some common approaches.


### 1. Long Call or Put


Buy a call when you expect the Sensex to rise, or a put when you expect it to fall.


This approach depends heavily on timing because the option has very little time left to gain value.


Before entering, define your:


-


Entry condition.


-


Strike selection.


-


Stop loss.


-


Target.


-


Maximum loss.


-


Exit time.


### 2. Long Straddle


A long straddle involves buying a call and put with the same strike and expiry.


You may use it when you expect a large Sensex move but are unsure about the direction.


The Sensex needs to move enough to cover the premium paid for both options. If the market stays quiet, both options can lose value quickly.


### 3. Short Straddle


A[short straddle](https://algotest.in/blog/the-920-straddle-strategy-defined) involves selling a call and put with the same strike and expiry.


It can benefit when the Sensex remains within a narrow range, but a sharp move can result in significant losses.


Before using one, understand the:


-


Maximum potential loss.


-


Margin requirement.


-


Stop loss.


-


Position size.


### 4. Iron Condor


An[iron condor](https://algotest.in/blog/iron-condor-strategy) uses four options to create a defined risk and reward structure.


You may consider it when you expect the Sensex to remain within a specific range. However, the position can still lose money if the index moves beyond your expected range.


### 5. Rules Based Directional Strategy


You can build a directional strategy around predefined market conditions.


For example, you could buy a call when the Sensex breaks a specific level and exit using a fixed target or stop loss.


Your rules could include:


-


Entry condition.


-


Strike selection.


-


Stop loss.


-


Target.


-


Maximum trades.


-


Position size.


-


Mandatory exit time.


Defining these rules in advance can help you avoid making emotional decisions during a fast expiry session.

## Sensex Expiry Day Risks You Should Know


Expiry trading can offer short term opportunities, but fast price movements can also increase losses.


Before trading Sensex expiry, understand these key risks.


### Rapid Premium Movement


Option premiums can change quickly when the Sensex moves. A position can turn profitable or move into a loss within a short period.


### Time Decay


If you buy an option and the expected move does not happen in time, time decay can reduce its value. This effect becomes more important as expiry approaches.


### Gamma Risk


Options close to expiry can become highly sensitive to movements in the Sensex. A small move in the index can cause a significant change in an option's delta.


### Short Option Risk


Selling options can expose you to significant losses during a sharp market move. A high probability of profit does not mean the trade has low risk.


### Overtrading


Fast markets can encourage you to take more trades than planned. Set your position size, maximum number of trades and loss limit before the session begins.
