# Untangled Ops episode 3: Smart Venture Media’s Grace Gong on AI as a multiplayer game

Investor, CEO & founder Grace Gong on why AI is a multiplayer game, how to tell real AI from hype, and who owns the handoff when it breaks.


Grace Gong’s calendar is a live read on what the market is actually talking about. She interviews founders, executives, and investors weekly, and hosts the dinners and summit stages where they compare notes. What she keeps hearing from that seat is that AI didn’t collapse work down to one person and one model — it multiplied the number of parties who have to stay in sync. Your AI, your teammate’s AI, your team’s AI, and the humans still accountable for the outcome. That’s the coordination tax, and it’s why every guest on this show answers questions while playing Snake. The snake grows with every pellet until it runs out of room. So does unmanaged coordination.


She’s the founder and CEO of Smart Venture Media, host of the Smart Venture Podcast and Venture with Grace, and the organizer behind the Smart AI Summit. She’s an angel investor and venture scout backing AI startups, a LinkedIn Top Voice with 59,000-plus followers, and the author of “ *How to be a VC* ,” a No. 1 Amazon bestseller in the VC category. She joins Dan O’Connell, CEO of Front, on[Untangled Ops](https://front.com/untangled-ops) to talk about why she’s bullish on the multiplayer AI era instead of the one-person billion-dollar company, how she tells real AI infrastructure from a wrapper, and who she thinks is accountable when a handoff breaks 18 months from now.


## **Key takeaways**


-


**AI turned work into a multiplayer game.** As more companies deploy AI agents, the coordination is about to become even more complicated between AI agents, and AI agents and humans.


-


**Shared context is the thing worth wiring up.** Grace points to Slack’s MCP: the value is pulling context out, not adding another tool.


-


**Being 10% better isn’t a moat for AI success.** The companies that hold up are reinventing how people work with AI and fulfilling meaningful use cases.


-


**When the handoff breaks, a human still owns it.** AI won’t take accountability alone. Humans will remain in the loop.


-


**Trust is the part AI can’t deliver.** People still prefer humans when trust matters most. Judgment, empathy, and context are why the hardest moments stay human.
