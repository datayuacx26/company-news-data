# Palforzia: FDA-Approved Peanut Allergy Treatment Explained

## What Is Peanut Allergy and Why Is It One of the Most Common Food Allergies?


Peanut allergy is an immune system reaction that occurs when the body mistakenly identifies peanut proteins as harmful substances. This triggers symptoms ranging from mild hives to severe anaphylaxis. Peanut allergies have become increasingly prevalent, particularly among children, making them a significant public health concern in developed countries.


### Prevalence in the United States


Peanut allergy affects approximately 2.5% of children in the United States, making it one of the leading causes of food-related anaphylaxis. The prevalence has increased significantly over the past two decades. Studies suggest that early introduction of peanuts to infants may help reduce the risk of developing this allergy.


### Impact on Daily Life


Living with peanut allergy requires constant vigilance and careful planning. Individuals must:


• Read food labels meticulously for hidden peanut ingredients • Communicate dietary restrictions at restaurants and social gatherings • Carry emergency epinephrine auto-injectors at all times • Educate family members, teachers, and caregivers about cross-contamination risks


The psychological burden extends beyond physical symptoms. Many families experience heightened anxiety around meal preparation and social events. Children with peanut allergies may feel isolated during school lunches or birthday parties where peanut-containing foods are present.


## What Is Palforzia and How Does It Work?


Palforzia (peanut allergen powder-dnfp) is an FDA-approved oral immunotherapy treatment designed to reduce the severity of allergic reactions to accidental peanut exposure. The medication works by gradually exposing the immune system to increasing amounts of peanut protein, helping the body build tolerance over time through a carefully monitored desensitization process.


### The Palforzia Dosing Protocol


The treatment follows a structured three-phase approach administered under medical supervision. Each phase serves a specific purpose in building peanut tolerance:


• **Initial dose escalation:** Administered on a single day at a certified healthcare facility with five increasing doses • **Up-Dosing Phase:** Occurs over approximately 22 weeks with 11 increasing dose levels taken daily at home • **Maintenance phase:** Continues indefinitely with a consistent daily dose to maintain desensitization


Patients must take their daily doses with food and avoid exercise for two hours afterward. The treatment requires ongoing commitment, as stopping Palforzia can result in losing the protection it provides against accidental peanut exposure.


## How Can Palforzia Help Reduce the Severity of Allergic Reactions to Peanut Exposure?


Palforzia helps reduce the severity of allergic reactions by gradually desensitizing the immune system to peanut protein through controlled, daily exposure. Clinical trials demonstrated that patients who completed the treatment protocol could tolerate significantly higher amounts of peanut protein compared to those who received a placebo, providing an added layer of protection.


### Clinical Trial Results


The FDA approval of Palforzia was based on a pivotal study involving children and adolescents with confirmed peanut allergies. After completing the up-dosing and maintenance phases, approximately 67% of participants could tolerate at least 600 mg of peanut protein without dose-limiting symptoms. This amount is roughly equivalent to two peanuts, offering meaningful protection against accidental exposure.


### Important Limitations


While Palforzia provides valuable protection, it does not cure peanut allergies. Patients must continue strict peanut avoidance and always carry emergency epinephrine. The treatment creates a safety buffer against small, accidental exposures rather than allowing unrestricted peanut consumption.


## What Are the Safety Information and Indications for Palforzia?


Palforzia carries important safety information that patients and caregivers must understand before starting treatment. The FDA requires administration of the initial dose and all up-dosing phases in certified healthcare settings due to the risk of severe allergic reactions, including anaphylaxis, which can occur during treatment.


### Who Can Use Palforzia


Palforzia is FDA-approved for patients ages 4 through 17 with a confirmed peanut allergy diagnosis. Adults 18 and older may continue treatment if they started as adolescents. Patients must have access to injectable epinephrine and be enrolled in the Risk Evaluation and Mitigation Strategy (REMS) program before beginning therapy.


Certain individuals should not use Palforzia, including those with:


• Uncontrolled asthma • Eosinophilic esophagitis or other eosinophilic gastrointestinal disease • History of severe or life-threatening anaphylaxis


### Potential Side Effects


Most patients experience some side effects during Palforzia treatment, particularly during the up-dosing phase. Common reactions include abdominal pain, throat irritation, itching, nausea, and vomiting. These symptoms typically occur within two hours of taking a dose and often decrease over time as the body adjusts.


Severe allergic reactions remain the most serious concern with Palforzia treatment. Anaphylaxis occurred in approximately 9% of patients during clinical trials. Patients must always have injectable epinephrine available and know how to recognize symptoms requiring emergency treatment.


## What Is It Like Living With Peanut Allergy?


Living with a peanut allergy requires constant vigilance and careful planning in nearly every aspect of daily life. Individuals must read food labels meticulously, communicate dietary restrictions to others, and always carry emergency medication. The condition affects social activities, travel plans, and dining experiences, creating ongoing challenges for patients and their families.


### Managing Peanut Exposure


Avoiding accidental peanut exposure demands attention to detail at home and in public settings. Cross-contamination poses significant risks, even when peanuts aren't a direct ingredient. Families often establish peanut-free zones in their homes and teach children to recognize unsafe foods early.


Key strategies for managing exposure include:


• Reading ingredient labels on all packaged foods before consumption • Asking detailed questions about food preparation at restaurants • Carrying injectable epinephrine at all times • Wearing medical alert identification • Creating emergency action plans for school and work environments


### Emotional and Social Considerations


The psychological burden of peanut allergy extends beyond physical health concerns. Children may feel isolated at birthday parties or school events where peanut-containing treats are served. Parents often experience anxiety about their child's safety when they're not present to supervise meals.


Social situations can become sources of stress rather than enjoyment. Dating, traveling, and attending gatherings require advance planning and open communication about dietary needs. Treatments like Palforzia offer hope by potentially reducing the severity of accidental exposures, which may ease some of this emotional burden.

### Why is Palforzia being discontinued?


Palforzia is being discontinued due to low patient uptake and commercial challenges. The peanut allergy treatment required frequent dosing, strict dietary restrictions, and carried risks of severe allergic reactions. Many families found the demanding protocol difficult to maintain, leading manufacturer Aimmune Therapeutics to withdraw the product from the market.


### What should I avoid while taking Palforzia?


While taking Palforzia, avoid consuming peanuts outside of your prescribed dose. Skip doses if you have fever, illness, asthma flare-ups, or open mouth sores. Avoid exercise, hot baths, and alcohol for three hours after dosing. Do not take Palforzia on an empty stomach.


### What are the side effects of Palforzia?


Common Palforzia side effects include abdominal pain, throat irritation, itching, nausea, and vomiting. More serious reactions can include anaphylaxis, which requires immediate medical attention. Gastrointestinal symptoms like stomach cramps and heartburn are also frequently reported. Patients must carry injectable epinephrine while taking this peanut allergy treatment.


### What are the top 3 rarest allergies?


The top three rarest allergies include aquagenic urticaria (water allergy), which causes hives upon skin contact with water; cold urticaria, triggering reactions to cold temperatures; and seminal plasma hypersensitivity, an allergic reaction to proteins in semen. These conditions affect very few people worldwide.


### What are the first signs of alpha-gal?


The first signs of alpha-gal syndrome typically appear 3-6 hours after eating red meat. Initial symptoms include hives, itching, and skin rash. Gastrointestinal issues like stomach cramps, nausea, and diarrhea are common. Some people experience swelling of lips, throat, or tongue, and difficulty breathing.


### What 8 foods cause 90% of allergies?


The eight foods responsible for 90% of food allergies are milk, eggs, peanuts, tree nuts, wheat, soy, fish, and shellfish. These are commonly referred to as the "Big Eight" allergens and are required to be clearly labeled on food packaging in the United States.
