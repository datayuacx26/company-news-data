## What is Microsoft Fabric (and Power BI)?


Microsoft Fabric is Microsoft's unified analytics platform. It brings data integration, data engineering, data warehousing, data science, Real-Time Intelligence, and business intelligence into one software-as-a-service environment built around OneLake, a single logical data lake for the whole organization. Instead of stitching together separate tools for ingestion, transformation, storage, and reporting, Fabric puts them on one foundation.


Power BI is the business intelligence and visualization layer of that platform, and for most attendees it is the entry point. Analysts and developers use Power BI to model data with DAX, build[semantic models](https://rollstack.com/articles/power-bi-semantic-models) , and publish interactive reports. With Fabric, Power BI reads from OneLake through Direct Lake mode, which gives import-mode performance over large datasets without the refresh latency, and Copilot adds AI assistance for authoring and analysis.


The platform has real scale behind it now. Microsoft cites 28,000+ organizations using Fabric and adoption across 80% of the Fortune 500, including EY, Bridgestone, and Epic. A Forrester study the FabCon team cites puts Fabric at 379% ROI over three years with payback starting inside six months. Those are vendor-sourced numbers, so treat them as directional, but they are the numbers your finance team will see if they go looking.


For teams running on Fabric and Power BI, the platform solves the hard part: getting data clean, governed, and queryable. But most executives, clients, and operators never open a Power BI report. They read board decks, operating reviews, QBRs, and recurring reports. That is the gap[Rollstack](https://rollstack.com/) closes. It sits downstream of OneLake and the semantic model and turns governed Power BI dashboards into refreshed presentations and documents.


## What is FabCon (and what is SQLCON)?


FabCon, short for the Microsoft Fabric Community Conference, is the largest global community-led event for the Microsoft data ecosystem. It covers the full Fabric suite, but it remains the home of the Power BI community and the successor to the standalone Power BI and Microsoft Business Applications summits.


The 2027 change worth understanding is the merge. The event is now branded **FABCON & SQLCON** , and the official framing is "the full Microsoft data ecosystem" rather than Fabric alone. SQLCON brings the relational database world into the same building: SQL Server, Azure SQL, Cosmos DB, migrations, performance tuning, and DBA practice. Your registration covers both conferences, and the organizers explicitly encourage mixing SQLCON sessions with FabCon workshops.


This matters practically. If your team has a Power BI side and a SQL Server side that historically sent people to different events, or nobody at all, 2027 is the year one trip covers both. It also means the hallway conversations are broader than in past years, which is useful if you sit between the database layer and the reporting layer.


The format is keynotes from Microsoft product leadership, hundreds of community-led breakouts, full-day pre-conference workshops, hands-on labs, an expo hall, and community traditions like the DataViz Championship. Note that the previous US edition sold out, so the "register later" plan carries some risk.

## FabCon and SQLCON 2027 tracks


Eighteen tracks on the FabCon side and nine on the SQLCON side, with three shared between them, so about two dozen distinct programs to choose from. The FabCon side:


**Best for Power BI practitioners:** Power BI, Data Development, Data Science in Fabric. Prioritize these if you build reports and models daily and want depth on DAX, semantic modeling, and AI-assisted authoring.


**Best for platform owners and admins:** Fabric Platform - Admin & Governance, Fabric Platform - Dev Experiences, Microsoft Purview, OneLake. Prioritize these if you are responsible for capacities, tenant settings, security, or CI/CD, or if governance is the thing blocking wider rollout.


**Best for data engineers:** Data Engineering, Data Integration, Data Warehousing, Real-Time Intelligence. Prioritize these if you own the pipelines feeding the reports and are weighing Direct Lake or streaming patterns.


**Best for AI and app builders:** Microsoft AI Foundry, Power Platform, Cosmos DB, Databases in Fabric. Prioritize these if you are being asked what Copilot and agentic analytics actually do in production.

The SQLCON side adds: SQL Server & Azure SQL, Databases in Fabric, Migrations, Modernization, Performance, Administration, SQL Tools (SSMS, VS Code extensions, PowerShell), Database Development, and Customer Stories.


The **Customer Stories** track is the one most people underuse. Vendor sessions tell you what a feature does; customer sessions tell you what broke when someone rolled it out at your scale. Full track detail is on the[FabCon tracks page](https://fabriccon.com/program/tracks) .

## Is FabCon 2027 worth it?


For anyone whose work runs through Power BI or Microsoft Fabric, usually yes, and the SQLCON merge makes the math better than in past years because one trip now covers two ecosystems.


FabCon 2027 is most worth it if you are:


- Standardizing or scaling Power BI and Fabric across multiple teams or a large tenant
- Adopting OneLake, Direct Lake, or Real-Time Intelligence and want patterns from teams who already did it
- Building governance, security, and CI/CD practices for Power BI and Fabric
- Running SQL Server or Azure SQL alongside Fabric and want both covered in one week
- Working out where Copilot and agentic analytics actually fit, past the demo


Be honest about the cases where it is weaker. If your team is brand new to the platform, the main conference will move faster than you can absorb, and a Workshops Only pass at $1,395 plus a hotel is a better first year than a full Diamond pass. If you only care about product announcements, those get published online for free within days of the keynote. And if your reporting problem is downstream of the dashboard rather than in it, a conference will diagnose that clearly but will not fix it.


Total cost of attending, realistically: a Gold pass at $1,725, four nights at $189 to $339, plus flights. Budget $2,800 to $3,800 per person for the standard conference, more if you add workshop days.

## Practical tips for FabCon attendees


- **Register before the discount lapses.** With no published end date, the current pricing could change without notice. This is the rare case where "book it now" is literal advice rather than urgency theater.
- **Pick your pass around workshop days, not conference days.** The difference between Gold, Platinum, and Diamond is entirely workshop access. Decide how much hands-on time you want before you buy.
- **Plan Thursday deliberately.** DataViz Championship at 8:00am, sessions all day, Power Hour at 5:30pm, then the aquarium until 10:00pm. It is a fourteen-hour day if you do all of it. Pick your two.
- **Do not book a Friday afternoon flight.** Friday runs a full session program through the afternoon with a DEI lunch in the middle. An evening departure gets you the whole day.
- **Use the SQLCON side even if you are a Power BI person.** The performance and migration tracks explain why your reports are slow in ways the Power BI tracks assume you already know.
- **Bring your laptop for the workshops.** They are hands-on and you need your own machine.
- **Spend real time at the expert booths.** Bringing a specific, hard, reproducible problem to a Microsoft engineer is the single highest-value hour of the week.
- **Comfortable shoes and a layer.** The Congress Center is enormous, you will walk between three venues, and session rooms are aggressively air-conditioned.
- **Take notes by problem, not by session.** Future-you wants one synthesis page tied to the problems you came to solve, not twelve sets of session notes.
