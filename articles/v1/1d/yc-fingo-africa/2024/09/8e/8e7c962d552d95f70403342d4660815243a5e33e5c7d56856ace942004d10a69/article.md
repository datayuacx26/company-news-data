# The Early Bird Advantage: How Investing Young Can Shape Your Financial Future

At 23, Aisha had just started her first job. It wasn’t glamorous, but it paid the bills. Like many young people, she figured she had time to worry about her financial future later. Savings and investments seemed like something for her 30s or 40s; things older established people did. Right now, she wanted to enjoy life: brunches with friends, concerts, and maybe even a little travel. What she didn’t realize was that time, her most valuable asset, was quietly slipping away.


One afternoon, while scrolling through social media, Aisha stumbled upon a post from an old high school friend, Juma. He had recently started a small investment portfolio, and in the caption, he excitedly shared that his investments were already growing. Curious, Aisha reached out to Juma, asking him what this whole “investing thing” was about.


Juma explained it simply: “The earlier you start, the more time your money has to grow — like planting a tree.” Aisha was fascinated, but unsure. “Isn’t investing risky?” she enquired.


Juma described his journey. He began investing KSh 1,000 every month, changing his costs to make it work. For six months, he remained constant, depositing his money in an investment bank and earning 9% interest each year. His savings gradually increased. “It’s like magic,” he explained. “Compound interest makes your money work for you, even while you sleep.” That small investment became the foundation of his future prosperity.


Aisha couldn’t stop thinking about their conversation. She realized that while she was busy living for today, she hadn’t given much thought to her future. She didn’t want to be the person who looked back in regret, wishing she had started investing earlier. She decided to take control of her financial destiny. Instead of spending all her disposable income on short-term pleasures, she committed to putting a portion of her earnings into savings and investments.


But where to begin? The world of finance seemed overwhelming at first, stocks, bonds, mutual funds there were so many options. That’s when she discovered Fingo, a platform that catered specifically to young people like her. Fingo not only made investing simple and accessible, but it also offered up to 15% interest on savings. That kind of return was unheard of in typical investments.


Aisha liked how easy Fingo made the process. She may start small, saving KSh 1,250 per month. It was a size she felt comfortable with, enough to test the waters without being overwhelmed. She began to notice an increase in curiosity over time. The platform provided her daily updates, and her tiny monthly payment began to rise quicker than she imagined.


Encouraged by her consistent progress, Aisha steadily boosted her savings to KSh 2,500, and then KSh 5,000 as she gained confidence. With Fingo giving up to 15% interest, her money wasn’t just sitting there; it was working for her. Every time she checked the app, she could see her fortune expanding in real time, which motivated her to keep going.
