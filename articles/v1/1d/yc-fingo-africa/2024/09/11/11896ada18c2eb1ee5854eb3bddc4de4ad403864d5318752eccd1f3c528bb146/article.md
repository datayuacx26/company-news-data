Barasa, Njeri and Mokeira were three friends in their early 20s, navigating the thrilling yet often confusing world of *adulting* . Each had big dreams of financial freedom, but as they soon learned, the road to a solid bank account is paved with unexpected (and pretty hilarious) potholes.


Njeri had just scored her first full-time job and was buzzing with excitement. Naturally, she decided to treat herself in the biggest way possible, a car. Sure, she lived in a tiny apartment with barely enough space for a bicycle but in her mind, nothing screamed ‘I’ve made it!’ louder than a shiny new ride.

Njeri proudly drove her new wheels to work, soaking up all the compliments from her coworkers. But reality hit harder than a pothole on a rainy Nairobi road. Between monthly loan repayments, insurance, fuel and the dreaded service, her once-fluffy bank account shriveled to an all-time low. What was supposed to be her reward for hard work turned into a monthly reminder of sacrifice. Those spontaneous weekend getaways? Canceled. Friday nyama choma with the squad? Also, a no-go unless they were treating.


Njeri’s lesson? Splurging can be fun, but it can drain your wallet faster than you can say ‘Vitz.’


Barasa, on the other hand, was living his best life too. Armed with his trusty mobile loan apps, he felt invincible. Why wait for payday when he could borrow a little here and there to fund his latest sneaker obsession or treat his crew to a fancy night out? Borrow, spend; borrow, spend; borrow, spend… Barasa was swiping away with the confidence of a CEO.


The plan worked… for a while. Until the repayment alerts started flooding in. Barasa quickly realized he had maxed out his mobile loan limit faster than he could say ‘loan interest.’ And just like that, the once-fun purchases began to feel like a heavy load on his shoulders, with nothing to show for all the money spent. The sneakers didn’t look so fresh when he was dodging repayment messages. Even worse, some of the Apps were sending messages to his family and friends to tell him to pay up or else they’d have to pay for him. Embarrassing!!!

The takeaway? Mobile loans are tempting, but they can grow into a mountain of debt if you’re not careful. Barasa learned the hard way that a few extra days of waiting before buying could’ve saved him from those stressful notifications.


Then there was Mokeira, who was just one Instagram scroll away from financial disaster. One fateful afternoon, she saw an ad for a limited-edition handbag. It was gorgeous and even better it was *on sale* . Her heart skipped a beat. Without hesitation, she clicked ‘buy,’ convinced she’d scored a once-in-a-lifetime deal.


The bag arrived and at first, Mokeira was thrilled. But after a week, she realized it didn’t really match her wardrobe or her lifestyle. The bag, much like Barasa’s mobile loan debt, became a source of regret. Worse, she had blown her rent money on this impulse purchase and now had to borrow from friends just to cover her bills. That handbag? It now lived in the back of her closet, next to last year’s failed DIY project.


Impulse buying can be your wallet’s worst enemy. Next time, give yourself a 24-hour cooling-off period before making any big purchases. Most likely, you’ll realize you didn’t need it in the first place.

One evening, the trio met at their favorite café, sipping cheap coffee (Njeri’s idea, of course) and laughing at their financial misadventures.


Barasa shook his head, “Remember when I thought borrowing from mobile loans was free money? I’m still paying for those sneakers.”


“And don’t get me started on my car,” Njeri groaned. “I swear it’s like the world’s most expensive parking space-filler.”


Mokeira chimed in, “At least you use your car. This handbag? Dust collector.”


As they swapped stories and laughed at their past mistakes, they also realized they had learned a lot. Their 20s were all about trial and error, making mistakes and figuring things out. Now, they were all a little wiser. They had started using budgeting apps, cut down on impulse spending and even built emergency funds for those inevitable curveballs life threw their way.


We’ve all made funny (and not-so-funny) money mistakes in our 20s. But the good news is, it’s all part of the learning process. The trick is to avoid getting stuck in those financial potholes and make a plan to get back on track. Want to avoid ending up like Njeri, Barasa or Mokeira? Start small, whether it’s tracking your expenses, saving up for emergencies or giving yourself a 24-hour buffer before impulse buying.
