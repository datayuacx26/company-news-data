# Nursing Careers: Roles, Career Paths, and Growing Demand

Every May, National Nurses Month recognizes the professionals at the center of patient care. Across hospitals, clinics, schools, and communities, nurses support patient safety, continuity of care, and access across every setting.


Their role extends beyond care into leadership, education, and care coordination. Nurses take on leadership roles, guide patients and families, and support care decisions that influence outcomes across the system.


## **The Role Nurses Play in Healthcare**


Nurses make up the[largest segment of the healthcare workforce](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK493175/) , supporting millions of patients across care settings.


Their work spans patient assessment, care coordination, treatment administration, and day-to-day support for patients and families. They work closely with physicians and care teams to keep care consistent and aligned.


From emergency departments to community-based care, nurses help keep healthcare accessible and functioning.


### **Nursing Career Paths**


One of the strengths of nursing is the range of paths available. Roles vary by setting, specialty, and level of training.


Common paths include:


- **Hospital-Based Nursing** – medical-surgical, ICU, emergency, operating room
- **Primary Care & Advanced Practice** – Nurse Practitioners (NPs), Clinical Nurse Specialists
- **Community & Public Health** – outreach, prevention, population health
- **School Nursing** – supporting student health and daily care needs
- **Specialized Fields** – pediatrics, oncology, geriatrics, mental health, informatics


Advanced roles continue to expand, with[projected growth of up to 38% through 2032.](https://www.aacnnursing.org/news-data/fact-sheets/nursing-shortage)


### **Understanding the Nursing Shortage**


Demand for nurses continues to rise.


The U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics projects approximately[193,100 registered nurse openings each year](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/healthcare/registered-nurses.htm) , driven by workforce demand and retirements.


At the same time, supply is not keeping pace. The Health Resources and Services Administration (HRSA) projects ongoing shortages in many states through 2030 and beyond, affecting access to care.


This gap continues to strain healthcare systems working to maintain consistent staffing and access to care.
