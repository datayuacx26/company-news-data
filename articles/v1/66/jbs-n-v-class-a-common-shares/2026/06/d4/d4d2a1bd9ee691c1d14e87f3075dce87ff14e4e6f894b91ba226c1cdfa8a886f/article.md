# A Three Generation 4-H Legacy That Led Me to JBS

## By: Michael Malloy


My 4‑H story began in 4th grade - the moment I was old enough to join the same local club that shaped both my mother and my grandfather. Even then, I was proud to be a third‑generation 4‑H’er with the Liberty Perry Pacers in Plymouth County, Iowa, carrying forward a tradition that ran deep in our family.


**A Legacy Rooted in Agriculture**


My maternal grandfather, Richard “Pops” Kalas, started 4‑H in 1939 at age 10. He showed beef projects every year through his freshman year of college, and exhibited swine for a time as well. We still have the ribbons that mark his decade of achievements with the club, from 1940 through 1950. Among them, a fifth-place ribbon from the 1950 Sioux City Interstate 4‑H Club Livestock Show & Sale, where Pops’ judging team competed against teams from Iowa, Minnesota, South Dakota, and Nebraska. One of his proudest achievements came in 1947 when he won champion in the Shorthorn class and then Reserve Grand Champion overall at the Plymouth County Fair. He even took that Shorthorn calf to the Iowa State Fair the same year – we know because we still have the photos.


Pops loved telling stories about sleeping in dormitories at the Iowa State Fair and the Aksarben fair in Omaha. By the time he left for Iowa State University to study agronomy, he had already built his own cow‑calf herd on the family farm in Liberty Township. That farm has been in our family since 1876, when my great‑great‑grandfather Kalas immigrated from Germany and settled there. Agriculture and livestock are more than interests to my family. They’re our heritage.


One of my favorite stories he told was about selling foot‑long pencils around the county in the early 1940s to help fund the land that would become the Plymouth County Fairgrounds. Next to his ribbons and photos, we still have one of those pencils in his 4‑H box, along with every one of his livestock record books. Pops later became a 4‑H leader for 25 years and served on multiple livestock committees. On his 83rd birthday, he was inducted into the 2012 Iowa 4‑H Hall of Fame, a moment our family treasures deeply, especially since he passed away less than a year later.


**My Mother’s Chapter in the Story**


The second generation began when my mom and her sisters joined the same club. She started in “girls’ 4‑H,” showing sewing, home economics, and baking projects, but quickly begged Pops to let her join “boys’ 4‑H” so she could show beef. She exhibited Black Angus, Red Angus, and Charolais cattle, staying active through her freshman year of college. She loved every part of her beef projects, except sale day, when she inevitably cried because she had grown so attached to her animals. She also competed in livestock judging and was runner‑up in the 1978 Plymouth County Fair Queen contest. Like Pops, she later became a 4‑H leader for more than 20 years.


**My Turn to Carry the Torch**


Then came the third generation – mine. With two older brothers in 4‑H, I couldn’t wait for my turn. We lived seven miles from the family farm, but I spent countless hours there preparing for the fair. By then, Pops had a sheep herd, so while my oldest brother showed both beef and sheep, my other brother and I focused entirely on sheep. Pops was diagnosed with Parkinson’s around the time I started 4‑H, and he worried beef projects might be too physically demanding for him to manage. But I didn’t mind, because all I wanted was to be on the farm with my grandparents.


Beyond livestock, I participated in Food & Nutrition, Consumer Management, Extemporaneous Speaking, Pride of Iowa, and Share the Fun, where I was able to show off my singing abilities. I held club officer roles, earned outstanding junior and intermediate 4‑H’er awards, and even won Grand Champion in Food & Nutrition, which took me to the Iowa State Fair. My brothers also had their own State Fair moments, one in the Bill Riley Talent Show and the other with a Consumer Management project. While we didn’t sleep in dormitories like Pops did in the 1940s, we loved taking our grandparents with us to Des Moines each year.


**The Skills That Shaped My Career**


By high school, balancing varsity sports and 4‑H became difficult, but the six years I spent in the program were foundational. 4‑H taught me how to set goals, track progress, and deliver results. I learned the realities of livestock care, including illness, loss, and the responsibility that comes with raising animals. I learned to keep detailed records, calculate feed costs, understand average daily gains, and evaluate profit margins. I developed communication and leadership skills early, and I learned the value of community service through highway cleanups, fairground setup, nursing home visits, and helping in the 4‑H building each summer.


Those lessons didn’t say in my childhood, they followed me into my career.


**From 4‑H to JBS: A Natural Path**


I joined JBS USA right out of college, and for the past eight years I’ve had the privilege of working across nearly every part of the beef business – trim and ground beef scheduling, ground beef pricing, raw material procurement for case‑ready facilities, sales, sales management, program management, and now program and specialty brands management. Every role has required the skills I learned in 4‑H: discipline, curiosity, problem‑solving, communication, and a deep respect for agriculture and the people who make it possible.


I know Pops would be proud to see me working in the beef industry, carrying forward the legacy he started in 1939.


**A Full‑Circle Moment**


This is why JBS’s new partnership with 4‑H means so much to me. It feels like a full‑circle moment, an opportunity to support the same organization that shaped my family for three generations and helped guide me into a career I love. I’m excited for the impact this partnership will have on young people across the country, and I’m grateful to work for a company that recognizes the value of investing in the next generation of agricultural leaders.


4‑H gave me my foundation. JBS has given me the opportunity to build on it. Together, I believe we can help create pathways for countless others to do the same.
