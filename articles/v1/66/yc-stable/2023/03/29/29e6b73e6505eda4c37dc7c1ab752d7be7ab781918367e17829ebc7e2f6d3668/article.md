## **What is a virtual business address?**


‍


A virtual business address is a real street address that businesses can use to:


- Receive mail and packages.
- Maintain compliance with state regulations.
- Boost brand image.


When your parcels and mail arrive at a virtual address, it’s securely handled and you (or authorized people in your company) get a notification. You can view it in an online dashboard and decide what action to take on each piece of mail, such as:


- Forwarding it to your physical address
- Scanning the contents
- Shredding it
- Depositing checks
- Automating mail actions


Likewise, a virtual[business address](https://www.usestable.com/blog/how-to-get-an-address-for-a-business) can be provided to your state government for compliance purposes or to[register an LLC](https://www.usestable.com/blog/can-i-use-a-virtual-address-for-llcs) or corporation. This address can also be furnished to the IRS for tax purposes and given to customers or vendors who may need to send your company mail.


‍


Essentially, you can use a[virtual address](https://www.usestable.com/blog/virtual-address-for-privacy) for just about anything you'd use a regular business address for.

## **Using a virtual mailbox vs. a P.O. Box for business**


‍


[Virtual mailboxes](https://www.usestable.com/blog/virtual-mailbox-for-small-business) aren’t just for startups, small businesses, and home-based businesses — they’re great for companies with physical locations as well. If you have multiple locations or are expanding rapidly, virtual mailboxes can give you a central location to receive mail. They’re also ideal for businesses that receive high volumes of mail and checks, as they remove the burden of sorting, consolidating, processing, and depositing manually.


‍


However, some businesses are more familiar with[P.O. Boxes](https://www.usps.com/manage/po-boxes.htm) , particularly if they’ve used them before. With a "traditional" P.O. Box, you will have access to a lockable box that is rented through your local post office and managed by the United States Postal Service (USPS).


‍


P.O. Boxes can be convenient for receiving mail, but the reality is that they aren't *real* street addresses — and unless the post office offers its limited street addressing option, they can't receive mail from other carriers like:


- FedEx
- DHL
- UPS
- Smaller third-party providers


Likewise, P.O. Boxes usually can't be used to incorporate a company,[register an LLC](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/limited-liability-company-llc) , or for tax reporting purposes.


‍


A P.O. Box doesn't always reflect positively on your company's reputation, either. If a potential customer searches for your[business address](https://www.usestable.com/blog/best-virtual-business-address-for-llc) and sees that it's a P.O. Box rather than a real street address, they may consider your company less reputable or trustworthy.


‍


And, of course, there's the general lack of mailbox services offered through a P.O. Box. With a P.O. Box, you're responsible for physically traveling to the post office yourself to pick up your mail. With a virtual mailbox, your mail can be scanned, digitized, forwarded, or securely shredded. You can manage everything without leaving your desk.


‍


For many businesses, setting up a virtual address will simply make more sense than using a P.O. Box.


‍


Not only does a virtual mailbox come with additional services to help you manage your mail, but using a real street address also reflects positively on your business and its professional image.

## Frequently asked questions


‍


### Is it worth getting a virtual address for my home-based business?


Yes. Listing your home address for your business exposes it to the public as soon as you register as an LLC or corporation. A virtual address protects your privacy and ensures you don’t miss important deliveries when you’re away from home.


### What is the difference between a virtual address and a registered agent?


A virtual address is a real street address used for everyday operations, like receiving and handling mail. A registered agent is a legally mandated entity required by the state solely to accept official government notices and legal documents during business hours. Stable serves as an all-in-one platform by offering[registered agent services](https://www.usestable.com/products/registered-agent) alongside a virtual mailbox in all 50 states, plus Washington, D.C., and Puerto Rico. Both your business mail and registered agent documents will be digitized and available to manage in your online Stable dashboard.


### How does the USPS Form 1583 process work for virtual mailboxes?


[USPS Form 1583](https://www.usestable.com/blog/usps-form-1583) is a legal consent form required by the United States Postal Service that authorizes a Commercial Mail Receiving Agency (CMRA) to open and handle your mail.


‍


Completing the form requires two forms of identification: a photo ID (a driver’s license and a passport, for example) and a secondary proof of address (a lease agreement, mortgage document, or vehicle registration, for example). Many virtual mailbox services require you to get this form notarized, but Stable handles this step using in-house digital identity verification.

The best services function as a complete digital mailroom, providing features like same-day scanning, worldwide package forwarding, and physical document storage.
