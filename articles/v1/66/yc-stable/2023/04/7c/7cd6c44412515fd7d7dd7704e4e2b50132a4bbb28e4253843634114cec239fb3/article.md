## Virtual mailbox vs. virtual address: What’s the difference?


‍


These two terms are often used interchangeably, but they're not the same thing.


‍


A **virtual address** is a real, physical street address your business can use for correspondence with banks, the IRS, state agencies, and vendors. It's ideal when you need a permanent professional address — for incorporation, tax filings, or with your vendors — but don't expect to receive much mail there. Unlike a P.O. box, a virtual address is accepted for business registration in all 50 states.


‍


A **virtual mailbox** includes a virtual address plus the full suite of mail management services: receiving, scanning, forwarding, shredding, check depositing, and more. If your business regularly receives physical mail, you need a virtual mailbox — not just an address.


‍

### Fewer restrictions than a P.O. box

## How to choose the right virtual mailbox for your small business


‍


Not all virtual mailboxes are built the same. Here's what to evaluate before you choose:


- **Facility ownership:** Does the provider own and operate its own mail processing facilities, or does it rely on third-party mail centers? Owned facilities mean consistent service quality, better security, and direct accountability. Providers that outsource to independent operators can't guarantee the same standards across locations.
- **Pricing transparency:** Look beyond the starting price. Many services advertise low entry plans but charge extra for scanning, forwarding, check deposits, or going over mail limits. Compare the all-in cost at your expected mail volume.
- **Compliance support:** If you're an LLC or corporation, you're legally required to maintain a registered agent. Some virtual mailbox providers (including Stable) offer registered agent services bundled into the same platform, so legal and compliance documents land in the same place as the rest of your mail.
- **Security standards:** Look for SOC 2 Type II certification, encrypted storage, and background-checked staff. For businesses handling financial, legal, or health-related documents, this is non-negotiable.
- **Form 1583:** All U.S. commercial mail receiving agencies (CMRAs) require customers to file USPS Form 1583. Some providers require you to get this notarized yourself. Stable pre-fills Form 1583 and handles the process for you — no notary required.
- **Team collaboration:** If more than one person needs to access or act on business mail, look for role-based permissions, tagging, and assignment features.
- **Integrations:** The best services connect with your existing tools. Stable offers native Slack notifications plus webhook/Zapier connectivity.
