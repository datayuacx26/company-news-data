# Florida LLC tax benefits: 5 advantages of a Florida business address

***Florida is one of the most tax-friendly states for LLCs: no state income tax on pass-through profits, no franchise tax, generous deductions — including the now-permanent 20% qualified business income deduction — and, as of October 2025, no sales tax on commercial rent. The tradeoff is weaker liability protection for single-member LLCs.***


‍


If you're thinking about[registering your business in another state](https://www.usestable.com/blog/can-i-register-my-business-in-a-different-state) for a more growth-friendly environment, the state of Florida has likely made your shortlist.


‍


With a booming and diverse economy, a large skilled workforce, and business-friendly tax regulations, it's easy to see why the Sunshine State is a top choice for entrepreneurs. ** In fact, Florida ranks fourth in the[Tax Foundation's State Tax Competitiveness Index](https://taxfoundation.org/research/all/state/2026-state-tax-competitiveness-index/) , ahead of every state with a major metro market.


‍


But are Florida's tax benefits really worth registering your business in the state?


‍


Here, we look at the advantages and downsides of registering your limited liability company (LLC) in Florida.


‍


**Key takeaways**


- Florida LLCs pay no state income tax on pass-through profits, and Florida is one of only nine states with no personal income tax.
- Pass-through taxation means LLC profits are taxed once, at the member level, avoiding the double taxation C corporations face.
- The 20% qualified business income (QBI) deduction is now permanent under the One Big Beautiful Bill Act, signed in July 2025.
- Florida charges no franchise tax, and it repealed its sales tax on commercial rent effective October 1, 2025.
- The main drawback: single-member LLCs get weaker creditor protection in Florida than multi-member LLCs.


## **1. Pass-through taxation: avoid double taxation on LLC profits**


‍


A key benefit of getting a[Florida business address](https://www.usestable.com/blog/florida-business-address) for your LLC is that your business will be treated as a pass-through entity for tax purposes.


‍


A pass-through entity passes its profits and losses directly to members' personal tax returns. The LLC itself pays no tax on its income. Members pay all taxes due at the individual level.


‍


A pass-through entity is a business that passes its profits and losses to shareholders' personal tax returns. Essentially, the LLC itself isn't subject to taxation on its income. Instead, all taxes due are paid at the individual level.


‍


One of the main benefits of pass-through taxation is that you[avoid double taxation](https://www.legalzoom.com/articles/what-are-the-benefits-of-pass-through-taxation) , which can be a huge cost saver for small businesses and startups.


‍


It's worth noting that many other states, including California and Massachusetts, also treat LLCs as pass-through entities. However, they impose[large annual state fees ($800 and $500)](https://www.llcuniversity.com/llc-annual-fees-by-state/) and[have some of the highest tax rates in the country](https://taxfoundation.org/research/all/state/2024-state-business-tax-climate-index/) , minimizing the impact of the pass-through tax advantage. Florida's annual report fee, by comparison, is $138.75.


‍


So, if you're looking for an all-around tax-friendly state, Florida is one of your best options.


## **2. No state income tax on LLC profits**


‍


LLC profits that pass through to members aren't taxed at the state level at all. Your Florida LLC's only obligations are federal taxes — including the self-employment tax paid to the Internal Revenue Service (IRS) — and Florida's[6% sales tax](https://www.investopedia.com/articles/personal-finance/101315/taxes-florida-small-businesses-basics.asp) if you sell taxable goods or services.


‍


The current federal[self-employment tax rate is 15.3%](https://www.goldenappleagencyinc.com/blog/florida-self-employed-taxes) of your net earnings — a 12.4% Social Security tax and a 2.9% Medicare tax. While establishing your LLC in Florida doesn't exempt you from this tax (since it's imposed at the federal level), registering your business in the state can lower your overall LLC tax obligation.


‍


Florida is one of only nine states with no personal income tax. The others include Alaska, Nevada, South Dakota, Tennessee, Texas, and Wyoming. Remember, they don't impact your commitment to the IRS, but they make it easier for you to meet it by reducing the cost of doing business.


‍


## **3. Generous business deductions, including the permanent 20% QBI deduction**


‍


Deductions are expenses you can subtract from your taxable income to reduce your tax obligation. They are vital for LLCs as they can place you in a lower tax bracket, opening your business up to significant tax savings. The more you save, the more funds you can re-invest in your business to support growth.


‍


Florida has numerous tax deduction opportunities that help create a business-friendly environment. LLCs can deduct the following expenses from their income:


- Equipment costs
- Employee benefits
- Business marketing and advertising expenses
- Startup costs
- Travel expenses
- Business asset depreciation costs


LLC owners can also claim a 20% deduction on qualified business income (QBI) from pass-through entities. Originally created by the 2017 Tax Cuts and Jobs Act and set to expire after 2025, the[QBI deduction](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/qualified-business-income-deduction) was made permanent by the One Big Beautiful Bill Act, signed into law on July 4, 2025. Starting with the 2026 tax year, the law also guarantees a minimum $400 deduction for owners with at least $1,000 of qualified business income.


‍


It's worth noting that Florida's deductions aren't limited to the expenses mentioned above. But you’ll need to consult a tax professional to fully understand the tax benefits Florida LLCs and their owners receive.


‍


## 4. No franchise tax and no more tax on commercial rent


‍


Unlike California or Delaware, Florida imposes no minimum annual franchise tax on LLCs. And Florida's 5.5% corporate income tax doesn't apply to LLCs unless you elect to be taxed as a C corporation.


‍


Florida also just retired its most unusual tax. Effective October 1, 2025, the state repealed its sales tax on commercial rent. Florida had been the only state in the country to tax commercial leases. If your LLC rents office, retail, or warehouse space in Florida, that line item is gone.


‍


Of course, if your business runs on a[virtual address](https://www.usestable.com/products/virtual-address) instead of a lease, you were never paying it in the first place.


‍


## 5. The option to elect S corporation taxation


‍


Florida LLCs can elect to be taxed as an S corporation with the IRS without changing their legal structure. Members who actively work in the business pay themselves a reasonable salary, and remaining profits distribute as pass-through income that isn't subject to the 15.3% self-employment tax. For LLCs netting roughly $50,000 or more per year, the savings can be substantial. Talk to a CPA about whether the election makes sense for your business.


‍


## **Downsides of forming an LLC in Florida**


‍


While Florida is undoubtedly one of the best options for entrepreneurs looking for tax-friendly environments, it may not be ideal for everyone. Here’s why.


‍


### **Single-member LLCs get weaker creditor protection in Florida**


‍


One of the main benefits of an LLC business structure is that it inherently provides a high degree of personal liability protection. Business assets are considered separate from personal assets, meaning that you can't personally be held liable for business risks, and creditors can't go after your business to resolve your personal debts.


‍


That said, some states offer remedies for creditors to help minimize their liability. In Florida, as in many states, creditors can get a charging order — a lien authorized by a court that allows them to seek distributions that an LLC would otherwise issue to the debtor.


‍


However, this order doesn't give creditors the right to participate in decision-making or any other LLC activity. As such, if the LLC decides not to issue distributions to a creditor, there's nothing they can do (at least in states like Nevada).


‍


In Florida, the charging order is considered the[only remedy available to a judgment creditor in the case of multi-member LLCs](https://www.munizzilaw.com/blog/the-advantage-of-multi-member-llcs-in-florida) . But there's less protection for single-member LLCs. A creditor can seek a charging order as a first step and foreclose on your membership interest if you refuse or are unable to make distributions.


‍


A foreclosure means that you'll have to sell your LLC interest. When you do, the purchaser (who is often the creditor) becomes a member of your LLC and can run "your" business however they see fit.


‍


As the new sole owner, they may dissolve the LLC and sell its assets to satisfy your debt. This makes the single-member LLC business structure unfavorable in Florida.


‍


So, if you're set on forming an LLC in the state, you may want to increase the number of members to ensure asset protection. To form a multi-member LLC, you can partner with another entrepreneur and share ownership interest — this means you'll share the rights to distributions, as well as profits and losses.


‍


If you don't want to dilute your ownership, you can always add another member without sharing interest. Instead of an economic interest, the other member has basic rights, such as the right to audit LLC records and participate in non-essential decision-making.


‍


This option is protected in Florida law through the[Florida Revised Limited Liability Company Act section 605.0401(4)](https://codes.findlaw.com/fl/title-xxxvi-business-organizations/fl-st-sect-605-0401/) .


‍

## Florida LLC tax FAQs


‍


### **Do Florida LLCs pay state income tax?**


‍


No. Florida has no personal income tax, so LLC profits that pass through to members aren't taxed at the state level. The exception: an LLC that elects C corporation taxation pays Florida's 5.5% corporate income tax.


‍


### **How much does it cost to start an LLC in Florida?**


‍


The state filing fee is $125, which includes registered agent designation. After that, Florida LLCs pay a $138.75 annual report fee, due May 1 each year.


‍


### **Do I need to live in Florida to form a Florida LLC?**


‍


No. Florida has no residency requirement for LLC owners. You'll need a registered agent with a physical Florida street address and a principal business address, both of which a service like Stable can provide.


‍


### **Does a Florida LLC need a registered agent?**


‍


Yes. Every Florida LLC must designate a registered agent with a physical street address in Florida to receive service of process and official state mail.
