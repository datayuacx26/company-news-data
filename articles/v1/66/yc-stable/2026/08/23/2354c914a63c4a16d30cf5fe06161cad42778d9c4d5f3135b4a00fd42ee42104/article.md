## What is a registered agent?


‍


A registered agent is a designated individual who is responsible for receiving legal correspondence on behalf of your business. In order to be a registered agent, an individual must be at least 18 years old, be available during normal business hours, and have a physical street address in the state where the business is registered.


‍


You’re required to have a registered agent as an LLC or corporation. If your LLC or corporation is registered in multiple states, you must have a registered agent in each of the states you’re registered in.


### What do registered agents do for property management LLCs?


Property management teams rarely spend every workday behind one desk. Between unit inspections, maintenance issues, resident meetings, vendor coordination, and owner reporting, a registered agent provides the fixed, reliable point of contact your LLC still needs for legal and state correspondence. For property management LLCs, that comes down to three core responsibilities:


‍


- **Service of process:** Legal notices and subpoenas have to be officially served, and property managers can face claims involving residents, vendors, contractors, or incidents at a managed property. Your registered agent receives those documents at a designated address, so a process server does not have to arrive at your leasing office while your team is meeting with residents, owners, or prospects. **‍**
- **State compliance and correspondence:** Y **‍** our registered agent can receive state compliance correspondence on your behalf, including annual report reminders, state tax notices, and official filing mandates. That becomes especially useful as a property management company adds entities or expands into additional states, where notices may otherwise be scattered across different offices and addresses. **‍**
- **Availability requirements:** Registered agents maintain a strict presence at a physical street address during standard business hours (9:00 AM to 5:00 PM) to accept official deliveries. That requirement can clash with the day-to-day reality of property management, where your team may be walking units, overseeing a turn, responding to an emergency maintenance issue, or meeting a contractor across town. P.O. boxes cannot be used in place of a registered agent.


## Risks of being your own registered agent


‍


The law simply states that you have to designate a registered agent as an LLC or corporation, but you don’t have to hire a professional. You can be your own property management registered agent, but there are several risks.


### Exposure of personal address


When you appoint a registered agent, the registered agent address appears in public state records. If you name yourself and use your home address, that can make your residence easier for residents, vendors, contractors, or other members of the public to find.


‍


For a property manager who handles sensitive situations such as disputes, evictions, security deposits, or vendor conflicts, keeping a personal residence separate from the business can provide an important layer of privacy.


‍


Hiring a professional registered agent gives your property management LLC a designated registered agent address instead of using your home address for that purpose. This keeps the privacy benefit focused where it belongs: on your public registered agent record.


‍


Your everyday business mail can still be handled separately through a business address or virtual mailbox.


### Potential embarrassing situations


Property managers routinely meet with residents, prospective tenants, property owners, and vendors at leasing offices and management locations. If you use that same address as your registered agent address, a process server could arrive during one of those interactions in connection with a resident claim, vendor dispute, or other lawsuit. Even when the matter is routine, receiving service in a public-facing office can create an awkward and distracting situation for your team.


‍


Using a professional registered agent routes service of process to the registered agent’s designated address instead of your leasing or management office. Your team can still be notified quickly, but residents, prospects, and property owners do not have to witness a process server walking into the office.


### Lack of mobility


A registered agent must be available at the registered office during standard business hours, which is a poor fit for many property management roles. A property manager may start the day at a move-in, head to an inspection, meet a roofer or plumber, and finish at another property handling a resident issue. Tying that person to one address just so the business can reliably receive official documents works against the way property management teams actually operate.

## How to appoint or change your registered agent


‍


The process of appointing or changing your registered agent varies depending on whether you’re a new or existing LLC.


‍


If you’re starting a new LLC, you can name your registered agent directly on your Articles of Organization during your initial state filings. You’ll need the name of your registered agent, the street address, and their consent to act.


‍


If you have an existing LLC, you can file a Statement of Change of Registered Agent with your Secretary of State and pay the administrative filing fee to change your registered agent. Fees vary by state. If you’re switching from another provider, Stable can help with the Change of Registered Agent filing.

## Frequently asked questions


### Can I be my own registered agent for my property management company?


Yes, you can be your own registered agent for your property management company. However, acting as your own registered agent means exposing your personal or business address, potentially harming your reputation when lawsuits are served, and limiting your mobility during normal business hours. For a full breakdown of the pros and cons, see our post on whether you can[be your own registered agent](https://www.usestable.com/blog/everything-you-need-to-know-about-registered-agents-and-how-to-get-one) .


### Do I need a separate business entity or registered agent for each rental property?


No. A registered agent is appointed for a business entity, not for an individual building or rental unit. If multiple properties are owned or managed through the same LLC, that LLC generally uses one registered agent in each state where it is registered or qualified. If your portfolio uses separate LLCs for different properties, each entity may have its own registered-agent requirement, so confirm the structure and state-specific rules with your legal or compliance advisor.


### What are the risks of using my home address for a rental business LLC?


Using your home address as your registered agent address can make that address publicly available and expose your personal residence to tenants, vendors, or other members of the public. A professional registered agent lets you use the agent’s address for registered agent purposes instead, helping keep your home address private while ensuring official legal and state correspondence is reliably received.
