# Heat Pump Maintenance: The Complete Homeowner's Guide (2026)

A heat pump is a low-drama machine, but it is not a no-maintenance one. A little regular care keeps it running efficiently, extends its life toward and past the fifteen-year mark, and catches small problems before they become expensive ones. The good news is that most of the routine work is simple enough to do yourself, and the rest is a quick annual visit from a pro. Here is exactly what your heat pump needs, what you can handle, and what to leave to a technician.


## The short answer


Heat pump maintenance splits into two parts. The homeowner part is easy and frequent: change or clean the air filter every one to three months, and keep the outdoor unit clear of leaves, debris, and plants. The professional part is an annual tune-up, ideally once before the cooling season and once before heating, where a technician checks the refrigerant, electrical connections, coils, and airflow. Skipping maintenance is the fastest way to lose efficiency, run up your bills, and shorten the system's life. Do the simple things regularly and book one pro visit a year, and a good heat pump will run efficiently for well over a decade.

## Why maintenance matters more than people think


Three things ride on it. Efficiency comes first: a clogged filter or a dirty coil forces the system to work harder for the same comfort, and that shows up directly on your energy bill. Lifespan comes next: a well-maintained heat pump commonly lasts fifteen years or more, while a neglected one wears out early. And early detection matters: a quick annual check catches a low refrigerant charge or a failing part while it is a small fix, not a mid-winter breakdown. Many manufacturer warranties also expect documented regular maintenance, so skipping it can put coverage at risk.


## What you can do yourself


None of this requires tools beyond a garden hose, and it covers most of what keeps a heat pump healthy between professional visits.


- **Change or clean the air filter every one to three months.** This is the single most important thing you can do. A dirty filter chokes airflow, drags down efficiency, and strains the system. Check it monthly and swap or wash it when it looks dirty.
- **Keep the outdoor unit clear.** Leaves, grass clippings, and debris block airflow. Keep at least two feet of clearance around it, and trim back plants and shrubs that crowd it.
- **Gently rinse the outdoor coil.** With the power off, a light spray from a garden hose clears dust and pollen off the fins. Do not use a pressure washer, which can bend them.
- **Keep indoor vents and registers unblocked.** Furniture, rugs, and curtains over vents cut airflow and make the system work harder.
- **Watch and listen.** Rising bills, weak airflow, ice on the outdoor unit outside of a normal defrost cycle, or new noises are all worth acting on early.


## What to leave to a professional


An annual professional tune-up covers the things that need training, tools, or refrigerant handling. A technician will typically check and adjust the refrigerant charge, inspect and tighten electrical connections, deep-clean the indoor and outdoor coils, test the defrost cycle, verify airflow, check the thermostat calibration, and, on ducted systems, inspect the ductwork. This is also where a small developing problem gets caught. The ideal cadence is twice a year, once ahead of summer and once ahead of winter, since a heat pump works year round, but at minimum book it once a year.


## A simple seasonal checklist


If you want a rhythm to follow, this is all it takes:


- **Monthly:** check the air filter, glance at the outdoor unit for debris.
- **Every one to three months:** change or clean the filter.
- **Spring, before cooling season:** clear and rinse the outdoor unit, book or complete a professional tune-up.
- **Fall, before heating season:** clear the outdoor unit again, confirm the system heats properly, ideally a second professional check.
- **Year round:** keep vents clear and watch for changes in bills, airflow, or noise.


## Signs your heat pump needs attention


Call a technician if you notice weak or reduced airflow, ice building up on the outdoor unit outside of a brief defrost cycle, the system turning on and off rapidly, a steady climb in energy bills with no other explanation, or new grinding, rattling, or hissing sounds. These usually point to something a professional can fix quickly if caught early, and can turn into bigger repairs if ignored.


## What does maintenance cost?


A professional tune-up is a modest, predictable cost, and it is small next to what it protects: the efficiency of a system you rely on daily and a replacement you would rather delay by years. The filters you change yourself are inexpensive. The real cost is skipping it, since neglected systems run less efficiently, cost more to run, and fail sooner. ## Frequently asked questions


### How often should a heat pump be serviced?


Have a professional tune-up at least once a year, and ideally twice, once before the cooling season and once before heating, since a heat pump runs all year. Between visits, check the filter monthly and change it every one to three months.


### Can I maintain a heat pump myself?


Much of it, yes. Changing filters, keeping the outdoor unit clear, gently rinsing the coil, and keeping vents unblocked are all homeowner tasks. Refrigerant, electrical, and deep-cleaning work should be left to a technician.


### How often should I change my heat pump filter?


Check it monthly and change or clean it every one to three months, more often if you have pets, allergies, or a dusty environment. This is the most important routine task for keeping efficiency up.


### What happens if you do not maintain a heat pump?


It loses efficiency and costs more to run, small problems go undetected until they become breakdowns, the system wears out sooner, and you may put your warranty at risk. Basic upkeep prevents nearly all of this.


### How much does heat pump maintenance cost?


A professional tune-up is a modest annual cost, and filters are inexpensive. It is far cheaper than the higher bills and early replacement that come from neglecting the system.


## The bottom line


Heat pump maintenance is not complicated. Change the filter, keep the outdoor unit clear, and book a professional tune-up once or twice a year. That simple routine protects your efficiency, keeps your bills down, and helps a good system last well past fifteen years. Skip it, and you pay for it in higher bills and a shorter life. The care is easy, and it pays for itself.
