# How to Create a Resume That Passes ATS | Step-by-Step Guide

In today’s competitive job market, many companies use **Applicant Tracking Systems (ATS)** to filter resumes before they reach recruiters. If your resume is not ATS-friendly, it might get rejected automatically—no matter how qualified you are.


This guide will teach you how to create a **resume that passes ATS checks** , ensuring your application reaches hiring managers and boosts your chances of landing an interview.


## Step 1: Understand How ATS Works


Before creating your resume, it’s crucial to understand how an **Applicant Tracking System (ATS)** works. ATS scans resumes for specific **keywords, formatting, and structure** to determine if they match the job description.


### Key Features of ATS


- - **Keyword Matching** – ATS looks for relevant keywords from the job description.
- - **Proper Formatting** – Avoid fancy fonts, graphics, and tables that ATS may not read.
- - **Clear Section Headings** – Use standard headings like "Education," "Work Experience," and "Skills."
- - **File Format Matters** – Submit your resume in PDF or DOCX format to ensure ATS compatibility.

## Step 2: Choose the Right Resume Format


Selecting the correct **resume format** plays a significant role in **passing ATS filters** . The best format for ATS is:


### ATS-Approved Resume Formats


- - **Reverse Chronological** – Lists work experience from most recent to oldest.
- - **Hybrid (Combination)** – Combines skills and experience for a well-rounded profile.


**Avoid creative resume formats** with columns, images, and infographics, as ATS might not read them properly.

## Step 3: Optimize Your Resume with Keywords


Many resumes get rejected because they lack **job-specific keywords** that ATS looks for.


### How to Find the Right Keywords?


- -Copy the job description and identify frequently used **skills and industry terms** .
- -Use an **ATS Resume Checker** to test keyword optimization.
- -Include **both abbreviations and full terms** (e.g., “SEO” and “Search Engine Optimization”).


**Example:** If applying for a marketing role, include keywords like **"SEO," "Google Ads," "content marketing," and "social media management."**

## Step 4: Structure Your Resume for ATS Compatibility


A well-structured resume increases your chances of passing ATS screening. Follow this standard structure:


### Essential Resume Sections


- - **Professional Summary** – A brief overview of your qualifications.
- - **Work Experience** – List jobs in reverse chronological order.
- - **Education** – Include degree, institution, and graduation year.
- - **Skills Section** – Highlight relevant hard and soft skills.
- - **Certifications & Achievements** – Showcase additional qualifications.


**Pro Tip:** Use **simple fonts (Arial, Calibri, Times New Roman, size 11-12)** and **bullet points** for easy readability.

## Step 5: Avoid Common Resume Mistakes


Even if your resume has the right keywords, formatting mistakes can still lead to rejection.


### Mistakes to Avoid


- - **Using tables, images, or columns** – ATS may not read these correctly.
- - **Not customizing your resume** – One-size-fits-all resumes fail ATS checks.
- - **Using uncommon fonts or colors** – Stick to professional black-and-white formatting.
- - **Incorrect file format** – Always submit **PDF or DOCX** format.
