# Zscaler and OpenAI: Zero Trust AI Cybersecurity Partnership

Generative AI is rewriting cybersecurity, and attackers can use AI-based techniques to move faster and scale. Enterprises must secure GenAI through a robust[AI security](https://www.zscaler.com/zpedia/what-is-ai-security) program, so sensitive software and services aren’t weakened by new vulnerabilities. Zscaler’s approach focuses on detecting, triaging, and mitigating issues earlier in the SDLC and patching faster to improve resiliency.


Zero Trust reduces exposure by making critical apps and software invisible to the Internet, systematically eliminating the internet-facing attack surface created by traditional VPNs and firewalls. When paired with frontier OpenAI models, Zscaler’s Zero Trust Exchange adds policy-controlled access plus strong protection around the apps AI helps build and defend.


Organizations adopting AI tools face risks from unpatched software flaws, weak controls, and adversaries who can model and exploit attack paths faster. AI agents and connected tools can introduce complex, hard-to-see exposure, which is why Zscaler emphasizes turning large security-signal volumes into actionable intelligence, prioritizing true risk, and accelerating remediation.


Through OpenAI’s Trusted Access for Cyber (TAC) program, Zscaler plans to integrate GPT 5.4-Cyber and Codex Security into its Zero Trust Exchange and secure SDLC workflows. That enables earlier vulnerability detection, faster patching, and offensive-informed posture hardening. It also strengthens Zscaler’s AI Red Teaming and Agentic SecOps capabilities for customers.


AI cybersecurity is protecting both AI-enabled defenses and the systems AI touches: data, models, prompts, agents, and integrations. In the article’s view, it includes securing software development with AI assistance, continuously red teaming and prompt hardening, analyzing AI assets and tool risk, and using agentic SecOps with guardrails and human validation.
