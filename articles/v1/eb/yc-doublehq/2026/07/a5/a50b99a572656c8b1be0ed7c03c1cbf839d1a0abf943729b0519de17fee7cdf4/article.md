## What Is Accounting Reconciliation Software


Accounting reconciliation software is a category of financial tech that compares records across two or more sources to confirm they match. Think[bank statements against your general ledger](https://doublehq.com/blog/bank-reconciliation-complete-guide-with-examples/) , or accounts payable against vendor invoices. When the numbers align, your books are clean. When they don't, the software flags the gap so you can investigate.


Before dedicated tools existed, this work lived in spreadsheets. You'd export data, paste it side by side, and manually hunt for discrepancies. Automated reconciliation software replaces that process by pulling records directly from your accounting system or bank feed, running comparisons on your behalf, and surfacing only the items that need attention.

## Types of Reconciliations Accounting Software Can Handle


Not every reconciliation workflow is the same, and the right software depends on which types you actually need to run.


Bank reconciliation matches bank statements to the general ledger. Credit card reconciliation compares card statements to expense records in your accounting system. Balance sheet reconciliation confirms every balance sheet account ties to documented support. Intercompany reconciliation eliminates cross-entity transactions before consolidated reporting. Accrual reconciliation handles prepaid expenses, accrued liabilities, and deferred revenue against recognition schedules.


Some tools handle all five. Others focus on bank feeds and stop there. Knowing your mix before you shop saves time and keeps you from paying for features you will never use.


## Core Features to Consider in Reconciliation Tools


Reconciliation tools aren't all built the same, and the features that matter most depend heavily on your volume, team size, and how complex your financial data is.


Here are the core capabilities worth reviewing before committing to any tool:


- Automated matching lets the software pair transactions across sources without manual input, which cuts down on hours spent cross-referencing spreadsheets.

## Common Implementation Challenges and How to Tackle Them


Switching to a new reconciliation tool rarely goes smoothly on day one. Here are the friction points teams run into most often, and what actually helps.


- Data migration headaches are common when historical transaction records exist in incompatible formats. Running a[parallel close during the first month](https://doublehq.com/blog/month-end-close-checklist/) gives your team a safety net while the new system proves itself.
- User adoption slows when staff are used to manual spreadsheet workflows. Short, role-specific training sessions work better than long all-hands demos.
- Integration gaps with existing ERP or payroll systems can delay go-live timelines. Confirming API availability before purchasing saves considerable rework later.
- Audit trail requirements vary by industry. Make sure the tool you choose logs every change with timestamps and user attribution before committing to a contract.


## How Reconciliation Software Supports Audit and Compliance Requirements


Auditors want more than a final number. They want to see how you got there. Reconciliation software builds that trail as you work: every match, exception, and sign-off gets logged with timestamps and user attribution, so audit requests don't turn into a week of digging through old files.


For teams covered by SOX, preparer and reviewer sign-off workflows are non-negotiable. Software that enforces those approval chains creates documented evidence of control compliance that holds up under scrutiny. GAAP consistency follows the same logic. When reconciliation rules run identically each period, the system captures that consistency for you instead of relying on institutional memory to prove it later.
