## What Is a Rollforward in Accounting?


A rollforward in accounting is a schedule that tracks how an account balance moves from one period to the next. Start with the opening balance, document every transaction and adjustment that occurred during the period, and arrive at the closing balance.


The formula is: Beginning Balance + Additions - Subtractions = Ending Balance.


That simple structure creates something valuable: an audit trail. Instead of comparing two numbers and wondering what happened between them, a rollforward shows exactly what moved an account from its starting point to its close.


## How Rollforward Accounting Works


The process is sequential by design:


1. Pull the prior period's closing balance as your opening figure
2. Record each addition (purchases, period-end accruals, new debt issued)
3. Record each subtraction (disposals, payments, amortization)
4. Sum the activity to arrive at a closing balance
5. Verify that closing balance matches your general ledger


Every line in the schedule should tie back to a source document. That traceability is what makes the rollforward useful beyond basic record-keeping. When the closing balance agrees with your ledger, you have a clean, documented account history. When it doesn't, the schedule itself tells you exactly where to look for[variance analysis](https://doublehq.com/blog/variance-accounting-guide/) .


## Rollforward vs. Reconciliation: Understanding the Difference


Both terms come up constantly in accounting workflows, so the confusion is understandable. A rollforward tracks movement: it shows how a balance got from Point A to Point B over a period. A reconciliation confirms accuracy: it verifies that two independent records agree at a single point in time.


The simplest way to think about it is that rollforwards explain change, while reconciliations confirm correctness. You often run both together, using the rollforward to build the ending balance and then tying that balance to an external source to catch any discrepancies.

### Fixed Asset (PP&E) Rollforward


Starts with the opening net book value, adds capital expenditures, subtracts disposals, and deducts depreciation to arrive at the closing balance. Auditors rely on this to verify asset additions and retirements.


### Accounts Receivable Rollforward


Tracks beginning AR, adds new invoices, subtracts cash collections and write-offs, and confirms the ending balance ties to the balance sheet.


### Debt Rollforward Schedule


Opens with outstanding principal, adds new borrowings, removes repayments, and accounts for any amortization of debt issuance costs.


### Deferred Revenue Rollforward


Begins with the prior period deferred balance, adds newly collected but[unearned revenue](https://doublehq.com/accruals/) , and subtracts amounts recognized as earned during the period.[Building accurate deferred revenue schedules](https://www.hubifi.com/blog/deferred-revenue-rollforward-steps) requires careful tracking of contract terms and performance obligation timing.


### Equity Rollforward Schedule


Tracks opening stockholders equity through net income, dividends, share issuances, and repurchases to reach the closing equity balance.


### ARR Rollforward


Common in SaaS finance, this tracks annual recurring revenue from the opening balance through new bookings, expansions, contractions, and churn to show net ARR movement.


## Fixed Asset Rollforward Schedules


A fixed asset rollforward schedule tracks the movement of long-term assets across a reporting period. It starts with the opening balance of property, plant, and equipment, then layers in additions, disposals, depreciation, and impairments to arrive at the closing balance.


Auditors rely on these schedules heavily. They verify that every addition ties to a capital expenditure authorization, disposals match proceeds recorded in cash, and accumulated depreciation is calculated correctly.


### Key Line Items in a PP&E Rollforward


- Opening gross asset balance per prior period financials
- Capital additions during the period, tied to invoices or capex approvals
- Disposals at cost, with corresponding removal of accumulated depreciation
- Depreciation expense for the period, verified against the income statement
- Closing net book value, which should agree to the balance sheet


## Rollforward Procedures in Audit


Roll forward procedures are audit techniques used to extend the coverage of audit evidence from an interim testing date to the financial statement date. When auditors complete substantive testing before year-end, they need assurance that the account balances remain fairly stated through the reporting date.[PCAOB Standards require these procedures](https://optro.ai/blog/roll-forward-testing) for public companies to update interim testing results through year-end.


There are several scenarios where roll forward procedures become necessary.


### When Auditors Use Roll Forward Procedures


- When interim audit work was performed months before the balance sheet date and conditions may have changed
- When testing manual controls, auditors perform roll forward procedures to confirm those controls remained effective through year-end
- When account balances tested at an interim date need validation through the final reporting period


### What Roll Forward Procedures Typically Involve


- Reviewing transactions that occurred between the interim testing date and year-end for unusual items or unexpected fluctuations
- Tying the interim tested balance to the year-end balance using a rollforward schedule
- Obtaining and testing additional samples from the roll forward period when risk warrants it
- Confirming that no major changes in internal controls occurred after interim testing was completed


The roll forward period refers to the gap between the interim audit date and the financial statement date. The longer this gap, the more substantive the roll forward procedures typically need to be.

## Best Practices for Creating Rollforward Schedules


A well-structured rollforward schedule does more than track numbers. It creates a clear audit trail and keeps your financial reporting defensible under scrutiny.


Here are the practices that separate reliable schedules from ones that create problems later:


- Start with a verified opening balance that ties directly to the prior period's closing balance, confirmed and signed off by the appropriate reviewer using[close management systems](https://doublehq.com/blog/best-close-management-systems-for-finance-teams/) .
- Use a consistent format across all schedules so that anyone reviewing the file can follow the logic without needing a walkthrough.
- Document every line item with a source reference, whether that's a journal entry number, invoice, or supporting sub-schedule.
- Verify the closing balance against the general ledger before the schedule is considered complete, not after review comments come in.
- Date-stamp all updates and track who made changes, especially in shared files where multiple team members contribute.
- Flag unusual movements, such as large unexplained decreases or activity that falls outside normal patterns, with a brief explanatory note directly in the schedule.


## Common Rollforward Mistakes and How to Avoid Them


A few mistakes show up repeatedly across rollforward schedules, and each one undermines what the schedule is supposed to accomplish.


- Removing only net book value on disposals instead of eliminating both gross asset cost and accumulated depreciation separately. This inflates both balance sheet line items and creates ghost balances that compound over time.
- Missing the reversal of a prior period accrual, which double-counts the expense and distorts the opening balance before a single new transaction is added.
- Timing differences where activity posts in the wrong period, so the schedule closes clean but against an incorrect number.
- Copying GL output line-for-line without a validation step. A rollforward that simply mirrors the ledger doesn't verify anything, which is why validation steps are required before finalizing any schedule.


That last issue points to something worth stating plainly: a rollforward without supporting documentation is not a true reconciliation. It records what happened. It doesn't confirm that what happened was correct.
