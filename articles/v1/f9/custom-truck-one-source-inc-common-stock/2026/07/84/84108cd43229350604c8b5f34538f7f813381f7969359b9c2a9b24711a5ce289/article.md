# The Ultimate Checklist for Buying Used Heavy Equipment

# Buying Used Heavy Equipment Ultimate Checklist


Buying used heavy equipment is a great way to expand your fleet or replace older machines. The key is knowing exactly what to inspect in the equipment before you commit. After all, you’ll need a quality piece of equipment to avoid downtime and repairs. Follow our equipment inspection checklist to make more confident purchases.


## What to Do Before You Inspect the Machine


The physical inspection is only half the battle when buying used heavy equipment. Before you ever lay eyes on the machine, follow these steps to position yourself for success:


- **Define your specs and job requirements:** Think like a fleet manager by defining the job the machine needs to accomplish. Know your required specifications, like lift capacity, engine power, GVWR and application-specific features, before you start shopping. This prevents you from overbuying capabilities you won’t use or underbuying power you’ll desperately need. Whether you’re considering[specialized vocational trucks](https://www.customtruck.com/used/vocational) or tracked equipment, knowing your requirements helps you focus the search and negotiate from a position of knowledge.
- **Know how to vet the seller:** You can source your used equipment from different places, whether it’s a dealer, auction or private seller. The most important thing across all three sources is the seller’s ability to provide transparent, verifiable information about a machine’s history and condition. A reputable dealer who services what they sell offers a significant advantage — they can back up claims with maintenance records performed by their own technicians.
- **Set a realistic budget beyond the sticker price:** The total cost of ownership extends well beyond the purchase price. Budget for immediate costs like transport, taxes and insurance, as well as potential near-term expenses for tires, fluids or other wear parts. Always have a contingency fund for initial repairs or maintenance. When evaluating equipment like excavators, dozers and wheel loaders, factor in the operating costs specific to each machine type.


## Heavy Equipment Prepurchase Inspection Checklist


Follow this used heavy equipment checklist to protect your investment and choose a top-quality machine:


### 1. Inspect the Frame and Structure for Cracks and Wear


Start with a thorough walk-around of the entire machine. Look for signs of structural stress, especially around high-load areas, as well as fresh paint that could be hiding rust or previous repairs. Inspect all welds carefully, especially around booms, cranes and mounting points.


Make sure the equipment doesn’t have any cracks, as these could mean it’s been stressed beyond its design limits. Examine the chassis for any bending or twisting that suggests the equipment has been in an accident or used improperly. Any inspection you perform should meet the[federal structural integrity requirements](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-49/subtitle-B/chapter-III/subchapter-B/part-396) outlined by the FMCSA.


### 2. Evaluate the Engine, Power Train and Exhaust


The engine can tell you a lot about how the machine was operated. Check for oil leaks around seals, gaskets and the oil pan. Listen closely during a cold start for any strange sounds, such as knocking, which could indicate worn bearings. Remove the oil fill cap while the engine is running and check for excessive blowby, which suggests worn piston rings or cylinder walls.


Note the color of exhaust smoke, as different colors indicate specific engine problems. Blue may mean burning oil, white suggests coolant in the combustion chamber, and black points to fuel system problems. Test the transmission through all gears and note how the machine shifts under load.


### 3. Test the Hydraulic System for Leaks and Performance


For vocational trucks and heavy equipment, a well-functioning hydraulic system is essential. Check all the hoses for brittleness, cracks, and signs of previous repairs. Look for leaks around hydraulic cylinders, pumps, and fittings.


Operate the equipment under a light load to test for hydraulic drift, where a raised implement slowly lowers on its own. This indicates worn seals or valves. Hydraulic fluid levels and condition show you whether the system is in good shape.


### 4. Check All Cab Controls and Electrical Systems


Get in the cab and test every single component. Make sure every light works, every gauge responds accurately, and every switch functions as designed. Test the PTO engagement if applicable.


A faulty electrical system can be difficult and expensive to diagnose and repair, so thorough testing now will save you from trouble later on. Check the condition of wiring harnesses for signs of rodent damage or previous amateur repairs.


### 5. Examine the Undercarriage, Tires or Tracks


For wheeled equipment, check for uneven tire wear patterns, as these usually indicate alignment or suspension issues. Measure tread depth and check for sidewall damage.


For tracked equipment, examine the sprockets, idlers, and the track itself for signs of wear, cracks, and missing components. An undercarriage replacement will be a major expense, so make sure these components are in good condition to help calculate your true acquisition cost.


## How to Verify the Equipment’s True History


Documentation is just as important as your physical inspection. Knowing how to buy used heavy equipment means understanding service records, as these will tell you the true story of how equipment was treated during its working life:


- **Review the service history:** A quality service history shows consistent, preventive maintenance rather than just reactive repairs. Look for regular fluid changes, scheduled inspections and proactive component replacements. This documentation can prove that the previous owner invested in the machine’s longevity. Equipment[maintained by expert technicians](https://www.customtruck.com/service/) will usually come with detailed records, so you can be more confident in the machine’s condition.
- **Know the difference between high hours and hard hours:** An hour meter doesn’t tell the full story about a machine’s condition. A truck with high highway miles might be in much better shape than a low-hour unit that spent its entire life in severe-duty applications. Consider engine idle time versus PTO hours. Idle hours[significantly impact engine wear](https://www.fleetequipmentmag.com/apus-used-truck-valuation/) , so you’ll need to factor them into the used equipment valuation. A machine with an auxiliary power unit might show high hours but less actual engine stress.
- **Confirm you’re getting a clean title with no liens:** Always run a formal lien check on any equipment you’re considering purchasing. This ensures the seller has the legal right to transfer ownership and that you won’t inherit outstanding debts attached to the machine. A clean title protects your investment from future legal complications.
