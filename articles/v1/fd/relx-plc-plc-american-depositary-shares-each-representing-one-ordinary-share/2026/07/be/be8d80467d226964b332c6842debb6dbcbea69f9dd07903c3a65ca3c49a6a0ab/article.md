# Advancing health, improving lives

H.E. Mrs Lordina Mahama, First Lady of Ghana, Dr Beatrice Wiafe Addai, co-Lead Commissioner, Hon. Kwaku Agyemang-Manu, Minister for Health, and David Collingridge in 2022 at the launch of The Lancet Oncology Commission on Cancer Control in Sub-Saharan Africa, in Accra, Ghana.


### **How do the Lancet journals advocate for health, and how do you define advocacy?**


For us, our advocacy work is focused on the best science and its potential for improving lives. We use many approaches, including our Series (a collection of commissioned articles on a single theme), Lancet sessions at major conferences or world health events, and editorial collaborations with academic leaders and policymakers. Our flagship platform for advocating for change is publication of large white papers, called Commissions, in which we bring together an eminent group of experts to investigate and devise solutions for major unmet needs in health and medicine. The Lancet journals have phenomenal convening power, combined with the highest standards of academic rigour, peer review and publishing expertise - we aim to produce practice changing reports, and work with authors to elevate recommendations to policy makers.


### **Can you point to examples where the Lancet journals’ advocacy has led to measurable policy or health outcomes?**


There are a lot of examples, but one project I’m particularly proud of is[The Lancet Oncology’s Commission on cancer control in sub-Saharan Africa](https://www.thelancet.com/commissions-do/cancer-in-sub-saharan-africa) . This report was the first comprehensive undertaking to map out the inequities, inequalities and deficits in cancer care across the African continent and, importantly, provide actionable solutions. The report was also undertaken by African experts providing African solutions, which is the only way real change is possible. To date, the Commission has helped drive the issue of cancer care firmly up political agendas in many African nations. For instance, we have already seen a new comprehensive cancer hospital being built in Ghana; 800,000 girls in Tanzania have been vaccinated against HPV; and in collaboration with Bloomberg, key academics, and the former White House Moonshot team, a new financing platform has been devised to inject funding into cancer care systems in Africa, starting with Uganda.
