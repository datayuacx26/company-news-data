# Making every voice count

Turning feedback into action often starts with understanding what sits behind the scores.


For Akiko Sumikawa, Head of Transfer Pricing, and her colleagues Jon Henderson, Mary Ann Horgan and Trevor Gladwin, who lead people initiatives across the RELX Corporate Tax function, the survey is more than a set of results - it's an opportunity to better understand employees' experiences.


Working in a business where increasing tax complexity leaves little spare capacity, the group deliberately shares responsibility for people initiatives to ensure they can dedicate time to listening, analysing feedback and following through on actions.


While the survey data helps identify priorities, they know the comments often tell the richer story. "We look at both the numerical scores and the free-form comments," Akiko explains. "Sometimes the comments can be difficult to interpret, so we create opportunities to explore them together."


Using dedicated sessions during Tax-wide calls, colleagues discuss the results through live polls and anonymous whiteboards, allowing teams to ask questions, test assumptions and safely explore the reasons behind the feedback.


In a prior EOS, employees highlighted workload in compliance processes as an area for improvement. Together, the team reviewed long-established ways of working in the US and introduced significant changes to compliance activities. While adapting to new processes took time, subsequent EOS results showed the changes had made a positive difference.


For Akiko, implementing change is only part of the journey. Communicating progress matters just as much. While some people matters must remain confidential, keeping colleagues informed wherever possible reinforces that their feedback has been heard - and acted upon . It's an approach that reminds employees that every response contributes to continuous improvement, and that making every voice count is a shared responsibility.
