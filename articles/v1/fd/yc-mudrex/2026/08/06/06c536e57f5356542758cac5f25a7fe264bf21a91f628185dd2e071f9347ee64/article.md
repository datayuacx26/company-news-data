# Futures Trading for Coin Set Investors

Coin Sets are built for riding a theme over weeks and months. But crypto also moves in short, sharp bursts, a good day for Bitcoin, a sudden move on some news, that a basket investment isn’t designed to capture. Futures let you act on those short-term moves directly, on top of the Coin Sets you already hold.


## **Take part in the market’s short-term moves**


A Coin Set tracks a theme over time, so a single day’s spike or dip barely moves the needle on your overall position, by design. Futures work differently: you’re taking a position on where a price goes over hours or days, not months, so a short-term move that a Coin Set would barely register can be a full trade on its own. It’s a way to act on a view you have right now, without touching your longer-term Coin Set holdings at all.


## **What futures trading actually is**


Where a Coin Set gives you ownership of a basket over time, a futures position is a direct bet on price direction over a much shorter window. Pick long if you expect a rise or short if you expect a fall, and what you make or lose is tied entirely to how far that price actually moves, not to owning any of the underlying coins.

## **What leverage actually does**


Leverage means you’re not limited to trading only as much as you deposit. Put up a small margin, and you can take a position several times its size, so a short-term move that would only be worth acting on with a large amount of capital becomes worth acting on with a lot less. It’s what turns a passing view on price into something actually worth trading.

## **Manage every trade with SL/TP**


For a short-term trade, having your exit points decided before you enter matters even more, since there’s less time to react if things move fast. A stop-loss closes the position automatically once price passes a level you set against you, and a take-profit does the same once it hits your target. Attach both directly to your futures order on Mudrex, so a quick trade still has a defined exit the moment it opens.

## **What liquidation is, and how it’s avoided**


When a losing position runs out of margin to draw on, the exchange closes it automatically, whether you were ready or not. That’s liquidation, and for a fast-moving short-term trade it can arrive quicker than you’d expect. Keeping leverage low and always using a stop-loss is what keeps you well clear of it.

## **Keep emotion out of short-term trades**


Short-term trades move fast, which makes it tempting to chase a move you missed or double down right after a loss. That instinct is how one quick trade turns into several bad ones in a row. Deciding your size and exit before you place the trade keeps a short-term position short-term, instead of turning into a stressed, oversized bet.
