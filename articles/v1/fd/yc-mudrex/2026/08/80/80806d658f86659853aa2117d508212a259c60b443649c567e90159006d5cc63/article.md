# A Guide to Hedging Your Spot Holdings with Futures

You already know how to buy and hold crypto. Futures let you do something spot trading can’t: protect what you already own, without having to sell it, and profit even when the market is heading down. If you’ve ever watched a holding drop and felt stuck just waiting it out, this is the piece that was missing.


## **What futures trading actually is**


Spot trading means owning the coin, which is why a falling price just means sitting on a loss until it recovers. Futures work on a different mechanic entirely: you take a position on price direction itself, long or short, without ever holding the coin, and your outcome depends purely on how far price moves from your entry. That mechanic, profiting on a move without owning the asset, is exactly what makes hedging a Spot position possible.

## **What leverage actually does**


Leverage is what makes futures capital-efficient: a relatively small deposit can control a much larger position, so you don’t need to tie up a large amount of margin just to open one. For a Spot holder, that matters specifically because it means sizing a hedge against your holding doesn’t require locking away anywhere near its full value.

## **Protect your Spot holdings with a 1x short**


Here’s the part most Spot holders don’t know they can do: if you hold crypto in Spot and you’re worried about a drop, you can open a short futures position of a similar size, roughly 1x your Spot holding, without selling anything. If the price falls, the loss on your Spot position is offset by a gain on the short, so your overall value stays close to flat through the dip. Once you’re comfortable holding through the volatility again, you simply close the short.

## **Manage the trade with SL/TP**


A stop-loss and a take-profit are how you fix a trade’s exit points in advance, rather than reacting to price as it happens. The stop-loss automatically closes the position if it moves against you past a level you set; the take-profit closes it once your target is reached. On Mudrex, both attach directly to your futures order as you place it, so every trade you open, hedge or otherwise, has a defined exit from the start.

## **What liquidation is, and how it’s avoided**


A position gets liquidated once its losses run through all the margin backing it, at which point the exchange closes it for you, on its terms, not yours. This is mostly a function of leverage: lower leverage and a stop-loss on every trade, hedges included, keep you well clear of it.

A hedge is meant to be a calm, planned decision, not a panicked one made mid-crash. Deciding your hedge size and exit conditions in advance, before you’re staring at a red portfolio, is what keeps a protective trade protective instead of turning into a stressed, oversized bet.
