# Influencer Marketing Budget Benchmarks: Beauty vs. F&B vs. Fashion in 2026

**TL;DR:** Beauty and fashion brands are running at nearly identical scale in creator marketing attention in 2025 — Traackr's Creator Advantage 2026 report found average Brand Vitality Scores of 687K for beauty and 672K for fashion across each category's top 25 brands, both far ahead of food and beverage at 258K — but beauty converts that attention roughly 4x more efficiently (5.9% VIT-per-reach vs. fashion's 1.4%). Overall US influencer marketing spend sits somewhere between $10.5 billion (eMarketer's 2025 estimate) and $37 billion (IAB's broader creator ad-spend measure), a gap that reflects real differences in methodology, not disagreement about growth direction. Real, vertical-specific budget figures are harder to find than headline market-size numbers: the clearest one is Kolsquare's finding that most European F&B brands spent between £130,000 and £870,000 on influencer marketing in 2025, and TikTok Shop's affiliate commission bands run meaningfully higher for beauty (15-30%) than fashion (10-15%) in 2026.


You've probably seen the stat: "34% of brands now allocate 40%+ of their marketing budget to influencer marketing," attributed to a Gartner CMO Spend Survey. It's repeated across dozens of marketing blogs. It also doesn't appear anywhere on Gartner's actual site — and Gartner's real, verified 2026 CMO Spend Survey found the opposite: influencer marketing was among the channels CMOs planned to cut. That's the problem with most "influencer marketing statistics" content: the numbers get copied from site to site until nobody can trace them back to a real study. Below is what we could actually verify, vertical by vertical, including what genuinely differs between beauty, food and beverage, and fashion, and what doesn't.


## How much do brands actually spend on influencer marketing in 2026, and why do the numbers disagree?


The exact size of the influencer marketing market depends heavily on what's being measured, and two credible, methodologically transparent sources land in very different places. eMarketer's forecast puts US influencer marketing spend at $10.5 billion in 2025, rising to $13.7 billion by 2027 (Source:[eMarketer, June 2025](https://www.emarketer.com/content/influencer-marketing-set-surpass--13-billion-by-2027) ). IAB's narrower measure of "intentional brand ad spend on creators" puts the number at $37 billion in 2025, up 26% year over year and growing nearly four times faster than the total media industry (Source:[IAB, November 2025](https://www.iab.com/news/creator-economy-ad-spend-to-reach-37-billion-in-2025-growing-4x-faster-than-total-media-industry-according-to-iab/) ). Both are real; they're just measuring different things — eMarketer's figure is closer to the broader "influencer marketing industry," while IAB's is scoped to paid ad spend specifically. That gap is the single most important thing to flag before quoting "the" market size to a CMO.


Cross-vertical creator-attention data tells a clearer story than dollar figures do. Traackr's Creator Advantage 2026 report, based on 760,000+ US creators and 10 million-plus pieces of branded content, found beauty and fashion essentially tied at the top of creator marketing attention in 2025, with food and beverage running at roughly a third of their scale (Source:[BeautyMatter / Traackr Creator Advantage 2026](https://beautymatter.com/articles/the-creator-economy-has-a-winner-and-its-beauty) ). CreatorIQ's sixth annual State of Creator Marketing report (1,723 respondents across 17 industries and 9 regions) found average reported creator marketing budgets grew 171% year over year, with 71% of organizations increasing investment (Source:[CreatorIQ, 2026](https://www.creatoriq.com/press/releases/creator-marketing-enters-the-era-of-efficacy-brands-demand-scalable-roi-as-creator-budgets-surge-171-creatoriq-report-finds) ). Set against that, Gartner's actual 2026 CMO Spend Survey (401 CMOs across North America, the UK, and Europe, fielded January-March 2026) found influencer marketing among the channels CMOs planned to reduce spend on, even as digital media overall rose to 67.5% of marketing budgets (Source:[Chief Marketer / Gartner 2026 CMO Spend Survey](https://www.chiefmarketer.com/gartner-cmo-spend-survey-budgets-reflect-increase-in-consumption-based-martech-paid-media-spend/) ). Both things can be true at once: specialist creator-marketing platforms report rising budgets among their own customer base, while a broader CMO survey shows more caution at the aggregate level — the same caution that shows up when agencies and in-house teams compare notes on[which discovery tools are actually worth the spend](https://syncly.app/blog/best-influencer-discovery-tools-agencies) . WARC's Voice of the Marketer survey (1,000+ marketers) found only 19% of brand marketers expect overall marketing budgets to rise in 2026, even though 59% expect business conditions to improve — but most still plan to increase online video, influencer, and social spend specifically (Source:[WARC, December 2025](https://www.warc.com/en/press/press-releases/25-12-03_budget-expectations-fall-faster-than-business-optimism-for-marketers-in-2026) ).


## How much do beauty brands spend on influencer marketing, and how do they allocate it?


Beauty is the clearest efficiency leader in creator marketing of any vertical in this comparison, and it's the only category where total creator-marketing attention actually grew in 2025. Traackr's data found beauty's average Brand Vitality Score (VIT — Traackr's own attention/engagement composite, not an industry-wide standard) rose 22% year over year despite the average audience size reached per post falling 30%, driven by 37% growth in the number of creators activated — meaning beauty brands are winning by working with more, smaller, more efficient creators rather than chasing reach per creator (Source:[BeautyMatter / Traackr Creator Advantage 2026](https://beautymatter.com/articles/the-creator-economy-has-a-winner-and-its-beauty) ). That same analysis put beauty's VIT-per-reach efficiency at 5.9%, well ahead of fashion's 1.4% and personal care's 1.9%.


Beauty also has a well-documented, if now dated, history of aggressive digital/influencer spend intensity. Estée Lauder disclosed in 2019 that roughly 75% of its marketing investment was going to social media influencers, up from 22% digital a few years earlier (Source:[Adweek, 2019](https://www.adweek.com/brand-marketing/75-of-estee-lauders-marketing-budget-is-going-to-influencers/) ), and L'Oréal separately disclosed shifting digital's share of spend from 50% to 70% during the pandemic period (Source:[Campaign, 2020](https://www.campaignlive.co.uk/article/loreal-boosts-digitals-share-marketing-spend-50-70/1686473) ). Both figures are historical evidence of an early, aggressive category shift, not current 2026 percentages, and shouldn't be quoted as this year's numbers.


**Creator tier** : nano (0-10K followers), micro (10K-100K), mid-tier (100K-500K), macro (500K-1M), mega/celebrity (1M+). Within beauty specifically, Traackr's data shows nano creators are by far the largest population (125,000 creators, averaging 9 VIT each) while mega creators are the smallest population (10,800 creators) but carry the highest average VIT per creator (Source:[Traackr, March 2026](https://www.traackr.com/influencer-marketing-blog/top-beauty-influencers-vit) ) — real evidence that beauty's tiers trade population size against per-creator attention, even without a verifiable per-post dollar-pricing breakdown by tier. On measurement, Glossy+ Research's 2026 survey (125 respondents, including named marketers at Ulta and Milani Cosmetics) found impressions (28%) and conversions (27%) as the top two metrics beauty and lifestyle marketers track, with 96% using influencers for social content creation specifically, not just paid amplification (Source:[Glossy, 2026](https://www.glossy.co/beauty/glossy-research-the-marketers-2026-guide-to-creator-marketing-with-duolingo-ulta-and-youtube/) ).


## How much do food and beverage brands spend on influencer marketing, and how does it differ from beauty?


F&B brands are expanding their creator rosters aggressively, but that expansion isn't translating into proportional attention growth the way it does in beauty. Traackr's data put F&B's average VIT at 258K in 2025 — roughly a third of beauty's and fashion's scale — even as F&B brands grew their creator base 22% year over year, with the category's overall VIT actually declining 7% in the same period (Source:[BeautyMatter / Traackr Creator Advantage 2026](https://beautymatter.com/articles/the-creator-economy-has-a-winner-and-its-beauty) ).


F&B also produced the clearest real budget-range figure found anywhere in this comparison. Kolsquare's State of Influencer Marketing in Europe: Food & Beverage report (35 brand-side respondents across seven European markets, fielded September-October 2025) found more than half of surveyed F&B brands spent between £130,000 and £870,000 on influencer marketing in 2025, with 74% planning to work with more influencers and 70% planning to increase budgets in 2026 (Source:[Kolsquare, 2025](https://www.kolsquare.com/en/free-ressources/the-state-of-influencer-marketing-in-europe-food-beverage) ). The same survey found 46% of F&B brands work with 11-49 influencers annually, while 15% work with more than 250 — a wider spread than "food and beverage is just a smaller category" would suggest. F&B buying criteria also skew differently than beauty's: 86% of respondents said influencer partners must align with corporate values and 74% expect ad-disclosure compliance, both ranked above raw follower count (43%) — and roughly 49% of F&B brands run influencer campaigns as part of PR and communications strategy rather than as a standalone paid-media line item, a structural difference from beauty and fashion's more performance-metric-first framing (Source: same).


## How much do fashion brands spend on influencer marketing, and how does creator-tier spend compare?


Fashion runs at nearly the same total creator-marketing scale as beauty — 672K average VIT versus beauty's 687K — but converts that attention far less efficiently, at 1.4% VIT-per-reach versus beauty's 5.9% (Source:[BeautyMatter / Traackr Creator Advantage 2026](https://beautymatter.com/articles/the-creator-economy-has-a-winner-and-its-beauty) ). Fashion brands are activating comparable creator volume and reach to beauty; they're just generating proportionally less engagement per impression from it.


Traackr's dedicated 2025 Fashion Influencer Engagement Rate Benchmark (342 fashion brands across the US, UK, and France) found US fashion engagement rates fell 0.30 percentage points year over year, with organic content (1.65% average) meaningfully outperforming sponsored content (1.14%) across creator tiers — a pattern that held in the UK (2.23% organic vs. 1.30% sponsored) but reversed in France, where overall engagement rose (2.84% organic vs. 1.84% sponsored) (Source:[Traackr, November 2024](https://www.traackr.com/influencer-marketing-reports/engagement-rate-benchmark-fashion-influencer-content) ). On the commerce side, TikTok Shop's Open (self-select) affiliate plan runs meaningfully different commission bands by category: beauty listings commonly run 15-30% commission versus fashion's 10-15%, against a roughly 13% average across all categories in 2026 — the clearest real, current, vertical-specific pricing differentiator found anywhere in this research, though it comes from a single practitioner source rather than an academic survey (Source:[Dashboardly, 2026](https://www.dashboardly.io/post/tiktok-shop-affiliate-commissions-2026-payouts-clawbacks-profit-math) ).


No source we could find splits creator-tier per-post dollar rate cards specifically by beauty vs. F&B vs. fashion with a verifiable methodology — circulating numbers for this varied wildly (anywhere from "$5,000-$50,000" to "$10,000-$500,000+" for the same macro tier, depending on the site) with no shared methodology behind them, so they're excluded here. Influencer Marketing Hub's own 2026 Benchmark Report (600+ respondents) gives general, non-vertical-specific cost bands instead: nano creators mostly under $500 per post, roughly 45.5% of micro-tier posts also under $500, mid-tier $2,000-$10,000, and macro dispersed above $10,000 (Source:[Influencer Marketing Hub, 2026](https://influencermarketinghub.com/influencer-marketing-benchmark-report/) ).

## Key Takeaways


-


Beauty and fashion run at nearly identical creator-marketing attention scale (687K vs. 672K average VIT), but beauty converts that attention roughly 4x more efficiently (5.9% vs. 1.4% VIT-per-reach) — the gap is about which creators get chosen, not how much gets spent.


-


US influencer marketing spend estimates range from $10.5B to $37B depending on methodology; both are real, and the disagreement is about scope, not growth direction.


-


Widely circulated stats like "34% of brands allocate 40%+ of budget to influencer marketing" (attributed to Gartner) don't trace to any real, live source — Gartner's actual 2026 survey found CMOs planning to cut influencer spend, not increase it.


-


F&B is a structurally different category, not a smaller version of beauty or fashion: it runs at about a third of their attention scale, weights brand-values alignment and disclosure compliance above follower count, and often sits inside PR strategy rather than a standalone paid-media budget line.


-


TikTok Shop affiliate commissions are a real, current, vertical-specific pricing signal: beauty runs 15-30% versus fashion's 10-15% — but verifiable creator-tier dollar rate cards by vertical don't exist in any source we could confirm.


## What should a brand actually do with these numbers?


Don't anchor a budget conversation to a market-size stat you can't trace back to its source — if a number can't be found on the cited organization's own site, treat it as unverified. Use the comparisons that do have real methodology behind them: beauty's efficiency advantage over fashion is a genuine, sourced signal that vendor fit and creator selection matter more than raw spend; F&B's PR-integrated buying pattern means influencer budget conversations there should probably start with the comms team, not just paid media; and fashion's near-beauty attention scale with a fraction of the efficiency suggests real room to close the gap through better-targeted creator selection rather than bigger budgets.
