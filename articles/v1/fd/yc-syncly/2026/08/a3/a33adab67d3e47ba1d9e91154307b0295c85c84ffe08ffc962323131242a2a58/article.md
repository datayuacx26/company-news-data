# Determ vs Awario vs Mention: Who Wins Brand Monitoring in 2026?

**TL;DR:** Determ, Awario, and Mention are the three budget-tier brand monitoring tools that keep showing up on the same shortlists — and each wins a different argument. Determ leads on traditional-media depth at a moderate price, Awario leads on affordability and a genuinely useful sales-intelligence feature, and Mention leads on raw platform count but became pricier and less independent after a 2025 acquisition by Agorapulse. None of the three actually analyzes TikTok video or audio content — the gap where consumer brands are losing the most signal in 2026.


You've got three names on your shortlist, and they all look similar on paper. Determ, Awario, and Mention all promise to track your brand across the web, all publish (mostly) transparent pricing, and all show up in the same "best brand monitoring tools" roundups.


Surface-level comparisons stop at "they all do keyword alerts." That's not enough to justify a year-long contract. What actually differs is who owns each company, what happened to Mention in the last two years, and whether any of them can see what's happening in a TikTok video rather than just its caption.


This comparison is built for lean marketing and comms teams — beauty, F&B, fashion, consumer goods — who need a real verdict, not another feature grid. We'll walk through where each tool wins, what changed at Mention that most comparisons haven't caught up to yet, and the gap all three still share.

## How They Compare Head-to-Head


Each tool was built to win a different job. Here's what each genuinely leads on — and where it falls short.


### Determ: Traditional Media and PR Depth


Determ started as an in-house tool built by Croatian communications agency iStudio/Degordian, launched publicly in 2014 as Mediatoolkit, then spun off as an independent company in 2016. It rebranded to Determ in 2023 and still operates out of Zagreb without outside investors or an acquisition on its record — a track record Awario shares, and one Mention no longer can claim.


That PR heritage shows in the product: Determ monitors over 100 million news sites plus print, TV, and radio alongside the usual social platforms (Facebook, Instagram, X, YouTube, Reddit, Bluesky, Medium, blogs, comments, forums). For a comms team tracking earned media alongside social chatter, that's a deep archive at a fraction of enterprise pricing.


**Where Determ wins:** Traditional-media reach most budget tools skip entirely. A straightforward, published pricing ladder (€99–€499/mo). Independent ownership with a decade of continuity.


**Where it struggles:** No TikTok coverage at any tier — Capterra reviewers flag this as a blind spot. Lower tiers cap history at 90 days, with occasional crashes noted too (Source:[Capterra, 2026](https://www.capterra.com/p/determ/) ).


Best for a PR or comms team that needs earned-media tracking alongside social and doesn't need TikTok. If Determ's ceiling is too low for your topic volume, see our[Determ alternatives breakdown](https://syncly.app/blog/determ-alternatives-for-10-tools-compared-on-pricing-and-video-coverage) .


### Awario: The Budget Pick With a Sales Angle


Awario is a bootstrapped sister product of Link-Assistant.Com (the company behind SEO PowerSuite), founded in 2015 by Aleh Barysevich and spun off as its own product in 2018. Like Determ, it's self-funded with no outside investors — a stability story that matters more given what happened at Mention.


Awario's pricing is the most accessible of the three: $49/mo for 30,000 mentions, scaling to $399/mo for 1 million mentions plus API access. What sets it apart isn't just price — it's Leads, a feature that surfaces purchase-intent conversations rather than just brand mentions. Awario's own case study with agency client Upstack describes the feature as generating "millions in ARR" and having "more than paid for itself" within the first month (Source:[Awario, 2026](https://awario.com/case-studies/) ).


**Where Awario wins:** The lowest entry price of the three with no compromise on core coverage breadth. Leads is a genuinely differentiated feature most competitors at this price point don't offer. Stable, independent ownership.


**Where it struggles:** No TikTok-specific or video-analysis capability surfaced anywhere in its plans, and its traditional-media coverage documentation is thinner than Determ's.


Best for a lean team that wants broad web/social coverage plus a sales-intelligence layer without enterprise pricing.


### Mention: Broadest Platform List, Now Under New Ownership


Mention lists more platforms than either competitor on paper — TikTok, Instagram, Facebook, Pinterest, and YouTube alongside X, Reddit, web, news, blogs, forums, and 75+ review sites. But the mechanism matters: Mention's own copy describes monitoring "specific keywords used on the app... as real-time as the social network itself" — caption- and hashtag-level text matching, not analysis of what's actually said or shown in a video.


The bigger story is what happened to Mention itself. Founded in Paris in 2012 by Arnaud Le Blanc, it was acquired by Mynewsdesk/NHST in 2018, then went through a French judicial bankruptcy recovery process around 2024, and was acquired out of that process by Agorapulse in March–April 2025. Reported deal values differ sharply by source — Dealroom cites €25M, while FrenchWeb's more granular reporting on the distressed-asset sale puts actual consideration closer to €4.67M, with only €61K distributed to creditors (Source:[FrenchWeb, 2025](https://www.frenchweb.fr/) ;[Dealroom, 2025](https://app.dealroom.co/) ). Mention now operates as "Mention by Agorapulse" — a product line, not an independent vendor.


That acquisition already changed the product. In July 2025, Mention discontinued its self-serve tiers (Solo $49/mo, Pro $99/mo, Pro Plus $179/mo), replacing them with a single Company Plan at $599/month, annual billing only ($7,188/year), capped at 50,000 mentions across all alerts, with historical data and API access sold separately. As of January 30, 2026, its publishing and engagement features were retired outright, requiring a separate Agorapulse subscription (Source:[Mention, 2026](https://mention.com/en/pricing/) ).


**Where Mention wins:** The widest nominal platform list, including caption-level TikTok tracking the other two don't offer, plus strong review scores (G2 4.3/5, Capterra 4.7/5). Its own customer-story page (MyWHYagency, UFC-Que Choisir, WPX Hosting) is qualitative testimonial content, though — it doesn't publish the kind of quantified outcomes Awario's Leads case study does.


**Where it struggles:** No self-serve entry point anymore — $599/mo, annual-only, is the floor, and product features are already being unbundled into a separate required subscription post-acquisition.


Best for a team that specifically needs Mention's platform breadth and is comfortable with its new pricing floor and ownership structure. If that math doesn't work, see our[Mention alternatives for video-first brands](https://syncly.app/blog/mention-alternatives) .


## Company Stability: Why Mention's Ownership Matters


**Vendor stability** : how consistently a company's ownership, leadership, and core product commitments have held over time — a signal worth weighing alongside price and features, especially for a contract you intend to renew.


Determ and Awario share something Mention no longer can: continuity, with neither ever going through a bankruptcy process or getting folded into a competitor's product line. Mention's January 2026 retirement of its publishing and engagement features — pushed into a separate required Agorapulse subscription — is a direct downstream effect of the 2025 acquisition, not an isolated decision. That doesn't make Mention a bad tool; it genuinely covers more platforms on paper than the other two. But a year-long contract is a bet on more than today's feature list, and Mention's is the only one of the three that comes with a recent ownership question mark attached.


## The Gap All Three Share: Native Video Analysis


Here's what the Determ vs Awario vs Mention debate keeps missing. All three were built around keyword and Boolean matching — a model designed for a text-first internet of tweets, articles, and forum posts. Even Mention's "TikTok coverage," the broadest of the three, is really just caption and hashtag text matching applied to a video platform.


Consumer conversations in beauty, F&B, and fashion have already moved past text. A beauty creator reviewing your product in a 45-second TikTok rarely types your brand name in the caption. A food creator recommending a snack in a Reel says it out loud. A fashion haul on YouTube Shorts shows the logo on camera without ever tagging it.


**Untagged mentions** : brand conversations in video content where your brand is spoken aloud, shown visually, or referenced contextually — but never written in a caption, tag, or hashtag. Keyword-matching platforms structurally miss them, no matter how many platforms they claim to cover.
