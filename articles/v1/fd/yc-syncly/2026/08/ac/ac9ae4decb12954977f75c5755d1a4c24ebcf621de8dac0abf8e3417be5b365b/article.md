# Khoros Alternatives for Enterprise Teams After the 2025 Shakeup

**TL;DR:** The best Khoros alternatives for enterprise teams in 2026 depend on what's actually pushing you to look — for most teams, that's Khoros's May 2025 acquisition by IgniteTech, the layoffs that followed, and an April 2026 ground-up product relaunch, not a missing feature. Sprinklr, Emplifi, Sprout Social, and Hootsuite all cover Khoros's listening-and-engagement territory without the ownership turbulence; Higher Logic Vanilla is the closest peer if your real need is Khoros's community-hosting side; and Syncly Social fits teams whose actual gap is video-content-native listening — a capability no primary Khoros source currently documents.


In May 2025, IgniteTech bought Khoros. Within three months, a Texas WARN notice confirmed 116 layoffs in its Austin office, and independent reporting describes something closer to a near-total team wipeout. A year later, Khoros relaunched its entire product line from scratch.


If your team runs on Khoros, that's not background noise — it's the thing your renewal conversation should actually be about.


Most "Khoros alternatives" content still treats this like a feature-comparison exercise: listening depth, community tools, price per seat. That's real, but it's not what's driving most searches for Khoros alternatives right now. The bigger question is whether the platform you're renewing with still has the team, the roadmap, or the institutional memory it had a year ago. A second, quieter question sits underneath it: does Khoros's listening product actually understand what's happening inside a video, or only what gets typed in a caption?


This guide profiles Khoros honestly — it's still a capable platform on its core fundamentals — then walks through why teams are evaluating alternatives, whether the video-listening gap is real, how pricing stacks up, and which of six named alternatives fits your specific situation.


## Why Are Enterprise Teams Evaluating Khoros Alternatives?


In 2026, the dominant reason is company stability, not features. Khoros's May 2025 acquisition by IgniteTech triggered layoffs affecting a large share of its workforce, and independent industry analysis describes ongoing customer concern about support, roadmap continuity, and whether the legacy product will keep being maintained.


Get the ownership chain precise, because it's easy to get wrong: Vista Equity Partners acquired Lithium Technologies (founded 2001) in May 2017 and Spredfast (founded 2008) in October 2018, then merged and renamed the combined company Khoros on March 5, 2019 (Source:[Wikipedia — Khoros, LLC, 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khoros,_LLC) ). That's the version of Khoros most buyers still have in their heads. It's out of date. **IgniteTech** , an ESW Capital company, acquired Khoros on May 27, 2025 — Vista Equity Partners is no longer the owner (Source:[PR Newswire — IgniteTech Acquires Khoros, 2025-05-27](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/ignitetech-acquires-khoros-to-transform-customer-connections-in-the-ai-answer-engine-era-302465365.html) ).


The fallout followed fast. A Texas WARN notice confirms 116 Khoros employees in the Austin office were laid off effective August 19–29, 2025 — about three months after the deal closed (Source:[Austin Post, 2025-06-21](https://austinpost.com/business/2025/06/21/austin-software-company-khoros-layoffs-after-acquisition) ). That's the official, documented number.


Separately, employee-review site RepVue and independent community-industry analyst FeverBee both describe something more sweeping: FeverBee's 2026 review states that "almost the entire Khoros team was let go within weeks," with ongoing platform work "now done by contractors with far less knowledge of community or the clients" (Source:[FeverBee — Khoros in 2026: An Honest Review, 2026](https://www.feverbee.com/khoros-in-2026-an-honest-review-and-why-id-leave/) ). Neither of these sources confirms Khoros is shutting down — there's no basis for that claim — but the gap between "116 confirmed layoffs" and "almost the entire team" is itself a sign of how much customers and reviewers are struggling to get a straight answer.


A recent G2 reviewer of Khoros Communities put it bluntly: "the new owner has completely abandoned the product roadmap, so they are not committed to solving anything for their customers" (Source:[G2 — Khoros Communities Reviews, 2026](https://www.g2.com/products/khoroscommunities/reviews) ). Khoros's response was a from-scratch relaunch — Aurora AI for community and Iris AI for social media management — in April 2026, with free migration off the legacy Khoros Communities Classic offered through the end of the year. That sounds generous until you read FeverBee's assessment that Aurora "does not have anywhere near the feature set of Classic," which turns a "free" migration into something closer to a forced downgrade for existing customers (Source:[PR Newswire — Khoros Launches Aurora AI, 2026-04-10](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/khoros-launches-aurora-ai-a-new-dawn-for-enterprise-community-302739636.html) ;[FeverBee, 2026](https://www.feverbee.com/khoros-in-2026-an-honest-review-and-why-id-leave/) ).


Two older, quieter reasons still matter, even if they're no longer the headline. Khoros's pricing is quote-only with estimates that vary wildly by source (more on that below), and its listening depth has historically depended on outside help — Khoros partnered with Talkwalker in September 2022 specifically "to deliver seamless social media management and listening," an admission that its native listening needed a specialist boost even before the ownership change (Source:[BusinessWire, 2022-09-27](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20220927005016/en/Market-leaders-Khoros-and-Talkwalker-partner-to-deliver-seamless-social-media-management-and-listening) ).


## Which Khoros Alternatives Are Best for Social Listening and Video Content Coverage?


This is the least resolvable question in this piece, and the unresolvability is itself the finding: no primary Khoros source — not its current Iris AI product page, not its April 2026 launch press release — documents a specific mechanism for analyzing video or audio content.


**Text/mention-matching listening** : a listening approach that identifies brand-relevant posts by matching keywords, hashtags, captions, or @mentions in a post's text layer — it never looks at what's actually said or shown inside a video. **Video-content-native listening** : analyzing the audio (via speech-to-text) and visuals (via computer vision or on-screen-text detection) inside a video itself, capturing mentions that are spoken or displayed on-screen but never typed anywhere.


Khoros's own Iris AI product page, fetched directly for this piece, describes "Listening & Intelligence" as monitoring "conversations at scale across billions of sources in 187 languages" across platforms including TikTok and YouTube — but nowhere on that page is there a mention of speech-to-text transcription, computer vision, or on-screen-text detection (Source:[Khoros — Iris AI, 2026](https://khoros.ai/iris/) ). The April 2026 launch press release for Iris AI describes capabilities in terms of "real-time monitoring and response" and "AI summary generation, sentiment analysis, and AI-powered classification" — language consistent with text and metadata analysis, not content-native video understanding (Source:[PR Newswire — Khoros Launches Iris AI, 2026-04-10](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/khoros-launches-iris-ai-your-messenger-across-every-brand-channel-302739658.html) ).


To be fair to Khoros: this doesn't mean it has no social listening. It has a real, if historically partnership-dependent, listening and engagement product with genuine platform breadth — the 2022 Talkwalker deal proves the company knew its native depth needed help even before the ownership change. The precise, defensible claim is narrower than "Khoros can't see video": nobody outside Khoros has confirmed it can, and the substantial team turnover since May 2025 makes it harder than usual to trust an undocumented claim either way. Treat this as an open question to ask Khoros directly, not a settled gap — the same honest-uncertainty treatment this applies to Hootsuite's own Talkwalker-powered listening, where no source confirms whether Talkwalker's video/audio features are actually exposed inside Hootsuite's product tier.


Set Khoros aside and the landscape splits cleanly. Sprout Social's own documentation confirms it "cannot search and sync sound" on TikTok — a confirmed, not unresolved, gap (Source:[Sprout Social — TikTok Social Listening](https://sproutsocial.com/insights/tiktok-social-listening/) ).


Emplifi's listening is confirmed text/mention-matching only, on every platform it covers, including TikTok. Sprinklr only just closed the gap — its May 2026 acquisition of ViralMoment adds frame-by-frame audio, visual, and on-screen-text analysis, but rollout scope and tier availability are undisclosed, and it now sits behind an enterprise-only sales process. Syncly Social, by contrast, built[video analysis](https://syncly.app/social/feature/video-analysis) natively and in-house from day one, running across TikTok, Reels, and YouTube Shorts at every tier — not as a partnership, an acquisition, or an undocumented marketing claim.

## Khoros Isn't Broken. Its Ownership Story Is the Reason to Look Elsewhere.


Khoros's Communities product still scores well with reviewers on discussion depth, moderation, and enterprise admin tooling — this isn't a case for writing off the platform on capability alone. What's changed is everything around it: a new owner, a gutted team by most independent accounts, and a product line rebuilt from scratch in the space of a year.


That reframes the real evaluation question. It's not "does Khoros still do what it used to do" — it's "will the team and roadmap behind it still be there in eighteen months." Pair that with an honestly unresolved video-listening question, and most enterprise teams have two separate, valid reasons to shop, not one.
