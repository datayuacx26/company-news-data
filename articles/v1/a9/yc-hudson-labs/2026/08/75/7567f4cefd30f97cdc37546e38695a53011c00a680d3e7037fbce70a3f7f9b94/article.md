## Summary and Conclusions


- **Broadcom is entering Q3 FY2026 with record momentum, driven by explosive AI semiconductor demand.** The company has guided to $29.4B in revenue (+84% YoY), with AI semiconductor revenue expected to exceed $16B (over +200% YoY).
- **Margins are under pressure from mix shift but remain robust.** Gross margin is guided down to ~74% (from 77% in Q2), but operating and EBITDA margins are expected to hold at 67% and 68%, respectively, due to strong operating leverage.
- **AI bookings and backlog are at unprecedented levels.** Q2 saw $30B in AI bookings, and management now has visibility into 2028, with expectations for AI semiconductor revenue to exceed $100B in 2027.
- **Non-AI semiconductor and infrastructure software segments are recovering/growing.** Non-AI semi revenue is forecast to grow +12% YoY in Q3, while infrastructure software is expected to accelerate to +31% YoY, driven by VMware Cloud Foundation adoption.
- **Key risks and questions:** Sustainability of AI demand, margin trajectory as system/rack sales scale, customer concentration, and ability to secure incremental supply if demand further accelerates.

## Year-on-Year Comparison and Setup


- **Q3 FY2026 faces a much easier comparable than prior years.** Last year’s Q3 revenue was $15,952M (+22% YoY at the time), while this quarter is guided to $29,400M (+84% YoY). The acceleration is driven almost entirely by AI semiconductor demand.
- **AI revenue is expected to more than triple YoY in Q3.** Q3 FY2025 AI semi revenue was $5.2B; Q3 FY2026 is guided to $16B+.
- **Gross margin is expected to compress YoY.** Q3 FY2025 gross margin was 78.4%; Q3 FY2026 is guided to ~74%, reflecting higher AI semi mix.
- **Operating leverage remains strong.** Despite gross margin compression, operating and EBITDA margins are expected to remain stable due to scale and cost discipline.


---


## Management Commentary and Intra-Quarter Color


- **AI Demand and Backlog:** Management repeatedly emphasized “insatiable” demand for AI compute, with Q2 AI bookings over $30B and backlog visibility now extending into 2028. “We expect AI semiconductor revenue to double from the first half we shipped this year.”
- **Customer Ramps:** Google, Anthropic, OpenAI, Meta, and two additional customers are all ramping multi-gigawatt deployments. New long-term agreements (e.g., Google, Anthropic) provide multi-year revenue visibility.
- **AI Networking:** Networking is nearly 40% of AI revenue in Q2, but management expects this to normalize toward 30% as custom XPUs ramp.
- **Supply Chain:** Management claims to have secured wafer, HBM, substrate, and packaging supply through 2028, supporting continued growth.
- **Non-AI Semi Recovery:** Bookings in non-AI semi exceeded $6B in Q2, with management indicating a “full cyclical recovery” is underway.
- **Software/VMware:** VMware Cloud Foundation 9.1 is driving strong on-prem cloud demand; ARR growth remains robust at +17% YoY.


---


## What to Watch in the Print


- **Sustainability of AI Revenue Acceleration:** Is the >200% YoY AI semi growth in Q3 sustainable into Q4 and FY2027? Any signs of order pushouts or cancellations?
- **Margin Trajectory:** Will gross margin stabilize at ~74% or continue to compress as AI/system sales scale? Can operating leverage offset mix headwinds?
- **Backlog Conversion and Bookings:** Does the $30B+ in quarterly AI bookings convert to revenue as expected? Any changes in backlog visibility or customer commitments?
- **Customer Concentration and Ramp Timing:** Are all six core customers ramping as planned? Any delays or changes in gigawatt deployment schedules?
- **Non-AI Semi and Software Trends:** Is the cyclical recovery in non-AI semi broadening? Does infrastructure software sustain double-digit growth as guided?
- **Capital Returns:** Any changes to buyback pace or dividend policy given cash flow strength?


---


## Conclusion


Broadcom enters Q3 FY2026 with historic momentum, driven by AI semiconductor demand and robust execution across its portfolio. The company faces an easy YoY comparable, but the market will be focused on the durability of AI growth, margin trajectory, backlog conversion, and the breadth of customer ramps. Management’s ability to deliver on these fronts will be critical for sustaining the current valuation and investor confidence.
