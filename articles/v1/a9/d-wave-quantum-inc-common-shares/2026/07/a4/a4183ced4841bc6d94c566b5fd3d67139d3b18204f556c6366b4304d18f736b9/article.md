# Quantum Computing Passes the Inflection Point: Reflections on Qubits26

## Article Highlights


As we take a close look at the breadth of conversations from Qubits26, we see a convergence of signals pointing to rapid acceleration in quantum computing. As the community of users and technologists continues to grow, we will see software acceleration spread the impact of existing quantum computing success stories across industries. The organizations that engage now will be able to help shape how that impact scales.


---


Quantum computing has passed an important inflection point: a shift from hardware-constrained pacing to software-accelerated progress.


In the past, the pace of quantum computing progress was set by hardware development timescales, gradually catching up to 70 years of advances in classical computing. Today, the most mature quantum computers have demonstrated capabilities far[beyond classical](https://www.dwavequantum.com/beyond-classical/) computing technology.


For example, a[study](https://www.science.org/stoken/author-tokens/ST-2490/full) published in *Science* last year presented a magnetic material simulation that would have required *the world’s entire annual electricity consumption* to compute on one of the world’s largest classical supercomputers. Completing the calculations on a D-Wave™ annealing quantum computer used only $1 worth of electricity.


Now consider that businesses are already using this technology in production applications, and today’s quantum computers are easily programmed with the ubiquitous and familiar Python programming language. This combination of accessible programming with clear commercial application success stories is poised to swell the developer community and lead to faster algorithm discovery.


This is why I believe we have already begun an era that will be characterized by fast iteration cycles, broad experimentation, and early competitive differentiation.


The evidence of this new era was on full display at D-Wave's annual user conference, Qubits26, in Boca Raton, Florida. The event was marked by multiple market trends converging to signal a rapidly maturing technology category. These trends indicate that measurable value is being delivered today, and further capabilities are growing rapidly.


Let’s take a closer look at these trends.


## **1. Quantum Optimization Use Cases Delivering Real Value Are Easy To Find**


During Qubits26, diverse use cases were presented from across industries, helping to highlight some of the best-fit applications for quantum computing.


When it came to optimizing its dispatch network, AT&T talked about reducing planning time from[one hour to 15 seconds](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=504OpgO5DWQ&list=PLPvKnT7dgEssLmMY3HVaVzqMgALYXF55L&index=5) . In a production scheduling proof of concept, BASF, one of the world’s leading chemical companies, reduced lateness by 14%, setup times by 9%, and tank loading durations by 18%—[computing in 5 seconds what used to take 10 hours](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=imIqOxjunlA&list=PLPvKnT7dgEssLmMY3HVaVzqMgALYXF55L&index=6) .


As Dr. Shahar Keinan, CEO and co-founder of PolarisQB, pointed out in her[presentation on quantum-assisted drug discovery](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GS-3uH_TbQo&list=PLPvKnT7dgEssLmMY3HVaVzqMgALYXF55L&index=12) , “The promise of quantum is happening today on quantum annealing.”


There are now an increasing breadth and depth of quantum optimization use cases that are demonstrating real value across industries *.* It’s why a Fortune 100 company recently signed a[$10 million QCaaS agreement](https://www.dwavequantum.com/company/newsroom/press-release/d-wave-announces-10-million-two-year-enterprise-qcaas-agreement-with-fortune-100-company/) to develop and deploy production quantum applications running on D-Wave's annealing quantum computers.


## **2. Value Is Achieved Quickly With Quantum Computing**


One moment that stuck with me was an interaction I had with an attendee who wanted to learn how to program quantum computing systems. Within a day of our first meeting, he had started programming our systems online using one of our open-source examples, queried our quantum platform, and got a solution back in seconds for a nurse workforce scheduling application that he was exploring.


The speed at which developers can begin to create software on these systems is a dramatic catalyst for unlocking value. And I expect this speed to value to accelerate further as open-source developer tools, like D-Wave's[Ocean™ library](https://www.dwavequantum.com/solutions-and-products/ocean/) , and learning resources continue to develop.


Multiple presenters promoted initiatives to develop the quantum workforce. In a[panel discussion](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZPBydvAOeuM&list=PLPvKnT7dgEssLmMY3HVaVzqMgALYXF55L&index=23) , Dr. Stella Batalama, dean of the College of Engineering & Computer Science at Florida Atlantic University, talked about standing up a D-Wave Application Academy to train students on the D-Wave Advantage2™ system that the University[recently agreed to purchase](https://www.dwavequantum.com/company/newsroom/press-release/florida-atlantic-university-signs-20m-agreement-to-purchase-advantage2-quantum-computer/) .


This early exposure to quantum computing at the university level will have compounding effects for the wider economy. “Students will go into industry and carry that knowledge to improve the efficiency of industry overall,” noted Tom Darden, executive director of[Q-Alliance.](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20251014093445/en/The-Birth-of-the-Worlds-Most-Powerful-Quantum-Hub)


The interest in the training exceeded even our expectations. At Qubits26, we offered a half-day training program for software developers to learn how to build applications—it was oversubscribed by 75%. More of these resources can be found at[D-Wave Learn.](https://training.dwavequantum.com/)


## **3. Quantum Technology Is Advancing in Multiple Dimensions**


Modern quantum computing platforms form a technology stack that provides value to different users at different levels.


At the top of this stack are business users and developers who want to realize value as quickly as possible. To make that easier, D-Wave has introduced new capabilities to the[Stride™ hybrid solver](https://www.dwavequantum.com/solutions-and-products/cloud-platform/hybrid-solver/) that enable customers to incorporate machine learning models directly within quantum-hybrid optimization pipelines. This can support a wider range of use cases, including predictive maintenance, surge pricing, and advertising campaign optimization.


At the heart of the technology stack are the quantum processors themselves: the engines that turn quantum mechanics into useful work. Different models of quantum computers are better suited to accelerate different sets of quantum applications. For instance, annealing quantum computers are the choice for optimization problems, while gate-model systems are chosen for quantum chemistry and 3D fluid flow applications.


Customers need the right quantum tool for the right problem—and D-Wave is now the only company building both annealing and gate-model systems with its dual-platform approach.


With the recent acquisition of Quantum Circuits, Inc., the ecosystem of deep technology users will benefit from gate-model dual-rail qubit technology, which combines the fidelity of neutral-atom or trapped-ion qubits with the operating speed of superconducting platforms. Meanwhile, the latest annealing controls give quantum scientists deeper visibility into quantum dynamics.


This combination of annealing and gate-model development, strengthened by hybrid software innovation, uniquely positions D-Wave to lead the software-accelerated era now underway. By advancing multiple quantum modalities while expanding access to tools and commercial applications, we are building not just more powerful systems, but a broader platform for rapid experimentation and deployment.


## **4. Quantum Provides Strategic Infrastructure for Government and Defense Applications**


Some of the biggest economic wins from technology—the internet and GPS for example—had their origins in a public sector project aimed at solving an important problem. This is particularly true when the core of the problem—for example, resource allocation—is broadly transferrable. In these situations, the private sector response is to multiply these gains across industries, thereby creating a compounding economic benefit.


This reciprocal benefit between government missions and private businesses is acting to accelerate quantum computing adoption today. As Tom Darden put it, “there's so many government-oriented applications for quantum optimization: transportation system design, energy grid management, improving confidence in the decision-making processes of government ...” the list goes on.


At Qubits26,[Anduril Industries, Inc. and Davidson Technologies, Inc. presented](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ac5u3bVFMac&list=PLPvKnT7dgEssLmMY3HVaVzqMgALYXF55L&index=4) a timely missile defense resource allocation application developed with D-Wave. In a simulated scenario, the solution developed on D-Wave's Stride™ hybrid solver stopped about 9% to 12% more missiles than a classical-only solution—computing in 25.6 minutes what took their classical solver 176 hours.


To put that in perspective, “these threats are targeting assets like hospitals, military installations, water treatment plants, power plants,” said Dr. Patrick Gemperline, Ph.D. , quantum computing engineer at Davidson Technologies. “Every single additional missile we can negate is another piece of critical infrastructure we're protecting. And that's not even going to get into the human element of things and the amount of lives we might save.”
