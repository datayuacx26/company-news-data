## Understanding Cross-border Payments in Africa


Cross-border payments are financial transactions where the payer and recipient are located in different countries. Unlike domestic payments that process through single-country infrastructure, international payments must navigate multiple banking systems, currencies, regulatory frameworks, and intermediaries.


### The Scale of Africa’s Cross-border Payment Market


Africa’s cross-border payments market represents a massive economic opportunity. As of 2025, the market is valued at approximately $329 billion and projected to triple to $1 trillion by 2035, representing a compound annual growth rate of about 12%.


This growth is driven by several converging factors:


**Intra-African Trade Expansion:** The African Continental Free Trade Area (AfCFTA), launched in 2021, creates a single market of 1.3 billion people with a combined GDP of $3.4 trillion. This integration is accelerating intra-African trade and creating demand for efficient cross-border payments.


**Remittance Flows:** Africa receives over $96 billion in annual remittances, making it one of the world’s largest inbound remittance regions. These international payments support millions of families and represent a critical component of many African economies.


**Digital Commerce Growth:** African businesses increasingly serve international customers and source from global suppliers, requiring reliable cross-border payment infrastructure.


**SME Expansion:** Small and medium enterprises represent 90% of African businesses. As these companies expand across borders, demand for accessible international payment solutions grows exponentially.


### The Current State: High Costs, Slow Speeds


Despite this massive market opportunity, Africa’s cross-border payments ecosystem faces significant challenges that create a real economic burden:


**Transaction Costs:** Cross-border payments in Africa average 7.4% to 8.3% in fees, far exceeding the G20 Roadmap target of 3% by 2027. Some corridors, like South Africa to Zimbabwe, see costs as high as 12.7%.


**Settlement Delays:** Traditional international payments can take 3-7 business days to settle, tying up working capital that businesses need for operations.


**Limited Transparency:** Businesses often lack visibility into payment status, which creates uncertainty and complicates cash flow management.


**Regulatory Fragmentation:** Africa’s 54 countries operate largely independent payment systems with limited interoperability, requiring businesses to navigate multiple regulatory frameworks.


**Foreign Exchange Challenges:** Currency liquidity issues result in an estimated $5 billion in annual losses, while reliance on offshore USD/EUR clearance increases transaction costs.

## The Critical Challenges of Cross-border Payments in Africa


Understanding the specific obstacles facing African cross-border payments helps businesses make informed decisions about solutions:


### 1. Infrastructure Fragmentation


Unlike Europe’s SEPA system or North America’s ACH network, Africa lacks unified payment rails enabling seamless cross-border payments at reasonable costs.


**Multiple Integrations Required:** A business serving customers in Nigeria, Kenya, Ghana, and South Africa may need separate integrations with 4-10 different payment providers, each with different APIs, documentation, and requirements.


**Currency Management Complexity:** Managing 42+ currencies across the continent, each with distinct exchange rates, volatility profiles, and conversion costs, adds a a significant operational burden.


**Limited Cross-border Rails:** Most African countries require international payments to be routed through offshore intermediaries, adding costs and delays.


### 2. Regulatory Complexity


Each African country maintains unique central bank regulations, licensing requirements, KYC (Know Your Customer) standards, and compliance frameworks.


**KYC Fragmentation:** Differences in KYC regulations, some lax, some stringent, create additional hurdles for cross-border payments. There’s no minimum standard for data protection and privacy across African countries.


**AML/CFT Requirements:** Stringent Anti-Money Laundering and Combating the Financing of Terrorism requirements, while necessary for security, add complexity and cost to international payments.


**Reporting Obligations:** Varying reporting requirements across countries create administrative burdens for businesses managing cross-border payments in multiple markets.


### 3. Foreign Exchange Volatility and Liquidity


Currency challenges significantly impact cross-border payments in Africa:


**Exchange Rate Fluctuations:** Some African currencies experience 5-20% annual volatility against major currencies. Extended payment terms expose businesses to significant exchange rate risk.


**Currency Pair Liquidity:** Many African currency pairs have limited liquidity. For example, direct exchange between Ugandan shillings and Rwandan francs is constrained by limited demand for each currency in the other market.


**Dependence on Reserve Currencies:** Most African cross-border payments require conversion through USD or EUR intermediaries, which adds costs and exposes them to multiple exchange rate spreads.


### 4. Last-Mile Access Challenges


Even when cross-border payments reach destination countries, last-mile distribution presents obstacles:


**Rural Access Limitations:** Mobile money agents and cash-out points are scarce in rural areas, limiting beneficiaries’ ability to access funds from international payments.


**Cash Dominance:** Lack of confidence in local currencies and limited financial access drive reliance on cash, increasing costs and limiting digital adoption for cross-border payments.


**Digital Literacy Gaps:** Many users lack awareness of new cross-border payment solutions or the digital skills to use them effectively.


### 5. Slow Settlement and Limited Transparency


Traditional international payment processes lack the speed and visibility modern businesses require:


**Multi-Day Processing:** Settlement times of 3-7 days create cash flow gaps and working capital challenges for businesses dependent on timely cross-border payments.


**Opaque Status Tracking:** Limited visibility into payment status leaves businesses unsure whether international payments are being processed, delayed, or failed.


**Reconciliation Complexity:** Matching cross-border payments to invoices across multiple currencies and payment systems consumes significant time for the finance team.

## Emerging Solutions Transforming Cross-border Payments


Despite these challenges, 2025 has brought significant progress in African cross-border payments through technological innovation and institutional initiatives:


### 1. Pan-African Payment and Settlement System (PAPSS)


Launched in 2022 by the African Export-Import Bank and African Union,[PAPSS](https://papss.com/) provides breakthrough infrastructure for cross-border payments in local African currencies.


**Key Features:**


- Enables instant, secure transactions in local African currencies
- Eliminates the need for intermediary foreign currencies (USD/EUR)
- Uses distributed ledger technology for real-time tracking
- Reduces transaction costs by up to 50%
- Shortens settlement times from days to seconds


**Current Coverage:** By early 2025, PAPSS enabled real-time cross-border payments across 17 countries, connecting 14 national switches and over 150 commercial banks.


**Impact:** PAPSS directly addresses the core challenges of African international payments by bypassing traditional correspondent banking and enabling direct currency connections.


### 2. Regional Payment Integration Initiatives


Beyond PAPSS, regional initiatives are improving cross-border payment efficiency:


**East African Payment System (EAPS):** The East African Community validated the Draft EAC Cross-Border Payment System Masterplan in March 2025, outlining 20 targeted initiatives to enhance the speed, security, and affordability of regional cross-border payments.


**CIPS Integration:** In November 2025, Standard Bank became the first African bank to directly participate in China’s Cross-border Interbank Payment System, enabling faster, cheaper RMB transactions for Africa-China international payments.


**BRICS Pay Network:** Emerging payment networks aim to settle cross-border payments directly in local currencies, avoiding USD/EUR routing.


### 3. Mobile Money Expansion


Mobile money platforms central to Africa’s payment transformation are extending into cross-border payments:


**Transaction Growth:** Mobile money transaction values in Africa reached $1.4 trillion in 2023, up 39% from $495 billion in 2020.


**Cross-border Capabilities:** Major mobile money platforms are building international payment features, enabling person-to-person and business remittances across borders.


**Last-Mile Reach:** Mobile money’s extensive agent networks provide crucial last-mile access for cross-border payment recipients, particularly in areas with limited banking infrastructure.


### 4. Fintech Payment Platforms


Modern fintech platforms are leveraging technology to address traditional cross-border payment challenges:


**Unified API Access:** Platforms provide a single API integration that connects multiple payment methods across various countries, dramatically reducing technical complexity.


**Transparent Pricing:** Fintech solutions often offer clearer pricing with fewer hidden fees compared to traditional banking channels.


**Real-time Tracking:** Modern platforms provide visibility into payment status, eliminating the “black box” of traditional international payments.


**Faster Settlement:** Some providers offer same-day or instant settlement for cross-border payments, dramatically improving cash flow compared to traditional T+3 to T+7 settlement.


### 5. Emerging Technologies


Several technological innovations are reshaping African cross-border payments:


**ISO 20022 Migration:** The migration to richer, more structured payment data enhances efficiency and interoperability of cross-border payment systems, enabling better tracking and reducing errors.


**Central Bank Digital Currencies (CBDCs):** Nigeria’s eNaira pioneered African CBDCs, while Ghana, South Africa, and Kenya have active projects. CBDCs could dramatically simplify international payment settlements.


**Stablecoin Infrastructure:** Companies are building networks that leverage stablecoins to bypass conventional banking channels, providing faster, more cost-effective cross-border payment solutions.


**Blockchain and DLT:** Distributed ledger technology enhances transparency through real-time tracking and anti-fraud protocols for international payments.


## Practical Solutions for Businesses


Understanding challenges and solutions is important, but businesses need actionable strategies for improving their cross-border payments:


### Selecting the Right Payment Provider


When evaluating cross-border payment solutions, businesses should assess:


**Geographic Coverage:** Does the provider operate in your key markets? Look for platforms with a strong presence in your specific trade corridors.


**Currency Support:** Verify support for all currencies you need, including major currencies and regional African currencies.


**Fee Structure:** Compare total costs including transaction fees, FX spreads, receiving fees, and any hidden charges. Calculate costs for your specific payment volumes and corridors.


**Settlement Speed:** Determine how quickly funds reach recipients. Same-day or T+1 settlement significantly improves cash flow compared to traditional T+3 to T+7.


**Integration Capabilities:** Assess how easily the solution integrates with your existing systems, including accounting software, ERP platforms, and business workflows.


**Transparency and Tracking:** Ensure real-time visibility into payment status and comprehensive reporting for all cross-border payments.


**Regulatory Compliance:** Verify the provider holds necessary licenses and maintains compliance in your operating jurisdictions.


**Customer Support:** Evaluate support availability, response times, and whether local support teams understand African market dynamics.

### Optimizing Cross-border Payment Operations


Beyond selecting providers, businesses can optimize their international payment operations:


**Multi-Currency Wallets:** Hold balances in multiple currencies to make strategic decisions about when to convert based on exchange rates, reducing FX costs.

**Hedging Strategies:** For businesses with significant currency exposure, explore FX hedging options to mitigate exchange rate volatility risks.

## The Role of Regulatory Frameworks


Successful cross-border payment transformation requires supportive regulatory frameworks:


### Current Regulatory Initiatives


**G20 Roadmap for Enhancing Cross-border Payments:** The G20’s multi-year program aims to address high costs, slow speeds, limited access, and insufficient transparency through 19 building blocks. The 2025 target is to reduce remittance costs to 3%.


**AfCFTA Payment Integration:** The African Continental Free Trade Area is driving initiatives to harmonize payment regulations and build cross-border payment infrastructure to support intra-African trade.


**National Fast Payment Systems:** Individual countries are implementing domestic real-time payment systems that can serve as foundations for improved international payments.


### Regulatory Challenges Requiring Attention


**Interoperability Standards:** Need for harmonized technical standards enabling different national payment systems to connect seamlessly for cross-border payments.


**KYC Harmonization:** Establishing minimum data protection and privacy standards across African countries would reduce friction in international payments.


**Licensing Reciprocity:** Enabling payment providers licensed in one African country to operate more easily in others would enhance competition and reduce costs.


**Consumer Protection:** Strengthening regulations protecting businesses and individuals in cross-border payments, including clear dispute resolution and liability frameworks.

## Future Trends in African Cross-border Payments


Looking ahead, several trends will shape the evolution of African international payments:


### Increased Digitalization


The shift from cash to digital cross-border payments will accelerate, driven by improved infrastructure, smartphone penetration, and digital literacy.


### AI and Machine Learning Integration


Artificial intelligence will enhance cross-border payment operations by improving fraud detection, automating compliance checks, enabling predictive analytics for cash flow management, and optimizing payment routing across multiple rails.


### Greater Regional Integration


Regional payment systems will become more interconnected, enabling easier cross-border payments within African regions before eventually connecting continents.


### Embedded Finance Expansion


Cross-border payment capabilities will increasingly be embedded into business platforms like e-commerce marketplaces, supply chain management systems, and accounting software. Making international payments seamless parts of business workflows rather than separate processes.


### Central Bank Digital Currency Adoption


As CBDCs mature, they could provide efficient infrastructure for cross-border payments, particularly for government payments, large B2B transactions, and formal remittances.


### Climate-Conscious Payments


Growing focus on environmental impact may drive adoption of more energy-efficient cross-border payment technologies and routes.
