# Turn Your API Into an Agent-Ready Revenue Stream

### With x402 and Circle Agent Stack, API providers can accept USDC payments from autonomous agents, unlock pay-per-use pricing, and reach a new market of machine customers.


A new market is forming, but the buyers are not people. They are agents.


Agents can pay per API call to search filings, scrape a page, enrich a lead, generate an image, or access specialized intelligence. They do not need a checkout page, sales conversation, subscription plan, or user account. They need an endpoint, a price, and a way to pay.


This is the shift toward a headless, agent-native internet. Services are no longer discovered and purchased only through websites or apps. They are called directly by agents at the moment a task requires them.


For founders and developers, that creates a new customer: autonomous software that can discover your service, call your API, and pay in USDC. With[x402](https://www.circle.com/blog/autonomous-payments-using-circle-wallets-usdc-and-x402) and[Circle Agent Stack](https://www.circle.com/agent-stack) , payment becomes part of the request, turning existing endpoints into agent-ready services and unlocking pay-per-use revenue that was previously too small to monetize.


## Services we want to see more of in Circle’s Agent Marketplace


The Circle Agent Marketplace already includes services that agents can discover, call, and pay for at the moment. We want to expand the range and depth of those offerings across three core categories, creating more opportunities for API providers to package existing capabilities as agent-ready services and unlock new use cases.


### Data and intelligence


Agents need trusted information like market data, financial analysis, company research, crypto intelligence, RWA data, risk signals, and specialized knowledge sources to make decisions. Concrete examples of the kinds of trusted information agents might need include stock and crypto market data APIs, token analytics, RWA pricing feeds, filings search, lead enrichment, compliance checks, and domain-specific expert models.


### Digital infrastructure


Agents need tools that help them accomplish tasks. This includes compute, storage, browser automation, web deployment, email, SMS, identity, and other infrastructure services they can use on demand.


Examples include browser automation endpoints, hosted compute, data storage, website deployment, email and SMS APIs, scraping services, authentication tools, and workflow infrastructure.


### Real-world execution


Agents need ways to act beyond the browser. This includes delivery, field services, verification, logistics, and other actions that require a person, device, or physical-world process.


Examples include local delivery, document pickup, property or vehicle inspection, storefront photography, in-person verification, equipment installation, and other task-based services.


Across each category, the opportunity is the same: turn your existing service into an agent-ready endpoint. With x402 and Circle Agent Stack, agents can find your API,[pay per call in USDC](https://www.circle.com/blog/circle-nanopayments-launches-on-testnet-as-the-core-primitive-for-agentic-economic-activity) , and use your service without a checkout page, account setup, or subscription.


## Why pay-per-use APIs matter for the agent economy


This is an early market, and the use cases are still being defined. That is the opportunity. The shape of agent-native services, their pricing, and their discovery are all being decided right now, and the decisions made in the next year will set how money moves when agents transact on behalf of people. Ultimately, an agent that can transact does more on your behalf than one that cannot.


Circle is helping to bootstrap this market through Circle Agent Stack. Builders already have the core infrastructure they need: instant USDC payments, agent-ready wallets and command-line tools, and a single integration that enables services to accept payments from any agent. As Agent Stack evolves, we will continue reducing friction for both buyers and sellers so more services can become discoverable, payable, and usable by agents.
