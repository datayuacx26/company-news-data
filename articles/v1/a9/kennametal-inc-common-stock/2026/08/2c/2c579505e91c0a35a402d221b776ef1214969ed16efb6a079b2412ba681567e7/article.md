# The Basics of Surface Finish

## Surface Roughness Units of Measurement & Parameters


### Units of Measurement


In the United States, surface roughness is measured by micro inches, or one millionth of an inch, and displays as **µ in** . Globally surface roughness is measured by micrometers, or one millionth of a meter, and displays as **µm or um** .


### Ra - Arithmetic Average Roughness


Ra is the most common parameter used for measuring surface roughness. It calculates the average of the absolute values of the profile heights over a length. So, if you drew a line down the middle of your surface profile, Ra is the average distance between that line and all the texture of the surface.


However, because Ra averages everything, it doesn't distinguish critical considerations like the height of peaks or valleys, making it more ideal for 2D measuring needs.


### Rz - Average Maximum Height


Rz considers the extremes of surface roughness, measuring the averages of the five highest peaks or Rp (maximum profile peak height) and five deepest valleys or Rv (maximum profile valley depth), so it can catch the irregularities that Ra would average out.


Rz is suited for sealing applications because it accounts for deviations that can impact how seals or sliding parts function, like leaks resulting from deep valleys.


### Rq - Root Mean Square Roughness


Also referred to as RMS, Rq differs from Ra due to the formula it uses for measuring peaks and valleys. Rq squares each deviation measured, then averages them and finally takes the square root. It’s more sensitive to surface outliers than Ra, so Rq measurements tend to show a higher number.


Similar to Rz, Rq is ideal for sealing applications, as well as optical surfaces and anywhere that extreme peaks or valleys may ultimately compromise function.


### Rmax - Maximum Roughness Depth


Rmax is straightforward: it identifies the single largest peak-to-valley distance within the entire sample. It prepares you for the worst-case scenario, and that’s critical for avoiding catastrophic failure in components that are highly strained, like turbine blades or medical implants.


With Rmax, it’s important to remember that because it only considers one extreme, it’s less statistically stable than Ra or Rz. Something as simple as dust or a quick tool vibration can impact Rmax, even when working with ideal surface quality, so it’s often used in tandem with Ra or Rz.
