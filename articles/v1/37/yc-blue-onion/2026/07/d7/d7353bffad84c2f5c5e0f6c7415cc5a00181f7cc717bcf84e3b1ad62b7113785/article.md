# Frequently Asked Questions About Ecommerce Audit Readiness

## **What makes ecommerce audits more complex than traditional retail audits?**


Ecommerce audits are more complex because financial data is spread across multiple systems, including ecommerce platforms, payment processors, and bank accounts. A single transaction may involve an order platform like Shopify, a payment processor such as Stripe or PayPal, and a delayed bank settlement.


Auditors must verify that every sale can be traced from the original order through payment processing and ultimately to a bank deposit. This requires transaction-level reconciliation across multiple systems, which many ecommerce finance teams still perform manually.


## **How do ecommerce companies reconcile payments with bank deposits?**


Ecommerce companies reconcile payments with bank deposits by matching orders, payment processor records, and bank settlements.


This process typically involves:


- Matching orders to payment transactions
- Accounting for processor fees and adjustments
- Reconciling refunds and chargebacks
- Confirming the final payout deposited into the bank


Without automated reconciliation, finance teams often rely on spreadsheets to manually match these transactions across systems.


## **What is an order-to-cash audit trail in ecommerce accounting?**


An **order-to-cash audit trail** is the documented financial path showing how an ecommerce order becomes revenue and ultimately a bank deposit.


A complete audit trail typically includes:


- The original order transaction
- The payment processor record
- Any fees deducted by the processor
- Refunds or chargebacks
- The final payout deposited into the company’s bank account


Auditors rely on this documentation to verify that recorded revenue and cash balances are accurate.


## **Why do ecommerce finance teams struggle with reconciliation?**


Many ecommerce finance teams struggle with reconciliation because transaction data is distributed across multiple platforms that were not designed to work together for accounting purposes.


Common challenges include:


- High transaction volumes
- Multiple payment processors
- Delayed settlement timing
- Refunds, exchanges, and chargebacks
- Processor fees and adjustments


When reconciliation is performed manually using exports and spreadsheets, these complexities make it difficult to maintain consistent and accurate financial records.


## **What is an ecommerce financial subledger?**


An **ecommerce financial subledger** is a system that reconciles transaction-level data from ecommerce platforms, payment processors, and banks before it is recorded in the general ledger or ERP.


It provides:


- Automated transaction matching
- Order-to-cash audit trails
- Continuous reconciliation
- Exception detection
- Clean journal entries sent to the ERP


By reconciling financial data before it reaches the general ledger, a subledger helps finance teams maintain accurate and audit-ready records.


## **How can ecommerce companies prepare for audit season?**


Ecommerce companies can prepare for audit season by ensuring their financial data is consistently reconciled and fully traceable throughout the year.


Best practices include:


- Automating transaction-level reconciliation across systems
- Maintaining a clear order-to-cash audit trail
- Identifying and resolving discrepancies in real time
- Reducing reliance on manual spreadsheets


Companies that automate these processes significantly reduce the time and effort required during audits.
