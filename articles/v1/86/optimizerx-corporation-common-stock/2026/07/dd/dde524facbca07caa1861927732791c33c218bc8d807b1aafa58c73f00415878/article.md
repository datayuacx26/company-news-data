# Effective Point of Care Media Strategies: How to Reach the Prescribing Moment

Point of care media has become an essential part of healthcare marketing strategy. For life sciences brands, it offers something few other channels can: the ability to reach healthcare providers when they are actively engaged in patient care.


But ooo often, point of care media is defined by where a message appears: inside a clinical environment, within an EHR, near the exam room, or alongside healthcare content. Those placements can be valuable, but location alone does not determine impact.


The most effective point of care media strategy is not simply about reaching healthcare providers during patient care. It is about reaching them during the prescribing moment, when treatment decisions are being made and brand information can directly support action.


In other words: point of care is not just a place. It is a decision.


## **Why Timing Matters in Point of Care Media**


Healthcare providers move through many moments during a patient encounter. They review medical history, assess symptoms, evaluate lab results, consider treatment options, discuss affordability, and ultimately decide whether to prescribe a therapy.


Each moment has value. But they do not carry the same level of influence.


A message delivered in the waiting room may help build awareness. A message delivered alongside clinical content may help reinforce education. But a message delivered when an HCP is actively selecting a therapy or preparing to send a prescription does something more powerful: it removes friction at the exact point where intent becomes action. That distinction matters.


For pharmaceutical marketers, the prescribing moment is where clinical relevance, patient need, access considerations, and brand choice converge. If a provider is considering which therapy to prescribe, this is the moment when the right information can make the difference between your brand being selected—or not.


An effective point of care media strategy should be built around that reality.


## **The Difference Between Reaching Care and Reaching Prescribing Intent**


Many point of care campaigns are designed to reach providers somewhere within the care journey. That may include digital screens, patient education materials, clinical workflow messages, or EHR placements.


These strategies can help brands stay visible. But visibility is only part of the equation. To drive measurable impact, marketers need to ask a more specific question: are we reaching the provider when they are most likely to act?


Prescribing intent is different from general clinical context. It reflects a moment when an HCP is not just thinking about a condition, but actively evaluating treatment for a specific patient. That is when information about therapy options, affordability, access, patient support, or brand relevance becomes most useful.


The closer a campaign gets to this moment, the more actionable the engagement becomes.


That is why the most effective point of care strategies prioritize timing, not just placement.


## **What Makes a Point of Care Media Strategy Effective?**


An effective point of care media strategy should do more than deliver impressions inside clinical settings. It should help brands reach the right HCP, with the right message, at the right moment in the treatment decision process.


The best strategies typically include five core principles.


***1. Prioritize prescribing moments over passive exposure***


Point of care campaigns should be designed around moments of clinical action, not just moments of attention.


A provider seeing a brand message in a healthcare environment may become more aware of a therapy. But a provider seeing relevant brand information while actively prescribing is in a different decision state. They are already evaluating treatment, considering patient needs, and determining next steps.


That is where point of care media has the greatest potential to influence outcomes.


Brands should evaluate point of care partners based on whether they can reach HCPs near or within the prescribing workflow, not simply whether they offer clinical adjacency.


***2. Use data to identify the right HCPs and patient contexts***


Effective point of care media starts before the message is delivered. It begins with understanding which providers are most relevant to the brand’s strategy.


Traditional targeting may rely on specialty, prescribing history, or broad audience segments. Those signals are useful, but they are not always enough to identify the most valuable opportunities.


More advanced strategies use clinical and behavioral signals to understand where patient need, provider activity, and brand opportunity overlap. This allows marketers to prioritize HCPs who are more likely to be treating relevant patients and more likely to encounter situations where the brand message can be useful.


The goal is not simply to reach more providers. It is to reach the providers most likely to have meaningful prescribing opportunities.


***3. Deliver information that supports the decision***


The prescribing moment is not the place for generic awareness messaging.


When an HCP is actively making a treatment decision, the most effective messages are practical, relevant, and immediately useful. Depending on the brand and patient context, that may include:


- Affordability information


- Copay or savings offers


- Access and coverage resources


- Patient support programs


- Clinical considerations


- Next-step guidance for appropriate patients


This is especially important as affordability continues to shape treatment decisions. Even when a provider believes a therapy is clinically appropriate, concerns about patient out-of-pocket cost can create hesitation. If savings information is not available at the moment a prescription is being considered, the opportunity to reduce that friction may be missed.


Point of care media should help providers move from consideration to action with greater confidence.


***4. Connect point of care to the broader omnichannel strategy***


Point of care should not operate in isolation.


A strong media strategy connects awareness, education, and activation across channels. HCPs may encounter brand messaging through programmatic media, endemic healthcare sites, email, social, peer content, conferences, or field engagement. Patients may see related messages through DTC campaigns, search, display, CTV, or other channels.


Point of care becomes more powerful when it connects those upstream efforts to the moment of care.


For example, an HCP may already be aware of a brand through previous media exposure. But when that same provider is treating an eligible patient and sees relevant information in the prescribing workflow, the message becomes more actionable.


The role of point of care is not only to create awareness. It is to convert awareness into treatment action when the opportunity is most immediate.


***5. Measure outcomes beyond engagement***


Clicks, impressions, and engagement rates can help marketers understand whether a message was seen or interacted with. But point of care media should also be evaluated based on whether it influenced meaningful business and patient outcomes.


For pharmaceutical brands, that often means looking at metrics such as:


- Prescription lift


- New patient starts


- Brand conversion


- Patient affordability engagement


- Script abandonment reduction


- Treatment initiation or adherence indicators


The ability to connect point of care engagement to prescribing behavior is critical. Without outcome measurement, brands may know that a campaign delivered media, but not whether it changed behavior.


## **Why the Prescribing Moment Is Hard to Reach**


If the prescribing moment is so valuable, why do so many point of care strategies stop short of it?


Because reaching that moment is difficult.


Many vendors can place media near care. Fewer can deliver messages within the workflows providers use to make treatment decisions. And fewer still can do so at scale, with the data, integrations, and measurement capabilities needed to support life sciences campaigns.


There is a meaningful difference between being adjacent to the clinical encounter and being present when a prescription decision is being made.


For marketers, that difference should shape how they evaluate point of care partners. The question is not simply, “Can this partner reach providers in a healthcare setting?” The better question is, “Can this partner reach providers when they are making prescribing decisions?”
