# Recruitment is a Team Sport: Harnessing Diverse Perspectives Without Bias

Recruitment is like a baseball game: even the best pitcher can’t win alone. It takes a coordinated team effort, with each player contributing their unique skills, to achieve success. Similarly, **recruitment is not a solo act** by HR or hiring managers; it’s a collaborative process where everyone’s strengths must align. When recruitment fails, it’s often due to a lack of collaboration and communication, leading to a “blame game” that benefits no one.


This article, which is part of ourRecruitment series , explores why recruitment can fail and how adopting a team sport mentality can transform the process into a seamless and successful effort.


### **Possible Outcomes from Recruitment Efforts**


Recruitment efforts often lead to mixed outcomes, each carrying its own challenges and uncertainties. Understanding these outcomes allows organisations to identify and address gaps in their recruitment processes Here are the possibilities that organisations frequently face:


1. **Successful Hire with Lingering Doubts**


:


- A candidate is hired and begins work, but there are concerns about how long they will stay or how well they will adapt.


2. **Compromise Due to Urgency**


:


- Unable to find the ideal candidate, the team hires someone who might not fully fit the role. This decision often comes with the hope that they will grow into it.


3. **Failure to Hire**


:


- No suitable candidates are found, leaving the position vacant and putting extra pressure on existing staff.


These outcomes often stem from underlying issues in the recruitment process, leading to uncertainty and inefficiency.


### **Why Recruitment Fails**


Recruitment failures often arise from misaligned efforts and a lack of cohesive strategy. Here are the key reasons:


1. **The Blame Game**


:


- When recruitment fails, teams often point fingers. HR may blame unclear expectations from hiring managers, while managers criticise HR for weak candidates.


2. **Disconnected Processes**


:


- HR and hiring managers often operate in silos, leading to miscommunication and misaligned goals. Without clear collaboration, candidates who may have been a good fit are overlooked.


3. **Outdated Practices**


:


- Using vague or recycled job descriptions can attract unsuitable candidates. This wastes time and resources, making the process feel more frustrating than productive.


4. **Gaps in Interview Skills**


:


- Some managers excel in their technical roles but may lack experience in conducting interviews that assess a candidate’s full potential. This can lead to missed opportunities to hire high-performing individuals.


5. **Candidate Experience Gaps**


:


- Delays, lack of follow-up, or a dismissive attitude during interviews can leave candidates with a negative impression, reducing the company’s appeal and increasing drop-off rates.


These challenges highlight the need for a collaborative and well-orchestrated recruitment strategy.


### **Recruitment Has to Be a Team Sport**


To overcome these challenges, recruitment must become a collaborative effort where everyone leverages their strengths and supports one another. Here’s how to make recruitment a team effort:


1. **Leverage HR’s Expertise**


:


- HR’s role is not confined to posting ads and managing offers. As specialists in understanding human behaviour, they should assess candidates’ non-technical traits, such as adaptability, teamwork, and growth potential.
- Train junior HR staff to handle entry-level roles while senior HR professionals manage high-stakes positions.


2. **Empower Hiring Managers**


:


- Provide hiring managers with training on how to conduct effective interviews. Focus on assessing candidates holistically, rather than just technical fit.
- Encourage managers to collaborate with HR to refine job descriptions and align on role expectations.


3. **Team Member Involvement**


:


- Team members can offer valuable insights into the day-to-day requirements of the role and how a candidate might integrate into the team. However, they need guidance to avoid biases or gatekeeping behaviours.


4. **Support Each Other**


:


- If an interview is delayed, HR or another team member can step in to engage the candidate, answer questions, or share insights about the role. This ensures the candidate feels valued and respected.


5. **No Finger-Pointing**


:


- Recruitment success or failure belongs to the team as a whole. When candidates leave prematurely, it’s an opportunity for everyone to reflect and improve rather than assign blame.


### **How to Make Recruitment a Team Effort**


Developing a collaborative recruitment strategy requires collaboration, communication, and shared responsibility at every stage of the process. Here are actionable strategies to ensure a cohesive and effective approach:


1. **Start with Clear Job Descriptions**


:


- Updating job descriptions is not just about adding a line or two; it’s about ensuring they reflect the evolving needs of the role and organisation.
- Collaborate with hiring managers and team members to create descriptions that are accurate and engaging.
- Outdated templates do no justice to attracting the right talent.


2. **Implement Structured Interview Processes**


:


- Once roles are clearly defined, the next step is to ensure the interview process evaluates candidates fairly and comprehensively.
- A structured interview process helps eliminate bias and ensures fairness.
- Use scoring rubrics that include both technical and non-technical criteria, such as adaptability and teamwork.
- This allows everyone involved to assess candidates on a consistent scale.


3. **Communicate Effectively**


:


- Timely and respectful communication is key.
- From coordinating interview schedules to keeping candidates informed, every touchpoint should reinforce the company’s professionalism and culture.
- If an interview is delayed, step in to make the candidate feel valued—a simple conversation can leave a lasting impression.


4. **Feedback and Continuous Improvement**


:


- Post-recruitment reviews are crucial for identifying strengths and weaknesses in the process.
- Involve all stakeholders, from HR to hiring managers, to ensure a well-rounded perspective.
- Use this feedback to refine strategies and prevent recurring issues.


5. **Unified Goals and Accountability**


:


- Aligning on what success looks like is essential.
- Whether it’s the qualities you seek in a candidate or the expectations for the role, every participant must understand their part in achieving the collective goal.
- Recruitment is not about pointing fingers; it’s about celebrating wins and learning from setbacks as a team.


### **Celebrating Recruitment Success**


Winning the recruitment game takes more than a star player—it requires a team working in harmony.[Research](https://pumble.com/learn/communication/communication-statistics/) shows that **86%** of employees and executives blame workplace failures on poor communication and collaboration. If collaboration is essential for improving productivity and solving challenges within organisations, shouldn’t recruitment—the process that brings trailblazers into the company—be just as collaborative?


By **leveraging each other’s strengths** , addressing gaps in skills, and fostering mutual respect, organisations can transform their recruitment processes into a winning effort. We may not hoist a new recruit like a trophy. However, building a stronger team together is a **shared victory** worth celebrating. By fostering collaboration in recruitment, you not only fill positions but also bring in **trailblazers** who will drive innovation and growth for years to come.
