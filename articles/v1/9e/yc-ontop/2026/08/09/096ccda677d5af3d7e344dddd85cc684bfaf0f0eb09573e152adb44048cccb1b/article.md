# How to pay independent contractors in Venezuela

## How Foreign Companies Can Hire Employees in Venezuela


### Three Primary Hiring Methods


Foreign companies can hire employees in Venezuela through three distinct approaches:


1. **Establishing a Local Entity** : Setting up a local entity reduces risk exposure and enables direct hiring, but can be costly and time-consuming
2. **Using an Employer of Record (EOR)** : An EOR serves as the legal employer of your international workforce, handling hiring, payments, and compliance with Venezuela's labor laws and tax regulations
3. **Hiring Independent Contractors** : Hiring contractors provides a flexible, affordable alternative to hiring local employees, though it involves unique misclassification risks


### Legal Requirements for Foreign Companies


To make recruitments in Venezuela, companies must register with concerned authorities and contribute to social security while maintaining all safety standards before hiring employees.


**Key Prerequisites:**


- Company registration with Venezuelan authorities
- Understanding of prevailing labor laws and norms
- Social security contributions setup
- Compliance with safety standards


## Understanding Venezuela's Labor Laws


### Workforce Composition Requirements


Venezuelan law requires that at least 90% of the payroll be Venezuelans for employers with 10 or more workers. Additionally, compensation for foreign workers cannot exceed 20% of those paid to Venezuelan employees.


### Employment Contract Essentials


Written employment agreements are mandatory in Venezuela and must include personal details of both parties, job duties and responsibilities, employment duration and probationary terms.


**Required Contract Elements:**


- Employee personal details and Venezuelan Identification Number
- Employer's corporate details and representative information
- Scope and location of work
- Start date and employment type
- Working hours and compensation
- Applicable collective bargaining agreements
- Contract duration


### Probationary Period


The probation period in Venezuela has a maximum term of 30 days from the beginning of employment, during which the employee can be dismissed without justified cause.


## Work Permits and Visa Requirements


### Visa Types for Foreign Employees


Venezuela offers various work visas depending on the employee's qualifications, nature of work, and duration of stay:


- **Temporary Work Visa** : Issued for employment purposes, typically valid for one year
- **Permanent Work Visa** : For longer-term employment arrangements
- **Specialized Work Visa** : For positions requiring specific expertise
- **Business Visa** : Required for individuals conducting business activities, generally granted for one year allowing stays up to 180 days


### Processing Requirements


Processing times typically range from 3 to 6 weeks for a work permit, with an additional 2-4 weeks for visa approval. Employers must demonstrate the foreign worker's qualifications, provide an employment contract, and pay necessary fees.


## Common Hiring Challenges in Venezuela


### Economic and Political Environment


Venezuela's economy faces significant challenges including hyperinflation, currency devaluation, and economic instability, which can impact business operations and employee compensation strategies.


### Compliance Complexities


Hiring in Venezuela means navigating local labor laws, mandatory employee benefits, payroll taxes, and strict employment regulations, which aren't always intuitive, especially if your team lacks in-country legal or HR expertise.


### Currency and Payment Issues


Managing currency exchange regulations and ensuring timely salary payments in local currency (Bolívar) requires careful planning, factoring in inflation and currency fluctuations when determining compensation packages.


## Key Employment Benefits and Obligations


### Mandatory Benefits


Employers must contribute between 9% to 11% to the social security system on behalf of their workers, covering retirement, disability, and healthcare benefits.


### Leave Entitlements


- **Annual Leave** : 15 days for full-time workers
- **Maternity Leave** : 26 weeks (6 weeks before and 20 weeks after childbirth)
- **Paternity Leave** : 14 days of paid leave
- **Sick Leave** : Employer covers first three days, then social security provides continued support


### Working Hours and Overtime


- Standard workweek: 40 hours over five days
- Maximum daily hours: 8 hours
- Overtime compensation: 50% extra pay for weekdays, 100% extra for weekends and holidays


### Salary Requirements


Venezuelan law mandates a 13th-month salary ("aguinaldo") paid at year-end, with some employers providing a 14th-month salary as additional benefit.


## Employee Registration and Social Security


### Mandatory Registrations


Companies must register employees with the Venezuelan social security system (Instituto Venezolano de los Seguros Sociales - IVSS) within the first three days of employment.


**Additional Requirements:**


- Join mandatory housing savings fund within 30 days of hiring
- Register at INCES (National Institute of Socialist Training and Education) within 45 working days
- Register in the National Registry of Work Entities (RNET)


## Termination and Severance


### Termination Procedures


Employment can be terminated for just cause, such as misconduct or poor performance, following due process and providing adequate documentation. Employers must navigate termination procedures carefully to avoid legal challenges.


### Notice Periods


Notice periods vary based on length of service, generally requiring 15 to 30 days depending on the employee's tenure with the company.


### Severance Pay


Severance pay is mandatory and calculated based on employee's length of service and last drawn salary, including a base amount plus additional compensation for each year worked.
