# Optimize your startup for growth by hiring remotely

Running a startup in today’s world means taking advantage of every opportunity available to you. That's why embracing remote workers can be the difference between a thriving startup and one that struggles to stay afloat. According to Harvard Business Review,[companies that offer remote work are 50% less likely to experience turnover.](https://hbr.org/2015/01/a-working-from-home-experiment-shows-high-performers-like-it-better)


However, there are many risks associated with hiring remotely that are often not discussed or considered when making the decision to build a remote team.
‍
Today, we'll explore the tips for making it worthwhile and how to balance risk and reward when hiring remotely and find out what it takes to make the most of remote opportunities to reap major benefits for your startup.


## **Assessment time: The Risks and Benefits ‍**


‍


Let's start with the basics...If your startup is dipping its toe into the remote work pool, you may think, **“Yikes! What have I gotten myself into?”** . Don't worry — it's only natural to feel a little overwhelmed when faced with the unknown.


But don't let that stop you from achieving success; if your goals and objectives for taking on remote workers are properly defined, then the risk you take will be worth the reward.


‍


### ‍ **Let's understand some of the potential risks associated with remote work: ‍**


• **Communication overload** – Working with a distributed team can lead to communication overload due to the sheer volume of messages coming from different channels.


• **Differing work ethics** – Different cultures may have different work ethics, and this can be a challenge if you don't have the same standards for remote workers.


• **Uneven workloads** – Working with a distributed team also means that some tasks may not be completed on time due to different time zones or language barriers.


These are just some things to keep in mind ...are you still with me?


‍


## **What's Right for Your Startup?**


**TL;DR:** It is essential for startups to be able to recognize when the risk is worth taking in order to stay competitive in their market.


Running a startup can be downright intimidating — especially when it comes to embracing remote work. At this point, you may be asking yourself, “Can my business really handle this?”


The answer is YES! With the right strategy and team in place, you can make remote working a reality.


But first, let's take a step back and ask yourself: **Is this type of setup really right for my company’s culture and current goals?**


**Checklist checkpoint:** ‍
‍ **Here are some questions to consider before deciding on a remote work setup:**


• Is the current company culture conducive to remote work?


• Are there any tasks that would be difficult or impossible to do with a remote setup?


• Does everyone have access to the tools and resources they need?


• What communication channels should be put in place?


• How can progress and accomplishments be tracked and reported?


When it comes to making a decision about hiring remotely, there are no hard-and-fast rules — sometimes the pros outweigh the cons and sometimes the opposite is true.


By examining your needs carefully and doing research on what others have done successfully, you'll be able to determine if taking on remote workers is the best fit for your business.


‍
