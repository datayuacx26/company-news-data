## Why Getting Paid Internationally Is Still Hard for Many Workers


‍


Remote work grew faster than the systems built to support it. Companies can post a job listing visible to people in 50 countries and hire someone within a week, but sending that person their first payment is where things get difficult.


### Why do fees and delays still eat into international contractor pay?


The cost problem is measurable and significant. According to the[Financial Stability Board's 2024 report](https://remittanceprices.worldbank.org/sites/default/files/rpw_main_report_and_annex_q224.pdf) , the average cost of sending a $200 international remittance was 6.4%, more than double the global target of 3%. In Sub-Saharan Africa, that figure climbed to 7.7%. Transfer fees,[exchange-rate markups](https://www.currencytransfer.com/blog/expert-analysis/how-to-send-large-international-business-payments-without-bank-markups) , and intermediary bank charges compound, reducing what a worker actually receives.


Speed makes the problem worse. Modern remote work happens in real time, yet many cross-border payment systems fall short of global targets for speed and accessibility. A contractor in Lagos or Manila who delivers work on Tuesday shouldn't wonder whether payment will clear by Friday or the following week. That uncertainty erodes trust between companies and their contractors.


### How does a patchwork payment approach create problems at scale?


Most teams handle international contractor payments through wire transfers, third-party apps, and manual currency conversions. As contractor headcount grows, this approach creates compounding delays, inconsistent records, and compliance gaps.[Payroll software](https://www.getontop.com/) built for[global contractor payments, like Ontop](https://www.getontop.com/ontop-reserve) , consolidates payments, compliance, and worker accounts into a single system, giving contractors a dedicated global account to receive and manage earnings without incurring unnecessary conversion costs.


### Why does compliance make cross-border payments even harder for workers?


Following the rules adds another layer of difficulty. Worker classification rules, tax documentation requirements, and local labor regulations differ significantly by country. A contractor in Brazil faces different documentation requirements than one in Portugal or the Philippines. When companies lack systematic processes to manage these differences, workers experience delays in contract finalization, payment approval, and tax form issuance.[The World Bank estimated](https://blogs.worldbank.org/en/peoplemove/in-2024--remittance-flows-to-low--and-middle-income-countries-ar) that global remittance flows reached $856 billion in 2024, underscoring the enormous demand for cross-border payments. The infrastructure serving that demand hasn't kept pace.


The barriers aren't technical mysteries. They're the accumulated weight of financial systems designed for a different era of work, where people stayed in one country, worked for one employer, and received payment through one local bank. The global contractor workforce has moved beyond that model. The payment systems many companies rely on haven't. But choosing the wrong provider can cost you far more than the fees you were trying to avoid.
