## What is FGTS in Brazil?


FGTS is a federal severance and savings fund that protects formal (CLT) workers in Brazil. Every month the employer deposits an amount equal to 8 percent of the employee's pay into an individual account held at Caixa Econômica Federal, and that balance is released to the worker in specific life events. It is a worker protection funded entirely by the employer.


Created in 1967, FGTS exists so that a worker dismissed without cause is not left with nothing. It sits alongside INSS (social security) and income tax withholding as one of the core payroll obligations for anyone employing in Brazil.


## How does the 8 percent FGTS contribution work?


The employer deposits 8 percent of the employee's gross monthly pay into their FGTS account, due by the 7th of each month through Caixa. The contribution is on top of salary, never deducted from it, and it applies to the full pay including most benefits. For young apprentices the rate is 2 percent.


Miss a deadline and the employer owes the deposit plus interest and penalties, and repeated failures invite labor inspections. Because the base is the full monthly salary, the deposit scales with every raise and bonus.


## When can a worker withdraw FGTS?


Withdrawals are allowed only in defined situations, not on demand. The most common is dismissal without just cause, which also unlocks the 40 percent fine. Others include retirement, buying a home under program rules, serious illness of the worker or a dependent, and accounts left dormant for three or more years.

### Can foreigners withdraw FGTS from abroad?


Yes. A foreign worker who was formally employed in Brazil keeps the right to their FGTS balance after leaving the country. The claim is made through Caixa with the worker's documents and account details, and it does not require being physically in Brazil, though the paperwork is stricter than for residents.


## What is the 40 percent FGTS fine for dismissal without cause?


When an employer dismisses a worker without just cause, they pay a fine equal to 40 percent of everything deposited into that worker's FGTS account over the whole employment period, paid directly to the employee. It is one of the largest single costs of ending a contract in Brazil, and it is why classification and clean records matter so much before you hire.


## What is FGTS Digital?


FGTS Digital is the modernized system Brazil rolled out from 2024 that ties FGTS contributions to eSocial and moves filing and payment onto a single digital platform with PIX-based deposits. For employers it changes the mechanics of how deposits are calculated and paid, so payroll processes set up before the reform need to be updated to stay compliant.


## What does FGTS mean for employers hiring in Brazil?


It means FGTS is one of several local obligations, alongside INSS and IRRF, that you cannot skip and cannot easily run from abroad. Deposits must reflect the full salary, every new hire must be registered, and records must be audit-ready. ## Frequently asked questions


### Is FGTS a tax?


No. It is a mandatory employer contribution into the worker's own savings account, not a government tax.


### How much is FGTS?


8 percent of the employee's monthly pay, deposited by the employer, plus a 40 percent fine on the balance if the worker is dismissed without cause. Apprentices are 2 percent.


### Who pays FGTS, the employer or the worker?


The employer, entirely. It is never deducted from the employee's salary.


### Can I withdraw FGTS if I leave Brazil?


Yes. Former workers, including foreigners, can claim their balance from abroad through Caixa with the right documentation.


### What happens if an employer does not pay FGTS?


They owe the missed deposits plus interest and penalties, and face labor claims and inspections.
