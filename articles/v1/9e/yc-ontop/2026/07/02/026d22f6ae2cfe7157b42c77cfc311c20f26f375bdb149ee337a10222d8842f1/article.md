Deel made it easier for companies to follow regulations and manage contractors across different countries, making it one of the most popular platforms for global hiring. Remote built its reputation on compliance infrastructure for international employment, handling legal coverage across jurisdictions, and maintaining documentation for distributed teams. Payoneer focuses on receiving international payments for freelancers and marketplace workers, handling multiple currencies and global transfers effectively. However, it solves only the payout problem; contracts, employment structure, and benefits management require separate platforms. Integration work falls on you and compounds with every additional client.


### 5. Wise (formerly TransferWise)


Wise became popular for offering clear exchange rates and low-cost international transfers. Freelancers and remote workers use it to move money between countries without the extra fees traditional banks charge. It works well for currency conversion, but it's not payroll infrastructure: it lacks support for contracts, a compliance framework, and structured income management. Money moves efficiently, but you must manually track earnings, manage multiple platforms, and reconcile income across disconnected systems.


### 6. Papaya Global


Papaya Global focuses on large companies managing teams across multiple countries, helping them with compliance reporting and payroll processing. From a worker's perspective, it functions like Rippling: the employer controls everything, and you interact with the results of their workflows. You receive payments on their schedule through their chosen methods. The platform excels at giving companies visibility and control, with workers receiving what remains after those priorities are met.


### 7. Oyster


Oyster helps companies hire and manage employees worldwide, simplifying onboarding and ensuring compliance across different countries. For workers, it provides job security but limited control, prioritising company workflows over worker flexibility.


### 8. Gusto (with global partners)


Gusto is a popular US payroll platform with limited international capabilities through integrations. Its strength lies in the domestic payroll. For global workers, it lacks independent flexibility unless tied to a specific employer setup.


### 9. Skrill


Skrill is a digital wallet for international payments, currency transfers, and quick access to your funds. However, it's only a payment tool and doesn't support contracts, structured payroll, or compliance, limiting its usefulness as a complete solution.


### 10. PayPal


PayPal is one of the most widely used global payment platforms, available in many countries. However, it carries higher fees and lacks infrastructure for managing global work: it's a payment layer, not a full system.
