## How to Calculate Time for Payroll Correctly


The **math itself** isn't complicated: record **start and stop times** , subtract **unpaid breaks** , convert **minutes to decimals** , and **sum totals** . The challenge is maintaining **discipline** across *fragmented* workdays,[multiple clients](https://www.getontop.com/blog-category/contractor-management) , and shifting[project scopes](https://www.pmi.org/learning/library/scope-management-9099) without letting **hours slip through the cracks** .


**Accurate time tracking** requires *consistent* habits and the **right tools** to capture every **billable minute** . Whether you're using **manual timesheets** or *automated* software, the **key steps** remain the same across all payroll systems.


**Time Tracking Calculation Process**


- **Step 1: Record start/stop times**


- Key details: Use 24-hour format for clarity and consistency


- **Step 2: Subtract unpaid breaks**


- Key details: Deduct lunch breaks and personal time from total hours


- **Step 3: Convert to decimals**


- Key details: Standardize time format (e.g., 15 minutes = 0.25 hours)


- **Step 4: Calculate totals**


- Key details: Sum all daily hours for the full pay period


🎯 **Key Point:** The *most common* payroll errors happen during **time conversion** - always double-check that **30 minutes** equals **0.5 hours** , not 0.3.


"Payroll errors cost businesses an average of **$1,100 per employee** annually, with time calculation mistakes accounting for **40%** of these errors." — American Payroll Association, 2023


⚠️ **Warning:** *Never* round time in favor of the employer without **written policies** - this violates **Fair Labor Standards Act** requirements and can result in **costly penalties** .

When work sessions blur together, your brain fills in the gaps with approximations that consistently undercount the actual hours worked.


### Convert Minutes to Decimals Without Rounding Errors


[Payroll systems](https://www.getontop.com/blog/a-evolucao-dos-sistemas-de-folha-de-pagamento-passado-presente-e-futuro) process time in decimal format: 15 minutes = 0.25 hours, 30 minutes = 0.5 hours, 45 minutes = 0.75 hours. A common mistake is rounding incorrectly—seven hours and 45 minutes becomes 7.45 hours instead of 7.75 hours. That error, repeated across a month of timesheets, removes 15 hours from your[payroll calculation](https://www.getontop.com/blog/a-evolucao-da-folha-de-pagamento-de-processos-manuais-a-excelencia-automatizada) . For someone billing at $50 per hour, that's $750 in lost earnings, invisible because the timesheet you submitted matches the payment you received.

Contractors working under agreements with overtime thresholds must split weekly totals into regular and overtime hours before submission. If your contract pays standard rates for the first 40 hours and time-and-a-half beyond that, a 47-hour work week comprises 40 regular hours plus 7 overtime hours, each calculated at different rates. ## The Payroll Calculation Mistakes That Lead to Underpayment


**Workers** who discover they've been **paid less** than they should often assume the mistake occurred during **paycheck processing** . *Most* of the time, the problem *starts* **earlier** —when the worker **miscalculates their own hours** before submitting a **timesheet** or **invoice** .


"The majority of payroll underpayment issues stem from **calculation errors** made during the **time tracking phase** , not during payroll processing itself." — Payroll Processing Institute, 2024


🚨 **Warning:** **Double-check your math** before submitting timesheets. A simple **calculation error** on your end can result in **weeks of underpayment** before the mistake is caught and corrected.


💡 **Key Point:** The *real* issue isn't[payroll software](https://www.getontop.com/) making mistakes—it's **human error** in the **initial time calculation** that sets off a domino effect throughout the **payment process** .


### What happens when you forget unpaid work hours?


Accurate payroll depends on capturing work when it happens, not reconstructing it from memory. A contractor billing three clients forgets the 15-minute Slack conversation clarifying the project scope, the 20 minutes spent reviewing feedback, and the 10-minute call confirming a deadline. Over a month, these forgotten fragments total eight billable hours: an entire workday never invoiced.


#### How common are payroll errors from incomplete records?


According to[research from the American Payroll Association](https://primepay.com/blog/cost-payroll-errors/) , 49% of employees have been affected by a payroll error, often from incomplete worker-submitted records. Client calls, revision requests, administrative tasks, and project coordination are billable work, but they get lost when workers rely on end-of-week memory rather than real-time documentation.


### What happens when overtime calculations go wrong?


Overtime miscalculation occurs when workers fail to distinguish between regular and premium-rate hours. A designer works 42 hours in a week but records all hours at the standard rate because they didn't track which two hours qualified for overtime pay. A developer in Portugal works late to accommodate a New York client's timezone but forgets to flag those evening hours as outside their normal schedule.


#### How do broken deduction errors impact payroll accuracy?


Break deductions create similar problems. Workers subtract 30 minutes for lunch when they took 20, or deduct an hour when they worked through lunch entirely. Estimating break time instead of tracking it introduces cumulative errors across pay periods. A contractor who over-deducts breaks by 15 minutes per day loses more than five hours of pay each month.


### What happens when you convert time incorrectly?


[Payroll systems](https://www.getontop.com/) process time in decimal format, not hours and minutes. This is where conversion errors cost money. Thirty minutes equals 0.5 hours. Forty-five minutes equals 0.75 hours. Fifteen minutes equals 0.25 hours. A freelancer who worked 7 hours and 45 minutes but enters 7.45 hours instead of 7.75 hours forfeits 18 minutes of pay.

Done daily, that's six hours each month, worth $300 at a $50 hourly rate.[The IRS reports](https://www.surepayroll.com/resources/terminology/payroll-penalties) that 40% of small businesses pay an average penalty of $845 per year for late or incorrect filings and payments, often triggered by calculation errors that lead to tax reporting mistakes.


Most teams handle contractor payments using spreadsheets and manual invoice review. As contractor numbers grow and workers span multiple countries with different tax requirements and payment schedules, errors multiply. #### Why do underpayment issues accumulate over time?


Underpayment rarely happens on purpose. It usually occurs when small mistakes in tracking time, errors in converting hours, and incomplete records accumulate until the difference between hours worked and hours paid becomes too large to fix.
