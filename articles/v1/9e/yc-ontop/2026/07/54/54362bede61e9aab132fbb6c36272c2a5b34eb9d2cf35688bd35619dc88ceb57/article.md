## What Are B2B Cross-Border Payments?


**B2B cross-border payments** are transactions where *one* business sends money to another across[international borders](https://hurricanecommerce.com/cross-border-trade-what-it-is-and-why-it-matters/) to[contractors, freelancers](https://www.getontop.com/blog-category/contractor-management) , **agencies** , **suppliers** , and **service providers** in *different* countries.


💡 **Example:** A **contractor in Colombia** gets payment from a **U.S. company** . A **freelancer in the Philippines** gets paid by a **German client** . A[remote employee](https://www.getontop.com/blog/5-reasons-for-companies-to-hire-remote-workers) in **Kenya** works for a **UK-headquartered business** .


For workers, these payments often work *invisibly* until **something goes wrong** . Each transaction requires money to[cross borders](https://stripe.com/resources/more/cross-border-payments-explained) before the worker can access **earnings** .


"Cross-border payments are the **backbone of global commerce** , enabling businesses to operate across **multiple countries** and access **international talent pools** ." — Global Payment Systems Report, 2024


🔑 **Key Takeaway:** **Cross-border B2B payments** are *essential* for **global business operations** , but they introduce **complexity** and **potential delays** that can impact **worker satisfaction** and **business relationships** .


### Common B2B Cross-Border Payment Scenarios


Companies regularly make international payments for contractor compensation, freelance project fees, agency retainers, vendor invoices, and salaries for distributed teams. These transactions involve considerably more complexity than domestic transfers.


Cross-border payments pass through multiple banks, payment networks, and intermediary institutions before reaching the recipient.[According to PYMNTS](https://ziphq.com/blog/b2b-payments-statistics) , 70% of businesses report challenges with cross-border payment processing times. Each additional participant introduces processing delays, fees, or documentation requirements.


Currency conversion adds another friction layer. When the sender and recipient use different currencies, funds must be exchanged at constantly fluctuating rates, and exchange-rate spreads can reduce take-home income without workers understanding why.
