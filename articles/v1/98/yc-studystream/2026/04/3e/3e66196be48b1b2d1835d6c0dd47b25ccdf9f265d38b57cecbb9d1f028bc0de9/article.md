# Why Study With Others Online? Perks of Online Study Groups

Procrastinating on purpose because the motivation just isn’t there, or trying hard to concentrate but failing miserably—if either sounds familiar, know you’re not the only one.


In fact, that’s how it usually goes when studying alone. Isolation and productivity are for some just not a good mix, especially when distractions abound, and there’s no one to keep you accountable.


Learning alongside peers, on the other hand, may lead to better focus, motivation, and overall experience for some people. And, you don’t have to be in the same room as your study buddies.


You can study with others online through “focus rooms” that combine real-time accountability with self-paced learning.


### Benefits of Studying with Others


Study groups, whether online or in-person, can impact how you study, how you stay focused, and even how much you retain from what you’ve learned.


**Accountability:** Studying with other people comes with a sort of accountability. Performing a task alongside others, even if they’re minding their own work in the process, still offers that push to actually finish what you’ve started or get a significant amount of work done.


[Studies on collaborative learning](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12015942/#S2) show that students take responsibility not only for their own progress but also for their peers’. This idea of a shared responsibility ramps up commitment and participation.


**Focus:** Studying in a group can also sharpen your focus. When you study with friends online, active discussions and shared tasks keep your mind engaged, encouraging deeper thinking.


Study groups online don’t have to be particularly collaborative, however. Even working in silence alongside peers can put meaningful learning at the forefront by shifting the attention away from academic performance and towards the specific task you’re doing.


**Shared Energy:** There’s a unique energy that comes from learning alongside others. Seeing them deep at work can inspire you to do the same, fostering motivation and focus while reducing hesitation around getting started.


### Should You Join an Online Study Group?


If you struggle with motivation and want[connection even while learning remotely](https://drexel.edu/soe/resources/student-teaching/advice/benefits-of-online-and-virtual-learning/) , then joining an online study group may be a great idea.


Members of these groups are usually people who prefer working alongside others, even without conversations. Being in the company of others already helps with getting started and staying motivated, especially when you see them focused on their own work.


Online study groups offer flexibility and independence, since you’re in full control of when you join and how you structure your learning. But it also comes with a sense of shared accountability that makes it far easier to keep moving.

### How Virtual Study Groups Work


Virtual study groups can work asynchronously: you simply share folders or exchange notes through chatrooms or emails instead of setting up live calls. This is a lot more flexible—good for when you and your buddies can’t find a mutual free time to meet online.


If you prefer connecting synchronously, video conferencing works best. You can either study with friends or strangers online—each option has its perks.


With friends, you may be able to[discuss insights](https://weingartencenter.universitylife.upenn.edu/online-study-groups-a-quick-guide/) more freely in real time. But for people who prefer working in silence to stay focused, working with strangers does the trick.

### How to Have a Good Virtual Study Session


What makes a great online study group session? Start by choosing a setup that feels most comfortable for you, whether that’s working with friends or sitting virtually with strangers.


Once you’ve landed on a choice, here’s a[quick checklist](https://meet.nyu.edu/academics/student-voices/tips-and-tricks-for-virtual-learning/) of tips to make your study sessions more productive:


- Bring snacks or a drink to stay energized
- Decide whether you want to keep your camera on or off (and communicate your preferences if needed)
- Find a quiet space where you can work freely and without distractions
- Prepare headphones just in case
- Put your phone on silent or in another room
- Take regular breaks when you feel tired or overwhelmed (if you need guidance here, consider trying the[Pomodoro technique](https://www.studystream.live/blog/pomodoro-timer-boost-your-focus-and-productivity/) )
- Be kind to yourself and don’t expect perfection
