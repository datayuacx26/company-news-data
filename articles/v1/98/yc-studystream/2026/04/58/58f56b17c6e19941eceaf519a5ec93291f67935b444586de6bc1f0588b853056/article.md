# How to Study When You Have No Motivation

Finding the motivation to study is often harder than studying itself. It’s very easy to feel overwhelmed, especially when studying feels like pressure instead of progress.


The thing is, you don’t necessarily need motivation to study, and it doesn’t have to feel hard. You only need help getting started. A few tips and tricks about focus when learning how to study when you have no motivation go a long way, which is what we’ll show you here.


### Why Waiting for Motivation to Study Doesn’t Work


Waiting for motivation to study never works. In fact, it actually works against you. As you wait or try to come up with ways to find motivation, you’re losing time you could spend, well, actually studying.


This only leads you further down the rabbit hole of feeling stressed, overwhelmed, and lacking time. Getting used to the pattern of waiting is equivalent to procrastinating.


And if you need a little bit of science behind it,[this study](https://econtent.hogrefe.com/doi/10.1024/2673-8627/a000074) concludes that even motivated students can procrastinate. Motivation isn’t a reliable catalyst for studying, so learning how to study without it is what actually helps achieve better progress.


### How to Study When You’re Unmotivated (Hint: Start Without It)


Now that you know that waiting for motivation won’t do you any good in the long run, here’s what to do instead: just start.


Have you ever noticed how motivation tends to show up shortly after you begin studying? Even 5 minutes can create momentum, and once you begin, it’s much easier to keep going.


It also helps to remember your “Why.” Why are you studying? What are your long-term goals? What will it help you to achieve in 3, 5, or even 10 years? Such clarity can help you take much-needed action when you feel unmotivated.

### Study Motivation Tips That Work


If you often find yourself lacking motivation, shift your focus to things you can control to make studying easier.


**Lower Friction:** Prepare your study space in advance by organizing your materials, having your laptop ready, and removing any distractions. The easier it is to start, the more likely you actually will.


**Time Block Your Study Sessions** : Instead of relying on motivation and mood, set a specific time to study. Get started with only 5 minutes of completing easy tasks that generate momentum, followed by a[Pomodoro technique](https://www.studystream.live/blog/why-pomodoro-technique-works/) (a 25-minute study block followed by a 5-minute break). This will help you stay consistent without feeling overwhelmed.


**Use Small Rewards:** After each Pomodoro session, give yourself a small reward to look forward to. During your short break, feel free to enjoy a snack, a stroll around the block, time on your phone, or anything that will help associate studying with positive outcomes.
