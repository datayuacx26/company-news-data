Reducing investigator workload starts with automation. Modern investigative platforms take the highest-volume tasks, such as media categorization, identity resolution, link analysis and cross-case correlation, and cut them from hours to minutes, so agencies can clear more casework without adding headcount.


## **Key Takeaways**


- **Spend less time on manual review and more time investigating.** Modern investigative tools automate media categorization, identity resolution and data correlation, allowing you to focus on developing leads instead of sorting through digital evidence.
- **Find connections that manual investigations can miss.** Cross-device and cross-case analysis automatically surfaces relationships, timelines and recurring people of interest, helping uncover leads that would otherwise remain hidden.
- **Protect your team while reducing burnout.** Automation can minimize exposure to traumatic content, eliminate repetitive investigative tasks and help teams manage growing caseloads without additional headcount.


The numbers speak for themselves in[Cellebrite’s 2026 Industry Trends Survey](https://cellebrite.com/en/2026-industry-trends/) , where 80% of agencies report worsening case backlogs year over year. The average case now spans between two and five devices, while the average phone holds 100,000 items. Investigators are spending 69 hours per case reviewing media and writing reports, without any actual investigative work.


Law enforcement agencies continue to face staffing shortages driven by declining applications, heightened operational demands and shifting generational expectations around work-life balance. In the meantime, current staff are already stretched to the max, in fact 50% of investigators say they lack the right tools to handle the volume they’re facing.


**The question now is how to reduce investigator workload to make your existing team sustainable.**


The good news is that technology built specifically for investigators already exists. For years, advanced analytical tools were the domain of the lab. Today, the capability has moved closer to the investigator. Solutions like Cellebrite Pathfinder are designed to sit directly in investigators’ hands, reducing the distance between raw data and actionable leads.


> *Technology isn’t only for examiners anymore. And for agencies carrying the weight of today’s caseloads, that shift matters.*


Below are four capabilities that help investigators do more — without the additional headcount that isn’t coming.


## 1. Stop Making Investigators Scroll


Every seized device is, in practical terms, a digital landfill. Images and videos from years of daily life mixed in with evidence. The old approach is someone sitting down and manually reviewing everything.


That’s not an investigation. That’s an excavation.


Modern investigative platforms can automatically categorize media during ingestion, sorting content into law-enforcement-oriented categories, like drugs, weapons, cash and more. From there, natural language search lets investigators describe what they’re looking for rather than scrolling to find it. OCR (Optical Character Recognition) applied to images makes text within photos fully searchable. Need photos where someone is holding an object? Need screenshots for a specific application? Just type it in the search bar.


This is what solutions like Cellebrite Pathfinder are built to do, so the sorting is done while the investigator focuses on other tasks. The time savings are significant — from hours or days to minutes. Instead of wondering what’s buried in a folder, investigators are diving right into clusters of interest.


## 2. Who Is This Person?


Sometimes a device owner’s identity isn’t in question, investigators know exactly who they’re dealing with from the first minute. But that’s not always how it goes.


Sometimes a device comes in and the ownership isn’t immediately clear. Maybe it’s a burner phone or registered to someone else. Perhaps the person of interest operates across multiple identities deliberately – different names on different platforms. The phone number is just one data point, and on its own, it doesn’t tell you much.


What investigators need to answer is a more layered question. They need to identify who this person is across all available communications, what email addresses they use and their social media handles.


Investigative analytics platforms address this through person attribution — automatically collecting scattered identifiers like phone numbers, email addresses, usernames and social accounts and merging them into one named profile. Instead of chasing a number through five different data sources, the investigator sees a person. The shift from identifier to identity changes the quality of the investigation from the start.


In Pathfinder, this unified persona becomes a working profile, showing the investigator how the individual appears across every communication channel, what names they use where and which identifiers are linked to them across the full dataset.


From there, the next question becomes the more interesting one. This is the point where investigators can see who the individual is connected to and is they talking to known players. They can also identify if they’ve previously encountered people in their daily communication orbit.


Link analysis maps this automatically, visualizing a person of interest’s full social network so investigators can drill into any connection and see the detail behind it. What used to require days of manual cross-referencing surfaces in minutes. And the connections that might once have been missed – because no one remembered seeing that number in a case from 14 months ago – now appear on their own.


## 3. Protecting Your People


This part isn’t talked about enough.


Investigators working child exploitation cases face documented psychological harm from sustained exposure to CSAM (Child Sexual Abuse Material). Vicarious trauma is a real occupational risk, and for units that regularly handle these cases, the exposure compounds over time. Retention suffers due to severe burnout.


Investigative technology directly addresses this issue. When platforms automatically flag and categorize explicit content — including suspected child exploitation material — investigators can understand the scope of what’s on a device without having to view every item. They review a representative sample, enough to build the case, confirm the categories and determine investigative next steps — without the lasting psychological cost of full exposure.


Pathfinder does this automatically on ingestion, so the protection is built into the workflow, not something an investigator has to configure or opt into.


Agencies that are serious about investigator welfare should be asking whether their tools are designed to protect the people using them, because how an investigation affects the person running it matters.


## 4. The Context Your Team Might Have Overlooked


No investigator can perfectly recall every detail across hundreds of cases. Patterns in criminal activity often span months or years, and the connections that crack a case are sometimes hiding in data from an investigation that was closed a year ago.


Cross-device and cross-case correlation are one of the more powerful capabilities available in modern investigative platforms. When evidence from multiple devices in the same case reveals overlapping locations, contacts or timelines, those correlations can surface automatically, without an investigator having to manually cross-reference every data point. The same applies when comparing new cases to older investigations.


Consider what this means in practice. A drug trafficking case closes and 18 months later, a new case comes in. One of the numbers in the new extraction matches a contact from the old case. Without cross-case correlation, that connection surfaces only if someone remembers the right detail at the right time – *if that person even still works the same cases in the same unit.*


These aren’t small gaps, they’re the difference between a stalled case and a cracked network.


In Pathfinder, cross-device analysis correlates events across devices simultaneously — identifying mutual contacts, communication spikes and shared locations that wouldn’t be visible from a single device extraction. The picture that would have taken a team days to assemble is surfaced almost immediately.


## Making the Team You Have Last


The burnout problem in law enforcement is real, well-documented and not going away on its own.


Yet a significant portion of what’s causing burnout isn’t the investigative work itself. It’s volume management, manual triage, the exposure to content no one should have to absorb alone and the sense that despite all the hours, connections are still being missed.


The right investigative technology doesn’t replace investigators. It removes the parts of the job that shouldn’t be theirs to carry in the digital age — so their time, judgment and expertise go where they actually count.


Platforms like Cellebrite Pathfinder are built with exactly that in mind. That’s not automation for its own sake, it’s how you build a team that’s sustainable.
