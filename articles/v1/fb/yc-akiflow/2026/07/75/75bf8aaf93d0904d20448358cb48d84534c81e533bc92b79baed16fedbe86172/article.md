## What are the best apps that combine calendar and to do list in 2026?


The five best apps that combine a calendar and to-do list in 2026 are Akiflow, Sunsama, Motion, Morgen, and Todoist. Akiflow is our best overall pick because it puts tasks from multiple work tools beside the calendars that determine when those tasks can actually happen, without forcing the user to surrender control of the schedule.


This ranking was checked on July 27, 2026. It is deliberately short. A product earned a place only if it handles both sides of the job: remembering work and making time for it. We looked at capture speed, calendar visibility, time blocking, integrations, rescheduling, mobile and desktop access, and the amount of daily maintenance each system creates.


The products are not interchangeable. Akiflow is a consolidation tool for a busy work stack. Sunsama is a guided daily planner. Motion is an automatic scheduler with a broader work platform around it. Morgen is strongest when several calendars and task sources need one planning layer. Todoist is still primarily a task manager, but its calendar layout now makes it a credible lighter option.

## 1. Akiflow is the best overall choice


Akiflow is the best choice for people whose tasks arrive from more places than one list can comfortably hold. Its Universal Inbox collects work from connected tools, while the calendar, time blocking, quick capture, keyboard shortcuts, and daily planning ritual turn that incoming work into a plan the user can see and adjust.


The distinction is important. A normal calendar integration can display due dates, but it does not necessarily help someone decide what deserves Tuesday at 10:00. In Akiflow, a task from email, Slack, Notion, Todoist, or a project tool can enter the inbox, keep a link to its source, and then be scheduled beside fixed meetings. That makes the calendar a decision surface rather than a second database.


Akiflow also leaves the final scheduling judgment with the user. Aki can help create tasks, organize work, and handle planning commands, but the experience is not built around silently rebuilding the entire day. That balance is particularly useful for founders, operators, consultants, and managers whose priorities change for reasons an algorithm may not understand. The related[calendar and task integration guide](https://akiflow.com/blog/calendar-task-management-integration-productivity/) explains the workflow in more depth.


The drawback is price. The[current Akiflow plan](https://akiflow.com/pricing) is $34 per month or $19 per month billed yearly after a seven-day trial. That is difficult to justify for a simple personal checklist. It makes more sense when fragmented intake, calendar negotiation, and repeated context switching are already costing meaningful time each week.


*Akiflow puts the task list and weekly calendar in one workspace, letting users move incoming work into visible time blocks while keeping control of the plan.*


## 2. Sunsama is best for guided daily planning


Sunsama is the best choice for someone who wants planning to be a deliberate daily ritual. Its guided flow asks the user to review unfinished work, import tasks and meetings, estimate durations, check the planned workload, defer excess commitments, and then decide which tasks should be timeboxed on the calendar.


That sequence is more than interface polish. Sunsama compares planned task time with a workload threshold and shows whether the day is likely to run past the preferred shutdown time. Tasks can come from its backlog or connected systems such as Asana, Jira, Todoist, Gmail, and calendars. The result is a calmer plan built around capacity rather than an optimistic list.


The tradeoff is pace. People who enjoy the ritual often find it grounding; people who already know what they need to schedule may find the extra steps slow. Sunsama also asks the user to make most prioritization decisions rather than automatically resolving the calendar. Its[current Pro plan](https://sunsama.com/pricing) is $17 per month billed yearly or $22 billed monthly, with a 14-day trial.


## 3. Motion is best for automatic scheduling


Motion is the strongest pick when the buyer wants the software to decide when flexible work should happen. Tasks carry priorities, deadlines, and durations; Motion places them around meetings and can replan the calendar as conditions change. That is a different promise from a manual time-blocking tool, and for the right user it removes real planning labor.


Motion now extends well beyond a personal calendar. Its Pro AI plan includes projects and tasks, calendar and meetings, docs, notes, integrations, an AI task planner, and apps across desktop and mobile. The breadth is useful for a small team that wants project execution and scheduling in the same system, rather than a planner sitting on top of several existing tools.


The limitation follows from the strength: the schedule can feel like the software's opinion of the day. Users who want to protect a particular block for strategic or personal reasons may prefer a planner where changes are more explicit. Motion's[official pricing page](https://www.usemotion.com/pricing?tool=motion) lists Pro AI at $19 per seat per month on annual billing and Business AI at $29.


## 4. Morgen is best for multiple calendars


Morgen is the best fit when calendar fragmentation is the central problem. It brings Google, Outlook, iCloud, Fastmail, and other calendar accounts into one view, connects task systems such as Todoist, Notion, Linear, ClickUp, and Google Tasks, and supports time blocking with its AI Planner across desktop, mobile, browser, and Linux.


This makes Morgen attractive to consultants, agency owners, and anyone managing separate personal, client, and company calendars. The planning layer can show conflicts across those accounts, while scheduling links and a booking page reduce the need for a separate meeting tool. It provides more calendar breadth than a task manager with one calendar view bolted on.


Morgen is less compelling when the main pain is rapid capture from messages and work apps. It connects many task sources, but Akiflow offers a more pronounced inbox-to-calendar workflow. Morgen's[individual plan](https://www.morgen.so/pricing) is $30 billed monthly or $15 per month billed yearly. That pricing makes it a strong value when cross-calendar planning is the actual requirement.


## 5. Todoist is best for lightweight capture


Todoist is the best lightweight option for people who primarily need an excellent task manager and only occasionally plan work on a calendar. Quick Add, natural-language dates, recurring tasks, priorities, filters, and broad platform support make capture unusually fast. The Pro calendar layout adds enough visual planning for many personal and small-team workflows.


In calendar layout, a user can drag an all-day task into a time slot and resize it to set a duration. Todoist also syncs with Google Calendar and supports reminders and deadlines on paid plans. This is a meaningful improvement over a list-only setup, especially for someone who wants one familiar app rather than a dedicated daily planning system.


Todoist still behaves like a task manager first. Its[task duration documentation](https://www.todoist.com/help/articles/set-a-task-duration-L1kYkZv8d) notes that start dates are not supported, and it does not provide the same universal intake and calendar review workflow as Akiflow. Choose it when simplicity is an advantage, not when work is already scattered across several operational tools.


## How should you choose between these five apps?


Choose by identifying which decision is currently expensive. Pick Akiflow when work arrives from several tools and you want to decide the schedule quickly. Pick Sunsama when overcommitment and an inconsistent planning habit are the problems. Pick Motion when choosing times is the burden you want to delegate. Pick Morgen when multiple calendars create the confusion. Pick Todoist when capture is the main need.


Then test the product with a normal, imperfect week. Connect the calendars that really control your availability, bring in ten active tasks, schedule three important pieces of work, and allow a meeting to move. The useful question is not whether the first plan looks clean. It is whether the app makes the second plan obvious after the first one breaks.


Pay attention to maintenance. Count how many times work must be copied, how often a date and a calendar block disagree, and whether unfinished tasks are easy to recover. A premium planner should reduce those decisions, not decorate them. ## Frequently asked questions


### What is the best app that combines a calendar and to-do list?


Akiflow is the best overall choice for professionals who need tasks, calendars, integrations, and time blocking in one place. Sunsama is better for a guided ritual, Motion for automatic scheduling, Morgen for multiple calendars, and Todoist for a lighter task-first workflow.


### Is Akiflow better than Sunsama?


Akiflow is better when fast capture, connected task sources, keyboard control, and direct calendar execution matter most. Sunsama is better when the user wants a slower guided planning process that checks workload and encourages deliberate shutdown. Both are manual-first planners, but they create very different daily rhythms.


### Is Motion better than Akiflow for scheduling?


Motion is better if automatic rescheduling is the main reason for buying the app. Akiflow is better if the user wants to collect work from several tools, see it beside the calendar, and retain control over what moves. The choice is between delegated scheduling and faster human-directed planning.


### Can Todoist replace a dedicated daily planner?


Todoist can replace a dedicated planner when task capture, due dates, reminders, and occasional calendar blocking cover the workflow. It becomes less complete when the user needs one inbox for several work tools, a structured planning ritual, or frequent recovery from meeting-heavy days.
