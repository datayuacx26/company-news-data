# The hardest part of a medicine delivery is proving it happened

# Twenty Facilities, Seven Days a Week, One Pharmacy in Fishers *‍*


##### *That is the route Lifespan Pharmacy runs today. Every delivery on it has to satisfy state regulations, pharmacy protocols, and patient privacy rules. And here is the kicker: they have to prove it arrived, at the right place, without anyone else opening the package along the way. That is the problem behind the letter of intent Arrive AI signed with Lifespan.*


##### **TL;DR:** Arrive AI signed a letter of intent with Lifespan Pharmacy in Fishers, Indiana. Lifespan serves long-term care facilities, which means medication moving to roughly 20 sites every day of the week. The hard part is not the driving. It is proving who touched the medication and when. That is what the Arrive Point was built to do.


## The news


Arrive AI has signed a letter of intent with Lifespan Pharmacy, a long-term care pharmacy based in Fishers, Indiana. The relationship runs through Matt Balla, Vice President of Pharmacy, who Dan LaReau (DL) has known for years.


Lifespan supplies medication to roughly 20 client facilities across Indiana. Deliveries run seven days a week. There is a related long-term care operation, CarDon, on the facility side of that relationship and taking care of their clients.


Add it up and you get a lot of logistics, a lot of expense, and a lot of daily coordination for a team that would rather be filling prescriptions.


## Why a pharmacy leader saw the fit immediately


Matt Balla is not new to technology in pharmacy. He was involved in the first internet pharmacy and has helped build other firsts in the space since.


So when DL described what Arrive AI is doing and where it is going, Matt did not need a long runway. He saw the fit for pharmaceuticals right away. Dan's assessment of the situation was blunt and generous at the same time: Matt got there before we did.


That is worth pausing on. The people who have spent decades inside pharmacy logistics are the ones who recognize the handoff problem fastest, because they live with it.


## Chain of custody is the product


Here is the part people miss when they picture autonomous delivery. They picture the robot. They picture the drone.


What matters in pharmacy is the handoff.


A pharmaceutical delivery is a chain of accountability. Every point where a person picks up the package, sets it down, or hands it off is a point where that chain can break, and a point where a facility has to be able to show a regulator what happened.


The Arrive Point is a secure, climate-controlled delivery endpoint. A robot, a drone, or a person can use it. The medication goes in, the chain of custody is recorded, and the right person retrieves it on their own schedule. Nobody waits for anybody, and nothing is left sitting in a hallway.


Security, privacy, and a record that holds up. That is the value Lifespan is looking at, and it is the same reason the Arrive Point works in a hospital.


## The roadmap: drone first, then the ground robot


LaReau laid out the sequence the two companies want to demonstrate.


Step one is a drone flight from Lifespan's production pharmacy to an Arrive Point at one of their facilities. That step depends on where drone regulations land, and Dan expects more clarity over the coming months.


Step two is a ground robot picking up from that Arrive Point and carrying the package the rest of the way to the patient care unit. That removes the internal walk that staff make today.


Put together, that is the whole ecosystem in one route. Air to endpoint. Endpoint to bedside. No handoff left to chance.


Lifespan is ready to move as fast as the rules allow. Local demonstration projects are the near-term goal.


## Meanwhile, at Hancock


Arrive AI's live deployment is at Hancock Regional Hospital in Greenfield, Indiana, where an autonomous ground robot moves lab specimens between Arrive Points on campus.


The results there: a 98 percent improvement in on-time delivery and a 50 percent reduction in lab transport time.


Now the hospital wants to extend it. The Parkway building sits about 100 meters from the main hospital. Staff make four or five trips a day from that outbuilding to the central lab. Arrive AI is working with the Hancock team to add that route using hardware already on site.


Every successful deployment makes the next conversation easier. Health care systems and pharmacies talk to each other. A working route in Greenfield is a better argument than any slide deck.
