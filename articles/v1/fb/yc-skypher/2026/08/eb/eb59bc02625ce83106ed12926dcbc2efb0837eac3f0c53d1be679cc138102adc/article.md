# What Are the Best Qvidian Alternatives in 2026?

‍


*Last updated: August 2026*


‍


The best Qvidian alternative depends on which of three jobs you are replacing: proposal production, security questionnaire response, or DDQ cycles. For proposal-led teams the closest like-for-like replacements are Responsive and Loopio; for questionnaire and DDQ volume the AI-native options are AutoRFP, Conveyor, Skypher, and Wolfia; for government and defence proposals AutogenAI is the only one holding FedRAMP High.


‍


Qvidian itself remains a reasonable choice if your requirement is regulatory audit depth rather than automation speed, and this article says where that is true.

## How we evaluated these tools


Seven criteria, applied identically to every tool including Skypher.


‍


**Portal coverage.** Whether the vendor's own integration documentation lists third-party risk portals such as OneTrust, ServiceNow, Archer, ProcessUnity or Whistic. Checked against each vendor's published integrations page, not their marketing claims. This criterion carries weight because[19.2% of security questionnaires arrive through portals](https://skypher.co/post/how-to-answer-security-questionnaires) , against 71.2% Excel and 9.6% Word, across 100,000+ questions processed through Skypher.


‍


**Pricing transparency.** Whether a figure is published on the vendor's own site. Prices quoted in competitors' comparison articles were not accepted as sources.


‍


**Knowledge model.** Whether the tool requires a curated question-and-answer library that someone maintains, or reads existing source documents.


‍


**Time to value.** Published implementation data where it exists. G2 aggregates this from real users for some tools and not others.


‍


**Independent review evidence.** G2 rating and review volume. Volume matters as much as the score.


‍


**Scope.** Which of RFPs, security questionnaires and DDQs the tool actually covers.


‍


**Format handling.** Excel with macros and conditional logic, Word with tables, PDFs, and portal-based submission.


‍


Where we could not verify something from a primary source, it is marked "not verified" rather than assumed absent.

## Proposal-led Qvidian alternatives


### AutogenAI


Builds a bespoke language model per customer trained on your own proposals and win themes, so drafts carry your voice from the start. **The only tool here with FedRAMP High** , which for US federal work handling controlled unclassified information is a procurement requirement rather than a preference. G2 4.4.


‍


**Genuine weakness:** no public pricing, minimum seat commitments and onboarding fees. Not built for high-volume questionnaire or DDQ work, so teams doing both need a second tool.


### Loopio


Cleaner interface than Qvidian with a well-governed content library and a 9.7 out of 10 G2 support score. G2 4.7. For teams whose main complaint about Qvidian is adoption friction among occasional contributors, this is the smallest change that fixes it.


‍


**Genuine weakness:** the same curated library model underneath, so the maintenance burden that frustrates Qvidian teams carries over. No public pricing. AI was added to an existing platform rather than built in.


### Responsive (RFPIO)


The deepest integration ecosystem in the category, with mature analytics and broad procurement portal connectivity. G2 4.5. The closest structural match to what Qvidian actually does.


‍


**Genuine weakness:** shares Qvidian's architecture, so teams leaving because of AI quality find the same ceiling. G2 reviewers flag navigation complexity for occasional contributors. No public pricing, and seat-based costs create the same friction when you loop in reviewers.

## Lightweight Qvidian alternatives

Surfaces answers inside Slack, Teams and Google Chat, so contributors work in tools they already use and there is effectively no learning curve. G2 4.9. A free plan exists.


‍


**Genuine weakness:** not built for complex multi-document proposal workflows with review gates and multi-stakeholder coordination. Teams needing both a questionnaire tool and a proposal platform will still need two.

Connects AI agents to live source systems including SharePoint, Confluence, Notion and Google Drive, so answers reflect current documents with no parallel library to curate. Confidence scores and citations on every answer. G2 4.9.

## Which problem are you actually solving?


Match the reason Qvidian stopped working to the shortlist. The wrong category wastes an evaluation cycle.


‍


- **"Our AI does not draft, it just searches."** Your reviewers are describing an architecture, not a setting. Look at tools built after 2023: AutoRFP, Conveyor, Skypher, Wolfia, Arphie. Loopio and Responsive share Qvidian's underlying model and will not fix this.
- **"We spend more time maintaining the library than answering."** You need a tool that reads source documents rather than curated Q&A pairs: Arphie, AutoRFP, Skypher, Wolfia.

## FAQ


### What is the best Qvidian alternative for enterprise proposal teams?


Responsive and Loopio are the closest like-for-like replacements, since both cover proposal production, content governance and multi-contributor workflows the way Qvidian does. Responsive has the deeper integration ecosystem; Loopio has the cleaner interface and a 9.7 out of 10 G2 support score. Neither publishes pricing, and both share Qvidian's curated-library model, so neither solves the AI architecture complaint.


### How much does Qvidian cost, and which alternatives publish pricing?


Qvidian does not publish pricing; it is custom enterprise pricing within the Upland Software portfolio. G2's aggregated user data reports 4 months to implement, 16 months to return on investment, and an average discount of 7%. Two alternatives publish figures on their own sites: AutoRFP at $899 a month for Scale and $1,299 for Accelerate, and Conveyor from $9,600 a year for Business. Loopio, Responsive, AutogenAI, Wolfia, Arphie and Skypher all require a sales conversation.


### Does Qvidian handle security questionnaires and DDQs?


It markets both, and DDQ writers on G2 confirm using it for annual cycles where the same questions repeat, describing the template linking as a real time-saver. The constraint is portal coverage: Qvidian's published integrations on both its own site and its G2 listing contain no third-party risk portals, so questionnaires arriving through OneTrust, ServiceNow or Archer have no native path in. Across 100,000+ questions processed through Skypher, 19.2% of security questionnaires arrive through portals.


### Should I replace Qvidian or run something alongside it?


If you chose Qvidian for audit trails and approval governance in a regulated industry, those remain genuine strengths and replacing them carries risk. Several teams keep Qvidian for formal proposals and regulatory workflow, and run a specialist for the security questionnaire and DDQ queue. Full replacement makes more sense when questionnaires are the majority of the volume and formal proposals are occasional.


### Can I migrate a Qvidian content library to another platform?


Most platforms accept structured exports. The better question is whether you should move all of it, since a library carrying stale or duplicated entries reproduces the same problem elsewhere. A common approach is identifying the fifth of the library that generates most of the reuse, retiring the rest, and migrating only verified content. Tools that read source documents directly rather than requiring a curated database avoid the migration question.


### How long does it take to get value from these tools?


G2 reports 4 months to implement Qvidian and 16 months to return on investment. Comparable published figures do not exist for most alternatives, so ask each vendor directly what the first questionnaire looks like versus the tenth. For Wolfia, UpGuard's comparison notes it can take "months of feeding corrections" before automation rates climb. Tools that read existing documents rather than requiring a curated library generally produce a usable first draft sooner, though output quality still depends on how well organised your source material is.
