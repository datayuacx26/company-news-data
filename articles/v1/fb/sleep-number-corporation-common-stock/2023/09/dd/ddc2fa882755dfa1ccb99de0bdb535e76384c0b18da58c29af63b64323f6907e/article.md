## SLEEP NUMBER AND ACS


The[powerhouse partnership](https://www.sleepnumber.com/post/landmark-research-to-improve-sleep-for-cancer-patients-survivors) between American Cancer Society and Sleep Number kicked off in February 2022 with the announcement of a landmark study on the connections between cancer and sleep.


Over the next several years, ACS may utilize Sleep Number’s 22 billion hours of longitudinal sleep and biometric data alongside data from ongoing cancer prevention studies. The goal is to determine the effects of sleep on cancer prevention and recovery, in hopes of developing the first-ever sleep guidelines for cancer.


Sleep Number also provides smart beds and other sleep innovations to A[merican Cancer Society Hope Lodge Communities](https://www.cancer.org/support-programs-and-services/patient-lodging/hope-lodge.html) to help patients and caregivers get the quality sleep they need while they undergo treatment away from home.


“When people face a cancer diagnosis, one of the most common barriers to receiving timely, high-quality care is actually access to lodging," said Dr. Arif Kamal, Chief Patient Officer, American Cancer Society.


For Sleep Number's support of ACS's mission via joint research, funding and support of its Hope Lodges, Sleep Number was named American Cancer Society's 2022 Corporate Partner of the Year and a[Game Changer](https://www.pr.com/press-release/895411) honoree.

## THE CONNECTION BETWEEN CANCER AND SLEEP


Sleep is vital when it comes to recovering from cancer, and for those still fighting. According to ACS, poor sleep quality may be associated with the risk of developing some types of cancer and sleep quality is a documented problem for cancer patients and longer term in cancer survivors.


The National Cancer Institute (part of the National Institutes of Health) estimates that as many as half of cancer patients have trouble sleeping, whether that's from stress, pain, cancer treatment or a number of other factors.
