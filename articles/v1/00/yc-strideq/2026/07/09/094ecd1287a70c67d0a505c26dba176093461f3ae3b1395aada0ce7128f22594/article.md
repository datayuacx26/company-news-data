## Best Practices for Designing Conversational Menus


Creating a menu that sounds natural when spoken is an art. Research shows that callers retain 70% more information when presented in short, conversational chunks rather than long, enumerated lists. Follow these guidelines:


1. **Group items logically:** Cluster similar items (e.g., “All‑day breakfast,” “Signature sandwiches”).
2. **Limit options per prompt:** Offer no more than three choices at a time to avoid cognitive overload.
3. **Use natural phrasing:** Instead of “Item number 4,” say “our popular chicken Caesar wrap.”
4. **Provide confirmation loops:** Repeat the order back to the caller for verification before finalizing.
5. **Include fallback options:** If the AI fails to understand, route the call to a human with a brief summary of the conversation.


Restaurants that adopted these practices saw a 22% reduction in repeat‑call rates, meaning fewer frustrated customers and lower operational costs.
