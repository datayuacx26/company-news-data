For decades, the call center has been the backbone of customer service. Largely dominated by countless cubicles of agents on phones answering inquiries, times have slowly changed. With AI redefining what’s possible, contact centers must move fast, think bigger and put the customer at the center of every transformation.


And while the phone still dominates,[our recent survey](https://www.five9.com/resources/e-book/business-leaders-cx-report) found that it remains the most preferred contact channel for customer service with consumers. The contact centers that CX leaders manage now have to do more than simply answer and make phone calls. Most importantly, contact centers must evolve to give businesses multiple opportunities to put their customers first — and strengthen their bottom line in the process.


### What is a customer contact center?


A contact center handles customer service inquiries across phone, email, chat and other channels, while proactively and reactively meeting customers where they are. Unlike a basic support queue, today’s contact center is built to solve problems, drive loyalty and deliver experiences that keep customers coming back.


The differences between a[contact center vs. a call center](https://www.five9.com/blog/call-center-vs-contact-center) are many, but the most important is that contact centers are erasing the notion of customer service as only a cost, since they provide opportunities to:


-


Improve brand reputation and promote customer loyalty


-


Get direct product or service feedback


-


Resolve issues in real time while building documentation and support articles that prevent them from recurring


-


Reduce appointment reschedules and no-shows


-


Upsell customers on additional products and services


### Contact center services


What sets a contact center apart from a call center comes down to one thing: the breadth of services it offers. The latter offers service primarily over the phone, while contact centers are typically multichannel or omnichannel. Phone, email and chat are nearly ubiquitous, but customers also expect to connect via social media, SMS, messaging apps and video. Meeting them there is no longer a differentiator; it’s the baseline.


### Contact center technologies


Behind every great customer experience is a customer contact center built on the right technology stack, including:


-


[IVRs](https://www.five9.com/faq/what-does-ivr-stand-for-in-call-centers) and AI voice agents: Automated agents that can help streamline intake, answer basic questions and help route customer inquiries to the right agent or department


-


[Call monitoring & recording](https://www.five9.com/faq/what-is-call-recording) : An incredibly important part of quality management, among others, is both live monitoring of calls and recording them for real-time and after-the-fact transcription and analysis.


-


[Call distribution & routing](https://www.five9.com/faq/what-is-call-routing) : Properly balancing agent workloads and expertise is imperative for good customer service, and call distribution and routing helps ensure customers are matched with the right agent, every time.


-


[AI](https://www.five9.com/faq/what-is-ai-in-customer-service) (chatbots, agent assist, analytics, etc.): AI has many places in contact centers, and while all the use cases are still being defined, it's becoming an increasingly important part of a CX leader's toolkit.


-


[Knowledge base](https://www.five9.com/faq/what-is-knowledge-management) : The champion of self-service, knowledge bases hold your customer contact center's wealth of information about common inquiries, for both your customers and your agents.


-


[Reporting & analytics](https://www.five9.com/faq/what-is-customer-experience-analytics) : Understanding a contact center's health and efficacy requires gathering and analyzing data (such as sentiment analysis from transcribed calls) to provide key information on trends that informs future decision-making.


-


System integrations: A contact center can't operate effectively in a vacuum and neither should the software it uses. No matter what contact center technologies are being used, they must integrate not only with one another (preferably in[a single platform](https://www.five9.com/products-solutions) ), but they must also sync with other key systems such as CRMs or CDPs.


### Designing the customer-first contact center


At its core, a customer contact center exists to deliver the right support at the right time. But becoming a truly customer-first contact center takes more than good intentions. It requires a fundamental shift in people, processes and technology.


The bottom line is: it's not about how many customer inquiries your agents handle in a day; it's about the actual outcome. Were issues resolved? Did customers keep or reschedule their appointments? Did their satisfaction with your company improve?


Ultimately, the best method for transforming your contact center into one that puts customers first is by building a seamless customer journey.


#### How to build a frictionless customer journey


1.


**Be where customers are.** A phone call still may still top the list, but it can’t be the only option. Consumer preferences on how to get support vary and meeting them on their preferred channel isn’t just good service; it’s good business. The right mix of channels ensures that every consumer can reach customer support the way they want, when they want.


1.


**Prioritize handoffs.** Especially with an omnichannel contact center, the handoff between agents (especially AI agents) is a critical point in the customer journey. No one likes repeating themselves, and besides the frustration it creates for customers, agents (whether they're human or AI) must have full history and context if they're going to provide good customer support.[Reducing system switching](https://www.five9.com/blog/hidden-cost-system-switching-how-frontline-friction-quietly-erodes-cx) can be especially helpful here, as information gaps and delays quickly erode customer patience and trust.


1.


**Ensure accountability.** While every CX leader would love a 100%[first-contact resolution (FCR) rate](https://www.five9.com/faq/what-is-first-call-resolution-fcr) , it’s unlikely to actually attain. But no matter your contact center's actual FCR, ensuring inquiries are followed up on within appropriate time frames is critical to putting customers first. Don't be afraid to set automated workflows in place to remind agents (and customers, if you're waiting for information) to keep your resolution rate high.


1.


**Learn from the past.** Mistakes are bound to happen. A typo in a support article or email, a misinterpretation from an agent, etc. But every mistake helps inform the future, from better processes to even improving your products or services. But it’s important to collect data and analyze past trends on both the mistakes and the successes ([especially with the help of AI](https://www.five9.com/blog/better-calls-happier-customers-how-genai-contact-centers-transforming-quality-management) ) to help you make data-driven decisions and improve the efficiency and efficacy of your customer contact center.


1.


**Augment agents with AI.** Your agents are the backbone of your contact center, and AI can help them in a number of ways, from reducing cognitive load to automating routine tasks such as[after-call work (ACW)](https://www.five9.com/faq/what-is-acw-in-call-centers) . But while AI can certainly be deployed to help improve[contact center productivity](https://www.five9.com/blog/how-can-ai-improve-contact-center-productivity) , among others, AI agent assistance is still in its infancy, and its total use cases are far from defined. CX leaders must continue to experiment and analyze where AI makes the most sense for their agents and contact center at the right stages in their customers' journeys.


### Start with the right foundation


Becoming a customer-first contact center starts with the right foundation. It’s built intentionally, with the right tools, processes and workflows that turn every interaction into an opportunity to deliver something better.


The foundation is there. Now it’s time to build on it.
