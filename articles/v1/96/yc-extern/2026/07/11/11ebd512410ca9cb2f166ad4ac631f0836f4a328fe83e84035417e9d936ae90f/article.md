## What Is a Modal Internship?


A Modal internship is a paid, on-site summer placement at a $4.65 billion serverless AI infrastructure company that lets developers deploy and scale AI workloads from zero to thousands of GPUs without managing servers, containers, or Kubernetes. Interns join a team of roughly 100 engineers, working alongside IOI medalists, the creator of Seaborn, and leaders from Spotify and Scale AI, to ship production code on Modal's custom-built runtime, scheduler, and filesystem. Past intern projects have included building container failure alert systems, implementing fault-tolerant async batch processors, and engineering zero host-overhead speculative decoding for GPU workloads. The program is deliberately small: seven to eight interns per cohort, each owning a real feature or system that reaches Modal's customers, which include biotech firms, hedge funds, and AI coding tool companies. Founded in 2021 by Erik Bernhardsson (ex-Spotify VP of Engineering, ex-Better.com CTO) and Akshat Bubna, Modal's culture is craft-focused and technically serious, with a pace that is fast but not frantic. At least one past intern continued working part-time during the school year, suggesting a conversion pathway that the company's hypergrowth makes increasingly attractive.

## What Are the Eligibility Requirements?


Modal's intern bar is set by the team you would join: roughly 100 engineers, including IOI medalists, the creator of Seaborn, and experienced leaders from Spotify and Scale AI. The explicit requirements are thin because the implicit bar is high:

## What Is the Modal Application and Interview Process Like?


Modal's intern interview process is a condensed version of its full-time loop, which techinterview.org rates at 8 out of 10 difficulty, comparable to Stripe and HashiCorp:


1. **Monitor[Modal's Ashby board](https://jobs.ashbyhq.com/modal) starting October 2026.** No intern listing was live as of July 2026. When the posting appears, submit immediately. With seven to eight seats and likely rolling admissions, speed matters more than polish.


2. **Recruiter screen (30 minutes).** Background, motivation, and team preference discussion. Modal's culture values customer empathy and comfort with ambiguity, so frame your experience around owned projects and shipping, not managed tasks.


3. **Technical interview (one to two rounds).** Coding problems in Python, Rust, or Go with a focus on systems fundamentals. Expect medium-hard difficulty covering algorithms and applied systems. Avoid generic Kubernetes answers: Modal deliberately does not use Kubernetes and built its own runtime, scheduler, and filesystem from scratch.


4. **Behavioral and culture fit.** Probes for owned projects, early-stage comfort, customer empathy, and technical communication. Modal's team is small and craft-focused, so demonstrating independent shipping ability matters more than team-management stories.


5. **Offer.** Decision typically within one to two weeks of the final interview. Modal's rapid growth creates strong retention incentive.


The single highest-leverage preparation move is deploying a real project on Modal's free tier before your first interview. Use Modal to run an inference endpoint, build a batch processing pipeline, or spin up a GPU-accelerated computation. Interviewers at a developer-tools company will know whether you have used the product, and product familiarity compresses the conversation from 'what does Modal do' to 'here is what I would improve.' Beyond that, study container internals (cgroups, namespaces, OCI runtimes), read Erik Bernhardsson's blog on Modal's architecture, and prepare two to three stories about systems you built and shipped independently.
