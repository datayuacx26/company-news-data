## What Do Sports Interns Actually Do?


**Sports interns** work inside professional teams, league offices, college athletic departments, talent agencies, and sports-adjacent brands. The roles cover operations, marketing, analytics, media production, ticket sales, and event management. It's fast-paced, season-driven, and way more varied than most students expect.


Something that catches people off guard: the sports industry is enormous, but the actual workforce is small. The NBA league office employs roughly 1,200 people. The NFL, around 1,100. That's it. So sports internships aren't resume filler. They're one of the few reliable ways into an industry where cold-applying to full-time jobs without prior experience almost never works.


What your day looks like depends entirely on where you land. An operations intern at an MLB team might coordinate logistics for a 12,000-person stadium event. A marketing intern at a sports agency might build pitch decks for a six-figure sponsorship deal. An analytics intern at an NBA team might pull shot-chart data and run regression models that end up in a pregame briefing. The range is massive. And that's the point. Sports organizations need people who can do real work across business functions, not just people who watch a lot of games.


## What Types of Sports Internships Are Out There?


**Sports internships fall into six main categories:** sports management and operations, sports marketing, sports analytics and data science, sports media and content, athletic training and performance, and esports and gaming. Each requires a different skill set and leads to a different corner of the industry. Before you apply everywhere, get specific about which lane fits you.


### Sports Management and Operations


The backbone of how sports organizations run: game-day logistics, facility management, scheduling, vendor coordination, ticket operations. Interns work at professional teams, college athletic departments, or event companies. You'll coordinate staff, track inventory, and handle last-minute problems on event days. Not glamorous, but it teaches you how the business actually works behind the highlights.


### Sports Marketing and Brand Partnerships


Social media management, sponsorship activation, fan engagement campaigns, and content strategy. You might run a team's Instagram during a game or help execute a sponsor's activation at a fan fest. Overlaps with general[marketing internships](https://www.extern.com/post/marketing-internships-summer-2027-guide) , but the sports context changes everything: content calendars revolve around game schedules, and campaigns spike during playoffs.


### Sports Analytics and Data Science


One of the fastest-growing areas. Teams and leagues use data for player evaluation, game strategy, fan modeling, and ticket pricing. Tools: Python, R, SQL, Tableau. You don't need to be a math major. Curiosity about data and the ability to tell a story with numbers is what matters. Our[data analytics internships guide](https://www.extern.com/post/data-analytics-internships-summer-2027-guide) goes deeper.


### Sports Media, Broadcasting, and Content


Editorial writing, video production, social content, podcast production, and broadcast operations. Employers range from ESPN and FOX Sports to team-owned content studios. Almost every NBA, NFL, and MLB team now runs its own media operation, which means more intern positions than ever. See our[media and entertainment guide](https://www.extern.com/post/media-entertainment-internships-guide) for the broader landscape.


### Athletic Training and Sports Science


For kinesiology, exercise science, and sports medicine students. You'd work alongside certified athletic trainers at college programs or pro team facilities. Fair warning: some positions require specific certifications, and many run during competitive season (early mornings, late nights, weekend travel).


### Esports and Gaming


Riot Games, Team Liquid, and FaZe Clan hire interns across event production, community management, and competitive operations. Riot alone fills 100 to 150 seats from 20,000+ applicants. To build portfolio evidence before applying, a remote[Externship](https://www.extern.com/externships) with a gaming company like Breaking Games can get you started.

## Which Companies and Organizations Run the Best Sports Internships for Summer 2027?


**The strongest sports internship programs for 2027** come from the NBA, NFL, MLB, NHL, MLS, NASCAR, NCAA, ESPN, Nike, and major sports agencies like CAA and WME. Most are paid, structured around cohort calendars, and recruit months ahead of the start date. If you're not watching career pages by September 2026, you're already behind on the top programs.


### Professional Leagues (NBA, NFL, MLB, NHL, MLS)


League office internships at the NBA, NFL, and other major leagues are among the most competitive and most structured programs in sports. Period.


**The NBA** runs a 10-week summer program from early June to mid-August. Pay sits between $30 and $35 per hour. Interns work full-time across departments including basketball strategy and analytics, broadcast operations, marketing, social responsibility, corporate partnerships, and legal. You need rising-senior or current-graduate-student status to be eligible. Applications typically open in fall 2026 on[careers.nba.com](https://careers.nba.com/early-career-programs/) . Perks include professional development sessions, executive fireside chats, and access to events like the NBA Draft and WNBA games.


**The NFL** Summer Internship Program is paid and open to undergraduate juniors, master's students, and law students. Applications historically go live in early December, with placements across three league offices in New York, New Jersey, and California. The NFLPA (the players' union) runs a separate program paying around $18 per hour with commuting costs covered.


**MLB, NHL, and MLS** all run summer programs, though they're smaller in scale. MLB clusters around offseason operations and spring training. The NHL posts positions at[nhl.com/careers](https://www.nhl.com/careers) . MLS has been expanding its intern pipeline as the league grows. And individual teams across all five leagues hire independently. Sometimes those team-level roles are actually less competitive than league office positions.


### NASCAR and Motorsports


NASCAR runs the **NASCAR Next Internship Program** , a paid 10-week summer program based primarily in Charlotte/Concord, North Carolina, and Daytona Beach, Florida. Interns work full-time across departments covering digital marketing, event operations, engineering, and broadcasting. You need at least sophomore standing, a 3.0 GPA, and U.S. work authorization. The 2026 window has already closed, so watch[careers.nascar.com](https://careers.nascar.com/nascar-internships/) for the 2027 posting in late fall 2026.


Motorsports is a smaller world than the major ball-and-stick leagues. That means network effects are stronger. People remember you. If you can't land a formal NASCAR internship right away, the[NASCAR NY Racing Sponsorship ROI Externship](https://www.extern.com/externships/nascar-nyracing-sponsorship-roi-externship) on Extern puts you on a real project with a NASCAR Cup Series team. You'll build a data-driven sponsorship scoring model using race data, social engagement metrics, and Python. It's remote, it produces a portfolio piece, and it's directly relevant when you apply to motorsports roles later.


### College Athletics and the NCAA


College athletic departments are the single largest source of sports internships by volume. D1, D2, and D3 schools all hire across compliance, communications, marketing, operations, event management, and fundraising. Quality and structure vary a lot. Some D1 programs run internships that rival league offices in rigor. Others are more informal.


The NCAA league office runs a postgraduate internship program that annualizes to roughly $33,000 with full benefits. It's a yearlong commitment (June through May), not summer-only, and it's one of the few entry-level positions in sports that comes with healthcare coverage. Check[ncaa.org](https://www.ncaa.org/what-we-do/programs/postgraduate-internships/) for details.


One thing to know: some college athletic department internships are unpaid and offer course credit only. That's not necessarily a dealbreaker, but it is worth knowing before you commit. Always confirm compensation before accepting.


### Sports Agencies and Talent Management


CAA Sports, WME Sports, Octagon, and Wasserman are the major names. Internships at these agencies typically fall into two tracks: the agent side (contract negotiation, client management, deal structuring) and the marketing/brand side (endorsement deals, sponsorship strategy, event partnerships).


These positions are extremely competitive, and they lean heavily on relationships. Got an alumni connection at one of these agencies? Use it. Don't have one? Focus on building relevant experience first. Agency recruiters care a lot about what you've actually done, especially anything involving client-facing work, business development, or event coordination.


### Sports Media and Broadcasting Companies


ESPN, FOX Sports, NBC Sports, CBS Sports, Bleacher Report, and The Athletic all run intern programs covering editorial, production, social media, and business operations. ESPN's program is widely regarded as one of the best in sports media. Past interns have worked on live broadcasts, SportsCenter segments, and ESPN+ original content.


For a fuller breakdown of media internship timelines and companies, see our[media and entertainment internships guide](https://www.extern.com/post/media-entertainment-internships-guide) .


### Sports-Adjacent Brands (Nike, Adidas, Under Armour, Gatorade)


You don't have to work for a team or league to work in sports. Consumer brands like Nike, Adidas, Under Armour, and Gatorade (a PepsiCo brand) hire interns across marketing, product development, supply chain, and brand strategy. Those roles carry a sports lens that's hard to find elsewhere.


Nike's internship? Among the most competitive in any industry. But the program is genuinely world-class. Adidas and Under Armour are more accessible entry points with similar exposure to sports product ecosystems. Gatorade's internship sits within PepsiCo's broader program, which means you get the structure and resources of a Fortune 50 company.

## How Much Do Sports Internships Pay?


**Most professional league and major brand sports internships are paid** , with hourly rates from $15 to $35+ depending on the organization and location. The industry has shifted toward paid programs over the past decade, though some corners (particularly college athletics) still rely on unpaid or credit-only models.


Here's how compensation breaks down across the major employers.


The NBA pays roughly $30 to $35 per hour, making it one of the highest-paying sports internship programs out there. NFL interns receive a monthly stipend (around $3,500/month in recent cycles) plus a $500 housing and transportation offset. NASCAR's Next Internship Program pays competitive hourly rates aligned with Charlotte and Daytona Beach cost of living. The NCAA postgraduate internship annualizes to about $33,000 with benefits. Not a summer-only gig, but notable for including healthcare.


On the brand side, Nike, Adidas, and PepsiCo (Gatorade) all pay interns in the $20 to $30+ per hour range. Consistent with major consumer brand programs.


Where it gets uneven is college athletics. Large D1 programs with big budgets (SEC, Big Ten) often pay hourly wages or stipends. But smaller programs (D2, D3, and even some D1 schools) may offer only course credit. If you're considering an unpaid position, weigh it honestly. An unpaid internship that gives you genuine access and mentorship can still be worth it. One that has you making copies and fetching coffee for 10 weeks is not.


## When Should You Apply for Summer 2027 Sports Internships?


**The timeline starts now.** Applications for summer 2027 sports internships opened in August 2026 and run through March 2027, depending on the organization. Professional leagues and major brands recruit earliest. Smaller orgs and college athletics post later.


### Fall 2026 (August through November): Early Movers


This is when the big programs open. The NBA, NFL league office, NASCAR, Nike, and major sports agencies like CAA and Octagon typically post summer internship listings between September and November. Riot Games (for esports roles) also opens around September, with software engineering positions closing by late October.


Set up job alerts now on the career pages of every organization you're interested in. Don't wait for someone to forward you the link.


### Winter 2026 to 2027 (December through February): Peak Window


The bulk of postings hit during this stretch. MLB, MLS, NHL, college athletic departments, ESPN, FOX Sports, and most mid-size sports organizations post between December and February. NFL applications specifically go live in early December.


This is also when interview processes run for early-posted positions. If you applied in September, expect to hear back (or not) by January or February.


### Spring 2027 (March through April): Late Openings


Don't panic if you missed the fall and winter windows. College athletics, minor league teams, local sports organizations, and some esports companies keep posting through March and into April. These positions tend to be less structured, but they can offer more responsibility and direct access to leadership.


This is also a good window for remote alternatives. An[Externship](https://www.extern.com/externships) doesn't follow the traditional recruiting calendar, so you can start one even after standard deadlines have passed.


## How to Get a Sports Internship With No Experience


**Getting a sports internship with no prior experience** means building visible proof of relevant skills before you apply. Sports hiring managers see hundreds of applications from students who "love sports." That's not a differentiator. What separates candidates is evidence that you've done something concrete.


### Build a Portfolio That Shows Transferable Skills


You don't need sports experience specifically. You need skills that translate.


Managed social media for any organization (a campus club, a local business, your sorority's philanthropy week)? That's content creation and audience engagement. Done a data analysis project in class or on your own, even analyzing fantasy football stats? That shows you can work with numbers. Coordinated an event of any size? Operations experience.


Put these into a simple portfolio. A Google Drive folder with 3 to 4 case studies works fine. The bar isn't perfection. It's proof that you've actually done things.


### Use Campus and Club Sports as Your Lab


This is the most underused path into sports careers. If your school has a club sports program, those teams need someone to run their social media, coordinate travel logistics, manage budgets, and handle game-day operations. Volunteer for that. Better yet, create a role that doesn't exist yet, like "digital content coordinator" for your club hockey team. That title on your resume tells sports employers exactly what you can do.


Intramural sports, campus recreation departments, and student-run sports publications all count. The work is real, the skills transfer directly, and nobody else is competing for these positions.

If you can't relocate for an in-person internship, or if you want to build experience before applying to the competitive programs, a remote[Externship](https://www.extern.com/externships) is a practical way to get portfolio-ready work on your resume.

You'll scrape race data, pull social engagement metrics from Reddit and YouTube, and build a sponsorship visibility scoring model in Python. That's the kind of specific, quantifiable project that makes a hiring manager at a sports organization stop scrolling past your resume.


On the gaming side, the[Breaking Games E-Commerce Data Analysis Externship](https://www.extern.com/externships/breaking-games-e-commerce-data-analysis-strategy) has you working with real marketing data from Google Analytics, Meta Ads, and Shopify. Concrete digital marketing and analytics skills that apply across the entire sports industry.


### Network the Way Sports Actually Works


The sports industry runs on relationships more than almost any other field. Cold applications work. Warm introductions work a lot better. Here's how to build connections as a student.


Join your school's sports business club. If one doesn't exist, start one. That alone is a resume line showing initiative. Reach out to alumni who work in sports via LinkedIn. Send a short, specific message (not a generic "I'd love to pick your brain"). Ask for a 15-minute informational conversation and actually prepare questions. Attend conferences like the MIT Sloan Sports Analytics Conference or NACDA conventions if they're accessible to you.


And here's something most guides won't mention: follow the people who do the hiring on social media. Sports executives are active on Twitter and LinkedIn. When they share something thoughtful, engage with it genuinely. That's how you get on someone's radar before you ever hit "submit" on an application.

## Sports Internships vs. Externships: What's the Difference?


**A sports internship is a 10-to-12-week, typically in-person placement at one company.** An[Externship](https://www.extern.com/externships) is a shorter, remote, project-based experience guided by a professional mentor from a real company. Both produce real skills and real portfolio work. They just serve different purposes.


Internships give you deep immersion in one organization's culture, operations, and network. You'll build relationships, learn how a sports business runs daily, and potentially convert to a full-time offer. The tradeoff: they require relocation (most sports internships are in-person), follow rigid recruiting timelines, and competition is intense.


Externships work differently. You tackle a defined project, like building a sponsorship ROI model for a NASCAR team or analyzing e-commerce conversion data for a gaming publisher, from anywhere. No relocation. No rigid application window. The output is a tangible portfolio piece you can point to in future interviews.


The smartest move? Treat them as complementary. Complete an Externship in fall or spring to build skills and portfolio evidence. Then apply to competitive summer internships with something concrete to show. Students who've done sports-adjacent Externships have told us it was the thing that made their applications stand out. Not the brand name itself, but the fact that they could walk into an interview and say "here's a project I finished, here are the results, and here's what I learned."

## FAQs


### Do you need a sports management degree to get a sports internship?


No. Sports organizations hire across business, communications, data science, marketing, engineering, and liberal arts backgrounds. Your major matters far less than your demonstrated skills and genuine interest in the industry. Some of the most successful sports professionals studied economics, computer science, or communications, not sports management. That said, a sports management program can provide solid networking and practicum opportunities if your school offers one.


### Are sports internships paid?


Most internships at professional leagues (NBA, NFL, MLB, NHL, MLS) and major brands (Nike, Adidas, ESPN) are paid, with hourly rates from $15 to $35+. College athletic department positions are more variable. Some offer stipends, others provide course credit only. Always confirm compensation before accepting an offer.


### What is the best sports internship program?


The NBA Summer Internship, NFL Summer Internship, and ESPN's program consistently rank among the most structured and well-compensated. But "best" depends on your goals. A smaller sports agency might give you direct client exposure that a league office can't. A college athletic department role might offer more responsibility earlier. Figure out what you want to learn before optimizing for brand names.


### When do sports internships for summer 2027 open?


Applications begin as early as August 2026 for professional leagues and major brands. The peak window runs from October 2026 through February 2027. College athletics and smaller organizations often post later, through March or April 2027. Set up job alerts on each organization's career page rather than relying only on aggregator sites.


### Can you get a sports internship as a freshman or sophomore?


Yes, though most structured league programs target juniors and seniors. College athletic departments, local sports organizations, campus recreation, and remote[Externships](https://www.extern.com/externships) are more accessible to underclassmen building early experience. Starting with a project-based Externship as a freshman or sophomore gives you a portfolio advantage by the time you're eligible for the marquee programs.


### What's the difference between a sports internship and a sports Externship?


An internship is a multi-week, usually in-person placement at one company where you're embedded in their daily operations. An Externship is a shorter, remote, project-based experience with professional mentorship. You work on a specific deliverable for a real company from wherever you are. Both count as legitimate professional experience. Externships are particularly useful if you can't relocate, want to explore the sports industry before committing to a full internship, or need portfolio evidence to strengthen your applications.
