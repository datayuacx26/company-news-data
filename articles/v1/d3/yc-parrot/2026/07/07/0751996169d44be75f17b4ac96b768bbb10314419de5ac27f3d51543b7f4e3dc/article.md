## What Makes English and Spanish Difficult in Different Ways


**English and Spanish** are hard in *genuinely* different ways. **English** puts most of its difficulty into **pronunciation** and **vocabulary breadth** . **Spanish** puts most of it into **grammar** and[verb morphology](https://www.parrotapp.com/grammar/how-to-conjugate-verbs-in-spanish) . *Neither language is uniformly harder* — they just make you **work in different places** .


"English and Spanish don't compete on the same difficulty — **English challenges you with pronunciation and vocabulary** , while **Spanish demands grammatical precision and verb mastery** ."


**English and Spanish present different learning challenges depending on the skill area:**


-


**Pronunciation** – English can be very difficult due to inconsistent spelling patterns, while Spanish is more consistent and predictable.


-


**Vocabulary breadth** – English has an extremely wide vocabulary, whereas Spanish vocabulary is generally more predictable with clearer patterns.


-


**Grammar complexity** – English grammar is relatively simpler, while Spanish requires more complex rules and structures.


-


**Verb morphology** – English uses fewer verb forms, while Spanish requires learning extensive conjugation patterns.


🎯 **Key Point:** The *real* question isn't which language is **harder overall** — it's which type of difficulty **matches your weaknesses** as a learner.


💡 **Tip:** If you struggle with **memorizing rules and conjugations** , English may feel *more natural* to pick up. If **irregular spelling and pronunciation** trip you up, Spanish's *phonetic consistency* gives you a real advantage.


### Where English actually trips people up


According to[Spanish Mama's comparison of Spanish and English](https://spanishmama.com/spanish-vs-english/) , English has roughly 20 vowel sounds, while Spanish has only 5. This difference means English learners must train their ears and mouths to distinguish sounds that many other languages never require. Spanish learners face the opposite challenge: fewer sounds to produce, but each must be exact and consistent, since vowels do not shift based on stress as they do in English.


#### How does vocabulary complexity differ between the two languages?


[Spanish Mama also reports](https://spanishmama.com/spanish-vs-english/) that English contains approximately 171,000 words in active use compared to Spanish's roughly 93,000. English uses phrasal verbs like "put off," "give in," and "run out of," where meaning cannot be deduced from individual words alone. Spanish builds complexity through verb conjugations that encode who is speaking, when, and how certain they are within a single word form.


#### Why does drilling conjugation tables fail in real conversations?


Most learners practice[Spanish verb conjugation](https://www.parrotapp.com/grammar) through repetition and flashcards, an approach that boosts test scores rather than conversational skills. When native speakers respond at natural speed, conjugation tables offer little help. The real problem is processing language fast enough to participate.


[Parrot addresses this](https://www.parrotapp.com/) problem through short-form video content pitched at the right level of difficulty, so learners hear Spanish as it is spoken. Rather than drilling grammar in isolation, our platform helps learners encounter conjugations in real sentences and contexts, enabling the brain to map form to meaning over time.


### Which difficulty actually slows you down?


[Spanish grammar complexity](https://www.parrotapp.com/grammar/how-to-learn-spanish-grammar) rarely stops learners from reaching[conversational fluency](https://www.parrotapp.com/use-cases/conversational-spanish) . What stops them is insufficient exposure to real spoken Spanish at an understandable level. English pronunciation irregularities rarely stop English learners either. What stops them is insufficient exposure to native speakers who use natural rhythm and connected speech. Structural difficulty is less predictive of progress than the quality and quantity of comprehensible input learners receive.

## What Actually Makes Learning Spanish Easier


**What makes Spanish easier** has more to do with *where you're starting* than the language itself. **English speakers** have a big structural advantage: Spanish shares over *3,000 cognates* with English, meaning words like "hospital," "animal," and "natural" transfer *almost instantly* .[According to Worlds Across Blog](https://blog.worldsacross.com/index/whats-the-most-effective-way-to-learn-spanish-in-2025) , this is a **head start** most learners *never fully use* .


"Spanish shares over **3,000 cognates** with English — meaning thousands of words transfer *almost instantly* for native English speakers." — Worlds Across Blog


🎯 **Key Point:** Your **native language** is one of your biggest assets when learning Spanish. **Cognate recognition** alone can fast-track your **vocabulary acquisition** by thousands of words before you even open a textbook.


💡 **Tip:** Don't overlook your **built-in advantage** . Actively scanning for **cognates** during reading and listening practice is one of the *most underused* strategies for **accelerating fluency** .


**Spanish cognates help English speakers accelerate vocabulary acquisition through familiar word patterns:**


-


**Hospital → Hospital** – Has an identical meaning in both languages, allowing instant recognition.


-


**Animal → Animal** – Transfers directly with no learning barrier.


-


**Natural → Natural** – Shares the same spelling and meaning, making it immediately recognisable.


-


**Possible → Posible** – Requires only a minor spelling adjustment for near-instant understanding.


-


**Important → Importante** – Follows a predictable pattern, enabling near-instant vocabulary transfer.


### Why do so many learners plateau before reaching real fluency?


The critical difference between learners who stop improving and those who continue is how they handle the gap between structured study and real-world use. Many learners spend months in apps or textbooks building grammar knowledge, only to freeze when a native speaker speaks at full speed. Real fluency is not about knowing more words; it is about training your brain to handle the rhythm, compression, and spontaneity of actual speech.


### Does adding more structured lessons actually build conversational fluency?


[FSI data cited by My Daily Spanish](https://mydailyspanish.com/if-i-wanted-to-learn-spanish-in-2025-id-do-this/) estimates that it takes 600 to 750 hours to reach conversational proficiency. ### What actually closes the gap


The learners who improve fastest treat listening as a skill to practice rather than passively consume. Adjusting playback speed on[Spanish video content](https://www.parrotapp.com/blog) calibrates your learning. Starting at 80% speed and slowly increasing it over weeks trains your ability to understand sounds, much like interval training builds cardiovascular strength. #### Why does speaking before you feel ready accelerate fluency?


Speaking practice works the same way. Learners often wait to speak until they feel ready, which usually means they never do. Readiness happens when you speak, not before you start. Talking to yourself, repeating what you hear, and[low-stakes conversation practice](https://www.parrotapp.com/grammar/how-to-say-hello-in-spanish) help you speak faster than silent study alone. Every mistake in real conversation teaches you something.
