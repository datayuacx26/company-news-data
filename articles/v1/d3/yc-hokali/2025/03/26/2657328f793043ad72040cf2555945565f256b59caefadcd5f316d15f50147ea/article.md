# Key Challenges in School Districts: Strategies for Sustainable After-School Programs and Enrichment Opportunities

## Addressing Funding, Staffing, and Program Inequities in K-12 Education.


School districts across the country face persistent challenges in managing after-school programs, from securing funding to maintaining staffing and ensuring equitable access for all students. These challenges directly impact the quality and availability of enrichment programs, affecting student engagement and long-term educational outcomes.


The insights presented in this white paper were gathered during the Winter R&D Institute organized by The Education Research & Development Institute (ERDI). During the event, discussions with superintendents and education leaders highlighted key obstacles school districts encounter and explored potential solutions. HOKALI presents an opportunity to address these issues with a flexible, scalable platform designed to streamline program management and enhance accessibility.


## Key Statistics


-


**Funding challenges:** The competition for federal funding sources, such as 21st Century Community Learning Centers (CCLC) grants and Title I and IV funding, has intensified, making it difficult for many schools to secure long-term financial support.


-


**Staffing shortages:** Many after-school programs[struggle to recruit and retain qualified instructors](https://www.hokali.com/hokali-blog/addressing-the-shortage-of-teachers) due to low wages, inconsistent work hours, and limited professional development opportunities.


-


**Program disparities:** Schools with strong parent-teacher associations (PTAs) and private funding sources often provide a variety of enrichment activities, while lower-income schools may lack even basic after-care options.


-


**Decision-making hurdles:** Bureaucratic delays often slow down the approval of new service providers, leading to interruptions in program continuity when districts reorganize due to budget cuts or declining enrollment.


-


**Data-driven accountability** : Districts are increasingly requiring program providers to present measurable student engagement and learning outcomes to justify funding and long-term partnerships.


## Exploring the causes and consequences


### Causes


-


**Budget Constraints and Funding Complexity:** Schools rely on a mix of federal, state, and local funding sources, each with its own requirements and application processes, making it challenging to secure sustainable financial support for after-school programs.


-


**Instructor Recruitment and Retention:** Many after-school staff members work part-time, which results in high turnover rates. The demand for background checks, training, and credentialing adds additional barriers to hiring qualified instructors. The challenge is even greater in rural areas.


-


**School Consolidation and Reorganization:** Districts experiencing enrollment declines or budget cuts often consolidate schools, disrupting established after-school programs and forcing administrators to reevaluate service providers.


-


**Lack of Standardized Program Management:** Without a centralized system to track and maintain program quality, after-school offerings vary widely between schools, leading to inequitable opportunities for students.


-


**Ineffective Communication and Bureaucracy:** Resistance from parents and teachers, coupled with slow administrative processes, creates delays in decision-making and program implementation.


### Consequences


-


**Disruptions in Student Learning and Engagement:** When programs are inconsistent or poorly managed, students miss out on valuable enrichment opportunities that support academic and social development.


-


**Inequitable Access to Programs:** The gap between schools with strong financial backing and those without exacerbates educational disparities.


-


**Difficulty in Long-Term Planning:** The lack of stable funding and administrative support makes it challenging for districts to implement strategic, sustainable enrichment programs.


-


**Increased Operational Burdens:** Without a streamlined system, districts must invest additional time and resources in program oversight, reducing efficiency.
