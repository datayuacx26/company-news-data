# Understanding the afterschool crisis and the path to a more equitable future

## **Overview**


Across the U.S., families are eager for high-quality afterschool programs that offer safety, learning, and social connection during the critical hours when parents are still at work. These programs are proven to help kids thrive, but despite strong demand, millions of children are missing out.


Recent studies show that 22.6 million kids whose parents want them in afterschool programs aren’t able to participate due to cost, availability, and access issues. This missed opportunity impacts not only children but also working parents, local schools, and communities.


At HOKALI, we believe all kids deserve the chance to grow and build important skills after school. We’re committed to closing this gap by providing accessible, high-quality programs that nurture confidence, creativity, and lifelong learning.


## **Key Data on Afterschool Demand and Missed Opportunities**


### 1. **High Demand for Afterschool Programs**


Nearly **30 million** parents want after-school programs for their children, but **3 out of** 4 children whose parents desire these programs **don’t have access.**


### 2. Parents See Clear Benefits


**95% of parents** are satisfied with after-school programs, citing benefits like safety, engagement, and connection for their children.


### **3. After-school Helps Parents Keep Jobs**


**4 in 5** parents say after-school programs help them stay employed by reducing absenteeism and job turnover.


### **4. Afterschool Improves Academic and Social Outcomes**


Participation in after-school programs leads to **higher school attendance, better homework completion, stronger social-emotional skills** , and l **ower rates of risky behaviors.**


### **5. After-school Supports Economic Stability**


Working parents lose an estimated **$122 billion** annually in productivity due to childcare issues, and nearly **50%** of parents say childcare affects their ability to work full-time.


## What Gets in the Way: Barriers Limiting Afterschool Participation


### **Affordability**


Cost is the main barrier to after-school programs. Families in higher income brackets spend up to **9 times** more on out-of-school activities. Since 2014, average weekly costs have increased by 89%, making even affordable programs unaffordable for many families.


### **Accessibility & Availability**


Demand for afterschool programs far exceeds supply. Over **70% of parents** say programs are too full, too far, or unavailable in their area. Rural areas are especially underserved, and urban/suburban families often face long waitlists.


### **Transportation & Schedule Misalignment**


**Lack of transportation** and misaligned schedules are major barriers. Many families cannot enroll because programs end before parents finish work or lack transport options to get their kids there.


### **Staffing Shortages**


Nationwide staffing shortages are forcing many programs to cut enrollment or close. **Low wages and high turnover** make it difficult to maintain consistent, high-quality care.


### **Equity Gaps**


Low- and middle-income families struggle the most. Children from wealthier families are more likely to have access to after-school programs, widening the achievement gap and limiting opportunities for disadvantaged youth.
