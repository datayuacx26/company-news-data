With the U.S. EV-charging infrastructure market already valued at over $5B and forecast to grow by more than[30% per year through 2030](https://www.marketsandmarkets.com/Market-Reports/electric-vehicle-supply-equipment-market-89574213.html) , adding charging stations isn’t just a tenant amenity; it’s a strategic investment.


Whether you manage a[workplace](https://www.ampup.io/use-cases/workplace-ev-charging-solutions) campus,[multifamily](https://www.ampup.io/use-cases/ev-charging-management-for-residential) building,[retail](https://www.ampup.io/use-cases/ev-charging-management-for-hotels) center, or mixed-use portfolio, adding commercial EV charging stations helps you meet the expectations of today’s drivers and the infrastructure goals of tomorrow. ## Understanding Your Commercial Property’s EV Charging Needs


The most successful charging projects start with a clear sense of who will use the stations and how often.


### Assessing Current and Future EV Demand


As with any business, it’s important to know your customers and their routines. Workplace properties, retail centers, fleets, and multifamily buildings all have different charging patterns, but across the board, adoption is rising fast.


As you think about your charge needs, here’s what you need to take into consideration:


- EV ownership among tenants, employees, and visitors.
- Local EV adoption forecasts (DOE’s AFDC[maintains updated data](https://afdc.energy.gov/data) ).
- A quick survey to understand expectations around availability, cost, and access.


### Matching Charge Speed to Dwell Time


Charging speed matters only in terms of how long people stay. Long stays favor Level 2 because it’s cost-effective and covers the whole workday. DC fast charging is better suited to quick-service: rest stops, convenience stores, all the places drivers want to charge and go.


- 8+ hours:[Workplaces](https://www.ampup.io/use-cases/workplace-ev-charging-solutions) ,[multifamily](https://www.ampup.io/use-cases/ev-charging-management-for-residential) → Level 2
- 2–4 hours: Retail centers, restaurants → Level 2, possibly paired with DC fast
- Under 2 hours: Convenience stores, quick-service → DC fast charging


### Determining the Right Number of Ports


Industry benchmarks suggest one charger per 10 to 15 parking spaces for[workplaces](https://www.ampup.io/use-cases/workplace-ev-charging-solutions) . Retail and[multifamily](https://www.ampup.io/use-cases/ev-charging-management-for-residential) properties don’t follow a single ratio (usage patterns dictate the mix; contact us to discuss your unique needs), so the right number depends on dwell time and expected demand. If capacity is limited, consider a phased rollout while still prepping the site for future growth.


## Level 2 vs. DC Fast Charging: Choosing the Right Technology


Not all properties are the same; likewise, the charging setup. Most commercial projects start with Level 2 charging because it covers the majority of daily use cases, keeps costs predictable, and fits neatly into existing electrical infrastructure. But depending on your dwell times, traffic patterns, and business model, adding DC fast charging can make a massive difference to your drivers and your bottom line, especially for locations with quick turnover or high visibility.


Level 2 and DC fast charging each solves different problems, so the goal isn’t to pick a “winner.” It’s matching the technology to how people actually use your property.

## EV Charger Installation FAQs


### What type of commercial EV charger do I need?


Most properties start with[Level 2 chargers](https://www.ampup.io/resources/approved-ev-charger-manufacturers) because they match all-day or multi-hour dwell times and cost less to install. DC fast charging is better suited for short-stop retail locations, highway stops, and public access sites where drivers expect quick turnaround. Your dwell time and electrical capacity determine the right mix.


### How many EV charging stations should a commercial property install?


A standard benchmark for workplaces is one charger per 10–15 parking spaces. Multifamily and retail properties vary more widely in terms of usage patterns. Most teams start with a phased rollout but design the site for 50–100% expansion to stay ahead of rising EV adoption.


### How much does a commercial EV charging station cost?


Typical total installed cost is $3,000–$7,000 per Level 2 port and $40,000–$150,000+ for DC fast charging, depending on electrical capacity, trenching, panel upgrades, and permitting. Hardware is only part of the cost — installation drives most of the variability.


### How long does installation take?


Most commercial projects follow a 10–12 week timeline, including site assessment, engineering, permitting, equipment procurement, installation, and final inspection. Complex sites may take longer, especially if electrical upgrades are required.


### What is OCPP, and why does it matter?


OCPP ([Open Charge Point Protocol](https://openchargealliance.org/protocols/open-charge-point-protocol/) ) ensures your chargers work with multiple software platforms and manufacturers. Choosing OCPP-compliant hardware prevents vendor lock-in and lets you scale or switch equipment in the future without replacing your entire system.


### Do I need networked chargers for my property?


Yes — for commercial use cases, networked chargers provide monitoring, payments, reporting, access control, and uptime management. Non-networked chargers lack the tools required for accountability, reliability, and revenue collection in workplaces, multifamily, and retail sites.


### What incentives are available to reduce the cost?


State and utility programs often provide rebates, make-ready construction support, demand-response payments, and LCFS credit revenue in eligible regions. These can significantly reduce installation costs and improve long-term ROI.


### How do commercial properties make money from EV charging?


Properties generate revenue through pay-per-kWh, time-based pricing, public charging fees, and differentiated pricing for tenants, employees, or visitors. When utilization is steady, most properties achieve a 3–5-year payback, with incentives, faster.


### How do I maintain high charger uptime?


High reliability comes from real-time monitoring, proactive alerts, quick remote resets, firmware updates, and strong support coverage. Aim for[98.5%+ charging success](https://www.ampup.io/blog/ev-range-anxiety-solutions) through a combination of quality hardware and a software platform that actively monitors performance.


### What ESG benefits does EV charging offer?


Commercial charging contributes to[Scope 2](https://ghgprotocol.org/scope-2-guidance) (electricity use) and[Scope 3](https://ghgprotocol.org/scope-3-calculation-guidance-2) (employee commuting, tenant behavior) reporting. Adding chargers also supports sustainability certifications and improves your property’s position in ESG-driven leasing decisions.
