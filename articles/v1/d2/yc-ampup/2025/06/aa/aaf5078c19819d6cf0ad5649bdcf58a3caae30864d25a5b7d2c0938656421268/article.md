# Managing the Load: Commercial vs. Residential EV Charging

As EV adoption steadily climbs, utilities are preparing for the load. But not all EV charging demand is created equal. While residential charging tends to follow predictable patterns and can often be managed with simple time-of-use rates and smart thermostats, commercial properties present an entirely different challenge for grid management. Let’s break it down.


## Residential vs. Commercial: A Tale of Two Grid Load Profiles


Residential Properties Commercial Properties


**Scale** Lower, individual household loads Larger, more complex energy demands


**Charging Behavior** Mostly evenings at home Varies widely; often high density and simultaneous


**Common Strategies** Time-of-Use (TOU) rates, managed charging, smart thermostats Real-time pricing (RTP), demand response (DR), load shedding


**Technologies** Home Energy Management Systems (HEMS), residential batteries, solar Building Management Systems (BMS), large-scale battery storage, advanced metering infrastructure (AMI)


**Operational Constraints** Minimal; changes impact only individual households High; changes must avoid disrupting tenants or operations


**Optimization Goals** Reduce peak load and improve energy efficiency Minimize energy costs, avoid demand charges, enable grid services


‍


Residential strategies are often simple and consumer-friendly. For example, managed EV charging programs allow utilities to control the timing and rate of EV charging to optimize grid performance or lower costs. Home battery storage, which is being adopted in a growing number of US households, adds flexibility with programs such as virtual power plants. When these tools are integrated through a home energy management system (HEMS), homeowners can easily monitor and adjust their usage to support the grid and reduce their electricity costs.


Commercial properties, on the other hand, can require deeper integration with more advanced systems that can monitor, predict, and respond to dynamic load conditions in real time. These strategies focus on minimizing demand charges, maximizing energy efficiency, and leveraging on-site generation and storage to reduce grid reliance.


Layering EV chargers on top of these existing commercial systems adds a whole new dimension of complexity. Unlike residential properties, where one or two chargers typically suffice, commercial and multifamily buildings can require dozens or even hundreds. These chargers introduce a significant, often simultaneous load that can quickly overwhelm local transformers, exceed demand thresholds, and trigger expensive infrastructure upgrades. ## AmpUp’s Adapt Program: Real-Time Relief for the Grid


AmpUp developed its[Adapt & Earn program](https://www.ampup.io/products/managed-ev-charging) in Connecticut to address commercial EV charging stress with smart software. Here’s how it works:


- Program Participation: The Adapt program uniquely enrolls publicly shared and commercial EV chargers by incentivizing site hosts to keep their ports active from day one, while empowering drivers with complete control to opt out of participation at any time.
- Real-Time Load Management: AmpUp collaborates closely with grid administrators to pinpoint instances where reducing demand is most crucial due to high demand.
- Driver Incentives: Through the "Adapt & Earn" feature, EV drivers get point of sale rebates in the form of wallet credits—and drivers get reimbursed as soon as the charging session ends—to avoid charging during peak hours by letting AmpUp pause charging. Drivers can resume charging at any point and are always in control. But drivers stay in control—they can always opt out.
- Utility Dashboard: A live dashboard shows utilities how much energy is being shifted, who’s participating, and how much incentive money is being spent.


"The live dashboard includes participating drivers, incentives disbursed to drivers and site hosts, and total peak energy shifted," says Ayush Garg, lead program manager, utility managed charging at AmpUp.


Since launching in April, AmpUp Adapt has shifted about 2 MWh of EV charging away from peak times across more than 400 charging sessions. That’s real load flexibility, delivered in real time.
