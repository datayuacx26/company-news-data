## Before you start: shirt fabric, dye timing, and workspace setup


For the brightest tie-dye colors, choose 100% cotton shirts whenever possible. Polyester blends can create softer or faded results because synthetic fibers absorb less dye.


### Should you tie-dye before or after adding Iron-On?


Always tie-dye first, then add Cricut iron-on after the shirt has been rinsed, washed, and fully dried.


Dyeing after applying iron-on can stain the design, cause lifting or peeling, prevent proper adhesion, and warp specialty finishes like glitter or metallic Iron-On.


### Set up a mess-friendly workspace


Tie-dye can stain surfaces quickly, so cover your table with a plastic tablecloth or large plastic trash bag before starting. Keep paper towels nearby and wear gloves throughout the dyeing process. You can also place a wire cookie cooling rack over a plastic shoebox or tray to keep your work surface clean and prevent muddy colors on your finished tee.


### Pre-wash your shirt


Right before dyeing, pre-wash your 100% cotton tees in hot water with detergent only (no fabric softener). Pre-washing will prevent shrinking later, and it removes any dirt or debris from the shirt, allowing the dyes to stick better.


**Note: Do not put your shirts in the dryer after washing them!** The dye applies best when the shirts are a little damp, so it’s easiest to begin tying up your shirts straight out of the washing machine.


## 4th of July tie-dye patterns to try


### Spiral tie-dye pattern


The spiral is one of the most popular patterns to try! Here’s how to create this classic look:


1. Pinch the center of the shirt.


2. Twist your shirt into a spiral.


3. Secure your twisted shirt with rubber bands.


4. Apply red and blue dye in alternating sections. Leave some white space in between sections for contrast.


5. Flip the shirt over and repeat the same dye color on the backside. For a spider spiral, dye the front side red and the back side blue in each pie section.


6. Wrap your dyed shirt in a plastic garbage bag or a large zip-top bag. Store in a warm place for 24 hours.


7. Unwrap your shirt from the plastic and cut off the rubber bands to reveal your tie-dye design.


### Bullseye tie-dye pattern


The bullseye pattern creates bold circular rings on your shirt. To create this look, follow these steps:


1. Pinch the fabric where you want the center.


2. Pull upward into a cone shape.


3. Add rubber bands evenly (about every 2-4 inches) down the fabric.


4. Apply alternating red and blue dye in the tied-off sections.


5. Flip the shirt over and repeat the same dye color on the backside.


### Patriotic stripes, ombre, and scrunch tie-dye patterns


Following the same general instructions from the above patterns, you can change how you fold or tie off your shirt in the following ways for more fun tie-dye patterns:


#### **Patriotic stripes**


Accordion-fold the shirt vertically or horizontally to create flag-inspired stripes.


#### **Ombre tie-dye**


Blend red into white or blue into white for a softer patriotic look.


#### **Scrunch tie-dye pattern**


Scrunch the shirt randomly and secure lightly with rubber bands for an abstract firework-inspired design.

## How to apply Iron-On to tie-dye shirts


### Step 1: Wash and dry the shirt


After tie-dyeing:


1. Rinse until water runs mostly clear
2. Wash separately
3. Dry completely
4. Smooth wrinkles before pressing


### Step 2: Create your design in Design Space


Upload or select a patriotic design, resize it for the shirt, and mirror the image before cutting.


Some great and easy design options from Design Space include the[Patriotic Peace Fingers](https://design.cricut.com/#/landing/project-detail/60d5ef1516680e1314e777ea?utm_campaign=Campaign&utm_medium=blogpost&utm_source=cricut_blog&utm_content=blog_4th_of_july_tie_dye&utm_term=ds_patriotic_peace_fingers) ,[Stars and Stripes USA](https://design.cricut.com/#/landing/project-detail/60d89a0595546405a025267d?utm_campaign=Campaign&utm_medium=blogpost&utm_source=cricut_blog&utm_content=blog_4th_of_july_tie_dye&utm_term=ds_stars_and_stripes_forever) , and the[Stay Cool America Patriotic Aviator Glasses](https://design.cricut.com/#/landing/project-detail/60d8998440ff0112947ebba8?utm_campaign=Campaign&utm_medium=blogpost&utm_source=cricut_blog&utm_content=blog_4th_of_july_tie_dye&utm_term=ds_patriotic_aviators) .


Once you’re ready to cut your design onto your Iron-On material, click “Make It” in the top right corner of the Design Space Canvas and follow the on-screen instructions.


### Step 3: Cut and weed the Iron-On


Place the Iron-On material shiny side down on the mat and cut the design. Weed away excess material.


### Step 4: Position the design


Center the design on the shirt using a ruler or folded alignment guide.


### Step 5: Press the design using a Cricut Heat Press


Lay your tie-dyed shirt flat on your Cricut Heat Press Mat. Use the[Cricut Heat Guide](https://cricut.com/en-us/heatguide) to select the correct time and temperature for your shirt fabric and Iron-On material.


Place your weeded design, iron-on face down, onto the front of the shirt, liner side up. Apply firm pressure and follow the recommended peel instructions.


### Step 6: Peel off the carrier sheet and re-press if necessary


Once the Iron-On material is cool to the touch, gently peel away the carrier sheet. If the edges lift slightly after you begin peeling the carrier sheet, place the carrier sheet back down (or cover the design with butcher paper) and press it again for 5-10 seconds.

## How to wash and care for tie-dye iron-on shirts


To keep both the tie-dye and iron-on looking bright, wait 24 hours after pressing your Iron-On design, then wash the shirts inside out using cold water and avoiding bleach. Tumble dry your shirts on low or hang dry them.


Tie-dye shirts may release excess dye during early washes, so wash them separately from your other clothes for their first few washes.


## Tips on layering Iron-On


If you’re layering several colors of Iron-On for your shirt design, start with the outline design. Doing so will allow you to keep the rest of the design centered and aligned correctly. Once the outline has been pressed, work in sections to press each remaining Iron-On design element. Before pressing each time, be sure to cover the sections of Already pressed Iron-On with a protective layer of butcher paper to prevent direct heat and wrinkling.


If your colors are side-by-side and not overlapping, you can alternatively press multiple colors at once by trimming the excess carrier sheet around the designs, then placing each design where it belongs on your shirt. Make sure that the Iron-On and carrier sheets from neighboring colors do not overlap.

## Troubleshooting


### Why are my tie-dye colors muddy?


Too many overlapping colors or oversaturated fabric can create muddy results. Leave white space between colors whenever possible.


### Why does my tie-dye look faded?


Polyester blends absorb less dye than cotton. For brighter colors, use 100% cotton shirts.


### Why is my carrier sheet difficult to remove?


Some materials require warm peeling while others require cool peeling. Follow the instructions for your specific Iron-On type.
