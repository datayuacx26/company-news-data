Custom screen-printed t-shirts are having a major moment after[Taylor Swift was spotted wearing one made by Alana Haim](https://www.vogue.com/article/taylor-swift-stevie-knicks-t-shirt) . And the good news is, screen printing with Cricut simplifies what can be a complex process traditionally, making it way more accessible for beginners. You can create your own custom screen-printing designs at home with just your Cricut machine, permanent vinyl, and a few basic screen-printing supplies readily available online or at a crafting store.


Whether you’re making personalized shirts, tote bags, home decor, or gifts, this beginner-friendly tutorial will walk you through every step of the process—from designing your stencil in Cricut Design Space™ to pulling your first print.


Before we dive in, you can find more help and a visual tutorial in our YouTube video. Let’s get started!


## What you’ll need


Before you begin, you’ll need to gather the following supplies:


### Cricut tools and materials


- [A Cricut cutting machine](https://cricut.com/en-us/cutting-machines)
- [Cricut Permanent Vinyl](https://cricut.com/en-us/materials/material-type/vinyl)
- [Cricut StandardGrip Machine Mat](https://cricut.com/en-us/tools-accessories/machine-tools/machine-mats)
- [Cricut Transfer Tape](https://cricut.com/en-us/materials/supplies/transfer-tape)
- [Weeder](https://cricut.com/en-us/tools-accessories/handheld-tools/crafting-tools/weeder/2006698.html)
- [Scraper](https://cricut.com/en-us/tools-accessories/handheld-tools/cricut-scraper-set-2-ct/2012900.html)
- [Spatula](https://cricut.com/en-us/tools-accessories/handheld-tools/wide-spatula/2012892.html)
- [Cricut EasyPress™](https://cricut.com/en-us/heat-presses/cricut-easypress) and[Heat Mat](https://cricut.com/en-us/tools-accessories/accessories/easypress-mats)


### Screen printing tools and materials


- Screen printing frame
- Screen printing ink
- Screen printing squeegee
- Cotton shirt (or another cotton fabric blank)
- Painter’s tape
- Cardstock sheet (to place inside the shirt)
- [Butcher paper](https://cricut.com/en-us/materials/material-type/infusible-ink/transfer-sheets/cricut-butcher-paper/2011640.html) or parchment paper


## Step 1: Design your screen-printing stencil in Cricut Design Space


If this is your first time trying screen printing, I’d suggest a simple, bold, single-color design.


To design in Cricut Design Space, follow these simple steps:


### Select an Iron-On project guide


From the Design Space home page, select the project that matches what you’d like to make. I suggest selecting an Iron-On project guide because doing so will automatically turn on the Mirror function for your design once you hit the cutting stage, meaning one less thing for you to worry about!


### Choose your project type


Select your project type (such as Women’s T-shirt) so you can preview your design on the blank. Make sure the design area size on the right side is accurate for how big or small you want your design to be, then select “Start Designing.”


### Design your stencil


Search the Cricut Image Library for a simple design you like or upload your own artwork. Edit your design on the Design Space Canvas until it matches your vision.


Make sure the operation is set to Cut and that the entire design is the same color in the Mat Details area before clicking “Make.”


### Beginner design tips


For your first screen printing project, here are a few tips to keep in mind:


- Choose bold shapes and image. Avoid intricate details or thin text.
- Keep your design one color.


Simple designs are best for two reasons: they’re easier to weed for those new to Cricut, and they produce cleaner prints for those new to screen printing.


## Step 2: Cut your vinyl stencil


Before cutting, make sure the Mirror option on the Design Space cut screen is toggled on.


Then center your design on the cutting mat to leave plenty of room around the stencil and follow on-screen directions to cut your design.


## Step 3: Weed your vinyl stencil


Unlike when making a normal vinyl decal, for a screen-printing stencil, you’ll weed your vinyl inversely. This means weeding out the design itself and leaving the negative space intact. The weeded areas are where the ink will pass through your screen and be applied to the fabric underneath.


Take extra care around any small details, thin lines, or the centers of letters.


Slow, careful weeding helps create crisp prints later.


## Step 4: Apply the stencil to the screen-printing frame


This is often the trickiest part, so don’t rush!


### Apply transfer tape


Cover the entire stencil with Transfer Tape. Burnish the transfer tape to the vinyl thoroughly using your Cricut Scraper.


Once the transfer tape is burnished onto the vinyl, slowly peel the vinyl and transfer tape off of the vinyl backing. If any vinyl stays behind, simply burnish again and continue peeling slowly.


### Transfer the design to the screen


Turn your screen-printing frame over and apply the stencil to the back of the screen. Burnish the vinyl to the screen from both the front and back to create a tight seal.


Once the stencil is burnished to the screen well, slowly peel away the transfer tape at a sharp angle. If pieces lift, simply reburnish before continuing. You can also gently use your weeder tool or the scraper tool to hold the pieces down on the screen as you pull away the transfer tape.


### Seal the screen


Use painter’s tape to cover any exposed mesh around your design. This prevents ink from leaking into unwanted areas during printing.


## Step 4: Screen print your design


Now it’s time for the fun part!


Before you start screen printing your design, be sure to do the following:


- Make sure your shirt is clean. I also like to use a lint roller to make sure there are no remaining pieces of lint, dirt, or hair to get trapped in the ink.
- Place a sheet of cardstock paper inside the shirt where you plan to print your design. This prevents ink from bleeding through.
- Position your screen where you’d like the design to appear. A common placement is about 3 inches below the neckline.


### Apply the ink


Using your Cricut spatula, create a line of screen-printing ink just above your stencil. Any excess ink after pulling can be scooped up and put back in the container, so be generous!


Holding your squeegee at a consistent angle and applying even pressure, pull the ink across the stencil in one smooth motion. Then, flip your squeegee and pull the ink back up over the stencil.


You want to be sure every part of the stencil design has been covered and there’s no buildup of ink over any one specific area in the design. Two or three passes is usually enough to accomplish this.


### Lift the screen


Carefully lift the screen to reveal your finished print!


### Clean up


Carefully peel the painter’s tape and the vinyl stencil off the screen and throw them away. Then, while your ink is still wet, be sure to wash your screen and tools immediately. Doing so ensures you can keep using them in the future for more designs!


## Step 5: Heat seal your screen print


Let your design dry completely before you attempt to heat seal it. This typically takes about 24 hours. Heat sealing your ink before it’s totally dry can trap moisture in the ink and leads to your design cracking faster.


Once the ink is completely dry, cover your design with butcher paper or parchment paper to protect it from direct heat.


Using your Cricut EasyPress or another Cricut Heat Press, follow the ink manufacturer’s recommended temperature and pressing time to heat seal the ink to the shirt. I used Speedball Ink and set my Cricut EasyPress to 320°F for 40 sec.


## Tips for successful Cricut screen printing


If you’re new to screen printing, these simple tips can make a big difference!


### Start with simple designs


Large, bold graphics produce cleaner prints on your first try and are much easier to weed.


### Always mirror your design


Because the stencil is applied to the back of the screen, your design must be mirrored before cutting. I suggest using an Iron-On guided flow in Design Space (like the Iron-On T-shirt or Iron-On Accessories options) because doing so will automatically mirror your design when you go to cut.


### Burnish more than you think


Good adhesion between the vinyl and the screen helps prevent ink from bleeding underneath the stencil, so really press that vinyl stencil on good from both the back and front of the screen.


### Use enough ink


Don’t be afraid to apply a generous amount! Any excess can be scraped back into the container when you’re done.


### Prepare a few back-up shirts


Plan to prepare 1-2 backup cotton shirts, especially if it’s your first time ever trying to screen print. Having backups makes it less stressful in the likely event that it doesn’t come out perfectly on your first try.


### Give yourself grace


Small imperfections are completely normal when learning. Every project helps build your skills. Have fun with it and keep trying!


## Troubleshooting Cricut screen printing


### My vinyl won’t transfer to the screen


Burnish both sides of the Transfer Tape again and peel slowly at a steep angle. If pieces still lift, use your scraper tool to gently hold the edges of the lifting pieces in place while you slowly peel off the transfer tape.


### Ink is bleeding outside the design


Check that your stencil is fully adhered to the screen and use painter’s tape to cover all exposed mesh before printing.


Also make sure that you’re not lifting or moving the frame at all during the printing process.


### My print looks patchy


You may not have used enough ink or enough pressure with the squeegee. Apply a thicker bead of ink and pull with even pressure. Even pressure is important because the goal is to push the ink through the screen and onto your shirt. Without enough pressure, the ink will remain sitting on top of the screen and will not transfer to the shirt.


### Small details lifted while weeding


Choose simpler artwork for your first project and weed slowly around tiny pieces.


### The design washed off


The ink likely wasn’t fully cured. Always follow your ink manufacturer’s drying and heat-setting instructions before washing.


## Start creating custom screen prints with Cricut


Learning how to screen print with Cricut is a fun way to expand your crafting skills and create personalized apparel, accessories, and home decor! By using permanent vinyl to make a custom stencil, you can achieve clean, professional-looking prints without investing in complex screen-printing equipment.


Start with a simple design, take your time during weeding and transfer, and don’t be afraid to experiment. Once you’ve mastered the basics, you’ll be ready to explore more advanced screen-printing techniques and create one-of-a-kind projects you’ll be proud to wear and share.
