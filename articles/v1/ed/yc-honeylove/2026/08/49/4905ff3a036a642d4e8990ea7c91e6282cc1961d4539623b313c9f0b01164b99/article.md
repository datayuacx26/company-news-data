## Side support bra FAQs


Side support is one of the most misunderstood areas of bra fit. Often, side spillage is caused by a construction issue, not a sizing error.


Below are answers to the questions that come up most often about side spillage, bra construction, and finding the right fix.


### What is a side support bra?


A side support bra is built with wider, taller wings and structured side panels that hold breast tissue inside the cup and prevent it from migrating toward the underarm. Unlike standard bras that rely on cup volume alone, side support bras use construction features like flexible boning, extended panel height, or bonded edges to actively manage lateral tissue.


### Why does tissue push out the sides of my bra even when the cup fits?


Because the cup might fit the front projection of the breast while the side panel is too narrow, too short, or too soft to contain lateral tissue. Breast tissue is mobile, and gravity pulls it sideways throughout the day. Without structured side coverage, the tissue migrates past the cup edge and creates a visible ridge under clothing.


### Does sizing up in the cup fix side spillage?


Not typically. Sizing up adds volume to the front of the cup without changing the side panel construction. The result is often a cup that gaps at the front while the tissue still spills at the sides.


The fix is a bra with taller wing height, firmer lateral panels, or bonded edges designed to hold tissue in from the side.


### What bra features actually prevent side spillage?


**Three construction features matter most for side containment:**


1.


Taller wing height


that extends coverage from the cup edge toward the underarm


2.


Flexible side boning or structured panels


that prevent the wing from collapsing and hold tissue in place


3.


Bonded or raw-cut wing edges


that eliminate the raised seam ridges that dig in and push tissue outward
