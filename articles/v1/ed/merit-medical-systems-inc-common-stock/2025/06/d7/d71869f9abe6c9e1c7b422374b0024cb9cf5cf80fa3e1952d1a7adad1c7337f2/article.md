## Benign Prostatic Hyperplasia (BPH)


## What is BPH?


The prostate is a small gland that sits below the bladder. Benign prostatic hyperplasia, commonly referred to as BPH, is when the prostate gland enlarges.


A[common non-cancerous condition](https://www.health.harvard.edu/mens-health/the-growing-problem-of-an-enlarged-prostate-gland) , by age 60, BPH will affect 50% of all men, and that number increases to 90% by age 85.1 **Bothersome**[symptoms](https://www.mayoclinic.org/diseases-conditions/benign-prostatic-hyperplasia/symptoms-causes/syc-20370087) **of BPH can include** urinary frequency and urgency, urinating more often at night, weak urine stream, and difficulty urinating.


### What is PAE?


To help treat BPH symptoms, there is a **minimally invasive treatment option called prostatic artery embolization (PAE** ). It is performed by a physician called an interventional radiologist (IR).


Under x-ray guidance, PAE begins with a tiny incision in the thigh or wrist. Through this incision, the IR guides a small tube called a catheter into the vessels that supply blood to the prostate. Once the IR reaches the desired location, embolic particles the size of a grain of sand are injected into the vessels, decreasing blood flow to the prostate. By depriving the prostate of blood, it will shrink, and symptoms will improve.

## Heart Disease


### What is heart disease?


Heart disease is a term used to describe conditions that affect the heart and can include coronary artery disease, congestive heart failure (CHF), and diseased valves, just to name a few.


Heart disease is the leading cause of death among men in the United States; however, in recent years there has been[a decline in prevalence](https://www.cdc.gov/nchs/hus/topics/heart-disease-prevalence.htm) .3[Improvements](https://www.cdc.gov/nchs/hus/topics/heart-disease-prevalence.htm) in medical care and an emphasis on reducing risk factors linked to the disease—such as smoking, obesity, and high blood pressure and cholesterol—are believed to play a role.3

## Gastroesophageal Reflux Disease (GERD)


### What is GERD?


**Gastroesophageal reflux disease, commonly known as GERD** , occurs when stomach acid flows back into the esophagus, causing irritation of the tissue lining. **Uncomfortable symptoms** can include heartburn, upper stomach or chest pain, trouble swallowing, and chronic cough.


Although GERD is a common disorder that affects more women than men, men are at a higher risk of serious complications, such as Barrett’s esophagus or esophageal cancer.4
