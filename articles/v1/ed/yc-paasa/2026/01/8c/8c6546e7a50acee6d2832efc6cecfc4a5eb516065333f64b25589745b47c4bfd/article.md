## Why are investors looking at gold exposure?


Investors are re-evaluating Gold because the thesis has shifted from "Safety" to "Strategic Necessity."


### 1. Recent Market Performance


- **The Data:** In 2025, gold recorded an annual return of[74%](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/mf/analysis/gold-clocks-74-return-in-2025-its-best-in-decades-should-investors-stay-invested-in-2026/historic-trend/slideshow/126249757.cms) , bringing the price per ounce to the **$4,600** level.
- **Asset Class Shift:** Historically viewed as a defensive hedge, Gold's correlation has shifted. Last year, its returns exceeded those of major equity indices (like the S&P 500), leading institutional investors to treat it as a primary component of portfolio growth.


### 2. Structural Demand from Central Banks


- **Official Sector Buying:** Central banks have become a dominant source of demand,[contributing 23%](https://www.reuters.com/business/finance/golds-record-breaking-rally-whos-keeping-it-going-2025-09-22/#:~:text=contributed%2023%25%20to%20total%20annual%20gold%20demand%20in%202022%2D2025%2C%20double%20the%20average%20share%20recorded%20during%20the%202010s.) to total annual gold demand in 2022-2025, double the average share recorded during the 2010s.
- **The Floor:** Major purchases by central banks in China, India, and Turkey indicate a strategic move to diversify reserves. This institutional buying provides a consistent demand floor, independent of retail investment sentiment.


### 3. Macroeconomic Factors and Debt


- **Counterparty Risk:** With global debt levels reaching[$346 Trillion](https://www.reuters.com/world/americas/mature-markets-push-global-debt-record-near-346-trillion-says-iif-2025-12-09/#:~:text=NEW%20YORK%2C%20Dec%209%20(Reuters,sustainability%20research%20at%20the%20IIF.) , investors are increasing allocations to assets that carry no counterparty liability.
- **Monetary Debasement:** As governments manage high debt loads, the purchasing power of fiat currencies remains a concern. Gold is the only asset that cannot be printed.


## How can Indian investors buy gold?


Indian investors essentially have three paths for adding gold to their portfolio:


1. **Sovereign Gold Bonds (SGBs):** Government-backed bonds that pay interest.
2. **Digital Gold:** Buying fractional gold via UPI apps.
3. **Global Markets:** Buying institutional-grade Gold ETFs on international exchanges (LSE/NYSE).


### The SGB Availability Constraint


Sovereign Gold Bonds are an excellent instrument for long-term holders due to the 2.5% annual interest. However, for new investors in 2026, there is a significant hurdle: **Availability.**


- **Primary Market:** The RBI issues these bonds in specific "tranches." If a window is not currently open, you cannot buy them directly from the government.
- **Secondary Market Liquidity:** Buying SGBs on the secondary market (stock exchange) is often inefficient. You may have to pay a premium over the fair gold price to find a seller, or struggle to buy large quantities without moving the price.


### The Cost of Digital Gold


While convenient, buying gold via payment apps is often the most expensive route.


- **The Spread:** These platforms typically charge a spread of **3-6%** between the buy and sell price. You start your investment with an immediate loss.


### Why global markets are the best option for gold exposure


Global Gold ETFs offer a solution to both the availability issue of SGBs and the cost issue of Digital Gold.


- **True Liquidity:** Markets like the London Stock Exchange (LSE) are some of the deepest in the world. You can execute large trades instantly at the live market price.
- **Lower Cost:** Global ETFs have expense ratios as low as **0.12%** , significantly cheaper than the spreads on digital gold or the expense ratios of domestic Indian Gold ETFs (often 0.50% - 1.0%).
- **Currency Hedge:** You own the asset in USD or GBP. If the Rupee depreciates against these currencies, your gold investment gains value in INR terms automatically.


## Types of global funds that provide gold exposure


Here is a breakdown of the gold investment options available on global markets:


### 1. Physical Gold Funds


These are funds designed to track the spot price of gold perfectly.


- **Mechanism:** The fund holds actual, numbered gold bars in secure vaults (typically in London or Zurich).
- **Security:** These are "Allocated" funds, meaning the gold legally belongs to the fund shareholders, not the bank.
- **Best For:** Investors who want pure wealth preservation and a hedge against inflation.


### 2. Gold Mining Stocks


Instead of buying the metal, you buy shares in the companies that mine it (e.g., Newmont, Barrick).


- **Operational Leverage:** Miners have fixed operating costs. Once the gold price passes their cost of production (e.g., $1,400/oz), every dollar increase in the gold price flows directly to their bottom line. With gold at $4,600, margins are at record highs.
- **Dividends:** Unlike the metal itself, profitable mining companies pay dividends.

## Why Indian investors should choose non-US gold funds


For Indians investing globally, where a fund is located should be an important consideration, as US-domiciled funds and stocks come with a significant taxation risk.


The United States enforces a strict **Estate Tax** on non-residents. If you hold US-domiciled assets (such as the **GLD** or **IAUM** ETFs) and pass away, the US government levies a **40% tax** on the value of those assets above $60,000.


This means that in the event of an untoward incident, your heirs could lose nearly half of your US-based portfolio to the IRS before the money is even repatriated to India.


For more information on how the Estate tax can affect your investments, read[How the US Estate Tax Works for Indians and NRIs](https://paasa.com/blog/how-us-estate-tax-works-for-indians-and-nris) .


### The Solution: Non-US Funds


Funds not domiciled in the US, like those listed on the London Stock Exchange (LSE) do not carry this risk.


By choosing the **iShares Physical Gold ETC (IGLN)** on the LSE, you get the **exact same gold exposure** as the US funds, often at a lower cost (0.12%), but completely eliminate the risk of a 40% tax wipeout.


For Indian investors focused on long-term wealth preservation, this structural safety makes London-listed funds the superior choice.
