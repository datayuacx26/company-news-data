## What is a Collection Letter?


A **collection letter** is a formal written notice sent to a customer or client who has an overdue or[outstanding invoice](https://upflow.io/blog/ar-collections/outstanding-invoices) . Its purpose is to remind the debtor of the due payment, establish a sense of urgency, and provide a clear request for the balance to be settled.


Unlike the initial friendly reminders, collection notices or letters typically signal a more serious stage in the[accounts receivable process](https://upflow.io/blog/ar-collections/accounts-receivable-process) , especially when prior[email reminders](https://upflow.io/blog/payment-reminders/payment-reminders) or[phone calls](https://upflow.io/blog/payment-reminders/collection-calls) have gone unanswered.


Collection letters often escalate in tone over time, from polite initial requests to firmer demands as the delinquency period increases.


They may also outline potential consequences of non-payment, such as late fees, service disruptions, or legal action. Businesses typically send these letters after exhausting softer communication approaches, but before taking formal legal action.


A well-structured collection letter should include details such as the amount due, invoice number, payment terms, deadline for payment, and instructions on how to resolve the matter.

## How to Create a Collection Strategy


A successful **cash** **collection strategy** is about balancing assertiveness with maintaining positive customer relationships.


Emilie Hart and the team at[WorkMotion](https://upflow.io/customers/workmotion) epitomise this approach. Emilie, who leads the Finance Operations Team, notes: “Our main goal is to create a positive customer experience when it comes to collections by ensuring we’re sending and triggering reminders when it matters most”.


By tailoring the payment experience to individual customers, using segmentation and contextual knowledge from past interactions, Upflow ensures businesses like WorkMotion are able to engage the right person at the right time and place, specifically within the finance pillar of the customer journey. It’s an approach we call[Financial Relationship Management](https://upflow.io/financial-relationship-management) .


But what are the common steps in a cash collection strategy?


It generally begins with a friendly reminder a few days before the due date, followed by another on the due date. Once the invoice has passed its due date, the first set of email reminders should be polite and gentle, assuming the delay might be unintentional. The goal is to prompt payment without damaging rapport.


If the first reminder after the due date fails to generate a response, the second and third communications should gradually become more assertive. By the third outreach, it may be time to escalate the situation by introducing phone calls, which allow for more direct interaction and quicker resolution. Phone calls also create a more personal connection, showing that you’re serious about receiving payment but still value the relationship.


If emails and phone calls do not yield results, the next step is to send **collection letters** . These formal collection notices indicate urgency, making it clear that further delays may result in more serious actions.


The final escalation is often a **legal notice** , indicating that the debt will be pursued through legal channels if it remains unpaid. This step should only be taken as a last resort, after all reasonable attempts to collect the debt have been exhausted.

## What to include in a Collection Letter?


A well-crafted collection letter helps ensure clarity and urgency while maintaining professionalism. Here’s what you should include:


1.


**Personal Information** : Address the letter directly to the recipient, including their full name, company name, and any details about the services or products you’ve provided. This personal touch not only ensures that the letter reaches the right person but also reminds them of your relationship and the value your business has delivered.


2.


**Your Contact Information** : Provide multiple ways for the recipient to contact you. Include your business address, email, and phone number, making it easy for them to resolve the payment issue or get in touch with any questions.


3.


**Attach All Overdue Invoices** : Always include copies of **all overdue invoices** . This ensures the client has a clear view of each outstanding balance, including invoice numbers, amounts due, and due dates. If there are multiple invoices, create a concise list in the body of the letter to ensure transparency and avoid confusion.


4.


**Invoice Details and Due Date** : Reference the original invoice numbers, the amount owed, and the number of days past due. Emphasize this information to highlight the urgency of the situation and remind them of the missed deadlines.


5.


**Updated Invoice with Late Fees (If Applicable)** : If your business charges[late fees](https://upflow.io/blog/ar-collections/late-fee) , include an updated invoice reflecting the additional charges. Make sure to clarify that the late fee will continue to accumulate if the payment is further delayed, depending on your terms and conditions.


6.


**Payment Methods** : Provide clear and convenient payment options. List all acceptable[payment methods](https://upflow.io/blog/business-to-business-payments/b2b-payment-methods) such as credit card, bank transfer, or online payment links. The easier it is for the client to pay, the quicker you’ll receive payment. Providing a direct payment link can significantly speed up the process.


7.


**Offer a Payment Plan (Optional)** : If applicable, offer the option of a payment plan. This is particularly helpful if the client is struggling financially. Outline potential terms for weekly or monthly payments, making it easier for them to manage the overdue balance.


8.


**Consequences of Non-Payment** : Detail the next steps if payment isn’t made within a specified time frame. This could include suspending services, transferring the debt to a[receivable management services](https://upflow.io/blog/ar-collections/receivable-management-services-alternatives) company, or taking legal action. Mention any contractual terms that support these actions, such as late fees or penalties. Be clear but professional when outlining these potential consequences.


By including all these elements, your collection letter remains clear, actionable, and professional, maximizing the chances of payment without harming your business relationship.

## Collection Letter Templates


A well-timed series of collection letters can help recover debts without damaging client relationships. Below are four collection letter template samples that escalate from a polite reminder to a formal legal notice, helping guide the communication process and ensure timely payment.


### **Formal Reminder of Due Payment: Collection Letter Template 1**


This first letter is a polite reminder following previous communication (emails, phone calls or even[SMS](https://upflow.io/blog/payment-reminders/payment-reminder-message) ) about overdue payments. It provides a clear summary of the outstanding invoices, includes payment details, and gently urges the client to take immediate action to avoid further escalation.


### **Warning of Service Restriction: Collection Letter Template 2**


This collection notice escalates the situation by warning the client that if payment is not received within a specified timeframe, their access to services or products will be restricted. The tone is more assertive, and it signals the seriousness of the situation, encouraging the client to pay promptly to avoid service disruption.


### **Service Restricted & Legal Warning: Collection Letter Template 3**


In this letter, the service has been restricted due to non-payment, and the client is informed of this. The collection notice is more of a warning for a legal notice as it also includes a firm warning about potential legal action if the payment is not made within the given timeframe. The tone remains professional but is more urgent and direct.


### **Legal Notice: Collection Letter Template 4**


This final letter is used when legal action has already been initiated due to continued non-payment. It informs the client of the legal steps taken and emphasizes the consequences, urging them to settle the debt to avoid further legal complications or costs.

## FAQs


Q: What is a collection letter?


A: A collection letter is a formal written notice sent to a customer or client with an overdue invoice. It reminds the recipient of the outstanding balance, reinforces payment terms, and creates a sense of urgency to settle the debt. Collection letters are typically sent after email reminders or phone calls have gone unanswered and may escalate in tone depending on how overdue the payment is.


Q: What is the difference between a collection letter and a debt collection letter?


A: In practice, the terms are often used interchangeably. A collection letter is a formal written notice sent by a business to a customer with an overdue invoice, requesting payment and outlining next steps if the debt remains unpaid. A debt collection letter typically refers to the same type of communication, though the phrase is more commonly associated with later-stage escalations or letters sent on behalf of a business by a third-party debt collection agency. For most B2B finance teams, the distinction is less important than having a clear escalation sequence in place: polite first notice, firmer second and third notices, and a formal legal notice if all else fails.


Q: When should you send a collection letter?


A: A collection letter should be sent after an invoice becomes overdue and initial payment reminders (such as emails or phone calls) have not received a response. Most businesses start with friendly reminders and escalate to formal collection letters as the number of days overdue increases.


Q: How many collection letters should you send?


A: Most collection strategies include a series of 3 to 4 collection letters, starting with a polite reminder and gradually becoming more assertive. The final letter is often a legal notice, sent only after all other attempts to collect payment have failed.


Q: What should be included in a collection letter?


A: A collection letter should include key details such as the invoice number, amount due, original due date, and clear payment instructions. It should also set a deadline for payment and outline any potential consequences of non-payment, while maintaining a professional tone.


Q: How do you write a collection letter without damaging customer relationships?


A: The key is to escalate gradually while staying professional and respectful. Start with a friendly tone, assume good intent, and provide clear payment instructions. As the invoice becomes more overdue, increase urgency while keeping communication constructive and focused on resolving the issue.
