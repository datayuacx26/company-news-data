# Vibe marketing: the AI-native strategy marketers are using to increase creative production

Developers have spent the past year directing AI to build apps, websites, and entire codebases by describing an outcome and steering the result, a practice called vibe coding. Marketers are now asking whether the same posture applies to campaigns, and the short answer is yes.


Vibe marketing is an AI-native way of working where marketers own strategy, taste, and judgment while AI handles execution: drafting, designing, testing, and shipping under that direction. One marketer can now cover ground that used to require a full team.


Speed, though, is a multiplier, not a strategy, and that distinction is where most of the vibe marketing conversation breaks down. AI executing in the wrong direction produces more of the wrong thing faster, not a better outcome. The version of vibe marketing that actually compounds rests on two foundations most of the hype skips: customer data so the AI knows who it is talking to, and a brand context layer so output stays on-brand at scale.

## The part most definitions miss: what is vibe marketing?


Most definitions of vibe marketing describe it as another form of marketing automation, but that explanation leaves out a key part of the story. While marketers direct AI tools (or agents) to handle execution (e.g., drafting, designing, testing, and shipping), humans own marketing strategies, judgment, and brand direction. They delegate tasks, but still own the outcome.


This allows a single person to function as a full team since the execution no longer requires a long chain of handoffs.


### How vibe coding inspired vibe marketing


The idea of vibe marketing began with vibe coding. According to[IBM](https://www.ibm.com/think/topics/vibe-coding) , “vibe coding” dates back to February 2025, when Andrej Karpathy introduced it to describe directing an AI tool to generate code rather than writing it manually.


Vibe marketing applies the same posture to campaigns and content: marketers describe an outcome — "launch an ad campaign around the World Cup targeting lapsed customers" or "build a re-engagement series for accounts that haven't logged in this quarter" — and AI creates the assets, selects the audiences, and ships the campaign under the marketer's direction. Speed and quality are equally important considerations that marketers must address with a robust foundation and guardrails to ensure goals are met.


Without a customer data foundation, AI can create marketing content that appears relevant but has no connection to the audience. Without brand knowledge, it can produce content that drifts from the brand messaging. Quality and authenticity are foundational concerns, not tooling problems.

## How vibe marketing works


The buzz around vibe marketing can make it sound vague. In practice, it is goal-based execution applied at the individual level. The marketer defines the outcome. AI helps carry out the work needed to reach it.


### From AI prompts to goals


The idea is not "type a prompt, get an asset." It is defining an outcome. That could mean improving 90-day retention or filling the demand generation pipeline. The AI then creates the campaigns, content, and ads needed to reach that goal — selecting audiences, generating creative, and choosing channels based on customer data and brand context.


### The vibe marketer becomes a manager of agents


The human stays in the loop where judgment is required. Strategy, positioning, taste, brand-sensitive decisions, and final accountability still belong to the marketer.


This way of working is already taking shape.[Microsoft’s 2025 report](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/worklab/work-trend-index/2025-the-year-the-frontier-firm-is-born) found that 844 of 9,037 surveyed leaders work at organizations using AI systems in a more advanced, agent-led way.


In this model, the vibe marketer becomes a manager of agents, overseeing AI agents that carry out parts of the work. As the name implies, their role shifts from operating flows to managing agents. They set goals, define guardrails, curate brand knowledge, and evaluate outcomes.


### The compounding advantage


Every action creates an outcome signal that feeds the next decision. The longer the system runs on your data, the better those decisions become. The advantage endures because the customer owns the data and the brand knowledge underlying it. Those assets stay with the business rather than being locked in a vendor's model.


## Why speed alone isn’t enough


Productivity is part of every marketer’s mantra, but increased speed without relevancy actually decreases productivity. Customer context is crucial to making every campaign relevant and maximizing open rates and conversions. Customer data is the foundation of your brand context layer, and it’s how you maintain clear brand standards across every piece of content you produce.


Without a brand context layer to drive your strategy, content can easily drift away from brand standards and miss campaign goals. Speed amplifies your direction, right or wrong, making the brand context layer and operational brand knowledge a foundational imperative.

## FAQs


### Q1: What is vibe marketing in simple terms?


Vibe marketing is an AI-native way of working where marketers use AI to execute. At the same time, they retain strategy, judgment, and brand direction. It allows one marketer to operate at the level of a full team, provided the right data and brand foundations are in place.


### Q2: How is vibe marketing different from traditional marketing?


Traditional marketing relies on specialized roles, handoffs, and planned-in-advance workflows. Vibe marketing places execution under a single marketer who oversees AI and uses live signals to guide decisions throughout the campaign. The focus moves from completing workflows to defining goals, setting guardrails, and evaluating outcomes.
