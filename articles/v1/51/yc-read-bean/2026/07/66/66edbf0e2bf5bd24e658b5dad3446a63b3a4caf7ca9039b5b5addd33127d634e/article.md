# Beyond "Nǐ Hǎo Ma": Learn some Conversational Openers Real Chinese People Actually Use

If you’ve taken a formal Chinese class, you were probably taught that the standard greeting is **"你好吗？" (Nǐ hǎo ma?)** .


Here is a quick reality check: native Mandarin speakers almost *never* say "Nǐ hǎo ma" in casual, day-to-day life.


To a local in Beijing or Shanghai, starting a conversation with "Nǐ hǎo ma?" sounds like walking up to a friend in English and asking, *"Greetings, fellow human, how is your wellness today?"* It’s grammatically correct, but socially stiff.


If you want to sound natural, warm, and culturally fluent, here are **10 conversational openers** real Chinese people actually use every day.


### 1. 吃了吗？(Chī le ma?) – "Have you eaten?"


This is the ultimate classic Chinese greeting. It’s not actually an invitation to dinner—it’s an expression of care and warmth, showing that you care about the other person's well-being.


-


**The Vibe:** Casual and caring. Perfect for neighbors, colleagues, or acquaintances you bump into around lunchtime or dinner.


-


**How to Reply:** A quick *"吃了，你呢？"* (I’ve eaten, how about you?) is all you need.


### 2. 最近怎么样？(Zuìjìn zěnme yàng?) – "How have things been lately?"


This is the true Chinese equivalent of "How’s it going?" or "What’s up?"


-


**The Vibe:** Relaxed and friendly. Great for friends, coworkers, or someone you haven't chatted with in a couple of weeks.


### 3. 去哪儿啊？(Qù nǎ'er a?) – "Where are you headed?"


If you pass a friend or neighbor on the street, in a hallway, or at an elevator, you don't just say hello—you acknowledge where they’re going.


-


**The Vibe:** Light and conversational. You don't need to give your exact GPS coordinates; a simple *"去买点东西"* (Going to grab some stuff) works fine!


### 4. 忙什么呢？(Máng shénme ne?) – "What are you busy with these days?"


In fast-paced modern Chinese culture, everyone is working hard or juggling projects. Asking what someone is currently busy with opens the door for them to share life updates.


-


**The Vibe:** Ideal for catching up with friends or checking in on a colleague.


### 5. 方便说话吗？(Fāngbiàn shuōhuà ma?) – "Is now a good time to talk?"


Before making a phone call or starting a audio chat on WeChat, texting this phrase is considered standard digital etiquette in China.


-


**The Vibe:** Polite and respectful of someone’s personal time.


### 6. 在干嘛呢？(Zài gàn ma ne?) – "What are you doing?"


This is the go-to text opener among young people on WeChat. It’s informal, low-pressure, and functionally identical to sending a quick "wyd?" text in English.


-


**The Vibe:** Ultra-casual, best used with close friends or romantic partners.


### 7. 帅哥 / 美女，请问... (Shuàigē / Měinǚ, qǐngwèn...) – "Excuse me, handsome / beauty..."


If you need to ask for directions, call a server at a restaurant, or get someone’s attention in a store, calling them **帅哥** (handsome guy) or **美女** (beauty) is standard polite slang.


-


**The Vibe:** Friendly street-level charm. It’s not flirty; it’s just the social norm for addressing strangers in service settings.


### 8. 好久不见！(Hǎojiǔ bùjiàn!) – "Long time no see!"


Did you know the English idiom "long time no see" actually comes from a literal translation of this Chinese phrase? Use it when meeting up with someone you haven't seen in a while.


-


**The Vibe:** Warm, enthusiastic, and universally applicable.


### 9. 周末过得怎么样？(Zhōumò guò de zěnme yàng?) – "How was your weekend?"


Monday morning at the office? Skip the textbook formalities and ask your teammates how their weekend went.


-


**The Vibe:** Great for workplace small talk or icebreakers during video meetings.


### 10. 朋友圈看到你... (Péngyǒu quān kàndào nǐ...) – "I saw on your feed that..."


In China, WeChat Moments (朋友圈) is the center of social life. Referencing something your friend posted—whether it was a cool trip, a bowl of ramen, or a funny meme—is the ultimate modern conversation starter.


-


**The Vibe:** Highly personal, warm, and shows you pay attention to their life.
