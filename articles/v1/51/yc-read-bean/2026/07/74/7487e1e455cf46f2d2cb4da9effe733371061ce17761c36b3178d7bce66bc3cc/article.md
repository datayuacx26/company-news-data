# Entering Your "Highly Chinese Era": 5 Lifestyle Habits You Adopt When You Start Learning Mandarin

Scroll through TikTok or Instagram Reels lately, and you’ve probably seen the viral trend: non-Chinese creators hilariously realizing they've entered their **"Highly Chinese Era."** It starts innocently enough. You pick up a language learning app, you practice a few phrases, and suddenly—without even realizing it—your entire lifestyle shifts. You’re no longer just learning Mandarin; you’re adopting deeply ingrained practical Chinese habits that your friends back home just don’t understand.


### **1. Water Should Be Hot (or at least warm)**


The absolute gateway habit of entering your "Highly Chinese Era" is abandoning ice water. In the West, ice cubes go in everything. In China, **多喝热水 (Duō hē rè shuǐ)** —"drink more hot water"—is the ultimate cure-all for fatigue, colds, and life's general problems.


**The Reality Check:** Suddenly, you’re carrying an insulated thermos everywhere you go, even in the middle of July. If a restaurant serves you room-temperature water, a tiny part of you feels slightly offended.


### **2. The Strict "No Outdoor Shoes" Border Control**


Many cultures take their shoes off at the door, but the Chinese household takes it to a precise science. There is an invisible, sacred border at the entrance of your home.


**The Reality Check:** Outdoor shoes don't just get taken off; they are immediately replaced by ultra-comfortable indoor slippers. Walking past the entryway in your street shoes suddenly feels unthinkable, and you find yourself politely setting out matching pairs for anyone who visits.


### **3. Protecting Your "Qi" from Cold Surfaces**


Along with drinking hot water comes a deep, sudden respect for **养生 (Yǎng shēng)** —the traditional Chinese art of health preservation. One of the biggest rules? Never let your body absorb the cold.


**The Reality Check:** You find yourself refusing to sit directly on cold concrete steps, metal benches, or a chilly floor because you're worried about ruining your internal balance or catching a "cold chill." You might even find yourself looking up *Ba Duan Jin* (八段锦) qi gong routines on your feed for a quick morning stretch.

### **4. Navigating the Complex World of "Custom Boba" Orders**


Back home, ordering a drink means picking a flavor and moving on. In China, ordering a milk tea (like HeyTea or Chagee) is basically an art form. You are handed a digital menu with a matrix of customizable options that require strategic planning.


**The Reality Check:** You no longer just order a "bubble tea." You confidently specify your ice levels ( **去冰** *qù bīng* - no ice), your precise sweetness level ( **三分糖** *sān fēn táng* - 30% sweetness), and you have strong, non-negotiable opinions on whether cheese foam belongs on top of a light oolong tea.


### **5. Saying "Goodbye" to Your Umbrella-Phobia**


In many Western countries, walking outside in a light drizzle without a hood is normal, and using an umbrella on a sunny day gets you weird looks. In China, weather protection is serious business.


**The Reality Check:** Your bag now permanently contains a lightweight, UV-blocking capsule umbrella. You don't just use it for rain—you unashamedly pop it open the second the summer sun hits the pavement to protect your skin from UV rays, just like the locals doing a "City Walk" through Shanghai.
