# Are You a "Da Gong Ren"? 5 Viral Workplace Slang Words Chinese Gen Z is Obsessed With

If you’ve been scrolling through TikTok or Instagram Reels lately, you’ve probably noticed a fascinating shift in internet culture. Young creators are obsessing over the hilarious, brutally honest, and deeply relatable world of Chinese workplace slang.


Gone are the days when textbooks only taught you stiff phrases like "Manager, this is my report." Today’s generation uses sharp humor to bond over corporate life.


Whether you are navigating a job in a Chinese-speaking environment or just want to understand the viral memes on your feed, here are **5 trending workplace slang words** you need to know to sound like a true native.


### 1. 打工人 (Dǎ Gōng Rén) – The "Wage Slave" Pride


This is the ultimate anthem of the modern working class. While it technically translates to "working person," **打工人** carries a badge of sarcastic honor. It describes anyone clocking in to make a living, from office interns to corporate executives.


-


**The Vibe:** It’s used to build camaraderie. On social media, you’ll see people posting morning coffee selfies with the caption: *"Good morning, Da Gong Ren! Let’s secure that bread!"*


### 2. 班味 (Bān Wèi) – The "Smell of Work"


Ever looked in the mirror after an exhausting 9-to-5 and realized your sparkle is completely gone? That is exactly what **班味** means. It refers to the unpolished, tired, and deeply drained aura that corporate life leaves on a person.


-


**The Vibe:** Creators love posting "Before vs. After" transformations on Instagram—showing how they look vibrant on the weekend versus how they look completely covered in *ban wei* by Tuesday afternoon.


### 3. 内卷 (Nèi Juǎn) – "Involution" / Endless Rat Race


Originally an academic term, **内卷** has become a massive internet buzzword. It describes a situation where everyone is working harder and harder, but the rewards stay exactly the same because the competition is too fierce.


-


**The Vibe:** Think of an office where one person stays until 8 PM, so the next person stays until 9 PM, and soon everyone is sleeping at their desks just to keep up. That is peak *nei juan* .


### 4. 摸鱼 (Mō Yú) – "Slacking Off" (Literally: Catching Fish)


Derived from the old Chinese idiom "catching fish in muddy waters," **摸鱼** is the ultimate counter-attack to corporate exhaustion. It means to secretly slack off or do personal tasks during paid working hours.


-


**The Vibe:** Filling up your water bottle 15 times a day, taking a 30-minute bathroom break, or hiding a miniature game tab behind your excel sheet? Congratulations, you are an expert at *mo yu* .


### 5. 躺平 (Tǎng Píng) – "Lying Flat"


When the *nei juan* (rat race) gets to be too much, Gen Z chooses to **躺平** . This means refusing to overwork, resetting your expectations, and doing the bare minimum to survive comfortably rather than stressing over a promotion.


-


**The Vibe:** It’s the ultimate form of peaceful silent quitting. It’s all about choosing mental health and work-life balance over burning out for a corporate ladder.
