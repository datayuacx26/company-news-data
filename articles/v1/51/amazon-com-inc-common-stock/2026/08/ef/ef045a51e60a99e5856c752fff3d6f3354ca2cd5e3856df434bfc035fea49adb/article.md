# 5 ways Amazon is investing in Arizona

---


## Key takeaways


- Amazon has


[invested more than $40 billion in Arizona](https://assets.aboutamazon.com/1f/43/abf47c1b446a8d99ce342371e82b/amazoninvestment-arizona-2025.pdf) and contributed over $40 billion to the state's economy.


- The company has more than 35,000 full- and part-time employees and supports more than 20,000 indirect jobs.


- Amazon partners with 14 educational institutions in Arizona through its


[Career Choice program](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/workplace/career-choice-free-education-for-amazon-employees) and has upskilled over 5,300 employees since 2019.


- More than 45 million items were sold by Arizona-based independent sellers through Amazon's store.


---


For more than 30 years, Amazon has been investing across the United States, strengthening local economies and creating opportunities for communities nationwide. Arizona is a prime example of this commitment, with Amazon building a substantial presence in the state since 2007.


Amazonian Craig McCormick (right) with a co-worker at the PHX6 fulfillment center in Arizona.


Today, the Grand Canyon State serves as a thriving innovation and logistics hub for Amazon, with investments spanning


[fulfillment centers](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/tag/fulfillment-center) to


[Whole Foods Market locations](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/tag/whole-foods-market) . The company's economic impact extends beyond direct employment, supporting thousands of additional jobs in construction, logistics, and professional services, and helping local businesses reach customers around the world.


Here are five ways Amazon is investing in Arizona:


## 1. Investing in our employees


Amazon provides


[competitive wages and comprehensive benefits](https://www.aboutamazon.com/workplace/employee-benefits) from Day 1. Operations employees in customer fulfillment and transportation earn an average hourly base wage of more than $23, with total compensation averaging more than $30 an hour when including benefits. These benefits include health, vision, and dental insurance starting on Day 1, free mental health resources, up to 20 weeks of fully paid parental leave, a 401(k) with 50% company match, and flexible time-off options that increase with tenure.


[Amazon is investing over $1 billion to raise pay and lower healthcare costs for US fulfillment and transportation employees](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/workplace/amazon-wage-increase-2025-fulfillment-transportation-employees)


Average total compensation is now over $30 per hour when you include elected benefits.


Beyond more than 35,000 direct jobs, Amazon's presence in Arizona supports more than 20,000 indirect jobs in industries such as construction, logistics, and professional services—suppliers and partners we rely on every day.


## 2. Investing in workforce development and upskilling


Amazon is committed to preparing its workforce for future opportunities through comprehensive education and


[skills training programs](https://www.aboutamazon.com/impact/amazon-ai-courses-certifications-aws-trainings) .


In Arizona, Amazon partners with 14 educational institutions through its Career Choice program, which prepays tuition for employees pursuing further education. These partnerships with colleges and universities across the state help Amazon employees gain in-demand skills that can propel them into new careers.


[Amazon commits $1 billion to Career Choice employee education program as part of Future Ready 2030 fund](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/workplace/amazon-future-of-work-skills-jobs-training)


The Future Ready 2030 fund supports students, employees, and those looking to expand their skills as the workforce adapts to a changing economy.


From Machine Learning University to Career Choice, these programs represent Amazon's investment in developing talent and creating pathways to opportunity for its workforce. Since 2019, more than 5,300 Arizona employees have participated in our free education and skills training programs.


## 3. Building infrastructure across the Grand Canyon State


Amazon has invested more than $40 billion in Arizona's infrastructure and employee compensation, contributing more than $40 billion to the state's gross domestic product.


An Amazon employee transports an MK30 drone in an Arizona fulfillment center.


These investments include 23


[fulfillment and sortation centers](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/tag/fulfillment-center) , 12 delivery stations, five Rural Super Rural delivery stations, and other facilities across the state. The company has also expanded its retail footprint with 10 Whole Foods Market locations across Arizona. Amazon continues to grow its


[sustainable infrastructure](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/tag/sustainability) as well, with four solar farms and two sites with on-site solar power in the state.


[Amazon Leo is building and launching satellites faster than ever](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/transportation/amazon-drone-delivery-arizona)


Amazon Leo will double its launches this year as it gears up for its first customers.


## 4. Empowering Arizona's small businesses


Amazon provides a powerful opportunity for Arizona's entrepreneurs and small businesses to reach customers worldwide.


[Arizona-based independent sellers](https://sellingpartners.aboutamazon.com/arizona) —primarily small and medium-sized businesses—sold more than 45 million items through Amazon's store, with the average seller generating more than $220,000 in annual sales.


Purvi Desai, founder of kitchen-born skincare brand,


[Zaaina](https://sellingpartners.aboutamazon.com/accidental-entrepreneur-takes-family-recipe-to-market-with-amazon)


, in her Arizona warehouse.


These independent sellers represent more than 60% of sales in Amazon's store. Their success contributes significantly to local economies, with more than 65% of U.S. independent sellers employing at least one person within 50 miles of their primary business location.


By providing infrastructure, logistics support, and access to millions of customers, Amazon enables Arizona's small businesses to scale their operations and create additional jobs throughout the state.


## 5. Investing as a good neighbor


Amazon uses its global scale and strengths to make a positive impact in communities. Working with trusted community partners, we address critical needs—


[food security](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/tag/hunger) ,


[education](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/tag/education) ,


[disaster relief](https://www.aboutamazon.com/impact/community/disaster-relief) —using our logistics, technology, and innovation, while investing as a good neighbor in the communities where our employees live and work.


An Amazon employee helps with a playset build for Roc Solid, a childhood cancer charity.


Amazon has delivered more than 5 million meals on behalf of food banks and nonprofits across the state. The company has reached more than 30,000 students and 1,000 teachers through education programs and supported more than 400,000 computer science learning hours.


Amazon has also contributed more than 2,000 disaster relief items to support emergency response efforts. More than 3,000 Amazon employees have volunteered in communities across Arizona.
