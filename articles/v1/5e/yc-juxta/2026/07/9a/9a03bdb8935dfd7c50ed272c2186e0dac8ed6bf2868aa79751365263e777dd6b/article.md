# Beyond GPS: Bringing Real-Time Visibility to In-Transit Shipments

Every day, millions of shipments move through global supply chains.


Shipping containers cross oceans. Truckloads travel thousands of miles between distribution centers. High-value equipment moves between job sites. Pallets are transferred between warehouses, ports, rail yards, and customer facilities.


While most organizations know when a shipment was dispatched and when it was delivered, they often have limited visibility into everything that happens in between.


If a shipment is delayed, arrives at the wrong location, or sits idle longer than expected, the first indication is often a missed delivery window or a customer inquiry.


For supply chains that depend on precision, that's no longer enough.


Juxta's NODE1 asset tag provides continuous visibility into shipments throughout their journey, giving logistics teams a live view of where freight is, how it's moving, and when operational issues begin to emerge.


## Tracking the Shipment Instead of the Vehicle


Most freight visibility today is built around vehicle tracking.


Fleet management platforms can show where a truck is traveling, while transportation management systems provide estimated arrival times based on planned routes.


That information is valuable—but it doesn't necessarily tell you where your shipment is.


Freight is routinely transferred between trucks, trailers, railcars, ships, warehouses, ports, and distribution centers. Containers are staged in yards before loading. Pallets are unloaded and temporarily stored before final delivery. A shipment may change hands multiple times before reaching its destination.


Tracking the vehicle alone cannot provide complete visibility throughout that process.


By attaching a NODE1 asset tag directly to the shipment, container, pallet, or trailer, organizations gain continuous visibility into the asset itself, regardless of how many vehicles or facilities it passes through.


## Designed for High-Value and Critical Shipments


Not every shipment requires continuous tracking.


For high-value freight, mission-critical equipment, sensitive materials, or reusable shipping assets, however, knowing exactly where a shipment is can significantly reduce operational risk.


NODE1 can be attached directly to:


- Ocean freight containers
- Truckloads
- Palletized shipments
- High-value cargo
- Industrial equipment
- Returnable transport containers
- Construction materials
- Medical equipment
- Defense logistics
- Project cargo


Because the tracking device stays with the shipment, visibility remains consistent throughout the transportation process.


## Improving Supply Chain Visibility


Supply chains rarely fail because of one major disruption.


More often, they are affected by dozens of small delays.


A container sits in a port longer than expected.


A truck waits several hours at a distribution center.


Freight is unloaded into the wrong staging area.


A shipment remains idle before final delivery.


Individually, these events may seem insignificant.


Across thousands of shipments, they become measurable operational costs.


Continuous shipment visibility helps logistics teams identify these delays as they occur instead of discovering them after delivery schedules have already been affected.


## Supporting Intermodal Logistics


Modern freight often moves through multiple transportation modes before reaching its destination.


A shipment may begin on a truck, transfer to rail, continue by ship, return to rail, and complete its journey by truck.


Each handoff introduces opportunities for delays, misplaced freight, or communication gaps.


Because NODE1 remains attached to the shipment rather than the transportation asset, organizations maintain visibility across the entire journey instead of losing continuity each time freight changes carriers or transportation modes.


This creates a single, continuous record of shipment movement from origin to destination.


## Monitoring Global Freight Movements


Large enterprises often manage thousands of shipments moving simultaneously across multiple countries and transportation networks.


Understanding where freight is at any given moment enables logistics teams to make faster decisions when disruptions occur.


For example:


A delayed container arriving at a port may require downstream production schedules to be adjusted.


A project-critical shipment arriving ahead of schedule may allow installation crews to mobilize earlier.


Freight delayed at a cross-dock facility can be identified before it affects customer commitments.


Rather than waiting for manual status updates from multiple logistics providers, organizations gain direct visibility into shipment movement throughout the supply chain.


## Reducing Lost and Misplaced Freight


Although transportation providers maintain detailed operational processes, misplaced shipments remain an expensive reality across global logistics networks.


Freight may be unloaded into the wrong warehouse, staged at an incorrect dock, transferred onto the wrong trailer, or left behind during cross-docking operations.


When these situations occur, locating the shipment quickly becomes critical.


With NODE1, logistics teams can immediately identify the shipment's last known location and movement history, helping reduce search time and accelerate recovery.


For high-value freight, even preventing a small number of misplaced shipments can generate meaningful operational savings.


## Supporting Reusable Logistics Assets


Many organizations rely on reusable transportation equipment to move products through their supply chains.


Shipping containers, roll cages, insulated containers, reusable pallets, industrial racks, and specialty transport equipment often circulate continuously between suppliers, warehouses, manufacturing plants, and customers.


Because these assets are valuable and frequently leave company facilities, maintaining visibility into their location is often just as important as tracking the freight itself.


NODE1 enables organizations to understand where reusable logistics assets are located, how frequently they are being used, how long they remain at customer sites, and when they return to the supply chain.


These insights help reduce unnecessary replacement purchases while improving overall asset utilization.


## Integrating With Existing Logistics Platforms


NODE1 is designed to complement existing logistics technology rather than replace it.


Location data can be integrated into transportation management systems (TMS), supply chain visibility platforms, enterprise resource planning (ERP) systems, warehouse management systems (WMS), and custom logistics dashboards.


Combining shipment location with transportation, inventory, and operational data provides logistics teams with a more complete understanding of how freight moves through their network.


Instead of relying solely on milestone updates, organizations gain continuous operational context throughout the shipment lifecycle.


## Built for Global Supply Chains


Supply chains are becoming increasingly complex.


Products move through more facilities, more transportation partners, and more international networks than ever before.


At the same time, customer expectations continue to increase, leaving little room for uncertainty.


By attaching a NODE1 asset tag directly to containers, pallets, trailers, or other freight, organizations gain continuous visibility that follows the shipment—not just the vehicle transporting it.


That visibility helps logistics teams identify delays earlier, improve shipment accountability, reduce misplaced freight, and make better operational decisions throughout the transportation process.


For organizations managing high-value cargo or time-sensitive supply chains, knowing where a shipment was yesterday is rarely enough. Continuous visibility throughout its journey provides the operational confidence needed to move freight more efficiently from origin to destination.
