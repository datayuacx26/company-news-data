# How Title Companies Use Escrow to Solve Common Closing Challenges

**Why Escrow Matters in Missouri Real Estate Transactions**


Real estate closings in Missouri—especially in the St. Louis area—aren’t always straightforward. Every property, every seller, and every buyer comes with unique circumstances that can complicate the timing of a closing. But there’s good news: an escrow agreement is one of the most effective tools a title company can use to keep a transaction on track, even when unexpected issues arise.


**What Is an Escrow Agreement?**


An escrow agreement allows a title company to hold funds temporarily until certain conditions are met. Instead of delaying or jeopardizing a closing, escrow provides a safe, legally structured way to move forward while loose ends are handled after closing.


**Common Reasons Escrow Is Used in Missouri Closings**


Title companies in the St. Louis region frequently hold escrow funds for situations such as:


-


Repairs that can’t be completed before closingWhether due to weather, contractor delay, or material shortages, unfinished repairs are one of the most common escrow triggers.


-


Divorce proceedings involving the sellersIf a divorce won’t be finalized before closing, the title company can hold funds and disburse them once legal documentation is completed.


-


A recent death of an ownerIf a property owner passed away within the last year and their estate has not yet been fully probated, escrow can help facilitate closing while legal matters continue.


These scenarios could otherwise delay the transaction—but escrow makes it possible to keep things moving.


**Who Is Involved in an Escrow Agreement?**


Depending on the situation, the parties to an escrow agreement often include:


-


The buyer


-


The seller


-


The lender


-


The title company, acting as the escrow agent


As the escrow agent, the title company ensures all conditions in the agreement are met and oversees the secure distribution of funds when those conditions are satisfied.


**How Escrow Agreements Are Created**


A well-structured escrow agreement begins with an amendment to the sales contract. This amendment outlines the reason for the escrow, how much money will be held, and what must occur before the funds can be released.


Because this document guides the title company’s responsibilities, it’s essential for both parties to work closely with their real estate agents to ensure clarity and mutual understanding.


**Why Escrow Agreements Matter**


When handled correctly, an escrow agreement can save a transaction that might otherwise fall apart. It creates a clear, legal path for closing—allowing buyers and sellers to move forward with confidence while the title company manages outstanding issues in a secure and impartial way.
