## 📜 Origin of the Brokerage Seat-Stack Problem


Realtor CRM pricing grew up around brokerages, not around individuals. That history explains nearly every frustration a solo agent has with the category.


Three forces shaped it:


1. **Lead generation was the product.** Vendors monetized portal leads, IDX websites, and consumer search. CRM was the retention layer that kept agents inside the ecosystem, which is why the CRM and the website are usually sold as one contract.
2. **Brokerages bought, agents used.** The buyer with the checkbook was a broker-owner with fifteen agents and a marketing coordinator. Feature roadmaps followed the buyer, not the user. Recruiting dashboards and agent-productivity leaderboards exist because broker-owners asked for them.
3. **Configuration became an admin job.** Once stages, drip campaigns, and routing rules were brokerage-standard, changing a field became a support ticket. That is fine at a brokerage with an operations person. It is friction when one agent knows exactly which field they need and has no one to ask.


Then two things changed. Texting became the default first touch, which made response-time discipline more valuable than campaign depth. And AI app builders collapsed the cost of a custom board, so the question shifted from "which realtor CRM brand?" to "do we rent a schema or own one?"


The seat stack is not a moral failure by kvCORE. It is a business model matched to bundled depth. Solo agents feel it when a brokerage-shaped contract prices a one-person business, or when the module they actually need sits one tier up.


That loop is what Taskade Genesis is built to own. IDX consumer search and MLS syndication are adjacent systems that stay where they are.
