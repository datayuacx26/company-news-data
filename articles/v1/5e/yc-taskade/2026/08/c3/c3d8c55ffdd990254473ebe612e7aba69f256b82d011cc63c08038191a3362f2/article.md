## 🧭 What Coaches Actually Need From a Coaching CRM


A coaching CRM needs five things: a client record with goals, a session log, homework with due dates, a check-in trail between sessions, and a login where the client sees their own progress and nothing else. Everything past that — video hosting, affiliate mechanics, launch funnels, checkout pages — is a course-business feature, not a coaching feature.


Most coaching weeks look like this:


1. A client books or confirms a session.
2. The session happens, and notes plus action items land somewhere.
3. Homework goes out, scoped to that one client or that one cohort week.
4. Something between sessions either happens or quietly does not.
5. The next session starts with "where did we leave off?"


Step five is the whole business. It is also the step that a course platform does not model, because a course platform assumes the content is the product and the learner is self-serve.

## 📜 Why the LMS Bill Outlived the LMS Need


All-in-one creator platforms were priced for a launch, not for a practice. The bundle made sense when the business was "record forty lessons, run a webinar, sell a thousand seats." Video hosting, funnels, email, and checkout in one login was genuinely cheaper than assembling five tools.


Three things changed the math for coaches:


1. **Delivery moved back to live.** Group programs, cohort pods, and 1:1 retainers sell better than evergreen video for many practitioners. Live delivery needs a roster and a progress record, not a player and a funnel.
2. **The real system of record drifted into tabs.** Goals in a spreadsheet, notes in Docs, homework in chat, invoices in a third tool. The platform kept billing while the daily truth lived somewhere it could not see.
3. **Generating a portal got cheap.** In 2026 you describe a client progress portal in a sentence and get a working app with linked records and real logins. That moves the decision from "which creator platform?" to "do I rent a schema or own one?"


None of this makes Kajabi or Teachable wrong. It makes them **priced for a different shape of business than the one many coaches actually run.** The tell is simple: open your platform bill, then list the modules you touched last month. If the video library and the funnel builder are not on that list, you are funding capacity you do not use.


That loop is the product a coaching CRM should own. Video files and checkout pages are attachments to it.

## 🧪 A Day in the Life on a Coaching Portal


**08:10** You open one board. Three clients have homework due today, one has been silent for nine days. No tab archaeology.


**09:00** A discovery call converts. The intake form writes a client record with goal and program tier already populated.


**11:30** A 1:1 session ends. Notes and two action items go on the session row while the call is still warm.


**14:00** The check-in agent drafts tomorrow's prep note for a client, citing the two homework items that slipped. You edit one line and confirm.


**16:20** A cohort announcement goes out through an automation. Every member sees it in their own portal; nobody sees anyone else's progress.


**17:00** A renewal is 30 days out. The automation already surfaced it, so the conversation happens before the deadline instead of after.


Nothing in that day requires an LMS video library or a funnel builder. It requires a record your clients trust and you can reshape.
