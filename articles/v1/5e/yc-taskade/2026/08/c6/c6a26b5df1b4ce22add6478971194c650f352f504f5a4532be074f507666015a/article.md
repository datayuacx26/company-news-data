## 📚 What Study Planning Actually Needs


A study planner is usually pitched as a calendar. In practice a tutor, cohort coach, or serious self-studier lives in a narrower loop every week:


1. Someone decides which topics matter this week (curriculum unit, weak section, exam date).
2. Those topics become dated study blocks a student can actually see.
3. The student completes some of them and skips others.
4. A practice score or quiz result says whether the completed work landed.
5. Next week's blocks get rebuilt around what was missed, not what was planned.
6. A coach or parent asks "is this student on track?" and expects an answer in seconds.


Steps 3 through 6 are where spreadsheets and pretty templates fall apart. Blocks are easy; the feedback loop is the product.

## 📜 Why Study Plans Break in Spreadsheets and Notion


Students and coaches face the same failure modes, and they are structural rather than a discipline problem:


- Weekly plans live in PDFs or shared docs with no completion tracking.
- Coaches rebuild schedules manually after each practice test.
- Parents ask for progress updates that require exporting screenshots.
- Notion templates look great until you need per-student auth and reminders.
- Nobody can answer "which topic is this cohort worst at?" without a merge step.


Three shifts made this worse rather than better:


1. **Templates got beautiful and stayed static.** A gorgeous study dashboard still needs a human to re-date every block after a bad practice test. Aesthetics do not reschedule.
2. **Everyone became a stakeholder.** Students, parents, assistant tutors, and program directors all want a view. Document tools answer that with duplicated files or all-or-nothing sharing.
3. **Score data arrived from everywhere.** Practice platforms, question banks, and school portals each export their own shape. The plan needs to react to that data, which means the plan has to be a system, not a page.


None of this makes Notion or Google Calendar bad software. It makes them the wrong layer for the loop. Note the honest split before you migrate anything:


- **Stay on Notion or Google Sheets** when one tutor tracks five students with honor-system updates.
- **Use Google Calendar** when time blocking alone is the goal.
- **Generate an owned dashboard** when students need logins, automations, and progress tied to scores.


That loop is what an owned study planner is for. Video hosting and question banks are adjacent systems.

## 🛟 Who Should Stay on Notion, Calendar, or a Full LMS


Stay when the incumbent is genuinely the better product for your week. Each of these has real strengths worth naming.


**Notion** is excellent at three things a generated dashboard does not try to beat: a genuinely delightful writing and page-design surface, a template ecosystem so large that someone has already built your exact subject, and instant sharing with no access model to think about. If one student owns one page and updates it because they enjoy the page, Notion is winning and you should leave it alone. If you are shopping the boundary, the[free Notion alternative comparison](https://www.taskade.com/compare/free-notion-alternative) is the honest side-by-side.


**Google Calendar and Todoist** own time itself. Recurring blocks, timezone math, native mobile notifications, and calendar interop with everything a school already runs. If the entire problem is "I need Tuesday 7pm to 9pm blocked for organic chemistry," a calendar solves it in ten seconds and no dashboard beats that.


**Canvas, Teachable, and Kajabi** bundle video hosting, DRM-ish content delivery, checkout, and enrollment marketing. When video *is* the product, that bundle is worth real money and rebuilding it would be foolish. Institutional LMS platforms also carry accreditation-recognized gradebooks and academic integrity tooling that a coach cannot replicate.

Run a parallel week. Do not cancel anything on day one.


### Day 0: Decide the nouns


- Name the four records you cannot lose: **student, topic block, practice score, exam date**
- Note optional records: cohort, unit, session log, parent contact
- Write down what stays put: video host, question bank, school calendar


### Days 1 to 3: Generate and load real data


- Generate the[study planner progress dashboard](https://www.taskade.com/generate/education/study-planner-progress-dashboard) with two **real** students
- Add weekly topic columns that match your curriculum units
- Import existing task lists from spreadsheets — see[Google Sheets to an ops app](https://www.taskade.com/blog/sheets-to-ops) for the general pattern
- Mirror the language students already use ("Reading 2," "MBE set," "Unit 4 lab")
- Set one real exam date so the countdown is honest


### Days 4 to 7: Blocks, portal, one automation


- Generate an[AI study schedule](https://www.taskade.com/generate/ai/study-schedule) for the next four weeks and edit before publish
- Invite the two students as[App Users](https://www.taskade.com/learn/genesis/app-users) and confirm each sees only their own rows
- Test mobile completion from an actual phone before any reminder exists
- Turn on **one** automation: a Sunday plan preview or an[overdue nudge](https://www.taskade.com/automate/education/late-submission-reminder)
- Set access levels — seven permission tiers from Owner down to Viewer let an assistant tutor edit while a parent stays read-only ([Learn: permissions](https://www.taskade.com/learn/projects/permissions) )

## 🧪 A Week in the Life of an Owned Study Planner


**Sunday 18:00** The Sunday preview automation fires. Each student opens the portal and sees five dated blocks for the week, not a wall of the whole syllabus.


**Monday 07:30** A student ticks two blocks from a phone on the bus. The coach's cohort view updates without anyone sending a message.


**Wednesday 21:10** Another student has ticked nothing. The overdue nudge fires once. No coach had to notice.


**Thursday 16:00** A practice test score lands. The weak section field flips from "Reading" to "Quantitative," and the linked unit priority changes with it.


**Friday 09:00** The[test preparation coach agent](https://www.taskade.com/agents/education/test-preparation-coach) drafts next week: heavier quantitative blocks, one reading maintenance block, spaced revision on last month's weak unit.


**Friday 09:20** The coach opens the draft, cuts one block as unrealistic, and approves. Students see the new week only after that edit.


**Friday 17:00** A parent opens their read-only summary instead of asking for a screenshot. The coach never exported anything.


That week needs no video CDN and no institutional gradebook. It needs a plan people trust, a score that changes it, and a human who approves the change.
