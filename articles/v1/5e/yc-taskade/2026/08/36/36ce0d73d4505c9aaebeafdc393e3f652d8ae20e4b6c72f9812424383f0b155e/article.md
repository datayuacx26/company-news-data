## 📜 Origin of the Problem: Why AR Aging Escaped Accounting


Aging reports were designed for a bookkeeper, and the bookkeeper was the only person who ever needed one. For decades that assumption held — one person owned the ledger, ran the report monthly, and handed a printout to an owner.


Three changes broke it for agencies:


1. **Collections became a delivery decision, not a finance decision.** Deciding whether to pause a sprint for a 60-day-late client requires knowing what shipped last week. That context lives in project records, not in the ledger.
2. **Retainers made "late" ambiguous.** A retainer invoice issued on the 1st for work performed all month is late in a different way than a milestone invoice for a delivered site. One aging column cannot express both.
3. **The people who chase are not the people with ledger logins.** Account managers do the calling. Giving every AM an accounting seat is expensive and risky, so they get spreadsheet exports instead — and the export is stale by Tuesday.


The result is the pattern almost every agency recognizes: authoritative books nobody works in, plus a shadow spreadsheet everyone works in that nobody trusts. Neither is wrong. The missing layer is an operational board that reads like the ledger and behaves like a task list.

## 🗂 The Data Model Worth Getting Right


Most failed AR boards fail at the schema, not the tooling. Five nouns carry the whole system.


Three schema decisions decide whether the board survives contact with reality:


- **Track` balance` , not` amount` .** Partial payments are the single most common reason a bucket lies. An invoice with 60% paid is not a full-value 60-day problem.
- **Link the invoice to the project.** Without it, a pause decision requires three Slack messages. With it, the delivered milestone is one click away.
- **Make` bucket` derived, never typed.** If a human sets the bucket, a human will forget. Let days outstanding drive it and let a[table view](https://www.taskade.com/learn/projects/table-view) group by it.


Taskade gives you **7 project views** — List, Board, Calendar, Table, Mind Map, Gantt, and Org Chart — so the same invoice records power a bucket board, a due-date calendar, and a table your finance lead sorts. Access uses **7 permission levels** from Owner down to Viewer, so a finance lead edits while an account manager comments and a client sees only status. Never hand a client a workspace seat; use[app users](https://www.taskade.com/learn/genesis/app-users) scoped per client instead.

## 🕗 A Day in the Life on an Owned AR Board


**08:10** Finance opens the board. Three invoices crossed into 31-60 overnight. Each already has an owner and an auto-created call task. Nobody hunted through an export.


**09:30** An account manager opens one of those tasks. The client's payment history, last delivered milestone, and previous promise date are on the row. The call takes four minutes instead of twenty.


**11:00** A client pays two of three open invoices. The balance field updates, the invoice drops to a partial row rather than vanishing, and queued reminders for the paid rows close themselves.


**13:45** A retainer client hits 62 days. The board adds them to the principal's review list automatically. No Slack thread required.


**15:20** The principal opens the review list. Five rows, one decision each: call, pause, plan, or write off. Every decision gets logged on the invoice, so next month's conversation starts from record rather than memory.


**16:40** An AR agent drafts next week's escalation notes from the touch log. A human edits two of them and sends. The board is the same one the whole team used all day.


That day requires no general ledger inside Taskade, and no exports. It requires a board people trust and a policy written before the pressure arrived.

Finance owns buckets and escalation. Account managers own relationship context. The board should encode that split rather than rely on goodwill: assign the account manager at 30 days, the principal at 60, and keep finance as the standing owner of everything younger.


Meeting notes on the client record prevent the "I already talked to them" surprise that makes an agency look disorganized to the one person holding a check. ## ❓ FAQ: AR Aging Trackers for Agencies


What is the best AR aging tracker for agencies in 2026?


Taskade Genesis for an owned ops board where collectors actually work — buckets, owners, chase history, and automations tied to delivery context. QuickBooks for authoritative books and filing. Harvest for shops where AR starts from tracked hours. Generate the[AR aging dashboard](https://www.taskade.com/generate/invoice/accounts-receivable-aging-dashboard) and validate it against your ledger before you change anything else.


Can Taskade Genesis replace QuickBooks?


No, and it should not try. The board owns operational aging, chase workflow, client-visible status, and escalation. QuickBooks keeps tax filing, payroll integration, reconciliation, and accountant handoff. Most agencies run both and make one system authoritative per noun.


Can I import open invoices from QuickBooks?


Yes. Export the open-invoice list as CSV, map columns into the board, and reconcile the total against the aging report to the dollar before you enable any automation. A board that disagrees with the ledger on day one will never be trusted.


How do I handle partial payments?


Model` balance` as the field the bucket derives from, and keep` amount` as history. A 60-day invoice that is 70% paid is a different collections problem than a full-value one, and treating them the same is how agencies chase the wrong client.


Does Taskade Genesis calculate interest on late invoices?


You can define fields and derived values for your own late-fee policy. Anything the general ledger must reflect for tax or audit purposes should still be computed in your accounting suite so the two never disagree.


How many automations should I start with?


One. Turn on the day-1 reminder, let it run a full week, confirm every send wrote a touch to the invoice record, then add the 30-day account manager task. Shipping seven automations on day one guarantees a client gets nagged three times before lunch.


Can clients see their invoice status without a workspace seat?


Yes. Scoped app users show each client their own rows only. Never give a client a workspace seat to check an invoice — pair the AR board with a[client portal](https://www.taskade.com/generate/client-portals/agency-client-portal) and read[app users](https://www.taskade.com/learn/genesis/app-users) for the setup.


How is this different from a BI dashboard?


BI charts summarize exported data. An AR ops board is where collectors work: sort buckets, log calls, snooze reminders, escalate. If nobody can take an action from a screen, it is a report, not a board. See[ops dashboard builders](https://www.taskade.com/blog/ops-dashboard-builders) for the broader category.


Do we need an AI agent for this?


Not on day one. An agent on empty sample rows is theater. Once the board holds real payment history, an agent can draft call scripts, summarize a client's pattern, and flag chronic late payers. Add it in week three, not week one.
