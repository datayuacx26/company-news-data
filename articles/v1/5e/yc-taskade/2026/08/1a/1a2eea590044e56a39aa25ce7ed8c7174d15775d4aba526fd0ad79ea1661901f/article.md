## 📋 What Field Ops Actually Need From an Agreement Tracker


A recurring service agreement tracker needs six fields and one link. The six fields are customer, contract value, term dates, visit frequency, next visit due, and renewal status. The one link is to the board that runs today's truck. Everything past that is reporting, and reporting is not what kills contracts.


The week that actually happens in a small shop:


1. A commercial account signs a quarterly maintenance agreement.
2. The signed PDF goes to the bookkeeper, who sets up recurring billing.
3. Nobody schedules visit two, because visit two lives in a different tool than today's emergency calls.
4. The customer pays for four visits and receives two.
5. At renewal, the customer has no memory of value delivered, so they cancel.


Step three is the whole failure. It is not a billing problem and it is not a CRM problem. It is a "the visit never reached dispatch" problem.

## 📜 Origin of the Membership Module Problem


Maintenance agreements predate maintenance software by decades. HVAC and plumbing shops started selling annual service contracts to smooth seasonal cash flow. You sell furnace tune-ups in October so the crew has billable work in February. The paperwork was a filing cabinet and a wall calendar, and it worked because one person held the whole thing in their head.


Field-service platforms productized that in the 2010s as "memberships," and they made a reasonable architectural choice: tie the agreement to the pricebook. A membership became a sellable SKU with tiered pricing, discount rules, and marketing attribution. That is genuinely useful when you are a 40-tech residential shop running seasonal campaigns.


Three things changed for small crews:


1. **The pricebook became the price of entry.** Membership billing depth usually rides on top of a configured pricebook. A five-truck commercial shop with per-property visit cadences gets the configuration burden without the campaign upside.
2. **Per-tech licensing outran the value.** Agreement tracking is office work. Paying a technician seat fee for a capability three office people use inverts the cost model.
3. **App builders collapsed the cost of a custom tracker.** In 2026 you can generate a contract board from a prompt and reshape the fields yourself. The buying question moved from "which platform?" to "do we rent this schema or own it?"


None of that makes packaged membership modules wrong. It makes them a specific answer to a specific shape of business.


That loop is what Taskade Genesis is built to own. The general ledger and the card charge stay in accounting.

## 🧭 Who Should Move Agreements Beside Dispatch


Move (or dual-run) when several of these are true:


- You run roughly **1 to 15 people** touching agreements, not a multi-branch residential operation with a marketing department.
- Your agreements are **counts of visits per site** , not tiered membership SKUs with pricebook discounts.
- You have **commercial accounts with multiple properties** on different cadences, which packaged residential membership shapes handle badly.
- Contract dollars currently live in **accounting only** , and no board answers "which agreements are behind?"

## 🛟 Who Should Stay on a Packaged Membership Platform


Stay when the packaged product is genuinely the product you operate. Three real strengths, named plainly:


1. **Pricebook-driven membership billing.** ServiceTitan and Housecall Pro can price a tiered membership as a catalog item, apply member discounts automatically at the point of sale, and reconcile it against the invoice. If your agreements are SKUs with discount logic, that is deep, tested machinery you should not rebuild in a board.
2. **Residential campaign attribution.** Packaged platforms tie membership sales to the marketing channel that produced them and to the technician who sold them. If you pay for lead generation and run tech-sold membership incentives, that reporting is the product.
3. **Integrated payments and financing at the door.** Card-on-file recurring charges, automated dunning, and consumer financing offers live inside the same system the tech is already holding. For high-ticket residential replacement work, that closes jobs.


Taskade Genesis does none of those three. It is a living workspace platform, not a residential field-service suite with a payment rail.
