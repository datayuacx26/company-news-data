## 📜 Why PM Contracts Die in Spreadsheets


Preventive maintenance is the most predictable revenue a service business has, and it is the revenue most often lost to a file nobody opens. The failure is structural, not lazy:


- **Renewal dates live in a tab dispatch never reads.** The office schedules from today's job list. A renewal three months out is invisible in that view.
- **Equipment notes sit in last year's email.** The tech who did the visit remembers the filter size. That memory leaves with the truck.
- **Seasonal tune-ups batch into chaos.** Nothing flags March furnace contracts in March, so 60 visits pile into one week in September.
- **Customers cancel because they forgot they had a contract.** No touchpoint between visits means the renewal conversation starts cold.
- **Two systems disagree.** The billing sheet says quarterly, the calendar says annual, and nobody knows which one is true.


Three things changed for small crews recently, and they explain why the spreadsheet stopped being good enough:


1. **PM became the margin, not the extra.** As new-install competition tightened, recurring agreements went from nice-to-have to the thing that funds slow months. Losing a renewal now costs real money, so tracking it casually stopped being acceptable.
2. **Enterprise suites moved PM up-tier.** Membership and recurring-service modules commonly sit above the entry plan in packaged field-service tools. A shop that wants PM discipline gets quoted the tier that also includes a call-center feature set it will never open.
3. **App generation collapsed the cost of a custom board.** In 2026 you describe a schedule board in a sentence and get a working app with your own fields. That moves the buying question from "which suite?" to "do we rent a schema or own one?"

## 🛟 Who Should Stay on ServiceTitan, Jobber, or Housecall Pro


These are good products. They are not the wrong answer because we sell something else. Stay when the packaged depth **is** the thing you operate:


**Stay on ServiceTitan when** memberships are a real product line with tiered pricebooks, when call-center booking and marketing attribution on every renewal drive your growth, and when you have office staff whose job is running the system. **Stay on Jobber when** you want good defaults on day one with no build step. Quoting, scheduling, invoicing, and client communication arrive already wired together, and for a lot of small crews that packaged coherence beats flexibility. Its recurring job templates handle straightforward PM cadences well.


**Stay on Housecall Pro when** residential booking, payment collection, and review generation are your funnel. Consumer-facing booking flow and card handling are mature there, and rebuilding a payments experience is not a weekend project.


**Stay on a per-seat CMMS when** parts inventory with costing and purchase orders, meter-based or condition-based intervals, or a certified offline mobile client for dead-zone techs are non-negotiable. Taskade Genesis has none of those.

## 🌡️ A Season in the Life: HVAC PM, March to October


**Early March.** The board filters to annual furnace agreements with a next-due date before fall. Forty-one rows. Nobody had to remember; the field sorted them.


**Mid March.** Office groups those rows by zone. Eleven are within four miles of each other, so they become two batch days instead of eleven separate truck rolls.


**April.** Confirmations go out through an[appointment reminder automation](https://www.taskade.com/automate/booking/appointment-reminder) . Six customers reschedule inside the same window. The board reflects the new dates, so the portal does too.


**Day of visit.** The row moves to the[HVAC daily dispatch board](https://www.taskade.com/generate/dispatch/hvac-daily-dispatch-board) with a tech and a window attached. The tech opens the record and already knows the filter size.


**On site.** A twelve-year-old condenser is flagged. The tech starts an[estimate to invoice](https://www.taskade.com/generate/invoice/estimate-to-invoice) record from the visit instead of writing it on a napkin.


**After visit.** Equipment notes update, next due date stamps forward, and a[review request](https://www.taskade.com/generate/crm/post-job-review-request-board) fires for the customers who were happy.


**45 days before contract end.** A renewal email drafts itself for the office to review and send. Nobody cancels because they forgot they had a contract.


**October.** The owner opens one board. Renewals closed, visits completed, overdue count, and upsell revenue traced back to PM visits — without exporting anything.

## ❓ FAQ


Can clients see upcoming PM visits?


Yes, through a[home services customer portal](https://www.taskade.com/generate/client-portals/home-services-portal) . Homeowners check their next tune-up window without calling the office, and because the portal reads the same board the office schedules against, a rescheduled visit shows the current window instead of a stale date.


How is this different from the recurring service agreement tracker?


The PM schedule board focuses on visit dates and equipment: model, serial, filter size, next due date. The[recurring service agreement tracker](https://www.taskade.com/generate/booking-system/recurring-service-agreement-tracker) focuses on contract billing rhythm and renewal dollars. Link both to the same customer and the office sees the next visit and the money behind it without opening two systems.


Does Taskade Genesis handle automatic billing?


Billing typically wires through Stripe or QuickBooks beside the board. Taskade Genesis tracks contract status and automates renewal reminders. It does not replace your accounting general ledger. Pair it with the agreement tracker for rhythm and the[estimate to invoice pipeline](https://www.taskade.com/generate/invoice/estimate-to-invoice) when a visit turns up upsell work.


Can I run PM and emergency dispatch on one system?


Yes. PM due dates feed the[HVAC daily dispatch board](https://www.taskade.com/generate/dispatch/hvac-daily-dispatch-board) while emergency no-cool calls stay on today's view. PM visits batch by zone on slower days. Both boards link to the same customer record, and a[dispatch triage agent](https://www.taskade.com/agents/field-service/dispatch-triage-agent) can sort urgent calls against already-booked windows.


Do I need to code to build a PM schedule board?


No. You describe the board in plain language and Taskade Genesis generates it with contract dates, visit frequency, equipment notes, and next due date. Office staff edit fields directly. Reshaping a field later is a chat message to Taskade EVE, not a support ticket. Read[living software](https://www.taskade.com/wiki/genesis/living-software) for how apps keep changing after they ship.


What does running a PM schedule board cost?


Taskade is free to start. New accounts get a one-time 6,000 credit grant — 1,000 at signup plus 5,000 with your first build — which is enough to generate a preventive maintenance board and look at real fields before paying anything. Paid plans are Pro at $10 per month billed annually ($20 billed monthly) and Business at $25 per month billed annually ($50 billed monthly). There is no per-technician meter.


Will I lose my maintenance history if I switch?


Not if you run the two-week parallel period instead of cutting over on an emergency. New contracts enter Taskade Genesis only, and the old sheet or suite stays read-only so past visits remain reachable. Load ten real contracts, get office feedback, and only then cancel anything.


Can we keep our current suite for billing and use Taskade Genesis for the board?


Yes, and dual-running is common. Put PM visibility, equipment memory, and customer-facing windows on the board. Keep membership billing and payments where they already work. Avoid double entry by naming one authority per noun: the board owns visit status, the suite owns the invoice of record.


Where do inspections and safety rounds fit?


On a[field inspection audit board](https://www.taskade.com/generate/dispatch/field-inspection-audit-board) , linked to equipment where possible. That is usually the second surface small crews add after the schedule board, especially when an insurer or commercial customer wants an audit trail. Fleet-side checks belong on a[fleet audit board](https://www.taskade.com/generate/dispatch/fleet-audit-board) .


What if PM keeps losing to reactive work?


Separate the queues. Keep callbacks on a[callback queue board](https://www.taskade.com/generate/dispatch/callback-queue-board) so they stop eating the PM windows, and give the crew a[daily run sheet](https://www.taskade.com/generate/timesheet/daily-crew-timesheet-generator) that shows both. When PM visits and emergencies share one undifferentiated list, PM always loses.
