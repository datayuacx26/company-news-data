## What Agencies Actually Need From a Retainer Tracker


A retainer tracker has one job: make the gap between agreed scope and delivered work visible while you can still price it. Most agencies do not need a full professional services automation suite for that. They need six things that live in the same place:


1. A client record with the retainer tier and the monthly or quarterly cap.
2. A request queue with a date on every ask, whoever asked.
3. An in-scope flag so nobody argues about it in week four.
4. A change order with a price and a dated client approval.
5. A running hours number the account manager can read mid-month.
6. A client-facing view that shows status without showing margin.


That spine is a scope board. It is not a payroll-grade time-billing rail, and it is not an accounting ledger. Confusing the three is how agencies end up paying for two per-seat tools and still losing the argument at month-end.

## Why Scope Creep Exists in the First Place


Scope creep is not a client character flaw. It is a structural gap between how retainers are sold and how work arrives.


Retainers are sold as a bundle: a monthly fee, a list of included deliverables, and an hours cap that nobody expects to be counted precisely. That framing is what makes retainers attractive to buy. It is also what makes them leak, because three things happen after the contract is signed:


1. **The request surface multiplies.** The contract names one point of contact. Reality gives you a marketing lead in email, a founder in Slack, and a product manager who tags your designer directly. Each channel is invisible to the others.
2. **Small asks are individually reasonable.** No single "can you swap this headline" justifies a change order. Twenty of them consume a designer's week. Nobody notices because no single request was ever priced.
3. **The cap is measured after the fact.** Hours land in a time tracker that the client cannot see and the account manager checks at invoicing. By then the money is spent and the only remaining move is an awkward conversation.


The result is a predictable failure shape. Work that never touched a board becomes invisible labor, and invisible labor becomes a month-end invoice dispute.

## Who Should Stay on Harvest, Productive, or a PSA Suite


These tools are not weak. They are purpose-built, and three of their strengths are genuinely hard to replicate:


- **Harvest owns the hours-to-invoice rail.** Timers, per-person billable rates, retainer invoicing, and clean export into QuickBooks and Xero. If your bookkeeper's month closes inside that pipeline, do not move it. Put the scope board beside it.
- **Productive models agency profitability properly.** Cost rates versus bill rates, budget burn, forecasted revenue, and per-service margin in one suite. If you need to answer "which service line is actually profitable" with defensible numbers, that is what the product is for.
- **Toggl Track is excellent at rate depth and reporting.** Billable rate hierarchies and approval flows that a finance lead already trusts. Switching those away from a working setup is downside risk with no upside.


Stay put when any of these are true:


- **Payroll or contractor payouts depend on approved timesheets** inside one system.
- **Cost-rate profitability reporting** is a board-level number, not a curiosity.
- You are **mid-rollout with training sunk** and adoption is finally sticking.
- Your finance team needs a **vendor audit trail** they already reconcile against.
- **Multi-currency invoicing and tax handling** are part of the product you buy.

## Migration Checklist: Spreadsheet or PSA to a Retainer Board


Run a parallel period. Do not cut over the week an invoice is due.


### Week 0: Decide the nouns


- Name the four nouns you cannot lose: **client, period, request, approval**
- Note the optional ones: requester, deliverable, change order price, renewal risk
- Write down the tools that stay: time tracker, accounting, e-signature
- Pick one system as the authority per noun. The board owns scope status. Your time tool owns billable hours


### Week 1: Generate and load real records


- Generate the[agency retainer scope tracker](https://www.taskade.com/generate/agency-workflows/agency-retainer-scope-tracker)
- Load your **three noisiest retainers** , not the easy ones
- Backfill open requests from Slack and email with their real dates
- Mirror the language your team already says for statuses
- Invite account managers first, delivery team second


### Week 2: Portal, automations, agent


- Invite **one** friendly client to the request portal
- Turn on a single automation: the 80 percent of cap alert
- Add the change order approval column and test it with a real ask
- Add a scope agent only after the board holds a full period of data
- Freeze the old spreadsheet for new rows. Keep it readable for history

## FAQ: Retainer Scope Tracking for Agencies


Can clients approve change orders in the portal?


Yes. Add an approval column your client App Users toggle when they accept added scope, and let an automation notify the account manager when it lands. Because the approval sits next to the original request and the in-scope flag, both sides read the same dated record instead of searching Slack at month-end.


Does Taskade Genesis replace Harvest for billing?


Not necessarily. Taskade Genesis tracks scope, deliverables, requests, and approvals. Harvest still wins payroll-grade time export into QuickBooks and Xero. Many agencies run Taskade Genesis for client-facing scope and Harvest for billing during a transition, and plenty keep both permanently. A parallel period is the low-risk way to decide.


How do I handle multiple retainer tiers?


Add a tier field and filter views per package. Smaller retainers see fewer included deliverables on the same board template, so you maintain one schema instead of a board per client. The tracker already carries tier, period, hours consumed, and the deliverable checklist, which makes a new tier a filter plus a cap value rather than a rebuild.


What about project-based work outside retainers?


Add a deal type field marking each record as retainer or project. Project deals then live in separate filtered views, or link out to your existing PM tool. Keeping both in one tracker lets account managers see total committed hours per client, which is exactly where creep hides when a project runs beside an active retainer.


How do I stop requests arriving in Slack?


You do not stop them, you re-route them. Publish an intake form as the one link you give clients, then answer every Slack ask with that link once. A[client intake form](https://www.taskade.com/generate/forms/client-intake-form) or the[creative request queue portal](https://www.taskade.com/generate/agency-workflows/creative-request-queue-portal) does this in a day. Reroute the habit, do not fight it.


Can an agent decide what is in scope?


An agent should propose, and a human should confirm. Train it on the deliverable list and past change orders so it reads your actual contracts, then let it draft the verdict and the change order language. Approving scope is a commercial decision and belongs to a person, which is why the approval column exists.


How is this different from a general PM tool with custom fields?


A PM tool gives you fields. It does not give you a client-facing approval surface, an agent reading those fields, and automations that fire before the cap is gone. Do clients need a paid seat to see their requests?


No. Clients arrive as App Users on a published app, which is a separate audience from your workspace members. Published app end users and AI credit usage follow Taskade plan rules, so confirm current limits for your client count on the[pricing page](https://www.taskade.com/pricing) .

What if creep is really a staffing problem?


Sometimes the cap is fine and the team is simply overbooked. In that case a scope board will document the pain without fixing it.
