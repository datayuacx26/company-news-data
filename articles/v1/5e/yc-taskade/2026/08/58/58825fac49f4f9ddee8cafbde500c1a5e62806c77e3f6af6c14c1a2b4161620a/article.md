## 📜 Where the Per-Tech Seat Tax Came From


Field-service software grew up solving a real problem: paper work orders lost in truck cabs. Charging per technician made sense when every tech needed a licensed mobile app, when the vendor funded GPS breadcrumbs and a price book, and when the alternative was a clipboard.


Three things changed for small crews by 2026:


1. **Office staff need write access too.** Dispatchers, bookkeepers, and the owner's spouse all touch the schedule. Seat-capped tiers push you up a tier or into per-user add-ons long before you hire a fifth tech.
2. **The real system of record is already a group chat.** Most shops under ten trucks coordinate in SMS and a spreadsheet. Paying a licensed seat for someone who updates a status twice a day feels like a tax on visibility, not a tool.
3. **Generating a custom board got cheap.** You can describe a work-order app in plain language and have it deployed the same afternoon, then rename a column without filing a support ticket. That moves the decision from "which residential brand?" to "do we rent a schema or own one?"


None of that makes Housecall Pro wrong. Per-seat pricing is a business model matched to packaged depth — someone has to fund the booking widget and the review engine. Crews feel the pinch at a predictable moment: when the features they actually need sit one tier up, or when the sixth hire costs another line item before booking a first job.


That loop is what an owned board is built to hold end to end. GPS trails and payment processing are adjacent systems that plug in beside it.

## 🛟 Who Should Stay on Housecall Pro


Housecall Pro is not a weak product with a pricing problem. It is a strong product aimed at a specific buyer. Stay when that buyer is you:


- **Consumer booking is your funnel.** The online booking widget, branded reminders, and homeowner-facing polish are tuned for residential conversion in a way a board you build yourself will not match on day one.
- **Review marketing is your growth engine.** Automated review requests wired into a reputation dashboard is a real product, not a checkbox. If Google reviews drive your phone volume, that packaging earns its price.
- **You want payments in the same contract.** Card-on-file, financing offers at the door, and payouts inside one product remove a wiring project you may not want.
- **You refuse to configure anything.** Some owners want screens that arrive finished. That is a legitimate preference and Housecall Pro respects it.
- **Your techs work in dead zones** and you rely on a mature offline mobile app plus GPS breadcrumbs.
- **You are mid-rollout** with training sunk and adoption finally sticking. Do not restart a rollout to save $60 a month.

## 🗄️ The Data Model Behind a Small-Crew Board


Most dispatch failures are modeling failures, not software failures. If equipment hangs off the job instead of the customer, you lose history the moment the job closes. Model it once and the board answers questions you did not think to ask.


Three modeling rules that save you a rebuild:


1. **Equipment belongs to the customer, not the job.** A twelve-year-old water heater outlives forty service calls. Hang it off the address and every future tech inherits the history.
2. **Estimates convert, they do not duplicate.** One record moves quote to job to invoice so nobody re-types a price. That is what the[estimate to invoice pipeline](https://www.taskade.com/generate/invoice/estimate-to-invoice) and[job to invoice flow](https://www.taskade.com/generate/invoice/job-to-invoice) exist for.
3. **Recurring agreements are their own record.** A maintenance contract is a promise; a visit is a truck roll. Keep them separate with a[recurring service agreement tracker](https://www.taskade.com/generate/booking-system/recurring-service-agreement-tracker) so the daily board stays readable.


Set the fields up once in[table view](https://www.taskade.com/learn/projects/table-view) , then read the same records in[board view](https://www.taskade.com/learn/projects/board-view) for dispatch or[calendar view](https://www.taskade.com/learn/projects/calendar-view) for windows. Taskade ships 7 project views — List, Board, Calendar, Table, Mind Map, Gantt, and Org Chart, with Timeline part of Gantt — over the same underlying records, so office staff and techs read the shape that suits them without duplicating data. The full tour is in[project views](https://www.taskade.com/learn/projects/project-views) .

Taskade Genesis for an owned dispatch board and portal. Jobber for packaged home-services defaults with different quoting and payment behavior. Housecall Pro when review marketing and consumer booking already drive revenue — do not leave a working growth engine to save a subscription line.


Can I import Housecall Pro data into Taskade Genesis?

Does Taskade Genesis replace Housecall Pro review automation?


Not one-for-one on day one. How do office staff and techs share one board?

How do customers get status without a workspace seat?
