## 📜 Origin: Why Dispatch Became a Seat-Tax Problem


Field service software priced itself per technician because that is how the value used to arrive. The vendor funded a rugged mobile app, a GPS trail, a price book, and a payments rail — and every tech needed a licensed login to touch any of it. Per-seat pricing was an honest match to packaged depth.


Three things changed for small shops between 2020 and 2026.


**Everyone became a user.** The office manager, the part-time dispatcher, the owner's spouse doing invoices on Sunday, the sub who covers refrigeration — all of them need write access to the same board. Vendors added free "requester" or "read-only" tiers, which helped, but paid full seats still stack the moment three people need to change a status.


**Chat and spreadsheets became the real system of record.** Most shops already coordinate in SMS and a shared sheet. Buying a full license for someone who updates two rows a day feels like a tax on visibility rather than a purchase of capability.


**Generating a working app got cheap.** In 2026 an owner can describe a dispatch board in a sentence and get a running app with a database, statuses, and automations behind it. That moves the buying question from "which suite?" to "do we rent a schema or own one?"


The seat tax is not a moral failing at Jobber or ServiceTitan. It is a business model matched to a product with real depth. Operators feel it at a specific moment: when the entry tier fits four people and hurts at eleven, or when the one feature they need lives two tiers up.


That loop is what owned dispatch is for. Parts warehouses and GPS trails are adjacent systems that should stay adjacent.

## 📋 Migration Checklist: Spreadsheet or FSM to Owned Dispatch


Run a two-week parallel period. Never cut over on a Monday emergency.


### Week 0 — decide the nouns


- Name the five you cannot lose: **customer, site, job, assignee, status**
- Note the optional ones: asset, arrival window, callback parent, invoice number
- Write down what stays put: payments, payroll, GPS, accounting
- Export customers and open jobs to CSV from the incumbent


### Week 1 — generate and load truth


- Generate the[dispatch board](https://www.taskade.com/generate/dispatch/crew-work-orders)
- [Import the CSV](https://www.taskade.com/learn/import/csv-import) — 15 to 30 **real** open jobs, no "Customer A"
- Mirror your spoken status language exactly
- Set[permissions](https://www.taskade.com/learn/projects/permissions) using the 7 role levels, Owner through Viewer
- Invite the office and leads first, techs second


### Week 2 — portal, money, automation


- [Publish](https://www.taskade.com/learn/genesis/publishing) a[client portal](https://www.taskade.com/generate/client-portals/home-services-customer-portal) , scoped per client
- Link an[invoice board](https://www.taskade.com/generate/invoice/job-to-invoice) to closed jobs
- Turn on **one** automation — overdue ping or emergency escalation
- Add[AR aging](https://www.taskade.com/generate/invoice/ar-aging-board) once invoices flow
- Add a[dispatch triage agent](https://www.taskade.com/agents/field-service/dispatch-triage-agent) only after statuses are trusted
- Freeze the old sheet for new work; keep it read-only for history

## 🧪 A Day in the Life on an Owned Dispatch Board


**06:50** The overnight[after-hours log](https://www.taskade.com/generate/dispatch/after-hours-callout-dispatch-log) shows two callouts. Both are already jobs with priority and site notes, not voicemails to transcribe.


**07:30** Morning huddle runs off one filtered view: today's jobs, unassigned emergencies, overdue invoices on active sites. A[run sheet agent](https://www.taskade.com/agents/field-service/runsheet-agent) drafted the summary overnight.


**09:15** A no-heat call comes in. Intake becomes a job. The[triage agent](https://www.taskade.com/agents/field-service/job-triage-agent) proposes "emergency / heating / Zone 3" and a nearest-available tech. A human confirms — always a human.


**11:40** A tech marks "waiting parts" and notes the model number. A[parts risk agent](https://www.taskade.com/agents/field-service/parts-risk-agent) flags that the same part stalled two jobs last month.


**14:20** Job closes with photos. An automation notifies the client through the portal and creates the invoice row. Cash flow tracks completion instead of trailing it by a week.


**16:45** The owner opens one screen. Open count, waiting-parts count, tomorrow's windows, and AR over thirty days — no export, no pivot table.


**17:30** A callback arrives on last week's job. It links to the parent, so the tech sees the full history before leaving the shop. That single link is the difference between a warranty visit and a lost customer.


None of that requires GPS breadcrumbs or a parts ledger inside the board. It requires a board people trust.

## 🌦️ Seasonal Demand, Warranty, and Subs


Trades swing seasonally, and owned boards absorb that without a vendor release cycle. Add a "storm response" status in October, a winter maintenance column in November, retire both in April. Ask Taskade EVE to add a freeze-risk checklist to every boiler job this week and it is done before the next call.


**Warranty and callbacks** need visible linkage to the original job so techs see history on site. Model the parent link once, then a[callback queue board](https://www.taskade.com/generate/dispatch/callback-queue-board) and a[callback agent](https://www.taskade.com/agents/field-service/callback-agent) do the chasing. Clients see continuity in the portal instead of re-explaining the problem.


**Subcontractor handoffs** should show prime vs sub assignment and an external contact on the job row. Compliance certificates stay where they already live — visibility alone prevents most missed coordination.


**Parts and photos** without over-engineering: a text field and a checkbox on the job row prevents most return trips. Add a[parts inventory board](https://www.taskade.com/generate/inventory-management/parts-inventory-board) only when stockouts actually cost you money. Starting narrow gets you dispatching sooner.


**Recurring work** — maintenance agreements, quarterly filter changes, seasonal tune-ups — belongs on a[PM schedule board](https://www.taskade.com/generate/dispatch/pm-schedule-board) with[recurring tasks](https://www.taskade.com/learn/projects/recurring-tasks) , not a calendar one person owns privately.[Recurring service agreements](https://www.taskade.com/blog/recurring-service-agreements) covers the revenue side.

## 📈 The Long-Term Ownership Thesis


Rental dispatch cost rises with headcount. Owned dispatch cost rises with customization time, which flattens once the board matches your trade. Pick the curve that matches your five-year hiring plan.


Three trucks planning for fifteen benefit most from owning the schema early, because the migration nobody wants is the one you do at twelve techs during peak season. The shops that regret switching are the ones who switched for price alone and discovered mid-January that they had traded away a payments rail they depended on.

## ⚠️ Common Mistakes


- **Portal before internal board.** Clients see chaos in real time. Board first, always.
- **Ignoring automations.** A board without reminders is a prettier spreadsheet that still needs manual nagging.
- **Demo data at launch.** Load real jobs or nobody trusts the board by Thursday.
- **Nine automations in week one.** Turn on one. Earn the second.
- **Modeling callbacks as new tickets.** You lose the history that prevents the second failed visit.
- **Switching to save money mid-season.** Migrate in your slow month, not during the first cold snap.
- **Franchise blind spot.** Franchisees with mandated corporate systems may not choose freely. Read your agreement before migrating off a rental suite; independent locations in loose networks are the sweet spot.
