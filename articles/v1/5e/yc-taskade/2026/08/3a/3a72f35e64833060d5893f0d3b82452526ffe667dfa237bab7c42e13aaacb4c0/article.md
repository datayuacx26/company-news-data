## 📜 Origin of the Realtor Seat-Stack Problem


Real estate CRM pricing grew up around teams and brokerages. Per-user licensing made sense when the vendor funded a dialer, a lead marketplace integration, an IDX site, and an accountability dashboard for a team lead who needed to see who was slacking.


Then three things shifted under solo agents:


1. **Lead sources fragmented.** Zillow, referrals, open houses, social DMs, and a personal site all produce leads now. Solo agents need one intake board more than they need routing rules, because every route ends at the same phone.
2. **Speed-to-lead became a clock problem, not a staffing problem.** Teams solve first-touch with an inside sales pod. A solo agent solves it with automation, because they are standing in someone's kitchen when the lead comes in.
3. **App builders collapsed the cost of a custom pipeline.** In 2026 you can generate a CRM from a prompt and rename stages to match your market that afternoon. That moves the buy question from "which realtor CRM brand?" to "do we rent a schema or own one?"


The seat stack is not a moral failure by Follow Up Boss. It is a business model matched to packaged team depth. Solo agents feel it when the per-user price is fine for a team of eight and irritating for a team of one, or when the feature they need lives one tier up next to nine they will never open.


The red branch is the entire product category. Everything Follow Up Boss, LionDesk, and an owned Taskade Genesis pipeline compete on is preventing that one arrow.

## 🛟 Who Should Stay on Follow Up Boss


Stay when the packaged team product is genuinely the product you operate. Follow Up Boss has three real strengths worth naming honestly:


1. **Team inbox and lead routing are mature.** Round-robin, ISA pods, market-based queues, and reassignment rules are solved problems there. If you have people to route to, that logic is worth paying for and tedious to rebuild.
2. **Speed-to-lead is cultural, not just a feature.** The product is opinionated about calling fast, and the reporting reinforces it. Teams that adopted it changed behavior, not just software.
3. **The realtor integration surface is deep and already paid for.** Portal connections, dialers, and lead-source plumbing that agents already subscribe to plug in without a project.


Stay when:


- You have **three or more people** who need write access and accountability views.
- You depend on **lead routing rules** and reassignment across a roster.
- Your **dialer and lead-source integrations** are load-bearing daily.
- You are **mid-rollout with training sunk** and adoption is finally sticking.

## 🚪 Open Houses, Sphere, and Compliance


**Open house leads belong in the same pipeline as web leads.** Tag the source` open_house` so nurture copy can reference the property, and text back within minutes while the kitchen is still fresh in memory. ## 🧹 CRM Hygiene for One Person


An owned pipeline degrades the same way a rented one does. Solo agents fail quietly when the board becomes a junk drawer nobody trusts, at which point the phone-notes habit returns.


- **One phone number per person.** Duplicates are how two agents text the same lead — even when both agents are you.
- **Merge duplicates weekly.** Ten minutes on Friday beats a spring-market mess.
- **Archive nurture away from hot leads.** A 900-row board with 12 live opportunities is a board you stop opening.
- **Delete dead portal contacts.** Importing five years of them was the mistake; keeping them is the compound interest.
- **Rename one confusing field per month.** That is the whole advantage of owning the schema — use it.


Part-time agents need this more, not less. When showing hours are limited to evenings and weekends, automation covers W-2 hours and hygiene keeps the board readable when you finally open it.
