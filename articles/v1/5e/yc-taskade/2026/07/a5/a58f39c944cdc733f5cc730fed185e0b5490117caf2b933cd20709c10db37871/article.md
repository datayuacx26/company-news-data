## Why Course Platforms Never Modeled Admissions


Kajabi, Teachable, and Thinkific grew up around one buyer: the solo creator selling evergreen video at scale. That buyer's whole business is landing page, checkout, modules, email sequence. Enrollment ops were never the product, and three shifts made the mismatch loud.


1. **Cohorts came back.** Live programs, bootcamps, tutoring pods, and after-school academies sell seats, not downloads. Seats have capacity, waitlists, and start dates. A cart models "in stock," not "12 of 14 filled, two on waitlist."
2. **Parents became a second audience.** In K-12 and youth programs the buyer and the learner are different people. Parents want schedules, forms, and announcements. A course login shows lessons, not the enrollment status a parent is actually asking about.
3. **Building a custom portal got cheap.** In 2026 you can describe an enrollment app and get a working one, then reshape fields by chatting with[Taskade EVE](https://www.taskade.com/learn/genesis/education) instead of filing a feature request. That moves the decision from "which course platform?" to "do we rent a schema or own one?"


None of that makes Kajabi wrong. It makes it a **content commerce platform** being asked to do **admissions operations** . The workarounds are familiar: waitlists in Sheets, parent questions in email, staff review in a separate notes tool, and a "student record" that is really three tabs.

## The Application Lifecycle, as States


Most enrollment pain is a missing state. Families assume silence means rejection, staff cannot tell "waiting on us" from "waiting on them," and nobody knows how many seats are truly free. Name the states explicitly and the portal answers those questions without a phone call.


Two rules make this work in practice. **Waitlisted is a visible state, not a silence.** Families should see their position, not guess. And **DocsPending belongs to the family, not to staff** , so your "waiting on us" queue stays honest and small.

## Waitlist and Capacity Logic


Popular programs need visible waitlist status so families do not read silence as rejection. Staff views should show seats remaining per cohort. Automations can offer the next waitlisted family when a seat opens, with a response deadline, so the offer moves down the list instead of stalling on one unread email. Course carts rarely model waitlist ethics well because a cart's only two states are "buy" and "sold out."


## Parent Communication Without Email Overload


Parents want one place for schedule changes, not scattered threads. Portal announcements plus optional SMS for urgent closures beat weekly newsletter blasts. Scope messages per cohort so parents only see relevant updates, and let a[weekly progress digest](https://www.taskade.com/automate/education/cohort-weekly-progress-digest) carry routine news so your urgent channel stays urgent.

## Migration Checklist: Kajabi or Spreadsheets to an Owned Portal


Run a two-week parallel period. Do not cut over the week applications open.


### Week 0: Decide the nouns


- Name the six you cannot lose: **family, student, application, cohort, enrollment, document**
- List the custom fields your program actually reviews on
- Write down what stays put: video host, payment processor, accounting, SIS
- Decide the visibility matrix above, in writing


### Week 1: Generate and load truth


- Generate the[student and parent portal](https://www.taskade.com/generate/education/student-parent-portal-generator)
- Load **10 to 20 real applications** , not sample "Student A" rows
- Mirror the status language your staff already say out loud
- Add the[application form](https://www.taskade.com/generate/forms/application-form) and one[liability release form](https://www.taskade.com/generate/forms/liability-release-form)
- Test a parent login with your own email address before anyone else sees it
- Invite admissions first, instructors later


### Week 2: Automations, then agents


- Turn on **one** automation: intake acknowledgment
- Add the waitlist seat offer only after capacity numbers are trusted
- Add a[cohort attendance board](https://www.taskade.com/generate/education/cohort-attendance-board) if the term is close
- Add a review agent only once statuses are stable
- Freeze the old sheet for new applications; keep it read-only for history


### Cutover rules


- New applications enter the portal only. Nothing else counts as submitted.
- Payments stay where they are; the portal holds status, not the card.
- Video stays where it is; the portal holds the link beside the student record.
- One system is the authority per noun. The portal owns enrollment status. The processor owns money.

## A Day in the Life on an Enrollment Board


**07:40** Two applications landed overnight. The board shows both, one flagged for a missing immunization form.


**08:15** A parent replies with a phone photo of the form. It lands on the student record, and the flag clears without an email thread.


**09:30** Admissions works the review column. Internal interview notes stay internal; the family sees only "under review."


**11:00** A spring cohort hits capacity. The next waitlisted family gets a seat offer with a 48-hour deadline, automatically.


**14:00** An instructor opens the term roster. Allergies and emergency contacts are there. Financial and interview notes are not.


**16:30** The director opens one board: applications this week, seats remaining per cohort, documents outstanding. No export, no spreadsheet reconciliation.


That day needs no video CDN and no payment gateway inside the portal. It needs a roster people trust and a visibility model nobody has to think about.


## Seasonal Enrollment Cycles


Run spring and fall intakes as separate cohorts or filtered views on the same schema. Clone last season's portal, update dates and capacity, archive graduated students into an alumni state. Because the schema is yours, next term is a copy, not a rebuild. Pair a[cohort progress dashboard](https://www.taskade.com/generate/education/cohort-progress-dashboard-generator) or a[student study dashboard](https://www.taskade.com/generate/dashboards/student-study-dashboard) when you want the term-in-flight view.


## After Enrollment: Ongoing Portal Value


Enrollment is day one, not the only job. Parents return for schedules, forms, and messages. Portals that go dark after acceptance feel abandoned, and the next enrollment cycle starts from zero trust. Keep attendance, progress, and announcements live through the program year with a[student progress and grade tracker](https://www.taskade.com/templates/education/student-progress-grade-tracker) , and the portal becomes the reason families re-enroll without a sales push.


## Document Uploads During Intake


Medical forms, waivers, and emergency contacts arrive as PDFs and phone photos. Accept uploads on the student record with a staff review flag, and families get one upload surface instead of attachments lost in threads. Collect what review actually requires and nothing more, and keep sensitive fields out of any view a non-admissions role can open.


## Accessibility and Mobile-First Parents


Parents enroll from phones in the pickup line. Forms must be thumb-friendly, and the portal login must work without a desktop. Test Android and iOS before launch. Published Taskade Genesis apps inherit responsive layouts, but verify your own custom fields do not break mobile grids, especially wide tables and long select lists.


## Handoff to Instructors


When enrollment closes, instructors need the roster without admissions notes. Filtered views and role scoping prevent oversharing medical or financial intake data. Design that boundary before the first real application arrives, and the handoff is a permission change rather than a manual export.


## Common Enrollment Portal Mistakes


- **Parent portal before the staff queue works.** Build the internal pipeline first. A family-facing surface on top of an untrusted queue amplifies confusion.
- **Over-collecting personal information on public forms.** Gather what review requires, then ask for the rest after acceptance.
- **No waitlist state.** Popular cohorts need a visible waitlist. Silence reads as rejection and costs you re-applications next term.
- **Agents on empty rows.** Wait until real applications exist before you add review agents.
- **Two systems claiming the same noun.** If both a sheet and the portal hold enrollment status, neither is true.
- **Skipping the parent login test.** Log in as a parent with your own email before opening enrollment. It is the fastest way to find an oversharing view.
