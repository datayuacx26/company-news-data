## Where the Per-Tech Seat Problem Came From


Field-service software grew up billing per licensed technician, and for good reason. The vendor funded a real mobile app, native card processing, SMS delivery, and a support desk. Charging per tech matched who consumed that value.


Three things shifted for small crews:


1. **Office staff became board users too.** A dispatcher, a bookkeeper, and an owner all need write access. Seat-capped tiers price for techs, then the office pushes you into the next tier or into per-extra-user fees.
2. **The board became the product.** Most owners open the same three screens daily: today's jobs, who owes money, and which quote is stale. Everything else is a tier they pay for and never open.
3. **Generating a custom board got cheap.** In 2026 you can describe a work-order app in plain language and reshape fields yourself. That moves the buying question from "which suite?" to "do we rent a schema or own one?"


None of that makes Jobber wrong. Per-seat pricing is a fair trade for packaged depth. It just stings in a specific place: when the tier you need for one feature drags along nine you do not, and when hiring a seasonal tech changes your software bill.


Every arrow in that loop is a column on a board or an automation on a status change. That is the layer Taskade Genesis is built to own. Fleet GPS trails and native card processing are adjacent systems.

## When Jobber Is Still the Right Call


Jobber earned its position, and three of its strengths are genuinely hard to replicate with an owned board:


1. **Payments are native and boring, in the best way.** Card and ACH collection sits inside the same screens as the invoice. No provider selection, no webhook wiring, no reconciliation project.
2. **Client communication ships configured.** Appointment reminders, on-my-way texts, and follow-ups work on install day rather than after you design an automation.
3. **Onboarding is measured in days.** Residential trades with standard job shapes can be dispatching by the end of the week without deciding what a status column should be called.


Stay on Jobber when:


- Native card processing and SMS bundles are non-negotiable this quarter.
- Nobody on the team wants to design a schema, and the defaults already fit.
- Training is sunk and adoption is finally sticking.
- Your accountant or franchise mandates the stack.

## Migration Path Off Jobber


Run a two-week parallel period. Do not cut over on a Monday emergency.


### Week 0: name the nouns


- Write down the four you cannot lose: **customer, job, assignee, invoice status**
- Note the optional ones: quote, visit window, access notes, photo set

- Freeze the old tool for new work, keep it readable for history

## Training Techs on a New Board


Techs adopt tools that answer three questions fast: where am I going, what is wrong, and how do I close the job. So:


- Hide admin fields from the tech view.
- Pin mobile-friendly views per crew.
- Use plain status names. "On site" beats "IN_PROGRESS_2".
- Show one screen on day one, not the whole workspace.
