## 📜 Why the Spreadsheet-and-CC Problem Exists


Transaction coordination grew up inside brokerages, so the tooling grew up around brokerage compliance. The vendor's buyer was the broker who needed an audit trail, not the independent coordinator serving five agents across three brokerages.


Three things pulled coordinators sideways into spreadsheets:


1. **Coordinators serve multiple brokerages.** An independent TC may touch Dotloop at one shop, SkySlope at another, and email at a third. Nobody wants to relearn five suites, so the shared truth drifts into a spreadsheet the TC controls.
2. **Compliance suites optimize the file, not the week.** A loop is excellent at holding one transaction's documents. It is weaker at answering "what closes in the next seven days and what is missing?" across every file at once.
3. **Client visibility was an afterthought.** Buyers ask "where are we?" by text. Coordinators answer by hand, hundreds of times a month, because the compliance suite was never designed as a client-facing status page.


So the real incumbent is not Dotloop or SkySlope. It is a spreadsheet plus a CC-heavy inbox, and it breaks the first time two assistants edit different copies of the same closing date.


That loop is what an owned coordinator board is built to run. E-signature envelopes and brokerage archives are adjacent systems.

## 📦 Migration Checklist: Spreadsheet or Suite to an Owned Board


Run a two-week parallel period. Do not cut over the week three files close.


### Week 0: Decide the nouns


- Name the four nouns you cannot lose: **deal, milestone, document, party**
- Note optional nouns: earnest money status, lender, title company, portal audience
- Write down what stays put: e-sign, brokerage archive, accounting, storage
- Pick your one metric for "this worked" (usually: zero missed contingency dates)


### Week 1: Generate and load real files


- Generate the[transaction coordinator CRM](https://www.taskade.com/generate/crm/transaction-coordinator-crm)
- Load **10 to 20 real active files** , not demo "123 Main St"
- Mirror the stage language your agents already say ("Pending inspection", "Clear to close")
- Add only the custom fields your niche actually needs
- Invite the coordinator and one trusted agent before anyone else


### Week 2: Deadlines, portal, automations


- Add a[contingency deadline board](https://www.taskade.com/generate/real-estate-crm/contingency-deadline-os) view
- Turn on **one** automation: the three-day pre-inspection nudge
- Publish the portal for **one** friendly client and ask what confused them
- Add a coordinator agent only after statuses are trusted
- Freeze the spreadsheet for new files; keep it read-only for history


### Cutover rules


- New files enter the board only. No exceptions, or you get two truths again.
- Executed documents still live where e-sign puts them; the board links to them.
- One system is the authority per noun: the board owns milestone status, the e-sign tool owns the executed envelope.

## ⚠️ Common Mistakes Coordinators Make on a New Board


- **One portal for all parties.** Buyer and seller views need different fields. Split them.
- **Duplicate deal records.** One source of truth per property under contract, always.
- **Skipping the audit trail.** Log who changed a closing date and when. Brokers will ask.
- **Automating before the data is clean.** Notifications built on stale statuses train people to ignore them.
- **Claiming IDX or MLS on a service page.** Promise milestone visibility and document discipline, which is what you actually deliver.
- **Building 30 fields on day one.** Ship four nouns, then add fields your files prove you need.
