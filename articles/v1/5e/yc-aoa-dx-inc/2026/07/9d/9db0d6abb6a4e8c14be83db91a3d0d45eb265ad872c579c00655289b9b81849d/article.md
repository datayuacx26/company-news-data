# AOA Dx Publishes Peer-Reviewed Study Validating the Biology Behind Its Multi-Omic Approach to Earlier Ovarian Cancer Detection

**Denver, CO — July 13, 2026** — AOA Dx announced the publication of a[peer-reviewed study in](https://www.mdpi.com/2075-4418/16/14/2143)


[Diagnostics](https://www.mdpi.com/2075-4418/16/14/2143)[(MDPI)](https://www.mdpi.com/2075-4418/16/14/2143) titled “Multi-omic profiling in a symptomatic cohort identifies coordinated biomarker signatures in ovarian cancer serum.” The study represents the


*only* comprehensive integrated multi-omic analyses of ovarian cancer serum conducted in a clinically relevant symptomatic population to date and provides the biological foundation underlying AOA Dx’s AKRIVIS GD diagnostic test and its GlycoLocate discovery platform.


**Study Overview**


The study enrolled 503 participants across six clinical groups, early- and late-stage ovarian cancer, borderline tumors, benign gynecological conditions, gastrointestinal disorders, and healthy controls. This cohort was designed to reflect the real-world clinical presentation of women experiencing vague abdominal symptoms – the precise population where diagnostic tools must perform for the early detection of OC. Lipidomic, metabolomic, and protein profiling were performed within the same cohort using LC-MS and immunoassay.


**Key Findings**


- **Cancer exhibits distinct molecular signatures across lipids, metabolites, and proteins.** While each molecular layer exhibited distinct profiles in ovarian cancer compared with benign serum, integrating all three revealed biological connections linking membrane remodeling, redox balance, energy metabolism, and immune signaling.


- **No single biomarker class is enough for early detection.** Standard-of-care markers CA125 and HE4 and other proteins showed significant overlap between early-stage ovarian cancer and benign conditions in symptomatic women, confirming that single-omics cannot solve early detection. The multi-omic framework revealed biological connections between molecular classes that remain invisible to individual tests.


- **The biology points toward earlier detection.** Borderline tumors and benign conditions showed molecular profiles that fall between healthy and malignant specimens, suggesting the platform can detect a biological continuum of disease, not just a binary cancer/no-cancer signal. This has meaningful implications for risk stratification and earlier intervention.


**Advancing the Science of Early Detection**


“This study reflects years of methodical work to understand not just whether molecular signatures exist in ovarian cancer serum, but how they are organized across biological systems,” said Oriana Papin-Zoghbi, CEO and Co-Founder of AOA Dx. “The cross-omic network we’ve characterized here demonstrates that ovarian cancer rewires multiple molecular pathways simultaneously and that the most diagnostically informative signals often live in the interactions between those pathways, not within any single one. This is the scientific foundation our platform is built on, and we believe it represents a meaningful advance for the field.”


AOA Dx is continuing to build on this biological foundation, advancing the GlycoLocate platform toward clinical application across oncology indications — a direction supported by broad industry consensus. According to the TD Cowen 5th Annual Tools/Dx Revolution Conference (June 2026), 81% of surveyed investors believe multi-omics will become standard of care or take off broadly within five years, driven by improving technology, decreasing costs, and a growing body of evidence that integrating multiple biological layers delivers superior clinical performance over single-modality tests.


**About the GlycoLocate Platform**


AOA Dx is the developer of GlycoLocate, a multi-omic discovery and diagnostics platform that integrates lipidomics, metabolomics, AI-driven biomarker discovery using advanced computational biology to detect cancer-specific molecular signatures from a non-invasive blood test. GlycoLocate is designed to identify the kind of coordinated cross-omic biomarker signatures, with applications spanning discovery and diagnostic development in oncology.


**About AKRIVIS GD**


AKRIVIS GD is AOA Dx’s multi-omic blood test for early detection of ovarian cancer in symptomatic women. AKRIVIS GD combines lipidomic profiling, protein immunoassay, and machine learning to deliver an objective, blood-based signal at the point of clinical routing — when a woman presents with symptoms and her physician needs to decide whether to refer urgently or continue monitoring.

AOA Dx is transforming early cancer detection with its proprietary platform, a first-of-its-kind, multi-omics liquid biopsy that integrates lipids, metabolites, proteins and clinical data using advanced machine learning.
