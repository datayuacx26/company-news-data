# Is Green Tea Really Better Than Coffee?

Caffeine shows up in many forms, from green tea to black coffee to everyday masala chai. Each one comes with unique benefits but also potential downsides. In this episode of the podcast, Dietician Nidhi Nigam clears the confusion and explains how much is too much.


***Watch the clip here:***[Green Tea Vs. Coffee](https://youtube.com/shorts/0Fg_Aw3zLeo?feature=share)


## Why One Tea May Work for You but Not for Others


Different teas have very different effects on the body. Hibiscus tea, for example, can feel like a miracle drink for someone with high blood pressure, helping to bring levels down. But the same tea can be risky for a person with naturally low blood pressure, making them feel weak or dizzy. This shows that what works as a health booster for one person may actually cause issues for another.


## The Antioxidant Power Hidden in Your Cup


Caffeine itself is not the villain it is often made out to be. Whether you are sipping green tea, black coffee, or masala chai, you are taking in antioxidants that help protect the body from cellular damage and stress. These compounds support energy, immunity, and overall health. The real challenge is not about choosing green tea over coffee, but about how much you consume.


## When a Healthy Habit Turns into a Problem


Too much of a good thing can backfire. Green tea is often marketed as the healthiest option, but having it ten times a day is far from healthy. The same goes for coffee or regular chai. Overloading your body with caffeine can disturb your system, increase restlessness, and wipe out the benefits. A safe range, according to experts, is about two cups a day, with a maximum of three. Beyond that, you may be doing yourself more harm than good.


## Finding the Right Balance for Your Body


The key takeaway is that no single tea or coffee is universally “better.” What matters most is how your body responds and how moderate your intake is. For some, one or two cups of coffee fit easily into a healthy lifestyle. For others, switching to tea may feel lighter. The focus should always be on balance and listening to your body.
