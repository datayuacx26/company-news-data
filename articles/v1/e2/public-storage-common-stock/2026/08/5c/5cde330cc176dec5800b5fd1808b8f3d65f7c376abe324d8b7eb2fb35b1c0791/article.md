# How to Stage a Home

The best way to stage a home for sale is to clean, declutter, depersonalize, make minor repairs, improve lighting, and arrange furniture to make rooms feel open and functional. The goal is to help potential buyers focus on the home itself and imagine how they could use the space.


You don’t necessarily need to completely redecorate or spend a lot of money. Many effective home-staging strategies involve things you’ll already need to do before moving, such as packing, cleaning, and deciding what you want to keep.


## What Is Home Staging?


Home staging is the process of preparing a house or condo for potential buyers. Unlike decorating, which reflects your personal style, staging generally focuses on presenting the home’s space, layout, features, and condition as clearly as possible.


That may mean removing some furniture, packing personal belongings, reorganizing closets, or making small cosmetic updates.


## Should You Declutter Before Selling Your Home?


Yes. Decluttering is one of the simplest ways to prepare a home for sale because it can make rooms, closets, cabinets, and other storage areas easier for potential buyers to see and evaluate.


Start by clearing:


- Kitchen and bathroom countertops
- Desks and dresser tops
- Bookshelves
- Entryways and hallways
- Closets and cabinets
- Excess furniture and décor


Avoid simply moving clutter into closets or cabinets. Potential buyers may open storage spaces during a showing, so keeping those areas organized can help buyers understand how much usable storage the home offers.


Since you’ll eventually need to pack for your move, decluttering before listing can also give you a head start.


## Should You Depersonalize Your Home Before Selling?


Depersonalizing can help potential buyers focus on the home rather than the current occupants.


Consider packing away:


- Family photographs
- Children’s artwork
- Large collections
- Refrigerator magnets and papers
- Highly personal décor
- Excess books and accessories


You don’t need to remove every trace of personality. The goal is to create a clean, welcoming space that allows buyers to picture themselves living there.


## Should You Talk to a Real Estate Agent Before Staging?


A real estate agent who understands your local market may be able to recommend which staging projects are worth considering before you list your home.


Ask potential agents which changes they would prioritize, what buyers in your area tend to look for, and whether particular improvements are likely to be worthwhile for your property.


You can also look at comparable homes currently for sale in your area. Pay attention to how they’re presented, which features stand out, and how your home compares.


## What Are Some Free or Low-Cost Home Staging Ideas?


You don’t need a large staging budget to make noticeable changes. Start with a few high-impact projects.


### Deep Clean the Home


Clean floors, windows, baseboards, molding, ceiling fans, appliances, bathrooms, and other visible surfaces. Pay particular attention to areas that may be overlooked during everyday cleaning.


### Let In Natural Light


Open curtains and blinds before showings when appropriate. Clean windows and remove window treatments that unnecessarily block natural light.


Mirrors can also help reflect available light and make some spaces feel brighter.


### Improve the Flow of Each Room


Arrange furniture so people can move comfortably through the home. Removing an oversized chair, extra table, or other unnecessary furniture can make a crowded room easier to navigate and allow buyers to better understand its layout.


### Make Minor Repairs


Take care of noticeable maintenance issues you can reasonably address before listing, such as squeaky doors, dripping faucets, loose hardware, damaged caulk, or small wall imperfections.


For larger repairs or potential structural, electrical, plumbing, or other significant issues, consider consulting an appropriate professional.


### Improve Curb Appeal


The exterior is one of the first things potential buyers see. Depending on your home, simple improvements could include mowing and edging the lawn, removing debris, refreshing mulch, trimming plants, cleaning the entryway, or adding a new welcome mat.


## Should You Paint Before Selling a Home?


It depends on the condition and color of your walls. If rooms have damaged paint or highly distinctive colors, repainting may create a cleaner and more neutral backdrop for potential buyers.


You don’t necessarily need to repaint the entire home. Ask your real estate agent whether painting particular rooms is likely to be worthwhile in your market before investing the time and money.


## How Should You Stage Each Room?


Give each room a clear, easy-to-understand purpose. If a spare bedroom currently doubles as an office, workout room, and storage area, consider choosing one primary function for staging.


You can also:


- Remove furniture that makes rooms difficult to navigate.
- Keep beds neatly made.
- Hang clean towels in bathrooms.
- Limit decorative items on surfaces.
- Add a plant or simple decorative element where appropriate.
- Address noticeable odors rather than trying to cover them with strong fragrances.


The goal is not to make every room look like a showroom. It’s to make the home’s space and function easy for buyers to understand.


## Where Should You Put Your Belongings While Staging a Home?


As you declutter, separate belongings into categories: keep and use, pack for the move, donate or sell, and discard.


Items you want to keep but don’t need before moving can be packed ahead of time. If you have enough room, neatly organized boxes may be stored in an appropriate area of your home without interfering with showings.


If boxes, furniture, seasonal items, or other belongings are making rooms and closets feel crowded, a self-storage unit can provide temporary space while your home is listed.


## How Can Self Storage Help When Selling a Home?


Self storage can give you additional space to temporarily move belongings out of your home while you’re staging, showing, and preparing to move.


A storage unit can be useful for items such as:


- Extra furniture
- Packed moving boxes
- Seasonal décor
- Sports and outdoor equipment
- Collections
- Extra household items
- Belongings you want to keep but don’t need before the move


Moving some belongings into storage can make it easier to declutter rooms, organize closets, and begin packing without filling your home with boxes.
