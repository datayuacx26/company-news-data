# How Does Storage Helps Short-Term Rental Hosts Stay Organized?

Short-term rental hosts need to keep their properties clean, organized, and ready for every guest. A self-storage unit can give hosts extra space for personal belongings, seasonal decor, backup linens, supplies, furniture, and other items that don’t need to stay at the rental.


By moving excess items off-site, hosts can create more usable space for guests, simplify cleaning and turnovers, and keep frequently used supplies organized and accessible.


## **Why Is Decluttering Important for a Short-Term Rental?**


Decluttering can make a short-term rental feel cleaner, larger, and more comfortable. Guests need room for their own luggage, clothing, toiletries, and other belongings, so crowded closets, countertops, and shelves can make an otherwise well-designed rental feel cramped.


Reducing clutter can also make it easier for hosts or cleaning teams to clean, inspect, and reset the property between stays.


Signs your short-term rental may need decluttering include:


- Guests have limited space for their belongings
- Closets, shelves, or countertops are full
- Rooms feel crowded or overly decorated
- Cleaning and turnovers take longer than necessary
- Personal belongings are mixed with guest-use items
- Backup supplies are taking over usable storage space


## **What Should You Remove From a Short-Term Rental?**


Start by removing anything that doesn’t contribute to the guest experience or isn’t needed regularly.


Items to consider removing include:


- Outdated or mismatched decor
- Personal mail, paperwork, books, or memorabilia
- Extra or bulky furniture
- Duplicate kitchenware
- Appliances guests rarely use
- Off-season bedding and linens
- Holiday and seasonal decorations
- Sports, camping, or recreational equipment
- Extra maintenance and cleaning supplies


The goal isn’t to make the rental feel empty. It’s to give guests enough room to comfortably use the space.


## **What Can Short-Term Rental Hosts Keep in a Self-Storage Unit?**


A self-storage unit can provide a centralized location for belongings and supplies you want to keep but don’t need at the property every day.


Depending on your needs, you might store:


- Personal belongings
- Backup linens and towels
- Off-season decor
- Holiday decorations
- Extra furniture
- Maintenance tools and equipment
- Bulk toiletries and paper products
- Extra kitchen supplies
- Replacement household items


Before storing anything, check your storage provider’s rules for prohibited items and make sure stored belongings are packed appropriately.


## **Can Self-Storage Help a Short-Term Rental Business?**


Self-storage can help hosts manage their properties more efficiently by separating guest essentials from personal belongings, seasonal items, and backup inventory.


Potential benefits include:


- **Easier turnovers:** Fewer unnecessary items can make cleaning and resetting rooms simpler.
- **More guest space:** Empty drawers, closets, and surfaces give guests room for their belongings.
- **Better organization:** Backup linens, supplies, and seasonal items can have a dedicated place.
- **Less clutter in listing photos:** A streamlined space can make it easier to showcase the rental’s layout and features.
- **More flexibility:** Hosts can rotate furniture, decor, and seasonal supplies without keeping everything at the property year-round.


While storage alone won’t guarantee higher ratings, more bookings, or increased revenue, it can support a cleaner and more efficient rental operation.


## **How Do You Decide What to Move Into Self-Storage?**


A simple rule is to ask whether an item needs to be at the property for the current guest experience. If it doesn’t, consider moving it into storage.


Divide items into three categories:


### **1. Items Guests Don’t Need Access To**


- Personal belongings
- Sensitive documents
- Maintenance tools
- Renovation materials
- Extra non-hazardous maintenance supplies


### **2. Items Guests Don’t Need for Every Stay**


- Extra decor
- Off-season bedding
- Holiday decorations
- Backup kitchenware
- Bulk supplies
- Replacement linens and towels


### **3. Items That Make the Rental Feel Crowded**


- Excess decorative objects
- Extra artwork
- Overcrowded bookshelves
- Duplicate kitchen gadgets
- Furniture that takes up unnecessary floor space


Keep guest essentials at the rental and move occasional-use or overflow items elsewhere.


## **Which Areas of a Short-Term Rental Should You Declutter?**


Go room by room and look for anything that takes up space without adding much value for guests.


- **Entryway:** Keep enough open space for luggage, shoes, coats, and bags.
- **Living room:** Clear surfaces and remove unnecessary furniture or decorative items.
- **Kitchen:** Eliminate duplicates and consider storing specialty appliances guests rarely use.
- **Bedrooms:** Keep nightstands clear and leave usable drawer and closet space.
- **Bathrooms:** Organize toiletries and move excess backstock elsewhere.
- **Closets:** Avoid filling every shelf and hanger with owner belongings.
- **Owner storage areas:** Move overflow off-site if an owner’s closet has become difficult to organize or access.


## **How Do You Organize a Storage Unit for a Short-Term Rental?**


Organize your storage unit so the items you use most often are the easiest to reach. Creating designated zones can make restocking, seasonal updates, and property maintenance more efficient.


Try these storage organization strategies:


- Divide the unit into clearly labeled zones
- Label bins on multiple sides so contents are easy to identify
- Use sturdy, freestanding shelving where permitted
- Keep frequently used supplies near the front
- Group seasonal decor and linens together
- Create a dedicated area for backup towels and bedding
- Keep replacement kitchen and household items together
- Maintain an inventory so you know what’s in storage
- Leave a clear walkway so you can reach items without moving everything


If multiple people access the unit, use a consistent labeling and inventory system so everyone knows where items belong.


## **How Do You Keep a Short-Term Rental Decluttered?**


Once you’ve decluttered the property, a simple routine can help prevent excess belongings from building up again.


Consider:


- Reviewing the property every few months for unnecessary items
- Keeping a designated bin for items that need to be removed
- Rotating seasonal decor instead of storing everything at the rental
- Checking inventory before buying additional supplies
- Asking cleaners or property managers to flag broken, duplicate, or unnecessary items
- Reviewing your storage unit periodically and removing items you no longer need


## **Is Self-Storage Worth It for Short-Term Rental Hosts?**


Self-storage can be useful for short-term rental hosts who don’t have enough on-site space for personal belongings, backup supplies, seasonal decor, extra furniture, or other occasional-use items.


Moving those belongings off-site can free up closets, cabinets, and living areas for guests while giving hosts a dedicated place to organize items they still need. It can be especially helpful for hosts managing multiple properties or regularly rotating decor, linens, and supplies.
