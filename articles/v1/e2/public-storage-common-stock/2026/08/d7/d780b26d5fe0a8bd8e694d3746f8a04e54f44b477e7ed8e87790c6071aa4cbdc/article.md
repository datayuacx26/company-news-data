# How to Organize a Pantry

The best way to organize a pantry is to remove everything, discard expired food, group similar items into zones, place frequently used foods within easy reach, and use containers or organizers to keep items visible. A simple pantry organization system can save time, reduce duplicate purchases, and help you use food before it expires.


You don’t need a huge pantry or matching containers to get organized. Start with what you have and build a system around the way your household actually cooks, eats, and shops.


## What Is the First Step to Organizing a Pantry?


Start by taking everything out of the pantry. Check expiration or best-by dates as appropriate, discard anything that is spoiled or no longer usable, and look for duplicate items.


If you find unopened, unexpired food you don’t expect to use, consider donating it to a local food pantry that accepts those items.


Once the shelves are empty, wipe them down and let them dry before putting food back.


## How Should You Group Items in a Pantry?


The easiest way to organize pantry items is to group similar foods together in designated zones. Your categories will depend on what you regularly keep at home, but common pantry zones include:


- Snacks
- Canned goods
- Cereal
- Pasta and grains
- Beans and legumes
- Baking supplies
- Breakfast foods
- Sauces and condiments


Organize around your habits. If you bake frequently, for example, keeping flour, sugar, baking powder, and other baking supplies together makes it easier to find what you need.


## Where Should Items Go in a Pantry?


Keep the foods you use most often at or near eye level so they’re easy to see and reach. Less frequently used items can go on higher or lower shelves.


As a general rule:


- **Eye level:** Everyday foods and frequently used ingredients
- **Lower shelves:** Heavier items, larger containers, and bulkier supplies
- **Upper shelves:** Lightweight items you use less frequently
- **Kid-accessible shelves:** Parent-approved snacks and foods children can safely reach


Keeping frequently used items visible can also help prevent forgotten food and unnecessary duplicate purchases.


## What Are the Best Organizers for a Pantry?


The best pantry organizers depend on the size and layout of your shelves. You don’t need every type of organizer; choose the ones that solve specific problems in your space.


Useful options include:


- **Clear bins:** Keep snacks and other small packages together and visible.
- **Shelf risers:** Add another level of usable space and make items in the back easier to see.
- **Turntables:** Make bottles, jars, and condiments easier to reach on deep shelves.
- **Under-shelf baskets:** Add storage for lightweight or shallow items.
- **Slide-out organizers:** Make it easier to reach items stored toward the back of deep shelves.
- **Door hooks:** Provide a place for lightweight kitchen items such as aprons or reusable bags.


Before buying pantry organizers, measure the **width, height, and depth** of your shelves. This can help you choose containers that fit without wasting valuable space.


## Should You Use Airtight Containers in a Pantry?


Airtight containers can be useful for foods such as flour, sugar, cereal, and other dry goods. They can help keep food contained and make it easier to see how much you have left.


If you transfer food out of its original packaging, label the container with the food name and any important information you may need later. Depending on the food, that could include an expiration or best-by date, cooking instructions, or allergen information.


## Can You Reuse Containers for Pantry Storage?


You don’t necessarily need to buy new containers to organize a pantry. Clean containers you already own may work for some storage needs.


Glass jars with secure lids, for example, can be useful for appropriate dry goods. Wash and dry them thoroughly before use, and label the contents so similar-looking ingredients don’t get mixed up.


Baskets, bins, and organizers from thrift or discount stores can also be affordable options. Make sure anything that will come into direct contact with food is intended for food storage and can be properly cleaned.


## How Do You Organize a Small Pantry?


In a small pantry, focus on visibility and vertical space. Shelf risers, stackable containers, bins, and door storage can help make better use of limited space.


Avoid filling every available inch. If shelves become too crowded, food can get pushed to the back and forgotten. Leaving enough room to see and reach what you have can make a small pantry more functional.


## How Should You Organize Pantry Items Bought in Bulk?


If you buy pantry staples in bulk, avoid letting backup items crowd out the foods you’re currently using. Keep the everyday supply accessible and designate another appropriate area of your home for unopened extras.


Before storing food in a garage or another area outside your kitchen, consider temperature, humidity, pests, and the storage instructions on the packaging. Some foods require specific storage conditions to maintain their quality.


## How Do You Keep a Pantry Organized?


The easiest way to keep a pantry organized is to follow a first-in, first-out system. When you bring home groceries, move older items toward the front and place newer ones behind them.


A few other habits can help:


- Return items to their designated zones.
- Clean spills when they happen.
- Check dates periodically.
- Look through the pantry before grocery shopping.
- Adjust your organization system as your household’s needs change.


You don’t need a perfectly styled pantry. The goal is to create a system that makes it easy to see what you have, find what you need, and use food before it goes to waste.


## How Can You Create More Kitchen Storage Space?


If your pantry and kitchen cabinets are overflowing, look beyond food when deciding what needs to stay in the kitchen. Seasonal serving pieces, specialty appliances, extra cookware, holiday kitchen decor, and other infrequently used household items can take up valuable cabinet and pantry space.


Moving items you don’t use regularly to another appropriate storage area can free up room for everyday essentials. If you need additional space outside the home, a storage unit can provide room for eligible household belongings that don’t need to be in your kitchen every day.


A pantry that works isn’t necessarily the prettiest one. It’s the one that helps you find the peanut butter before you buy another jar.
