# How Do You Store Camping Gear?

Packing for a camping trip is easier when your gear is already organized and ready to go. The best way to store camping gear is to clean and dry everything after each trip, organize equipment by category, and keep frequently used items together in labeled bins or other easy-to-access storage.


Tents, sleeping bags, coolers, camp chairs, cooking equipment, and outdoor gear can take up a lot of room at home. Creating a consistent storage system can help you save space, find what you need, and spend less time packing before your next trip.


## How Should You Organize Camping Gear at Home?


Organize camping gear by function so related items are stored together. This makes packing faster and helps you see what needs to be cleaned, repaired, replaced, or restocked before your next trip.


Consider creating categories such as:


- **Shelter:** Tents, stakes, guylines and tarps
- **Sleeping:** Sleeping bags, sleeping pads and camping pillows
- **Camp kitchen:** Stoves, cookware, utensils and other cooking supplies
- **Lighting and essentials:** Lanterns, flashlights, first-aid kits and tools
- **Outdoor activities:** Backpacks, fishing equipment and other recreational gear
- **Camp furniture:** Folding chairs and tables
- **Family supplies:** Kids’ or pet-specific camping gear


Store smaller items in labeled bins or containers so you can quickly find and pack an entire category.


## How Can You Store Camping Gear in a Small Space?


To store camping gear in a small home or apartment, take advantage of vertical and underused spaces. Shelving units, stackable bins, over-the-door organizers, wall-mounted storage, and under-bed containers can provide additional room without taking over your everyday living areas.


Keep frequently used equipment somewhere easy to reach, while seasonal or bulky gear can go in less accessible storage areas.


If tents, coolers, folding chairs, outdoor equipment, and other bulky items are taking up too much room at home, a self-storage unit can provide additional space between trips.


## How Do You Keep Camping Gear Ready for the Next Trip?


Keep camping gear ready for your next trip by cleaning, drying, restocking, and reorganizing it as soon as you return home. Once everything is ready for storage, return it to the same designated bins, shelves, or containers.


You don’t necessarily need to unpack every camping bin after each trip. If items are clean, dry, and don’t need to be replenished, keeping your basic camping setup together can make it easier to grab what you need next time.


A digital camping checklist can also help you track consumable or trip-specific items, such as food, toiletries, clothing, and other supplies that shouldn’t remain packed between trips.


## How Should You Store Tents and Sleeping Bags?


Tents and sleeping bags should be completely clean and dry before being stored. Packing damp outdoor gear can encourage mildew, odors, and material deterioration.


After a trip, unpack your tent and allow it to air-dry completely before putting it away. Sleeping bags should also be thoroughly dry before storage. Follow the manufacturer’s storage instructions for your specific tent or sleeping bag, since recommendations can vary by material and design.


## How Should You Prepare Camping Gear for Storage?


Clean and inspect camping equipment before putting it away so it is ready for your next trip.


Before storing your gear:


- Air-dry tents, tarps and sleeping gear completely.
- Clean cookware, dishes and utensils.
- Empty and clean coolers.
- Remove food and other perishable items.
- Check flashlights, lanterns and other equipment.
- Remove batteries from devices before longer-term storage when appropriate.
- Restock first-aid kits and other supplies.
- Keep small accessories together in labeled pouches or bins.


Taking care of these tasks immediately after a trip can help prevent unpleasant surprises the next time you go camping.


## Can You Store Camping Gear in a Storage Unit?


A storage unit can be useful for bulky or seasonal camping and outdoor equipment that you don’t have room to keep at home. Depending on what you own, that might include extra tents, coolers, folding tables and chairs, hiking packs, fishing equipment, or other outdoor gear.


Keeping less frequently used equipment in a storage unit can free up closets, garages, and other areas of your home while giving your camping gear a dedicated place between trips.


Before storing any camping equipment, make sure it is clean and completely dry and check any applicable storage restrictions for items such as fuel, propane, or other potentially hazardous materials.


## How Can You Make Packing for Camping Faster?


A consistent storage system can make packing for camping faster because you don’t have to reorganize your gear before every trip.


Keep a master camping checklist, restock supplies when you return home, store equipment by category, and use labeled grab-and-go bins for items you regularly bring with you. Before each trip, you can focus on adding food, clothing, and destination-specific supplies instead of rebuilding your camping setup from scratch.
