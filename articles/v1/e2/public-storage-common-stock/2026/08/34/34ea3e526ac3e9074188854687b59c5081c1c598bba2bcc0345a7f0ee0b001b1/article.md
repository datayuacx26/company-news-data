# How Do Maximalists Store Their Stuff?

The best way to organize a maximalist home is to create designated zones, use vertical and closed storage, rotate collections, keep walkways clear, and regularly edit what you display. The goal isn’t to own less, it’s to give the things you love a clear place to live.


Maximalist style embraces color, pattern, texture, collections, and personal objects. That might mean shelves filled with books and vintage glassware, a growing record collection, walls covered in art, or a closet that reflects every era of your personal style.


The key is making all those layers feel intentional rather than overwhelming. Here’s how to organize a maximalist home without sacrificing personality.


## Choose Focal Points for Your Favorite Collections


When everything is on display, individual pieces can get lost. Instead, choose a few focal points, or “hero” areas, where your favorite items can stand out.


A gallery wall, bookshelf, vintage bar cart, record display, or cabinet filled with collectibles can become a focal point. Allow those areas to carry more visual weight while giving the surrounding space a little room to breathe.


This creates contrast and helps a maximalist room feel intentionally styled rather than crowded.


## Create Zones for Different Types of Items


One of the easiest ways to organize a maximalist home is to group similar items together and give each category a designated space.


For example:


- Keep books together on dedicated shelves.
- Store candles or decorative objects in one area.
- Organize hobby and craft supplies in labeled containers.
- Arrange clothing by type, color, or season.
- Dedicate specific kitchen cabinets or shelves to different categories.


Creating zones makes it easier to find what you need while maintaining the layered look that defines maximalist style.


## Use Vertical Space to Create More Storage


If shelves, tables, and other surfaces are getting crowded, use your walls and vertical space for additional storage.


Tall bookcases, floating shelves, wall-mounted cabinets, pegboards, and hooks can add storage without taking up additional floor space. Bags, hats, books, artwork, kitchen supplies, and collectibles can all take advantage of vertical storage.


Using the full height of a room can also help keep floors and walkways open, even when you have a lot to store or display.


## Keep Walkways and Doorways Clear


A maximalist room can contain plenty of furniture and decor without becoming difficult to use. Keeping walkways, doors, and frequently used areas clear helps the space remain functional.


Avoid stacking belongings in doorways or placing furniture so close together that it becomes difficult to move through the room. Maintaining comfortable traffic flow can make even a heavily decorated space feel more organized.


## Choose Storage That Fits Your Maximalist Style


Storage doesn’t have to mean plain plastic bins. Choose storage pieces that complement your decor while keeping smaller or less visually appealing items contained.


Depending on your style, that could include:


- Decorative baskets
- Vintage trunks
- Colorful cabinets
- Bookshelves
- Display cabinets
- Decorative boxes
- Ottomans with hidden storage


A mix of open and closed storage can work especially well in maximalist homes. Open shelving gives favorite pieces a place to shine, while closed storage helps contain items you don’t want on display.


## Rotate Collections Instead of Displaying Everything


If you have a large collection, you don’t have to display every piece at the same time. Rotating collections can reduce visual clutter while giving you opportunities to refresh your decor throughout the year.


Choose a portion of your collection to display and store the rest until you’re ready to switch things out. You might rotate artwork, books, seasonal decor, vintage finds, collectibles, or other decorative pieces every few months.


Rotating collections can also help you rediscover items you already own instead of constantly adding something new.


## Edit Your Space Without Becoming a Minimalist


Organizing a maximalist home doesn’t mean getting rid of everything. Instead, periodically reassess what deserves space in your home and what you still enjoy displaying.


Ask yourself:


- Do I still love this?
- Does it reflect my current style or interests?
- Would I choose it again today?
- Is there somewhere else it would work better?
- Does it need to be displayed right now?


Removing, donating, selling, or storing pieces that no longer fit your space can give your favorite belongings more room to stand out.


## Store Items You Don’t Need to Display Right Now


Some belongings don’t need to take up space in your home year-round. Self-storage can provide additional space for items you want to keep but don’t currently need to display or use.


Depending on your needs, that might include seasonal decor, extra furniture, hobby equipment, collectibles, business inventory, or items you plan to rotate back into your home.


Additional storage can be particularly useful for collectors who rotate displays, thrifters with growing collections, vintage resellers managing inventory, or creatives with supplies they use seasonally.


When storing collectibles or other valuable or delicate items, consider the item’s specific storage requirements before packing it away.


## Create a Simple Maximalist Organizing Routine


Maximalist spaces can be easier to manage with small, regular organizing sessions instead of occasional major cleanouts.


Every few weeks or months, take time to dust shelves, return items to their designated zones, reassess displays, and identify areas that are becoming crowded. You can also rotate decor or move pieces into storage as your needs and style change.


Regular maintenance helps keep collections manageable without requiring you to constantly reorganize your entire home.

## Frequently Asked Questions


### How do you organize a maximalist home?


Organize a maximalist home by grouping similar items into zones, using both open and closed storage, taking advantage of vertical space, keeping walkways clear, and rotating collections rather than displaying everything at once.


### How do you make maximalism look less cluttered?


Create a few intentional focal points, group similar objects together, leave some visual space around major displays, and keep everyday surfaces and walkways functional. Closed storage can also help contain smaller items.


### How do you organize a large collection?


Group items by type, size, color, theme, or frequency of use. Display favorite pieces together and consider rotating portions of a large collection so everything doesn’t have to be displayed at the same time.


### Where should you store decor you aren’t currently using?


Seasonal or rotating decor can be stored in labeled containers in closets, cabinets, garages, or other appropriate storage areas. If you don’t have enough space at home, a self-storage unit may provide additional room for items you want to keep.


### Can self-storage help if you have too many collectibles?


Self-storage can provide additional space for collectibles and other belongings you don’t need to keep at home all the time. Before storing collectibles, consider their material, value, fragility, and any temperature or humidity requirements so you can choose an appropriate storage option.
