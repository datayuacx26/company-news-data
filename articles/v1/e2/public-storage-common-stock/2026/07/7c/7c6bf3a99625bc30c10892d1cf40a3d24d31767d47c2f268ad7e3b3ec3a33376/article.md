# What Are the Most Secure Self-Storage Facilities?

A self-storage facility is generally more secure when it uses multiple layers of security rather than relying on a single feature. Electronic gate access, video surveillance, quality locks, good lighting, perimeter fencing, and on-site management can work together to help discourage unauthorized access and improve visibility across the property.


No security feature can eliminate all risk, but facilities that combine multiple security measures often provide a stronger overall security program than those relying on a single solution.


## What Are the Most Secure Self-Storage Facilities?


When comparing self-storage facilities, consider whether the property offers multiple security features that work together. Common features include:


- Video surveillance
- Electronic gate access
- Good lighting
- Perimeter fencing
- On-site management
- Quality locks
- Dedicated security or loss prevention programs


Because every facility is different, it’s worth reviewing the available features before reserving a storage unit.


**Video Surveillance**

Cameras can support incident investigations, help deter unauthorized activity, and provide an additional layer of visibility across the property.

**Electronic Gate Access**


Many self-storage facilities use electronic gate access to help manage who enters and exits the property during designated access hours. Customers are typically assigned a unique access code, allowing authorized individuals to enter the property while creating a record of gate activity. When combined with other security measures, controlled access can help limit unauthorized entry.


Availability varies by location.


**Lighting and Visibility**


Lighting is an important part of a property’s overall security program.


Well-lit storage facilities improve visibility throughout the property, making it easier for customers to access their units during early morning or evening hours. Good lighting also helps staff and video surveillance systems monitor common areas more effectively while discouraging unwanted activity in darker spaces.


**Perimeter Fencing**


Perimeter fencing helps define the property’s boundaries and directs traffic through designated entry points.


While fencing alone isn’t enough to secure a property, it becomes more effective when paired with electronic gate access, video surveillance, lighting, and on-site management. Together, these features create multiple layers of security throughout the facility.

## Security Starts with Smart Storage Habits


The security features at a self-storage facility are important, but they’re only part of the equation. How you pack, lock, and manage your storage unit can also help reduce risk and protect your belongings over time. These simple best practices can help you get the most out of your storage experience.


**Use a Quality Lock**


Your lock is the first line of defense for your storage unit. A high-quality disc lock or cylinder lock, when compatible with your unit, is generally more resistant to cutting and tampering than many standard padlocks. If you’re unsure which type of lock is recommended, ask your storage facility before move-in.


**Keep Your Gate Access Code Private**


Many storage facilities use personalized electronic gate access systems to help manage who enters the property. To help maintain that layer of security, avoid sharing your gate access code with friends, family, movers, or contractors unless absolutely necessary. Keeping your code private helps reduce unauthorized access associated with your account.


**Create a Photo Inventory**


Before placing items into storage, take photos of valuable belongings and make a simple inventory list. Include details such as electronics, furniture, collectibles, or other high-value items. Keeping this documentation can make it easier to identify belongings and may be helpful if you ever need to file an insurance claim.


**Visit Your Unit Periodically**


Checking on your storage unit from time to time allows you to inspect your lock, look for signs of moisture or pests, and confirm your belongings remain in good condition. Regular visits can also help you reorganize items, rotate seasonal belongings, and identify any issues early.


**Keep Your Unit Locked When You’re Away**


When loading or unloading your belongings, it’s easy to become distracted. Avoid leaving your storage unit open and unattended, even for a short time. Close and lock the unit whenever you step away to help reduce the opportunity for unauthorized access.


**Consider Insurance Coverage**


Even with good storage practices and facility security features, unexpected events can happen. Many storage facilities require customers to maintain insurance or property protection coverage for the items stored in their unit. Review your available coverage options and make sure you understand what is and isn’t covered before storing valuable belongings.


**Pack and Organize with Security in Mind**


How you organize your storage unit can also make a difference. Place valuable items toward the back of the unit, avoid storing cash or irreplaceable documents, and keep an inventory of what you’ve stored. Using sturdy boxes, labeling containers clearly, and stacking items safely can also make future visits easier while reducing the risk of accidental damage.
