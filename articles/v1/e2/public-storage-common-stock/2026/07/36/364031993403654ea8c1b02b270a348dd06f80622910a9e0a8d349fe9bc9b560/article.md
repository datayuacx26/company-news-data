# Essential Tips for Renting with Pets

For pet owners, finding rental housing can be a challenge. Many landlords hesitate to accept tenants with pets due to concerns about property damage, noise complaints, and liability. However, with millions of Americans owning pets, the rental market is gradually becoming more accommodating. Successfully renting with pets requires preparation, honesty, strategic searching, and demonstrating that you’re a responsible pet owner. Here’s your comprehensive guide to navigating the pet-friendly rental market and securing a home where your entire family—furry members included—can thrive.


****


## **Start Your Search with Pet-Friendly Filters**


Save time and frustration by focusing exclusively on pet-friendly properties from the beginning. Most rental listing websites offer pet-friendly filters that immediately narrow results to landlords who accept pets. Explicitly searching “pet-friendly apartments” or “dog-friendly rentals” in your target area reveals properties where you won’t face automatic rejection.


However, “pet-friendly” definitions vary widely. Some properties welcome all pets without restrictions, while others impose limitations on species, breeds, sizes, or numbers. Always read the fine print carefully before getting excited about a listing.


Consider expanding your search beyond traditional apartment complexes. Individual landlords renting single-family homes, duplexes, or small multi-family properties often demonstrate more flexibility than corporate property management companies with rigid policies.


****


## **Understand Common Pet Policies and Restrictions**


Before viewing properties, familiarize yourself with typical pet-related rental requirements and restrictions.


Pet deposits and fees: Most pet-friendly rentals require additional financial commitments. Pet deposits (usually refundable if no damage occurs) typically range from $200 to $500 per pet. Some landlords charge a non-refundable pet fee instead, while others impose a monthly “pet rent” of $25-$75 added to your monthly payment—budget for these extra costs when calculating affordability.


Breed restrictions: Many properties maintain banned-breed lists, typically including breeds perceived as aggressive, such as Pit Bulls, Rottweilers, Dobermans, German Shepherds, and Huskies. These restrictions often stem from insurance company requirements rather than landlord preferences. If you own a restricted breed, finding housing becomes significantly more challenging but not impossible.


Size and weight limits: Properties frequently cap pet weight at 25, 50, or 75 pounds. Some restrict the number of pets per unit or prohibit certain species entirely (often exotic pets, birds, or reptiles).


Age requirements: Some landlords prefer older, calmer pets and may reject puppies or kittens due to housetraining concerns and higher energy levels.


Understanding these common restrictions helps you identify properties worth pursuing and those to skip.


****


## **Create a Compelling Pet Resume**


Stand out from other applicants by presenting your pet professionally. A pet resume demonstrates responsibility and addresses landlord concerns proactively.


Include the following information:


- Your pet’s name, age, breed, weight, and spayed/neutered status


- Current vaccination records and veterinary contact information


- Training certifications or obedience class completion


- Temperament description emphasizing positive qualities (friendly, quiet, well-behaved)


- Professional photos showing your pet looking calm and well-groomed
- Reference letters from previous landlords, neighbors, or veterinarians attesting to your pet’s good behavior and your responsible ownership


This documentation shows you’re a serious, conscientious pet owner who takes rental responsibilities seriously. Many landlords appreciate this proactive approach and view it favorably during the application process.


****


## **Offer Additional Assurances**


When competition for pet-friendly rentals is fierce, offering extra assurances can tip the scales in your favor.


Obtain renter’s insurance with pet liability coverage: Many policies include liability if your pet causes injury or property damage. Providing proof of this coverage addresses a major landlord concern and demonstrates financial responsibility.


Offer a higher security deposit: If financially feasible, proposing an increased security deposit shows confidence in your pet’s behavior and your commitment to maintaining the property. This extra cushion provides landlords peace of mind.


Provide additional references: Beyond standard employment and personal references, include references specifically addressing your pet ownership. Previous landlords who can vouch for your pet’s behavior and your property care carry significant weight.


Agree to professional cleaning: Commit in writing to professional carpet cleaning upon move-out, addressing concerns about pet odors or stains.


****


## **Be Completely Honest**


Never hide pets or misrepresent their size, breed, or number. Dishonesty violates lease agreements and provides grounds for immediate eviction. Landlords will eventually discover undisclosed pets—neighbors report them, maintenance workers encounter them, or barking gives them away.


Beyond ethical considerations, honesty builds trust with landlords. Transparent communication about your pet’s characteristics, any behavioral challenges you’re actively addressing, and your commitment to responsible ownership creates a foundation for positive landlord-tenant relationships.


If your pet has past incidents (like a noise complaint or minor damage), address them upfront while explaining steps you’ve taken to prevent recurrence, such as additional training or behavioral modification.


****


## **Meet Your Landlord with Your Pet**


If possible, arrange for your potential landlord to meet your pet during the application process. A well-behaved, friendly animal makes a positive impression that paperwork alone cannot achieve. This meeting allows landlords to see firsthand that your pet is calm, trained, and socialized.


Prepare your pet for this meeting: ensure they’re clean and well-groomed, practice basic commands beforehand, and bring them on a leash or in an appropriate carrier. A positive in-person interaction can overcome hesitations and differentiate you from other applicants.


****


## **Know Your Rights**


Familiarize yourself with fair housing laws regarding assistance animals. Emotional support animals (ESAs) and service animals receive legal protections under the Fair Housing Act and


Americans with Disabilities Act, respectively. Landlords must provide reasonable accommodations for legitimate assistance animals, even in properties with no-pet policies, and cannot charge pet fees or deposits for them.


However, obtaining legitimate ESA documentation requires a genuine need and a proper healthcare provider evaluation. Fraudulent ESA certifications undermine protections for people with genuine disabilities and create problems for all pet owners.


****


## **Maintain Excellent Tenancy**


Once you’ve found pet-friendly housing, be an exemplary tenant. Address any pet-related issues immediately, maintain cleanliness, respect quiet hours, follow all lease terms, and respond promptly to any concerns. Positive experiences with responsible pet owners make landlords more likely to accept pets from future tenants, benefiting the entire pet-owning community.


Renting with pets requires extra effort, but millions of pet owners successfully find wonderful homes for their families every year. With preparation, honesty, and professionalism, you can too.
