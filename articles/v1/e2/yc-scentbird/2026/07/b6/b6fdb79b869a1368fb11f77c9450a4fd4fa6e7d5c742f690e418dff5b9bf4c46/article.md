# Find Your Summer Signature Scent with this Fragrance Quiz

Perfume isn’t one-and-done. It’s an ever-evolving situationship, and nothing stirs the pot like a change in weather. All those fragrances you were happy to go steady with for autumn, through the winter holidays, and into spring… come July, they don’t stand a chance at being your summer signature scent.


Part of it is, when the temperature rises and the sun comes out, so does the summer version of us. (We like to think of her as more fun than our winter self). We want to wear the clothes we only get to wear in the heat, spend more time outside, and feel all… summery. But the hot weather doesn’t just change how *we* feel and act. It also changes how your perfume behaves—it pushes scent louder, turns sweet notes syrupy, and burns the light stuff off by noon. We need scents that are built for the heat.


So you’re thinking, okay, I need to[find my signature scent](https://www.scentbird.com/blog/find-your-perfect-scent-with-the-new-scentbird-quiz/) for summer, sure. But where to start? What perfume *should* I wear when it’s 90 degrees out? Well, surprise, we’ve got the answer. And it’s easy: take the[Scentbird perfume quiz](https://www.scentbird.com/smart-recommendations) .


##
Meet the Fragrance Families


The first thing you’ll learn from the quiz is what kind of[fragrance family](https://www.scentbird.com/notes) you’re best suited to. Let’s do a quick recap:


### Fresh


Clean, airy scents built on notes like[cotton](https://www.scentbird.com/note/cottonwood) ,[mint](https://www.scentbird.com/note/mint) , and green leaves; crisp and weightless, which is why they work best in heat. Picture the feeling of a cool summer shower you didn’t want to step out of—the kind of scent that feels refreshing on a blazing-hot day, when anything heavier would just be too much.


### Citrus


Bright, zesty scents led by[lemon](https://www.scentbird.com/note/lemon) ,[bergamot](https://www.scentbird.com/note/bergamot) ,[grapefruit](https://www.scentbird.com/note/ruby-grapefruit) , and orange; sharp and energizing, but they fade fast, so reapplication is part of the deal. These scents feel like cracking open something cold and fizzy on a hot day. This is the instantly wide-awake option—the one to reach for on a sluggish, sticky morning when you need a rejuvenating citrusy reset.


### Aquatic


Cool, clean scents that recreate[water](https://www.scentbird.com/note/water?tab=colognes) ,[sea air](https://www.scentbird.com/note/sea-note?tab=colognes) , and[rain](https://www.scentbird.com/note/fresh-rain-accord?tab=colognes) through marine notes like calone, for a blue, just-out-of-the-ocean feel. All the cool freshness of a summer swim (with none of the sand or salty hair). When the heat feels inescapable, this is the fragrance family that feels like a long drink of water.


### Solar-gourmand


Sweet, edible notes like[vanilla](https://www.scentbird.com/note/vanilla) ,[coconut](https://www.scentbird.com/note/coconut) , and[caramel](https://www.scentbird.com/note/caramel) met with warm solar accords that mimic sunshine on skin; smells like sunscreen, sand, and a tropical vacation. This is summer’s sweet tooth—golden, a little hedonistic, like a coconut dessert at a beach bar. Best after dark, when you want warmth without weight.


### Light floral


Soft, fresh flowers like neroli,[freesia](https://www.scentbird.com/note/freesia) ,[peony](https://www.scentbird.com/note/peony) , and[orange blossom](https://www.scentbird.com/note/orange-blossom) ; the airier, less heady side of florals—perfect for warm weather. Florals, but make it summer. This is the easy-wearing, hard-to-get-wrong family—pretty without being precious, and just as nice on a Tuesday as for a special occasion.


## Just a Few Easy Questions


The quiz starts with a few easy questions, like the ones below. No overthinking required here, because there are no wrong answers—just give your instinctive response and notice which way you lean.


### Day or night?


A sparkling-bright citrus or fresh scent for daylight; or something warmer and deliciously skin-close for once the sun goes down. (We can’t lie, it’s pretty great having one of each).


### Fresh or sweet?


Cool and clean (aquatic, green, citrus), or warm as summer sunshine and a little edible (solar-gourmand)? This is one of the big either-ors—this question splits your whole summer wardrobe in two.


### Subtle or statement?


A quiet scent that stays close, or one that announces you from across the rooftop bar? Heat amplifies everything, so a “statement” in July hits louder than it would in December.


### Signature or rotation?


One summer scent you wear on repeat, or a little lineup you switch between by mood and plan?
Familiar or adventurous?


There’s a lot to be said for a safe bet you already know you love—but then again, something nobody else at the party is wearing is kind of exciting too (summer’s a good time to try the second one).


Answer those and just like that, you’ve sketched the shape of your ideal summer fragrance. Once you’ve completed the Scentbird fragrance quiz, not only will you know what kind of fragrance family suits you best but you’ll be given a list of specific scent recommendations for your new summer signature scent.

## Frequently asked questions


### How do I find my summer signature scent?


Start by identifying the fragrance family that suits you, then test a few scents on skin in real summer conditions. The Scentbird quiz does the first step for you, matching your preferences to a family and giving specific recommendations you can try before committing to a bottle.


### What fragrance families work best in hot weather?


Fresh, citrus, aquatic, and light floral scents work best in heat because they are lighter and more refreshing. Solar-gourmand scents also suit summer, especially after dark, when you want warmth without heaviness.


### Why does my perfume smell different in summer?


Heat pushes fragrance to project louder, turns sweet notes syrupy, and burns off lighter top notes faster. That is why scents you loved in winter can feel like too much in July, and why lighter, heat-friendly families tend to wear better in summer.
