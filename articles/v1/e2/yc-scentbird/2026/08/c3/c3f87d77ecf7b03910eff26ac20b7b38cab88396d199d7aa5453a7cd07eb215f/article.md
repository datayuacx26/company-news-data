# Gucci Bloom Ambrosia di Fiori vs Gucci Bloom: Which Should You Wear?

Gucci Bloom Ambrosia di Fiori usually gets summed up as[Bloom](https://www.scentbird.com/perfume/gucci-gucci-bloom-1427) with the volume turned up, and that shortcut sells both perfumes short. The real Gucci Bloom Ambrosia di Fiori vs Gucci Bloom question is not stronger versus weaker. It is whether you want your white flowers green and dewy, or deepened into rose and honey, because that is the actual choice[Ambrosia di Fiori](https://www.scentbird.com/perfume/gucci-gucci-bloom-ambrosia-di-fiori-2010) puts in front of you.

## Ambrosia di Fiori vs Gucci Bloom: the similarities


Family resemblance runs deep here, and no wonder: both come from the same nose. Both are unapologetic white florals with tuberose and jasmine at heart, both skip the usual sparkling-citrus overture and dive straight into petals, and both wear like a statement rather than an afterthought. If white flowers are not your thing, neither bottle will convert you. If they are, the choice between them comes down to light.


## Gucci Bloom


The original[Gucci Bloom](https://www.scentbird.com/perfume/gucci-gucci-bloom-1427) , composed by Alberto Morillas in 2017, is a white floral in the most literal sense. You step straight into tuberose, jasmine, and Rangoon creeper, and the whole thing smells like a florist’s cool back room, cut stems, white petals, and damp greenery included. What makes it modern is what Morillas left out: no vanilla cushion, no candied edge, so the flowers stay green and watery from the first hour to the last. It projects confidently for the first couple of hours, then wears closer for a total of about seven to nine. Its one genuine risk is the tuberose itself, which can turn heady in a warm, crowded room and reads distinctly bridal to some noses. Skip it if white florals have ever felt like a wedding bouquet you did not order.


## Gucci Bloom Ambrosia di Fiori


Ambrosia di Fiori, Morillas again in 2019, starts from the same bouquet and then changes the light. Damask rose and orange blossom move into the heart, and a honeyed warmth settles underneath, so the same flowers now read as if the garden had shifted to dusk. Where Bloom stays linear and airy, Ambrosia evolves: the rose swells through the middle hours and the finish turns plush and faintly sweet, clinging to scarves and collars well into the next morning. It is the stronger performer at roughly eight to ten hours with a heavier trail. The trade-off is exactly that density. In summer heat or a small office it can feel stifling, and the honeyed finish may frustrate you if you love your florals crisp. Skip it if dewy and green is the whole reason you liked Bloom in the first place.

## Which one is better, Ambrosia di Fiori or Gucci Bloom?


The differentiator is not volume, it is temperature. Bloom is green water; Ambrosia is warm nectar. That one distinction answers most practical questions before they get asked: mornings, offices, and warm afternoons belong to Bloom, while dinners, cold evenings, and nights you want the fragrance to outlast the event belong to Ambrosia. Owning both is not redundant, either, since they cover opposite halves of the same floral year.


The short answer


Choose Gucci Bloom if you want white flowers kept fresh, green, and easy to wear anywhere in daylight. Choose Ambrosia di Fiori if you want the same bouquet deepened with rose and honey for evenings and cooler months, and do not mind it announcing itself.

## Frequently asked questions


What does Gucci Bloom smell like?


Gucci Bloom is a white floral built on tuberose, jasmine, and Rangoon creeper. There is no citrus introduction; it opens straight into cool, dewy petals and stays there, airy and green rather than sweet, like standing in a florist’s back room among cut stems and damp greenery.


What does Gucci Bloom Ambrosia di Fiori smell like?


Ambrosia di Fiori takes Bloom’s tuberose and jasmine and deepens them with Damask rose and orange blossom over a honeyed base. The effect is the same garden at dusk: warmer, rosier, and noticeably sweeter, with a rich finish that lingers on clothes into the next day.


What is the difference between Gucci Bloom and Ambrosia di Fiori?


The rose and the honey. Bloom keeps its white flowers green, watery, and fresh from start to finish. Ambrosia di Fiori pours warmth over the same bouquet, so the drydown turns honeyed and plush instead of dewy. Think green water versus warm nectar and you have the difference.


Which lasts longer, Bloom or Ambrosia di Fiori?


Ambrosia di Fiori. As the intense version it runs about eight to ten hours with a heavier trail, while the original Bloom sits around seven to nine and wears lighter after its strong opening. On fabric, Ambrosia can still be there the next morning.


Is Ambrosia di Fiori good for the office?


Use a light hand. Its density and honeyed sweetness can feel like a lot in a small, warm room, so one spray is plenty for shared spaces. The original Bloom is the easier office pick because it stays airier and greener through the day.


Is Gucci Bloom better for summer?


Mostly yes. Its dewy, green character suits mild and warm days, though in real heat and a packed room the tuberose can turn heady, so spray lightly. Ambrosia di Fiori is happier in cool weather and after dark, where its honeyed depth has room to breathe.
