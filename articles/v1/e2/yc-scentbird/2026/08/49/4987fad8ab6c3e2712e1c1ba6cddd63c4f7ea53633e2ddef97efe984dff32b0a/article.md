# How to Apply Perfume So Your Scent Lasts All Day

As a teenager, we were mostly taught the same thing: a couple sprays on the neck and the wrists, maybe a rub, and then *voila* –the job is done. While this isn’t exactly incorrect, knowing how to properly apply perfume changes how a fragrance actually performs. There are many questions that come into play, and knowing where, how much, and when in your beauty routine to apply perfume is only the beginning. Let’s walk through the steps together, shall we?


## Where to apply perfume (and why pulse points work)


While there are a few tried-and-true places to apply perfume, experimenting with unexpected spots can be just as rewarding.


### Hit your pulse points


Spraying perfume on your pulse points is effective because they’re[where your body emits the most heat](https://www.scentbird.com/blog/do-pheromone-perfumes-actually-work-and-other-questions-about-addictive-scent/) , helping to activate and diffuse fragrance oils. These spots include your wrists, around your neck, your inner elbows, and even behind the ears. As we’ll get into more in the next point, there are a few other areas that work too, such as behind the knees and the chest, just below your collarbone. Applying to these warm areas ensures your favorite fragrance develops its full character throughout the day.


### Try these unexpected spots–as long as they make sense to you


If you’re in the mood to pleasantly surprise your everyday audience, try applying perfume to less obvious places that align with your daily habits. Are you heading to a picnic? The back of the knees, as mentioned above, leaves behind a pleasant trail. In a Vogue Adria article, Emma Vernon, host of the perfume podcast Perfume Room, mentioned that she likes to apply perfume to her wrists, forearms and elbows because she naturally gestures when she speaks.


Hair also holds scent longer than skin does, since it doesn’t shed oils and sweat the way skin does, so a light spray on dry hair (never wet, since alcohol can be drying) is one trick for making a fragrance last into the evening. A friend of mine, who commutes across Paris every day for his job in beauty marketing, swears by this trick. As he put it, he does it “to smell pleasant to my fellow commuters… and to overpower any unpleasant smells I may encounter.”


### Where not to spray


Just as important as knowing where to spray is knowing where not to. While spritzing your clothes might seem like a good way to make the scent last, it’s not always the best idea. Perfume oils can easily stain delicate fabrics like silk, and the alcohol can sometimes discolor materials. You should also avoid applying fragrance to irritated skin, as the alcohol may possibly cause further irritation. It’s one of the most common perfume mistakes people make, but it’s an easy one to fix by simply applying your scent to healthy, moisturized skin instead.


## Perfume application tips: prepping your skin to make scent last all day


In this section, we’ll break down the essential steps to prep your skin before applying perfume, so your favorite fragrance can linger from morning to night.


### Start with clean skin


The best time to apply perfume is right after a warm shower, on clean skin that is later moisturized (more on that later). When you wash your body, you rinse away any sweat, oil, and leftover scents from the day before, creating a fresh slate for your fragrance. This preps your body, leading the way for step two.


### Moisturize to make it stick


This might be the most important tip of all: hydrated skin holds fragrance longer. Dry skin doesn’t hold onto fragrance molecules as effectively, so a scent applied on parched skin fades faster compared to the same scent applied over a body lotion or oil. Unscented lotion is the safest bet if you want your perfume to be the only scent story happening, but a lightly scented one–enter: fragrance latering–in a complementary note (vanilla under a gourmand, citrus under a fresh fragrance) can not only extend but deepen how a fragrance wears.


### Why timing matters: when should you apply fragrance?


Timing and taking your routine into consideration matters too. Applying right before you leave the house means you’re smelling a fragrance at its rawest; the initial burst of top notes fade the fastest and aren’t necessarily representative of how the scent will read an hour later. If you have the option, applying 15 to 20 minutes before you actually need to smell your best gives the top notes time to settle into the heart notes underneath, which is usually a more accurate, pleasant version of the fragrance anyway.


### Introduce fragrance layering


One of the best ways to get more mileage out of your perfume is through layering (Read:[How to Layer Fragrances: A Step-by-Step Guide to Layering Warm, Cozy Scents](https://www.scentbird.com/blog/how-to-layer-fragrances-warm-cozy-scents/) ). This means using body products–like a body wash, lotion, or oil–in the same scent as your fragrance. This technique creates a scented base on your skin that the perfume can cling to, deepening the aroma and helping it last much longer. If you don’t have the matching body lotion for your favorite perfume, an unscented moisturizer works just as well by giving the fragrance oils something to adhere to. Applying lotion to damp skin right after a shower is the perfect first step before you spritz.


### Know when to reapply


Even the longest-lasting perfume will eventually begin to fade. The reality is that most fragrances are designed to last for about six hours, so a little touch-up is often necessary. This is where a travel-sized atomizer comes in handy. Instead of carrying a full, fragile bottle in your bag, you can easily slip a smaller vial into your purse for a quick refresh (Read:[Your Vacation Packing List: Travel-Size Perfume Picks for Beach, City, and Mountain Trips](https://www.scentbird.com/blog/vacation-packing-list-travel-size-perfume-picks/) ). You don’t need to re-do your entire routine, either. A single, light spray on your pulse points is usually all it takes to revive your scent and carry you through the rest of your day or into the evening.


## How much to apply?


Two to four sprays is the range that works for most fragrance concentrations and most people, and it’s genuinely easy to overdo it from there. A good gut check: if you can still smell your own perfume clearly an hour after applying, you probably used too much… by that point, fragrance should be settling into something you catch occasionally, not something that follows you around the room. If you’re wearing an eau de parfum or parfum, lean toward the lower end of that range. Their higher concentration means they’ll naturally project farther and last longer, so a lighter touch is all you need.


## Common perfume mistakes to avoid


Even the most beautiful fragrance won’t perform at its best if it’s applied incorrectly. From rubbing your wrists together to relying solely on your clothes, these are some of the most common perfume mistakes.


### Rubbing your wrists together


It’s a classic movie moment, but rubbing your wrists together after applying perfume is one of the biggest fragrance mistakes you can make. The friction generates heat, which can alter the way the perfume develops by breaking down its delicate top notes more quickly. As a result, the fragrance may lose some of its freshness and complexity before it has a chance to unfold naturally. Instead, spray your wrists and simply let the perfume dry on its own. If you prefer to transfer a little scent to the other wrist, gently press them together rather than rubbing.


### Spraying a cloud and walking through it


It may look glamorous, but spraying a cloud of perfume into the air and walking through it is far less effective than applying fragrance directly to your skin. Most of the perfume ends up floating away instead of landing where you actually want it, making it difficult to control how much you’ve applied. A few targeted sprays on your pulse points will give you better longevity, as well as less waste.


### Spraying only on clothes


Clothing can hold fragrance longer than skin, which is why many people spray perfume directly onto their outfits. The downside is that perfume is designed to interact with your skin’s natural warmth and chemistry, so it often smells flatter on fabric. Some fragrances can also stain delicate or light-colored materials. The best approach is to apply perfume primarily to your skin, then lightly mist clothing from a safe distance if you’d like an extra layer of scent.

## Frequently Asked Questions


**Why does my perfume seem to disappear after just a few hours?** The most common reason a scent fades quickly is dry skin. Your skin will absorb the fragrance oils much faster if it isn’t properly hydrated, causing the scent to vanish. The best fix is to apply an unscented body lotion a few minutes before you spritz your perfume. This creates a moisturized base for the fragrance molecules to cling to, giving your scent serious staying power.


**Is it really that bad to rub my wrists together after applying perfume?** Yes, this is one habit you’ll want to break! When you rub your wrists together, the friction creates heat that breaks down the delicate top notes of the fragrance. Those top notes are the first impression of the scent, and damaging them can alter the perfume’s intended character and make it fade much faster. Instead, simply let the perfume air-dry on your skin or gently press your wrists together if you need to transfer the scent.


**Can I make my perfume last longer by spraying it on my clothes or in my hair?** While it might seem like a good idea, it’s best to avoid spraying fragrance directly on your clothes, as the oils can stain delicate fabrics like silk. Perfume is also designed to interact with the warmth of your skin, which allows the scent to develop fully. As for your hair, the alcohol in most perfumes can be very drying. If you want to scent your hair, a safer way is to lightly mist your hairbrush and then run it through your strands.


**How do I find the right number of sprays without overdoing it?** There’s no single magic number, as it depends on the strength of the fragrance you’re wearing. A great rule of thumb is to start with two to three sprays on your pulse points. Stronger concentrations, like an Eau de Parfum, often only need a couple of spritzes, while lighter Eau de Toilettes might need a few more. It’s always better to start with less and add another spray later if you feel you need it.


**Does the weather actually change how my fragrance smells and lasts?** Absolutely. Heat and humidity can intensify a fragrance and also cause it to evaporate more quickly from your skin. This is why heavy, spicy scents can feel overwhelming in the summer, while light, citrusy notes feel perfect. In colder weather, fragrances don’t project as much, so richer, warmer scents with notes of wood or amber tend to perform beautifully and last longer.
