# HR Pulse: Building Tomorrow’s Workforce: The strategic value of upskilling and reskilling

---


## Understanding Upskilling and Reskilling


Upskilling and reskilling share some similarities, but each serves a distinct purpose.


### Upskilling


> Data shows students who gain new skills during their academic journey earn, on average, 15% more than peers who wait to upskill after graduation. (Source:[Skill Up & Stand Out while at College! | LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/skill-up-stand-out-while-college-subhankar-dey-k8j4c/) )


Upskilling focuses on closing skill gaps to advance in a current career path. There are several benefits to prioritizing upskilling whether you are in college or already building your career. Upskilling can enhance your employability because employers increasingly value unique in-demand skills over and above academic degrees. This is particularly relevant in areas like digital literacy, artificial intelligence, data analysis, and other emerging technologies that are revolutionizing industries. By upskilling there also comes the advantage of receiving higher starting salaries. Furthermore, acquiring skills that are less susceptible to automation can help secure long-term job stability.


### Reskilling


Reskilling, on the other hand, involves training employees in entirely new skills to help them transition to a completely different role. For instance, a project manager might learn new skills to become a data scientist.


In 2025, companies see reskilling as a crucial strategy. By reskilling, they can quickly develop talent that is scarce in the market and address key gaps. This approach helps organizations gain a competitive edge and achieve their strategic goals ahead of their rivals.

## The Strategic Value of Upskilling and Reskilling


In today's workplace, mentors, coaches, sponsors, and advisors each play crucial roles in personal and professional development, contributing uniquely to growth. Mentors offer insight and long-term guidance, while coaches focus on structured skill-building, such as upskilling in new areas or reskilling for career transitions. Sponsors advocate for visibility and advancement, creating key opportunities, and advisors provide specialized strategic insights to align decisions with broader objectives. Together, these roles form a powerful network that fosters continuous learning, adaptability, and success.


> **[Between 2023 and 2033, STEM occupations are projected to grow by 10.4%, significantly outpacing the 4% growth rate for all occupations. This growth is driven by increasing demand in areas such as computing, engineering, and advanced manufacturing.](https://www.idtech.com/blog/stem-education-statistics) *(*[Via US Bureau of Labor Statistics](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/stem-employment.htm) *)* .[Specifically, software development employment is projected to grow 17%](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/computer-and-information-technology/software-developers.htm) .**


The tech and aerospace industries are experiencing rapid growth, causing the market to evolve at an unprecedented pace. Staying informed is crucial for maintaining a competitive edge in your profession. With the advancement of technologies like artificial intelligence, machine learning, quantum computing, cloud solutions, and cyber security, all professionals, including students and those early in their careers, can benefit from lifelong learning. By staying ahead of trends, continuously acquiring knowledge, prioritizing improvement, embracing change, and quickly adapting to industry shifts allows employees to distinguish themselves and thrive in their careers.


## New Skills - Advantages and Opportunities


When employees acquire new skills, they not only advance their personal development but also contribute significantly to an organization's progress toward future objectives and deliverables. Organizations that prioritize and invest in training can enhance talent retention, as employees feel valued and supported. This investment, in turn, can elevate job satisfaction, motivation, and morale. When employees are provided with clear goals and the necessary tools to achieve them, overall engagement increases.

## Steering Towards Success - How to Drive Your Own Career Development


Gone are the days when mastering a single skill could carry you through your entire 40+ year career. The modern workplace evolves too quickly for that. But here is the silver lining: you do not have to wait for your manager to hand you a development plan. You can and should take charge of your own career growth.


**Begin with Self-Reflection** Before you run and jump into acquiring new skills, pause and reflect on where you genuinely want to go. What kind of work makes you lose track of time? Which projects invigorate you rather than exhaust you? Your career goals should align with what genuinely excites and interests you, not just what looks impressive on a resume.


**Chart Your Career Path** Once you have a clear destination in mind, outline the steps to reach your goal. If you are aiming for a role in project management, for example, what skills and certifications will facilitate that transition? Connect with people who already hold your dream job and learn about their career journey. You can connect internally within an organization or externally on professional forums and platforms such as LinkedIn — many professionals are happy to share their experiences over an in-person, or virtual coffee chat.


**Develop Your Learning Pipeline** Think of skill development as a pipeline: some skills you are actively learning, others you are practicing, and still others you are mastering. Focus on one or two skills at a time. Perhaps you spend six months mastering data visualization, then move on to honing your presentation skills while continuing to apply your new data skills in real projects. The learning pipeline is a journey that helps employees continuously develop their skills and advance their careers, leading to better job performance and satisfaction.


**Apply Theory to Practice** Reading about new skills is beneficial but applying them is where real learning occurs. Volunteer for projects that challenge your abilities. If your company does not offer these opportunities, think about how to create them, for example, start a lunch-and-learn series, take on side projects, or contribute to open-source initiatives in your field.


**Build Your Portfolio** As you acquire new skills, document your progress. Maintain a portfolio of projects where you have applied your new skills. Keep a work journal to track your learning journey and highlight concrete achievements. When promotion time comes, you will have a ready-made record of your growth you can share.


**Make Learning a Daily Habit** The most successful professionals treat learning like brushing their teeth or exercising —it is a daily routine. Set aside regular time for development, even if it is just 20 minutes each morning or several times per week to read industry news, practice a new tool, or attend an online learning event. Small, consistent efforts accumulate faster than you would expect.


**Own Your Career Development** Your career development is too important to leave to someone else; it is imperative you take ownership. While your company can provide resources and opportunities, it is up to you to identify what you want to learn. Be proactive and seek growth opportunities and put new skills into practice—think of yourself as the CEO of your own career, because that is exactly what you are.


## Building tomorrow’s workforce is a shared journey between employers and employees


As automation and AI reshape industries, our most valuable assets remain our employees —professionals who blend deep expertise with adaptability and growth.


In today's fast-paced world, talent development is proactive. Thriving organizations will see upskilling and reskilling not as mere reactions to change, but as ongoing processes of growth and adaptation. They will foster cultures where learning is continuous, expertise is celebrated, and innovative technologies are embraced as tools to amplify employee potential.


**In an era where continuous change is the only constant, our dedication to nurturing our people must be steadfast. The future of work is not solely about advanced tools and systems; it is about evolving ways of working, learning, and growing together. Let us commit to this journey, ensuring a brighter, more innovative future for all.**
