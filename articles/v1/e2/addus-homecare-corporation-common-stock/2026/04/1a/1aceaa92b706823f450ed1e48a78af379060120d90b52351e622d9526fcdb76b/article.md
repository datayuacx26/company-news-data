# Occupational Therapy Month: Helping People Live Safely and Independently at Home

April is Occupational Therapy Month, a time during the year to highlight the critical role occupational therapists play in helping individuals maintain independence, safety, and quality of life at home.


While many people associate therapy with recovery after an injury or hospital stay, occupational therapy focuses on something much broader: helping individuals continue doing the everyday activities that matter most to them.


From getting dressed in the morning to preparing meals or moving safely throughout the home, occupational therapy supports people in living life on their terms.


## **What is Occupational Therapy?**


Occupational therapy (OT) focuses on helping individuals safely and independently perform the everyday activities that make up daily life. These are often referred to as “activities of daily living,” and they include essential tasks like bathing, dressing, eating, and moving around the home.


But occupational therapy goes beyond simply completing these tasks. It looks at the whole person, their physical abilities, cognitive function, environment, and routines, to understand what may be making daily activities more difficult and how to improve them.


Occupational therapists are trained to evaluate not only *what* someone is struggling with, but *why* . For example, difficulty getting dressed may be related to limited mobility, decreased strength, balance issues, or even challenges with memory or sequencing steps.


From there, OTs develop personalized approaches to help individuals maintain or regain independence. This can include:


- **Improving strength, coordination, and balance** to make movements safer and more manageable
- **Teaching new techniques** to complete tasks in a way that reduces strain or risk
- **Introducing adaptive tools** , such as grab bars, shower chairs, or modified utensils
- **Recommending changes to the home environment** to prevent falls and improve accessibility
- **Supporting cognitive function** , including memory, organization, and problem-solving skills


Occupational therapy is highly individualized. What works for one person may look completely different for another, depending on their health condition, living situation, and personal goals.


It also plays a role across many different situations, including:


- Recovery after surgery or hospitalization
- Managing chronic conditions such as arthritis or stroke
- Supporting individuals with neurological conditions
- Helping older adults maintain independence as their needs change over time


At its core, occupational therapy is about adapting to life’s changes without losing independence. It helps individuals continue doing the things that are important to them, even when faced with physical or cognitive challenges.


## **Supporting Independence Across Every Level of Care**


Occupational therapy plays an important role across the full continuum of care, but how it is applied can look different depending on an individual’s needs.


From hands-on clinical therapy to daily support and comfort-focused care, each level plays a unique role in helping individuals remain safe, independent, and supported at home.


****Home Health (HH): Skilled Therapy and Functional Recovery****


Home health is where occupational therapy is most directly delivered as a clinical, goal-oriented service.


Occupational therapists in home health work one-on-one with patients to address specific challenges and help them regain or improve their ability to perform daily activities.


This often includes:


- Evaluating physical and cognitive limitations impacting daily life
- Developing personalized treatment plans focused on functional goals
- Teaching safer, more effective ways to complete tasks like bathing, dressing, and meal preparation
- Recommending and training patients on the use of adaptive equipment
- Identifying necessary home modifications to improve safety and accessibility


Home health occupational therapy is typically short-term and focused on recovery, rehabilitation, and measurable progress, often following a hospital stay, illness, or injury.


**Personal Care Services (PCS): Applying Therapy in Everyday Life**


Personal care services focus on **non-medical, day-to-day support** , which makes them essential in carrying occupational therapy strategies into real life.


While occupational therapists may develop techniques and recommend safer ways to complete tasks, PCS caregivers are often the ones who help individuals apply those strategies consistently each day.


This can include:


- Assisting with bathing, dressing, and grooming using techniques that promote safety and independence
- Supporting mobility throughout the home, helping prevent falls and reinforce proper movement
- Encouraging routines that align with therapy goals, such as structured daily activities
- Observing changes in ability and communicating concerns to the broader care team


PCS plays a critical role in maintaining progress over time, ensuring that the skills developed through therapy continue to be practiced and supported in a real-world setting.


**Hospice (HOS): Adapting Care for Comfort and Quality of Life**


In hospice care, the focus of occupational therapy shifts from recovery to comfort, dignity, and meaningful engagement at the end of life.


Rather than working toward long-term improvement, occupational therapy in hospice helps individuals adapt their daily activities to match their current abilities and energy levels to promote their quality of life.


This may involve:


- Modifying routines to reduce fatigue and discomfort
- Recommending positioning techniques to improve comfort and prevent strain
- Supporting safe, gentle movement to maintain as much independence as possible
- Helping patients continue participating in meaningful activities, even in small ways


In this setting, success is not measured by progress or recovery, but by quality of life, ensuring individuals can continue to experience comfort, familiarity, and connection in their daily routines.


**Different Roles, One Shared Goal**


While personal care, home health, and hospice services each play a different role, they are all connected by a shared purpose: supporting individuals in living safely and comfortably at home.


Occupational therapy is a key part of that continuum, whether it is helping someone regain independence, maintain their abilities, or adapt to life’s changes with dignity.
