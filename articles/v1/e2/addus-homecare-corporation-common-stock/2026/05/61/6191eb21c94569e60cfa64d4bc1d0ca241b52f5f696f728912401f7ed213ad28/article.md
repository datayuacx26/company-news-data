# Supporting Dignity and Choice at Home

When someone you love begins to need more support as they age, it is natural to want to step in and help right away. You may notice changes in their daily routine, concerns about safety, difficulty recovering after an illness, or a need for more comfort and support at home. For many families, this can bring up difficult questions: How much help is enough? When is it time to get support? How do you step in without making your loved one feel like they are losing control?


Care decisions are deeply personal. While families often want to protect the people they love, it is important to remember that support does not have to mean taking over. In many cases, the right kind of care can help a loved one feel more secure, more comfortable, and more involved in the choices that affect their daily life.


At its heart, helping a loved one at home is about preserving dignity. It is about listening first, offering support with respect, and creating a care plan that honors who they are, what they value, and where they feel most comfortable.


## **Support Starts with Listening**


When a loved one’s needs begin to change, families may feel pressure to make quick decisions. While some situations do require immediate action, many conversations about care are more successful when they begin with listening.


Instead of starting with what you think needs to happen, try asking what matters most to your loved one. They may be concerned about staying in their home, keeping a familiar routine, maintaining privacy, spending time with family, or continuing to make decisions about their own care.


Simple questions can help open the conversation:


“What would make you feel more comfortable at home?”
“What parts of your routine are most important to you?”
“What kind of help would feel useful?”
“What are you worried about right now?”


These conversations may not always be easy, and they may take time. However, giving your loved one the opportunity to share their thoughts can help them feel respected rather than managed.


## **Support Does Not Mean Taking Away Choice**


One of the biggest fears many people have when they begin receiving help is that they will lose their independence, privacy, or voice in decision-making. Families can help ease that fear by offering choices whenever possible.


That may mean allowing a loved one to help decide what time of day support is provided, which daily tasks they want help with, what meals they prefer, where they feel most comfortable receiving care, or who should be included in care conversations.


Even when a person needs more assistance, they can often still participate in meaningful decisions. Including them helps reinforce that care is something being done with them, not simply for them.


The goal is not to remove responsibility from your loved one. The goal is to build support around them in a way that protects their safety, comfort, and dignity.


## **Matching Support to the Person’s Needs**


The right type of care depends on the person’s situation. Some individuals may need help with daily activities. Others may need skilled care after an illness, injury, surgery, or hospitalization. Some families may be facing a serious or life-limiting illness and need guidance focused on comfort and quality of life.


Home-based care can take many forms, and each type of support can help preserve choice in a different way.


**Personal Care Services** can help individuals remain at home while receiving support with everyday activities such as bathing, dressing, meal preparation, mobility, errands, light housekeeping, or companionship. This kind of help can make daily life safer and more manageable while allowing the person to continue familiar routines in the place they know best.


**Home Health** can provide skilled support for individuals recovering from illness, surgery, injury, or hospitalization, or those managing a health condition at home. Through services such as nursing, therapy, and education, home health care can help patients stay involved in their recovery goals and better understand how to manage their care.


**Hospice** focuses on comfort, dignity, and quality of life for individuals facing a life-limiting illness. For patients and families, hospice can help honor personal wishes, manage symptoms, support emotional and spiritual needs, and allow care to take place in a familiar and comforting environment whenever possible.


Across each of these services, the focus remains the same: meeting people where they are and helping them receive the right support at the right time.


## **Keeping Your Loved One Involved**


As care needs change, it can be easy for conversations to shift toward schedules, appointments, tasks, and responsibilities. While those details matter, it is just as important to keep your loved one involved in the process.


This may include asking for their preferences, explaining changes before they happen, including them in care planning conversations, and checking in regularly about how they feel. Even small choices, such as what to wear, what to eat, when to rest, or how to spend the day, can help a person feel more in control.


For families, this approach can also help reduce tension. When loved ones feel heard, they may be more open to accepting support. When care is introduced as a way to help them continue living according to their values, it can feel less like a loss and more like a partnership.


## **Helping Families Feel More Confident**


Supporting a loved one at home can be emotional and overwhelming. Family members may wonder if they are doing enough, making the right decisions, or recognizing when additional help is needed.


Professional care can offer reassurance by giving families extra support, education, and guidance. It can also help reduce some of the pressure family caregivers may feel as they balance work, family, personal responsibilities, and the needs of a loved one.


Most importantly, care at home can help families focus on what matters most: spending meaningful time together, honoring their loved one’s preferences, and helping them feel safe, comfortable, and respected.


## **Care That Honors Choice, Dignity, and Home**


Helping a loved one does not have to mean taking over. The right support can help people remain connected to their routines, their families, their wishes, and the place they call home.


Whether a loved one needs help with daily activities, skilled support after a health change, or comfort-focused care during a serious illness, home-based care can help families navigate changing needs with compassion and respect.
