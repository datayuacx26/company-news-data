# How to Send Money from Nigeria to Spain

I got a message a few months ago from someone I'd met at a Lagos tech event. He had relocated to Madrid for a postgraduate programme, and he was frustrated. His family needed money back home urgently, and he had spent two days trying to figure out how to do it from Spain. Not the other way around. He wanted to send naira to Nigeria from Europe, but his question pointed at something bigger: the whole Nigeria-Spain corridor is confusing in both directions.


That conversation stuck with me because it captures something I hear often. Nigerians in Spain need to send money home. Nigerians in Nigeria need to send money to family members studying or working in Spain. The corridor is real, the volume is real, but the clear guidance has been almost nonexistent.


This post is my attempt to fix that.


## The Nigeria-Spain Corridor Is Bigger Than Most People Realise


Spain has become a notable destination for Nigerian professionals and students over the past several years. Healthcare workers recruited directly by Spanish hospitals, postgraduate students at universities in Barcelona and Madrid, and a growing number of IT professionals working for European companies have all contributed to a diaspora that now moves money regularly between both countries.


When I looked at what was actually available online for someone asking "how do I send money from Nigeria to Spain," I found mostly service landing pages from Wise, Remitly, and WorldRemit. Helpful as comparison tools, but they don't actually explain the mechanics: what documents you need, why rates vary so much, or what the real cost is once you account for exchange rate margins.


So let me walk through it properly.


## What You Are Actually Paying (And What It Really Costs)


I'll be direct about something that trips people up every time: the fee you see advertised is rarely the full story. Most services charge a small flat fee or percentage, then make their real margin on the exchange rate spread. When you're converting naira to euros, that spread can be significant.


The official NAFEM (Nigeria Autonomous Foreign Exchange Market) rate for the naira has been volatile, and anyone who has tried to fund an international transfer from a Nigerian bank account in the past two years knows the process can be unpredictable. Some banks require documentation for outward remittances above certain thresholds. Some charge transaction fees that are opaque until the money has already left your account.


My habit is to check the mid-market rate on a currency data site before I do any transfer. That mid-market rate is the reference point: the number neither side is actually willing to give you. Any service you use will offer something worse than that. The question is how much worse.


For the NGN/EUR pair, a rate gap of 1.5% to 3% is typical across reputable services. Above 3%, you're paying more than you should. I've seen some traditional bank channels sit at 5% to 7% margin on this corridor, often without advertising it explicitly.


## Sending Money from Nigeria to Spain: Your Real Options


### Online Transfer Services


One thing to watch: not all services support NGN as a source currency at the same level of reliability. Some route the conversion through USD or GBP first, which can add friction and occasionally an extra spread. Check that the service you're using genuinely supports NGN-to-EUR, not a workaround.


### Nigerian Bank International Transfers


The major Nigerian banks (Access, GTBank, Zenith, UBA, First Bank) can all process international wire transfers to Spanish bank accounts. This is the most traditional route and works reliably. The tradeoff is cost and speed. Bank wire transfers to Europe typically take two to five business days, and the exchange rates are generally less competitive than specialist transfer services.


If you are sending a large amount, say, school fees or a significant rent payment, a bank wire might make sense for the paper trail and institutional trust. For smaller regular transfers, it is usually not the best value.


One practical note: if you are sending from a Nigerian corporate account rather than a personal account, the documentation requirements are more stringent. You will need an invoice or a clear purpose declaration. This applies to any channel, not just banks.


### Sending from Spain to Nigeria


For Nigerians already in Spain who want to send money back home, you actually have more options, not fewer. Most European-based transfer services handle EUR-to-NGN well because Nigeria is a major remittance destination.


The key consideration when sending from Spain is timing. The naira rate fluctuates, and the exchange rate your recipient gets depends on when the funds are converted, not when you initiate the transfer. For very large amounts, it is worth checking what rate lock mechanisms your platform offers.


## What Most People Get Wrong


The single biggest mistake I see is people treating all transfer services as interchangeable. They pick the first result on Google, which is usually Wise, and assume the rate is the best available. Sometimes it is. Often it isn't.


My actual recommendation is to compare at least two services before each transfer, particularly for amounts above 100,000 naira or 100 euros. The spread difference between services on any given day can be enough to matter.


The second mistake is ignoring daily transfer limits. Some platforms cap individual transfers, which means a single school fees payment might require splitting across multiple transactions with added fees each time. Check the limits before you commit to a service for a specific purpose.


The third mistake is underestimating documentation. If you are sending from Nigeria above a certain threshold, your bank may require documentation of the purpose. Preparing those documents in advance, rather than scrambling after the transfer is flagged, saves significant stress.


## For Business Payments Between Nigeria and Spain


The picture looks different if you are a Nigerian business paying a Spanish supplier or a Spanish company paying a Nigerian contractor. Standard retail transfer apps are often not built for this. They have lower limits, less favourable business rates, and customer support that isn't set up for corporate queries.


Business-oriented platforms give you more tools: invoicing integration, purpose codes that satisfy compliance requirements, and account structures that make reconciliation straightforward. Grey Business and Afriex both support B2B cross-border flows. I'd encourage you to look at what fits your specific transaction size and frequency before defaulting to a consumer app.


One thing that came out of the Montran Africa Market Infrastructure Summit held in Nairobi in July 2026 is worth knowing: the Central Bank of Kenya and several other African central banks are actively working toward more interoperable African payment infrastructure. The target is to reduce the reliance on correspondent banking for intra-African transfers. That doesn't directly affect the Nigeria-Spain corridor, but it reflects a broader shift in how African financial infrastructure is being built. The cost of cross-border transfers is going down over time, and that will eventually include African-European corridors as well.


## What to Have Ready Before You Send


Before you initiate a transfer, regardless of the platform you choose, prepare these:


Your identification documents. A valid Nigerian international passport is the most universally accepted. A national ID can work on some platforms for smaller amounts but may not suffice for larger ones.


Your recipient's full bank details. For a Spanish bank account, you need the IBAN (International Bank Account Number), which is a 24-character code beginning with ES. You may also need the bank's SWIFT/BIC code depending on the platform. Getting this wrong causes delays that can take several days to resolve.


A clear purpose for the transfer. This is especially relevant for larger amounts. "Family support," "school fees," "business payment" are all valid purposes, but you may need to document the last two with supporting paperwork if amounts are significant.
