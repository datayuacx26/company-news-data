# How Upsolve Is Free

**In a Nutshell**


A note from our CEO on why transparency matters to us.


**Written by[Jonathan Petts](https://upsolve.org/authors/jonathan-petts/) .**
Updated July 8, 2025


---


Dear Community,


Transparency is one of our nonprofit’s core values. That’s why it’s important to us that you know how we can offer[Upsolve's services](https://upsolve.org/learn/how-upsolve-works/) for free, how we spend our money, and who owns Upsolve.


## How Is Upsolve Free?


First, we receive funding from the[Legal Services Corporation](https://www.lsc.gov/grants-grantee-resources/resources-topic-type/legal-aid-organizations-upsolve-offers-way-do) (LSC), a government-funded nonprofit established by the U.S. Congress. We’re lucky that Congress believes that low-income people should receive free access to their legal rights. LSC funding helps us build and maintain our free tool.


Second, we receive funding from charitable foundations and individuals. Some of these individuals are wealthy people who want to give back. Some of them are Upsolve users who want to pay it forward. These include leading foundations like the[Robin Hood Foundation](https://www.robinhood.org/) , the[Hewlett Foundation](https://hewlett.org/) , and the[New York Bar Foundation](https://tnybf.org/) . They also include individual donors like[Eric Schmidt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Schmidt) , the former CEO of Google;[Chris Sacca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Sacca) , an early investor in Instagram, Uber, and Twitter; and[Jim Breyer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Breyer) , an early investor in Facebook.


Third, we receive funding from private independent attorneys who provide free evaluations to people on our website who ask for them. This is important because we recognize:


-


Only an attorney can give you legal advice.


-


Only an attorney can tell you whether you should file for bankruptcy or not.


-


Our self-service software tool is not a good fit for everyone.[Read more about the limits of who we can help](https://upsolve.org/learn/frequently-asked-questions-about-upsolve/) . We make no representation of the quality of these private attorneys and they have no affiliation with Upsolve. Many people decide, following a free evaluation, not to file for bankruptcy, or, if they qualify for our tool, to use it for their bankruptcy.


Finally, we also partner with trusted organizations like[Solo](https://link.upsolve.org/solosuit) and[Cambridge Credit Counseling](https://calendly.com/camupsolve-appt/upsolve-appointment) . If you sign up through the links on our site, we may earn a small referral fee. This kind of support helps keep Upsolve free for everyone who needs it — and we only recommend partners we truly believe can help.


To be a more effective, sustainable, and impactful nonprofit, we believe it's important to generate earned revenue.[Read more on why](https://upsolve.org/learn/frequently-asked-questions-about-upsolve/) . About 70% of our total revenue comes from government funding and charitable donations, and about 30% comes from program service revenue.


## How Does Upsolve Spend Money?


Our number one expense is salaries. Running a technology nonprofit is expensive, as it requires a specialized set of skills that we need to hire for. We also spend money on our office rent, traveling to conferences, insurance, software, and marketing. We spend a small amount of money on accounting and bookkeeping, team building, and office supplies.


## Who Owns Upsolve?


We’re a nonprofit. This means that the American people own Upsolve. You own Upsolve just as much as I do. You can learn more about[how Upsolve got started here](https://upsolve.org/learn/our-story/) .


Best,


Jonathan Petts


CEO and Co-Founder
