# How to Turn Buyer Signals Into Pipeline Before the Moment Passes

Your rep did everything right. Every email, every call, the LinkedIn touch, all on schedule. The sequence finished clean. And the deal still never happened.


Somewhere in the middle, the buyer made up their mind. Your rep never saw the moment it happened.


For years, sales ran on activity. More emails, more calls, more steps completed. Activity still matters, but it has become a weak signal of progress. A rep can finish every step in a cadence and create no pipeline


. A buyer can get ten touches and feel like none of them were relevant. Think about your own inbox this week and how many sales emails had nothing to do with anything you care about.


There is often a delay between when a buyer shows interest and when a seller acts on it. That is the gap the


[Vidyard and Salesloft](https://www.vidyard.com/press-releases/clari-salesloft-partnership/) partnership helps close. Someone engages, the signal sits buried in a tool, and the follow-up lands a day too late. Salesloft helps your team see who to focus on and when. Vidyard helps sellers show up in that moment with something personal and relevant.


## Buyers decide before they talk to you


Buyers have more access to information than ever. They compare vendors, read reviews, ask their network, and run research through AI, all before they talk to a seller. By the time someone books a demo, they usually have a strong point of view already. According to


[6sense’s B2B Buyer Experience Report](https://6sense.com/newsroom/84-of-b2b-deals-are-decided-before-marketers-even-know-about-them/) , the first vendor a buyer contacts wins the business 84% of the time. You often enter a process that is already well underway.


The good news is that buyers leave a trail. Every click, every search, every video they watch is a signal. The problem was never a shortage of signals. It was the lack of structure around them. Winning here comes down to reading the right signals and acting at the right moment.


## Not every signal carries the same weight


An email open tells you a message was probably seen. A click tells you someone was curious. Both are useful, but neither tells you how much attention you earned.


Video changes the read. When someone clicks play, they choose to spend time with what you sent. You can see how much they watched, where they dropped off, whether they replayed a section, and whether they forwarded it to a colleague. A new viewer is a buying group expanding in real time. Someone scanning an email and someone watching a personalized video to the end are different buyers, and they should get different follow-up.


## Where Salesloft and Vidyard come together


When a buyer watches a Vidyard video, that engagement becomes a signal Salesloft can act on, and it all happens in one place.


Reps record or pull a video and send it without leaving Salesloft. When the buyer watches, Vidyard sends the signal back to Salesloft, which surfaces a


[prioritized task in the rep’s workflow](https://www.salesloft.com/platform/rhythm) with the context of what triggered it. When someone watches more than 75% of a video, the rep gets a real-time notification while the interest is still live.


That notification is the visible part. Underneath it, the signal flows through Salesloft’s Predictive Revenue System. There it gets weighed against everything else happening on the account.


Salesloft looks at the whole buying group, not just the person who watched the video. It combines that signal with every other touchpoint on the account. It scores the combination against deal stage and win patterns. Then it ranks the account against everything else in the rep’s queue. What comes out is a prioritized task, with the reasoning attached. The rep knows exactly which account to act on first, and why.


Once Salesloft surfaces the moment and prioritizes the action, the next question is how to respond. Vidyard gives you two paths.


### Path one: the rep responds


The follow-up should match the signal. Someone who watched an onboarding


video gets next-step recommendations. Someone who booked a meeting gets a short confirmation video that previews the agenda. Someone who replayed the pricing section gets a follow-up on that exact topic.


This works because it meets the buyer where they are. Once you know what held their attention, the follow-up can speak to it directly. A relevant note tied to what they actually watched lands far better than another generic touchpoint.


### Path two: the system responds


Sometimes the rep is busy and the moment will not wait.


[InsideSales research](https://www.insidesales.com/response-time-matters/) found conversion rates can be up to 8x higher in the first five minutes after a signal. Speed matters because buyer attention has a short shelf life, not because every buyer needs a call the instant they engage. The longer you wait, the more likely the buyer has moved on to another priority or a vendor who answered faster.


This is where Vidyard’s


[Video Agent](https://www.vidyard.com/products/video-agent/) comes in. Teams can trigger a personalized video response automatically, based on what a buyer just did. Submit a demo request, and the system sends a personalized intro from the assigned rep, built with


[their digital likeness.](https://www.vidyard.com/products/ai-avatars/) Book a meeting, and a confirmation video previews the agenda. Go quiet after signing up, and a feature highlight brings them back.


Both paths do the same job. They keep the next touch personal and relevant, and they make it happen while the buyer still remembers why they engaged.


## Start with one place, not everywhere


Adding video everywhere at once is overwhelming and it rarely works. Pick the one place where conversion is weakest, give it a metric you can measure, and expand from there.


Here is how that played out for us at Vidyard. Our no-show rate was already ahead of the industry, but we still had about 13% of booked meetings going unattended. That is more than one in ten opportunities lost.


So we focused on the moment right after someone books, when intent is highest and attention drops fast. We set up Video Agent to automatically send a personalized confirmation video from the assigned rep the second a meeting was booked. The buyer got to pre-meet the person they would be talking to, with no extra work for the rep. In the first month, our


[no-show rate dropped from 13% to 9%, a 33% reduction](https://www.vidyard.com/blog/video-sales-agent-no-show-rate-success-story/) . From there we expanded the same approach across the funnel.


That was our starting point. Yours might be speeding up proposals, getting prospects to a second meeting, or helping new customers hit a milestone. Wherever conversion is weakest, start there.
