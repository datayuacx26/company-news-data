# AARTO compliance: what South African fleets need to do now

South Africa’s AARTO rollout is no longer something fleet operators can put in the “coming soon” folder. Phase 2[took effect on 1 July 2026](https://www.sanews.gov.za/south-africa/implementation-aarto-continues) , extending the administrative handling of traffic infringements and putting more pressure on fleet compliance records.


The Administrative Adjudication of Road Traffic Offences Act, better known as AARTO, moves many road traffic infringements out of the criminal courts and into an administrative process run by the Road Traffic Infringement Agency. Phase 2 extends AARTO to 62 issuing authorities, covering 60 local and metropolitan municipalities, building on the earlier rollout in Johannesburg and Tshwane.


The rollout has expanded beyond Johannesburg and Tshwane, while implementation in Western Cape municipalities[has been suspended](https://www.moneyweb.co.za/news/south-africa/western-cape-aarto-implementation-suspended/) until a later phase. Further phases are planned as the system moves towards national coverage. Other parts of the framework, including a rehabilitation route for repeat offenders and an independent appeals tribunal, are also expected to follow later.


For fleets, the bigger issue is not only the wider footprint. It is the need for clean records, quick responses and evidence that stands up to scrutiny. AARTO is designed to make traffic infringement enforcement more consistent and more administrative. That means a fleet must be able to show, clearly and quickly, who was driving, whether the vehicle was licensed and roadworthy, whether a notice was received, and what was done about it.


## **Demerit points are coming, but not yet**


The demerit points system is the part most drivers worry about, so it is worth being precise: it is not yet in force. Once active, points will be added to a driver’s licence or operator card when a fine is paid, or an enforcement order is issued. A licence will be suspended once a driver exceeds the threshold, currently set at 15 points, and one point will be cleared for every three months without a further infringement.


That part of AARTO is expected in a later phase, currently envisaged for 2027, although no confirmed commencement date has been gazetted.


## **Why records matter more now**


However, fleets should not wait until they receive demerit points before tightening compliance. The administrative process is already changing the day-to-day work of fleet infringement management. If a business cannot show who was driving, how a vehicle was being used, whether licensing and maintenance were up to date, or what happened around a disputed incident, it may struggle to protect the company, the driver, or both.


That is especially difficult with company vehicles. Many fleets work across provinces, depots, customer sites and shifts. The same vehicle may have different drivers on different days. Notices may arrive centrally, electronically, or at an operational site. Without a connected record, a fleet team can lose days piecing together what happened while response deadlines keep moving closer.


This is where fleet management technology becomes more than an efficiency tool. It becomes part of the AARTO compliance record.

## **What fleet operators should focus on**


That is the real opportunity for fleet operators under AARTO. It should not be seen only as a fines process. It is a reason to reassess how the business manages driver accountability, vehicle compliance, speeding, fatigue, distraction, licensing, maintenance and evidence.


The best-prepared fleets can answer four questions quickly. Who was driving? Was the vehicle licensed, properly maintained and fit for the road? What evidence exists if the incident is disputed? And what is being done to stop the behaviour from recurring?
