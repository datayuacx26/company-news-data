## What is ERP software?


Enterprise Resource Planning (ERP) software is a comprehensive suite of integrated business applications that helps organizations automate and integrate their core business processes. ERP software centralizes data and operating information so people get real-time insight into back- and front-office activities instead of waiting for teams to reconcile separate systems.


Typical ERP scope includes Manufacturing/Shop Floor, Inventory, Material Resource Planning (MRP), Financial Processes, Supplier Documentation, Data and Communications, Sales Activities, and Customer Data and Interactions (CRM). In a conventional company, these functions connect purchasing, production, sales, finance, suppliers, customers, and reporting.


Most modern ERP software solutions are deployed via the web and use Cloud-based platforms because they support easier implementation and scalability, easier collaboration between multiple locations or departments, and the ability to automate workflows and dynamically update data across applications. That model works well for common business records. Life science companies need more because the operating record also includes scientific context, material lineage, instrument data, methods, quality checks, and regulated approvals.

## What is ERP software for life sciences?


What is a life sciences ERP? It is ERP software tailored for life sciences companies, including pharmaceutical, biotech and medical device companies, whose business processes face strict regulatory guidelines and require more documentation than a general commercial workflow. A life sciences ERP must track what happened, which version governed the work, which material lots were used, who reviewed the result, and how the final record connects to the source data.


Pharmaceutical companies often need detailed recipe and formula documentation, Electronic Batch Records (EBRs), Master Batch Records (MBRs), and robust inventory controls. They also need lot or serial number tracking so teams can locate affected material quickly during a recall, deviation, investigation, or other correction. Medical device teams may need Device Master Record/Device History Record (DMR/DHR) controls that connect design specifications and production processes to the actual units, lots, inspections, results, and approvals.


Scispot treats this scientific context as core ERP data. Samples, reagents, kits, consumables, equipment, lots, aliquots, plates, batches, protocols, test results, reports, storage locations, shipments, and approvals can live in one connected model. That makes Scispot a life sciences ERP for the work a general enterprise system does not model well.


The distinction matters in practice. A finance ERP can show that a reagent was purchased. Scispot can show which lot was received, where it was stored, when it expires, which protocol consumed it, which sample and instrument run used it, whether the QC checks passed, who approved the result, and which report or COA went to the customer. That is the operating detail life science companies need to run faster and defend the record later.

## Batch Records


Both international and country-specific rules require life science manufacturers to control how products are made. Current Good Manufacturing Practice (cGMP) requires documentation that shows the approved process was followed and the correct inputs were used during each step of production. Batch Records create that evidence. EBRs show what happened during execution, while MBRs define the approved master process or master recipe.


The ability to create and capture EBRs and MBRs electronically removes paper batch record forms and reduces repeated transcription. ERP software with batch record capabilities should connect instructions, materials, execution, checks, exceptions, review, and release in one seamless workflow. That improves efficiency, reduces errors, increases productivity, and strengthens traceability across compliance tasks.


Scispot supports this model through versioned protocols, Labspaces, Labsheets, Inventory Automation, Activity Logs, Page Signing, reviewer approval, QC gates, and controlled reporting. A batch or work order can link the material lots consumed, equipment used, process data, calculations, deviations, signatures, and release output. For formal regulated use, Scispot can be configured and validated for the customer's intended use, with Trust Vault holding the related evidence.


## Device Master Record/Device History Record (DMR/DHR)


Device Master Record/Device History Record (DMR/DHR) workflows play a similar role in medical device development and production. A DMR defines how a device should be made. A DHR shows what happened for a specific lot, unit, or production run and provides evidence that the approved instructions were followed.


Scispot can configure DMR and DHR records by linking controlled specifications, methods, bills of material, work steps, material lots, test results, inspections, deviations, approvals, and final reports. The result is simplified traceability across product design, inventory and bill of materials management, manufacturing evidence, and regulatory requirements.
