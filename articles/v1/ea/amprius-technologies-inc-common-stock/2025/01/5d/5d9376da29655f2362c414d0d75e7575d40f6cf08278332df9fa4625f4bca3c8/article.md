# Understanding New Battery Tech for Electric Vehicles

The global electric vehicles (EV) market is expected to reach over $1 trillion[by 2029](https://www.statista.com/outlook/mmo/electric-vehicles/worldwide) , with a projected growth rate of 6.63% between 2024 and 2029. This clear shift toward electric vehicles is a clarion call for better battery technology.


## Is new battery technology on the horizon for electric cars?


Lithium-ion chemistries currently dominate the market for electric car batteries, but the race is on to develop and implement new technology that gives better performance, cost-effectiveness and sustainability.


With significant advancements being made every day, we are certainly in an era of EV battery transformation.


## The Evolution of EV Battery Technology


### Current EV battery technologies


Lithium-ion batteries are commonly used in today’s electric cars. Their high energy density and long cycle life make them perfect for countless everyday technologies, not just EVs.


Lithium-ion batteries have mainly replaced nickel metal hydride (NiMH) batteries in electric cars. This older technology had lower energy density and discharge rates, which meant shorter driving range and longer charge times.


### Advantages and limitations


Lithium-ion batteries can store a lot of energy in a smaller, lightweight package. Batteries for electric vehicles need to provide enough power without being too heavy, and lithium-ion batteries meet this purpose with a high power-to-weight ratio. In addition, a long lifespan and a low self-discharge make them highly efficient and durable, maintaining longevity and charge.


Despite their advantages, lithium-ion batteries do have some limitations. They are expensive, especially when used in electric vehicles, and can sometimes overheat.


Using cobalt in lithium-ion batteries also poses some[ethical](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-021-01735-z) and cost considerations, which should be considered and mitigated.


### Recent progress in energy density, charging times and cost reductions


Scientists and engineers are constantly working to improve lithium-ion batteries by improving performance, reducing costs and increasing safety.


EV manufacturers are also dedicated to improving electric vehicle performance by improving the batteries used, as these advancements are critical to longer driving time, reduced charge time and, ultimately, higher consumer confidence in EVs.


[Tesla’s new 4680 cell design](https://europe.autonews.com/automakers/tesla-recruits-asian-partners-speed-4680-battery-introduction) , for example, aims to boost energy density by 16%. A single charge will provide longer ranges, and the vehicle will have a lower overall weight, improving performance and efficiency.


Thanks to this progress in battery technology, EVs will be able to accelerate faster, handle better and charge faster.


## Emerging Battery Technologies


### Solid-state batteries


The critical[difference of solid-state battery technology](https://www.laserax.com/blog/solid-state-vs-lithium-ion-batteries) is in the “state” of the electrolyte, which is solid, instead of the liquid or gel electrolytes found in most conventional batteries.


Often a type of ceramic or polymer, the solid electrolyte helps ions move between electrodes without the risks of liquid leaks or fire hazards.


Replacing the liquid electrolytes used in conventional lithium-ion batteries with a solid electrolyte can significantly reduce risks associated with leaks, flammability, and thermal runaway. Ceramic solid electrolytes typically offer high ionic conductivity and mechanical stability, while polymer electrolytes provide better flexibility and ease of processing. Both types contribute to improved safety and, potentially, higher energy density.


Benefits of solid-state batteries:


The design of solid-state batteries means they can potentially pack more energy into a smaller space.
An EV with a solid-state battery should be able to travel further on a single charge and charging could be faster.


Solid-state batteries could improve consumer safety and reduce recalls and insurance costs due to battery incidents.


Although there is significant potential, solid-state batteries face challenges like costly manufacturing, material stability issues, performance limitations and integration hurdles. Advancements in materials and engineering are key to overcoming these barriers for high-performance applications.


Solid-state batteries are a focus for many companies, including QuantumScape,[Samsung](https://chargedevs.com/newswire/the-tech/samsung-sdi-announces-plan-to-mass-produce-solid-state-batteries/) and[LG Chem](https://www.kedglobal.com/batteries/newsView/ked202109240010) .


As a pioneer in solid-state battery research, Toyota’s plans to begin[solid-state battery production by 2026](https://www.cbtnews.com/toyota-to-launch-solid-state-battery-production-by-2026/) , with mass production projected to start around 2030. The company plans to first put solid-state batteries into hybrid vehicles since they use smaller battery packs than fully electric cars.


### Lithium-Sulfur Batteries


With lithium-metal used as the anode and sulfur as the cathode, lithium-sulfur (Li-S) batteries have greater energy capacity and are less expensive than lithium-ion batteries.


Lithium-sulfur has several potential advantages:


- Li-S batteries have more energy per kilogram than lithium-ion batteries because sulfur has a higher theoretical capacity, enabling electric cars powered by these batteries to travel longer distances.
- They’re lighter, which makes them more efficient.
- Compared to lithium-ion battery materials like cobalt, sulfur is abundant and cheap.
- Li-S batteries have the potential to be less expensive to manufacture and make electric vehicles more affordable.


The main problem with lithium-sulfur batteries is their[shorter lifespan](https://www.samaterials.com/lithium-sulfur-batteries-lithium-ion-batteries.html) , which has prevented their widespread adoption as EV car batteries.


Sulfur tends to dissolve into the electrolyte during charging and discharging, causing the “shuttle effect.” Because of that, the battery can’t be charged and discharged as often without its performance degrading.


The lithium-metal anode makes the battery prone to dendrite growth, which can cause thermal runaway or short-circuiting, which can be hazardous.


This means Li-S batteries would need to be replaced more often, which would counteract cost savings from cheaper materials.


Along with longevity issues, Li-S batteries are less conductive. To compensate for this reduced performance, other conductive materials must be added, making the battery design more complicated and heavier.


Small-scale testing of Li-S batteries[shows promise](https://arpa-e.energy.gov/technologies/projects/lithium-sulfur-batteries) , such as Sion Power’s work on batteries for electric vehicles. However, scalability is currently limited, as there are material degradation issues, and no reliable supply chain.


Until these challenges are addressed, lithium-sulfur batteries remain in the research and development (R&D) phase.


### Graphene Batteries


Graphene is a single layer of carbon atoms arranged in a two-dimensional honeycomb lattice. Its unique properties such as high electrical conductivity, mechanical strength and flexibility make it an ideal material for enhancing battery performance.


Here are the potential[benefits of graphene batteries](https://www.graphene-info.com/graphene-batteries) :


- Graphene can be used in a battery’s electrodes to improve conductivity and energy capacity. These batteries work well for devices that need high power output, like electric vehicles, with improved performance and faster charging times.
- Graphene is also a fantastic thermal conductor, helping to dissipate heat quickly and effectively. This means safety improvements for electric vehicles, with less risk of thermal runaway and greater thermal stability.
- Finally, graphene’s structure makes it more durable, reliable and resistant to physical damage so that consumers can expect fewer replacements and less environmental waste with new production methods and cost savings.


Although graphene has many advantages, companies and researchers are still working on overcoming the challenges associated with these batteries.


Producing high-quality graphene is expensive and complicated, making it tough to scale battery production in a cost-effective way.


Graphene components also have problems with stability, for which researchers are looking at coatings and composites to make the batteries last longer and perform consistently.


As a two-dimensional material, graphene is the thinnest in the world while boasting exceptional electrical and thermal conductivity. Samsung recently announced they are leveraging graphene technology to enhance their[watch display’s performance](https://beyond-battery.com/en-us/blogs/beyond-battery-blogs/an-engaging-story-samsungs-curved-watch-and-graphene) . Their curved watch uses graphene to enhance its battery performance, allowing for faster charging and longer battery life.


### Silicon Anode Batteries


Battery technology has seen a recent breakthrough in performance with silicon anode batteries.


Anodes in these lithium-ion batteries use silicon to replace the graphite, with storage up to ten times more lithium ions. This provides a significantly higher energy capacity and longer battery life.


This provides significant advantages over traditional lithium-ion batteries:


- Since silicon can hold more lithium ions, these batteries can store more energy, which means longer usage times for electric vehicles without the need to recharge frequently.
- They offer faster charging times and longer battery life while maintaining consistent performance thanks to higher lithium ion capacities and better conductivity.


Batteries using silicon anodes offer a higher energy density, but during charging, the silicon expands significantly, causing mechanical stress and potentially damaging the battery’s structure.


Through innovative material and cell engineering, Amprius Technologies has[developed silicon anodes](https://amprius.com/technology/) that address the challenges associated with silicon expansion during cycling. The newest silicon anode battery cells have about ten times the capacity of a graphite anode battery! In practical terms, if a graphite anode battery could take an EV 310 miles, a comparable silicon anode Amprius battery could power it for 547 miles.


Other technology improvements include silicon composites, electrolyte optimization and the development of protective coatings for silicon particles.


### Impact on cost


Silicon is one of the most abundant elements on Earth, making it a cost-effective alternative to graphite. Manufacturers would have lower raw material costs and greater scalability potential without material shortages with silicon.


## Industry Trends and Investments


### Automakers and tech companies investing in new battery tech


Automakers are expected to invest around[$1.2 trillion by 2030](https://www.reuters.com/technology/exclusive-automakers-double-spending-evs-batteries-12-trillion-by-2030-2022-10-21/) in developing and producing EVs, batteries and raw materials.


By 2030, automakers intend to produce 54 million electric vehicles, indicating how quickly this industry could potentially move.


## Challenges and Considerations


### Technical and logistical difficulties in adopting new battery technology


[Several logistical challenges](https://www.evengineeringonline.com/what-is-the-ev-battery-supply-chain/) slowing the introduction of new battery technology stem from supply chain limitations, including material supply, manufacturing scalability and the need for specialized infrastructure.


Battery researchers and manufacturers will need strategic planning and investment in coordination with automakers, governments and other stakeholders to make promising technologies a success.


### Regulatory hurdles and market readiness


Battery regulations vary by region, so the industry faces complex regulatory environments.


A clear and consistent battery safety, recycling and environmental standard system will help with market entry and consumer acceptance, as will consumer education about range anxiety, charging infrastructure and total cost of ownership barriers.


### Environmental and sustainability considerations


Consumers are often drawn to electric vehicles because they are eco-friendly and avoid fossil fuels. But battery mining can be harmful to the environment, destroying natural environments and polluting wildlife and local communities.


To make batteries more eco-friendly, we need to improve how EVs and battery technologies are designed and used. By reducing the demand for raw materials and ensuring better sourcing when these materials are a must, batteries can become more sustainable.


As EVs and batteries become more common in our everyday lives, it’s also important to manage their end-of-life. Responsible recycling and disposal takes effort but also reduces the impact of these technologies on the planet.
