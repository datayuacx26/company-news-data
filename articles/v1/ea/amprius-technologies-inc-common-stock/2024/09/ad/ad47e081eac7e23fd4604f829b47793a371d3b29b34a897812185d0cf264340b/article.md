# The History and Future of Vertical Flight

## History of vertical flight – a timeline


### Early aviation milestones


Vertical flight began with Leonardo da Vinci, who drew designs for[flying machines](https://airandspace.si.edu/stories/editorial/leonardo-da-vinci-and-flight) , including a kind of helicopter, around 500 years ago.


While his name may not be as widely recognized as some of his contemporaries, Sir George Cayley’s profound insights and pioneering work significantly advanced our understanding of flight principles.


He conducted extensive research and experimentation to explore how the[design and movement of wings](https://airandspace.si.edu/stories/editorial/leonardo-da-vinci-and-flight) influenced an aircraft’s ability to remain aloft. This groundbreaking work led to critical insights into the generation of “lift” (the force that keeps an aircraft airborne) and “drag” (the resistance that opposes its forward motion).


In the mid-19th century, French inventor Gustave de Ponton d’Amécourt built a small,[steam-powered](https://philadelphiaencyclopedia.org/essays/helicopters/) model helicopter, marking one of the earliest practical attempts at vertical flight.


### Developments in helicopter design


In the 20th century, significant progress in vertical flight occurred. In 1939, Igor Sikorsky’s[VS-300 helicopter](https://www.asme.org/wwwasmeorg/media/resourcefiles/aboutasme/who%20we%20are/engineering%20history/landmarks/95-sikorsky-vs-300-helicopter.pdf) (PDF) prototype demonstrated the practicality of vertical flight.


During World War II, helicopters played vital roles in medical evacuations and reconnaissance. After the war, the Bell 47 achieved[commercial success](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/air-space-magazine/ode-to-the-bubble-32230906/) beyond the military. The 1950s saw iconic helicopters like the Bell UH-1 Huey.


In terms of design, tandem-rotor helicopters like the Piasecki H-21 Shawnee and the Boeing CH-47 Chinook brought increased lifting power and stability. The coaxial rotor system, as used in helicopters like the Kamov Ka-50 and Ka-52, enhanced maneuverability.


The Fenestron tail rotor design, adopted in helicopters like the Eurocopter EC135, reduced noise and increased safety.


The 21st century brought fly-by-wire technology, composite materials and NOTAR systems that improved stability, performance and safety. Variable rotor systems, as seen in helicopters like the Eurocopter X3 and Sikorsky X2, enabled both vertical and high-speed flight.


Today, modern helicopters incorporate advanced avionics with global positioning systems (GPS) and glass cockpit displays which[enhance safety and efficiency](https://www.ntsb.gov/safety/safety-studies/Documents/SS1001.pdf) .


### Modern advancements in VTOL aircraft


For VTOL aircraft (Vertical Take Off and Landing), the Tiltrotor aircraft, typified by the[Bell Boeing V-22 Osprey](https://www.boeing.com/features/2023/06/22-fast-facts-on-the-bell-boeing-v-22-osprey) , stand out as a significant development.


These aircraft can execute vertical takeoff and landing like helicopters but also have the ability to tilt their engines to function like traditional airplanes. This design allows them to attain higher speeds and cover longer distances efficiently.


Another noteworthy innovation is the emergence of electric VTOL ([eVTOL](https://amprius.com/evtol/) ) aircraft. These aircraft utilize electric propulsion systems instead of conventional internal combustion engines. Advantages of eVTOLs include reduced noise, lower greenhouse gas emissions and enhanced cost efficiency.


Ducted fan aircraft represent another intriguing development. These VTOL aircraft incorporate enclosed rotor blades within a duct, enhancing safety and operational efficiency. Unmanned aerial vehicles ([UAVs](https://amprius.com/energy-density-uas/) ), commonly known as drones, have also evolved with VTOL capabilities.


## Advantages of vertical flight


### Mobility and maneuverability at low speeds


Vertical flight allows for precise control and maneuverability at low speeds. This capability hinges on the unique ability of these aircraft to hover in place and control their lift and direction with great accuracy.


Vertical flight is particularly valuable in tasks that demand precision, such as search and rescue missions or aerial surveys.


### Greater safety in challenging landing and takeoff conditions


Unlike traditional fixed-wing aircraft that need long runways, vertical flight enables takeoff and landing in confined spaces.


In challenging environments, like mountainsides or small ship decks, the ability to hover and execute precise landings or takeoffs significantly mitigates risks. Hovering enhances safety for both the aircraft and its occupants.


### Flexibility for short-distance travel compared to horizontal flight


As noted, the ability to perform vertical takeoffs and landings eliminates the need for extensive runway infrastructure.


This attribute becomes particularly significant in urban or densely populated areas, where space for airports and runways is limited.


When the requirement is to move swiftly across a city or reach nearby destinations, helicopters and VTOL aircraft can utilize helipads, rooftops or open spaces for takeoff and landing, minimizing the time and logistics associated with ground transportation to airports.


### Increased fuel efficiency over longer distances


While traditional helicopters have been associated with limited fuel economy for extended travel, innovations such as tiltrotor aircraft can perform vertical takeoffs and then transition to a more efficient airplane-like mode for cruising at higher speeds. This technology offers improved fuel efficiency over extended distances.


Additionally, the development of eVTOL powered by electricity promises enhanced fuel efficiency, contributing to the quest for sustainable long-distance vertical flight solutions.


### Reduced noise pollution from engines


Traditional airplane jet engines can be quite loud, especially during takeoff and landing.


However, with vertical flight, the noise can be significantly reduced. Some helicopters use “quiet” rotor designs and advanced technology to keep their noise levels lower.


Electric VTOL aircraft have the potential to reduce noise pollution further. Electric propulsion systems generate less noise than traditional jet engines, enhancing their suitability for urban and suburban operations where minimizing noise disruption is critical.


### Lower operating costs due to simplified maintenance requirements


Vertical flight offers the advantage of lower operating costs thanks to simplified maintenance requirements.


There are fewer moving parts and shorter runways, resulting in reduced wear and tear. Further, their engines and components are frequently more accessible, simplifying routine inspections and repairs.


With eVTOL, maintenance requirements are poised to become even more straightforward, as electric propulsion systems typically entail even fewer intricate components and demand less maintenance than conventional engines.


### Potentially lower risk of accidents due to reduced airspeed and better visibility


Unlike fixed-wing aircraft that require a certain minimum airspeed for lift, helicopters and VTOL aircraft can operate at lower speeds and hover in place.


This characteristic enhances safety during critical phases of flight, such as takeoff and landing, where precise control and slower speeds are helpful.


The design of these aircraft often provides better visibility to the pilot, as the cockpit configuration allows for a more unobstructed view of the surroundings, further reducing the likelihood of accidents caused by limited visibility.


## Challenges of vertical flight


### Increased vulnerability to turbulence, wind, and weather conditions


Vertical flight aircraft can be more susceptible to turbulence and adverse weather compared to fixed-wing aircraft.


Their ability to hover and operate at lower speeds makes them more sensitive to gusty winds and turbulence, affecting stability and control. Pilots need to exercise caution and skill when flying in challenging weather conditions.


### Costly development and maintenance requirements


Innovative technologies like tilt-rotors or electric propulsion systems often come with research and development expenses, translating into higher acquisition costs.


In addition to the initial acquisition costs, the operational expenses of vertical flight technology can also be significant.


The maintenance and repair of complex rotor systems and avionics demand skilled technicians and specialized facilities, contributing to ongoing operating costs. And the need for comprehensive safety inspections and regular upkeep to ensure airworthiness adds to the financial commitment.


Despite these challenges, technological advancements and economies of scale gradually reduce costs, making vertical flight more accessible and cost-effective for a broader range of applications, including urban air mobility and commercial transport.


### The complexity of design for multi-rotor aircraft


Multi-rotor aircraft’s multiple rotors provide enhanced stability and maneuverability. However, managing the interactions between these rotors, ensuring redundancy for safety, and optimizing weight and efficiency while maintaining performance can be intricate.


Noise control is also a consideration, as the proximity of multiple rotors can lead to increased noise emissions.


Precise flight control systems are essential for multi-rotor aircraft to maintain stability, balance, and responsiveness during flight, allowing them to hover in place, maneuver precisely, and navigate safely in complex environments.


Without this precision, the coordinated actions of multiple rotors would be challenging, and the aircraft’s performance and safety would be compromised.


Ongoing research and development efforts continue to address these complexities and refine the design of multi-rotor aircraft to optimize their performance for a wide range of purposes.


## The future of vertical flight – applications and innovations


### Military use


The future of vertical flight holds promising applications and innovations, particularly in the military sector. One of the critical areas of development is in enhancing evacuation operations and supply deliveries.


Helicopters and VTOL aircraft can swiftly access remote or challenging terrains, evacuating injured soldiers or civilians from areas that may be difficult to reach by other means.


Advancements in autonomous flight and telemedicine can further enhance the efficiency and effectiveness of evacuation missions.


As an example, the Pentagon’s Defense Advanced Research Projects Agency (DARPA)[is working with](https://www.defenseone.com/technology/2023/03/darpa-launch-new-program-high-speed-no-runway-aircraft/383491/) the Special Operations Command on the Speed and Runway Independent Technologies (SPRINT) program on a new aircraft that could be used for combat medical evacuations or to aid soldiers trying to reach remote locations. The proposed aircraft does not have helicopter rotors, and the emphasis is on speed.


Vertical flight also offers innovations in the timely delivery of supplies to troops on the ground. Uncrewed aerial vehicles with vertical flight capabilities can transport essential goods, including medical supplies, ammunition and equipment, quickly and efficiently to military units in remote or inaccessible locations.


Developments in cargo drones and eVTOL aircraft are poised to revolutionize the logistics and resupply efforts of military operations.


### Integration into autonomous systems


Autonomous ground vehicles like self-driving cars may integrate VTOL technology for vertical takeoff and landing capabilities. This could enable vehicles to overcome traffic congestion or access areas with limited road infrastructure.


While fully autonomous cars with VTOL capability are not yet a reality, companies like[Terrafugia](https://evtol.news/terrafugia-tf-x/) have been working on the development of flying cars.


Vertical flight, especially eVTOL aircraft, is gaining traction for urban air mobility and delivery services. Companies are exploring using eVTOL drones to deliver packages quickly and efficiently in urban environments. These autonomous aerial vehicles could navigate city traffic and congestion, offering a faster and more reliable delivery option.


In 2019, Amazon unveiled its[Prime Air delivery drone](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/transportation/amazon-prime-air-prepares-for-drone-deliveries) , designed for autonomous delivery of packages. It is one of only three drone-delivery companies that has gone through the process to earn an Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) air carrier certificate.


### Potential uses in urban transport


VTOL aircraft have the potential to serve as “sky taxis” in congested cities, providing on-demand transportation services that bypass traffic and shorten commuting times.


Urban air mobility (UAM) also extends to airport connectivity, enabling passengers arriving at airports to seamlessly transfer to eVTOL air taxis for efficient journeys to their final city destinations.


Joby Aviation has developed an eVTOL aircraft designed for urban air mobility. The company[acquired Uber Elevate](https://www.jobyaviation.com/news/joby-aviation-welcomes-new-75m-investment-from-uber-as-it-acquires-uber-elevate-and-expands-partnership/) and is partnering with Uber to seamlessly integrate ground and air travel for future customers one day.


### Innovative designs to increase efficiency and reduce emissions


To conclude, electric propulsion systems are at the forefront of reducing emissions in VTOL aircraft.


Increasing energy density means modern batteries can store more energy in a given volume or weight, allowing eVTOL aircraft to carry larger battery packs without significantly increasing their size or weight. This translates to longer flight ranges and enhanced operational capabilities.


Electric motors powered by advanced batteries provide a cleaner, more energy-efficient alternative to traditional internal combustion engines. Ongoing advancements in battery technology, increasing energy density and extending the range of eVTOL aircraft will further reduce emissions.
