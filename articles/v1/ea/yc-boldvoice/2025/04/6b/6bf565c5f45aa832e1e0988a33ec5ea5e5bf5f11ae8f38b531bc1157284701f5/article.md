## 2. Speechling


Speechling takes a coach-based approach to pronunciation.


‍


It provides structured speaking exercises that you record and send to real human coaches for feedback, making it especially helpful for learners who benefit from accountability and external input. The app also includes dictation,[listening](https://www.boldvoice.com/blog/listening-comprehension) , and speaking challenges.


‍


However, the feedback isn’t instant—you may need to wait hours or even days. It also lacks visual elements like IPA guides or articulation diagrams, which can make it harder to self-correct without expert support.


‍


Still, it’s a good option for supplementing other American accent apps with coaching.


‍


## 3. Speakometer


Speakometer is a user-friendly, AI-driven app designed to help learners improve their[American (and British) English pronunciation](https://www.boldvoice.com/blog/american-english-vs-british-english-pronunciation) . It offers personalized lessons based on your native language and tracks your progress over time. The app provides real-time feedback, guiding you through mouth positioning and sound articulation to enhance your accent.​


‍


A standout feature of Speakometer is its extensive word library, allowing you to practice and compare your pronunciation with native speakers. It also includes an interactive International Phonetic Alphabet (IPA) chart, helping you understand the nuances of English sounds.


‍


While Speakometer is excellent for focused pronunciation practice, it lacks comprehensive features like roleplay scenarios or custom conversation exercises found in more advanced apps. Nonetheless, it's a valuable tool for those seeking a straightforward, effective way to refine their American accent.

## 4. Say It: English Pronunciation


Say It was developed with Oxford University Press and uses dictionary-based pronunciation models. It allows you to hear native speaker audio, record your own voice, and compare sound waveforms to see how closely you match. This can be especially useful for isolating and correcting individual sounds.


‍


Just be aware: the app leans heavily toward British English by default. You’ll need to tweak the settings to focus on an American English accent. And while it’s great for word-level practice, it doesn’t help much with full-sentence fluency or rhythm.


## 5. American English Pronunciation Tutor


As the name suggests, this app zeroes in on[the sounds of American English](https://www.boldvoice.com/blog/american-english-ipa-chart) . You’ll find animations of the mouth, tongue, and lips for each consonant and vowel sound, along with sample words and practice exercises. It’s a practical choice for learners who want to dive deep into phonetics.


‍


That said, it’s not exactly sleek or engaging. The design feels more academic than interactive, and some users may find the repetition a bit dry. Still, if you're working on specific problem sounds, this app offers excellent value as a supplemental tool.


‍


## 6. Forvo


Forvo is like the Wikipedia of pronunciation. You type in a word, and it delivers native speaker recordings from around the world—including American English speakers. It’s perfect for quick checks or learning how names,[slang](https://www.boldvoice.com/blog/american-slang-words) , and[regional](https://www.boldvoice.com/blog/regional-american-accents) terms are actually said.


‍


But it’s important to note that Forvo isn’t a teaching tool. There are no lessons, no guidance, and no feedback. It’s best used alongside a proper American accent app that offers structured learning or coaching.


‍


## 7. Master the American Accent (Book + Audio)


Okay, technically this isn’t an app—but this classic program is still widely used and available in digital formats. It walks learners through all the essential elements of[American pronunciation](https://www.boldvoice.com/blog/standard-american-accent) , from vowel shifts to syllable stress and intonation.


‍


While the content is solid, it lacks the interactivity of modern American English accent apps. You’ll get practice, but you won’t get feedback, and the lessons can feel static. Still, if you’re a self-motivated learner who enjoys structured material, it’s a valuable resource.
