## 2. Speechling


Speechling focuses on mimicry as a learning tool. You listen to native speakers and repeat their phrases to train your ear and speech muscles. It’s a good option if you're looking to improve your rhythm and intonation in English.


‍


The app does offer feedback from human coaches—but it’s delayed. After submitting your recordings, it can take up to 24 hours to get a response.


‍


While not ideal for real-time learning, it’s still a strong resource for muscle memory and pronunciation drills.


‍


## 3. Pronuncian


Pronuncian is great for beginners who want to understand the basic building blocks of American pronunciation. It features phonetic charts, instructional videos, and practice tools to help you learn[vowel](https://www.boldvoice.com/blog/vowel-sounds-in-english) and[consonant](https://www.boldvoice.com/blog/sounds-of-each-english-consonant) sounds, stress patterns, and intonation.


‍


You can record your voice and compare it with native speakers, but the app doesn’t go very deep in terms of feedback or lesson customization. It’s a useful supplement, but not a standalone solution if you’re aiming for advanced accent reduction.

## 4. SpeechAce


SpeechAce gives you instant, AI-powered feedback on your pronunciation and fluency. You can practice individual sounds, full sentences, and more realistic speech patterns—not just isolated vocabulary words.


‍


The lessons are practical and well-organized, making it easy to target common trouble spots like[“th” sounds](https://www.boldvoice.com/blog/how-to-pronounce-th-english) or syllable stress. However, advanced learners may find the feedback a bit too general, and it doesn’t offer much in terms of deeper phonetic instruction or conversation-based learning.


‍


## 5. The Mimic Method


The Mimic Method’s philosophy is simple: if you can’t hear the difference, you can’t make the difference.


‍


It trains you to break down language into elemental sounds and mimic native speakers through[deep listening](https://www.boldvoice.com/blog/listening-comprehension) and repetition.


‍


This can dramatically improve your ear and mouth coordination. But the program leans heavily on sound alone, with less focus on grammar, vocabulary, or real-time conversation practice. For many learners, that makes it better as a supplemental tool rather than a complete solution.


## 6. Speakometer


Speakometer is ideal for exam prep, particularly for[IELTS](https://www.boldvoice.com/blog/best-apps-ielts-speaking-practice) or[TOEFL](https://www.boldvoice.com/blog/toefl-speaking-practice-apps) speaking practice. It offers a massive word library—over 65,000 words—along with example sentences and instant feedback on your pronunciation.


‍


You can focus on tricky vocabulary, identify specific mispronunciations, and improve clarity at the word level. That said, Speakometer doesn’t offer much in the way of sentence-level practice or connected speech, which limits its usefulness for fluency and flow.


‍


## 7. Accentify


Accentify targets the[sounds that make your accent stand out](https://www.boldvoice.com/blog/speaking-english-with-accent) and helps you correct them through structured practice. It adapts to your native language background and delivers exercises tailored to the most common pronunciation pitfalls.


‍


However, the accent removal app is still in beta, and some features are under development. While it’s promising, it may not yet have the stability or depth needed for serious learners—but it’s one to watch as it evolves.
