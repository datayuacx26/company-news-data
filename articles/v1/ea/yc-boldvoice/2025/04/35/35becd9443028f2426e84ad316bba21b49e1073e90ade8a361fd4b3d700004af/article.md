## 2. Anki


Anki is a flashcard app built on spaced repetition, making it a powerful advanced English vocabulary app. You can customize your own cards, add examples, and review them in timed intervals to retain words long-term.


‍


If building a stronger vocabulary is your main focus, Anki delivers. It’s ideal for memorizing complex or technical terms that might appear in professional or academic settings.


‍


However, Anki doesn’t address speaking, pronunciation, or conversational fluency. It’s not an advanced English speaking app, and it lacks features that teach rhythm, stress, or natural speech.


‍


If your goal is to speak fluidly and confidently, you’ll need to pair Anki with another tool.


‍


## 3. LingQ


LingQ helps advanced learners strengthen reading and[listening comprehension](https://www.boldvoice.com/blog/listening-comprehension) by immersing them in real-world content. You can import texts from news sites, audiobooks, and even YouTube transcripts to study vocabulary in context.


‍


As an advanced English app for reading-focused learners, LingQ is excellent. It supports deep engagement with complex material and helps reinforce language patterns naturally.


‍


That said, LingQ offers no[speaking practice](https://www.boldvoice.com/blog/ai-english-speaking-practice) . You won’t get feedback on your pronunciation, pace, or delivery.


‍


To truly master spoken English, you’ll need to add a dedicated advanced English speaking app to your study routine.

## 4. Tandem


Tandem is a language exchange app that connects you with native English speakers for text, voice, or video chats. It’s a great platform to learn[idioms](https://www.boldvoice.com/blog/50-american-idioms) , informal speech, and[native pronunciation](https://www.boldvoice.com/blog/how-to-speak-english-like-native-speaker) in a conversational setting.


‍


For learners who want real-world practice, Tandem can be energizing. You can build friendships, practice daily English, and observe how fluent speakers use advanced phrases.


‍


However, many users find the app overwhelming at first. New members often receive dozens of messages, and it can be hard to find a consistent language partner.


‍


Additionally, there's no structured curriculum or feedback—so you're learning through immersion, not instruction.


## 5. Orai


Orai is a[public speaking app](https://www.boldvoice.com/blog/best-public-speaking-apps) that focuses on clarity, engagement, and pacing. It evaluates your recordings and offers targeted feedback—flagging[filler words](https://www.boldvoice.com/blog/filler-words-in-english) , suggesting pacing adjustments, and analyzing your[tone](https://www.boldvoice.com/blog/tone-of-voice) .


‍


This is one of the few advanced spoken English apps that gives detailed, real-time insights into your speaking style. It’s especially helpful for professionals preparing for[presentations](https://www.boldvoice.com/blog/how-to-learn-public-speaking-in-english) or[interviews](https://www.boldvoice.com/blog/how-to-speak-english-confidently-in-interviews) .


‍


The drawback? Orai sometimes misinterprets natural pauses or suggests edits that feel robotic. It also doesn’t focus on[accent or pronunciation training](https://www.boldvoice.com/blog/ai-accent-training-tools) , which are essential for many advanced learners.


‍


## 6. Grammarly


Grammarly has evolved into more than just a grammar checker—it’s now a helpful advanced English grammar app that supports everything from[emails](https://www.boldvoice.com/blog/email-in-english) to essays. It highlights issues with structure, tone, and word choice, helping you write with more clarity and polish.


‍


For advanced learners, Grammarly can refine written communication and expose grammar patterns you may not have noticed.


‍


However, the app can be overly reliant on automated suggestions. If you're not careful, you may end up accepting changes passively instead of learning from them.


‍


Plus, Grammarly doesn’t always understand nuance or intent, which can sometimes lead to awkward phrasing.


‍


## 7. The Economist Espresso


The Economist Espresso delivers high-level news in digestible, five-minute reads. The vocabulary is rich and professional—ideal for advanced learners who want to absorb the tone and cadence of native-level writing.


‍


As an advanced English app for comprehension, it’s excellent. You’ll encounter idiomatic expressions, formal constructions, and economic vocabulary that help elevate your reading level.


‍


However, there’s no built-in language support. No definitions, no grammar explanations, and no guided learning.


‍


You’ll need to look up unfamiliar terms and break down complex sentences yourself—so this is best for learners who already have a strong grasp of English.


‍


## 8. Preply


Preply is an advanced spoken English app that connects you with tutors from around the world. You can choose instructors based on your goals—whether it’s[job interview prep](https://www.boldvoice.com/blog/interview-questions-and-answers-in-english) ,[business English](https://www.boldvoice.com/blog/business-english-vocabulary) , or academic speaking.


‍


The 1-on-1 interaction makes Preply ideal for advanced learners who want personalized coaching and real-time corrections. A good tutor can challenge you to articulate your thoughts more fluently and confidently.


‍


But Preply comes with a few limitations. Not all tutors are trained educators; some are simply fluent speakers. This means the quality of instruction can vary. Also, there's no built-in curriculum—so lesson planning is your responsibility.
