## 2. HelloTalk


HelloTalk is a global language exchange app that connects you with native English speakers for real-time practice. You can chat via text, send voice messages, or even jump on live calls or video chats.


‍


It’s an immersive way to get comfortable with[conversational English](https://www.boldvoice.com/blog/english-conversation-practice-exercises) in real-life scenarios. That said, the experience depends heavily on who you're paired with. Some partners are helpful and reliable—others, not so much. So while HelloTalk is a great way to build fluency informally, results can vary.


‍


## 3. Promova


Promova offers short, everyday English lessons in a casual, user-friendly format. The focus is on real-life topics like[travel](https://www.boldvoice.com/blog/useful-travel-phrases-in-english) ,[small talk](https://www.boldvoice.com/blog/english-small-talk-topics-casual-conversation-in-america) , and daily routines—ideal for learners who want to gain confidence speaking naturally.


‍


You’ll get pronunciation drills, listening exercises, and quizzes to reinforce what you’ve learned. But Promova is best for beginners and lower-intermediate learners. If you’re looking for advanced challenges or detailed speaking feedback, you may find it limited.

## 4. Lingopie


Lingopie lets you learn English by watching TV shows and[movies](https://www.boldvoice.com/blog/learn-english-with-movies) , with interactive subtitles and instant translations. You can click any word to get its meaning and create flashcards to review later.


‍


It’s a great way to pick up vocabulary,[slang](https://www.boldvoice.com/blog/american-slang-words) , and cultural context. But because Lingopie doesn’t follow a structured curriculum, it can be overwhelming for beginners. There’s little direct speaking practice, so it’s best used alongside another app that focuses on pronunciation and conversation.


## 5. Cambly


Cambly connects you with certified English tutors for one-on-one live video sessions. The experience is highly personalized: you can choose your tutor, schedule lessons anytime, and focus on[your specific goals](https://www.boldvoice.com/blog/pronunciation-and-accent-training-plan) .


‍


Many users love Cambly’s flexibility and friendly interface. But tutor quality can be inconsistent. Some are exceptional—others less so. It might take time (and money) to find the right teacher who matches your learning style and skill level.


‍


## 6. Speechling


Speechling is all about fluency and pronunciation. You’ll record your voice, complete drills, and get personal feedback from coaches to help you sound more natural and confident.


‍


The app doesn’t dive into grammar or vocabulary—it’s focused strictly on speaking. Speechling is best for intermediate learners looking to refine their pronunciation. If you're a complete beginner, you might want to start elsewhere.


‍


## 7. LanguaTalk


LanguaTalk connects learners with a small, highly vetted pool of professional tutors. With fewer than 10% of applicants accepted, quality is a top priority (and costs reflect that). Lessons are customized and flexible, with time zones and busy schedules in mind.


‍


You can also practice with AI chat partners, review transcripts of your conversations, and receive real-time feedback. The downside is availability: because tutors are in high demand, you might have to wait for your preferred slot.
