# SharePoint and Knowledge Management Systems: What’s the Difference?

### [Digital transformation](https://www.egain.com/blog/category/digital-transformation/)[Knowledge management](https://www.egain.com/blog/category/knowledge-management/)


## SharePoint and Knowledge Management Systems: What’s the Difference?


Most organizations already run SharePoint, and it earns its place: it’s a strong platform for storing documents, building intranets, and supporting day-to-day collaboration. But storing content is not the same as managing knowledge. Organizations now need to give accurate, consistent answers to the people they serve and to the staff who serve them, increasingly through AI assistants. Many discover that a content and collaboration platform was never designed for that job. Here’s how the two differ, and how to tell when you need a dedicated knowledge layer.


## **Is SharePoint a knowledge management system?**


**Short answer: no.** SharePoint is a **content and collaboration platform** , a place to store, organize, and share files and to build team sites and intranets. A **knowledge management system (KMS)** is purpose-built for a different outcome: turning information into trusted, structured answers that can be found instantly and delivered wherever work happens. Put simply, SharePoint manages *documents* ; a KMS manages *answers* .


## Content platform vs. knowledge management system, at a glance


**Dimension** **Content & collaboration platform (e.g., SharePoint)** **Knowledge management system (e.g., eGain)**


**Primary job** Store, organize, and share documents; build intranets and team sites Turn information into trusted, findable answers


**Core unit** Files, pages, and folders Structured, self-contained knowledge articles


**Findability** Keyword search across sites and silos Semantic search that reads intent and returns the answer


**Accuracy & governance** No built-in review or verification workflow Expert verification, content-health scoring, review cycles


**Delivery** Users navigate to the document Answers pushed into the tools where work happens


**Analytics** Limited insight into usage or gaps Knowledge-gap and adoption analytics


**AI readiness** AI tools can read the files, but answers inherit whatever is there: duplicates, stale versions, conflicts Governed, verified content served to AI assistants through native connectors and APIs


## **Signs you’ve outgrown SharePoint for knowledge**


SharePoint can hold a knowledge base, but the strain shows as you scale. Common signals include:


- Knowledge is scattered across many sites and silos, with no single source of truth.
- Search returns a long list of documents instead of the one right answer.
- No clear owner or process keeps articles current, so trust erodes.
- Employees and agents give inconsistent answers to the same question.
- There’s little visibility into what people search for and fail to find.
- AI assistants answer from the content as it stands, so duplicates, stale pages, and conflicts flow straight into their answers.


## **When to add a knowledge management system**


A purpose-built KMS such as eGain adds the layer SharePoint lacks: structured articles that both people and AI can consume, semantic search that understands intent, expert verification and content-health scoring that keep answers accurate, and delivery that surfaces answers inside the tools staff already use. The same governed content then feeds AI assistants through native connectors, so every channel draws on one verified source.


Published eGain customer results give a sense of the impact. A large US federal agency diverted up to 70% of incoming calls to AI virtual assistants and cut case handling time by 25%. A leading utility built and updated knowledge five times faster and improved answer findability six-fold. And a health insurer unified more than 20,000 SharePoint and document-store files into eGain to give consistent answers to 8,000 users.


Adding a KMS doesn’t mean ripping SharePoint out. Many organizations keep it for document storage and collaboration and add a KMS as the *answer layer* on top, migrating or connecting the content that people actually need answers from. The result is a single trusted source of answers built on top of the content you already have.


## **Frequently asked questions**


**Can SharePoint be used as a knowledge base?**
Yes, and for small, stable content sets it can be enough. As volume, change, and the need for verified answers grow, its document-and-keyword model starts to work against you.


**Do we have to replace SharePoint?**
No. SharePoint can remain your intranet and document hub; a KMS handles the answer layer and can migrate or connect the relevant content.


**What makes a knowledge system “AI-ready”?**
Structured, self-contained articles, built-in governance so answers are verified, and native connectors that let AI assistants retrieve trusted content, rather than crawling loosely organized files.


**Doesn’t Microsoft Copilot make SharePoint AI-ready?**
Copilot is good at retrieving what’s in SharePoint, but it answers from the content as it stands. If that content is duplicated, outdated, or unverified, the answers inherit those problems. A KMS governs the content layer that any AI assistant, Copilot included, depends on.


**How disruptive is migration?**
Content is typically imported in bulk and restructured into articles, then improved with verification and analytics over time, so value starts early and compounds.


The bottom line: keep SharePoint for what it does well, and give knowledge a system built to deliver answers.
