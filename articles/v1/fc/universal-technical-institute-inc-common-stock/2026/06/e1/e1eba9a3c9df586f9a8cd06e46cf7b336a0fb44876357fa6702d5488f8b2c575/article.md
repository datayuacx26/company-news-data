# Is It Hard To Become a Certified Auto Technician?

If you love working on cars, you've probably wondered whether getting certified is worth the effort. Maybe the idea of tests and requirements sounds intimidating, or maybe you just aren't sure where to start. But how **** hard is it to become a certified auto technician? While it takes effort and commitment, it is achievable with the right preparation.


In this blog, we'll break down what automotive certification means, walk through the certification process step by step, clear up some common misconceptions, and show you how the right training program can set you up for success.


## **What Does It Mean To Be a "Certified" Auto Technician?**


When someone calls themselves a certified auto technician, it typically means they have passed one or more standardized exams that demonstrate their knowledge and competency in specific areas of vehicle repair and maintenance.


Certification is not the same as having a license (which may be required in some states) or simply having work experience. It’s a formal, third-party validation that you have the right education and skills to make repairs. In the automotive world, the gold standard for that validation comes from the[National Institute for Automotive Service Excellence](https://www.ase.com/) , or ASE. ****


## **H2: What Are ASE Certifications?**


Founded in 1972, ASE is a nonprofit organization that develops and administers certification exams for auto technicians, collision repair specialists, service consultants and more.


ASE offers certifications across a wide range of specialty areas, including:


- Engine Repair (A1)
- Automatic Transmission/Transaxle (A2)
- Manual Drive Train & Axles (A3)
- Suspension & Steering (A4)
- Brakes (A5)
- Electrical/Electronic Systems (A6)
- Heating & Air Conditioning (A7)
- Engine Performance (A8)
- Light Vehicle Diesel Engines (A9)


Technicians who pass all eight of the core automobile tests (A1–A8) earn the distinction of ASE Master Automobile Technician, one of the most respected credentials in the industry.


There are also certifications for medium/heavy trucks, collision repair, service advisors, parts specialists and more, so there’s a certification pathway for nearly every corner of the automotive industry.


## **What Does the Certification Process Actually Look Like?**


Understanding the automotive technician certification process can make it feel a lot less overwhelming. Here’s what it looks like from start to finish:


### **1. Meet the work experience requirement**


Before you can take any ASE exam, you need to have relevant hands-on work experience. The standard requirement is two years of on-the-job training; however, graduates of UTI’s[automotive technician training](https://www.uti.edu/programs/automotive) can substitute their training for one of the two years of experience required to take ASE tests.


### **2. Register for your exam(s)**


ASE exams are administered at testing centers nationwide. You’ll need to create an account on the ASE website, select which test(s) you want to take, pay the exam fee and schedule your appointment.


### **3. Study and prepare**


Each ASE exam covers specific technical content, and preparation is key. Most successful test-takers use a combination of study guides, practice tests and review of real-world repair scenarios.


### **4. Take the exam(s)**


ASE exams are computer-based and consist of multiple-choice questions. The number of questions and time allotted vary by test, but exams are generally 50–75 questions.


### **5. Recertify every five years**


ASE certifications are valid for five years. To maintain your credentials, you’ll need to retest, which keeps your skills current as vehicle technology evolves.


## **So, is it hard?**


The exams test real technical knowledge, and you can’t fake your way through them. But “hard” is relative. If you’ve put in the hands-on time and studied the material, passing is very realistic. ## **Automotive Certification FAQs**


### **Is automotive technician certification hard to get?**


The automotive technician certification process can be challenging but achievable. The exams require real technical knowledge, and you’ll need documented work experience to qualify. With the right preparation and training, you can improve your chances of passing.


### **What are the requirements for automotive technician certification?**


To sit for most ASE exams, you’ll need two years of relevant work experience. However, successfully completing UTI’s Automotive program can count as one of those two years. You’ll also need to register through ASE, pay exam fees, and pass the computer-based multiple-choice exam for each certification area.


### **Is it too late to become an automotive technician if you have no prior experience?**


Not at all. Many students enter automotive training programs with zero professional experience. A quality program will give you the foundational knowledge and hands-on auto mechanic training you need to build your skills from the ground up. After completing your program and gaining the required on-the-job experience, you’ll be eligible to pursue certification.


### **Do you need certification to get a job as an automotive technician?**


Technically, no. Certification is not legally required to work as a technician in most states. However, certified auto technicians are significantly more competitive in the job market. Many employers require it. If you’re serious about a long-term career, pursuing certification is one of the smartest moves you can make.
