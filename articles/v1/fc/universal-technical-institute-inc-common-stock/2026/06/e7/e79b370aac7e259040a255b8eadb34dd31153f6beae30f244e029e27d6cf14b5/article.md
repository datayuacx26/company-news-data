### How do scholarships work?


**Scholarships** are financial awards that typically do not need to be repaid. They may be based on factors like academic achievement, financial need, career interest, location, leadership or other eligibility requirements.


Some scholarships are offered through schools, foundations, professional organizations or industry groups. Others may require may require students to complete the[Free Application for Federal Student Aid](https://www.usa.gov/fafsa) (FAFSA) before eligibility can be determined.


UTI offers a variety of scholarship opportunities for eligible students. Some are available across multiple campuses, while others may be tied to a state, school district, career interest or start date. A[Financial Aid](https://catalogs.uti.edu/financial-aid) Advisor can help students review available options and understand which opportunities they’re suited for.


### What about grants?


**Grants** are another form of financial aid that typically does not need to be repaid if students meet the required conditions. Many grants are need-based or connected to specific circumstances, such as commuting distance, relocation, natural disasters or academic background. Like scholarships, some grants may require completion of a FAFSA.


UTI offers grant programs that may help eligible students reduce certain education-related costs. These can include institutional grants, commuter grants, relocation grants and other options depending on the student’s situation. Because eligibility rules vary, students should review current grant details and speak with a Financial Aid Advisor before applying.


### Will loans help?


**Loans** are borrowed funds that can help cover education costs and must be repaid later, usually with interest. Federal student loans generally begin with the FAFSA, which helps determine what types of federal aid a student may qualify for. Unlike scholarships and grants, loans should be reviewed carefully because repayment terms matter.


At UTI, students can complete the FAFSA to see what aid options may be available to them. Financial Aid Advisors can help explain offers, loan types and next steps so students understand what they are accepting. This can be especially useful for students comparing local auto mechanic school options and trying to make a clear financial plan before enrolling.

## How To Complete FAFSA for Auto Mechanic School


The FAFSA is one of the most important first steps for students exploring financial aid for auto mechanic school. It can help determine eligibility for federal grants, loans and certain school-based or state aid options.


### Step 1: Create your FSA ID


Start by creating your Federal Student Aid (FSA) ID at[StudentAid.gov](https://studentaid.gov/) . This username and password allow you to log in securely, sign your FAFSA electronically and access your federal aid information.


Students and required contributors, such as a parent or legal guardian for dependent students, may each need their own FSA ID. Set this up before starting the form so the application process moves more smoothly.


### Step 2: Gather your documents


Before filling out the FAFSA, collect the information you may need. This can include:


- Social Security number or Alien Registration number, if applicable
- Driver’s license or state ID
- Federal tax returns, W-2s or income information from the previous year
- Bank statements and investment records
- School codes for the institutions you want to receive your FAFSA information


If you are considered a dependent student, your parent or legal guardian will also need to provide financial information.


### Step 3: Complete the FAFSA online


Visit StudentAid.gov to complete the FAFSA form online. Enter your personal and financial information carefully, then list the schools you want to receive your FAFSA results, including UTI if you plan to apply.


You can save your progress and return later if needed. This is helpful if you need to gather additional information or coordinate with a parent, guardian or spouse.


### Step 4: Review your financial aid offers


After your FAFSA is processed, you’ll receive information summarizing what you submitted. Schools that receive your FAFSA can then use it to create a financial aid offer if you are eligible.


At UTI, students can meet with a Financial Aid Advisor who can help explain their offers. This can include reviewing grants, loans and other potential aid options so students understand the difference between funds that must be repaid and funds that typically do not.


### Step 5: Apply for scholarships and grants


FAFSA is not the only step. Students should also review scholarships and grants they may qualify for through UTI, outside organizations or other resources.


Apply early when possible. Some opportunities may have limited funding, deadlines or requirements that take time to complete.
