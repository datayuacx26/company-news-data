# Tacos, Tones, and Technology

# Tacos, Tones, and Technology


August 11, 2026


by Brian Heath


##


## An Evening with Clinton Township Fire-Rescue-EMS Station 4


I recently had the opportunity to spend an evening with the firefighters and paramedics at Station 4 in Clinton Township, Michigan. It was an unforgettable experience, one that combined candid conversations, hands-on learning, and a deep appreciation for the people who answer the call when the rest of us need help most.


On any given day, or night, these crews respond to everything from structure fires to medical emergencies to false alarms. It’s a profession defined by unpredictability, urgency, and an unwavering commitment to service.


## Inside the Station


The visit began with a tour of the station: the engine bay with its organized and meticulously maintained space, the administrative workspace, the dorms where crews catch what sleep they can, and the shared living area where they spend much of their downtime together.


One of the most fascinating pieces of equipment I saw was the LUCAS automated CPR device, a machine that delivers consistent, precise chest compressions during cardiac arrest. While the device works tirelessly, paramedics are freed up to focus on other lifesaving interventions such as airway management and IV access. It was just one example of how technology is augmenting, not replacing, the expertise and coordination of these professionals.


I also gained insight into the different roles within the department and how each team member relies on technology differently. Whether it’s the lieutenant coordinating response strategy, the firefighter navigating hazardous environments, or the paramedic delivering critical care, their tools are tailored to help them act quickly, decisively, and safely.


## Tacos and Real Conversations


After the tour, I joined the crew for dinner in the station’s main gathering area. On the menu: tacos prepared by the team. It was relaxed, genuine, and exactly what you’d expect from a group that operates like a second family.


Over dinner, the conversation turned to the realities of the job.


We talked about the high volume of non-emergency 911 calls, which continue to strain resources and limit availability for true emergencies. We discussed the long-term health risks firefighters face, particularly elevated cancer rates tied to exposure to toxins and carcinogens at fire scenes. And we talked about *the tones* , the piercing alarms that can go off at any moment, day or night, jolting crews from sleep into immediate action.


It’s hard to imagine being awakened by bright lights and loud tones and, within seconds, needing to absorb critical dispatch information, gear up, and head out the door. But for them, it’s routine.


## When the Tones Drop


And then, right on cue, it happened.


About halfway through dinner, the tones rang out. A dispatcher’s voice filled the station, describing the call. At the same time, that same information was instantly pushed to mobile devices, phones and tablets carried by the team.


Within moments, dinner was paused and we were headed out.


This is where Tyler’s technology in action, supporting first responders in real time, under real pressure.


As we responded to calls, I watched how dispatch data flowed seamlessly into the hands of the crew:


- Real-time call notes from 911 dispatch
- Turn-by-turn navigation and mapping
- GIS overlays showing building layouts, hydrant locations, and other critical information
- Pre-incident planning data, including access points and hazards


Even on calls that ultimately turned out to be false alarms, the importance of this information was clear. Seconds matter. Having accurate, consolidated data, accessible in a single interface, helps teams respond faster, make better decisions, and operate more safely.


Tyler’s platform brings together dispatch, GIS, records, and inspection data into one unified experience, ensuring that from the moment a call is received to the final report, responders have the information they need.


## A Real Call, Real Impact


Later in the evening, around 10:00 PM, we responded to a medical call that required patient transport. Firefighters/paramedics and an ambulance crew worked side by side, assessing the patient, gathering medical history, stabilizing conditions, and preparing for transport.


Again, technology was present at every step:


- Capturing patient information on scene
- Coordinating across agencies
- Supporting documentation and reporting


After returning to the station, the crew still had work to do. Reports needed to be completed, including detailed documentation of the incident, patient condition, and care provided. Even with modern tools, this process takes time and precision.

## From Dispatch to Disposition


After nearly five hours, my visit came to an end. For the crew, it was just another stretch of a 24-hour shift, with more calls likely ahead before morning.


Personally, the experience left me with a deep appreciation for the people behind the profession: their bravery, resilience, teamwork, and quiet dedication to serving others in moments of crisis.


Professionally, it reinforced something I’m incredibly proud of.


At Tyler, we often talk about supporting the public safety ecosystem, from dispatch to disposition. Seeing that come to life inside Station 4 was powerful. It’s not just software; it’s a critical layer of support that helps first responders do their jobs more effectively, improves outcomes for the communities they serve, and ultimately helps save lives.


## Final Thoughts


Tacos, tones, and technology.


It’s a simple phrase, but it captures the essence of that evening:


- The human connection around the dinner table
- The relentless urgency of the tones
- And the technology quietly working in the background to tie it all together


We all hope we never need to call 911. But if we do, it’s reassuring to know that behind that call is a team of highly trained professionals, equipped with both the courage *and* the tools to respond when it matters most.


And when they help us get home safely to our families, it means everything to know we’re helping them do the same.
