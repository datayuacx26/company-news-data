# Why Manual Processes Are Unsustainable for Small Agencies

# Why Manual Processes Are Unsustainable for Small Agencies


July 02, 2026


by Shauna Seaver


##


Government efficiency is often framed as a question of speed: faster approvals, less paperwork, or streamlined workflows. But in many public sector organizations, especially smaller districts and departments, the larger operational risk is dependency on manual processes.


For[SAU 14 Epping Schools](https://www.tylertech.com/resources/case-studies/tea-winner-district-modernizes-hr-processes) in New Hampshire, that reality became difficult to sustain.


Like many districts, Epping Schools operates with a lean administrative team responsible for finance, HR, payroll, compliance, transportation, and daily operations. Before modernizing many of their workflows, the district relied heavily on paper-based coordination and disconnected systems.


As Business Administrator Christine Vayda described it, “The biggest thing that I saw is just recognizing the fact that the manual processes just weren’t sustainable.”


## When Manual Work Becomes an Operational Constraint


In government organizations, inefficiency often comes from the accumulation of small administrative burdens: duplicate data entry, paper forms, delayed approvals, and staff spending time tracking information instead of acting on it.


At Epping Schools, those burdens created delays across everyday operations. Leave requests moved between buildings before reaching the business office. Payroll and HR data required manual reconciliation. Contracts were mailed, signed physically, and returned through traditional routing processes. Budget reporting often depended on spreadsheets and separate systems that did not consistently align.


Those inefficiencies become more significant in organizations with small teams and limited capacity. Across government, staff often spend significant time managing disconnects between systems rather than supporting higher-value work such as reporting, budgeting, and operational planning.


## Reducing Dependency Through Connected Systems


Epping Schools’ modernization efforts focused on reducing operational friction across the organization.


The district expanded their use of cloud-based HR, payroll, and finance systems to centralize operational workflows and improve visibility across departments. Approval workflows that once depended on paper routing were automated and became visible across departments. Employees gained self-service access to payroll information, contracts, leave requests, and onboarding materials. Administrators could review purchasing activity directly within the system rather than relying on manual handoffs between offices.


The impact went beyond convenience.


Contracts that previously took weeks to process through mail routing could now be completed electronically within hours. Financial records and invoices became searchable within the system rather than manually retrieved during audits. Personnel data flowed directly into budgeting and reporting processes, reducing the need for spreadsheet reconciliation and constant verification that information had been entered or calculated correctly.


Epping Schools’ success highlights that the goal of efficiency is not simply reducing steps in a process. It is reducing how many routine operations depend on institutional memory, manual coordination, or individual intervention to function reliably.


## From Reactive Work to Proactive Operations


Technology alone does not reduce operational dependency. Staff also need confidence in the processes being introduced.


Vayda emphasized that modernization efforts succeeded because staff understood why the changes mattered, not just how the technology worked. Training and communication helped the team see how connected workflows reduced friction in their day-to-day tasks and supported a more proactive operating model.


As Vayda described it, the goal was to create systems that “better supported our decision-making and allowed us to be a little more proactive instead of reactive.”


## Why Sustainability Matters More Than Speed


Epping Schools’ experience reflects a broader challenge across government. The pursuit of operational efficiency must expand beyond completing work faster. It requires building operations that remain sustainable as staffing pressures, reporting demands, and service expectations continue to increase.


The long-term value of modernization is creating resilient organizations that are less dependent on manual coordination, less vulnerable to operational bottlenecks, and better positioned to adapt as demands evolve.
