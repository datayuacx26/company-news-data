# When Fire Technology Goes Quiet, Value Becomes Clear


July 28, 2026


by Kate Nadolski


##


For today’s fire departments, access to information is just as important as access to physical equipment. Responders rely on technology to deliver incident details, location data, building information, and other critical insights that help them make informed decisions on every call.


Although the department’s contingency plans kept communications operational, firefighters suddenly found themselves without many of the digital tools they used every day.


"We still had our radios, but crews got into the rig and didn’t have any of their information in front of them on the MDT," Hebbeln said.


The outage lasted only a few hours, but it immediately highlighted how heavily responders depend on mission-critical information while heading to a call.


CAD details, call notes, location information, building access points, and pre-plan data that had become second nature were suddenly unavailable, all due to the construction crew’s error.


"We’ve gotten so used to just having the information," Hebbeln said.


"When it doesn’t work, it becomes painfully obvious that we really rely on this stuff. It makes our job that much easier."


The experience wasn’t about losing technology — **It was a reminder of the role technology plays in helping crews respond efficiently and confidently.**


Shortly after the incident, the fiber was repaired, and all access to technology was restored.


For the Davenport Fire Department, the fiber outage served as an unexpected reminder of something easy to overlook: **when technology works consistently, firefighters can focus on the job in front of them rather than the tools behind it…**


Sometimes, it takes a brief disruption to fully appreciate the value of having critical information available when every second counts.
