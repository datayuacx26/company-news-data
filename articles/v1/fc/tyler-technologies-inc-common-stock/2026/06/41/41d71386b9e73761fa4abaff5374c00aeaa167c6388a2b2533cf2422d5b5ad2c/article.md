# Thriving Amid Constraints: Strategies for Assessment Offices

# Thriving Amid Constraints: Strategies for Assessment Offices


June 08, 2026


by Michael McFarlane


##


Walk into almost any assessment office right now and you’ll hear some version of the same story: fewer staff, more parcels, sharper questions from the public. None of those pressures are new on their own. Feeling all three at once is what is wearing assessment offices down.


One of the biggest benefits of my role at Tyler Technologies is the exposure to what’s happening across the assessment industry. I spend most of my time talking with assessors, appraisers, and tax auditors working through these mounting pressures. The offices weathering it best are the ones tightening up a handful of unglamorous fundamentals. Here’s what I keep seeing work.


## Build a Reliable Data Foundation


Limited staffing leaves little room for data rework. When records are incomplete or inconsistent, the time cost compounds quickly.


My conversations with assessors have underscored the importance of maintaining data that is:


- **Clean** : Free from errors that slow review and analysis
- **Consistent** : Governed by shared standards for items such as grade and condition
- **Current** : Kept in sync across tax years and systems
- **Complete** : Populated with the fields needed to support valuation and review


Maintaining these fundamentals reduces manual correction, improves defensibility, and allows offices to take better advantage of analytical and review tools.

## Use Workflow to Maximize Staff Time


When staff capacity is tight, how work is routed matters as much as who performs it.


Structured workflows can help offices:


- Assign and track review tasks more efficiently
- Apply consistent quality control steps
- Prevent reviewers from validating their own work


Centralizing parcel data, imagery, GIS layers, and sketches within a single workflow reduces time spent searching for information and gives supervisors clearer insight into progress. The result is more consistent output without increasing workload.


## Strengthen Spatial Accuracy


Accurate spatial data supports both review efficiency and analytical confidence. One example is incorporating georeferenced sketches into routine parcel maintenance rather than treating them as a separate task.


Aligning sketches with aerial imagery and parcel boundaries helps identify discrepancies. It also supports desk reviews when field visits are limited and improves the value of building footprint layers for future analysis.


## Make Large Datasets Easier to Review


As datasets grow, visual context becomes increasingly important.


Visualization tools allow users to review sold and unsold properties together, compare neighborhoods visually, and identify outliers more quickly than through tables alone. Applying standardized statistical measures within these tools helps maintain consistency while reducing ad hoc analysis.


These capabilities support experienced team members while also making it easier to involve less experienced team members to participate in review and analysis.


## Apply Advanced Modeling Thoughtfully


Advanced modeling techniques,[including machine learning](https://www.tylertech.com/resources/resource-downloads/feature-overview-ai-modeling-statistics) , can support, rather than replace, professional judgment when applied carefully.


Key considerations include:


- Ensuring data quality before applying models
- Validating results against established standards
- Using models and automated output analysis to reduce repetitive manual tasks and highlight areas that warrant closer review


## Moving Forward


Nobody fixes all of this in a single year. The offices that pull ahead don’t try to. They pick the one place where added structure or a better tool would relieve the most pressure — usually a workflow bottleneck or a data quality problem everyone already knows about — and they start there.


The pressure on assessment offices isn’t easing. But the difference between drowning in it and thriving inside it usually comes down to a handful of practical choices, made deliberately, year over year. I remain excited about how the assessment industry will be reshaped in the coming years; it’s time for us, collectively, to seize the moment!
