# What to Know about Dwarfism in Dogs?

## What Is Dwarfism in Dogs and Can Dogs Have It


Dwarfism in dogs is a genetic condition that causes abnormally short stature, large head, and an overall disproportionate body. It is caused by a genetic mutation, and dogs can suffer from two types of dwarfism:


- **Achondroplasia** : Achondroplasia is caused by abnormal bone growth. “Some popular dog breeds body shapes are due to achondroplasia, one of the most common causes of dwarfism in humans,” explains a[Reddit user](https://www.reddit.com/r/askscience/comments/2a0oer/can_dogs_be_dwarfs/?ref=petcube.com) . It is seen in Dachshunds and Corgis.
- **Pituitary Dwarfism** : Pituitary dwarfism occurs when part of the pituitary gland fails to develop properly or when brain tumors affect the area, leading to excessive depletion or a lack of growth hormone. German Shepherds are predisposed to this condition.


## Dog Breeds That Can Have Dwarfism


Theoretically, all dogs can suffer from dwarfism. However, the specific dwarfism types are more prevalent in certain breeds.


For example, achondroplasia is typically reported in Corgis, Dachshunds, Basset Hounds, and Bulldogs.


> “Pituitary dwarfism is most common in German Shepherd dogs,” according to[studies](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/jvim.70193?ref=petcube.com) .


It is also more frequent among Tibetan Terriers, Karelian Bear Dogs, and Lapponian Herders.


## Symptoms, Health Concerns, and Life Expectancy


[Studies](https://www.academia.edu/96055833/Pituitary_dwarfism_in_German_Shepherd_Dogs?ref=petcube.com) conclude that “not all dwarfs display the same clinical signs and symptoms.” The clinical signs depend primarily on the type of dwarfism. Here is a general overview of what to expect:


- **Stunted Growth** : Popularly known as “forever puppies,” dwarf dogs fall behind and look much smaller than their littermates.
- **Disproportionate Body** : Dwarf dogs have unusually large heads, short or bowed limbs, and enlarged joints.
- **Coat and Skin Problems** : Dwarfs often retain their puppy coats or experience alopecia (abnormal hair loss).
- **Skeletal Abnormalities** : Spinal deviations and deformities, undershot jaws with crooked teeth, and bowed front legs are common in dwarf dogs.


## Health Concerns in Dwarf Dogs


Some dogs with dwarfism often suffer from co-existing health problems, such as “spina bifida, spinal deviations, hemivertebrae, brachycephalic syndrome, angular/rotational limb deformities, and intervertebral disc disease,” warns Patty Khuly, DVM, in an article for[Hill’s Pet](https://www.hillspet.com/dog-care/healthcare/understanding-dwarfism-in-dogs?ref=petcube.com) .

## Life Expectancy in Dogs with Dwarfism


Dogs with dwarfism often have shortened lifespans compared to breed members with normal growth and appearance.


This is because the genetic mutation causing dwarfism is associated with other abnormalities, such as endocrine system failures - including[hypothyroidism](https://petcube.com/blog/hyperthyroidism-in-dogs/) (low thyroid hormone levels) and diabetes (high blood sugar levels).


## Conclusion


Dwarfism in dogs is a genetic condition. It is relatively uncommon, but it remains significant as it affects the dog’s life expectancy.


Dwarfism is not directly treatable, but dwarf dogs usually suffer from co-existing conditions that do require proper treatment.


Breeding dogs exclusively for dwarfism is discouraged by vets and ethically contentious. Dogs with pituitary dwarfism should never be bred.
