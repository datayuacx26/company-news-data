## Ayurvedic View: Time, Rhythm, and Mental Clarity


#### **“In Ayurveda, everything from eating to sleeping to detoxification and healing is aligned with time because staying in tune with natural rhythms keeps your body and mind in balance.”**


### Dinacharya (Daily Routine) for Mind-Body Balance


Ayurved focuses on the prevention of diseases, of which Dinacharya, or the daily regimen, is an inseparable part.


Dinacharya includes a set of habits such as waking up early in the morning, brushing your teeth, oil massage, eating at consistent times, exercising, and sleeping at appropriate times to balance the body and mind.


All of these habits create a sense of fixing and regularity, fundamental components for cultivating a relaxed mind.


### Why Do You Need to Follow a Daily Regimen?


Following a daily routine helps you stay in sync with the natural rhythm of the day, which promotes a sense of mental clarity, balance, and calmness.


Following this routine supports Dosha balance and improves digestion (Agni) and vitality (Ojas).


### Vata Imbalance and Scattered Schedules


Vata Dosha in your body is like the wind in nature – it governs all movements. When Vata is balanced, it carries out functions like healthy circulation, transport of nutrients, movement of body parts in the body and enthusiasm and creativity in the mind.


An abnormal increase in Vata Dosha can result in anxiety, restlessness, difficulty concentrating, and a scattered mind.


When you’re constantly hurrying, stressed, or managing time poorly, this stress further aggravates Vata, making this condition worse.


### Cultivating Sattva with Predictable Routines


There are many studies that demonstrate how following a routine in daily life can have significant effects on health and well-being, including the prevention and treatment of mental health disorders.


How does Ayurveda frame it?


Sattva is understood as goodness, a quality of positivity, purity, and peacefulness that Ayurveda strives to cultivate.


When you embrace a regular and predictable routine, you cultivate the Sattva guna, a mental quality of clarity, harmony, and balance.


A consistent routine provides predictability for your mind to remain calm and gathered.


## Practical Time Management Tips for Mental Clarity


We have seen how time management is necessary for mental health and how poor time management can lead to mental health issues. Let’s move to the interesting part. Here are some tips for practical time management.


### Begin Your Day With Serenity


- Begin your day with a clear idea about what you intend to achieve.
- Avoid using your phone as soon as you get up from bed.
- Take a few minutes to do breathwork (Pranayama) or Yoga to set the tone for a calm and focused day.


### Prioritise Your Tasks


It’s not possible to do everything, and that’s okay. Prioritise your tasks and focus on what truly matters. Categorising your tasks or preparing a to-do list can help.


### Time Block Your Activities


- Ayurveda divides the day into three main energy cycles based on the Dosha’s predominance.
- Kapha time begins from 6 AM and lasts till 10 AM. This is not the best time for tasks that require high performance, but instead, use this time to do gentle exercises or your daily morning routines.
- Pitta time lasts from 10 AM to 2 PM. During this time, your mental fire is the strongest, making it the perfect time for deep work, decision-making, and strategic thinking.
- Vata time extends from 2 PM to 6 PM, during which your mind is more creative and imaginative and also prone to distractions. Avoid tasks that require heavy concentration during this time.


### Avoid Multitasking


Focusing on too many things at once can cause brain clutter and scatter your energy and concentration. Focus on one task at a time to work more efficiently.


### Schedule Breaks


Work is important, but so are breaks. They help reduce stress and boost productivity. You can do this by pausing to take breaks every 90 minutes, enjoying a quiet walk, sipping a glass of herbal tea, or just taking a few deep breaths to relax.

## What to Let Go of to Protect Your Mental Space


You can’t control everything or get everything done. Sometimes, you just need to relax and learn to say no to unrealistic expectations or anything that pulls you into stress and hinders your peace of mind.


Guilt-Driven Multitasking:


- Overly high expectations and pressure lead to guilt-driven multitasking and, eventually, stress, distractibility, and burnout.


Emotional People-Pleasing:


- Similarly, when you take on tasks that do not align with your values or goals but do so out of fear of disappointing other people, it can be mentally draining and leave you feeling burnt out and overwhelmed.
- Remember, emotional people-pleasing is not productivity - rather, it is a need for someone else’s validation or acceptance that takes away your mental health and well-being.


Perfectionism:


- Perfectionism is another substantial barrier to productivity and a peaceful mind. Perfectionism can give the feeling of high standards, but it tends to trap you in a spiral of not being good enough, affecting your mental well-being.
