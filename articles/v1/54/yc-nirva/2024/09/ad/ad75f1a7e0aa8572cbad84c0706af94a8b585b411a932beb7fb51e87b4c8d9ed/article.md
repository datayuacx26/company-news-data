## What is 'Gut' and 'Gut Health'?


Gut refers to your gastrointestinal (GI) tract, which includes your stomach, intestines, and colon. It's responsible for breaking down food, absorbing nutrients, and eliminating waste.


In fact, research shows that our


[gut microbiome](https://www.nirvahealth.com/blog/gut-microbiome-chronic-inflammation) can affect every organ in our body.


*" **Every disease starts from the gut, in ayurveda we phrase this as 'Sarva Rogam Udara Prabhavam** '"* , says Dr.Israa Ismail, Senior Ayurveda consultant at Nirva, *" **Every single disease must be treated by checking your gut and keeping it healthy. There is no second opinion in that.** "*


**So, what is gut health?**


Gut health is a measure of how well your gut is functioning. It's not just about digestion; a healthy gut plays a crucial role in overall well-being.


Here is why your gut health is super important for your overall health.


1. **Microbiome** : Your gut is home to trillions of microorganisms, including bacteria, viruses, and fungi. This collection is known as your gut microbiome. A diverse and balanced microbiome is essential for optimal gut health.


2. **Digestion:** A healthy gut efficiently breaks down food into nutrients that your body can absorb. This helps you maintain energy levels and support overall growth and development.


3. **Immune function:** Your gut is a significant part of your immune system. A healthy gut helps protect your body from harmful pathogens.


4. **Mental health:** Research suggests a connection between gut health and mental health. The


[gut-brain axis](https://www.nirvahealth.com/blog/gut-brain-connection-how-your-gut-health-affects-your-mental-wellness) , a communication system between your gut and brain, can influence mood, stress, and cognitive function.


5. **Weight Management :** Gut health can influence metabolism, which affects how your body burns calories and stores fat. The gut also plays a role in producing hormones that regulate appetite and satiety.


6. **Skin Health** : Do not be surprised, even your radiance and glow is maintained by a healthy gut.


[The gut-skin axis](https://www.nirvahealth.com/blog/connection-between-gut-health-clear-skin) can impact inflammation throughout the body, including the skin. If you experience any skin conditions like eczema, acne and psoriasis, the first thing you must check is your gut health.


7. **Heart Health:** Your gut can also amazingly influence how your heart works! Your gut influences your cholesterol levels, which impacts your heart. Gut health also plays an essential role in regulating blood pressure.


A healthy gut efficiently absorbs nutrients from food, which are vital for growth, development, and overall health. Research is still being carried out to understand the extensive role of your gut and gut microbes in your health.

## 5 Gut Types in Modern Research


According to modern research understanding, gut types are 5.


They are;


- Stressed Gut


- Autoimmune Gut


- Toxic Gut


- Gastric Gut


- Dysbiotic Gut


Let's see what these gut types mean.


### **1. Stressed Gut**


This type of gut is seen in people who are always stressed. Stress and gut health are closely linked. The brain and digestive system are two-way connections.


A stressed gut will experience;


- Hormonal Imbalances


- Constipation and Bloating


- Sleep Issues


- Adrenal Fatigue


### **2. Autoimmune Gut**


If you have an autoimmune gut, you likely have more than one food sensitivity. This type of gut is also caused by long-term antibiotic usage.


Someone with an autoimmune gut will experience;


- Skin problems


- Autoimmune diseases like celiac disease


- Allergies


- Weakened immune system


### **3. Toxic Gut**


Just as it sounds, you get a toxic gut when your gut is exposed to different types of toxins on an everyday basis. From where? From the food you eat to the clothes you wear, cleaning products, cosmetics, and a lot more. When these toxins come into your body and burden your gut, you experience a toxic gut type.


If you have a toxic gut, you may face;


- Brittle nails and hair


- Skin problems


- Feeling fatigued


- Small Intestinal Bacterial Overgrowth


### **4. Gastric Gut**


If you think you have a gastric gut, you may experience;


- Constant bloating


- Feeling full after meals


- Feeling hungry shortly after eating


- Gas or Flatulence


### **5. Dysbiotic Gut**


You will get a


[dysbiotic gut](https://www.nirvahealth.com/blog/gut-dysbiosis-causes-symptoms-treatment) if your diet is high in carbohydrates, sugar, and alcohol. Dysbiosis is when your gut microbiome is out of balance. A situation where your bad bacteria overpowers your good ones.


If you have a dysbiotic gut, you may face;


- Candida


- SIBO


- Constipation


Now, all these types of gut can be recognised when you face unhealthy symptoms. So, until then, what?

## Different Types of Gut or Koshta in Ayurveda


Now, coming to the point of the three languages of Koshta or Gut. The major understanding of your gut type in Ayurveda is noted in the behaviour of your bowels or stools. Read on to learn how to understand what your gut is trying to tell you about your body.


They are:


### **1. Pitta Type Koshta - Mrudu Koshta ( Soft Bowel or Soft Stool Clearance)**


*'Mrudu'* means soft, smooth, or easy. This is usually seen in a person with a *pitta* temperament.


If you have a Pitta-type gut, then you may experience;


- Passing the stool is smooth and easy.


- Intense appetite and digestion.


- Soft and smooth stools with or without loose stools.


- The person does not need to spend much time in the loo.


- Coffee or hot water is not required to get the bowel moving.


**What is the Right Diet and Lifestyle for Pitta-type gut?**


- Add food items that taste sweet and sour, as well as a pinch of salt.


- Always remember to add cooling foods (not frozen or refrigerated), such as cucumber, coconut and mint, into your dishes.


- Avoid a lot of spicy food as it can put Pitta into an exaggerated state.


- Drink plenty of water, coconut water or cooling herbal teas.


- It is great if you practise cooling activities like swimming or taking a cooling shower.


**Best Herbs that Can Balance Pitta**


- Shatavari (Asparagus root)


- Brahmi ( Indian Pennywort)


- Triphala


### **2. Kapha Type Koshta - Madhyama Koshta ( Moderate Bowel Movement)**


*'Madhyama'* means moderate, which is neither too hard nor too soft. This is usually seen in *Kapha's* predominant temperament people.


If you have a Kapha-type gut, then you may experience;


- Stools get excreted with moderate easiness.


- Stool evacuation is neither difficult nor too easy.


- This is associated with an average type of digestion and appetite.


- The stools may be sticky and sometimes block the pathway.


- More stool bulk.


**What is the Right Diet and Lifestyle for Kapha-type gut?**


- Add spices to your food such as pepper, turmeric, ginger cumin and fennel to give a push to your sluggish digestion.


- Foods that taste bitter help stimulate digestion a lot.


- It's best if you avoid or limit the consumption of heavy, greasy and dairy-rich foods.


- Practise morning routines like oil pulling and tongue scraping to stimulate digestion and remove all the accumulated kapha.


**Herbs that Can Balance Kapha**


- Ginger


- Triphala


- Coriander


- Fennel


### **3. Vata Type Koshta - Krura Koshta ( Hard Bowel Clearance)**


*'Krura* ' means complex or challenging. This is usually seen in people with a *vata* -predominant temperament.


If you have a Vata-type gut, then you may experience;


- Difficulty in passing the stools


- Unpredictable appetite and digestion.


- Tendency to constipate.


- The person needs to spend a lot of time in the loo.


- Bowel motivation needs a cup of coffee or hot water.


- Stools will be dry, pellet-like, and lack smoothness.


- Pain and straining with the passage of stools. Sometimes, there is pain in the abdomen and bloating.


**What is the Right Diet and Lifestyle for Vata Type Gut?**


- Add moistening foods such as soups, stews and cooked veggies.


- Consume grounding and heavy food such as root vegetables and grains, which will balance Vata's lightening quality.


- Always choose warm foods to compensate for Vata's cold nature.


- Eat at regular intervals.


- It's essential to practise stress management like


[yoga](https://www.nirvahealth.com/blog/gut-detox-through-yoga-poses) , meditation and deep breathing.


- Practising oil massage (abhyanga) works great in balancing Vata in your body.


**Herbs that Can Balance Vata**


- Triphala


- Amalaki (Indian Gooseberry)


- Ashwagandha (Indian Ginseng)


There! Now you can evaluate yourself and know which category of *Koshta* you belong to!


**Note :** All these do not mean that a person of *vata* temperament will always have *Krura Koshta* or hard stools. *Koshta* type is the normal inborn tendency that is always present in you. This tendency can become from better or worse. This depends on the food and lifestyle you follow.


For example, you are a Vata Prakriti person, and you experience constipated stools or flatulence most of the time, you can avoid these symptoms of vata by practising a lifestyle that balances worsening of vata in your body.

### Research on 3 types of Gut


[The European Molecular Biology Laboratory](https://www.embl.org/news/science/whats-your-gut-type/) says, from its most recent research findings, that humans have three different gut types. This resonates with what the ancient knowledge of Ayurveda mentions.


The research study uncovers microbial genetic markers that are related to traits like age, gender and body mass index. The findings will allow experts to diagnose and predict outcomes for diseases like colorectal cancer and get informed treatment - just like predicting health conditions from your prakriti type.


The types of gut represent the composition of gut microbes;


- Enterotype-1 (Dysbiotic Gut)


- Enterotype-2 (Eubiotic Gut)


- Enterotype-3 (Mixed Type Gut)


**"We found that the combination of microbes in the human intestine isn't random,"** says Peer Bork, who led the study at EMBL: ‘our gut flora can settle into three different types of community - three different ecosystems.’

## How to Improve Your Gut Health Based on Your Type


Understanding the symptoms can help you recognise your gut type and take necessary steps to improve your gut health. Let us now look at specific measures required to enhance the gut health of each gut type.


### Tips to Improve Gut Health for a Stressed Gut


Healing begins when you stop what’s hurting your gut. If you have a stressed gut, your focus should be on relieving stress. Here are some useful tips


- Practise stress-relieving techniques like yoga, meditation, breathing exercises, or mindfulness to calm yourself.


- Practise mindful eating – avoid distractions while you eat. Focus on what’s on your plate, chew well, and enjoy your meal.


- Ensure you get adequate sleep of at least 7 to 9 hours every night. This helps your body get the rest it needs and enhances healing processes.


- Stay physically active by engaging in gentle physical activities like stretching, jogging, swimming, or cycling. This can stimulate digestion, improve your mood, and also relieve stress.


### Tips to Improve Gut Health for an Autoimmune Gut


The autoimmune gut is caused by your body’s immune system mistakenly attacking healthy cells of your gut, causing inflammation and damage to your gut lining. If you have an autoimmune gut, here are some methods by which you can improve gut health:


- Maintain an


[anti-inflammatory diet](https://www.nirvahealth.com/blog/anti-inflammatory-food-guide) rich in whole foods and antioxidant-rich ingredients like fruits (especially berries), leafy greens, healthy fats, and spices like black pepper, ginger, and turmeric.


- Include foods rich in omega-3 fatty acids, like walnuts, flaxseeds, avocados, and chia seeds, and fatty fish like salmon, mackerel, and sardine to reduce gut inflammation.


- Avoids foods that can cause allergies or intolerances, like gluten and dairy.


- Cut out excessively sugary, oily, or spicy foods, as they may worsen the inflammation of your gut.


- Including gut-healing foods with soothing and anti-inflammatory properties, like bone broth and aloe vera, or supplements like glutamine, collagen, or probiotics in your diet can help heal your gut lining faster


- Avoid overuse of medications like pain-relieving pills and antibiotics without your doctor’s advice.


### Tips to Improve Gut Health for a Toxic Gut


A toxic gut is caused when harmful chemicals from the environment, certain medications, or an unhealthy diet damage your gut lining. In this condition, removing exposure to toxins or harmful substances is key to improving your gut health. Here’s how you can achieve it:


- Avoid exposure to environmental factors that can affect your gut health, like pollutants and pesticides. Choose organic foods, stick to natural skin care products, and use non-toxic cleaning products.


- Avoid foods that contain additives like artificial sweeteners, colours, or flavours, as these can contribute to gut toxicity.


- Drink plenty of water or herbal teas prepared from herbs like turmeric, ginger, fennel, and liquorice root to help detoxify by flushing out the toxins from your gut.


- Include antioxidant-rich foods like berries, citrus fruits, leafy greens, carrots, and beets. Antioxidants neutralise the harmful free radicals produced in the gut and protect it from damage and inflammation.


- Limit the intake of alcohol and caffeine as these may delay the healing.


- Intake of


[probiotics](https://www.nirvahealth.com/blog/probiotics-for-gut-health-during-travelling) and prebiotics as food or supplements can help


[improve your gut microbiome](https://www.nirvahealth.com/blog/tips-to-boost-gut-microbiome) balance by preventing the overgrowth of harmful gut bacteria.


### Tips to Improve Gut Health for a Gastric Gut


A gastric gut is a condition where your gut lining is inflamed. This lining, called the gastric mucosa, protects your stomach from strong digestive enzymes. When damaged, the protective action of this lining is compromised. In individuals with a gastric gut, gut health can be improved by these methods:


- Eat slowly and mindfully, and avoid distractions while eating. Do not rush through your meals, as it can lead to indigestion, bloating, and abdominal discomfort.


- Eat smaller meals more frequently. This helps with easier digestion, keeps you feeling full for longer, and reduces feelings of fullness or bloating soon after meals.


- Avoid foods that can irritate your gut lining, like spicy, citrus, fried and oily foods, alcohol, and caffeine.


- Include herbs that are high in mucilage and soothe the gut lining by forming a protective barrier against digestive enzymes, such as slippery elm, liquorice root, aloe vera, and chamomile.


- Avoid lying down immediately after meals. Eat your dinner at least 2 to 3 hours before going to bed to prevent indigestion and acid reflux.


### Tips to Improve Gut Health for a Dysbiotic Gut


Gut dysbiosis occurs when there is a decrease in beneficial gut bacteria or when there’s an overgrowth of harmful gut bacteria, leading to an imbalance in your gut microbiome. These tips can help you improve digestion and gut health if you have a dysbiotic gut:


- Eliminate foods that cause gut dysbiosis, like refined sugar and processed goods. Include nutrient-rich foods like fruits, vegetables, whole grains, legumes, and healthy fats to support a healthy


[gut microbiome](https://www.nirvahealth.com/blog/tips-to-boost-gut-microbiome) .


- Include fermented foods that contain probiotics (live microbes that have positive health benefits when consumed), such as yoghurt, fermented soy products, kimchi, sauerkraut, and kefir.


- Consume foods that are rich in fibre. A type of fibre called prebiotics acts as a food source for beneficial bacteria in your gut. Some foods that contain prebiotics are onions, garlic, artichokes, leeks, asparagus, oats, and so on.


- Use herbs and spices with antimicrobial properties, such as ginger, cumin, clove, garlic, and turmeric, while cooking or preparing herbal teas.


- Avoid overuse of antibiotics as it may kill the beneficial bacteria in your gut and lead to dysbiosis.
