# The Next 250 Years Start With What We Build Now

If the next 250 years are shaped by speed, adaptability and integration, then one of the most important advances may be not just what gets built, but *how* it is built. That is the idea behind Modular Open Systems Approach, or MOSA — designing systems with standardized, interoperable architectures so components can be added, upgraded or replaced without redesigning the entire platform. In practical terms, that can make it easier to integrate new sensor, software, radio and mission applications as technology changes.


That matters because many legacy systems were not built for rapid modernization. Upgrades could be slow, costly and dependent on closed architectures that limited flexibility. MOSA, similar to software-defined architectures, offers a different path, helping operators modernize faster, improve interoperability and bring new technologies into the field more efficiently. It is a model built for a future in which threats evolve quickly and systems need to keep pace.


L3Harris is helping advance that approach through its work supporting the[U.S. Air Force’s development of modular open systems](https://www.l3harris.com/newsroom/editorial/2024/09/l3harris-successfully-completes-critical-design-review-us-air-force) for multiple air platforms. Together, those efforts reflect a broader shift in American innovation: moving away from static, closed designs and toward adaptable systems that can evolve over time.


In the next era of national defense technology, that kind of flexibility may be one of the biggest advantages of all.
