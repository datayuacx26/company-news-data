## The blind spot in most strategies


When you ask purchasing managers about their approach, they usually highlight strategic sourcing. They talk about preferred supplier lists, request for proposal (RFP) processes, and volume discounts. These are all essential tools. Yet, a significant portion of the budget often skips this careful review.


Purchasing generally falls into two broad buckets: direct and indirect spend. Direct spend covers the raw materials and parts that go straight into what you make or sell. Indirect spend covers everything else. It includes the products and services that keep your business running, even if they are not part of your core product.


Industry experts often call this the “tail spend.” It is a long list of smaller supplier relationships that add up to a surprisingly large share of your total budget. Think of office supplies, IT services, maintenance, workwear, and facility services.


Companies often set up facility and[uniform programs](https://unifirst.com/uniform-rental-company/) , let them renew automatically, and rarely review them. Without proper scrutiny, you miss out on massive opportunities for improvement.

## Taking action: A smarter approach to indirect spend


Here is a helpful way to think about your supplier network. Procurement data shows that about 80% of an organization’s total spend flows through roughly 20% of its suppliers. The remaining 20% is tail spend, which is spread across the other 80% of suppliers.


That means a huge number of vendor relationships, and a lot of your budget, receives very little attention.


A single uniform program might not equal a massive raw material contract. However, the cumulative opportunity to save money is very real. A mature procurement strategy builds a system that actively prevents budget bleeding across all categories.
