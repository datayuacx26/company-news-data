## What is Direct Drive Force Feedback?


Direct drive force feedback represents a quantum leap in sim racing technology. Unlike traditional gear-driven or belt-driven systems that rely on multiple mechanical components to transmit motor output to your steering wheel,


direct drive motors connect directly to the wheel shaft


. This eliminates the mechanical "middlemen" that can introduce lag, noise, and imprecision.


When you're racing through Eau Rouge at Spa-Francorchamps, every subtle road surface change, tire slip, and aerodynamic shift gets transmitted instantly to your hands through the wheel. The motor responds to game physics in real-time, creating an unfiltered connection between your virtual car and your physical steering input.


Traditional wheels use complex gear trains or belt systems that create what engineers call "compliance." These are tiny delays and dampening effects that blur the connection between game and driver. ## Comparing Direct Drive to Gear and Belt-Driven Wheels


****


Gear-Driven Wheels:


Traditional gear-driven systems use multiple gears to transfer motor power to the wheel. While affordable and reliable, they introduce mechanical "notchiness." This stepwise feel becomes apparent during slow steering inputs. These systems typically max out around 2-3 Nm of force.


Belt-Driven Wheels:


Belt systems eliminate the notchy feel of gears, providing smoother operation and better detail transmission. However, belts can stretch over time, introducing play and reducing precision. Most belt-driven wheels produce 3-8Nm of torque.


Direct Drive Systems:


Direct drive wheels like the VelocityOne Race eliminate all mechanical intermediaries. The motor connects directly to the steering shaft, providing unfiltered feedback with no lag, no mechanical play, and no degradation over time. The 7.2Nm output delivers professional-level force while remaining manageable for extended racing sessions.
