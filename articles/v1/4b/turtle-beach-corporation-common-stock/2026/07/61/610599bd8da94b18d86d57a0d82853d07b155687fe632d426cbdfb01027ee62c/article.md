# How to Connect a Gaming Headset to PS5, Xbox Series X|S & PC

You bought a gaming headset, plugged it in, and... nothing. Or worse, audio is blasting from your TV while your headset does nothing. Every platform handles headset connections differently, and what works on PC might need a completely different approach on Xbox or PS5.


The good news? None of this is complicated once you know what each platform actually expects. Here is every connection method, broken down by platform, so you can stop troubleshooting and start fragging.


### TL;DR


Wired 3.5mm headsets work on every platform through the controller (PS5, Xbox) or directly into the PC. Wireless headsets connect via USB dongle for the best low-latency experience. Bluetooth works on PC and mobile, but is not supported for game audio on PS5 or Xbox. For a hassle-free


[wireless setup across multiple platforms](https://www.turtlebeach.com/blog/wireless-headset-pc-console-compatibility) , a headset with a dedicated USB wireless transmitter is the move.

## How Do You Connect a Headset to PS5?


Wired (3.5mm):


Plug the 3.5mm cable into the headphone jack on the bottom of your DualSense controller. PS5 detects the headset automatically and routes audio to your ears. Go to Settings > Sound > Audio Output to confirm the headset is selected, then use the PS button quick menu to dial in volume and chat balance.


Wireless (USB Dongle):


Plug your headset's USB wireless dongle into one of the PS5's USB ports, power on the headset, and the PS5 handles the rest. Select the headset in Settings > Sound > Audio Output and enable 3D Audio for Headphones to get spatial sound in supported games.


One critical thing to know: PS5 does not support standard Bluetooth for game audio. Your Bluetooth-only headphones will not cut it here. You need a


[wireless gaming headset](https://www.turtlebeach.com/collections/headsets) with a USB dongle for wireless PS5 connectivity. Sony's official


[PS5 audio settings guide](https://www.playstation.com/en-us/support/hardware/ps5-change-audio-output/) has the full rundown on output options.


## How Do You Connect a Headset to Xbox Series X|S?


Wired (3.5mm):


Plug the 3.5mm cable into the headphone jack on the bottom of your Xbox controller. Audio routes automatically. Press the Xbox button and navigate to the Audio section in the Guide menu to adjust volume and chat balance. For format settings, head to Settings > General > Volume & Audio Output.


Wireless (USB Dongle):


Plug the USB wireless dongle into a USB port on your Xbox, power on the headset, and wait for pairing. Adjust audio through the Xbox Guide's Audio controls. Set Headset Format to Stereo Uncompressed for the cleanest output. For spatial audio, you can enable Windows Sonic (free) or Dolby Atmos through the same menu, though Dolby Atmos requires the separate Dolby Access app from the Microsoft Store.


Xbox Wireless (Direct Pairing):


Some headsets use Xbox Wireless, the same protocol your Xbox controllers use. Put the headset into pairing mode, press the pairing button on your console, and the headset connects directly with no dongle needed. Look for the "Designed for Xbox" badge on headset packaging to confirm Xbox Wireless compatibility, as standard Bluetooth headsets cannot pair this way.


All three methods also work on Xbox One, though original Xbox One controllers without a 3.5mm jack require the Xbox Stereo Headset Adapter for wired connections.


Same deal as PS5 here: Xbox does not support Bluetooth audio natively. You need either Xbox Wireless support or a USB dongle. Microsoft's official


[Xbox audio output guide](https://support.xbox.com/en-US/help/hardware-network/display-sound/choosing-speaker-audio-output) covers every format option. Wondering whether


[wired or wireless makes more sense](https://www.turtlebeach.com/blog/should-i-stick-with-wired-or-try-wireless-gaming-headsets) ? USB dongles deliver the best balance of freedom and low latency.


## How Do You Connect a Headset to a PC?


PC supports four connection methods: 3.5mm wired, USB wired, USB wireless dongle, and Bluetooth. More options mean more flexibility, but also more room for things to go sideways.


Wired (3.5mm):


Plug into your PC's headphone jack (usually the green port on desktops). Laptops typically have a combo headphone/mic port. If your headset uses separate audio and mic plugs, grab a Y-splitter adapter to combine them. Right-click the speaker icon in your taskbar, open Sound Settings, and select the headset as your output and input device.


Wired (USB):


Plug the USB cable into any available port. Windows installs drivers automatically, and you select the headset in Sound Settings for both output and input.


Wireless (USB Dongle):


Plug in the dongle, power on the headset, and Windows detects everything automatically. Select the headset in Sound Settings. USB wireless delivers low-latency performance ideal for gaming, which matters when you care about


[sound quality and latency differences](https://www.turtlebeach.com/blog/wired-vs-wireless-gaming-headphones-sound-quality-latency) between connection types.


Bluetooth:


Go to Settings > Bluetooth & Devices > Add Device, put your headset in pairing mode, and select it from the list. Set the headset as your default audio device. Bluetooth works for casual gaming and media, but expect latency in fast-paced shooters. USB wireless remains the competitive pick.


## How Do You Fix Common Headset Connection Issues?


Headset Not Detected:


Try a different USB port (rear panel ports on desktops tend to be more reliable). Restart your console or PC. Confirm the headset is charged for wireless models or firmly plugged in for wired. Update your controller firmware when using a 3.5mm connection through the controller.


Audio Coming from the Wrong Device:


Check your platform's audio output settings and manually select the headset. On PC, right-click the speaker icon and set the headset as your default device.


Mic Not Working:


Confirm the mic is unmuted (check for flip-to-mute switches or physical mute buttons). Verify input device settings on your platform, and update controller firmware for 3.5mm headsets plugged into controllers. A


[wireless vs wired microphone quality](https://www.turtlebeach.com/blog/wireless-vs-wired-headset-microphone-quality) comparison covers what to expect from each connection type.

## FAQs


### How do I connect a gaming headset to my PC?


Plug in via 3.5mm, USB, or USB wireless dongle. Open Windows Sound Settings and select the headset as your output and input device. USB wireless dongles deliver the lowest latency for gaming, while Bluetooth works for casual listening but can introduce delay in competitive titles. For the best wireless experience, use a USB dongle rather than Bluetooth to avoid the latency penalty in fast-paced games.


### How do I connect a headset to an Xbox One controller?


Plug a 3.5mm headset cable into the headphone jack on the bottom of the controller. Audio routes automatically. Original Xbox One controllers released without a headphone jack require the Xbox Stereo Headset Adapter, but all newer Xbox controllers have the 3.5mm jack built in.


### Do I need an adapter to connect a headset to the Xbox?


Not for 3.5mm headsets on modern Xbox controllers. If you have one of the original Xbox One controllers without a headphone jack, the Xbox Stereo Headset Adapter is required. Wireless headsets connect through USB dongles or Xbox Wireless with no adapter needed.


### How do I connect a wireless gaming headset to PS5?


Plug the headset's USB wireless dongle into a PS5 USB port, power on the headset, and select the headset in Settings > Sound > Audio Output. PS5 does not support standard Bluetooth for game audio, so a USB wireless dongle is required for wireless connectivity. Once connected, enable 3D Audio for Headphones in the sound settings to get spatial audio in supported games.


### Why won't my gaming headset connect to my Xbox?


Confirm your controller firmware is updated, the 3.5mm plug is fully inserted, or the USB dongle is seated in a working port. Restart the console and try a different USB port if the issue persists. Xbox does not support Bluetooth audio natively, so standard Bluetooth headsets will not connect for game audio. If using Xbox Wireless, make sure the headset is in pairing mode before pressing the console's pairing button.


### Can I use any gaming headset on Xbox Series X|S?


Any headset with a 3.5mm jack works through the controller. For wireless connectivity, the headset needs Xbox Wireless support or a compatible USB dongle. Standard Bluetooth headsets will not work for game audio on Xbox. Set the headset format to Stereo Uncompressed in audio settings for the cleanest output, or enable Windows Sonic for free spatial audio.
