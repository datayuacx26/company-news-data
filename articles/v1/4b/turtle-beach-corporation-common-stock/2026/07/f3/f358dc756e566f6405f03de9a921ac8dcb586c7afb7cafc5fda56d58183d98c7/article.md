# Sim Racing for Beginners: Wheel, Pedals, and Settings Explained

Sim racing in 2026 is absurdly accessible. Direct drive wheels that used to cost well over $500 now come bundled with pedals for under $300, and the games have never been better at teaching newcomers how to actually drive fast.


You do not need a full cockpit rig, a dedicated room, or a second mortgage. You just need the right gear and a willingness to eat a few walls at turn one.


Here is everything you need to build your first sim racing setup, pick the right settings, and start shaving seconds off your lap times.


### Quick Answer


- Minimum gear:


A console or PC, a racing game, and a controller


- Best wheel type for beginners:


Direct drive (3 to 5 Nm), skip belt drive entirely


- Where to spend first:


Pedals over the wheel, load cell or hall-effect brakes make the biggest difference


- Best starter games:


Forza Motorsport (Xbox/PC) or Gran Turismo 7 (PS5)


- Settings:


Start with all driving assists on, then disable them one at a time


## What You Actually Need to Start Sim Racing


A console or PC, a racing game, and a controller. Seriously, that is the minimum. Plenty of competitive sim racers post blistering lap times on a


[gamepad](https://www.turtlebeach.com/collections/controllers) alone, and a wireless controller with precise thumbsticks handles casual racing just fine. Need inspiration for a low-key


[couch gaming setup](https://www.turtlebeach.com/blog/ultimate-couch-gaming-setup-wireless-controllers-with-30-foot-range) ? A controller and your favorite racer are a perfectly valid starting point.


When you want more immersion, a racing wheel and pedals change everything. Steering with a physical wheel and braking with a real pedal feel far more natural than thumbstick inputs, and the jump in consistency is noticeable almost immediately.


## How to Choose Your First Racing Wheel


Go direct drive. In 2026, direct drive wheels have dropped into beginner price ranges and deliver noticeably smoother force feedback than belt drive options. The wheel is your main connection to the car, and force feedback motors inside the wheelbase push resistance and vibration through the rim so you can feel tire grip, road surface changes, and weight transfer in real time.


### Belt Drive vs Direct Drive


Belt drive wheels use a belt-and-motor system. Affordable, but the feedback can feel delayed or mushy under hard cornering.


Direct drive wheels connect the motor straight to the wheel shaft, delivering faster and more detailed feedback. In 2026,


[direct drive racing wheels](https://www.turtlebeach.com/blog/vevelocityone-race-kd3-direct-drive-racing-wheel-3-2nm-force-feedback-motor) have dropped into price ranges that used to be belt-drive territory, and going direct drive from the start means you skip the inevitable upgrade itch six months later.


### Torque: How Much Do You Actually Need?


Torque, measured in Nm (newton-meters), controls how strong the force feedback feels.


- 3 to 5 Nm


works perfectly for beginners on a desk mount


- 8 to 12 Nm


is where most racers settle long-term


- 15+ Nm


is enthusiast territory, absolutely not necessary to start


A 3.2Nm wheel will teach you everything you need to know about weight transfer and tire behavior. Do not let anyone convince you that bigger numbers automatically mean better racing.


## Why Do Pedals Matter More Than the Wheel?


Here is the truth most beginners miss: experienced sim racers will tell you pedals matter more than the wheel for getting faster.


Consistent braking wins races. Standard potentiometer pedals measure how far you press. Load cell pedals measure how hard you press. Pressure-based braking builds muscle memory faster and makes your lap times far more repeatable, exactly like braking in a real car.


### What to Look For in Pedals


- Two-pedal sets


(throttle + brake) cover most racing games


- Three-pedal sets


add a clutch for manual shifting


- Load cell brakes


are the single biggest performance upgrade available


One rule of thumb: if your budget only allows one upgrade, put the money into better pedals before upgrading your wheel. Hall-effect pedals also offer excellent long-term reliability without sensor degradation.


## Mounting Your Setup


Force feedback means the wheelbase fights back, so stability matters.


A desk clamp is the simplest option and handles lower-torque wheels without issue. Wheel stands offer more stability and fold away when you are done. Dedicated cockpit rigs eliminate all flex and feel the most realistic. Start with what you have. A sturdy desk and a clamp get the job done for your first few months.


## What Is the Best Sim Racing Game for Beginners?


Forza Motorsport (Xbox/PC) is the most beginner-friendly sim in 2026, with adjustable assists that ease you in gradually. Gran Turismo 7 (PS5) has an excellent license test system for mastering fundamentals like braking points and racing lines. Assetto Corsa Competizione leans harder into realism for players wanting a genuine challenge. iRacing is the go-to for structured competitive online racing with skill-based matchmaking.


Pick whatever matches your platform and interests. You can always jump to a more serious sim later. And if


[flight simulation](https://www.turtlebeach.com/blog/rise-of-home-flight-simulation-best-flight-stick-2025) is more your speed, understanding the difference between


[flight sticks and gamepads](https://www.turtlebeach.com/blog/flight-sticks-vs-gamepads-joysticks-gaming-gear) matters too.


## Beginner Settings That Actually Help


When starting out, driving assists save you from spinning out every other corner.


Traction control prevents wheelspin during acceleration. ABS (anti-lock braking) stops your tires from locking up under hard braking. Stability control catches slides before they turn into full spins.


Turn these off one at a time as your confidence grows. Most racers drop traction control first, then ABS, then stability control last.


### Force Feedback Settings


Every game handles force feedback differently. Start with the default settings, then adjust gradually. Too much force fatigues your hands after a few laps. Too little makes the wheel feel lifeless. Finding your sweet spot takes a few sessions, but the difference in feel is night and day.


## How to Actually Get Faster


Master one track first. Repeating the same circuit builds consistency far faster than jumping between a dozen different layouts.


Brake earlier than you think you need to. New sim racers almost always brake too late. Slow in, fast out is the oldest racing mantra for a reason, and getting the braking point right matters more than nailing the perfect racing line.


Watch your inputs. Most Sims display throttle, brake, and steering data in real time. Smooth inputs always beat aggressive ones.


Race against AI before going online. Build confidence in single-player before entering multiplayer lobbies where everyone thinks they are the next champion.

## FAQs


### What do I need to start sim racing?


A gaming platform (PC or console), a racing game, and a controller. A racing wheel and pedals add immersion and consistency, but are not required to begin. Many competitive racers use controllers successfully.


### What is the best sim racing wheel for beginners?


A direct drive wheel in the 3 to 5 Nm range offers the best balance of price and performance for new sim racers in 2026. Direct drive provides smoother, more detailed force feedback than belt drive alternatives at similar price points.


### How much does a sim racing setup cost?


A basic controller-only setup costs nothing extra if you already own a console or PC. Wheel and pedal bundles with direct drive force feedback start around $250 to $450 for entry-level options.


### Do you need a wheel and pedals for sim racing?


No. Controllers work well, and many competitive racers rely on them. A wheel and pedals add realism and help build consistency over time, but a controller is a valid way to race at every skill level.


### What is the best sim racing game for beginners?


Forza Motorsport and Gran Turismo 7 are the most beginner-friendly options with built-in tutorials, adjustable driving assists, and progressive difficulty that scales with your skill.


### Can you start sim racing with a controller?


Absolutely. A controller is a perfectly valid way to race. Upgrading to a wheel later is optional and entirely depends on how deep you want to go into the sim racing hobby.
