# What Is a HOTAS? Flight Stick and Throttle Setup Explained

If you have been poking around flight sim communities or shopping for


[flight simulation gear](https://www.turtlebeach.com/collections/racing-simulation) , you have almost certainly run into the term "HOTAS." Sounds intimidating. The reality? Dead simple, and knowing what a HOTAS actually does will save you from buying the wrong setup.


Here is a no-nonsense breakdown of what a HOTAS is, how each piece works, and what separates a great first setup from a regrettable impulse buy.


Quick Answer:


HOTAS stands for Hands-On Throttle And Stick. A HOTAS setup pairs a flight stick for steering with a separate throttle for speed control, giving you two-handed, button-rich command of your aircraft. The concept originates from military fighter jets and has become the standard control method for flight simulators like Microsoft Flight Simulator 2024, DCS World, and X-Plane 12.


## What Does HOTAS Stand For?


HOTAS stands for Hands-On Throttle And Stick. The concept comes straight from military aviation, where fighter pilots needed every critical control within finger reach at all times. Looking down at a button panel mid-dogfight is not exactly an option.


In gaming, a HOTAS setup means two separate pieces of hardware working together:


- A flight stick (joystick) in one hand for steering the aircraft


- A throttle in the other hand for controlling speed


- Buttons, switches, and hat switches across both pieces so you never have to lift your hands


Real talk: once you fly with a proper HOTAS, going back to a keyboard feels like trying to parallel park with oven mitts on.


## How Does a HOTAS Work?


The flight stick handles pitch (nose up and down) and roll (banking left and right). Most sticks also include a twist axis for yaw, which rotates the nose left or right. Dedicated


[rudder pedals](https://www.turtlebeach.com/products/velocity-one-rudder?Title=Default+Title) can take over that yaw function for more precise control, but a twist axis covers the basics just fine when starting out.


The throttle controls engine power. Push forward to go faster, pull back to slow down. More advanced throttles add sliders, toggles, and rotary dials for managing multiple aircraft systems simultaneously. A keyboard and mouse can technically get you airborne, but the difference in feel is massive.


## HOTAS vs Joystick: What Is the Difference?


A standalone joystick gives you flight control with one hand. You handle speed through keyboard keys or a small throttle slider on the stick base. Functional, sure, but limited.

## What to Look for in Your First HOTAS


Not all HOTAS hardware is created equal, and the wrong pick can sour the experience before you ever leave the runway. Here are the specs that actually matter when choosing your first setup.


### Button Count and Layout


More buttons mean fewer trips to the keyboard. Look for at least 10 to 12 programmable buttons across the stick and throttle. Hat switches, those small directional nubs on top of the stick, are especially clutch for camera control and trim adjustments.


### Axis Count


Axes are your directional controls. A basic HOTAS offers 3 to 4 axes covering pitch, roll, yaw, and throttle. Better setups include 5 or more, which opens up independent control for flaps, mixture, propeller pitch, and other aircraft functions.


### Build Quality


Plastic HOTAS sets work fine for weekend fliers. Metal internals and weighted bases improve feel and durability for longer sessions. If you are planning to log serious hours in Microsoft Flight Simulator or DCS World, investing in a sturdier build saves you from replacing cheap gear later.


### Sensor Type


Budget sticks typically use potentiometer sensors. Reliable out of the box, but physical contact between internal components means they can develop drift over time. Hall effect sensors use magnets instead, eliminating physical wear entirely and delivering a longer lifespan with zero drift, the same contactless technology found in


[gaming controllers built to prevent stick drift](https://www.turtlebeach.com/blog/5-stop-hall-effect-clutch-triggers-hair-trigger-mode) . Turtle Beach's VelocityOne™ Flightstick uses hall effect sensors across its main axes, putting precision-tier accuracy in a compact, console-ready package.


### Platform Compatibility


Not every HOTAS works with every platform. Some sticks are PC-only, while others support Xbox or PlayStation natively. Console-specific flight gear ensures plug-and-play compatibility without adapters.


## Setting Up Your HOTAS


Getting started is straightforward. Connect both pieces via USB, as most HOTAS sets use two cables, one per unit. Install any required drivers or companion software for button mapping and calibration. Launch your flight sim and check the controls section, since most modern games auto-detect popular HOTAS hardware. From there, customize your bindings and map the most-used functions to the buttons you reach easiest. Position the stick directly in front of you or slightly to the right, with the throttle on your left within comfortable reach.


## What Games Support a HOTAS?


Most flight simulators support HOTAS input natively. Microsoft Flight Simulator 2024 offers full HOTAS support with automatic profile detection, and the franchise has attracted over 15 million pilots and more than one billion flights since the 2020 relaunch (Microsoft, June 2024). DCS World is essentially built around HOTAS controls for military aircraft. X-Plane 12 supports extensive custom bindings for civilian aviation fans. Elite Dangerous and Star Citizen both support HOTAS and HOSAS setups for space flight, and Ace Combat 7 offers HOTAS support on console.


The bottom line: if it involves flying, chances are strong your HOTAS will work with it.


## Do You Need Rudder Pedals?


Rudder pedals handle yaw independently from your stick. Most HOTAS sticks include a twist axis that covers basic yaw control, so pedals are optional when starting out.


Adding pedals later gives you more precise yaw input and frees up the twist axis entirely. For helicopter flying and complex multi-engine aircraft,


[dedicated pedal hardware](https://www.turtlebeach.com/products/velocity-one-rudder?Title=Default+Title) makes a genuinely noticeable difference. Comparing


[flight sticks and gamepads](https://www.turtlebeach.com/blog/flight-sticks-vs-gamepads-joysticks-gaming-gear) is also worth exploring if you are still deciding which setup suits your style.

## FAQs


### What does HOTAS stand for?


HOTAS stands for Hands-On Throttle And Stick. A joystick and separate throttle give you full aircraft control without removing your hands from the hardware.


### What is the difference between a HOTAS and a joystick?


A joystick is one piece of hardware for flight control. A HOTAS adds a separate throttle, giving you two-handed control and more buttons for managing aircraft systems.


### Is a HOTAS worth it for flight simulators?


Yes, for most players. A HOTAS adds precision and immersion that keyboard or gamepad controls cannot match, especially in realistic sims like Microsoft Flight Simulator.


### Can you use a HOTAS on PS5 and Xbox?


Some HOTAS hardware supports consoles natively, but availability varies by manufacturer. Check compatibility before buying, as many HOTAS sets are PC-only.


### Do you need a HOTAS for Flight Simulator?


No. You can play with a keyboard, mouse, or gamepad. A HOTAS makes the experience significantly more immersive and precise, but the game works without one.


### What games support a HOTAS?


Microsoft Flight Simulator, DCS World, X-Plane 12, Elite Dangerous, Star Citizen, Ace Combat 7, and many other flight and space simulation games support HOTAS controls.
