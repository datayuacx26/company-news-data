# Spatial Audio vs Dolby Atmos: Which Is Better for Headphones?

Spatial audio and Dolby Atmos are often thrown around as if they mean the same thing, but they do not. One is a broad category of technology, the other is a specific product inside that category. The two are not competing technologies. Dolby Atmos is a specific format within the broader spatial audio category, and confusing it with the broader category leads to bad audio decisions. Here is exactly what separates them, which one matters for your platform, and how to get the best 3D sound out of your gaming headset.


## Quick Answer


Spatial audio is the umbrella term for any technology that simulates 3D sound through headphones. Dolby Atmos is one specific spatial audio format made by Dolby Laboratories, and arguably the most widely supported option for Xbox and PC gaming. PlayStation 5 gamers use Sony's Tempest 3D AudioTech. No matter which format you choose, any quality stereo gaming headset works. The processing happens in software, not hardware.

## What Is Spatial Audio?


Spatial audio refers to any technology that makes sound feel positioned around you in three-dimensional space, above, below, behind, not just left and right. Standard stereo gives you two channels. Spatial audio uses processing algorithms to simulate directional sound through those same two drivers.


Multiple technologies fall under this umbrella. Dolby Atmos is available on Xbox, PC, and some mobile devices. Windows Sonic comes free on Xbox and Windows PC. DTS Headphone:X is a paid alternative on select platforms. Sony Tempest 3D AudioTech is built into PS5. Apple Spatial Audio covers Apple devices. Each uses different processing methods, but the goal is identical: convincing your brain that sound is coming from specific positions in 3D space using two speakers in your headset.


## What Is Dolby Atmos?


Dolby Atmos is a specific spatial audio format created by Dolby Laboratories. Originally built for movie theaters, Dolby Atmos treats sounds as individual objects positioned in 3D space, including overhead, rather than locking them to fixed channels like traditional surround sound.


For headphones, Dolby Atmos applies head-related transfer function (HRTF) processing to simulate those positioned sounds through stereo drivers (Dolby Laboratories). The result is audio that genuinely feels like it surrounds you from every direction.


On Xbox and PC, Dolby Atmos for Headphones requires the Dolby Access app, a one-time purchase of $14.99 from the Microsoft Store (Dolby Access, Microsoft Store). Games with native Dolby Atmos support send positional audio data directly to the Atmos renderer, delivering the most accurate spatial effect possible.

## Which One Should You Pick for Gaming?


Your platform makes this call for you.


Xbox Series X|S:


Dolby Atmos or Windows Sonic. Atmos offers slightly better spatial accuracy in natively supported games, but Windows Sonic is free and handles positional audio well. Pairing either with a


[wireless gaming headset](https://www.turtlebeach.com/blog/should-i-stick-with-wired-or-try-wireless-gaming-headsets) that connects directly to your console bypasses controller audio limitations and delivers a cleaner signal.


PS5:


Sony Tempest 3D AudioTech for headphones. Performance is excellent in PS5 titles, and Tempest works with any stereo headset at no extra cost.


PC:


Dolby Atmos, Windows Sonic, and DTS are all available. Atmos has the widest native game support and generally provides the best experience for competitive and immersive titles alike.


One critical rule applies across every platform, which is to only run one spatial audio solution at a time. Stacking Dolby Atmos through Windows while a game also processes its own spatial audio creates phase conflicts that actually reduce positional accuracy instead of improving it.

Adding height information to the audio mix makes a real difference on multi-level maps. Pinpointing whether an enemy is above or below becomes much easier with spatial audio active. That said, some competitive players prefer flat stereo because the simplicity can make left-right directional cues sharper. Test both and see which gives you a better read on enemy positioning. ## Does Spatial Audio Matter for Music and Movies?


Music is where Atmos gets interesting, but only if you are listening to tracks specifically mixed in Dolby Atmos on Apple Music, Tidal, or Amazon Music. Standard stereo tracks do not benefit from Atmos processing, so do not expect a universal upgrade across your entire library.


Movies are where Dolby Atmos makes the biggest impact. Films mixed in Atmos on Netflix, Disney+, and Apple TV+ sound noticeably more immersive with overhead and surround effects through headphones. A good


[wireless headset with cross-platform compatibility](https://www.turtlebeach.com/blog/wireless-headset-pc-console-compatibility) pulls double duty here, handling both gaming and movie night without switching gear.

Spatial audio is the umbrella. Dolby Atmos is the standout option underneath it for Xbox and PC gaming, while Windows Sonic holds its own as a free alternative. PS5 gamers should enable Tempest 3D AudioTech for headphone use. Whichever format fits your platform, pair it with a quality

## FAQs


### What is the difference between Dolby Atmos and spatial audio?


Spatial audio is the broad category covering any technology that simulates 3D positional sound through headphones. Dolby Atmos is one specific spatial audio format made by Dolby Laboratories. Other options include Windows Sonic, DTS Headphone:X, and Sony Tempest 3D AudioTech.


### Is Dolby Atmos or spatial audio better for gaming?


Dolby Atmos tends to offer slightly better positional accuracy in games that support it natively on Xbox and PC. For PS5, Tempest 3D AudioTech is the primary headphone spatial audio option and performs excellently in supported titles.


### Is Dolby Atmos the same as spatial audio?


No. Dolby Atmos is one type of spatial audio, similar to how a Mustang is one type of car. The two are not competing technologies. Dolby Atmos is a specific format that sits inside the broader spatial audio category alongside Windows Sonic, DTS Headphone:X, and Sony Tempest 3D AudioTech.


### Does Dolby Atmos help you hear footsteps in games?


Spatial audio formats like Dolby Atmos can improve directional awareness, including height detection on multi-level maps. Some competitive players prefer flat stereo for its simplicity, so test both options and see which gives you a better edge.


### Which is better, Dolby Atmos or DTS spatial audio?


Dolby Atmos has broader native game support and wider platform availability across Xbox and PC. DTS Headphone:X performs similarly in terms of audio quality, but fewer games implement it natively.


### Do you need special headphones for Dolby Atmos?


No. Any stereo headphones or gaming headset works with Dolby Atmos and every other spatial audio format. The spatial processing happens entirely in software, though higher-quality headsets with better drivers will make the spatial effects more convincing and accurate.
