# Best Audio Settings for Competitive Valorant (Hear Every Step)

Rounds in Valorant come down to information. You can have the sharpest crosshair placement on the server, but none of it matters if a flanking Jett catches you off guard because your audio settings buried her footsteps under round-start music and agent voice lines.


A few minutes in your settings menu can change how much you hear. We are talking reloads, ability activations, and footsteps that were always there but never made it through the noise.


### Quick Answer


Set Speaker Configuration to Stereo, enable HRTF, keep Sound Effects at 100%, drop Music to 0, and disable Windows Spatial Sound. Match your system sample rate to 48kHz. These six changes cover 90% of what competitive audio optimization is about.


****


## What In-Game Audio Settings Should You Change?


Open your Valorant settings and head to the Audio tab. Here are the values that matter.


### Volume Levels


Master Volume:


50 to 70%, depending on your headset sensitivity. Cranking to 100% makes gunshots painfully loud and trains your brain to tune out audio cues over time. Listening fatigue is real, and it kills awareness in late rounds.


Sound Effects:


100%. Footsteps, reloads, ability activations, and spike sounds all live under this slider. Never lower it.


Voice-Over:


25 to 40%. Agent callouts like "Spike down" are helpful, but they should never overpower movement sounds.


All Music Volume:


0 to 10%. Round-start music can mask early aggression sounds. Turn it off or keep it barely audible. Your ears should be locked on to what the enemy team is doing, not vibing to the lobby soundtrack.


### Speaker Configuration


Set Speaker Configuration to


Stereo


, even if you own a 7.1 surround sound


[gaming headset](https://www.turtlebeach.com/collections/headsets) . Valorant's audio engine is built around stereo output. Selecting "Surround" can actually reduce your ability to pinpoint direction because the game was never designed to render sound that way.


### HRTF (Head Related Transfer Function)


Enable HRTF.


HRTF simulates how your ears process 3D sound, making it significantly easier to tell whether footsteps are above, below, or behind you. On standard stereo headphones, front-back distinction is nearly impossible without it.


A few things worth knowing about HRTF:


- Sounds may feel slightly different when you first enable it. Give it 5 to 10 games before making a judgment call.


- HRTF works best with stereo headphones, not speakers.


- Disable any third-party surround sound software (Dolby Atmos, Windows Sonic, DTS) while HRTF is active. Running two spatial audio layers at once creates phase issues that smear directional cues, as


[Riot confirmed in their Patch 8.0 notes](https://playvalorant.com/en-us/news/game-updates/valorant-patch-notes-8-0/) .


## What Windows Audio Settings Matter for Valorant?


Three settings outside the game need your attention: Spatial Sound, sample rate, and audio enhancements.


Disable Windows Spatial Sound.


Right-click the speaker icon in your taskbar, open Sound Settings, select your output device, and make sure Spatial Sound is set to Off. Windows Sonic and Dolby Atmos conflict with Valorant's HRTF.


Set Sample Rate.


In your device properties under Advanced, set the default format to 24-bit, 48000 Hz (Studio Quality). Most games output audio at 48kHz, so matching your system prevents resampling artifacts that quietly degrade clarity.


Turn Off Audio Enhancements.


Disable any bass boost, virtual surround, or loudness equalization in your audio device properties. Clean, unprocessed audio gives Valorant's sound engine full control over what you hear.


## How Does Footstep Audio Work in Valorant?


Running footsteps are audible from about 50 meters, walking from roughly 15 meters, and crouch walking from around 12 meters. Shift walking is completely silent. Valorant's audio engine prioritizes making sure footsteps are heard over portraying exact distance, which means the volume difference between a close footstep and one at the edge of audible range is smaller than you might expect.


Footsteps also sound different depending on the surface. Metal produces a sharp, high-pitched ring. Wood is deeper and more resonant. Learning each map's surface types adds a whole other layer of awareness that most players ignore entirely. Hearing metal footsteps on Icebox? Someone is on pipes. That kind of knowledge wins rounds before a single bullet flies.


## What EQ Settings Help You Hear Footsteps?


Boost 1kHz to 4kHz by 1 to 3 dB. Most footstep audio lives in this frequency range. If your


[wireless gaming headset](https://www.turtlebeach.com/blog/should-i-stick-with-wired-or-try-wireless-gaming-headsets) has companion software with EQ controls, this single adjustment makes the biggest difference.


Cut bass below 200Hz slightly


(1 to 2 dB). Reducing low-end rumble makes mid-frequency sounds like footsteps and reloads noticeably clearer.


Leave highs mostly flat.


A small boost above 4kHz can improve directional awareness, but too much introduces harshness that wears your ears out fast.


Avoid dramatic EQ curves. Subtle adjustments always work better than extreme boosts. Headsets with companion apps and advanced EQ, like a


[headset with 10-band EQ](https://www.turtlebeach.com/products/stealth-700-gen-3-headset?Platform=Xbox&Color=Black) and companion app control through Swarm™ II, let you dial in exactly what you need without guessing.


## Should You Use Headphones or Speakers for Valorant?


Headphones, and it is not even close. Stereo


[gaming headphones](https://www.turtlebeach.com/blog/wired-vs-wireless-gaming-headphones-sound-quality-latency) provide dramatically better directional accuracy than any speaker setup, no matter how expensive. Room acoustics and audio reflections smear positional cues on speakers in ways no settings adjustment can fix.


## Common Audio Mistakes


Running multiple spatial audio layers.


Pick one: Valorant's HRTF or your headset's surround processing, never both. Stacking them makes everything sound worse.


Master volume at 100%.


Loud gunshots cause listening fatigue, making you less aware of quiet sounds over time. Your brain literally starts ignoring audio cues to protect itself.


Leaving music on.


Round-start music masks early pushes and rushes. Turning it off is free information.


Ignoring Windows settings.


The wrong output device or sample rate causes subtle audio problems that are surprisingly hard to diagnose.

## FAQs


### What are the best audio settings for Valorant?


Sound Effects at 100%, Voice-Over at 25 to 40%, Music at 0 to 10%, Speaker Configuration on Stereo, and HRTF enabled. Keep Master Volume between 50 and 70% to avoid listening fatigue from loud gunshots.


### Should HRTF be on or off in Valorant?


On, for most players using headphones. HRTF improves directional sound accuracy, especially for telling whether footsteps are above or below you. Give it several games to adjust before deciding if it works for your setup.


### How do I hear footsteps better in Valorant?


Max out Sound Effects, lower music and voice-over, enable HRTF, disable Windows Spatial Sound, and consider a small EQ boost in the 1kHz to 4kHz range. Running footsteps carry about 50 meters, so the information is always there if your settings let it through.


### Is stereo or surround better for Valorant?


Stereo. Valorant's audio engine is optimized for stereo output. Using surround processing can reduce positional accuracy because the game was never designed to render multichannel audio.


### What is the best EQ for hearing footsteps in Valorant?


A mild boost between 1kHz and 4kHz and a slight cut below 200Hz helps footsteps stand out without distorting other important sounds.


### Do pro Valorant players use HRTF?


Many do. HRTF improves front-back distinction, which stereo alone handles poorly. Some pros prefer a clean stereo without any processing, so personal preference and headset quality both play a role.
