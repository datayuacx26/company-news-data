## What a Cannabis Dispensary Mobile App Actually Does


A cannabis dispensary mobile app is a native app, downloaded from the App Store or Google Play, that gives your customers a direct line to your shop: browse a live menu, place an order, earn and redeem loyalty points, and receive offers, all under your brand rather than a third party's.


The difference between an app and a mobile-friendly website is control and attention. A website waits for someone to visit. An app sits on the home screen, sends notifications the customer opted into, and remembers who they are between visits. For a dispensary, that pays off three ways:


- **Selling directly.** No per-click fee to reach a customer who already chose you, and no competitor's ad sitting next to your listing.
- **Building loyalty.** Points, tiers, gamified experiences, and perks that give people a reason to buy from you instead of the shop down the street.
- **Reaching customers.** Push notifications and targeted promotions that land without paying a marketplace to deliver the message.


The rest of this guide is the playbook for doing all three well to drive repeat purchases. The Meadow and Digital Awesome integration is how you run it without the manual work.

## Play 1: Own the Channel Your Customers Already Live On


Most dispensaries reach customers through channels they rent: a marketplace listing, a Google result, a paid ad, foot traffic on a busy corner. Every one of those puts a middleman between you and the sale, and you pay for each visit.


An owned channel flips that. The most common owned channels are an email list and an SMS list, and both work. The catch is that they depend on people opening email and on you remembering to send. A branded app is the always-on version: it lives on the phone, and you can reach the customer directly the next time you have a reason to.


**Start this week:** List every channel you use to reach customers and mark which ones you own (your customer list) versus rent (marketplaces, ads, search). Then count roughly what share of last month's orders came from owned channels. That number is your baseline.

## Play 2: Turn Every Purchase Into a Reason to Come Back


The most reliable retention mechanic in retail is a loyalty program customers can see themselves making progress in. Points per dollar, tiers that unlock perks, birthday rewards, and milestone bonuses (e.g. a reward after a customer's tenth order) all give someone a concrete reason to choose you again.


Loyalty only works if it is easy to earn and easy to redeem, in-store and online. The programs that build habits go a step further with gamified elements: missions, achievement badges, streaks, and referral rewards that turn a discount into something a customer wants to keep engaging with.


**Start this week:** Sketch one simple mission your customers could complete this month, such as making two purchases, trying a featured brand, or visiting on consecutive weeks. Decide the reward up front: bonus points, an exclusive offer, or early access to a drop.

## Play 3: Bring the Right Customers Back With Targeted Messages


A message sent to everyone is a message ignored by everyone. Retention comes from sending the right nudge to the right customer at the right time: early access to a drop for your VIPs, a "we miss you" offer to someone who has not ordered in 90 days, a restock alert to someone who only buys one brand.


That requires two things: knowing each customer's history, and being able to split your list into segments. You can deliver those messages by SMS, email, or push notification. Push has a clear edge for app owners: it lands on the lock screen, costs nothing per send, and reaches customers who already chose to download your app.


**Start this week:** Build three segments from the data you already have: VIPs (top spenders), lapsed (no order in 90 days, but two or more orders before), and brand loyalists. Write one message for each.

## Play 4: Make Reordering Effortless


Most repeat purchases are lost to friction. The customer already chose you; a clunky reorder is what sends them elsewhere. If a returning customer has to re-enter their details, hunt for the product they bought last time, or discovers at checkout that it is out of stock, they bounce.


The fix is a menu that reflects live inventory, saved customer details and payment, and a reorder path that gets a known customer from open to checkout in a few taps. Accuracy matters as much as speed. Nothing erodes trust faster than ordering something the shop does not actually have.


**Start this week:** Time yourself placing a repeat order through your current online menu. Count the taps, and note every point where you had to re-enter something or hit an out-of-stock item. Each one is a place you could lose repeat orders.

## What to Look For in a Dispensary App


The features below are what separate an app that grows retention from one that just creates work:


- **Real-time, two-way sync with your POS.** The menu should reflect live inventory, and app orders should land in the same system your team already fulfills from. No manual CSV exports, no double entry.
- **One customer record across every channel.** A shopper's history and loyalty balance should be the same whether they buy in-store, online, or in the app. Siloed data means broken rewards and marketing you cannot target.
- **Data you own.** Confirm you can export your customer list, and that the relationship belongs to you, not the vendor.
- **Native iOS and Android apps.** A real app on the home screen, with push notifications, does more for retention than a mobile web page wrapped to look like an app.
- **Loyalty and messaging built in.** Points, tiers, segmentation, and push should come with the platform, not require a second tool bolted on.
- **A realistic launch timeline and real support.** Ask how long a branded app takes to ship, and who you call when something breaks.


An app that checks these boxes becomes part of how you operate. One that does not becomes another screen your team has to reconcile.
