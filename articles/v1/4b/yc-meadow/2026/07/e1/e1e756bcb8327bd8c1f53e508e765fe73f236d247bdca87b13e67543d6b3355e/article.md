### 1. What You **Must Do** to Pass the CRC Inspection


- **Demonstrate use of compliant cannabis software:** Inspectors will ask you to run them through your inventory management system and test orders on your point of sale. Retailers must use a system capable of reporting all activities to Metrc for track and trace.
- **ID Verification:** Check and confirm customer IDs to verify legal age before allowing entrance to your dispensary.
- **Provide Detailed Receipts:** Customer receipts must include specific information as mandated by the CRC.
- **List Product Prices on Your Website:** If you have an online presence, product pricing must be displayed clearly and accurately.
- **Charge Correct Taxes:** Collect and remit the appropriate taxes for each sale.
- **Sell Only Compliant, Tested Products:** Ensure that all products meet state testing standards.
- **Maintain Employee ID Credentials:** Every employee who accesses the facility or handles cannabis must hold a valid CRC-issued Cannabis Business Identification Card before their first day on-site (N.J.A.C. 17:30-8.1). Inspectors check this on the spot.
- **Annual Reporting:** Submit specified data to the CRC annually to maintain compliance.
- **Sell Only to Consumers (No B2B Sales):** New Jersey dispensaries are limited to consumer sales and must not engage in B2B transactions.


---


### 2. What You **Must Not Do** According to the CRC


- **Exceed Legal Purchase Limits:** Avoid "looping," where customers make multiple transactions to exceed legal limits.
- **Underage Sales:** Do not permit anyone under the legal age to purchase or even enter your premises.
- **Cultivate or Manufacture:** Dispensaries are not allowed to cultivate or manufacture cannabis or cannabis products on-site.
- **Sell to Non-Consumers:** Only sell directly to consumers, no wholesale or B2B transactions.
- **Improper Storage:** Cannabis must be stored in enclosed, locked areas with limited staff access.
- **Let Metrc Fall Out of Sync:** The CRC has taken real enforcement action over this. In a[February 2026 case](https://www.nj.gov/cannabis/documents/meetings/2026-02-12/Memorandum%20Recommending%20Approval%20of%20CREAM%20Retail%20Dispensary%20Enforcement%20Action.pdf) , a retailer was cited after compliance officers' regular monitoring found it had failed to track its cannabis inventory in Metrc, a violation that led to a[formal Commission resolution](https://www.nj.gov/cannabis/documents/meetings/2026-02-12/CRC%20Resolution%202026-02-12-24%20Approval%20of%20CREAM%20Retail%20Disp%20Enforcement%20Action.pdf) and financial penalty. Treat Metrc reconciliation as a daily task, not a pre-inspection scramble.


---


### 3. What You **Can Do** Under CRC Regulations


- **Offer Various Cannabis Products:** Learn which types of products you can legally purchase and sell in New Jersey.
- **Deliver Cannabis:** Follow delivery regulations if you plan to offer home delivery services to your customers, including GPS-equipped vehicles, locked storage, and vehicle documentation on file with the CRC (N.J.A.C. 17:30-14.8).
- **Curbside Pickup:** Conduct curbside retail sales in compliance with state regulations (e.g., orders must be placed in advance).
- **Operate a Consumption Lounge:** Class 5 Retailers and medical dispensaries can apply for an on-premises consumption area endorsement. New Jersey's first lounges opened in August 2025, and the CRC has continued approving new endorsements across the state since. This requires municipal approval and CRC-approved SOPs.

## Common Questions


### What does the CRC inspect before a New Jersey dispensary can open?


Inspectors verify that your security alarm and video monitoring systems are fully operational, your internal management system (POS and inventory tracking) is functioning and synced to Metrc, and your approved SOPs are actually in use on the floor. They also expect full, immediate access to your facility and records during the visit.


### What happens if my dispensary fails its CRC inspection?


In our experience working with New Jersey operators, many dispensaries don't pass their first CRC inspection. The CRC will typically flag specific deficiencies for you to remediate, sometimes on the spot, before scheduling a follow-up review. Treat every issue as fixable, and document your corrective action as you go.


### Can New Jersey dispensaries sell to other cannabis businesses?


No. Class 5 Retailer licenses are restricted to consumer sales only. Wholesale or B2B transactions are handled by separately licensed cultivators, manufacturers, and distributors, not retailers.


### What is "looping" and why does the CRC prohibit it?


Looping is when a customer completes multiple separate transactions in the same visit to exceed New Jersey's legal purchase limits. It's a violation inspectors watch for directly, and a compliant POS should block it automatically rather than relying on staff to catch it manually.


### Can New Jersey dispensaries operate a consumption lounge?


Class 5 Retailers and medical dispensaries can apply for a CRC endorsement to operate an on-premises consumption area. New Jersey's first lounges launched in August 2025, and the CRC has approved a steady stream of additional endorsements since. Endorsements require municipal approval and CRC-approved SOPs.


### How often does the CRC inspect dispensaries after the initial launch?


The CRC doesn't publish a fixed inspection calendar. Beyond the mandatory pre-opening (or conditional-to-annual conversion) inspection, the[Office of Compliance and Investigations](https://www.nj.gov/cannabis/businesses/compliance-investigations/) runs a mix of scheduled check-ins, unannounced visits, and investigations triggered by complaints, operational changes, or renewals. Your permit also requires you to grant CRC investigators complete and immediate access to your facility and records at any time, so "inspection-ready" should be a permanent state, not a pre-visit scramble.
