## What Makes BioTrack Different


BioTrack occupies a position no other vendor in this market holds: it sells a commercial POS and ERP to dispensaries, and it has historically built and operated government track-and-trace systems for states.


As of 2025, BioTrack held government track-and-trace contracts in roughly eight states, including Florida, which launched statewide in November 2024, along with Hawaii, New Mexico, Connecticut, Delaware, Arkansas, and North Dakota. It also ran Washington's system until 2017. In August 2025, BioTrack and Metrc announced a partnership that moved BioTrack's government contracts to an independent entity, BT Government, Inc., with BioTrack refocusing on its commercial POS and ERP business.


**These contracts are in flux, and at least one has already changed hands.** New York moved from BioTrack to Metrc, with all licensees required to be live in Metrc by December 17, 2025. If you operate in a state that used BioTrack traceability a year ago, confirm what your state runs today before you shop for anything.


Here is why the distinction decides your options:


- **If your state mandates BioTrack traceability,** you report into BioTrack regardless of which POS you buy. Switching POS changes your commercial layer only, and your replacement has to support BioTrack reporting.
- **If your state runs on Metrc** and you happen to use BioTrack's commercial POS, you have the full market open to you. You are replacing a product, not a mandate. Sort out which of those you are before you take a single demo. It eliminates half the shortlist immediately.


On the commercial side, BioTrack's strengths are real and worth naming. Deep inventory, genealogy, and seed-to-sale tracking. An extensive, customizable reporting suite reported at 100+ reports. And a genuine compliance pedigree earned from years inside the traceability business, which is not something a newer vendor can claim.

## Questions to Ask Before You Sign


Bring these to every demo. On a legacy migration, questions four and seven matter more than anything on a feature grid.


#### 1. Which track-and-trace system does my state require today?


Ask the regulator, not the vendor. Contracts have moved recently, and the answer determines your entire shortlist.


#### 2. How does the provider make money?


Subscription only, or subscription plus a cut of every transaction? Ask whether payments revenue is part of their model, because it determines whether you can shop your own processor later.


#### 3. What is the documented uptime, including 4/20?


Outage history on peak days is a matter of public record. Ask for the specific number and check it independently.


#### 4. What happens to years of legacy inventory and package data?


This is the question that separates a clean migration from a painful one. A long tenure on an older system usually leaves package and inventory discrepancies. Ask who reconciles them, and when.


#### 5. What is included versus what is an add-on?


Get eCommerce, delivery, loyalty, and reporting itemized in writing. "Integrated" often means "integrates with a product you will pay someone else for."


#### 6. Who answers when something breaks on a Saturday?


Ask for the response time target, whether support is free or ticketed, and then ask a current customer whether it holds.


#### 7. Can I export my data and leave?


If the answer requires a conversation with an account manager, that is your answer.

## How to Switch Off a Legacy POS Without Downtime


A modern migration is a planned overnight cutover, not a shutdown. Data is moved, traceability records are cleaned, and staff are trained before go-live. Most operators are live within days.


On a legacy switch specifically, one step protects you more than any other: **the traceability audit and inventory reconciliation.** Years on an older platform tend to accumulate package discrepancies, orphaned items, and inventory that does not match what is on the shelf. Those problems do not disappear at migration. They either get resolved before cutover, or they get imported into your new system and become your new system's problem.


The sequence that works:


1. **Choose a partner who has done migrations in your state,** not a generic retail vendor with a cannabis module.
2. **Run the audit and cleanup first.** Reconcile packages and inventory against the state system before any data moves.
3. **Migrate overnight.** Hardware installed, data transferred, system live outside business hours.
4. **Train before go-live, not after.** Staff should have completed training before their first shift on the new platform.
