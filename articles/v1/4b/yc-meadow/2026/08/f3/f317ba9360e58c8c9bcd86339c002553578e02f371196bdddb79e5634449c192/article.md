## Why is proper training important?


If you fail to take the time to properly train your new staff, they will become frustrated, confused, and will make costly mistakes. Your dispensary will become vulnerable to theft and increased inventory shrinkage, and you will experience more staff turnover. In the worst-case scenarios, your customers will feel this too: longer than usual wait times due to slow processes, or errors like receiving the wrong product or being charged an incorrect total at the point of sale.


Turnover is also where poor training gets expensive. With more than half of budtenders leaving within a year, every hire you lose to a rushed onboarding means paying for recruiting, hiring, and training all over again, while your experienced staff cover the gap. Structured training is one of the few retention levers entirely within your control. It also protects your inventory: well-trained staff make fewer of the mistakes that show up later as shrinkage, a topic we cover in depth in[How to Prevent Loss at Your Dispensary](https://getmeadow.com/blog/dispensary-loss-prevention) .


Take the necessary time required to properly train your staff. It will benefit you in the long run!


[A 2020 survey by Indeed](https://www.indeed.com/hire/c/info/new-employee-training) revealed that 39% of people who quit their jobs within 6 months of being hired reported that better training would have kept them in the role longer.


In this guide, we'll delve into the step-by-step process of training budtenders effectively, ensuring they possess the knowledge, skills, and mindset required to thrive in their role and contribute to the long-term success of your dispensary. Let's explore how to cultivate a team of knowledgeable and customer-focused professionals who can elevate the dispensary experience for every client.

## Begin with the basics


### Understanding the role


Before diving into the specifics, it's crucial that both you and your new staff understand the importance of the budtender's role within the dispensary. Budtenders are not only salespeople; they are frontline representatives of your brand and educators of your customers. They should have a beginning understanding of cannabis products and their effects, or at least be willing to learn on the job. They should be able to provide personalized recommendations to clients based on their needs and preferences. If your dispensary requires other responsibilities, be sure to communicate that effectively to your new staff member.


### Identify their Learning Style


It's important to note that not all employees retain information the same way.


According to[Business News Daily](https://www.businessnewsdaily.com/15839-new-hire-training.html) , there are three learning styles that you'll want to keep in mind as you prepare to onboard new dispensary staff:


1. **Visual.** Some staff learn processes by watching and observing tasks.
2. **Hands-on.** Other new hires may benefit from a hands-on approach and benefit from opportunities to practice executing tasks themselves.
3. **Reading.** Other employees may prefer to read instructions in their early days on the job.


Identify what type of learner your new budtender is, and tailor your training plan to accommodate their preferred learning style.

## Topics to cover: Budtender Training 101


### General Product Knowledge


The cornerstone of any effective budtender training program is product knowledge. The role of a budtender at a dispensary is typically entry-level, so they do not need to be cannabis experts immediately. Of course, experience is preferred, but a basic level of product knowledge should include:


1. Strain dominances & their corresponding effects (Indica, Sativa, Hybrid, CBD)
2. Consumption methods & their duration (Smoking, vaping, consuming edibles)
3. Cannabis terminology & product categories


The standard level of knowledge required could differ from store to store, but before starting their first shift, a budtender should be able to speak confidently with a customer about the core product categories on your shelves and how they differ. There are countless resources online for them to study; otherwise, a great way to begin is by exploring products on their own. **Ask them to shop around the retail floor and read product packaging. Have them record any questions they have and take the time to respond to them.**


### Customer Service Skills


Exceptional customer service is what sets apart a good dispensary from a great one. Train your budtenders to actively listen to customers, ask curated questions to understand their needs, and provide informed recommendations based on their preferences. Emphasize the importance of professionalism, empathy, and discretion in every interaction. **A great way to begin is by having them shadow your top sellers and best dispensary employees.**


### Basic Point of Sale Software Understanding


Before their first shift, new budtenders should complete any training courses that your point of sale system provides to have a general understanding of the system before they step out onto the retail floor. ## Structure the First 30 Days on the Floor


Nearly a quarter of budtenders who leave do so within their first month, so the first 30 days are where a structured plan pays off most. A simple week-by-week schedule turns "training" from a vague first-day orientation into a repeatable ramp that both the new hire and the trainer can follow.


**Before the first shift:**


- Complete POS training courses (like Meadow Mastery's Getting Started, Point of Sale, and Check-In).
- Read the training manual, store policies, and SOPs.
- Walk the retail floor and study product packaging.


**Week 1 (supervised):**


- Shadow a designated trainer or top seller for full shifts.
- Complete supervised transactions: a standard sale, a payment, a return or edit, and an ID check.
- Learn the opening and closing checklist, even if they won't own it yet.


**Weeks 2-3 (guided independence):**


- Run the register solo with a trainer nearby, escalating anything unfamiliar.
- Practice hardware troubleshooting: scanners, receipt printers, cash drawers.
- Sit in on one inventory task, such as receiving a delivery or a cycle count, to see how floor mistakes become inventory problems.


**Week 4 and the 30-day check-in:**


- Fully independent shifts.
- A scheduled one-on-one to review what's working, answer their recorded questions, and set the next learning goal (a Meadow Mastery course, a product category deep-dive, or cross-training).


Depending on your operations, it's standard for a budtender to receive 2-4 fully supervised training shifts before going solo, with the rest of the month as guided independence. The 30-day check-in is the step most operators skip, and it's the one that signals to a new hire that there's a future in the role.

## Advancing the Role: Turn your green employee into a successful budtender!


### Continuing Education


The cannabis industry is constantly evolving, with new products, research findings, and regulations emerging regularly. Establish a culture of continuous learning within your dispensary by providing ongoing education opportunities for your budtenders.


Employees value opportunities for growth and development in their careers. Continuing education programs provide avenues for individuals to acquire new skills, expand their knowledge, and stay updated on industry trends. **When employees feel that their professional development is supported and encouraged by their employer, they are more likely to feel satisfied and fulfilled in their roles, leading to higher retention rates.**


### Expanding Product Knowledge


This aspect of training goes beyond basic familiarity with cannabis products and delves into the nuances of different strains, their chemical compositions, and how they affect the human body. Whether it's the calming and sedative properties of indica strains, the energizing effects of sativas, or the balanced blend of both in hybrids, budtenders should be able to explain these distinctions to customers. **They should understand the role of cannabinoids such as THCV, CBN, CBG, and others in producing specific effects.**


Additionally, budtenders should be familiar with the varying potency levels of different products to guide customers in selecting the appropriate options based on their tolerance and preferences. Empowered with this expertise, budtenders can confidently navigate customer inquiries, make personalized recommendations, and contribute to the overall success of the dispensary.


### Sales Techniques


While the primary goal is to provide excellent customer service, budtenders should also be proficient in sales techniques to maximize revenue for the dispensary. **This includes upselling complementary products, introducing new offerings, and effectively communicating the value proposition of premium items.** Teach them how your store uses promotions too; a budtender who understands the thinking behind your deals sells them better, which is why we recommend sharing the fundamentals from our dispensary[discount strategy guide](https://getmeadow.com/blog/dispensary-discount-strategy) with your floor team. ## Ensure you have a complete process for onboarding new dispensary staff


Your budtender training program & onboarding process should be well documented and easy to repeat.


### Create your training materials


Typically a dispensary manager offers an onboarding packet that includes important details that the new hire can reference on their first day and continuously thereafter.


1. Create a personalized document that includes the job description & specific responsibilities related to their role.
2. Create and offer a copy of your standard shop training manual for their future reference. This should include an overview of your shop's mission and values.
3. Clearly communicate store policies that they are required to know.
4. Offer a copy of your standard operating procedures related to budtending. If they're shared, make sure your staff is aware of where they are located.
5. Publish their schedule and slowly conclude training sessions.


Typically trainers are managers or shift leaders who can dedicate their time to properly training your new staff.


### Complete and file their official paperwork


1. File their completed tax documents (I-9)
2. Make copies of their credentials (License, SSN)
3. File their completed schedule availability sheet and adhere to it
4. Onboard them into your tech stack (Company email, Slack, Asana, etc)
5. Require their signature on SOPs and other required documents related to their training materials
6. Enroll them in your Time Clock (Clock-in and Clock-out) software
7. Assign them their designated personal space or locker assignment
8. Issue Employee Identification Card or Badge


#### Here are a few additional key questions you'll want to ensure you have answers for to make it easy to ensure your team is set-up for success:


- Have we defined our company mission and values, and in what way do we ensure new staff embody these guiding principles when interfacing with each other and with customers?
- How do we ensure front of house staff know the products on our shelves and can help gently guide customers and recommend the right products?
- Have we trained budtenders and customer-facing staff to follow sales best practices?


With proper training and onboarding, it's easy to build a culture where your whole team is empowered and educated.

## Common Questions


### How long does it take to train a new budtender?


Most dispensaries plan for 2-4 fully supervised training shifts before a new budtender works solo, followed by two to three weeks of guided independence. A complete ramp to fully confident, independent work typically takes about 30 days, which is why a structured first-month plan with a 30-day check-in works well.


### What should a budtender training manual include?


A budtender training manual should include the job description and responsibilities, your store's mission and values, store policies, compliance basics (age verification, purchase limits, packaging rules), POS procedures, and the standard operating procedures for budtending. Keep it accessible from the floor so staff can reference it between customers.


### Why do budtenders quit, and how does training help retention?


High turnover in cannabis retail is driven by factors like weak onboarding, limited growth paths, and inconsistent scheduling. As of the writing of this article, Headset data cited across the industry puts annual budtender turnover around 55%, with nearly a quarter leaving in the first month. Structured training addresses the first-month cliff directly, and continuing education gives experienced staff a reason to stay.


### Do budtenders need a license or certification?


It depends on your state. Some states require dispensary employees to hold an agent card, permit, or state-mandated responsible vendor training before working, while others leave training requirements to the employer. Check your state regulator's current requirements before a new hire's first shift.
