## How Finasteride Works on Hair Follicles


Male pattern baldness comes down to one hormone: DHT (dihydrotestosterone). DHT forms when testosterone interacts with an enzyme called 5-alpha reductase. In men with a genetic sensitivity to DHT, this hormone binds to receptors in the scalp and gradually shrinks hair follicles, a process called[the stages of male pattern hair loss](https://www.phoenix.ca/blog/the-7-stages-of-hair-loss) .


Prescription DHT blocking treatments work by targeting that enzyme directly. By reducing how much testosterone converts into DHT, these treatments lower DHT levels at the follicle and slow the shrinking process driving thinning at the crown and hairline.


This is a hormonal approach, not a topical one. It works on the root cause of miniaturization, going beyond the visible symptoms on the scalp. Understanding this mechanism matters: as follicles adjust to a lower DHT environment, some men may notice temporary changes in their hair before things stabilize.


## Why Finasteride Shedding Happens


Hair loss treatments work by targeting the enzyme that produces DHT, and that hormonal shift affects all follicles on the scalp at once, including those in a resting phase. When DHT levels drop, follicles that had been sitting dormant are signalled to re-enter the active growth cycle. To do that, they first need to shed the old, resting hair still sitting in the follicle. This synchronized exit of resting hairs, a process similar to[telogen effluvium explained by the BAD](https://www.bad.org.uk/pils/telogen-effluvium) , is what causes the noticeable increase in shedding early in treatment. The shed hairs themselves are typically thin, short, or club-shaped: signs of a follicle resetting and not continuing to miniaturize. Because the process affects multiple follicles cycling through together, the shed can look alarming even though it reflects the treatment engaging with the follicle cycle, not working against it.


## When Finasteride Shedding Starts and How Long It Lasts


For most men, noticeable shedding may begin within the first several weeks of starting treatment, as the drop in DHT levels prompts resting follicles to cycle through together and release old hairs. Some men notice an uptick early on, though a more pronounced shed typically develops within the first one to two months as the hormonal shift reaches more follicles across the scalp. The shed generally tapers as the follicle cycle stabilizes: most men find shedding settles within a few months of starting treatment. A smaller number of men may experience shedding that takes longer to resolve before the cycle stabilizes, which can look alarming but often still reflects follicles resetting and not continuing to miniaturize. After the shed phase resolves, early regrowth, typically finer, shorter hairs cycling in, tends to become visible in the 3 to 6 month window, with clinical data suggesting roughly 70% of men notice visible regrowth within that period, and more substantial density changes typically requiring longer consistent use to fully show.

## What to Expect at Each Stage of Finasteride Treatment


The first couple of months are typically the most disorienting. DHT levels drop, resting follicles begin cycling through together, and the shed phase described above gradually tapers as the follicle cycle adjusts: this is the stage most men find hardest to sit through, because the visible change feels like the wrong direction. As shedding slows, follicles begin to stabilize in the lower DHT environment. Around months three to six, finer, shorter hairs begin cycling into the growth phase at the hairline and crown, with clinical data suggesting roughly 70% of men notice visible regrowth within that window, and this early regrowth is often subtle and easiest to catch in monthly comparison photos, not day-to-day mirror checks. From there, density improvements tend to become more visible as those follicles complete full growth cycles and produce progressively thicker strands. Consistent, uninterrupted use through the full treatment window matters: stopping and restarting resets the process and does not pick up where it left off.


## Known Side Effects and Risks to Understand Before Starting


Prescription hair loss treatments carry known risks beyond temporary shedding, and a balanced view matters before starting one. Sexual side effects are the most commonly reported, including reduced libido and erectile difficulties, along with mood changes that some men notice within the first months of use.


A smaller subset of men report symptoms that persist after stopping treatment, sometimes called post-finasteride syndrome. This includes ongoing sexual, neurological, and psychological symptoms, according to a[post-finasteride syndrome review on NIH](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7253896/) . It remains uncommon and is still debated within the medical community, but it should not be dismissed when weighing treatment.


A licensed Canadian healthcare provider can walk through your full medical history, discuss these risks in plain terms (including treatment costs), and help you decide whether a DHT blocking treatment fits your situation. Bringing questions about side effects to that conversation is a reasonable step, not an overreaction.


## Managing the Shedding Phase


Watching more hair fall out while trying to trust a treatment is hard. A few practical habits can make that stretch easier to get through.


- Take photos monthly, same lighting, same angle, same time of day. Mirrors lie because you check them daily and miss gradual change.
- Hold off on judgment before the 3 to 6 month mark. Follicle cycles move slowly, and early conclusions are usually wrong.
- Keep a simple log of shedding intensity week to week. Patterns matter more than any single bad day in the shower.
- Stick to the same routine every day. Prescription hair loss treatments need regular use to maintain their effects, and stopping and restarting resets progress instead of protecting it.
- Bring your photos and notes to follow-up consults so your provider has real data instead of a general impression.
