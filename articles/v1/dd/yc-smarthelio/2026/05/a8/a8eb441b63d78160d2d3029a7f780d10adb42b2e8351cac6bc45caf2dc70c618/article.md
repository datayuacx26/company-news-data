### A real-world comparison of ChatGPT, Gemini, and GAIA Lens across solar use cases


If you work in solar operations, you’ve probably found yourself copy-pasting data


into an AI tool


at least once, hoping it would make sense of it faster than you could.


And honestly? Sometimes it works. General-purpose AI tools like ChatGPT and Gemini are impressive, they’re articulate, fast, and surprisingly capable across a wide range of topics. But here’s the question that keeps coming up in solar O&M teams:

, Gemini


, and GAIA Lens


with the exact same question. No coaching, no rephrasing. Just the screenshot and the ask.


The results were telling.

## Conclusion


AI is already becoming part of the day-to-day workflow


in solar operations. But this comparison highlights something important: Not all AI tools approach solar data in the same way.


Across fault prioritization and portfolio performance, general-purpose tools like **ChatGPT and Google Gemini were able to describe patterns, surface trends, and provide reasonable suggestions.**


That’s valuable especially when you need a quick overview.


But when it came to **understanding context and translating it into clear actions, GAIA Lens consistently went a step further.** It didn’t just explain what’s happening it helped answer what to do next, in a way that aligns with how solar teams actually operate.


In the third use case, Gemini stood out with a clear and focused diagnosis


, showing that general AI can still be effective especially when the problem is more contained and pattern-driven.


So the takeaway isn’t that one tool replaces the others.


It’s this:


- General AI helps you **understand the data**
- Domain-specific AI helps you **act on it**


And in solar operations where performance, uptime, and revenue are directly impacted that difference matters.
