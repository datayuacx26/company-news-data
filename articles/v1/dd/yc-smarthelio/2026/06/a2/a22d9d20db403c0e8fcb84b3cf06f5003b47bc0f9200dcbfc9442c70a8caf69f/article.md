# Real-World Insights About AI Agents in Solar Operations

June 19, 2026


In[AI](https://smarthelio.com/category/ai/) ,[Article](https://smarthelio.com/category/article/) ,[Webinar](https://smarthelio.com/category/webinar/)


## AI is no longer a future concept for solar.
The real question is not whether it works, but whether we can trust it to support real-world operational and investment decisions.

That was the central question behind SmartHelio’s recent webinar, ***Solar AI Agents: Buzzword or Breakthrough?*** , where experts from asset management, operations, engineering, and strategy discussed what AI is actually delivering today and where it still falls short.


Rather than making bold claims about fully autonomous solar plants, the panel focused on practical applications, real-world limitations, and the role AI can play


in supporting solar professionals.


## When AI Gives Three Different Answers


One of the most revealing moments of the webinar came when **SmartHelio Co-Founder** **Shankaransh Srivastava** shared a simple experiment. The team provided the same solar performance data to multiple leading AI models and asked them to analyze a curtailment event, estimate energy losses, and recommend a battery size.


- All models identified the issue.
- All models generated reasonable answers.
- And all models arrived at different conclusions.


Battery sizing recommendations **varied significantly.** Estimated energy losses differed. Even the duration of the **curtailment event** was interpreted differently.


### AI Model Comparison


The results highlighted how different AI models can interpret the same dataset in different ways.


*The comparison presented during the webinar showed significant variation between model recommendations, despite all three models analyzing the same solar asset data.*


For solar professionals, **finding a problem is only the first step.** Deciding what action to take and whether that action makes financial and operational sense still requires human judgement.


Different AI models can analyze the same event and arrive at different operational recommendations.

## Why Context Matters in Solar Operations


**Solar assets rarely operate under perfect conditions.** Solar assets do not operate in perfect conditions. Operators deal with incomplete data, grid constraints, changing site conditions, equipment limitations, and commercial realities that are often invisible to an AI model.


During the discussion, **Vincenzo Capogna** shared an example where a drop in solar production appeared to be curtailment. However, the actual cause was linked to diesel generator operation and site-specific constraints that were not visible in the available data.


> “We don’t want to be a company where people are simply passing AI information left to right.”
>
>
> — **Vincenzo Capogna, Managing Director, Westa Solar**


AI can accelerate investigations, prioritize issues, and suggest possible explanations. But understanding the full operational picture still requires engineering expertise.


## Where AI Is Already Creating Real Value


***AI is already delivering measurable value today.***


For solar operators, one of the **biggest challenges** is managing the growing volume of data generated across portfolios. Performance data, alarms, maintenance records, and fault reports can quickly overwhelm even experienced teams. **AI helps reduce that burden .**


**Shankaransh Srivastava** shared how **SmartHelio** uses AI agents to automate parts of the **fault validation process.** Tasks that previously required extensive manual review can now be completed automatically.


The outcome is not fewer experts. The outcome is that experts can spend more time solving complex problems and less time reviewing


repetitive information.


---


## The Biggest Mistake Companies Make With AI


One of the strongest warnings came from Jan Mastny, who compared AI adoption to handing Ferrari keys to someone who is still learning how to drive


> “AI in the hands of unskilled people can be dangerous.”
>
>
> — **Jan Mastny, Independent Consultant & Solar Expert**


His point was simple. The problem is not AI itself. The problem is using AI without understanding its limitations. Organizations often rush to implement AI before addressing the fundamentals: **data quality, operational processes, and clear objectives** .


Without reliable data, even the most advanced AI system will struggle to provide reliable answers. As several panelists noted, **AI cannot compensate for poor inputs.** It can only work with the information it receives.


## So, Buzzword or Breakthrough?


The answer is **somewhere in between.** AI is not magic. But it is not hype either. It is already helping solar teams process more data, identify issues faster, automate repetitive tasks, and improve operational efficiency. At the same time, the panel agreed that **human expertise remains essential.** Context, judgement, relationship building, and decision-making cannot simply be delegated to an algorithm.


### What Will Remain Human?


> “What will remain with humans is relationship building.”
> — **Jan Mastny, Independent Consultant & Solar Expert**


### What Should AI Be Able to Tell Us?


> “Tell me what you don’t know.”
> — **Robin Hirschl, Managing Director, PV-Invest**


Because the future of solar operations isn’t about replacing people with AI. It’s about combining the speed of AI with the experience, judgement, and expertise of the people who operate and manage solar assets every day.


As SmartHelio CEO **Govinda Upadhyay** concluded:


> “The winner won’t be AI alone. It will be humans and AI working together.”


And perhaps that is the real breakthrough.
