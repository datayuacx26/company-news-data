# The Next Era of Road Safety: How Modern Technology, Policy and Public Trust Will Define the Future Beyond Vision Zero

[Why Stopping on Red Matters as Micromobility Grows August 6, 2026](https://www.verramobility.com/why-stopping-on-red-matters-as-micromobility-grows/)


After years of rising roadway deaths, the U.S. is beginning to see signs of progress.


NHTSA estimates that traffic fatalities declined to 36,640 in 2025, down 6.7% from 2024, even as vehicle miles traveled increased. However, with more than 36,000 lives lost in a single year, one thing is clear: there is still significant work ahead.


Verra Mobility’s new white paper,[The Next Era of Road Safety: How Modern Technology, Policy and Public Trust Will Define the Future Beyond Vision Zero ,](https://www.verramobility.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/State-of-Road-Safety-White-Paper.pdf) examines the future of road safety from multiple angles, from state policy to autonomous vehicles, AI, and lessons from communities in the U.S. and abroad. It also highlights real-world examples of how transportation leaders are combining technology, policy, public education, and transparent program design to address specific roadway risks.


One of the most persistent challenges facing communities: **changing driver behavior.**


Speeding, distraction, red-light running, and illegal passing of stopped school buses remain difficult behaviors to address through education and infrastructure alone. For example, the report notes that speeding contributed to 28% of all traffic fatalities in 2022.


Public attitudes illustrate the complexity of the challenge. In Verra Mobility’s 2025 distracted-driving survey, 85% of respondents said distracted driving is as dangerous as, or more dangerous than, drinking and driving, while 63% said the average driver has gotten worse in recent years.


[The white paper](https://www.verramobility.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/State-of-Road-Safety-White-Paper.pdf) looks beyond today’s roadway safety challenges to examine the policies, technologies, and practical lessons that could shape what comes next.


Readers will explore:


- **What is working in U.S. communities.** Examples from Washington, D.C., San Francisco, Hawaii, Atlanta, Philadelphia, and Pittsburgh show how communities are applying different combinations of policy, technology, public education, and enforcement to address local safety challenges.
- **What the U.S. can learn from other countries.** International experience shows how sustained progress can come from layering speed management, safer infrastructure, consistent policy, public education, and other interventions over time.
- **How autonomous vehicles, connected vehicles, and AI could reshape road safety.** Emerging technology creates new opportunities to identify risks and help prevent crashes, as well as new questions about governance, data sharing, and human oversight.
- **Why driver behavior remains one of the hardest variables to change.** The report examines speeding, distraction, red-light running, and illegal school bus passing, along with approaches designed to encourage safer behavior.
- **How policy can enable safer, more trusted programs.** State laws determine which tools communities can use and how programs address civil penalties, privacy, human review, warning periods, and other safeguards.
- **Why data, privacy, and public trust matter.** Modern safety programs increasingly depend on data, but transparency, clear rules, equitable deployment, and accountable oversight are essential to earning community confidence.
