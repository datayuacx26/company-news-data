# How to Optimize Printing Speed in Screen Printing

Speed is one of the most misunderstood challenges in screen printing. Many shops assume that printing faster means buying a faster press, adding heads, or pushing operators harder. In reality, most delays happen


*before* the first shirt ever hits the platen and after the press stops.


Optimizing printing speed isn’t about racing the press. It’s about removing friction across the entire workflow, from artwork intake to final delivery. For many modern shops, that also means rethinking the whole tech stack.


## **Key Takeaways**


- Most screen printing delays happen in preparation, setup, and approvals


- Traditional optimizations help, but they plateau quickly with short runs and high SKU counts.


- Hybrid screen printing–DTG workflows remove the slowest, least profitable tasks from screen printing.


- Speed improvements directly increase capacity and margins


## **Where Screen Printing Really Loses Time**


[Screen printing](https://www.kornit.com/es/glossary/screen-printing-definition/) is extremely efficient once it’s running, but getting there is where time quietly disappears. These delays are often accepted as “part of the process,” even though they add hours (or, sometimes, even days) across a production week.


### **Screen Preparation, Color Work, and Setup Delays**


The largest time losses occur long before ink hits fabric. Screen coating, drying, exposure, and reclaiming are labor-intensive steps that scale poorly as order volume increases. Add color separation, test prints, and sampling, especially for complex or brand-sensitive artwork, and hours disappear.


This front-loaded effort is identical whether the job is 12 pieces or 1,200. This is why small runs quietly destroy throughput.


### **Press Idle Time and the Hidden Cost of Small Runs**


Even a perfectly tuned press spends significant time waiting.


- Waiting for approvals.


- Waiting for corrections.


- Waiting for screens to be remade.


- Waiting for the next job to be set up.


Small orders, reprints, and last-minute changes interrupt production flow and force presses to stop and start repeatedly. The result isn’t slower printing.


It’s less printing.


Most “speed issues” are workflow issues, not press speed issues.


## **Practical Ways to Speed Up Screen Printing Without Sacrificing Quality**


Most shops begin optimization by tightening existing processes. These improvements matter, and they often deliver immediate gains. However, they also have a clear ceiling.


The following changes reduce friction and increase consistency across jobs.


- **Standardizing artwork and separations:** Using consistent file formats and separation rules reduces revisions and prevents late-stage corrections.


- **Reducing color counts strategically:** Merging colors and optimizing underbases early lowers the number of screens needed.


- **Improving shop layout and job sequencing:** A logical floor layout and smarter job order reduce unnecessary movement.


- **Batching similar jobs together:** Grouping orders with shared garments or ink colors cuts repeated setups and increases productive press time per shift.


- **Simplifying or automating screen preparation:** Consistent or automated screen prep reduces variability and limits remakes.


These optimizations work well but struggle to keep up with short runs, high SKU counts, and on-demand production. At that point, efficiency gains flatten because the workflow itself becomes the constraint.


## **Why Hybrid Screen Printing: DTG Workflows Are Faster in Real-World Production**


Hybrid production changes how work moves through the shop instead of forcing every job through the same bottleneck.


### **Removing Setup-Heavy Jobs From the Screen Printing Line**


[DTG](https://www.kornit.com/glossary/dtg-direct-to-garment-definition/) removes the most time-consuming tasks from the screen printing workflow entirely. Short runs, samples, test prints, and reprints no longer require the full setup (screens, separations, press setup). Jobs that once stalled production for hours can now be completed in minutes.


This allows screen printing to focus exclusively on long, repeatable runs where its efficiency is unmatched.


### **Parallel Production Instead of Sequential Bottlenecks**


In a hybrid environment, work moves in parallel. While screen presses run high-volume jobs,


[DTG printers](https://www.kornit.com/direct-to-garment) handle low-volume and urgent orders simultaneously. Nothing waits in line.


The result is:


- Faster turnaround


- Fewer interruptions


- Lower waste


- Higher daily output


You don’t need to consider the


[differences between DTG and screen printing](https://www.kornit.com/magazine/dtg-printing-vs-screen-printing-kornit/) . You need to consider the advantages of the hybrid setup.


## **How a Hybrid Setup Saves Time and Money for Screen Printing Businesses**


Speed only matters if it improves profitability. Hybrid workflows do exactly that by aligning production methods with order economics.


- Reduced labor hours per order by eliminating unnecessary setup


- Lower consumables waste from fewer screens, inks, and chemicals


- More jobs completed per day without extending shifts


- Ability to accept rush and low-volume orders profitably


- Smoother scheduling with fewer production bottlenecks


This is the way to print smarter instead of just printin faster.


## **Speed Comes From Smarter Workflows, Not Faster Presses**


True optimization doesn’t come from pushing presses harder. It comes from removing friction, choosing the right production method per job, and letting each technology do what it does best.


Hybrid screen printing–DTG workflows unlock speed where it actually matters: before setup, between jobs, and during approvals. Screen printing businesses that adapt gain flexibility, protect margins, and scale without burning out their teams.
