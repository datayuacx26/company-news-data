# Screen Printing in 2026 and Beyond: A Look Into the Future

Screen printing has been around for decades, and it’s not going anywhere. However, the world continues to change quickly. In 2026, printing looks very different from what many screen printer businesses are used to.


New technology, changing customer expectations, and rising business pressures are reshaping how screen printers work and grow.


Understanding how the screen printing market is changing and following the biggest trends shaping 2026 can help printers remain successful in the volatile environment.


## **Key Takeaways**


- Screen printing is becoming more automated and technology-driven


- Orders are getting shorter, with more demand for customization and faster turnaround


- Sustainability and compliance are now core business requirements


- Hybrid production models are becoming more flexible and profitable


- DTG is increasingly used alongside screen printing to handle short runs and complex designs


## **How the Screen Printing Market Is Changing**


The global screen printing market is going through a clear shift. According to recent industry research*, the biggest change is not disappearing demand. It’s how that demand looks and the ways it needs to be served.


### **Automation Becoming the New Standard**


One of the biggest changes is the rapid expansion of automation. Automated exposure units, screen handling systems, and workflow software are reducing manual steps.


This shift helps print shops rely less on skilled labor for repetitive tasks while delivering more consistent results. Automation also reduces operational risk by making processes more predictable and easier to control.


### **Integrated Supplier Solutions Strengthening Value Chains**


Print shops are increasingly choosing fewer, more comprehensive suppliers. Instead of managing separate vendors for inks, pretreatments, equipment, and service, many businesses now prefer bundled solutions.


This approach simplifies troubleshooting, improves consistency across production, and strengthens the overall value chain. This is a must-have for times when workflows become more complex.


### **Customization Reshaping Production Workflows**


Customer demand is changing quickly. Brands and buyers now expect more customization, smaller order quantities, and faster delivery times.


Traditional screen printing workflows were built for long runs and stable designs. To keep up, manufacturers are investing in agile, reconfigurable machinery that supports frequent setup changes. Of course, it must do so without sacrificing efficiency or quality.


### **Ink Innovation and Hybrid Methods Influencing Equipment Decisions**


Advances in ink formulations


are affecting how print shops plan their production. New inks, especially those designed to meet environmental regulations like


[NeoPigment Inks](https://www.kornit.com/glossary/neopigment-ink/) , impact curing and handling.


At the same time, hybrid manufacturing methods are becoming more common. Compliance now spans multiple stages of production, from ink chemistry to waste management. This has a significant impact on both operational decisions and capital investments.


### **Industry-Specific Demands Driving Niche Applications**


Different markets are pulling screen printing in different directions. Apparel, technical textiles, and specialty substrates each come with unique performance requirements.


These demands are pushing manufacturers to invest in targeted, application-specific solutions. The one-size-fits-all equipment is becoming a thing of the past.


### **Shorter Orders Are Becoming the Norm**


One of the most important realities heading into 2026 is that orders are getting shorter. High-volume runs still exist, but they are no longer the default.


Flexibility is now just as valuable as speed. Print shops that can handle small batches and frequent changes have higher chances of succeeding in the evolving market


## **2026 Screen Printing Trends**


The trends shaping screen printing in 2026 are not about replacing the craft. They are mostly about adapting it to a new production reality.


### **Automation**


Automation is moving from “nice to have” to essential. Screen printers are using automation to reduce setup time, minimize human error, and keep quality consistent across shifts and locations. Automated workflows also help shops scale without constantly hiring and training new staff.


### **Customization**


Customization is no longer limited to premium orders. Customers expect personalized designs, limited editions, and fast changes. This forces print shops to rethink workflows that were designed around repetition rather than variation.


### **Sustainability**


Environmental responsibility is now tied directly to brand trust and regulatory compliance. Printers are adopting cleaner ink systems, reducing water usage, and improving waste handling.


[Sustainable textile printing](https://www.kornit.com/our-impact/) is becoming a competitive advantage.


### **Bolder and Brighter Colors**


Brands want prints that stand out on shelves and online. This is driving demand for stronger color performance, higher opacity, and more consistent color reproduction. Achieving this while staying compliant with environmental standards is a growing technical challenge.


### **Hybrid Solutions**


Hybrid production is one of the most important trends of 2026. Instead of choosing between screen printing and digital, leading shops are using both. They are selecting the right method based on order size, design complexity, and delivery speed.


## **Complementing Screen Printing with DTG**


[Direct-to-Garment (DTG)](https://www.kornit.com/glossary/dtg-direct-to-garment-definition/) printing


excels at short runs, detailed graphics, and fast turnaround times. In 2026, DTG is becoming a new element in the screen printers’ operations.


Screen printing remains the most efficient option for large runs with simple designs. DTG fills the gaps: small orders, last-minute changes, sampling, and highly detailed artwork. Together, they allow print shops to accept a wider range of jobs without sacrificing efficiency or quality.


Hybrid workflows also help reduce bottlenecks. Instead of forcing every job through the same process, printers can route orders intelligently based on what makes the most business sense.
