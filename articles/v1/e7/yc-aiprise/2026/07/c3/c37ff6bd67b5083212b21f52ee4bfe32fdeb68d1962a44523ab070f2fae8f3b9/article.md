## What Is KYC Automation?


KYC is a process that helps businesses verify that a potential customer is legitimate before engaging with them. Financial services companies and other regulated businesses are legally required to verify an individual’s identity before letting them send or receive money. Automating KYC involves using technologies such as artificial intelligence (AI), machine learning, and automated workflows to handle these verification tasks. You’re essentially upgrading from a paper-based assembly line to a digital one while still keeping humans in the process.


Automation handles the repeatable, high-volume work:


- Validating government-issued IDs
- Running watchlist and sanctions checks
- Scoring risk based on customer profiles and geography
- Flagging cases that need a closer look


This frees your compliance team to focus on the cases that genuinely need human judgment. For example, high-risk customers or complex ownership structures.

## FAQs


### What are the 5 stages of KYC?


The five core stages are: customer identification (CIP), customer due diligence (CDD), enhanced due diligence (EDD) for high-risk customers, ongoing monitoring, and periodic review or re-verification. Every stage builds on the previous one, so you can go from basic identity confirmation to deeper risk assessment.


### What should you look for in an automated KYC solution?


Look for global coverage with country-specific regulatory workflows, real-time sanctions and PEP screening, and continuous monitoring. Your platform should also have high security standards (SOC 2 Type 2 certification) with clear data retention policies.


### What is the AI in the KYC process?


AI in KYC refers to the use of artificial intelligence, machine learning, and automation technologies to handle KYC verification tasks. For instance, AI agents can automatically extract data from identity documents, match customer information against watchlists, score risk based on multiple signals, and flag cases that need human review. The goal is to reduce manual workload and improve consistency without removing human oversight from high-risk decisions.
