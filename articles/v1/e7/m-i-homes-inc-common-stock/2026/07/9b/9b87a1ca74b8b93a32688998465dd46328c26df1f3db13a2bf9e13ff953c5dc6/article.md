# What Is Private Mortgage Insurance and How Does It Work | Welcome to Better - M/I Homes

Posted by[Patrick Begg](https://www.mihomes.com/blog/patrick-begg) in[Home Buying](https://www.mihomes.com/blog/home-buying)


If you are exploring the steps to buying your first home, you have likely come across the acronym “PMI” while reviewing potential monthly payments. For many first-time buyers, seeing this extra line item can lead to immediate questions: *What is PMI insurance, and why is it required on a mortgage?*


Understanding every part of your monthly home payment is essential to feeling confident in your home purchase. To take the guesswork out of private mortgage insurance, our financing experts have broken down everything you need to know about what PMI is, how it works, and how it impacts your home buying budget.


## What Is PMI on a Mortgage?


**PMI stands for Private Mortgage Insurance.** It is a type of insurance policy required by conventional mortgage lenders when a buyer puts down less than 20% of the home's purchase price.


When asking *what is PMI in real estate* , it is helpful to understand its purpose: PMI protects the lender—not the buyer—in the event that a borrower defaults on their mortgage payments. Because a smaller down payment represents a higher financial risk for the lender, private mortgage insurance allows lenders to comfortably approve buyers who might not have saved a full 20% down payment yet.


The biggest misconception about a PMI mortgage is that it is a penalty. In reality, PMI is a helpful tool that allows millions of first-time homebuyers to purchase a home years sooner rather than waiting to build up a massive savings cushion.


## How Does PMI Work and How Much Does It Cost?


In most cases, PMI is paid as a monthly fee that is added directly into your total mortgage payment alongside your principal, interest, property taxes, and homeowners insurance.


So, how much is PMI? On average, private mortgage insurance typically costs between **0.5% and 1.5% of your total loan amount annually** . However, the exact cost of your PMI depends on a few key financial factors:


- **Your Credit Score:** Borrowers with higher credit scores generally receive lower PMI rates.
- **Your Down Payment Amount:** The closer you get to a 20% down payment, the lower your monthly PMI payment will be.
- **Your Loan-to-Value (LTV) Ratio:** The total size of your loan compared to the appraised value of the home.


For example, if you take out a $300,000 mortgage with a 1% annual PMI rate, your yearly PMI cost would be $3,000, which adds $250 per month to your mortgage payment until the requirement is satisfied.


## How to Avoid or Remove PMI Insurance


While PMI insurance is common for first-time buyers, it is not a permanent fixture of your home loan. As you make monthly mortgage payments and build equity in your home, you will eventually reach a point where PMI is no longer needed.


Here are the primary ways buyers remove or completely avoid paying private mortgage insurance:


- **Put Down 20% at Closing:** If you make an initial down payment of 20% or more on a conventional loan, PMI is not required at all.
- **Automatic Cancellation at 22% Equity:** Under federal law, your lender must automatically cancel PMI once your loan balance reaches 78% of the home's original appraised value (meaning you have built 22% equity through principal payments).
- **Request Cancellation at 20% Equity:** Once your loan balance drops to 80% of the original home value, you have the right to submit a written request to your loan servicer to cancel your PMI early.
- **Refinance or Leverage Home Appreciation:** If local property values rise significantly, the increasing market value of your home increases your overall home equity, allowing you to re-appraise your home or refinance to eliminate PMI.


## Choosing the Right Financing Path for Your Budget


Deciding whether to pay PMI or wait until you have a larger down payment depends entirely on your personal timeline and financial goals. For many buyers, paying a small monthly PMI fee is well worth the advantage of building equity in a new home today rather than waiting years to save a 20% down payment while home prices rise.
