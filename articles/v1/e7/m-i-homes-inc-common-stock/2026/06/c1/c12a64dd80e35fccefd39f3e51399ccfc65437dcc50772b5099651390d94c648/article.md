# HOA Laws in Texas

Posted by[Bruce Borden](https://www.mihomes.com/blog/bruce-borden) in[Home Building Industry](https://www.mihomes.com/blog/home-building-industry)


Understanding HOA laws in Texas is key if you live in or are buying in a managed community. Texas has thousands of[HOA-governed](https://www.mihomes.com/blog/about-community-hoas) communities, and recent updates have strengthened homeowner protections while adding new requirements for transparency and governance.


### How HOA Laws Work in Texas


HOA laws in Texas are primarily governed by[Chapter 209 of the Texas Property Code](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/PR/pdf/PR.209.pdf) , also known as the Texas Residential Property Owners Protection Act.


This law outlines how associations operate, including:


- Rule enforcement
- Assessments and collections
- Board governance and meetings
- Homeowner rights and protections


Like other states, Texas HOAs also follow their own governing documents, but state law overrides those rules if there’s a conflict.


### What Texas HOAs Can Do


Texas HOAs have the authority to:


- Collect dues and assessments
- Enforce community rules and restrictions
- Maintain shared spaces and amenities
- Place liens on homes for unpaid fees


These powers are balanced by legal requirements designed to protect homeowners.


### Key Homeowner Protections


Texas law gives homeowners clear rights within HOA communities:


- Access to records: Owners can request financial documents, contracts, and meeting records
- Open meetings: Most board meetings must be open to homeowners with advance notice
- Due process for fines: HOAs must provide notice and allow a hearing before enforcing penalties
- Foreclosure limits: HOAs cannot foreclose on a home solely for unpaid fines


## New HOA Laws in Texas


Recent legislation, especially from 2025 updates, introduced several important changes for condominium associations to align with single family and townhome associations.


**Increased Transparency** : Associations must keep records up to date and, in many cases, make governing documents more easily accessible to homeowners.


**Digital Access and Filing** : HOAs are now required to file management certificates with local counties and electronically, improving access to key community information.


**Expanded Homeowner Protections** : New laws strengthen rights related to:


- Installing solar features like panels or roof tiles
- Participating in community or political gatherings
- HOAs cannot fine residents for lawn conditions during active water restrictions


**Clearer Rules on Fines** : HOAs must follow defined procedures and cannot issue fines without proper notice and an opportunity to resolve violations.


**More Flexible Participation** : Recent updates allow for electronic voting and virtual meetings, making it easier for homeowners to participate in community decisions.


### Why It Matters for Buyers and Homeowners


These updates are designed to create a more balanced approach between HOA authority and homeowner rights. For buyers, that means more visibility into community rules and fees, stronger protection from unfair penalties, and easier access to important information about how the neighborhood is managed.


---


HOA laws in Texas continue to evolve, with a clear focus on transparency, fairness, and homeowner protections. As new HOA laws in Texas take effect, staying informed helps you better understand your rights and what to expect when living in an HOA community.
