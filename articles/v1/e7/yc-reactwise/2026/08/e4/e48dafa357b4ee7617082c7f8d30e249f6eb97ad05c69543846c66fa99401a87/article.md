# A process optimization campaign isn't run by one person - so why is most software built for one kind of user?

A process optimization campaign isn't run by one person - so why is most software built for one kind of user?


In any real project, the same campaign passes through several sets of hands. A bench chemist runs the reactions and reads the plate. A process scientist weighs scale-up, robustness, and operating windows. A data scientist digs into what drives results across projects. Each needs something different from the same underlying data.


When those people work in separate tools - a spreadsheet here, a modeling script there, an ELN entry somewhere else - the campaign fragments. Context is lost at every handoff, and the reasoning behind a decision made three weeks ago becomes anyone's guess.


ReactWise keeps everyone on one platform, working from a single shared campaign and integrating with ELNs - each using the parts that fit their role. The bench chemist gets recommendations in a few clicks and captures results in place. The process scientist can understand robustness and trade-offs through the visualization suite - response surfaces, Pareto fronts, parameter interactions. The data scientist digs into feature-level drivers through SHAP analysis and moves data in and out via the API, giving them a holistic view across the organization.


One campaign, one source of truth - different people drawing on different tools within it. The campaign holds its context as it moves across the team, so decisions stay explainable & traceable, and nothing is lost in translation.
