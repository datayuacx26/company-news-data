# The Hidden Question Beneath Workplace Stress

When people talk about workplace stress, they often focus on what is visible: long hours, heavy workloads, constant deadlines and increasing demands.


These pressures are real. Yet in therapeutic conversations, another theme often emerges beneath them.


It is not simply, "I have too much work."


It is:
"Why am I doing all of this?"


Many professionals find themselves functioning efficiently on the surface while feeling increasingly disconnected underneath. They continue meeting expectations, attending meetings and achieving goals, yet experience a growing sense that something is missing. What initially appears to be stress can sometimes be a deeper struggle with meaning, purpose and fulfilment — similar to[when everything looks fine but something still feels off](https://mindfi.co/blog/when-everything-looks-fine-but-something-still-feels-off) .


From a Cognitive Behavioural Therapy (CBT) perspective, workplace stress is influenced not only by circumstances but also by how we interpret them. Thoughts such as "I should be able to cope", "I can't let people down" or "My worth depends on my performance" can intensify pressure and make it difficult to step back when demands become overwhelming.


An existential perspective invites us to ask different questions.


What if stress is not only about workload?


What if it is also a signal that something important requires our attention?


Many of us spend a significant portion of our lives working. Naturally, questions about meaning, identity and purpose become intertwined with our professional roles. When work aligns with our values, we may experience challenge without losing ourselves. When it does not, even success can feel strangely empty.


This does not mean everyone needs to change careers or dramatically reinvent their lives. Often, it begins with reflection.


- What matters most to me?
- What am I prioritising?
- What am I sacrificing?
- Am I living according to my values, or simply responding to expectations?


These questions can feel uncomfortable because they move beyond productivity and into the realm of personal responsibility. Existential therapy suggests that while we cannot always choose our circumstances, we can choose how we relate to them. We can decide what gives our lives meaning and whether our daily actions reflect that meaning.


Perhaps this is why some forms of[workplace stress](https://mindfi.co/blog/when-stress-becomes-the-baseline) persist despite time off, wellbeing initiatives or improved time management. The issue is not always that we are doing too much. Sometimes it is that we have lost connection with why we are doing it.


The challenge, then, is not simply to reduce stress but to understand what it may be asking of us.


Because beneath many experiences of workplace stress lies a quieter question:


"Is the way I am living still meaningful to me?"


Does this resonate with you? If any of these feels familiar, you are not alone. It may be worth considering what kind of support could help during this phase.
