## A Quick Refresher: What Are DORA Metrics?


First, a quick level-set. The **DORA metrics** originated from the DevOps Research and Assessment (DORA) team, a long-term research program now part of Google. These four metrics are proven indicators of high-performing teams and are generally grouped into two categories: throughput (speed) and stability (reliability).


### Throughput Metrics (Speed)


These metrics help you understand the velocity of your development process.


-


**Deployment Frequency:** Simply put, how often does your team successfully release code to production? A higher frequency, as the research from DORA shows, means you're delivering value to users faster and creating tighter feedback loops[\[1\]](https://dora.dev/guides/dora-metrics) .


-


**Lead Time for Changes:** This is the time it takes for a committed piece of code to make it all the way to production. It’s a key measure of your entire development pipeline's efficiency.


### Stability Metrics (Reliability)


These metrics measure the quality of your code and the resilience of your systems.


-


**Change Failure Rate (CFR):** What percentage of your deployments result in a failure that requires a fix (like a hotfix or rollback)? As noted by sources like Datadog, a low CFR is a direct reflection of your team's quality practices[\[2\]](https://www.datadoghq.com/knowledge-center/dora-metrics) .


-


**Time to Restore Service (MTTR):** Also known as Mean Time to Recovery, this measures how long it takes your team to bounce back from a production failure. A low MTTR shows your team and systems are resilient.


These four metrics give you an excellent, high-level view of your delivery health. To get a full rundown, check out this complete[DORA metrics guide for 2026 teams](https://weaveos.com/blog/boost-delivery-speed-dora-metrics-guide-2026-teams) .


## The Problem: Why DORA Metrics Alone Aren't Enough


Let's be clear: DORA metrics are fantastic for measuring the health of your *delivery system* . The problem is, they have a major blind spot—they don't measure the *substance of the work* flowing through that system. They answer "how fast?" and "how stable?" but they can't answer crucial questions like "what did we actually build?" or "how complex was that feature?"


Think of it this way: DORA tells you how fast your factory's assembly line is moving. But it can't tell you if you're producing a simple toy car or a complex jet engine.


This lack of context is[the fundamental problem with using DORA metrics](https://weaveos.com/blog/problem-with-dora) as your only source of truth. A sudden spike in your Lead Time for Changes could signal a process bottleneck. Or, it could mean your team is tackling a highly strategic, incredibly complex feature that naturally takes more time. DORA alone can't tell you which it is. Acting on that number without context could lead you to pressure a team that's actually performing brilliantly on a difficult task.


Worse, when leaders rely only on DORA for performance evaluation, it can encourage teams to game the system by shipping tiny, trivial changes just to boost Deployment Frequency. This kills morale and shifts focus away from the impactful work that truly matters. It's time to move[beyond simple metrics like lines of code](https://weaveos.com/blog/beyond-lines-of-code) or PR counts.
