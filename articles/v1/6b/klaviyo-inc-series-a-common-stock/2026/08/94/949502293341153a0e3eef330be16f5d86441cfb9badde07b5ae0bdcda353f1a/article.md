Staying profitable during Black Friday Cyber Monday is a balancing act. This year,[economic uncertainty](https://www.klaviyo.com/blog/3-profit-protecting-tactics-during-economic-uncertainty) is keeping brands on their toes—and making it a good time to focus on resiliency.


While BFCM is one of the best times to boost sales and acquire new customers, the key to long-term success lies in not just increasing revenue, but also maintaining healthy profits throughout this high-stakes shopping season. Remember: BFCM is not a revenue contest. It’s a contribution-margin contest.


And contribution margin is not a number you can read off your dashboard the morning after Cyber Monday. Your November scoreboard is provisional. The real number lands in January, once returns clear, refunds leave your account, support tickets close, and the post-sale slowdown plays out. Plan for the settled number, not the spike.


Of course, BFCM success is easier said than done.


I’m the[founder](https://www.linkedin.com/in/klaviyo/) of[Polaris Growth](https://www.polarisgrowth.com/en) , where we’ve helped more than 80 ecommerce brands use Klaviyo to optimize their marketing efforts. And each year, I see opportunities for businesses to increase growth and profitability during this short but peak shopping season.


For every tactic in this guide, use this one-line check: does it raise contribution margin after cost of goods sold, shipping, payment fees, expected returns, and media? If not, don’t run it.


## 1. Offer profit-friendly incentives


Intense discounts can put a squeeze on your margins during BFCM. But it doesn’t have to be that way. Here are some ways to create value for customers without sacrificing a good chunk of your profits.


### 1. Bundle products


Before you decide how to discount, decide what to discount. Putting your best full-price sellers on sale just hands margin to customers who were going to buy anyway. That’s the pull-forward trap: you move a future full-price sale into a discounted window and call it growth. Save your deepest cuts for overstock and slow movers, and protect your heroes.


This approach involves combining multiple products into a single package deal, at a price that’s slightly lower than buying each item separately. Customers feel they’re getting more for their money—even if the discount per item is modest. By carefully selecting which[products to bundle](https://www.klaviyo.com/blog/product-bundle-promotion-strategies) , you protect your overall margins.


### 2. Implement tiered discounts


For instance, you can offer 10% off orders over $50, 15% off orders over $100, and 20% off orders over $150. This approach incentivizes customers to add more items to their cart to reach the next discount tier, boosting your overall[average order value](https://academy.klaviyo.com/en-us/courses/increase-average-order-value-with-owned-marketing) (AOV).


### 3. Offer free gifts with purchase


Instead of discounts, you can offer customers a sample-sized product, a branded accessory, or a limited-edition item. The perceived value of a free gift often outweighs its cost to you, making this a great way to attract customers without significantly impacting your margins.


“Tiered free gifts are one of the few BFCM incentives that let you create perceived value without putting your margins through the meat grinder,” says Ben Zettler, founder of[Zettler Digital](https://zettlerdigital.com/?srsltid=AfmBOooogELRYkUaOIoiqb8_uYJyrAHytEX9zkejQkTR4Hv0Luw-MQ1C) . “Choose gifts that are high impact for the customer, low cost for you, and aligned with what you want to move.”


“This is also a smart way to clear out slow-moving inventory, as long as each tier feels intentional,” Zettler adds. “Position the gifts as exclusive, not just leftovers. That way, your AOV climbs and your profit doesn’t get torched.”


> Tiered free gifts are one of the few BFCM incentives that let you create perceived value without putting your margins through the meat grinder.


Ben Zettler Founder, Zettler Digital

Consider running unique, limited early-access deals. If you bake in a higher margin, and the segment properly, the sale can feel like a win for the shopper without you eroding your margins.Black Friday profit pro tip: Send this deal to subscribers who have been on your list for 3+ months but haven’t purchased yet.

## 3. Take advantage of opportunities to up- and cross-sell

For example, if you’re a footwear brand and a customer is browsing for running shoes, you can display a section titled “Complete Your Running Gear”, featuring moisture-wicking socks, running shorts, and a water bottle. You can even position these as stocking stuffers to spark gift ideas for customers.

## 4. Lean on your highest-margin channel


The cheapest revenue you will earn all week comes through email and SMS, because there’s no media cost attached to it. Paid, meanwhile, is at its most expensive during BFCM, when every brand is bidding for the same impressions. That gap is why your owned channels do more for your margin in this one week than at any other time of year.


My default is to send more than feels comfortable: if you planned one email, send two or three. Under-sending the inbox you already own, while paying peak prices for paid, is the more expensive mistake. The catch is that volume is only safe if you scale your exclusions with it.


- Test and ramp before BFCM. Do not jump from one send a week to several a day overnight, or you will trip provider throttling and tank your reputation right when it matters.
- Cut the dead weight first. Re-engage or suppress chronically unengaged profiles and remove bouncing addresses before the rush, not during it.
- Watch deliverability by provider. Klaviyo's deliverability reporting shows which inbox providers are flagging you. If Gmail or Outlook is struggling, drop to engaged-only on that provider rather than slowing every send down.
- Give people an exit that is not the spam button. Send a pre-BFCM email, or add a preference link, that lets subscribers opt out of your Black Friday sends specifically. The ones who self-select out would otherwise have reported you as spam.


[Google](https://support.google.com/mail/answer/81126?hl=en#spam-rate) sets an explicit limit: keep spam complaints under 0.3%, and ideally under 0.1%.[Yahoo](https://senders.yahooinc.com/best-practices/) now surfaces effectively the same thresholds in its Sender Hub. Since late 2025, Google also rejects non-compliant bulk mail outright rather than quietly filtering it, so everything above is how you push volume hard without crossing that line.Everything above is how you push volume hard without crossing that line.


**Black Friday profit pro tip** : The brands that win the inbox during BFCM are not the ones who send the least to stay safe. They are the ones who send the most to a list they aggressively monitor and segment during this period.


## 5. Optimize for profit on ad spend


Make every ad dollar count during BFCM by optimizing for profit on ad spend (POAS) rather than just return on ad spend (ROAS).


While ROAS measures the gross revenue generated for every dollar spent on advertising, POAS takes into account your profit margins. So even though a campaign with a 4x ROAS might seem successful, your POAS may show otherwise.


This is especially important during BFCM when many businesses offer significant discounts. POAS also factors in costs like shipping, payment processing fees, and product costs, which can fluctuate during high-volume sales periods like BFCM.


To optimize your POAS, focus on advertising high-margin products. While the definition of a high margin will depend on the industry, in general I’d encourage brands to focus on the top 20% most profitable products that they’re selling.


A POAS nuance worth flagging: a customer you acquire on a deep discount won’t necessarily repeat the same way a full-price customer does. The research is mixed, but, in my experience, deep-discount cohorts often repeat at lower rates, and many holiday buyers are gift-givers or one-time seasonal shoppers who were never likely to return.


So, judge BFCM acquisition on expected lifetime value and second-purchase rate, not first-order ROAS. The brands that win BFCM are not the ones with the biggest November, they are the ones who turn one-time holiday buyers into a profitable second purchase in Q1.


You can also utilize CLV in your[CRM strategy.](https://www.klaviyo.com/platform/crm-strategy) Again, I like to use Klaviyo’s segmentation to create highly targeted audiences for BFCM ads, such as a[lookalike audience](https://www.klaviyo.com/features/social-advertising) based on the highest-CLV customers.

## 6. Turn expensive holiday traffic into owned contacts


During BFCM you’re paying peak prices to bring visitors to your site, and most of them won’t buy on the first visit. Anyone who leaves without giving you an email address or phone number is gone, and you will pay that same peak price to reach them again. So treat data capture as a margin lever, not a list-building nicety.


A well-targeted sign-up form, an exit-intent offer, or a short quiz turns an expensive one-time visitor into an owned contact you can sell to at almost no marginal cost for months. That is how a peak-CAC customer can become profitable: the full-margin purchase often shows up in Q1, not on Black Friday. Capture a preference or two, such as category or size, so your January follow-up is relevant rather than generic.


## 7. Avoid promoting high-return products


One easy way to minimize costly returns is to avoid heavy promotion of products with high return rates.


While the[National Retail Federation](https://nrf.com/research/2025-retail-returns-landscape) (NRF) found returns in 2025 were down by about 1% from 2024, return rates can vary significantly depending on the industry. Use[benchmark data](https://www.klaviyo.com/products/email-marketing/benchmarks) to gauge whether the rates for a certain product are considered high. Or, if you have access to your BFCM data from last year, use that to identify which of your products had the highest return rates.


The main problem you’re trying to prevent is promoting products that, on average, have the highest return rate in your assortment.


While many factors can impact returns, you want to minimize the chances of paying top dollar to get people to your shop—only to create even more work and costs for your team because you chose to market the wrong product.


Here are some ways to manage the problem of high returns around BFCM:


- Build a returns reserve into your profit and loss statement.
- Cap discounts on historically high-return categories.
- Put a lower ceiling on discounts for categories with above-average return rates.
- Promote bundles and add-ons for those categories to raise perceived value without deep cuts.


*When* returns hit matters as much as how many. The revenue you book in November starts coming back in late December and January, and extended holiday windows push some of it even later. That creates three problems: revenue gets clawed back weeks after you celebrated it, cash leaves your account after you have already paid for product, shipping, and fees, and support gets buried exactly when it is most thinly staffed.


Plan for all three: hold a returns reserve, staff or deflect the January support wave, and use your post-purchase flow to set sizing and usage expectations so fewer orders come back at all.Returns will always cost businesses money, but these tactics will better protect your profits and ensure a better customer experience.


## 8. Promote profitable payment and shipping methods


While most brands focus on the shopping experience, it’s equally important to pay attention to what comes after: payment and shipping.


Here are two ways to guide customers toward options that benefit your bottom line:


### 1. Offer payment methods with lower processing fees


Different payment methods come with varying transaction costs for your business. Find ways to nudge customers toward the lower-fee options.


For instance, you can offer a small discount for using certain payment methods. To figure out how much of a discount to offer, start by comparing payment methods and measuring the difference between those options. The goal, of course, is to promote the cheaper alternative for your business. Keep in mind that returns may have additional handling costs as well.

## 9. Prevent buyer’s remorse


Reduce returns and build customer satisfaction by implementing[post-purchase email flows](https://www.klaviyo.com/blog/post-purchase-emails) that reinforce the value of people’s BFCM purchases. A few ideas:


- Send a celebratory “Your order is on the way” email after the package ships to build excitement.
- Share usage or educational tips for the product someone purchased to help them get the most value out of it.
- Showcase[user-generated content](https://www.klaviyo.com/blog/user-generated-content-ugc) featuring the product a customer purchased for inspiration.


Customer Agent can also support this goal directly. When a customer reaches out post-purchase with a question (about sizing, usage, or returns) Agent resolves it immediately, with full context from their order history and profile, without your team having to step in. That kind of fast, informed response does more to prevent remorse than almost any follow-up email.


For brands running Customer Hub, the post-purchase experience extends to the website itself — giving every logged-in customer a personalized view of their orders, loyalty rewards, and product recommendations the moment they return to your site.


Also make sure you think about who you’re excluding from each campaign send. There’s nothing that frustrates a customer more than receiving an email about bigger discounts or price drops 24 hours after they placed their order at a higher price.


**Black Friday profit pro tip:** When a return is unavoidable, treat it as a second chance to sell, not just a loss to process. A refund reverses the revenue and sends cash out the door. An exchange or store credit keeps the revenue on your books and the cash in your business, and it keeps the customer. Platforms like[Loop Returns](https://marketplace.klaviyo.com/en-us/apps/01hvrhq1e9960ppzk6bpv8w5x1/) and[AfterShip](https://marketplace.klaviyo.com/en-us/apps/01hyw0fgrp7bk99n794gwbrjbg/) make instant exchanges and bonus credit the default path.


One caution: incentivize the exchange, but don’t trap people into it. NRF found that[57% of shoppers won’t buy from a retailer again](https://nrf.com/research/2025-retail-returns-landscape) after being charged for a return.


## 10. Give back to a good cause


Consider donating a portion of your BFCM profits to a charity that aligns with your brand values.


If you sell outdoor gear, for instance, you might partner with an environmental conservation organization.


You can then create a special BFCM collection where a higher percentage of sales goes to charity, or offer customers the option to round up their purchase to the nearest dollar for donation.


This approach not only contributes to a good cause, but can also account for premium pricing and attract customers who want their purchases to make a positive impact. Just ask[London Sock Co.](https://londonsockcompany.com/) When the brand donated two pairs of socks to a homeless charity for every purchase during BFCM, the strategy contributed to a[180% increase in AOV](https://www.klaviyo.com/customers/case-studies/london-sock-co) during the sale period.

## 12. Lower your winback and acquisition costs with direct mail


Direct mail sits outside the ad auction, so its cost stays fixed while paid prices spike through BFCM. In certain cases that makes it the cheaper way to reach a customer, since a postcard can cost less than serving someone ads at peak-season prices, especially people who are expensive to reach with paid but cheap to reach by mail.


It is also the only owned channel that still reaches customers who have stopped opening your email or unsubscribed, the ones your biggest send of the year never lands with.


Two ways I use it during BFCM:


- **Inbox backup:** mail a postcard to subscribers who did not open your sale email.
- **Retargeting layer:** trigger postcards for cart and checkout abandoners. On Klaviyo, you can fire these from inside your existing flows.


Two things to plan for:


- **Lead time:** you commit print and postage weeks ahead, so this is an October decision, not a Black Friday one.
- **Measurement:** judge it on incremental ROAS against a holdout. That is the only number that proves the channel paid for itself.


[PostPilot](http://link.polarisgrowth.com/postpilot) , Klaviyo's premier direct mail partner, reports postcards starting around $0.55 including postage and median incremental ROAS over BFCM of roughly 3.5x to 5.8x depending on size, strongest on winback and retention rather than cold prospecting.
