# 7 Must-Visit Cities for Fans of Ancient Greece

Greece is a destination rich in history. But it’s also a place steeped in captivating legend and lore. The region’s fantastical mythological tales provide adventure seekers with a unique opportunity to go beyond the pages of literary masterpieces and step into a real-life visual wonderland filled with hilltop temples, ancient theaters that were once home to epic spectacles and Instagram-worthy landscapes set against a backdrop of impossibly blue waters.


For travelers who are captivated by gods, heroes and monsters, Greece offers an unforgettable journey through time. From the birthplace of democracy in Athens to the legendary palaces of Crete and the sacred grounds of Olympia, every city in this storied country opens another chapter of a tale that’s inspired the imagination for centuries.


### 1. Athens is Where Myth and Modern Life Meet


Athens, Greece - Photo Credit: rawf8/Shutterstock


Athens is a “must” for any Greece itinerary. It’s here that many myths were born, and this capital city is the perfect launching point for a deep dive into fables of ancient monsters and gods.


Start at the Acropolis and take in views of the Parthenon, the magnificent marble temple built in the 5th century BC in tribute to Athena, goddess of wisdom. Continue through the Ancient Agora, where Socrates and Plato once debated ideas that shaped Western philosophy and democracy, before visiting the towering Temple of Olympian Zeus.


History is found waiting in every corner of the city. Meander the charming Plaka neighborhood, where narrow streets and neoclassical homes sit alongside ancient ruins, before settling into a stay at the nearby[Anise Aluma Athens, Tapestry Collection by Hilton](https://www.hilton.com/en/hotels/athaaup-anise-aluma-athens/) . Nestled in the middle of it all, this serene haven is just steps from the historic city center. Or, get a front-row seat to the city’s cultural and social energy from the newly opened[Conrad Athens The Ilisian](https://www.hilton.com/en/hotels/athgrci-conrad-athens-the-ilisian/) , where resort-style living awaits. Here, guests can discover wellness, gastronomy and curated experiences in one dynamic destination.

### 2. Corinth is the Gateway to Ancient Trade and Engineering


Ancient Corinth - Photo Credit: zedspider/Shutterstock


Just west of Athens sits Corinth, once one of the wealthiest and most influential city-states in Greece. Strategically positioned on the narrow isthmus connecting mainland Greece to the Peloponnese, Corinth’s enviable location made it a thriving center of trade for merchants and sailors from across the Mediterranean.


Today, visitors can wander through the Archaeological Site of Ancient Corinth, exploring crumbling columns, market squares and the Temple of Apollo, one of the oldest Doric temples in Greece. Nearby, the Acrocorinth fortress rewards climbers with panoramic sea views.


After a day immersed in history, unwind at[Isla Brown Corinthia Resort & Spa, Curio Collection by Hilton](https://www.hilton.com/en/hotels/athibqq-isla-brown-corinthia-resort-and-spa/) , a stylish seaside retreat where contemporary design, sweeping views of the Saronic Gulf and restorative wellness experiences provide the perfect complement to a journey through one of Greece’s most storied regions.

### 3. Olympia is the Birthplace of the Olympic Games


Temple of Olympian Zeus - Photo Credit: Lucky-photographer/Shutterstock


Olympia offers one of Greece’s most inspiring settings for adventure. Tucked into a lush valley in the western Peloponnese, this UNESCO World Heritage Site is where athletes from across the ancient Greek world gathered every four years to compete in honor of Zeus.


Walking through Ancient Olympia is a powerful experience. Visitors can step onto the original stadium track and pass beneath the stone archway used by athletes more than two millennia ago. The arena is remarkably preserved, making it easy to picture the history that took place here. Nearby, the ruins of the Temple of Zeus offer a glimpse into what was once considered one of the Seven Wonders of the Ancient World.


To further bring history to life, be sure to check out the Archaeological Museum of Olympia, which is home to extraordinary sculptures and local artifacts.


### 4. The Peloponnese Gave Rise to Warriors and Kings


Mycenae, Greece - Photo Credit: Olya Solod/Shutterstock


Beyond Corinth and Olympia, the Peloponnese is bursting with striking cities full of wonders to behold. Travel across a landscape of rugged mountains and olive groves to find some of the most important archaeological sites in Europe.


Head for Sparta, famed for discipline and military excellence, to see the ruins of the Sanctuary of Artemis Orthia. Then travel northeast to Mycenae, one of the great centers of Bronze Age civilization and a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Enter through the iconic Lion Gate, where massive stone blocks said to be built by giants mark the entrance to a fortified citadel that once ruled much of the ancient Aegean world.


Next, seek out the coastal town of Nafplio, the first capital of modern Greece. Explore its Venetian architecture, cobblestone alleys and vibrant waterfront promenade for a true taste of seaside serenity. And soon this region will be home to another waterfront beauty, as[Waldorf Astoria Scarlet Bay](https://stories.hilton.com/emea/releases/waldorf-astoria-greece-peloponnese-scarlet-bay) will bring Waldorf Astoria’s legendary service to one of Greece’s most picturesque corners in 2029.


### 5. Crete is the Land of the Fabled Minotaur


Palace of Knossos - Photo Credit: Neirfy/Shutterstock


Crete, Greece’s largest island, is overflowing with mythology. According to legend, this is the realm of King Minos, who ruled from a vast palace and hid a fearsome Minotaur within an elaborate labyrinth.


At the heart of Crete’s age-old legacy is the Palace of Knossos, the ceremonial and political center of the Minoan civilization. Dating back nearly 4,000 years, Knossos is considered Europe’s oldest major city and offers a fascinating glimpse into a culture known for advanced architecture, colorful frescoes and maritime trade.


But beyond antiquity, Crete is equally enticing to those seeking stunning mountain landscapes, secluded beaches and charismatic towns filled with Greek, Venetian and Ottoman influences. Head for Chania, which enchants travelers with its atmospheric Old Town and dazzling harbor, and settle into[Hilton Chania Old Town Resort & Spa](https://www.hilton.com/en/hotels/chqiahi-hilton-chania/) . Now open, this sophisticated retreat is nestled in the heart of this historic city.


For a tranquil coastal escape, check into[The Royal Senses Resort & Spa Crete, Curio Collection by Hilton](https://www.hilton.com/en/hotels/herrsqq-the-royal-senses-resort-and-spa-crete/) , where sweeping sea views, private pools and world-class wellness experiences showcase the island’s more indulgent side.

### 6. Mykonos is a Sacred Neighbor of Delos


Delos, Greece - Photo Credit: trabantos/Shutterstock


Mykonos might be best known for its glamorous beach clubs and postcard-perfect villages, but it also serves as the gateway to Delos, one of the Mediterranean’s most important archaeological sites and a UNESCO World Heritage Site steeped in mythology.


According to ancient tradition, Delos was the birthplace of Apollo and Artemis, transforming the tiny island into one of the holiest sanctuaries in the Greek world. Today, visitors can wander among remarkably preserved temples, marketplaces, mosaic-adorned homes and the iconic Terrace of the Lions.


Mykonos provides the perfect base for exploring this extraordinary site while also enjoying access to local sights like Mykonos Town, where chic boutiques, waterfront cafés and the island’s famous windmills offer a captivating allure. Amid it all is the stylish[Semeli Coast Mykonos, Curio Collection by Hilton](https://www.hilton.com/en/hotels/jmkmbqq-semeli-coast-mykonos/) , which offers an elegant sanctuary with easy access to both Delos and Mykonos’ celebrated hotspots. Boasting sweeping sea views and a tranquil atmosphere, it’s an ideal home base for history seekers.

### 7. Santorini is Home to Ancient Thira Above the Sea


Ancient Thira - Photo Credit: Konstantin Mizikevitch/Shutterstock


Santorini is renowned worldwide for its white and blue villages, but beyond the caldera’s dramatic beauty lies a fascinating chance to explore Greece’s past. Ancient Thira, one of the island’s most compelling historic sites, was founded in the 9th century BCE atop Mesa Vouno mountain, and this elevated settlement was inhabited by successive Greek, Roman and Byzantine communities.


But Santorini offers far more than these ruins. The island itself was shaped by one of the most significant volcanic eruptions in human history, inspiring legends of lost civilizations. At Akrotiri, a remarkably preserved Bronze Age settlement buried beneath volcanic ash offers an extraordinary glimpse into the sophistication of the ancient Aegean world, with multi-story buildings, frescoes and advanced urban planning.


After days spent exploring archaeological wonders, Santorini rewards visitors with unforgettable settings like the cliffside lanes of Oia and Fira, volcanic beaches and idyllic wineries. Savor it all from the comfort of[Sandblu Santorini, LXR Hotels & Resorts](https://www.hilton.com/en/hotels/jtrblol-sandblu-santorini/) , which offers elevated luxury near Kamari at the foothills of Ancient Thira. With refined Cycladic design, exceptional dining and panoramic views of the Aegean, the resort allows guests to immerse themselves in both the island’s ancient heritage and its timeless beauty.
