## New product updates


### IMAT added to body-composition analysis


Nucleo’s body-composition platform now includes IMAT, or intermuscular adipose tissue, as a separate segmentation output. Until now, the core body-composition workflow focused on skeletal muscle, subcutaneous adipose tissue, and visceral adipose tissue. With IMAT added as an additional tissue class, users can now examine fat located within and between muscle groups more directly.


This update gives researchers and clinical teams a more detailed view of muscle quality, not just muscle quantity. IMAT can be especially useful when studying cachexia, sarcopenia, metabolic health, and longitudinal changes in oncology patients, where changes in muscle composition may matter alongside changes in skeletal muscle area.


In the viewer, IMAT appears as its own mask alongside the existing body-composition masks (skeletal muscle, visceral fat, subcutaneous fat), so users can inspect the segmentation visually and compare it with the underlying CT image


### Organ segmentation added for research workflows


We have also added organ segmentation to the Nucleo platform for research use. The new module supports automatic segmentation of a curated set of abdominal and thoracic organs, including the liver, spleen, kidneys, pancreas, gallbladder, stomach, aorta, adrenal glands, duodenum, esophagus, and lungs.


The organ segmentation workflow is designed to make broader CT analysis easier to explore. After running organ segmentation, users can view organ masks in the platform, toggle individual organs on and off, and inspect organ outlines while working in other modules. This helps provide anatomical context when reviewing body composition, lesions, or other image-derived findings.


The module also computes organ volumes from voxel dimensions, giving users a quantitative starting point for organ-level analysis. Over time, this opens the door to richer workflows that combine tissue composition, organ-level measurements, and longitudinal imaging changes in one environment.
