# NERC Power Tariff Review: Impact on Nigerian Consumers

NERC regulates electricity pricing in Nigeria under what is known as the **Multi-Year Tariff Order (MYTO)** framework.


This framework allows periodic reviews based on changes in key factors such as:


- Exchange rate fluctuations
- Inflation rates
- Gas prices
- Generation and distribution costs
- Service levels provided by DisCos


When any of these variables change significantly, tariffs may be adjusted to reflect current economic realities.


In simple terms, tariff reviews are meant to ensure:


- Electricity providers can cover operational costs
- The power sector remains sustainable
- Customers receive improved service over time


However, implementation and service delivery have often remained a major concern for Nigerians.


## **Is There a Nationwide Tariff Hike?**


Not necessarily.


Tariff adjustments in Nigeria are often **Band-based** , meaning:


- Customers in higher service bands (e.g., Band A – typically 20+ hours of supply daily) may experience different pricing from those in lower bands.
- Customers who do not receive improved service are not expected to pay for higher service levels.


This is a key point many people miss: tariffs are increasingly tied to **service hours delivered** .


## **Why Nigerians Are Concerned**


Many Nigerians are asking:


- Will electricity supply improve?
- Why increase costs when supply is inconsistent?
- How does this affect small businesses already struggling with fuel and inflation?


With rising costs of living, any increase, especially on essential utilities, feels heavy. For small business owners, traders, and artisans, electricity is not a luxury; it’s a survival tool.


The real issue isn’t just pricing. It’s **value for money** .

If tariffs increase in your band, here are practical steps you can take:
