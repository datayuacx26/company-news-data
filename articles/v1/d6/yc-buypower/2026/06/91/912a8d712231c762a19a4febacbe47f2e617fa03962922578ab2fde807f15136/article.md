# FG Seeks Private Investment to Power Hospitals: Why Reliable Electricity Matters for Everyone

Reliable electricity is more than convenience; it’s a necessity.


Whether it’s powering homes, running businesses, or saving lives in hospitals, access to stable electricity affects everything.


This week, the Federal Government announced plans to attract private sector investment to improve the electricity supply across hospitals nationwide. The move comes under the **Nigeria Power for Health Initiative (NPHI)** , an effort designed to provide cleaner, more reliable energy to public health facilities through an **Energy-as-a-Service (EaaS)** model.


It’s a major step.


But beyond the headlines, this raises an important question:


**If hospitals, some of the country’s most critical institutions, are struggling with power reliability, what does that mean for everyday Nigerians?**


Let’s break it down.


## What the Government Announced


The Federal Government, through the Federal Ministry of Health and Social Welfare, is seeking private investment to improve electricity access in hospitals across Nigeria.


The goal is simple:


To ensure hospitals have a stable, uninterrupted power supply for critical operations.


This initiative follows concerns over rising electricity costs, inconsistent supply, and the growing pressure on public healthcare facilities.


Under the new model, private investors will help finance and manage energy infrastructure for hospitals, reducing dependence on unreliable grid power.


This could include:


- Solar-powered systems
- Backup power solutions
- Hybrid energy systems
- Improved electricity management systems


The long-term aim is to make hospitals more energy-efficient, reduce costs, and improve healthcare delivery.


It’s a strong reminder that power reliability is now a national priority.


## Why Hospitals Need Stable Electricity


In hospitals, electricity is directly tied to life-saving services.


Without stable power:


### Surgeries can be interrupted


Operating rooms rely heavily on uninterrupted electricity for lighting and medical equipment.


### Vaccine storage can be compromised


Vaccines and medications often require cold-chain storage. A power outage can destroy valuable medical supplies.


### Oxygen systems can fail


Critical care units depend on electricity-powered oxygen concentrators and ventilators.


### Laboratory services can be disrupted


Testing equipment needs consistent power to function properly.


## The Bigger Electricity Problem in Nigeria


The hospital power crisis is part of a larger electricity challenge affecting millions of Nigerians.


Homes and businesses face similar problems every day:


- Unpredictable outages
- Voltage fluctuations
- Rising electricity tariffs
- Fast-draining prepaid units
- Damage to appliances from surges


For small businesses, unstable power often means:


- Lost sales
- Higher generator costs
- Reduced productivity


For households, it means:


- Food spoilage
- Disrupted work-from-home routines
- Poor entertainment access
- Increased expenses


The truth is, electricity reliability affects everyone.
