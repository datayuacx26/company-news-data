# Treat trauma more efficiently

## **What does an average trauma treatment look like?**


Trauma therapy is a specialized approach aimed at minimizing the physical, emotional, and psychological effects of trauma. Depending on the specific trauma, the severity of symptoms, and the individual needs of the patient, treatment may vary.


**EMDR:**
A widely used therapeutic approach is Eye Movement Desensitization and Reprocessing (EMDR). EMDR involves identifying and processing traumatic memories to help reduce the distressing emotional charge associated with them. During an EMDR session, the patient is asked to focus on a traumatic memory while moving their eyes back and forth, often in response to the therapist’s finger movements or other stimuli. This process can help change how the trauma is stored in the brain, thereby reducing negative reactions to the memory.


**CBT:**
In addition, Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT) is often used to treat trauma. CBT helps patients identify and change negative thought patterns, enabling them to better cope with the consequences of trauma. Trauma-Focused CBT (TF-CBT) is a specific form of CBT that addresses the unique needs of individuals who have experienced traumatic events.


**Exposure Therapy:**
Exposure therapy is also a popular treatment method for trauma. It involves gradually and repeatedly exposing the patient to thoughts, feelings, and situations that remind them of the trauma, helping to reduce fear and increase control over emotional responses.


**Ways to treat better in less time**


Several strategies can help make trauma treatment more efficient:


-


**More intensive therapy sessions:** Holding sessions more frequently and for longer durations can shorten the overall treatment period and provide faster relief for clients.


-


**Group therapy:** This is not only a cost-effective method but also allows clients to learn from others’ experiences, which can accelerate the healing process.


-


**Digital tools and e-health:** These may include online counseling, mental health mobile apps, and self-help guides that support self-care and reduce the need for face-to-face therapy.


**Treating trauma with the help of VR**


Virtual reality (VR) is increasingly being used as a valuable tool in the treatment of trauma. Particularly in exposure therapy, VR can provide a safe and controlled environment in which patients can be exposed to traumatic memories or situations under the guidance of a therapist. Through this “virtual exposure,” patients can process their trauma responses and learn to manage them more effectively. Moreover, VR allows the conditions of exposure to be precisely tailored to the patient’s needs, making treatment more efficient.
