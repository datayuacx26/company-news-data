# Treat depression more efficiently

**What does an average depression treatment look like?**


Depression is a complex condition that often requires a multifaceted treatment approach. The most common forms of treatment are psychotherapy (also known as talk therapy) and medication.


In psychotherapy, such as cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT), patients learn to recognize and change negative thought patterns and to cope with stressful situations. Another form of therapy that has proven effective in the treatment of depression is interpersonal therapy (IPT), which focuses on improving personal relationships and social functioning.


Antidepressants may also be prescribed to help restore the chemical balance in the brain and reduce symptoms of depression. For some people, a combination of psychotherapy and medication is the most effective approach.


**Ways to treat better in less time**


To make the treatment of depression more efficient, several strategies can be used:


-


**Short-term intensive therapy:** More intensive sessions can reduce the overall treatment duration and provide faster symptom relief.


-


**E-health solutions and digital tools:** The use of online tools can save both therapists and patients time and energy by eliminating the need for travel and enabling more flexible scheduling. In addition, digital tools such as mental health apps can offer support outside therapy sessions. Virtual Reality can also be considered a highly effective tool in this context.


**Treating depression with the help of VR**


Virtual reality (VR) can be a valuable tool in the treatment of depression. With VR, therapists can create a **safe** and **controlled** environment in which patients can learn to cope with stressful situations, explore and challenge negative thoughts and emotions, and practice **self-care strategies** . In addition, VR environments can be used to teach mindfulness and relaxation techniques, which can be beneficial in treating depression. The advantage of VR is that it allows patients to practice learned skills and techniques in a safe environment that still feels realistic, making it easier to transfer these skills to real-life situations.
