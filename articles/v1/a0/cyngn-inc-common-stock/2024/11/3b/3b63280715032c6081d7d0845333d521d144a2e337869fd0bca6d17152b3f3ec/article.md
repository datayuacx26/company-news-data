# Artificial Intelligence (AI) and Robotics

For over six decades, robots have been deployed across the industrial landscape, yet only recently have advancements in artificial intelligence begun to drive their hardware capabilities to new heights.


These AI processes are supercharging the ability of industrial robots to make decisions, augment traditionally-human workflows, and ultimately help companies stay competitive.


AI brings a new level of precision to industrial processes, with robots handling tasks faster and more accurately than ever before. In fact, a[recent study](https://praxie.com/ai-for-error-proofing-in-manufacturing/) found that AI-enabled robots could reduce errors by 50% or more.


This shift not only accelerates production timelines but also drives down costs, making AI and robotics a true transformation for modern industries.


According to[market projections](https://market.us/report/ai-in-robotics-market/) , the global AI in robotics market is expected to grow from $12.3 billion in 2023 to $146.8 billion by 2033. Leaders are embracing these technologies as essential tools for creating smarter operations that can quickly respond and adapt to shifting demands.


Let’s explore the evolving role of AI in robotics and its impact across multiple sectors. Along the way, we’ll highlight key advancements, use cases, and the future potential of these intelligent machines.

## Role of Artificial Intelligence (AI) in Robotics


##


Before delving into how artificial intelligence, machine learning, and deep learning fuel robotics innovations, it’s important to first recognize that these are interconnected yet distinct approaches to creating intelligent systems.


AI encompasses the broad field of developing intelligent machines, while machine learning is a subset focused on enabling systems to learn and improve from data. Deep learning, a more specialized subset, uses neural networks to solve highly complex problems.


### **1. Artificial Intelligence (AI)**


Since their invention in the 1960s, robots have always been able to act, but increasingly, they are being given the ability to think, reason, and solve problems. The shift is significant, as it enables robots to complete vastly more complex tasks, like navigating dynamic environments or performing intricate quality control inspections, without having to rely on a human to define their every action.


The basic building blocks of a sophisticated, AI-powered robot are threefold: robots need the ability to perceive their environment, process information, and make autonomous decisions. Artificial intelligence supports all of these processes.


AI further powers the decision-making capabilities of robots, enabling them to perform complex tasks autonomously. AI systems analyze data from sensors, cameras, and other input sources, helping robots interact more effectively with their surroundings.


For example, Zenni Optical, an online eyewear retailer,[used](https://aimresearch.co/market-industry/were-not-just-automating-warehouses-were-driving-the-future-of-smart-robotics-inside-osaros-global-ai-revolution) AI-driven robotics from OSARO to enhance order fulfillment. By integrating OSARO's robotic picking system with AI-enhanced computer vision and machine learning, Zenni Optical dramatically improved its operations.


This integration resulted in a 99.9% order accuracy rate, reducing order mix-ups from 20 to 2.5 per 100,000. Additionally, they increased hourly order processing by 50% and cut fulfillment steps from five to two. Pretty impressive, right?


### **2. Machine Learning (ML)**


Machine Learning (ML), a subset of AI, focuses on developing algorithms that enable robots to learn from and make predictions or decisions based on historical data. By identifying patterns and trends in large datasets, ML algorithms are used to fine-tune robotic operations and improve their accuracy over time, enhancing performance in tasks like assembly, picking, and inspection.


Researchers are using machine learning techniques, like Artificial Neural Networks (ANNs) and Support Vector Regression (SVR), to improve the precision of industrial robot movements.[One study](https://www.sae.org/publications/technical-papers/content/2023-01-1000/) showed that ANNs reduced mean positioning errors by 46-96%, while SVR reduced errors by 42-98%.


This innovation helps robots meet the tight tolerances needed in aerospace and other precision manufacturing, making them viable for high-accuracy tasks.


### **3. Deep Learning (DL)**


Deep Learning (DL) takes machine learning (ML) a step further by using artificial neural networks to model and understand complex patterns in large, unstructured datasets. These networks mimic how human and animal brains adapt to dynamic inputs. With the ability to process vast amounts of information, robots can recognize patterns, make predictions, and even comprehend human commands.


IBM, for example,[uses](https://www.autodesk.com/design-make/articles/machine-learning-robotics) deep learning in IIoT devices to spot defects more accurately and efficiently than human inspectors. This automated visual inspection catches flaws that are often missed by humans.


This is a prime example of deep learning because it involves neural networks analyzing vast streams of visual data in real-time. These neural networks allow the system to continually learn from the data, refining its ability to detect defects with each new image processed.


## How AI Improves Industrial Robots


### **Perceiving Environments**


AI enables robots to perceive and interpret their surroundings with greater accuracy, leading to smarter decision-making. From detecting obstacles to identifying objects, robots equipped with AI can respond in real-time. This improved perception helps robots navigate complex spaces and interact safely with human workers and other machines.


For instance, the DeeperSense project, led by the German Research Center for Artificial Intelligence (DFKI), is using AI[to enhance](https://jpt.spe.org/using-ai-to-optimize-the-environmental-perception-of-robotic-underwater-vehicles) how robotic underwater vehicles perceive their surroundings. By combining visual and acoustic sensors through deep learning, the project aims to help robots operate better in tough conditions like murky waters, tight ship tanks, and dark caves.


DeeperSense will test its approach in three scenarios: monitoring divers in cloudy waters, exploring coral reefs, and mapping the seabed. This sensor fusion has the potential to greatly improve the autonomous robots’ ability to navigate complex underwater environments, paving the way for new maritime applications.


### **Autonomous Robots**


#### AMRs


[Autonomous Mobile Robots](https://www.cyngn.com/vehicles) (AMRs) mark a significant evolution from Automated Guided Vehicles (AGVs), fueled by advancements in artificial intelligence.


While AGVs rely on fixed paths and predefined routes, AMRs use AI algorithms for real-time decision-making, enabling them to navigate dynamically, avoid obstacles, and adapt to changing environments in real time.


This intelligence allows AMRs to operate more flexibly in complex settings, making them ideal for modern warehouses, manufacturing, and logistics where agility and efficiency are crucial.


By 2025, over 50% of autonomous mobile robots[are expected](https://scoop.market.us/autonomous-mobile-robots-statistics/) to integrate advanced AI technologies to improve their capabilities.


#### AGVs


AGVs leverage AI mainly to optimize their operations within the fixed routes that they follow to transport materials within a facility. AI helps them with tasks like efficient route scheduling, load balancing, and fleet coordination, improving throughput in controlled environments like production lines or warehouses with well-defined paths.


Unlike AMRs, which use AI for real-time navigation and obstacle avoidance,[AGVs](https://www.cyngn.com/blog/fundamentals-of-automated-guided-vehicles) depend on external markers or wires to move and do not adapt to changes in the environment. This makes AGVs more suited for repetitive tasks in predictable settings, where consistent performance is prioritized over flexibility.


We can, however, see the expansive use of AI in their FMS systems.


#### Cobots


AI gives cobots real-time responsiveness and task adaptation. Unlike AGVs and AMRs, cobots use AI to refine precision tasks like assembly and welding by adjusting force and positioning based on sensor feedback.


AI also helps cobots recognize human gestures and adapt to unpredictable workflows, making them more suitable for tasks that require direct human interaction and fine motor skills.


Universal Robots[produces](https://blog.hirebotics.com/universal-robots) collaborative robots, or cobots, designed to safely work alongside human employees in shared workspaces. Their UR3e and UR5e models can perform tasks such as assembly and material handling, using advanced sensors to detect nearby humans and adjust their movements accordingly. Machine learning algorithms enable these robots to learn from human actions over time, continuously improving their performance and adapting to new tasks.


By taking on repetitive or physically demanding tasks, these cobots also enable human workers to focus on more complex aspects of production.


### **Predictive Maintenance**


Predictive maintenance heavily relies on AI to identify potential equipment issues before they escalate, reducing downtime and costs.


IBM’s Watson IoT platform[offers](https://aimagazine.com/top10/the-top-10-predictive-maintenance-companies-using-ai) one such solution. Using machine learning algorithms, AI analyzes sensor data to detect anomalies and identify patterns, predicting equipment failures before they occur. This system helps businesses adjust maintenance schedules, cut repair costs, and reduce downtime.


Machine learning can also quickly mine maintenance logs to find the best repair strategies and maintenance cycles. As a result, IBM’s solution is designed to optimize operations and extend equipment life across industries like manufacturing, energy, and transportation.


### **Cloud Computing**


Cloud computing enables robots to process data remotely and store vast amounts of data, boosting their capabilities without heavy onboard computing. By connecting to the cloud, robots can access advanced analytics and machine learning models that would be too complex to run on local hardware. This approach not only enhances efficiency but also brings affordability and scalability to the entire robotics ecosystem.

### **AI and Robotics in Healthcare**


### ****


In healthcare, AI-driven robots are increasingly assisting in surgeries, patient care, and logistics, ensuring higher precision and better outcomes.


In a major medical breakthrough, the Smart Tissue Autonomous Robot (STAR)[performed](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/autonomous-robot-improves-surgical-precision-using-ai/) a laparoscopic surgery entirely on its own—without any human guidance. Using AI-powered algorithms, including convolutional neural networks trained on over 9,000 motion profiles, STAR predicts tissue movement and plans sutures. Its 3D imaging and real-time tracking enable it to adjust to shifting tissues and patient breathing.


This autonomous surgery marks a leap forward in making complex procedures safer, more efficient, and more reliable.

### **AI and Robotics in Agriculture**


AI robotics in agriculture enhance productivity and sustainability through precision farming, crop monitoring, and automated harvesting.


French startup Meropy has[created](https://newatlas.com/robotics/sentiv-robot-crop-inspecting-robot/) SentiV, an agricultural robot transforming crop monitoring. Weighing just 33 pounds, SentiV’s rimless spoked wheels let it navigate fields with minimal crop impact.


Unlike traditional robots, it inspects crops from above and below using two cameras. Its AI algorithms analyze images to detect pests, diseases, and plant needs, covering up to 50 acres daily.


Farmers can customize SentiV’s width and height with modular wheels, making it adaptable to different crops. While still a prototype, SentiV offers precise, AI-driven insights to help optimize water, fertilizer, and resources, paving the way for more sustainable and higher-yield farming.

## **Frequently Asked Questions**


### How does artificial intelligence work in robots?


Artificial intelligence in robots enables them to perceive their surroundings and make decisions based on their environment. By using sensors like cameras and LiDAR, robots gather data, which is processed through machine learning algorithms to recognize patterns and improve performance over time. This allows them to adapt to new situations and interact with humans through natural language processing, enhancing their capabilities in navigation, object manipulation, and collaboration.


### What are AI robots used for?


AI robots are used in various fields to enhance efficiency and productivity. In healthcare, they assist with surgeries and patient care. In manufacturing, they automate assembly lines and perform quality checks. Agriculture benefits from robots that harvest crops and monitor conditions. In customer service, AI robots interact with clients through chatbots or physical assistants. They are also employed in logistics for navigation and in security for monitoring, showcasing their versatility across industries.


### What are the benefits of using AI in industrial spaces?


AI is transforming industrial spaces by taking over repetitive tasks, boosting both efficiency and productivity. It sharpens quality control with real-time monitoring, catching defects and cutting down on waste. AI’s predictive maintenance minimizes downtime and keeps equipment running longer. In supply chains, it optimizes inventory and fine-tunes demand forecasting, driving cost savings. The result? Processes become more agile, adaptive, and ready to meet shifting market demands.


### What are the obstacles to bringing AI to industrial robots?


Bringing AI to industrial robots faces challenges like high implementation costs, processing power limitations, and complex algorithms. Safety concerns, data privacy issues, and the need for skilled workers further complicate integration. Additionally, adapting to unpredictable environments and ensuring effective data collection are significant obstacles to overcome.
