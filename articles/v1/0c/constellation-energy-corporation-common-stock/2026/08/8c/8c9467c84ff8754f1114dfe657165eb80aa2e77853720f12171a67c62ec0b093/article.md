# Addressing Peak Energy Use with Demand Response Programs

# Addressing Peak Energy Use with Demand Response Programs


Article by Constellation


**


3


min read


As the electricity grid evolves, businesses have a growing role in helping manage when and how energy is used. Electrification, extreme weather and the continued growth of variable renewable energy sources are putting more pressure on the grid. At the same time, rising demand from large energy users such as data centers is contributing to increasing electricity demand across many regions.


Addressing these shifts requires tools that can respond quickly, scale efficiently and work within existing infrastructure. Demand response is one of the ways to help meet that need.


By enabling customers to adjust when and how they use electricity, demand response helps balance the grid in real time. It strengthens reliability, reduces overall system costs and supports progress toward a cleaner energy future.


### **Understanding Demand Response**


Demand response is a temporary change in electricity use by customers during periods of high demand or constrained system conditions. In most programs, these are voluntary adjustments made when a utility, grid operator or demand response program signals customers to reduce or shift electricity use to help manage grid conditions. This typically happens during peak demand or system emergencies, when grid operators need to quickly decrease strain on the system.


Customers can participate in several ways:


- **Reducing load:** Using less electricity for a short period after receiving notice
- **Shifting load:** Moving energy use to an earlier or later time
- **Switching sources:** Using on-site generation or battery storage during an event


At a system level, demand response plays an important part in reducing peak demand. The grid is built to meet the highest levels of demand, even if those peaks occur only a few hours each year. Lowering demand during those periods alleviates strain on infrastructure and can help delay costly upgrades that ultimately impact all customers. It is also important for balancing supply and demand. As more renewable energy comes online, variability increases and demand response adds flexibility on the customer side, helping better align energy use with available supply.


### **Identifying the Factors Driving Demand Growth**


Demand response has supported grid operations for decades, and its role continues to expand as grid conditions evolve. Several factors are driving this growth, including increasing pressure for reliability. Rising demand, the retirement of legacy generation assets, more frequent extreme weather events and constrained transmission systems are all contributing to grid stress. Demand response provides a fast, scalable way to help manage these risks and support reliability when the system is under pressure.


Peak hour economics are also changing and accelerating demand growth. During periods of high demand, the grid relies on more expensive generation resources and wholesale prices can increase significantly. Reducing consumption during these times can lower overall system costs, with financial incentives often available for participation.


Additionally, flexibility is essential to integrating renewable energy. When customers can reduce or shift usage, it can decrease reliance on carbon-intensive peaking generation and support a cleaner energy mix without compromising reliability.


### **Highlighting the Key Benefits of Demand Response**


Demand response creates value on both sides of the meter, supporting grid performance while helping customers better manage energy use and costs. Customers who participate in these programs help support grid stability while responding more strategically during peak events.


Benefits for the grid include:


- **Improved reliability:** Supporting grid performance during peak demand and emergency conditions
- **Lowering system costs** : Reducing reliance on high-cost peaking resources and delaying infrastructure investments
- **Supporting renewable integration:** Enabling more demand-side flexibility to accommodate variable energy sources
- **Reducing emissions** : Displacing higher-carbon generation during peak periods
- **Increasing available capacity:** Helping support economic growth and increased electricity demand from expanding businesses


Benefits for participating customers include:


- **Earning incentives:** Receiving payments tied to performance during demand response events
- **Lowering energy costs:** Reducing usage during high-price periods, especially when paired with load management strategies, which can lead to bill savings
- **Increasing visibility:** Gaining deeper insight into energy usage and controllable load
- **Strengthening decision making:** Managing cost, risk and sustainability goals with a more strategic approach
- **Supporting emissions goals:** Making progress toward emissions reduction through more targeted energy use


These programs can make a significant impact for businesses by supporting more informed decision-making and improving how energy is planned for and managed during peak events.
