# FDA-Approved Second-Line Therapies for Hepatocellular Carcinoma

## FDA-approved second-line therapies for hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC)


### Direct answer


Four agents carry an FDA indication explicitly for HCC patients previously treated with sorafenib, and a fifth (nivolumab) now covers the previously-treated setting only as part of a combination regimen:

### Detail by agent


#### Regorafenib (Stivarga)


The label indicates Stivarga for HCC patients "who have been previously treated with sorafenib," an explicitly second-line/previously-treated indication with no biomarker requirement[30](https://rhizomeai.com/articles/what_are_the_fda_approved_second_line_therapies_for_hcc?showSourceNumber=30) . The pivotal HCC study (RESORCE) administered regorafenib 160 mg once daily plus best supportive care versus placebo plus BSC, so it is used as a single oral agent rather than a biomarker-selected combination[21](https://rhizomeai.com/articles/what_are_the_fda_approved_second_line_therapies_for_hcc?showSourceNumber=21) . The HCC efficacy supplement under NDA 203085 was approved on 2017-04-27, and the product remains marketed (prescription)[232](https://rhizomeai.com/articles/what_are_the_fda_approved_second_line_therapies_for_hcc?showSourceNumber=232)[231](https://rhizomeai.com/articles/what_are_the_fda_approved_second_line_therapies_for_hcc?showSourceNumber=231) .


#### Cabozantinib (Cabometyx)


Cabometyx is indicated "for the treatment of patients with hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) who have been previously treated with sorafenib," again a previously-treated/second-line setting with no biomarker requirement[91](https://rhizomeai.com/articles/what_are_the_fda_approved_second_line_therapies_for_hcc?showSourceNumber=91) . It is used as monotherapy at 60 mg once daily[91](https://rhizomeai.com/articles/what_are_the_fda_approved_second_line_therapies_for_hcc?showSourceNumber=91)[103](https://rhizomeai.com/articles/what_are_the_fda_approved_second_line_therapies_for_hcc?showSourceNumber=103) . The HCC efficacy supplement under NDA 208692 was approved on 2019-01-14 (CELESTIAL) and the product remains marketed[197](https://rhizomeai.com/articles/what_are_the_fda_approved_second_line_therapies_for_hcc?showSourceNumber=197) .


#### Ramucirumab (Cyramza)


Cyramza is the only biomarker-selected second-line HCC agent. The label indicates it "as a single agent" for HCC patients "who have an alpha fetoprotein (AFP) of \[>=400\] ng/mL and have been treated with sorafenib"[49](https://rhizomeai.com/articles/what_are_the_fda_approved_second_line_therapies_for_hcc?showSourceNumber=49)[50](https://rhizomeai.com/articles/what_are_the_fda_approved_second_line_therapies_for_hcc?showSourceNumber=50) . This is the second-line setting restricted to AFP-high patients, based on the REACH-2 trial. The HCC efficacy supplement under BLA 125477 was approved on 2019-05-10 and the product remains marketed[180](https://rhizomeai.com/articles/what_are_the_fda_approved_second_line_therapies_for_hcc?showSourceNumber=180) .


#### Pembrolizumab (Keytruda)


Keytruda is indicated for HCC patients "who have been previously treated with sorafenib," as monotherapy with no stated biomarker requirement[58](https://rhizomeai.com/articles/what_are_the_fda_approved_second_line_therapies_for_hcc?showSourceNumber=58) . This indication originated as an accelerated approval and its confirmatory evidence is described in the label via the KEYNOTE-394 study[252](https://rhizomeai.com/articles/what_are_the_fda_approved_second_line_therapies_for_hcc?showSourceNumber=252) . (The specific accelerated-approval/continued-approval contingency sentence was not captured verbatim in the label pages retrieved, so that language should be confirmed against the current full label[252](https://rhizomeai.com/articles/what_are_the_fda_approved_second_line_therapies_for_hcc?showSourceNumber=252) .)


#### Nivolumab (Opdivo) plus ipilimumab


The nivolumab program illustrates how the second-line HCC landscape has shifted. Nivolumab originally held a monotherapy accelerated approval for HCC after sorafenib; the current label no longer presents nivolumab monotherapy as an active HCC indication and instead frames that as historical study evidence[211](https://rhizomeai.com/articles/what_are_the_fda_approved_second_line_therapies_for_hcc?showSourceNumber=211)[217](https://rhizomeai.com/articles/what_are_the_fda_approved_second_line_therapies_for_hcc?showSourceNumber=217) . Current labeled use is nivolumab in combination with ipilimumab, both for first-line unresectable/metastatic HCC (CHECKMATE-9DW)[210](https://rhizomeai.com/articles/what_are_the_fda_approved_second_line_therapies_for_hcc?showSourceNumber=210)[212](https://rhizomeai.com/articles/what_are_the_fda_approved_second_line_therapies_for_hcc?showSourceNumber=212) and for previously treated patients who progressed on or were intolerant to sorafenib[1](https://rhizomeai.com/articles/what_are_the_fda_approved_second_line_therapies_for_hcc?showSourceNumber=1)[204](https://rhizomeai.com/articles/what_are_the_fda_approved_second_line_therapies_for_hcc?showSourceNumber=204)[206](https://rhizomeai.com/articles/what_are_the_fda_approved_second_line_therapies_for_hcc?showSourceNumber=206) . So in the previously-treated (second-line) setting, nivolumab is now available only as the nivolumab + ipilimumab combination, not as single-agent nivolumab[211](https://rhizomeai.com/articles/what_are_the_fda_approved_second_line_therapies_for_hcc?showSourceNumber=211)[217](https://rhizomeai.com/articles/what_are_the_fda_approved_second_line_therapies_for_hcc?showSourceNumber=217) .


### First-line agents (excluded, for context)


The following systemic HCC agents are labeled for the first-line setting (no prior systemic therapy) and therefore are not second-line therapies:


- Sorafenib (Nexavar): indicated for unresectable HCC; the label does not require prior systemic therapy and it is the historical first-line comparator[119](https://rhizomeai.com/articles/what_are_the_fda_approved_second_line_therapies_for_hcc?showSourceNumber=119)[120](https://rhizomeai.com/articles/what_are_the_fda_approved_second_line_therapies_for_hcc?showSourceNumber=120) .
- Lenvatinib (Lenvima): "first-line treatment of adult patients with unresectable HCC"; the pivotal study enrolled patients with no prior systemic therapy[161](https://rhizomeai.com/articles/what_are_the_fda_approved_second_line_therapies_for_hcc?showSourceNumber=161)[142](https://rhizomeai.com/articles/what_are_the_fda_approved_second_line_therapies_for_hcc?showSourceNumber=142) .
- Atezolizumab (Tecentriq) + bevacizumab: for patients "who have not received prior systemic therapy" (IMbrave150), a first-line combination[125](https://rhizomeai.com/articles/what_are_the_fda_approved_second_line_therapies_for_hcc?showSourceNumber=125)[141](https://rhizomeai.com/articles/what_are_the_fda_approved_second_line_therapies_for_hcc?showSourceNumber=141) .
- Durvalumab (Imfinzi) + tremelimumab: for patients who "had not received prior systemic treatment for HCC" (HIMALAYA), a first-line combination[76](https://rhizomeai.com/articles/what_are_the_fda_approved_second_line_therapies_for_hcc?showSourceNumber=76) .


### Practical notes for RA reviewers


- The classic second-line oral TKIs (regorafenib, cabozantinib) and the AFP-selected antibody (ramucirumab) were all approved as post-sorafenib options in 2017 to 2019 and remain on the market[232](https://rhizomeai.com/articles/what_are_the_fda_approved_second_line_therapies_for_hcc?showSourceNumber=232)[197](https://rhizomeai.com/articles/what_are_the_fda_approved_second_line_therapies_for_hcc?showSourceNumber=197)[180](https://rhizomeai.com/articles/what_are_the_fda_approved_second_line_therapies_for_hcc?showSourceNumber=180) .
- Ramucirumab is the only second-line agent with an on-label biomarker gate (AFP), which matters for eligibility and companion-diagnostic considerations[49](https://rhizomeai.com/articles/what_are_the_fda_approved_second_line_therapies_for_hcc?showSourceNumber=49)[50](https://rhizomeai.com/articles/what_are_the_fda_approved_second_line_therapies_for_hcc?showSourceNumber=50) .
- The checkpoint-inhibitor second-line indications were built on post-sorafenib populations, but the field is moving toward first-line immunotherapy combinations (atezolizumab+bevacizumab, durvalumab+tremelimumab, nivolumab+ipilimumab), which reshapes what "second-line" means once a patient has already received an IO-based first-line regimen[210](https://rhizomeai.com/articles/what_are_the_fda_approved_second_line_therapies_for_hcc?showSourceNumber=210)[125](https://rhizomeai.com/articles/what_are_the_fda_approved_second_line_therapies_for_hcc?showSourceNumber=125)[76](https://rhizomeai.com/articles/what_are_the_fda_approved_second_line_therapies_for_hcc?showSourceNumber=76) .
- "Previously treated with sorafenib" is the exact label anchor for the second-line indications; none of these labels define second-line after first-line immunotherapy, so post-IO sequencing is off-label and a reasonable follow-up question to research further.
