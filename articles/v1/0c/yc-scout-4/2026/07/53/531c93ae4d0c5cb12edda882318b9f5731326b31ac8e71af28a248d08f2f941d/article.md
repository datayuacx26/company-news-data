# Scout vs. Aeries for California Independent Study Compliance


## The Challenge: Managing Independent Study Compliance in a Generalist SIS


For school districts across California, Aeries serves as a robust and effective Student Information System (SIS). It is a powerful tool for managing the complex operations of traditional, seat-based K-12 programs, from enrollment and scheduling to tracking A-G requirements[\[1\]](https://www3.cde.ca.gov/researchfiles/cadashboard/Pr72022.txt) . However, when districts operate independent study (IS) programs, they face a different set of regulatory demands that expose the limitations of a generalist SIS.


The California Department of Education (CDE) enforces strict rules for independent study that create significant administrative and compliance burdens[\[2\]](https://cde.ca.gov/sp/eo/is/quickguideistudy.asp) . These specialized workflows—including Master Agreement management, daily student engagement tracking, weekly work sample collection, and specific CALPADS reporting—are not native to systems designed for traditional attendance. This gap often forces administrators and teachers into time-consuming manual processes, increasing the risk of non-compliance and potential funding loss. This article compares how a generalist SIS like Aeries handles these tasks versus a purpose-built system designed for **independent study compliance** .


## Aeries for Independent Study: Strengths and Manual Workarounds


In districts that use Aeries, it rightly serves as the central system of record for student demographics, enrollment data, and traditional grade reporting. Its strength lies in its comprehensive management of standard school operations. However, to meet the unique requirements of independent study, administrators must rely on a series of manual workarounds.


- **Master Agreements:** While Aeries can store a scanned PDF of a signed agreement, it does not automate the workflow. Staff must manually manage the distribution, track signatures via spreadsheets or email, and upload the final document. There is no automated system to flag students who lack a signed agreement.
- **Engagement and Attendance:** The Aeries attendance module is built around seat time. For independent study, where attendance is based on engagement and work product, teachers must track this data outside the system. This often involves maintaining separate logs or spreadsheets, which are then used to manually enter attendance data into Aeries, creating duplicate work and a high potential for error.
- **Work Samples:** Aeries can act as a document repository, but it requires teachers to manually collect work samples, follow a strict naming convention, and upload each file. There is no automated connection linking the work sample to the student's learning log or the specific date of completion, making audit preparation a difficult collation task.
- **Audit Preparation:** When preparing for an **independent study audit guide** , administrators must manually assemble documentation from disparate sources. This involves exporting reports from Aeries, collating data from external spreadsheets, and locating specific scanned documents—a process that is inefficient and prone to human error.

## Head-to-Head Comparison: Aeries vs. Scout for Key IS Functions


To select the **best SIS for California independent study** , administrators must compare how each system handles the core functions that auditors scrutinize.


### Master Agreement Management


- **Aeries:** Functions as a static repository for scanned documents. Requires manual tracking of signatures and compliance status.
- **Scout:** Provides a full-service digital workflow with e-signatures, automated reminders, and a live dashboard for at-a-glance compliance monitoring.


### Daily Engagement & Attendance


- **Aeries:** Relies entirely on manual entry of attendance data derived from outside sources. The system is not designed to capture evidence of asynchronous engagement.
- **Scout:** Automatically ingests engagement data from learning platforms, providing time-stamped, third-party verification of daily student participation that stands up to audit scrutiny.


### Work Sample Collection


- **Aeries:** Requires teachers to manually collect, name, and upload files. The process is disconnected from daily learning logs.
- **Scout:** Automates the collection and organization of work samples, linking them to specific dates and assignments to build a complete, audit-ready student portfolio.


### Audit Preparedness


- **Aeries:** Preparing for an audit requires administrators to manually collate data from reports, scanned documents, and external spreadsheets—a time-intensive and high-risk process.
- **Scout:** Consolidates all necessary compliance documentation—agreements, engagement logs, and work samples—into a single, centralized record for each student, available on demand. ## The "Both/And" Solution: Integrating Scout with Aeries


Adopting a specialized tool for independent study does not mean replacing Aeries. The most effective and efficient solution for a district is to leverage both systems for what they do best. In this model, Scout is not a competitor to Aeries but a necessary, supplementary system.


The workflow is straightforward:


1. **Aeries** remains the district's primary SIS and system of record for demographics, enrollment across all programs, and master scheduling.
2. **Scout** acts as the dedicated SIS for the independent study program, automating all IS-specific compliance, data collection, and operational workflows.


This integrated ecosystem ensures data consistency while deploying a purpose-built tool to manage the high-stakes, high-risk area of independent study compliance. By connecting the two, districts create a powerful infrastructure that is more efficient than forcing a single system to perform tasks it was not designed for.
