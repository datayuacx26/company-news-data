# How to Sublease Office Space in NYC | Tandem

Subleasing office space in NYC means taking over another company's existing lease — either part of their floor or the whole thing — so you get a move-in-ready office, usually at below-market rates, on a faster timeline than a direct lease. Sublease rents in Manhattan typically run 10–20% below comparable direct leases, and most available terms fall between 12 and 36 months. The catch: you need landlord consent, and the original tenant's lease terms become your ceiling.


## What Exactly Is an Office Sublease in NYC?


When a company has more space than it needs — or is shutting down, pivoting, or relocating — it may list that space as a sublease. As the subtenant, you sign an agreement with the original tenant (the "sublandlord"), not with the building owner directly. The building owner remains the landlord of record and must approve the sublease before it's binding.


This is different from a direct lease, where you negotiate with the building owner, and from a shared space arrangement, where one company informally carves out desks for another. A sublease is a formal legal document governed by the original master lease. That means you inherit whatever terms the original tenant negotiated.


Key structural facts about NYC office subleases:


- **Term length is fixed** : You can only sublease for as long as the original lease runs. If the master lease expires in 18 months, that's your ceiling.
- **Rent is often negotiable** : The sublandlord wants to offset their costs, not profit. This typically creates real room to negotiate.
- **Buildout is usually done** : Most sublease spaces come furnished or built out, so you can move in weeks after signing, not months.
- **Landlord consent is required** :[NYC Real Property Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landlord%E2%80%93tenant_law) and nearly every commercial lease require the building owner to approve any sublease. Budget 1–4 weeks for this.


For companies that need space quickly and aren't ready to commit to a 5-year direct lease, a sublease can be a genuinely smart move. Especially in Manhattan, where direct lease inventory moves fast and landlords routinely push for longer commitments.

## What Are the Legal Requirements for Subleasing in NYC?


This is where founders get caught off guard.


**Landlord consent is mandatory.** Nearly every commercial master lease in New York requires the building owner's written approval before a sublease is valid. The NYC Bar Association and commercial real estate attorneys flag this as the most commonly overlooked step — and the most consequential.


Here's what the consent process looks like:


1. The sublandlord formally requests consent from the building owner
2. The landlord reviews the proposed subtenant's financials and business
3. The landlord approves, denies, or requests modifications
4. Written consent is issued (or withheld)


This typically takes **2–4 weeks** , sometimes longer in large institutional buildings. Build this into your timeline. Do not plan to occupy the space while consent is pending.


What happens if you skip it? A sublease executed without landlord consent is typically voidable — meaning the landlord can terminate both the master lease and your sublease. You could lose the space on short notice with little recourse.


A few other legal points:


- **Assignment vs. sublease** : Some deals are structured as assignments, where the original tenant exits entirely. The legal mechanics differ from a sublease. If you're not sure which structure you're looking at, talk to an attorney before you sign anything.
- **Letter of Intent (LOI)** : Even in sublease deals, a non-binding LOI should come before the formal agreement. It aligns both parties on rent, term, furniture, and responsibilities before the lawyers get involved.
- **Security deposit** : Unlike direct leases (where NYC landlords often require 3–6 months plus a[Good Guy Guarantee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Good_guy_clause) ), sublease deposits vary widely. Some sublandlords accept 1–2 months; others want more, depending on your financials.

## What Should You Negotiate in a NYC Office Sublease?


Don't treat the sublandlord's first offer as final. They're paying rent on empty space every month and want to fill it. That's your leverage.


### What's worth pushing on?


- **Rent** : Even a 5–10% reduction on a 24-month sublease can save you tens of thousands. Start below their ask.
- **Furniture and equipment** : Most sublease spaces come furnished. Get specific about what stays — desks, chairs, conference tables, monitors, whiteboards. Get it in writing.
- **Free rent period** : A sublandlord may offer 2–4 weeks of free rent to close faster. Ask directly.
- **Permitted use clause** : The sublease should explicitly allow your type of business. Some master leases restrict use to specific industries, and you don't want to discover that problem after you've signed.
- **Signage and access** : Confirm you'll have your company name in the lobby directory and that after-hours HVAC and building access are either included or clearly priced.
- **Exit provisions** : Understand what happens if the master lease terminates early. Does your sublease survive, or are you exposed?


It is wise to have an attorney review any commercial lease before signing. For a sublease, that means reviewing both the sublease agreement and the relevant sections of the master lease — not just the summary the sublandlord sends over.

## Sublease, Direct Lease, or Shared Space — Which Makes Sense?


Here's the practical framework:


- **Sublease makes sense if** you need space in under 60 days, want a furnished move-in-ready office, have a stable team size for the next 12–24 months, and aren't ready for a 5-year direct lease.
- **Direct lease makes more sense if** you want customization, you're planning significant headcount growth, and you have more time to work through buildout and the LOI process.
- **Shared space makes sense if** you need fewer than 10 desks, want month-to-month flexibility, and are comfortable with a less formal setup.

The right space at the right term length matters more than squeezing out a few extra dollars per square foot.
