# How to Track Your Design System’s Health With Supernova

Documentation and communication are at the core of design systems. If your[components](https://www.supernova.io/blog/fundamentals-of-design-system-components) and[tokens](https://www.supernova.io/blog/what-are-design-tokens) are the building blocks of your design system, then[documentation](https://www.supernova.io/blog/design-system-documentation-why-you-need-it-and-how-to-do-it) and[communication](https://www.supernova.io/blog/building-effective-communication-and-collaboration-for-design-systems-teams) are manual and instructions that guide them. Your design system is only effective if people understand and use it. Design system health — or status — is a critical piece of information that impacts everyone in the product lifecycle.


In this article, we'll go over what design system health means, why it's important to track, and how you can use Supernova to track and communicate it with your entire team.


## What is design system health?


Design system health refers to the robustness and efficiency of a design system which includes the reliability, functionality, and consistency of the components within the system. It's a measure of how well the design system is performing, both individually in terms of its components and collectively as a whole. Here are some aspects that are commonly considered when evaluating design system component health:


- **Functionality** : Is the design system component functioning as intended? Are they interacting seamlessly with each other and with external systems?
- **Relevance** : Is the component up-to-date in both documentation and implementation? Are the design principles and guidelines being adhered to across all components?
- **Reliability** : Is the component reliable and stable? Are there any issues or bugs that need to be addressed?


## Why is tracking design system health important?


So now that we know what design system health is — why is it important to track? We need to look back at the[benefits of design systems](https://www.supernova.io/blog/design-system-benefits) , including streamlining the product cycle, improving consistency, and effectively scaling, to name a few. Getting the benefit out of a design system relies on it being an effective source of truth. Getting to that source of truth requires clear and simple information communication — that's where the design system health metric comes into play. Tracking design system health comes with a lot of benefits, but here are the main three:


### 1. **Early issue identification and resolution**


Component health is an all-encompassing metric that lets you know at a glance if there is an issue with a component that needs to be addressed. Proactively monitoring design system health helps you identify and rectify issues quicker and prevent minor hitches from snowballing into major setbacks that could derail your project.


### 2. **Maintaining quality and consistency**


A design system acts as a single source of truth, ensuring consistency across the product and user experience. Keeping a close eye on the health of your design system components helps ensure that any specific component conforms to the defined standards and guidelines and is ready to be implemented.


### 3. **Informed decision-making**


Having detailed insights into the health and performance of your design system components equips your team to make well-informed decisions. These decisions include resource allocation for maintenance, deploying new components, or prioritizing fixes. It forms a solid foundation for factual, data-driven decision-making, which is key for successful project management and continuous improvement.


## Track the health of your design system with Supernova


To guide you on how to track the health of your design system, we'll be using Supernova. With a robust set of tools specifically catered to design and product teams, you can easily set up health tracking with a few simple steps. Go ahead and log in to your[Supernova workspace](http://cloud.supernova.io/) to get started. Don't have a Supernova workspace yet? Get started with a free account by[signing up](https://cloud.supernova.io/signup) .


### Components view


After connecting your design data to your Supernova design system, we can start tracking component health.


From the left sidebar, head to the **Design system data** section and then the **Components** tab to get the components view. As soon as you add your components, you'll see a column titled "Status” that lets you choose your component's current health.


Click on the status of an individual component to alter and update your component health.


Supernova helps you take it a step further by letting you edit, add, and remove status options for health tracking. Each company has specific needs and tailoring your status options helps you convey more information about your components faster. To do this, click on the **Status** column header, then **Edit** to make your changes easily. You can add more granular details like the different kinds of issues if it's design or code-related, for example.


Tracking design system health from the component view helps you gain contextual knowledge on all your components quickly. However, design systems are used by two different groups: practitioners who build and maintain it and users who consume it. To communicate component health or anything else to users of a design system, it needs to be in a concise and tailored format. Let’s look at how to use Supernova to create a tailored documentation site for your users to track component health easily.


## Documenting component health


First, head to the **Documentation** section in your Supernova workspace. If you need a refresher, check out our previous blogs on[documenting your design system](https://www.supernova.io/blog/how-to-use-supernova-for-design-system-documentation) and[components](https://www.supernova.io/blog/how-to-create-accessible-design-system-components) . After getting a good sense of how to use Supernova's documentation, let's look at the different ways to track component health. To add your component status, you have three documentation block options:


### Component health


The[Component health](https://learn.supernova.io/latest/documentation/types-of-blocks/component/component-health-sezSxEED) block adds a line status of a specific component. This option is great for individual component pages and lets you clearly communicate the status of a component in a visual way. You can also click the Show details option for more component details as a pop-up.


### Component checklist


With the[Component checklist](https://learn.supernova.io/latest/documentation/types-of-blocks/component/component-checklist-NXepPIpZ) block, you can communicate a lot more information about a component. It's typically used at the beginning of a component page, and it helps convey a lot more than just the health of a component. You can mix and match what properties you want to show in a checklist for more tailored information.


### Component overview


Component overview table from Userlane's[Burrito](https://userlane.supernova-docs.io/latest/components/overview-iLsbd7kI) design system


Similar to the Components view that we covered, the[Component overview](https://learn.supernova.io/latest/documentation/types-of-blocks/component/component-overview-table-W8eOtJ5t) block gives you a table of all your components along with the relevant properties you want to highlight. This gives you a lot of flexibility regarding where in your documentation site you want to give an overview of your components and not have your team go into Supernova to check it out manually.


### Adding health blocks to your documentation


To add these blocks to your documentation page, type '/’ or click the + button to add a **Component checklist, Component overview,** or **Component health** block. Select your component, then select which properties you want to showcase. Once you publish your documentation, users can get critical information on your component when they open the page.
