# How AI Is Changing the Way Students Take Notes and Prepare for Exams

# How AI Is Changing the Way Students Take Notes and Prepare for Exams


The modern lecture hall is a far cry from the hallowed halls of yesteryear, where a professor stood, lecturing to rows of students taking notes by hand. These days, while some do take notes by hand, most utilize a laptop, tablet, or other device to take notes in class and to help organize and review later for exams, such as midterms and finals. But, endless pages of typing later, these too can become an overwhelming chore to review for an exam. However, there are intelligent software tools that support students in their attempt to manage the amount of information in their notes and to really understand what they read.


## Beyond Passive Recording


For decades, students wrote notes to redistribute to other students to study for tests. Long after having been written, these notes sat in storage until they were finally discarded. What value did the student gain from having taken the notes in the first place? The simple act of taking notes in writing does not in itself equal learning. In fact, such activities typically utilize the greatest portion of a student’s available cognitive resources.


New intelligent software enables information to be captured in entirely new ways. For example, audio processing tools can now automatically transcribe lectures in real time, so students can focus on the lecture and engage with the material during the class. Other software and tools can then automatically create a text‑based summary, organized and structured, including outlining long written materials and defining terms and concepts.


When students are freed from the basic tasks of note‑taking (such as recording information), they can engage with the material during class. This means that they can ask better questions, make connections to previous learning, and be more successful in their studies.


## Studying Smarter with Personalization


One of the biggest struggles in exam preparation is knowing what to study. Traditional study methods often involve re‑reading notes or highlighting textbook passages. Research has shown that these passive methods are among the least effective ways to prepare for exams.


Modern study platforms analyze class notes and automatically generate targeted review materials. For instance, using an[AI flashcard generator](https://www.remnote.com/blog/best-flashcard-maker) allows students to turn lecture summaries into interactive study decks within seconds. This shifts the focus from manually creating flashcards to actually testing one's knowledge.


Spaced repetition systems take this a step further. These platforms track which concepts a student struggles with and schedule reviews at optimal intervals. If a student easily remembers a formula, the system shows it less frequently. If a concept confuses, it reappears sooner. This approach optimizes study time and prevents last‑minute cramming.


## Breaking Down Complex Subjects


There are many topics or subjects that are just not easy to learn or to get across to students, and that is why they have been taught the way they have in the past: using a textbook.


An interactive study assistant is also a patient tutor, available 24/7. You paste in a challenging section of a reading or essay, and ask for an explanation in simpler terms. Perhaps you even request an analogy, or examples that illustrate the application of the information. If the assistant can’t explain it in a way that resonates with you, perhaps there is another way of equal or greater value.


Finally, we can use technology in our studies to better understand the course material. By using tools that let us ask questions at odd hours of the night, we are better able to satisfy our curiosity and, therefore, study independently.


## Preparing for Exam Day


The way in which a student prepares for an exam is not just about memorizing a large amount of information. It involves applying their knowledge of a subject in a variety of different ways and in a range of different situations and contexts within a time frame.


But as students use technology to learn, there are many areas where the human brain is still necessary for academic success. For instance, preparing for exams to apply what has been learned in real test situations is still best accomplished by having a human brain. This is because many areas are better learned through practice in situations that mimic actual exams.


## The Human Advantage


Using the smartest tools to aid learning does not have to mean outsourcing your brain to machines. While your computer can aid your studies by organizing your work, by repeating large amounts of work for you and in many cases doing your work for you, there remains much for your brain to do, such as in‑depth analysis of any problems you encounter, using your creativity to think outside of the box of your work and finding new solutions to any problems you encounter as well as the total of your knowledge.


And as educational technology continues to evolve, so too does our definition of the good student. Instead of rewarding the student who can write the fastest or memorize the most facts, we now look for students who can use these tools to gain a deeper understanding of the material and apply what they have learned in a variety of situations.
