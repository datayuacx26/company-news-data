# Purchase requisition forms: how to use in your procurement workflow

In a previous post, we walked through the ins and outs of a purchase order. In this guide, we talk all things purchase requisition forms. What they are, what they contain, and the steps involved in the purchase requisition workflow.


## What is a purchase requisition?


A purchase requisition, also called a[procurement request](https://www.vendr.com/blog/procurement-requests) , is an internal document created by employees to request the purchase of goods and services for a business. Purchase requisition forms are created for all kinds of purchasing needs like manufacturing goods, hardware, inventory, or any other good or service the business needs to run.


Once a purchasing department receives a requisition form, they go through an approval process, from which they can then create a purchase order that’s sent to the appropriate vendor. Once the order has been agreed on and fulfilled, the vendor sends back an invoice.


This whole purchasing process—from purchase requisition creation to approvals and drafting purchase orders for vendors—is established to ensure accurate business expense record keeping. It’s also a way to uphold transparency amongst employees in each department and protect against[procurement fraud](https://www.vendr.com/blog/procurement-fraud) in each department throughout[the procurement lifecycle](https://www.vendr.com/procurement) .


## What is a purchase requisition form?


A purchase requisition form is used by an employee, sometimes called the requestor, to describe the need for a specific good or service. It also describes exactly what is needed.


If you work in the HR department and you run out of printer paper, you fill out a purchase requisition form. Submitting this to your procurement team prompts them to approve the purchase and create a purchase order. From there, the approved order is sent to the office supply vendor, who accepts and fulfills the order. The vendor then invoices the business for the reams of printer paper requested.


## Purchase requisition form vs. purchase order form


It’s important to distinguish between a[purchase requisition](https://finance.duke.edu/procurement/procure/purchreq) form and a purchase order form. They’re often confused for each other and incorrectly assumed to be the same thing.


A purchase requisition form is an internal document drafted for internal approval of company purchases. A purchase order form is created once the purchase order requisition is internally approved.


Unlike a purchase requisition form, a purchase order form is a document—these days often a digital document—that’s created to be sent externally to vendors. In other words, a purchase requisition form comes first, and the purchase order form comes second.


## Benefits of purchase requisition forms


A first glance, creating a purchase requisition form may seem like just another step in a process. Still, this step offers benefits to businesses, including—


- Enforcing transparency across departments
- Keeping a historical track record of expenses for reference
- Enabling the procurement team to[build better vendor relationships](https://www.vendr.com/blog/long-term-relationship-suppliers)
- Cost savings
- Better spend management
- Improved inventory management
- Being prepared for financial audits
- Ensuring accurate tracking of accounts payable records


## Contents of a purchase requisition form


Though requisition forms aren’t exactly alike across every business, they do tend to contain the same general information.


- Date of purchase request
- Requisition number
- Name of employee filling out the purchase requisition
- The department the purchase is for
- A description of the goods or services requested
- Size, quantity, price, and other relevant request metrics
- The reason for the expense

## Steps involved in the purchase requisition workflow


Here’s a general step-by-step overview of the purchase requisition workflow.


### \[1\] Purchase request creation and submission to department manager


Let’s say you work at a honey manufacturing plant and you ran out of mason jar lids to properly package your goods. As an employee in the packaging department, you notice you need more lids and decide to fill out a purchase requisition form. You include—


- The date of the requisition request
- Your name
- Requisition number
- The name of your department
- A description of the lids you need
- Lid size, quantity, price, and any other relevant lid metrics
- The reason you need more lids (in this case, production)


### \[2\] Department manager screens and approves request


Once you’ve completed the PR form, you hand it to your manager so he can approve it and submit it to the finance department or procurement team.


### \[3\] Procurement team receives the purchase request


Once the procurement team receives the PR request for more mason jar lids, they go through an approval process. Once the requisition form is approved, the procurement team creates an official purchase order. Much like PR forms, PO forms contain a few key fields.


- PO date
- Purchase order number
- Company address and contact information
- Billing and shipping information
- Purchase order terms
- Delivery date
- Items with their description, quantity, and unit cost
- Total cost for that purchase order
- Any taxes
- Special notes or instructions


### \[4\] Purchase order creation and submission to vendor


At this point, your requisition request form has done its job. It’s a paper trail of goods requested that a[procurement department](https://www.vendr.com/blog/procurement) can track. It ensured PO accuracy and streamlined the communication of internal needs from one department to another.


Now, it’s up to the procurement team to create a PO and send it to the appropriate seller. Once the seller agrees to fulfill the purchase order according to the terms and delivery dates outlined, it moves into fulfillment.


### \[5\] Delivery of items


The purchase requisition form has led to its intended outcome. The end goal is fulfilled successfully with the delivery of the requested goods or service. The cycle starts once again any time there’s a new request.
