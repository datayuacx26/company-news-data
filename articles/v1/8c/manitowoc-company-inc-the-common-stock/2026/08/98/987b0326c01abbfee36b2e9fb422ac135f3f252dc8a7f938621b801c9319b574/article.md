# Understanding the hidden risks of generic parts in crane maintenance

## Half price, half lifespan


Because generics are typically manufactured with lower-grade materials to reduce costs, their lifespan is often significantly shorter than genuine parts. This means they must be serviced or replaced more frequently, eroding initial savings, increasing downtime, and potentially endangering the jobsite. In many cases, opting for a cheaper alternative does not make financial sense in the long-term, and introduces additional risk for owners.


“As a manufacturer, it is in Manitowoc’s interest to maintain the high value of our equipment,” said Yannick Richaud. “Generic parts can cause premature wear of other components because they are built by companies that do not fully understand the overall specifications of a crane or are solely focused on profit. At Manitowoc, we engineer our cranes to offer reduced maintenance frequency, limited wear of components, and minimized downtime. Introducing non-OEM parts into these cranes puts the long-term performance and value of a crane at risk.”


## Real consequences


Beyond operational risks, generic parts can expose crane owners to additional consequences. One of the most critical considerations is the loss of manufacturer support. Load charts for Grove, Manitowoc, and Potain cranes are invalidated when non-OEM components are used. If a structural component is repaired in an unknown way; or an OEM component is replaced with a generic component, the load chart for the crane is no longer valid. It’s worth keeping in mind that while some generic parts suppliers may offer limited warranties, these typically cover only the part itself, not any resulting damage to the crane.


Additionally, the use of non-original components will invalidate regional certifications and OEM warranties. In practical terms, that means operators and crane owners could be held responsible, even if a relatively inexpensive non-original component causes extensive downstream damage. Genuine parts, on the other hand, allow crane owners to benefit from high-quality technical support, helping maximize uptime and protect the long-term value of the equipment.


## A growing global problem


The issue of generic parts is especially prevalent in regions with limited regulatory oversight or where enforcement of safety and material quality standards is less stringent. Manitowoc and other manufacturers have identified generic versions of all kinds of components, from small parts to complex structural elements like mast sections for tower cranes or jib extensions for mobile cranes.


“Manitowoc views the reliable operation and productivity of its cranes as essential pillars to project success. Cutting corners on critical components is a risk no crane owner or operator should ever take,” Richaud said.
