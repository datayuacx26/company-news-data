# How to Calculate Your Accounting Automation ROI

Every accounting firm considering automation eventually faces the same question: *is this actually worth it?* The answer isn't complicated — but most firms never sit down to calculate it rigorously.


This guide walks you through the exact framework we use to estimate ROI for our customers at Rima. You can do this with a spreadsheet, or just use[our free calculator](https://www.getrima.ai/accounting-automation-roi-calculator) if you want the result in seconds.


## The four components of accounting automation ROI


ROI from automation isn't a single number — it's the sum of four distinct value streams:


1. **Labour cost savings** — time your staff no longer spends on manual processing
2. **Error reduction value** — cost of mistakes you no longer have to fix
3. **Capacity freed for advisory work** — the opportunity cost of hours spent on data entry
4. **Speed improvements** — faster close cycles, faster AP processing, faster client turnaround


For a simple ROI calculation, we focus on the first two — they're easily quantifiable. The third and fourth are real but harder to put a number on.


## Step 1: Measure your current manual processing load


Start by answering these five questions:


- How many documents do you process per month? (invoices, receipts, bank statements, expense reports)
- How long does it take staff to process one document, on average?
- What's the fully-loaded hourly rate of your accounting staff? (salary + benefits + overhead)
- What percentage of documents require correction or re-work?
- How many people on your team spend time on document processing?


For a typical mid-size CPA firm processing 500 documents/month with 15 minutes per document at $45/hr:


> 500 documents × 15 min = 7,500 minutes = 125 hours/month of processing time


## Step 2: Calculate labour cost savings


AI automation doesn't eliminate all manual work — review, exceptions, and judgment calls still need humans. A realistic efficiency gain is 75–85%. We use 80% as our baseline.


> Hours saved/month = total monthly processing hours × 80%
> Annual labour savings = hours saved/month × 12 × hourly rate


Using the example above:


- Hours saved/month: 125 × 0.80 = **100 hours**
- Annual labour savings: 100 × 12 × $45 = **$54,000/year**


## Step 3: Quantify error reduction value


Manual data entry errors in accounting are expensive. Each error typically costs $100–$200 to correct when you factor in: time to identify the error, rework, client communication, and reputational damage. We use $150 as a conservative midpoint.


> Monthly errors = monthly documents × error rate
> Errors reduced = monthly errors × 80%
> Annual error reduction value = errors reduced × 12 × $150


At a 3% error rate on 500 documents:


- Monthly errors: 500 × 3% = 15 errors
- Errors reduced: 15 × 0.80 = 12 per month
- Annual value: 12 × 12 × $150 = **$21,600/year**


## Step 4: Calculate payback period and 3-year ROI


With total annual value established, payback period is straightforward:


> Payback period (months) = monthly subscription cost ÷ monthly value generated


At $499/month for Rima, and $75,600/year total value ($6,300/month):


- Payback period: $499 ÷ $6,300 = **~0.8 months** (under 4 weeks)


For 3-year ROI:


> 3-year ROI = ((3-year value − 3-year cost) ÷ 3-year cost) × 100


- 3-year value: $75,600 × 3 = $226,800
- 3-year cost: $499 × 36 = $17,964
- 3-year ROI: (($226,800 − $17,964) ÷ $17,964) × 100 = **1,163%**


## What these numbers mean in practice


A 1,000%+ ROI sounds unrealistic — but it's consistent with what accounting automation actually delivers. The key insight is that document processing is *very* labour-intensive, *very* error-prone, and *very* amenable to automation.


The firms that see the highest ROI share three characteristics:


1. High document volume (500+ per month)
2. Relatively senior staff handling routine data entry ($40+/hr)
3. Active error correction workflows (re-work is often invisible but costly)


The firms that see more modest returns typically have low document volumes or already use offshore/lower-cost staff for data entry.


## Beyond the numbers: the advisory opportunity


The financial ROI is compelling, but many firm leaders tell us the bigger win is what their team does with the time they recover. When staff aren't manually keying in invoice data, they can:


- Spend more time on client advisory conversations
- Take on more clients without hiring
- Focus on exception handling and judgment calls that actually need expertise
- Reduce overtime and burnout on the team


None of that shows up in a simple ROI calculation — but it's often the reason firms cite most often when explaining why they adopted automation.
