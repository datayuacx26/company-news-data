# The Most Common Warehouse Safety Violations and How to Prevent Them


May 13, 2026


Warehouse safety is about more than just ensuring basic compliance. A safe facility can help reduce injuries, minimize downtime, improve productivity, and protect employees throughout the workday. Unfortunately, many of the most common warehouse safety violations are also among the most preventable.


Whether managing a distribution center in


[Michigan](https://materialhandling.altg.com/locations/) , a manufacturing facility


[in Illinois](https://materialhandling.altg.com/locations/) , or a warehouse operation in


[Ontario or New England](https://materialhandling.altg.com/locations/) , understanding common risks can help create a safer workplace and reduce the likelihood of costly OSHA violations.


Here are some of the most frequently cited warehouse safety issues and practical steps to address them.


## **** 1. Inadequate Forklift Operator Training


****


Forklift-related incidents continue to be a major source of workplace injuries and OSHA violations. Operators who lack proper training may struggle to recognize hazards, follow safe operating procedures, or respond appropriately in high-traffic environments.


How do you prevent this issue?


- Provide initial and refresher operator training


- Evaluate operators regularly


- Document all training activities


- Review site-specific hazards with employees


Strong


[operator training programs](https://materialhandling.altg.com/warehouse-safety/training/) should be an ongoing process rather than a one-time event.


## 2. Blocked Exits and Emergency Access Routes


Emergency exits, fire extinguishers, electrical panels, and evacuation routes must remain accessible at all times. Warehouses that use temporary storage areas or experience seasonal inventory surges can unintentionally create obstructions.


You should:


- Conduct routine aisle inspections


- Clearly mark emergency routes


- Establish designated storage locations


- Include exit checks in daily safety walkthroughs


Even temporary obstructions can create significant safety concerns during an emergency.


## 3. Poor Housekeeping Practices


Cluttered work areas increase the risk of slips, trips, falls, and equipment accidents. Debris, shrink wrap, broken pallets, and spilled materials can quickly create hazards if not addressed promptly.


Your facility should:


- Implement regular cleaning schedules


- Assign housekeeping responsibilities


- Remove damaged pallets immediately


- Address spills as soon as they occur


Good housekeeping is one of the simplest ways to improve compliance with warehouse safety regulations.


## 4. Improper Pallet Storage


Pallets that are stacked incorrectly or overloaded can collapse, creating serious hazards for employees and equipment. Some of the most common issues with pallets that lead to unsafe environments include damaged pallets, uneven stacking, and unstable loads on forklifts or material handling equipment.


Train your employees on proper stacking procedures, but go beyond that with regular inspections, stacking guidelines, and replacing damaged pallets rather than using unsafe materials. Safe storage practices help protect both inventory and personnel.


## 5. Insufficient Pedestrian Protection


Many warehouse safety violations occur when pedestrians and


[powered industrial vehicles](https://materialhandling.altg.com/catalog/) share the same workspace without adequate separation.


Busy facilities often face challenges such as:


- Cross-traffic intersections


- Limited visibility


- Congested picking areas


- Mixed vehicle and pedestrian routes


The safest facilities will have traffic management procedures that are carefully enforced. It is so important to use mirrors for blind corners, install appropriate barriers, and mark pedestrian walkways clearly. Creating clear separation between people and equipment can significantly reduce collision risks.


## 6. Failure to Inspect Equipment


Daily inspections are required for many types of warehouse equipment. When inspections are skipped or documentation is incomplete, safety issues can go unnoticed. A missed inspection might lead to unplanned repair costs, equipment failure, OSHA violations, and disruption of operations.


To prevent failed inspections, we recommend:


- Use standardized inspection checklists


- Require pre-shift inspections


- Document maintenance concerns


- Remove unsafe equipment from service immediately


Consistent inspections can help identify problems before they become serious incidents.


## 7. Inadequate Fall Protection


Warehouses with elevated work platforms, mezzanines, dock areas, or


[high-level picking operations](https://materialhandling.altg.com/catalog/forklifts/order-pickers/) may face fall hazards that require additional safeguards.


To prevent this issue:


- Follow applicable fall protection requirements


- Inspect guardrails regularly


- Train employees on fall prevention procedures


- Address damaged safety equipment promptly


Falls are one of the leading causes of workplace injuries across many industries.


## 8. Missing or Unclear Safety Signage


Employees and visitors rely on signs to identify hazards, traffic patterns, restricted areas, and emergency procedures. When signage is missing, damaged, or difficult to understand, compliance issues can follow. Audit signage from time to time and replace any damaged or missing signs promptly. Be consistent with all inspections and visual standards. Clear communication is an important component of effective warehouse safety rules.


## 9. Lack of Ongoing Safety Audits


Many facilities focus heavily on safety after an incident or inspection but fail to maintain consistent oversight throughout the year. Regular audits help identify concerns before they result in injuries or citations. Continuous improvement often leads to stronger compliance and safer working conditions.


## Building a Safer Warehouse Environment


Preventing warehouse safety violations requires ongoing attention to training, housekeeping, inspections, traffic management, and employee engagement. While every facility has unique challenges, many of the most common OSHA violations stem from issues that can be identified and corrected through proactive safety practices.

## FAQs


**What are the most common warehouse safety violations?**


Some of the most common warehouse safety violations involve forklift operation, blocked exits, poor housekeeping, inadequate fall protection, improper pallet storage, and failure to perform equipment inspections.


**Why are OSHA violations common in warehouses?**


Warehouses often have complex environments with moving equipment, elevated storage, pedestrian traffic, and changing inventory levels.


**How often should warehouse safety inspections be performed?**


Many facilities perform daily equipment inspections and regular facility walkthroughs. More comprehensive safety audits are often conducted monthly, quarterly, or annually depending on operational requirements.


**How can warehouses improve overall safety performance?**


Improving safety performance typically involves ongoing training, routine inspections, clear safety communication, housekeeping programs, and regular reviews of workplace procedures.
