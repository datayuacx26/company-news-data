## **Streamlining Processes with Water Restoration Software**


Any restoration company worth its salt understands that water doesn’t just cause damage to structures. Damage can also result from deficient remediation after the initial disaster. In other words, if water damage isn’t handled expediently, it can cause even more ruin.


Ceilings, floors, and walls damaged by water can collapse if not dealt with expediently. Mold, disease, vermin, and other factors can lead to health risks for occupants and even those in surrounding areas. Contractors with the right skill sets and experience can restore a building damaged by water, but restoration software makes these efforts more efficient and effective.


## **Benefits of Using Water Restoration Software**


Monitoring the drying process is an integral part of water restoration. Software makes this task easier by measuring and reporting moisture levels, creating a more efficient process. It frees up personnel and time that can be better utilized elsewhere, which is why savvy restorers are increasingly looking towards technology for managing restoration of water-damaged structures.


**Water restoration software offers the following benefits:**


- **Augmented efficiency:** By automating many elements involved in structural water restoration, software makes such things as making work orders, scheduling projects, and sending out invoices more efficient, which saves contractors both time and money while offering better quality service to customers.
- **Increased accuracy:** Modern water restoration software includes moisture mapping applications that allow a more accurate view of what parts of a building require the most urgent attention; of course, this meticulousness also extends to office staff and project managers through the provision of more accurate invoices and estimates that in turn improve customer satisfaction.
- **Enhanced communications:** Regardless of what causes the damage, restoration software helps improve communications both internally within a company and externally with other stakeholders; software that includes automating status updates, remote scheduling online, secure messaging, and other communication features helps keep everyone involved in a project informed about the progress.
- **Better compliance:** Complying with industry standards and municipal regulations requires careful documentation, for which water restoration software can help immensely via tools that can document damage, map structural moisture, and track equipment, among other capabilities.
- **New opportunities:** For contractors that perform water damage restorations, software can also help them grow their businesses; by streamlining processes, restorers can take advantage of automated marketing and lead generation to increase sales, while also improving customer relationships to produce more referrals.


Automation, analytics, and digital documentation are three general areas where water restoration software can aid restorers.

Water restoration software allows project managers to swiftly collect and store customer contact information while enabling others to track every job detail. As any experienced contractor knows, projects often don’t go as planned, with constantly changing schedules that make shifting scarce resources around more difficult. - Provide resources when and where they’re needed, along with any relevant notes about how they should be used.

### **Real-Time Reporting and Analytics**


Data is an important aspect of any water restoration. Software analytics are ideal for analyzing this data, allowing smaller restoration companies countless benefits that previously only bigger businesses could afford. When dealing with damage from water, restoration software can help gather onsite data so that field teams can do their jobs more efficiently.


The best water restoration software will allow field technicians to gather information like moisture mapping readings, enabling others involved in the job to check readings and points where water infiltration is most concerning, along with other measurements. This, in turn, can guide techs on where to place drying equipment in real-time.


**For water restorations, software tools can aid with:**


- Capturing images during inspections, in turn, allows these to be attached and shared in detailed reports for homeowners, insurance companies, and other stakeholders.
- Easily incorporating photos within reports after water damage inspections.
- Generating reports based on water damage inspections that can be shared in various configurations, such as document file, PDF, rich text, web link or other file formats.
- Utilizing apps from a mobile device to efficiently assess what’s needed to restore a water-damaged structure.


Equipping field teams with the right tools for any water restoration job is important. Software apps that support these efforts help project managers better evaluate the project, ensuring that the right equipment and teams get to where they’re needed quickly.


### **Digital Documentation**


Properly documenting both damage and the work involved in a project is essential for a contractor to get fully paid for any water restoration. Software helps restorers do so more effectively. Not having proper documentation of work done to mitigate water damage runs the risk of invoices being unpaid and future work being denied by insurers.


Without modern technology, recording the necessary data to get paid can become a real headache, so having the capabilities to both evaluate a job and share information with other stakeholders seamlessly allows for a more efficient operation.


**A good water restoration software platform should record:**


- All types of information about a job can, in turn, be readily shared via communication tools.
- Contents of a building with water damage, including inventories and what was packed out.
- Floors plans that can accurately identify problem areas.
- Forms like contracts in digital format, along with the ability to sign these via e-signature and send these on to keep a record of work authorizations.
- Logs that note drying times and equipment used to deal with excessive moisture.
- Notes, photos, and video footage of any water damage.


Documentation that isn’t done correctly can also create red flags that delay payment. Capturing this data for all water mitigation activities for which a contractor bills means fewer headaches for a restoration company’s accounting staff.
