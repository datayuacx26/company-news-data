# Cleva vs Wise: Which Is Better for Sending Money to Nigeria from the U.S.?

With Cleva’s US remittance product recently launched, the Cleva vs Wise question is a question that’ll definitely come up where Nigerians in the United States trade tips on the cheapest, fastest way to send money home. Both platforms are trusted names in cross-border payments, both advertise transparent pricing, and both let you move dollars into naira in a matter of minutes. But when you look past the marketing and into what each app actually does for a Nigerian family depending on support from abroad, the Cleva vs Wise comparison stops being close. This guide breaks down fees, exchange rates, speed, and the extra features that matter most to the diaspora, so you can decide with confidence.


## Wise: A Quick Overview


Before getting into the full Cleva vs Wise breakdown, it helps to know what each platform actually is. Wise built its reputation on the mid-market exchange rate and upfront fee transparency, and it remains a solid general-purpose option for moving money between countries. Sending 1,000 USD to Nigeria the cheapest way, using funds already sitting in a Wise balance, costs 5.78 USD, and the platform shows the fee before you confirm anything ([Wise](https://wise.com/us/send-money/send-money-to-nigeria) ). Funding a transfer with a debit card through direct debit instead adds a fee of roughly 1.17 USD plus 0.8% of the amount sent ([RemitAnalyst](https://remitanalyst.com/send-money-to-nigeria-from-usa/remit-profiles/transferwise/) ). Delivery speed depends heavily on how you fund the transfer: some payments land in seconds, while direct debit transfers can take four to five business days to reach a Nigerian bank account. Wise is not built specifically for Nigerians. It serves over 40 currencies and millions of customers worldwide, which means the product is generalized rather than tailored to how the Nigerian diaspora actually spends: covering a parent’s electricity bill, topping up data for a sibling, or setting money aside for family goals back home.


## Cleva: A Quick Overview


Understanding Cleva’s design philosophy is the second half of the Cleva vs Wise picture. Cleva was built specifically for Nigerians and Africans in the diaspora, and that focus shows up in almost every part of the Cleva vs Wise comparison. With Cleva, U.S. users get a real USD account with a routing and account number, can fund transfers instantly with a debit card, and send naira directly to any Nigerian bank account in minutes with no conversion markup. Beyond moving money, Cleva lets you pay a loved one’s electricity bill or buy them mobile data directly from the U.S., something Wise simply does not offer. Every transfer also earns Cleva Points, which can be redeemed for real USD, and Cleva’s savings feature, Pots, lets you set money aside while earning 3% in rewards. This is the core of the Cleva vs Wise divide: Wise moves money, while Cleva is built around everything a Nigerian family abroad actually needs to do with that money.


## Cleva vs Wise: Fees and Exchange Rates


Fees are usually the first thing people compare, and it is where the Cleva vs Wise conversation gets interesting. Wise is transparent about its costs, but transparency does not always mean cheap: card-funded transfers carry a percentage-based fee on top of a flat charge, and that cost scales up as the amount you send grows. Cleva takes a different approach for the diaspora use case: transfers funded by debit card carry no conversion markup, and naira sent from your Cleva USD account to a Nigerian bank arrives without the layered fees that come with Wise’s card-funding route. On top of that, Cleva rewards every transfer with Cleva Points redeemable for real dollars, effectively giving money back on transactions where Wise gives nothing back at all.

## Cleva vs Wise: Speed and Reliability


Wise advertises that a majority of its transfers arrive in under 20 seconds, but that figure applies mainly to balance-funded transfers and does not reflect every route. Direct debit transfers, which many first-time U.S. users default to, can take four to five business days to clear before the recipient in Nigeria sees the naira. Cleva, by contrast, is built around one consistent promise for its core U.S.-to-Nigeria route: fund with a debit card and the recipient’s Nigerian bank account is credited within minutes, not days. For a family waiting on money for rent, school fees, or an emergency, that reliability is the difference between a stressful wait and instant peace of mind, and it’s another point in Cleva’s favor in the Cleva vs Wise comparison.


## Cleva vs Wise: Bill Payments That Actually Help Family Back Home


This is where the Cleva vs Wise comparison stops being close. Sending cash is useful, but it does not always solve the actual problem a family in Nigeria is facing. Cleva lets you pay for data or settle an electricity bill directly from the U.S., in real time, at a discount: 3% off data purchases and 1% off electricity payments. Instead of wiring naira and hoping it gets used the right way, you can settle the exact bill yourself, straight from your phone in the U.S. Wise has no equivalent feature. It is designed purely as a money-transfer and multi-currency account tool, so any bill payment still has to happen as a separate, manual step on the receiving end in Nigeria.


## Cleva vs Wise: Rewards and Long-Term Savings


Every transfer on Cleva earns Cleva Points, which are redeemable for real USD, turning routine sending into an activity that pays you back over time. Cleva also offers Pots, a savings feature that lets users set money aside and earn 3% in rewards while it sits there, something particularly useful for diaspora families saving toward school fees, a wedding, or a home project back in Nigeria. Wise has no comparable loyalty or savings-reward system; its value proposition begins and ends with the transfer itself. When you add up the rewards, the discounts on bills, and the savings features, the Cleva vs Wise comparison shows that Cleva is doing more with every dollar sent.
