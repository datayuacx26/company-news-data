# Upwork’s New $2.99 Withdrawal Fee: Don’t Let It Cost You Twice

Starting September 1, 2026, Upwork will apply a new $2.99 Upwork withdrawal fee to every Direct to U.S. Bank withdrawal made by freelancers whose tax address is outside the United States. The announcement caught many freelancers off guard, since Direct to U.S. Bank has long been the one free withdrawal option on the platform. For Nigerian freelancers, the new fee directly affects a withdrawal method millions have relied on for years.


Here’s what the new Upwork withdrawal fee actually says, why it’s happening now, and how you can stop it from quietly eating into your earnings.


## **What the new Upwork withdrawal fee actually says**


According to[Upwork’s own support documentation](https://support.upwork.com/hc/en-us/articles/227022468-Direct-to-U-S-Bank-timing-and-fees-explained) , starting September 1, 2026, a $2.99 flat fee will apply to every Direct to U.S. Bank withdrawal for freelancers whose tax address sits outside the United States. Withdrawals scheduled before that date still go through free of charge. Anything initiated on or after September 1 will incur the new fee. The fee applies regardless of how much you’re withdrawing.


This matters because Direct to U.S. Bank has long been the default withdrawal method for Nigerian freelancers holding a USD account, precisely because it used to be the one free option. Every other Upwork withdrawal route, Direct to Local Bank, wire transfer, PayPal, or Payoneer, already carries a fee ranging from roughly $1 to $50 per transfer. Direct to U.S. Bank was the exception. As of September 1, 2026, that exception is gone.


## **Why Upwork is introducing this withdrawal fee now**


Upwork frames the change as a cost-recovery measure, needed to keep offering Direct to U.S. Bank as a payout option for international freelancers at all. But the timing hasn’t gone unnoticed.[Freelancer commentary tracking Upwork’s fee history](https://hackernoon.com/upwork-fees-from-2016-to-2026-from-nada-to-prada) points out that this is far from the platform’s first fee increase in the past decade, and that frustration has been building steadily across freelancer communities as each new charge stacks on top of the last.


For Nigerian freelancers, Upwork fees already add to the cost of getting paid. An extra $2.99 might seem small on its own. But, it doesn’t stay small once you withdraw regularly.


## **How the new $2.99 Upwork fee affects your earnings**


Do the math and it adds up fast. Withdraw weekly, and that’s 52 withdrawals a year, meaning the new Upwork withdrawal fee alone costs you roughly $155 annually just to move your own money out of the platform. Withdraw biweekly instead, and you’re still looking at close to $78 a year. Freelancers often rely on smaller, frequent withdrawals to manage their cash flow. That $2.99 fee can quickly become a meaningful loss of income.


Freelancers are already talking about the change. Reactions on X have ranged from resignation to visible frustration, echoing a sentiment showing up across freelancer circles this week. That reaction is understandable. Nobody enjoys waking up to a new fee on money they already earned.


But the freelancers who come out ahead over the next few weeks won’t be the ones venting online, they’ll be the ones quietly updating their withdrawal setup before September 1 arrives.


## **Does receiving into a USD account like Cleva change this?**


A common assumption might be that receiving your withdrawal into a USD account with a US-based routing number gets you around the fee. It doesn’t. The U.S. address attached to that account number is simply the location of the partner bank’s processing branch, used to route your ACH and wire transfers domestically. It has nothing to do with your actual tax residency.


Upwork determines whether you pay the $2.99 fee based on the tax address registered on your Upwork profile, the same address tied to your W-8BEN tax form, not the bank account you’re withdrawing into. If that address is in Nigeria, you’ll be charged the fee starting September 1, 2026, no matter which USD account receives the funds. The only freelancers exempt from it are those whose Upwork profile is genuinely registered with a U.S. tax address.


For the vast majority of Nigerian freelancers, whose Upwork accounts are legitimately registered with a Nigerian address, that means the $2.99 fee is unavoidable at the point of withdrawal. What you can still control is what happens after that.
