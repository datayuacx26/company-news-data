## Why Proof of Account and Account Statements matter for visa applications


When you submit documents for visa applications, embassies and consulates want to verify a few things: that you have a legitimate bank account, that funds exist in that account and that your financial activity is consistent. The two documents that address all of this are:


- Proof of account — a document confirming that the account belongs to you, usually showing your name, account number, and the bank’s official stamp or letterhead.
- Account statement — a record of your transactions over a specified period, showing money coming in and going out.


For visa applications to destinations like the UK, Schengen countries, Canada, and the US, these documents are almost always required. According to[VFS Global’s documentation guidelines](https://www.vfsglobal.com/) , applicants are typically asked to provide bank statements covering the last three to six months.


Many remote workers earning in USD relied on fintech apps that didn’t always provide the documents they needed. As a result, visa applications became stressful because of missing paperwork, not insufficient funds.

## Other things to note when preparing financial documents for visa applications


While Cleva makes it easy to generate your documents, here are a few additional tips to strengthen the financial portion of your visa applications:


- Ensure your account has consistent activity in the months leading up to your visa application. Large single deposits with no prior history can raise flags.
- If you have multiple income sources or accounts, provide statements from all of them to paint a fuller financial picture for visa applications.
- Some visa applications require statements to be no older than 30 to 90 days. Generate your Cleva statement as close to your visa application submission date as possible.
- If your statement is in USD but the embassy requires amounts in NGN, you may need to include a currency conversion note. Check the specific visa application requirements beforehand.
