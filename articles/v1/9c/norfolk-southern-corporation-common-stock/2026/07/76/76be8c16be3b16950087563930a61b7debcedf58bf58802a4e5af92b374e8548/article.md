# From railfan to railroader: A childhood passion comes full circle

Long before he worked for Norfolk Southern, before engineering school and leadership roles, Brian Yeager was the kid asking his parents to turn the car around so he could watch a train pass. What began as a childhood fascination eventually became a career on the railroad he admired for years.


**Every weekend felt like a railroad adventure**


Growing up in Piscataway, New Jersey, trains were a constant part of Brian's childhood. Although he didn't come from a railroad family, his parents and grandparents encouraged his interest from an early age. Weekends often included riding New Jersey Transit with his grandfather, reading train books, watching railroad videos, and visiting Pennsylvania's Strasburg Railroad. Those experiences helped turn a childhood hobby into a lifelong passion.


"Every weekend we'd either buy a train book or a VHS tape and spend the afternoon reading or watching videos," Yeager recalled.

**Finding his way back to the railroad**


Like many childhood interests, railroading eventually faded into the background as school and other priorities took center stage. But while attending engineering school, Brian's interest was reignited when a Norfolk Southern recruiter visited campus.


"It brought back all these memories," Yeager said. "I've always enjoyed the work that I've done, and it's interesting taking a childhood passion and relating it to a career that I plan on being in until I retire."


Brian joined Norfolk Southern as a co-op student in 2013 and began his full-time career in 2014. During the past decade, he has helped advance technologies that strengthen safety and improve asset health, including[machine-vision portals](https://norfolksouthern.com/en/newsroom/story-yard/norfolk-southern-deploys-second-digital-train-inspection-portal-) ,[wheel integrity systems](https://norfolksouthern.com/en/newsroom/story-yard/introducing-the-wheel-integrity-system--ns--latest-safety-revolution) , AI-enabled analytics, and other data-driven inspection tools. Today, he serves as Senior Director of Mechanical Operations and Support. In his current role, Brian oversees teams that support freight-car reliability, inspection technologies, engineering programs, and wayside systems that help improve safety and operational performance across Norfolk Southern's network.


**Living the dream**


For Brian, the railroad never stopped feeling special.


"I can legitimately say I'm living my dream—my childhood dream—working for the railroad," he said.


Earlier this year, Brian had the opportunity to ride aboard[Union Pacific's Big Boy](https://norfolksouthern.com/en/newsroom/story-yard/norfolk-southern-collaborating-with-union-pacific-for-the-first-ever-big-boy-tour-of-the-northeast) steam locomotive. Watching families and railfans gather along the route reminded him of the same excitement trains sparked in him as a child.


**Seeing the Magic Through a Child's Eyes**


****


As a lifelong railfan, Brian says riding[Union Pacific's Big Boy](https://www.up.com/about-us/history/steam/schedule) gave him a chance to experience the same excitement he felt as a child. Standing on the rear platform and waving to families gathered along the route, he was reminded of the joy trains can inspire and why so many young people are drawn to railroading in the first place. For a child who once made his parents turn the car around so he could watch a train in the distance, the opportunity to ride aboard Big Boy was the kind of experience his younger self could only have dreamed about.


"It was so cool to see all those happy kids. I mean, it was just thousands of happy people, town after town. Nobody was angry. Nobody was upset. Everybody was just happy. It was great," said Yeager.


**A full-circle family story**


Today, trains remain part of Brian's family story. Both he and his wife work at Norfolk Southern, and their daughter is already developing her own interest in trains. She enjoys watching passing trains and now plays with the same wooden train set Brian used as a child.


Seeing those interests emerge in the next generation has given Brian a greater appreciation for the support his parents and grandparents provided when he was young.


"You never know what is going to drive your kid," he said. "Encourage them when they're young. Embrace their interests and help them explore them. You never know where it might lead."
