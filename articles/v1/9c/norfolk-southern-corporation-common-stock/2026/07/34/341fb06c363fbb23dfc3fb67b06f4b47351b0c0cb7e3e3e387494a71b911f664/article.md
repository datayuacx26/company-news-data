# The Yinglings: six generations on the rails

In Altoona, Pa., generations of the Yingling family have worked across the region’s rail shops for more than a century. Today, that legacy continues at[Juniata Locomotive Shop](https://norfolksouthern.com/en/newsroom/story-yard/norfolk-southern-powers-past-1-000th-locomotive-modernization) , where David “Dave” Yingling Jr., machinist and team leader at Norfolk Southern, is part of that ongoing story.


A fifth-generation railroader, Yingling hired on with Conrail in 1993 and has built a career spanning more than three decades across multiple shops and roles. That legacy now extends to the next generation, as his twin sons, Joshua and Jacob Yingling, both machinists, represent the sixth generation working for the railroad.


“I was probably 12 when I first walked into the shops, and I was just amazed,” Yingling said. “The scale, the efficiency, everything about it just stuck with me.”


**From early curiosity to service and skill**


That early experience sparked a fascination with machines that shaped his path forward. Yingling went on to serve in the U.S. Navy as a hull technician, where he developed welding and mechanical skills through hands-on experience.


Within weeks of returning home from active duty, he began his machinist career in Altoona, putting those skills to work in an environment that felt familiar from a young age.


His curiosity continues to shape the work as evolving systems, software, and diagnostics change how equipment is maintained and returned to service.


**A legacy that still guides the work**


Railroading spans every generation of the Yingling family, with each generation shaping how the next approaches the work.


On the overhaul line, that perspective is reflected in how locomotives are stripped, rebuilt, and tested before returning to service. Each stage depends on coordination, precision, and trust across the team to ensure equipment is returned safely and reliably.


“It takes everybody working together,” Yingling said. “If we don’t get it right, it impacts more than just us. It impacts the railroad and the country.”


Over time, those lessons were reinforced through family stories. Yingling’s grandfather often reminded the family that the railroad plays an essential role beyond the shop, supporting industries and communities across the network.


Dave's uncle Denny (left), grandfather Clair (center) and dad David Sr. (back right).


**Putting leadership into practice**


As a team leader, Yingling brings that mindset into how he works with his team. His father, a former carman gang leader, prepared him for the realities of representing both frontline employees and leadership expectations.


“He told me you’ve got to be able to handle both sides,” Yingling said. “You have to respect people, stay professional, and make sure messages flow either way.”


In practice, that guidance shows up in his focus on safety, accountability, and communication on the overhaul line.


“I tell the team, if something’s not safe or there’s a better way, speak up,” he said. “We’re going to address it, and we’re going to fix it.”


**Where generations meet**


The[legacy](https://norfolksouthern.com/en/about-us/our-history) feels most tangible when Yingling sees his sons working in the same facility where earlier generations built their careers.


“When I walk through the shop and see them working, it makes me the proudest dad in the world,” he said. “It shows the railroad really does build something across generations.”


For the Yingling family, the railroad has meant more than steady work. It has created[opportunity](https://norfolksouthern.com/en/careers/find-your-future) , shaped values, and kept generations connected to their community. That continuity, from the earliest days of railroading in Altoona to today’s evolving technology and shop operations, reflects something deeper than tradition.


It reflects a shared commitment to doing the work right, adapting as the industry advances, and passing that standard on to the next generation.
