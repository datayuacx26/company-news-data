## Why Intercompany Transactions Derail the Financial Close


Intercompany accounting can be an extremely complex and risk-prone area of the Record-to-Report (R2R) cycle. It’s a critical activity that spans record-to-report, from initiating transactions throughout the period through to consolidation, yet intercompany is often mistaken for a consolidation exercise. It involves a high volume of transactions that must be recorded, reconciled, and eliminated perfectly. A breakdown in the process can quickly create a domino effect of errors and delays that ripple through the entire financial close.


Teams need solutions that unify intercompany activities from transaction creation through to elimination. The ultimate goal is a


**touchless accounting framework** , where a central system manages the end-to-end process, doing the heavy lifting and allowing your team to focus on managing exceptions or more critical work. If you're not there yet, you're likely seeing some of the following warning signs.


The 10 Symptoms of an Outdated Intercompany Process


## 1. Spreadsheets Drive Your Intercompany Journal Entries


If your team lives in spreadsheets to create and manage intercompany journals, you’re exposed to significant operational risk. Manual data entry is prone to errors, lacks version control, and offers no clear audit trail, making the reconciliation process a painful exercise in forensic accounting.


## 2. Dispute Resolution Only Happens During Period-End


When disagreements between entities and their corresponding imbalances are only identified and addressed during the close period, it creates significant strain for accounting teams. This reactive approach increases the likelihood of delays as teams scramble to investigate and resolve issues that could have been eliminated. Proactive, continuous resolution is key.


## 3. Tax Compliance and Invoicing Require Manual Intervention


Managing the intricate web of global tax rules, transfer pricing policies, and invoicing requirements manually is a recipe for compliance failures. Without automated logic to govern these transactions, your team is forced to spend valuable time on low-value tasks, inviting risk and inefficiency.


## 4. Disparate ERPs Create Data Blind Spots


A business-first growth strategy often results in a patchwork of different ERP systems across entities. This creates data silos and blind spots, making it impossible to get a single, harmonized view of intercompany activities. An ERP-agnostic platform is essential to unify data and create one source of truth.


## 5. Consolidation Highlights Massive Out-of-Balance Exceptions


Do your consolidations consistently reveal significant intercompany imbalances? This is a classic symptom of a broken intercompany process. The frantic, end-of-month scramble to reconcile accounts is a clear sign that underlying issues are not being addressed before consolidation.


## 6. Organizational Expansion Breaks Your Current Operating Model


Manual, people-dependent processes simply cannot scale to handle the increased volume and complexity that comes with expansion. If every new entity adds a disproportionate amount of manual work to your team's plate, your operating model is not sustainable.


## 7. Intercompany Operations Live Outside Your R2R Workflow


Your team loses efficiency when they must constantly toggle between isolated tools, spreadsheets, and email to manage intercompany processes. Integrating these operations directly into the broader Record-to-Report workflow is critical for visibility and control.


## 8. Working Capital is Locked Up


Slow, inefficient intercompany operations directly impact cash flow. When balances remain unsettled for long periods, it ties up working capital that could be used for strategic initiatives. Optimizing your intercompany strategy is not just an accounting task; it's a financial imperative.


## 9. Foreign Exchange Translations Consume Days of Effort


For global organizations, the manual calculation of FX translations across complex hierarchies is an enormous drain on resources. This painstaking work is often error-prone and introduces unnecessary delays into the financial close and consolidation cycle.


## 10. Audit Trails are Fragmented and Unreliable


Without a centralized system, producing a complete and reliable audit trail for intercompany transactions is nearly impossible. Managing compliance and proving materiality is a constant struggle when evidence is scattered across spreadsheets, emails, and disconnected systems.
