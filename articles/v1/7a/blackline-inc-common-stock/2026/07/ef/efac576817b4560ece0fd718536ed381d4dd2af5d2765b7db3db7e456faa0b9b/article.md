# The Practical Blueprint for an Unstoppable Finance Function

Key Takeaways


##


• Strategy-Led Technology: CFOs must align with the CEO’s vision and define specific executive KPIs before evaluating any technology solutions.


• The Power Couple: The CFO-CIO partnership is no longer optional. Co-owning the data strategy is the only way to eliminate the silos that make the current financial scale "physically impossible" to manage manually.


• Hiring for the Future: Finance now requires "finance technologists"—hybrid professionals who combine Big Four accounting rigor with a systems-first, automation-oriented mindset.


• AI as a Collaborator: Treat AI as the "fifth person at the table." It provides the quantitative heavy lifting, allowing the executive team to focus on qualitative judgment and strategic context.


• The 24/7 Function: An unstoppable function uses automation and AI to process and analyze data around the clock, ensuring the business moves forward even when the team is offline.


## Your Practical Blueprint


A few weeks ago, I shared my perspective on "[The Unstoppable CFO's Agenda for 2026](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/unstoppable-finance-function-more-than-just-numbers-blackline-9irjc/) ", outlining the core mandates finance leaders must embrace to thrive in an increasingly complex landscape. While that agenda defined the "what," I’ve spent a lot of time recently discussing the "how."


Building a truly unstoppable finance function—one that serves as the strategic engine of the enterprise—is not a software project. It is a fundamental evolution of how we align with leadership, how we partner across the C-suite, and how we redefine the very DNA of our teams. To move from a static agenda to a high-velocity operation, finance leaders need a practical blueprint.


## Step 1: Strategic Alignment Before System Selection


It is tempting for CFOs to start their transformation journey by looking at tools. But becoming an unstoppable leader begins long before you evaluate a single piece of code. It starts with deep, strategic listening.


Historically, a CFO’s value was found in the "what"—the accuracy and timeliness of the close. Today, my time is overwhelmingly spent on the "why" and the "how"—coordination, communication, and strategic partnership. The first step in this blueprint is to sit down with your CEO to anchor yourself in the one-, three-, and five-year roadmap.


From there, you must act as a bridge. I meet with each member of the executive team to ask two fundamental questions: "What is your role in executing this corporate strategy, and what specific data points do you need to measure your success?" You cannot build an effective financial infrastructure in a vacuum. You must first create a complete inventory of the[metrics that drive decision-making](https://www.blackline.com/blog/the-cfos-mandate-for-mastering-working-capital/) across the business. Only once you know what needs to be measured can you begin to build the systems to measure it.


## Step 2: The New Indispensable Partnership


Once you have identified the data requirements of the business, the focus shifts to infrastructure. This is where the partnership between the CFO and the CIO becomes the most critical relationship in the building.


In the past, many organizations operated successfully within silos. If the data didn't flow correctly between Finance and IT, we used human horsepower to bridge the gap; manual reconciliations, complex spreadsheets, and long hours to stitch together a narrative. Back then, that brute-force approach was enough.


In my view, that is now a physical impossibility. The sheer volume and velocity of data in the typical enterprise means that if you aren't integrated, you are stoppable. The conversation with your CIO should be: " *Here is the data the business needs to win. What architecture do we need to co-own to deliver this seamlessly?* " Finance can no longer be a "customer" of IT; the two functions must be co-architects of the company’s data strategy.


## Step 3: Recruiting the Finance Technologist


A new blueprint for finance requires a new kind of builder. The profile of the ideal finance professional has changed more in the last five years than in the previous twenty.


We still value the work ethic, technical diligence, and foundational expertise that comes from a Big Four background. However, those are now the baseline, not the ceiling. To build an unstoppable function, we are now prioritizing "finance technologists."


These are professionals who are inherently tech-oriented and automation-minded. They don't just know how to record a transaction; they understand the systems and data flows that make that transaction possible. They are as comfortable discussing data architecture as they are discussing GAAP. This shift in talent is essential because the expectation for strategic insight now trickles down through the entire organization. We need people who can bridge the gap between financial principles and new, automated systems.


## Step 4: AI as the "Fifth Person" at the Table


The conversation around AI in accounting is often framed by fear or skepticism. I tell my team the opposite: "Don't be afraid of AI. The only way it will take your job is if you refuse to adopt it."


I view AI as the ultimate augmenter of human capability. When our leadership team discusses guidance or complex financial modeling, I think of AI as the "fifth person" at the table. It is a counterpart capable of processing more variables—macro trends, historical patterns, and supply chain shifts—than any human could.

To be truly unstoppable, your finance function must operate 24/7, even if your people do not.


This is the goal of the blueprint. By investing in an agentic digital workforce powered by AI like Verity, you ensure that data is being processed, anomalies are being flagged, and insights are being generated around the clock. If your function stops moving the moment everyone goes home for the night, you are, by definition, stoppable.
