At its core, a **remittance** is the transfer of funds from a buyer to a supplier to settle an outstanding debt. In B2B finance, the term encompasses both the payment itself and the accompanying remittance advice, the documentation that explains exactly how the payment should be applied across the ledger.


When a customer pays a single invoice in full, the process is straightforward. The complexity arrives when enterprise customers send bulk payments covering dozens of invoices while taking early payment discounts, applying credit memos, and issuing short pays. In these scenarios the remittance advice becomes the master key to accurate cash application. Without it, an AR team is left reconciling a lump sum against a ledger with no map.


Remittance processing is the point in the accounts receivable cycle where incoming cash is matched to open invoices, and its efficiency governs both how fast revenue becomes deployable working capital and how accurately credit exposure is measured. Operationally, it is the workflow of capturing remittance advice, normalizing it across formats and channels, and matching payments to open invoices, including the deductions, discounts, short pays, and multi-invoice payments that have to be resolved before anything posts to the ledger. Modern remittance processing handles that complexity by combining Vision AI for document extraction with agentic AI that interprets context, resolves exceptions, and matches payments across ERP data. Built to be ERP-agnostic, it ingests from email, vendor portals, and bank feeds into a single data engine rather than depending on one ledger's native logic, so slow or inaccurate handling no longer suppresses liquidity or inflates DSO.

Accounts receivable represents a substantial portion of an organization's working capital, and how efficiently a company processes remittances dictates how much of that capital is actually usable. Remittance processing sits directly on that gap.


**Unapplied cash** is a silent drag on financial agility. When payments arrive but the AR team cannot immediately match them to open invoices because remittance data is missing or complex, that cash sits idle. It cannot be reinvested, it artificially inflates DSO, and it distorts the view of customer credit risk because paid accounts still appear to carry open balances.


DSO is the signal here, not the goal. The goal is working capital. By mastering remittance processing, finance teams free trapped cash back into the business, replenish customer credit limits accurately, and keep the broader invoice-to-cash cycle operating without friction. ## What Are the Most Common B2B Remittance Methods?


The shift toward digital finance has expanded how businesses send and receive money. Understanding these six common remittance methods is the first step toward optimizing accounts receivable operations.


**ACH transfers** : Automated Clearing House payments are a staple for domestic B2B transactions. While cost-effective, ACH payments often arrive completely decoupled from their remittance advice, which is typically sent separately by email.


**Wire transfers** : Used frequently for high-value or cross-border payments, wires are fast but often lack the field space to carry detailed remittance information, forcing the advice into a separate channel.


**Virtual credit cards** : Increasingly popular for their security and rebate potential, virtual cards shift cost and effort onto the supplier. AR teams must log into web portals, process the card manually, and extract the remittance details by hand, and the interchange fees deducted from each payment mean the supplier collects less than the full invoice amount.


**Electronic Data Interchange (EDI)** : Standardized formats like EDI 820 transmit payment and remittance data together. While highly efficient, EDI requires significant IT setup and is usually reserved for a company's largest trading partners.


**Lockbox** : Bank lockbox services aggregate incoming payments and deliver batched files to the supplier. The files vary in quality and structure across banks, and image-based lockbox data still requires accurate extraction before it can be matched.


**Paper checks** : Despite digital advances, paper checks remain stubbornly persistent. They require manual handling, scanning, and data entry, slowing the Invoice-to-Cash cycle and introducing transcription errors.


## How Can Finance Teams Overcome Remittance Challenges?


Even skilled AR teams face systemic hurdles when processing payments. The most glaring is **decoupled data** . Customers frequently send an ACH payment to the bank but email the remittance advice to a shared AR inbox in a different format entirely, whether a PDF, an Excel spreadsheet, or a pasted table in the email body.


Relying on manual labor to cross-reference disaggregated data against ERP systems creates a severe audit risk and strains static finance teams facing growing transaction volumes. Modernizing remittance processing establishes a centralized, single source of truth that strengthens internal controls, reduces risk, and provides a scalable financial foundation.


Standardizing data ingestion is the foundation. Consolidating all incoming remittance advice into a single digital stream removes the format-by-format scramble. An **ERP-agnostic** strategy ensures that regardless of the underlying ledger, AR workflows stay agile and centralized rather than hard-coded to one system's native logic.


The decisive move is going beyond basic Vision AI and deterministic automation toward **agentic AI** . Vision AI reads a document. ## Metrics That Signal Remittance Health


• **Days Sales Outstanding (DSO)** : The average days to collect after a sale. It is a signal of working capital health, not the objective itself. Rising DSO often traces back to unapplied cash, not slow customers.


• **Straight-Through Processing (STP) rate** : The share of payments matched and applied with no manual intervention. Higher STP means faster cash and lower cost to collect.


• **Unapplied Cash Balance** : The dollar value of received payments not yet matched to invoices. A direct measure of trapped working capital.


• **Cost to Collect** : The fully loaded cost of applying cash and pursuing collections. Manual remittance handling is a primary driver, and automation compresses it.
