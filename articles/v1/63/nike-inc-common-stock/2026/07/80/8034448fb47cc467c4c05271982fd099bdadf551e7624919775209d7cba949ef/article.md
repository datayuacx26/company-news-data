# The Making of Aero-FIT: The Material Innovation Keeping Footballers Cooler on the Pitch

**A single Nike Aero-FIT jersey begins as recycled yarn on a spool.** Before it reaches an athlete on the world’s biggest stage, it will move through a specialized creation process — knit on a proprietary circular machine; dyed in precise lots; and extracted, finished, sublimated, cut, printed and hand-stitched panel by panel.


Each step exists to protect the performance built into the material itself, a highly texturized knit that combines open and closed mesh zones to move air across the body, lift the fabric off the skin, and deliver more than twice the airflow of legacy Nike fabrics. That’s not nothing during a summer of sport that’s delivered some of the hottest conditions footballers have ever seen.


Fitting, then, that[Aero-FIT](https://about.nike.com/en/newsroom/releases/nike-aero-fit-official-images) is also Nike’s first elite performance apparel made from 100 percent textile waste. Nike’s contract manufacturing suppliers use a textile-to-textile system enabled by advanced chemical recycling, creating a process that produces yarn comparable to virgin material.


That material comes together through a blend of technology and human expertise, yarn and machine, hand and eye, made for the[athletes taking the pitch](https://about.nike.com/en/newsroom/collections/nike-football-unveils-2026-federation-kits-featuring-aero-fit-performance-cooling-technology) and the fans standing with them in the same colors.
