# What Building Voice AI for 160 Florida Governments Taught Us

Ahead of one Florida conference this summer, we did something slightly unhinged. We built a working voice AI demo for 160 Florida governments. Not slides. Live agents, each configured from a real jurisdiction's own website and service structure: cities, counties, sheriffs, tax collectors, clerks of court, water and transit districts.


Building that many in one campaign is a strange and useful vantage point. A few things became obvious.


##


Local government is more alike than its org charts admit


Every jurisdiction believes its structure is unique. In aggregate, they are variations on a theme. A front door that fields general questions. A handful of high-volume service departments. A set of constitutional or elected offices with narrow, transactional call patterns: the tax collector fields payments and deadlines, the clerk fields records and filings, the supervisor of elections fields registration and ballots. The org charts differ. The routing problem does not.


##


The routing is the hard part, not the answers


For most departments, the answers are already written down somewhere: hours, fees, forms, eligibility. The genuinely hard part is getting a caller to the right one of forty possible destinations without a fourteen-option phone tree. That is where legacy systems fail and where a main line that understands plain language earns its keep. The knowledge is public. The navigation is the product.


##


Special districts are the quiet stress test


Cities and counties have Wikipedia pages and tidy websites. A regional transit district or a housing authority often does not, and its departments do not map to the usual buckets. Configuring those forced the system to handle the messy middle of local government, the agencies that are real, busy, and nobody's tidy example. If it works for a council of governments, it works for your parks department.


##


Why we build the demo, not just the deck


There is a reason we stood up real agents instead of a booth video. A demo you can call is a claim you can check. Anyone could dial their own government's line and hear how it handled their actual questions. That is the same standard we would ask any jurisdiction to hold a vendor to: not "watch this happy path," but "here is a live number, try to break it."
