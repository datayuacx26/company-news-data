# Making the Budget Case for Citywide Voice AI

Voice AI dies in budget meetings for a simple reason. It gets pitched as a vision instead of a line item. Finance directors have heard "transformational" before. Here is how to frame it so it survives markup.


##


Lead with staffing, not technology


The strongest case is not "AI is the future." It is "this deflects a majority of our routine calls and gives back the equivalent of several positions." Convert your deflected volume into full-time equivalents using real contact-center math, occupancy and shrinkage, not minutes over an eight-hour day. In Sumter County, 72 percent deflection across roughly 12,000 monthly calls pencils out to about five FTEs. (The math is in[the five FTEs post](https://www.effigov.com/blog/voice-ai-fte-savings-phone-system) .)


##


Put it next to the loaded cost of the alternative


The comparison that lands is not voice AI versus nothing. It is voice AI versus the fully loaded cost of the roles it backfills or the overtime it prevents, plus the recruiting and turnover you avoid on hard-to-fill front-line positions. When the annual cost of the system is a fraction of the staffing it absorbs, the argument makes itself.


##


Count the things you cannot hire for anyway


Many of these positions are chronically open. You are not necessarily cutting people. You are covering work you cannot staff: nights, weekends, the second language, the storm surge. A 24/7 line in English and Spanish is capacity you would struggle to hire even with the budget in hand.


##


Bring proof, not adjectives


Finance believes reference deployments and numbers. Walk in with a deflection rate, named live jurisdictions, and monthly call volume. EffiGov handles more than 12,000 resident calls a month across live deployments including Sumter County, Florida and Huber Heights, Ohio, both north of 70 percent handled without staff. That is a checkable claim, which is exactly what a skeptical budget office wants.


##


Anticipate the two objections


Accuracy and security. Have the answers ready: real-time QA that catches wrong answers during the call, and a security posture you can point to rather than describe. (See[accuracy](https://www.effigov.com/blog/government-voice-ai-accuracy-hallucinations) and[resident data security](https://www.effigov.com/blog/government-voice-ai-security-resident-data) .)
