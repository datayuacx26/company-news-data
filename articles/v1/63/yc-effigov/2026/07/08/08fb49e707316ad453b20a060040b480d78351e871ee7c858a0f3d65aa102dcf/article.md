# Context Sharing: Why Residents Should Never Repeat Themselves to a Second AI Agent

Ask a vendor how their voice AI "transfers" a call and watch the hand-waving start. The handoff is the most important unglamorous feature in a multi-agent phone system, and most systems do it badly.


Here is the problem it solves. A resident calls the county about a dog running loose, then remembers they also never renewed the rabies license. That is two departments, one call. In a typical phone tree, the second topic means a cold transfer: new hold music, new person, start over. On the resident's third repetition, they hang up and drive down to the office. You just converted a phone call into a counter visit.


##


What context sharing actually is


Context sharing is a handoff between two AI agents that carries the caller's identity and intent with it. When the main line passes a caller to the Animal Services agent, the second agent already has the thread: who is on the line, what they have said, where they are in the task. It reads as one continuous conversation, because it is.


This is not a warm transfer to a human, which is a different thing: the agent calling a staff member, briefing them, then bridging the caller. Context sharing is agent to agent, and it is live today. It is what lets a county run six specialist agents that behave like one organization.


##


Why it is hard, and why it matters


Most multi-agent systems are really one prompt pretending to be many, or many bots with no shared memory. The first cannot go deep in any department. The second makes the resident the integration layer, re-keying their information at every hop. Context sharing is the harder middle path: distinct expert agents that pass state cleanly.


The payoff is measurable. Calls that would have bounced or dropped instead resolve. In Sumter County, the handoff is why a caller can move from the main line to Animal Services to a license renewal without ever repeating themselves, and part of why the county handles 72 percent of its roughly 12,000 monthly calls without a person stepping in.


##


How to test it


On any demo, do this. Give the AI a task in one department, then abruptly switch to another. Then switch back. Watch whether it remembers. If it makes you repeat your address, imagine your most frustrated resident doing the same at 8 a.m. on a Monday.
