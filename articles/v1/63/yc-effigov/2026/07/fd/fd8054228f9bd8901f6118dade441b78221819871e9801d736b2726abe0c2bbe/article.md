# One Number, Every Department: What Citywide Voice AI Actually Looks Like

Most AI phone pitches to local government automate a single line. A 311 bot here, a utilities FAQ there. It demos well and it deflects almost nothing, because residents do not call one department at a time. They call the county.


Citywide voice AI treats the phone system as one organization, not a stack of unrelated bots. Three pieces make that work.


##


The main line answers first


Every call lands at one intelligent front door. In Sumter County, Florida, that is the Citizens Information Center, the CIC. It answers general questions outright, and when a caller needs a specialist it routes them, in English or Spanish, around the clock. Nothing rolls to voicemail because it is 4:55 on a Friday.


##


A specialist agent sits behind each department


The main line is not trying to know everything. Behind it sit department agents, each with its own knowledge base, hours, and routing: Animal Services, Building, Veterans, Fire, and the Community Responder line. Sumter runs six departments this way. Each agent is an expert in one thing, which is exactly how a good front desk works.


##


Context travels with the caller


Here is the part vendors skip. When the CIC hands a caller to the Building agent, the handoff carries who is calling and what they need. The second agent picks up mid-thought. The resident does not re-explain their permit number to a fresh robot. We call it context sharing, and it is the difference between routing a call and transferring a headache. (More on that in[context sharing](https://www.effigov.com/blog/context-sharing-ai-agents-local-government) .)


##


Why whole-organization beats one department


Deflection compounds when the front door is shared. A single-department bot can only help the fraction of callers who dialed the right number and asked the right thing. A main line helps everyone, then sends the rest to the right place with context intact. That is why the number that matters is jurisdiction-wide.


In Sumter County, the system now handles 72 percent of roughly 12,000 calls a month without a staff member picking up. Not 72 percent of one line. Seventy-two percent of the county's calls. Huber Heights, Ohio runs the same model across multiple departments and lands north of 70 percent.


##


What it takes


You do not need to boil the ocean. You need a main line, a way to stand up department agents quickly, and real handoffs between them. Start at the front door, prove deflection, then add departments one at a time. (See[the 90-day rollout](https://www.effigov.com/blog/countywide-voice-ai-rollout-plan) .)
