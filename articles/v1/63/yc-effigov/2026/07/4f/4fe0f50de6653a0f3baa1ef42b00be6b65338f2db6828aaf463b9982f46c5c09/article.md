# Real-Time Oversight: How Voice AI Catches a Wrong Answer Mid-Call

Ask a voice AI vendor how they ensure accuracy and most will say some version of "every call is recorded and we review the transcripts." That sounds responsible. It is not oversight. It is a paper trail.


By the time a staffer reads the transcript, the resident has already hung up and acted on whatever the AI told them. The wrong permit fee has been paid, the wrong office visited, the deadline missed. Reviewing it later documents the failure. It does not prevent it.


##


What real-time oversight actually is


Real-time oversight is a second process running alongside the call. It listens to every live conversation, evaluates each AI response against the approved knowledge base as it happens, and watches for three things: an answer that does not match approved content, a confident answer with no source, and audible caller confusion.


When it catches one, it acts during the call. It can correct the response, surface the verified information, or escalate to a human, all while the resident is still on the line. The caller never hears the answer that would have been wrong.


##


Why "during the call" is the whole point


On a website, a wrong answer is one signal among many. On the phone, the AI's answer is the only answer the resident hears, and they act on it immediately. That collapses the window for correction to zero. Any oversight that operates on a delay, nightly transcript review or a weekly QA sample, is oversight for the record, not for the resident.


This is the line between a demo that sounds good and a system you can put in front of 12,000 calls a month. Sumter County runs at that volume and handles 72 percent without staff, which only holds up because the answers are trustworthy in real time.


##


The question that exposes the difference


When you evaluate a vendor, ask one thing: when the AI gets something wrong, when does your system find out, and what does it do? If the answer is "we see it in the transcript later," you have your answer. Real oversight finds out in the same second the resident would have.
