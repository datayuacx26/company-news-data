## **How Location Sharing on Google Maps Actually Works**


[When you share your location](https://www.life360.com/blog/temporary-location-sharing?icid=blog_how-to-share-location-with-family_us) through the Maps app, your current location broadcasts to whoever you've chosen in real time. They open the app on their end and see exactly where you are on the map.


The person receiving your location information can also see your name, your profile photo, your device's battery level, and your estimated time of arrival if they've set up a notification. That last one is genuinely useful. No more ten minutes away texts that turn into thirty-minute waits.


One thing worth knowing upfront: Location sharing works even when Location History is turned off on your account. Those are two separate settings, and turning one off does not affect the other.

## **How to Share Your Location on Android or iPhone**


The steps are nearly identical across both platforms, so follow along no matter what device you're on.


### **Step 1: Open the Maps App**


Open the Google Maps app and make sure you are signed into the correct Gmail account. If you have multiple accounts on your phone, double-check which one is active before you start sharing.


### **Step 2: Tap Your Profile Picture**


In the top right corner of the Maps app, tap your profile picture or initial. A menu will appear with several options.


### **Step 3: Tap Location Sharing**


Select Location sharing from the menu. If you see a warning message here, it may mean location sharing is restricted by your account settings, your region, or your age. Google restricts users under 18 from sharing for more than 24 hours, and parents can manage those settings through Family Link.


### **Step 4: Tap New Share**


Hit the New share button to begin. This is where you choose how long your location stays visible.


### **Step 5: Set Your Time Limit**


You can choose a specific duration anywhere from 15 minutes to one full day. You can also select Until you turn this off, which keeps sharing active until you manually tap stop sharing. Choose whichever fits the situation.


### **Step 6: Select Who to Share With**


A list of your contacts will appear. Scroll through or search to find the person you want. Tap their name and then tap Share to send it.


That's it. Your contact will get a notification and can open your live location in the Maps app on their end.

## **How to Stop Sharing Your Location**


Stopping is just as simple as starting. Open Google Maps, tap your profile picture, and select Location sharing. You will see everyone you are currently sharing with listed there. Tap the name of the person you want to stop sharing with, then tap Stop or tap stop sharing to confirm.


Your location information disappears from their Maps view immediately.


If you set a time limit when you started, sharing will end automatically when that time runs out. You do not have to do anything extra.


## **How to Share Your Estimated Time of Arrival**


If you're navigating somewhere and want someone to follow your progress, Maps has a built-in option for sharing your estimated time of arrival without giving ongoing access to your full location.


While actively navigating, tap the small arrow or the up arrow at the bottom of the screen. Look for the option that says something along the lines of Share trip progress. Tap it, choose your contact, and send. They will be able to see where you are along your route and your estimated arrival time without you sharing your full location history.


This is a great option for situations where you want a one-time update rather than ongoing access.


## **Sharing From a Computer**


If you are on a desktop or laptop instead of your phone, you can still access location sharing through Google Maps in a browser.


Open and sign in to your Gmail account. Click the three horizontal lines in the top left corner to open the menu. Select Location sharing, choose your duration and contact, and click Share. The process is essentially the same, just on a bigger screen.


Keep in mind that sharing from a computer uses that computer's location, not your phone's. If you want someone to track your movement while you're traveling, sharing from your phone is the better option.

## **Common Problems and Quick Fixes**


**Your contact cannot see your location after you shared it.** Make sure their Gmail address is correct in your contacts, and confirm that location services are enabled on your phone. On an Android device, you can check this in your settings under Location. On an iPhone, look under Privacy and Security, then Location Services, then Maps.


**You're getting a warning message when you open Location sharing.** This typically means one of a few things: location sharing is not available in your region, your Google Workspace account has restrictions in place, or your account has age-based limitations applied. Review your account settings or check with your account administrator if this is a work or school account.


**Location sharing is draining your battery.** Location apps use battery because they keep GPS active. Restarting the Maps app can help if something seems off. You can also choose a shorter sharing duration so your phone is not broadcasting continuously longer than needed.


**You want to share your location but do not want to use the Maps app.** Some people prefer to share location through Google Messages instead. You can send a static location pin through a text, though keep in mind that a pin shows where you are at that one moment, not an ongoing live location. For a live update, the Maps app is the tool for the job.
