# How to turn off call forwarding on any device, iPhone, Android

To turn off call forwarding on most US landlines and phones, dial


***73** for Verizon,


***73#** for AT&T, and


**##21#** for T-Mobile. Then wait for the confirmation tone.


**On an iPhone** , go to Settings > Apps > Phone > Call Forwarding and toggle it off.


**On Android** , open the Phone app > Settings > Calls > Call forwarding and turn off every option. If forwarding turned on without you setting it up, that typically means forwarding was left on by a previous user, enabled by your carrier, or switched on by a code dialed by accident.


This guide walks you through the steps to turn off call forwarding so you[never miss an essential call again](https://www.quo.com/product/call-management/never-miss-a-call) . Then, we introduce a better alternative to call forwarding so you can provide an excellent experience for both customers and team members.


##


How to turn off call forwarding using star codes


Using star codes, turning off call forwarding is a straightforward, if tedious, process:


### On a landline or traditional phone


Landlines are the simple case. One code works across essentially every US provider, because deactivation is a standardized vertical service code, not a carrier-specific one.


1. Lift the handset and listen for a dial tone, so you know the line is live.
2. Dial *73. On a rotary or pulse-dial phone, dial 1173 instead. (Some older switches accept 73# in place of *73.)
3. Wait for the confirmation — usually two short tones — indicating forwarding is off.


### On a mobile phone


On mobile, the code depends on your carrier, because GSM carriers and Verizon use different systems.


1. Check if your phone has a dial tone, so you know if it’s working.
2. Different phones have different codes to turn off call forwarding:


1. For[Verizon phones](https://www.verizon.com/articles/verizon-unlimited-plans/call-forwarding-everything-you-need-to-know/) , **** dial ***73**
2. For[AT&T phones](https://www.att.com/support/article/u-verse-voice/KM1000459/) , **** dial ***73#**
3. For[T-Mobile phones](https://www.t-mobile.com/support/plans-features/self-service-short-codes) , dial **##21#**
4. For other carriers, try the universal disable call forwarding code **##002#** — just keep in mind it may stop your calls from reaching voicemail.


3. Wait for a confirmation tone, usually a few short beeps, indicating you’ve turned off call forwarding.


##


How to turn off call forwarding on iPhones


Here’s how to remove[call forwarding on an iPhone](https://www.quo.com/blog/call-forwarding-iphone/) :


1. Navigate to **Settings** , then **Apps** .
2. Select **Phone** .
3. Choose **Call Forwarding** from the menu.
4. Switch the **Call Forwarding** feature to the off position.


**💡 Pro tip:** If you don’t see the toggle, it could be because your carrier manages forwarding server-side. Use the carrier app or portal instead, or try the star codes for your carrier.


##


How to turn off call forwarding on Android phones


If you have[call forwarding on an Android](https://www.quo.com/blog/call-forwarding-android/) device enabled, follow these steps to turn off forwarding. These should work on Samsung Galaxy devices and other Android phones:


1. Launch the Phone application.
2. Select the three-dot menu in the top right corner and choose **Phone Settings**
3. Choose **Calls** or **Supplementary services.** On Google Pixel phones, tap **Calling accounts** and select the correct SIM card.
4. Choose **Call forwarding**
5. Turn off all the options for call forwarding:


- Always forward
- Forward when busy, or when you’re on the phone
- Forward when unanswered, or if you reject the call or don’t pick up
- Forward when unreached, or when your phone is off or doesn’t have a signal


**💡 Pro tip:** If you’re using a dual-SIM, make sure to choose the right line when you’re setting up forwarding.


##


Call forwarding you didn’t set up: common causes


If you never turned on forwarding but your phone shows it active, there are a few usual causes. Most aren’t cause for alarm:


1. **Conditional forwarding is on by default.** This is the most common reason, and it’s why forwarding seems to keep turning back on. Carriers route unanswered and busy calls to voicemail using conditional forwarding, so it registers as active whenever you check — even though you never touched it. Because it’s a default rather than a setting you added, clearing it often doesn’t stick: the carrier re-establishes the voicemail route.
2. **A previous owner or your carrier left it on.** Recycled phone numbers and refurbished phones can carry over forwarding set up by the last person who had them. Carriers also occasionally leave forwarding enabled after a port, an account change, or a support call.
3. **A company phone has it set centrally.** On a managed device, an IT or telecom admin can enable forwarding through a work profile or MDM policy, so it appears without you doing anything on the handset itself.
4. **It only looks like forwarding.** Do Not Disturb, Focus, and Quiet Mode silence incoming calls and send them to voicemail, which feels identical to forwarding even when none is set. On Verizon, it’s also worth checking call blocking and Verizon Smart Family filters.


To clear real forwarding, use the codes or in-app steps above. If you’ve already turned forwarding off and calls still divert, that’s a different problem. See what to do when[call forwarding isn’t working](https://www.quo.com/blog/call-forwarding-not-working/) .


You might be noticing how often manual forwarding fails. Whether you set it once and forgot, inherited it from a previous owner, or never touched it at all, you usually don’t notice until a call has already routed to the wrong place. A business line shouldn’t depend on a setting you can’t see.


##


4 drawbacks of using call forwarding


[Unconditional call forwarding](https://www.quo.com/blog/conditional-call-forwarding/#what-is-conditional-call-forwarding) is a standard feature on many phone systems, but it comes with major drawbacks you should keep in mind.


For example:


1. **Calls may slip through the cracks:** You may forget to remove call forwarding when you no longer wish to[forward calls to another phone number](https://www.quo.com/blog/how-to-forward-calls/) automatically. You might miss mission-critical calls or get a ring at night when you’re not expecting it.
2. **Manual setup:** On many traditional phone systems, you may need to manually set up your call forwarding feature each time you want to forward calls to another number or device. This gets tedious fast, especially if you constantly add or remove team members to your[business number](https://www.quo.com/blog/get-business-phone-number/) .
3. **No call forwarding for specific groups:** You can’t automatically forward business calls if you use one phone number for business and one for personal. Apart from not knowing[how to answer the phone](https://www.quo.com/blog/how-to-professionally-answer-the-phone/) , this could blur the lines between your personal and professional life.
4. **Challenges with shared numbers:** If multiple team members share a phone number, forwarding could limit their access to calls on the original number — and the history of their conversation with a contact. Less context equals less prepared team members, as well as awkward conversations if customers have to repeat themselves.


Balancing a business phone number and a forwarding number is usually more trouble than it’s worth.


Even if you remember to deactivate call forwarding, using an old phone system to manage your business could take up time from your day.


A call forwarding alternative is your best bet —[virtual phone numbers](https://www.quo.com/blog/get-virtual-phone-number/) .

FAQs


**What are some tips if call forwarding won’t turn off?**


Here are a few troubleshooting tips if you can’t disable call forwarding: ****
**1. Try these quick fixes:**
– Restart your phone
– Try dialing the universal call forwarding deactivate code ##002#
– Check WiFi or data signal strength when disabling


**2. If codes don’t work:**
– Remove and reinsert your SIM card
– Toggle airplane mode to reset the network
– Use your phone carrier’s app
– Contact customer service


**3. Confirm call forwarding is disabled:**
– Make a test call
– Check the settings in your phone
– Listen for confirmation tones


**What does *73 do on Verizon?**


*73 is[Verizon’s star code](https://www.verizon.com/support/pound-star-codes/) to remove unconditional call forwarding, or forwarding all calls to another number. If you’ve set up other types of call forwarding on Verizon, you’ll need to use different codes:


– **Call forwarding busy line:** turn on *90; turn off *91
– **Call forwarding busy don’t answer:** turn on *92; turn off *93


****Why does call forwarding keep turning back on?****


The cause is usually conditional call forwarding, which diverts calls only when you’re busy or don’t answer. That’s why it looks active again after each missed call. The fix most people miss: turn off every conditional option (busy, no answer, unreachable) individually, not just “always forward,” which only clears unconditional forwarding.


****Will turning off call forwarding affect my voicemail?****


Usually turning off the “always forward” setting won’t touch your voicemail. Unanswered calls just ring your phone and reach your own carrier voicemail again, instead of the forward-to number’s voicemail.


**The one exception:** if you dial ##002#, the universal code that clears every forwarding type at once, it can stop calls from reaching your voicemail. That’s because voicemail itself runs on conditional forwarding (the busy and no-answer routes). If you only want to stop forwarding to another number, turn off unconditional forwarding and leave the conditional routes alone.


**How do I turn off call forwarding #62?**


Just dial ##62# and you’ll disable call forwarding for when your phone is unreachable or out of service. This code works on major carriers like T-Mobile and should cancel the forwarding settings.


**How do I turn off call forwarding on my Samsung Galaxy?**


Head to your Phone app, tap Settings, then Calls or Supplementary services, Call forwarding, and you’ll see options for each forwarding type that you can turn off. It’s usually just a matter of tapping each one and selecting “Disable.”


**How do I turn off call forwarding with *72?**


Since *72 ‌turns call forwarding on, you’ll want to dial *73# to turn it off instead.


**What are the steps to cancel call forwarding on my phone?**


The easiest way is to dial the right disable code like ##21# for all forwarding, restart your phone, and then make a test call to make sure everything’s working normally. Most carriers use similar codes, so these universal ones usually do the trick.


**What’s the difference between conditional and unconditional call forwarding?**


Unconditional forwarding sends all your calls to another phone right away. Conditional forwarding only works when you’re busy, don’t answer, or your phone can’t be reached. Think of conditional as your backup plan and unconditional as an all-the-time redirect.


**How do I check if call forwarding is active?**


On mobile phones, you can try dialing *#21# to see the status of unconditional forwarding, though this may not work on every carrier. You can also open your phone’s call forwarding settings. On a landline, call your number from another phone to test.


**Does airplane mode turn off call forwarding?**


No, airplane mode only disconnects your device from the network; carrier-level forwarding still diverts calls before they reach your phone. Toggling airplane mode can refresh the network so a deactivation code takes, but it doesn’t disable forwarding by itself.
