# How to use shared numbers for clear oversight and delegating work

Say one of your reps is out sick, and a customer calls back with a question. Whoever picks up has no idea what they’re talking about because they weren’t on the first call. The customer sighs and starts over.


Multiply that by every call and text your team handles, and that’s the real cost of not having a shared inbox: customers repeating themselves, two teammates replying to the same message, or a question sitting unanswered for a day because everyone assumed someone else had it.


None of that means you need bigger systems or a huge headcount. You may not have a shared inbox yet or be fully utilizing it.


A shared inbox gives everyone visibility into who’s handled what. It makes it easy for a teammate to follow up. And it lets a teammate step into a conversation cold and still sound like they’ve been there the whole time.


##


1. Work together from a shared inbox


For some teams, everyone works from their own number. If a customer texts Sarah directly and she replies, that conversation lives on her phone. If Sarah’s out, nobody else can see it. They have to ask her directly or hope she left a note somewhere.


A shared inbox puts every call, text, and voicemail on a number in one place where the whole team can see it. Anyone assigned to the number can pick up a call or reply to a text, and the call recording or sent message shows up the same way for everyone else.


Christopher Sands ran into this exact problem at Hannon De Palma, the law firm where he’s CEO. Client conversations were split across individual phones and a Google Voice account. If a sales rep went on leave, clients would have to wait for that person to return before getting an update. With a shared number, the whole[Hannon De Palma team can view intake conversations](https://www.quo.com/blog/legal-client-relationships/) . Paralegals and attorneys don’t need someone to walk them through notes to get up to speed. They can simply open the thread.


### How to set up a shared inbox in Quo


1. Navigate to **Settings** and **Members** in your Quo workspace.
2. Click **Invite your team** or **Invite a member** .
3. Enter email addresses; use a comma-separated list for multiple emails.
4. Select the dropdown menu in **Number assignment** and choose the number you want to share.
5. Choose **roles** for each team member: **Owner** , **Admin** , or **Member** .
6. Click **Send invitations** .


##


2. See who said what, without asking


Even with a shared inbox, you can lose track of who’s handling what. Two people can jump on the same call, or two texts can go out with conflicting information.


Quo shows who handled what directly in the conversation. Every call and outgoing message is tagged with the team member who sent it. Typing and viewing indicators show when someone’s already handling contacting someone. And if a call needs a second look, call recordings and transcripts mean anyone can review what was said, without asking the person who took the call to walk them through it from memory.


### How to enable call recordings and transcripts


1. Go to **Settings, Phone numbers,** then select the specific phone number.
2. Open the **Automations** tab and find the **Transcribe and summarize calls** setting. Keep in mind you’ll need to be on the[Business or Scale plan](https://www.quo.com/pricing) to use transcripts and summaries.
3. Toggle **Transcribe and summarize calls** on. Transcripts and summaries are on by default for Business and Scale plans, so this is where you’d confirm it’s enabled or re-enable it if it’s been turned off.
4. Any teammate with access to the shared number can now open a call and review the recording, summary, or transcript directly in the conversation for future calls. There’s no need to ask the person who took the call what happened.


##


3. Hand off a conversation without another meeting


Answering calls is the easy part. The more difficult part is what happens when someone’s out and a teammate needs to take over the conversation. Without a shared system, that context lives in one person’s head. Handing it off could mean an extra phone call, a text on the side, or a teammate winging it and hoping they don’t ask the customer something they’ve already been asked twice.


Quo lets you easily manage handoffs with[Tasks](https://www.quo.com/blog/quo-task-examples/) . From any conversation, you can create a task, assign it to a specific teammate, and set a due date, so the handoff has an owner instead of living in someone’s head.


Contact notes and custom properties fill in the rest. Contact notes capture the kind of detail that’s easy to lose between conversations: a customer’s nickname, a note about delivery timing, or a mention of an upcoming event worth following up on. Plus, custom properties work as a lightweight CRM layer if you have more standardized information, such as dates, addresses, and other fields you define yourself, shared across the whole team.


E-commerce custom jeweler[Zazzy Box](https://www.quo.com/blog/personalize-ecommerce-customer-experience/) uses contact notes and custom properties in Quo instead of a separate CRM, so any team member can quickly get up to speed on a customer. An example contact profile includes the detail: “customer prefers calling over text.”


### How to assign tasks


1. Open the conversation you want to hand off and find the **Tasks** section in the right sidebar, or hover over a specific call or message to create a task from that activity directly.
2. Click **Create new task** , enter a name and description, then add an assignee and a due date. You can also optionally set a specific time for the task to be due.
3. Click **Create task** to save it. The assignee is notified, and the task stays linked to the conversation it came from, both in the sidebar and in the inbox-level Tasks view.


##


Oversight and handoffs, solved


Real oversight and clean handoffs don’t require hiring a receptionist or setting up an overly complicated system. They just require one inbox, shared with your whole team. Contact notes give the conversation context. And the whole team gets the same visibility, whether they were on the first call or not. Plus, Tasks assigns an owner and a deadline to every follow-up, rather than relying on good intentions.
