# How to forward a landline to a cell phone in 4 steps

If there’s one thing every customer can agree on, it’s that businesses should answer the phone. You can avoid long hold times and missed calls by using the call forwarding option on your landline.


To forward a landline to a cell phone, lift the handset, dial *72, wait for the dial tone, enter the 10-digit cell number, then press # and wait for the confirmation tone. To turn it off, dial *73. On most US carriers (AT&T, Verizon, CenturyLink) *72 turns forwarding on and *73 turns it off. Some carriers also offer Selective Call Forwarding, which lets you forward calls only from specific numbers you choose — for example, CenturyLink’s


*63


. Carrier call forwarding is usually included in your plan, but forwarded long-distance or international calls may be billed per minute.


Here’s what you need to know about how to forward a[landline](https://www.quo.com/blog/what-is-a-landline-phone-number/) to a cell phone and a solution that makes the process easier.


##


How to forward a landline to a cell phone


Using the unconditional call forwarding feature on your landline, you can reroute your calls to your cell phone number. Keep in mind the exact process can vary depending on your desk phone model.


To forward your landline to a cell phone, follow these steps:


1. Dial ***72.**
2. Type in the 10-digit phone number that you want to forward calls to.
3. Press pound ( **#** ).
4. Wait for a confirmation tone before hanging up.


**Note:** After the confirmation tone, some carriers like[AT&T](https://www.att.com/support/article/home-phone/KM1000582/) automatically call the forwarding number to confirm it’s reachable. If that line is busy or doesn’t answer, forwarding won’t activate. If you hear an error message, just repeat the steps. On other carriers, the call going to voicemail counts as answering.


If you only want calls to be forwarded when you’re on another call, you can set up[busy call forwarding](https://www.quo.com/product/call-forwarding/busy-call-forwarding) to automatically redirect calls. To[turn off call forwarding](https://www.quo.com/blog/turn-off-call-forwarding/) and take calls on your landline again, dial *73 and wait for the confirmation tone.


Keep in mind you can still place outgoing calls from the landline while forwarding is on.

##


5 Limitations of forwarding a landline


Carrier forwarding solves the basic problem of missed calls when you’re busy, but it comes with real gaps:


1. **You can only forward to one number at a time.** There’s no way to split calls across a team or route them based on who’s available.
2. **You can’t answer on the landline while forwarding is on.** You may hear a brief ring as a reminder, but the call itself has already gone elsewhere.
3. **Forwarded long-distance or international calls may bill per minute** , even if your base plan includes forwarding at no charge.
4. **No call recording, screening, or analytics** . You don’t get a record of calls for compliance or training, and spam callers get directed to the same forwarding number. There’s no shared access either, so if more than one person needs visibility into calls, forwarding alone can’t provide it.
5. **You’re toggling codes by hand every time you leave or come back** , with no way to schedule it.


If the code isn’t taking effect at all, or calls are still ringing the landline instead of forwarding, see[why call forwarding isn’t working](https://www.quo.com/blog/call-forwarding-not-working/) for troubleshooting steps.

FAQs


****Is it free to forward a landline to a cell phone?****


Activating forwarding with *72 is usually included with landline service at no extra monthly charge. But your carrier still bills the forwarded leg of the call — so forwarding to a long-distance or international number can incur per-minute charges even if the base feature is free. With a virtual number like Quo, forwarding to numbers in the US and Canada is included.


****What are the call forwarding codes for major landline providers?****


On AT&T and CenturyLink, *72 turns forwarding on and *73 turns it off. AT&T also offers busy (*90/*91#) and no-answer (*92#/*93#) forwarding. CenturyLink offers selective forwarding (*63) but doesn’t publish busy/no-answer codes. Codes vary by region and equipment — contact your provider for the most accurate information.


****Can I still receive calls on my landline while forwarding is on?****


Not with standard (*72) forwarding. Every call goes straight to your cell phone, and you can’t answer on the landline while it’s active. To keep taking calls on your landline and only forward the ones you miss, you’ll need busy or no-answer forwarding instead.


****How do I check if call forwarding is active on my landline?****


Most landline carriers don’t publish a dedicated status-check code. The *#21# check used on mobile phones is a cellular-specific code and doesn’t apply to landlines. The most reliable way to confirm forwarding is active: call your landline from another phone and see where it rings, or check your account through your carrier’s website or app.


****Why isn’t call forwarding working on my landline?****


A few common causes: the forwarding number is busy or doesn’t answer when your carrier tries to verify it (voicemail picking up usually counts as answered), the number was entered incorrectly, or the feature isn’t included in your plan.


****How do I turn off call forwarding on my landline?****


Dial *73 and wait for the confirmation tone. If you set up busy or no-answer forwarding instead of standard forwarding, you’ll need the matching deactivation code.
