### Canadian phone number structure explained: Formats and area codes


**


Here’s what you should know before you get a Canadian phone number:


#### Canadian phone number format


Canadian phone numbers follow the same structure as US numbers. That’s because both countries are part of the North American Numbering Plan, or NANP. This is an integrated telephone numbering plan that serves over 20 countries across North America and the Caribbean. But some elements are different.


Here’s a breakdown of your Canadian number’s format:


- **Country code:** NANP numbers share the same code — +1.
- **Area code** **:** The three-digit area code signifies where a number is located. Canada’s area codes are divided among its provinces and territories. More on this soon.
- **Central office code:** This three-digit code is used to identify the specific network exchange within the broader area code.
- **Line code:** This four-digit code signifies the specific line connected to the telephone network.

**What is a VoIP Canada number and how does it work?**


A VoIP Canadian number is a virtual phone number you can use to call and text from almost anywhere else. Whether in Montreal or Madrid, you can access your virtual phone number from your VoIP provider’s app.
VoIP numbers work by sending calls and texts over the internet. They convert analog voice signals into digital data packets and route them through a VoIP server to your recipients. They then convert these packets back into voice signals to facilitate your conversations. VoIP providers can do this in a matter of milliseconds thanks to high-bandwidth internet connections.


**What are the legal requirements for using a Canadian phone number for business purposes?**


If you’re using your Canadian number for business SMS, you’ll need to follow:


–[CASL](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/canada-anti-spam-legislation/en/protect-your-business-and-comply) **, Canada’s Anti-Spam Legislation, guidelines:** This includes getting consent before sending marketing texts and including opt-out mechanisms in every message.
– **National Do Not Call List:** Businesses must register with the[DNCL](https://crtc.gc.ca/eng/phone/telemarketing/infograph03.htm) and scrub call lists before any outbound telemarketing calls.
– **PIPEDA:** Handle any customer data collected via calls or messages in compliance with Canada’s privacy law.
– **Call recording:** One-party consent is generally required federally, but it’s best to inform callers as a best practice. Businesses subject to[PIPEDA](https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/surveillance/02_05_d_14/) must inform callers that they’re recording a call.[Call recording disclosures](https://www.quo.com/blog/call-recording-disclosure/) are fundamental to staying compliant.
