**What is call routing?**


Call routing is a phone system feature that automatically directs incoming calls to the right person or team based on rules you set. Instead of every call going to the same place, your system decides *where* calls should go and *what* happens if nobody answers.


Most businesses choose to create routing rules based on factors like:


- Business hours
- Team availability
- Regions and service areas
- Skills and specialties
- Departments


You can also send calls to fallback options like emergency numbers, voicemail, or an AI voice agent like Quo’s Sona.


### **What are the different types of call routing?**


Some of the most common types of[call routing strategies](https://www.quo.com/blog/call-routing-strategies/) include:


- **Simultaneous ring.** Multiple team members get notified about the same call at once.
- [Time-of-day routing](https://www.quo.com/product/call-routing/time-of-day-routing) **.** Directs calls to different destinations based on the time of day.
- **Shared number routing.** Lets multiple users share the same phone number to route incoming calls to different reps.
- **AI call routing.** Routes incoming calls to an AI voice agent to answer questions and provide customer support.
- [IVR call routing](https://www.quo.com/blog/ivr-call-routing/) **.** Stands for interactive voice response, which lets people route themselves using their voices or keypad inputs.
- **Round-robin routing.** Equal call distribution so all reps have a chance to speak with customers.
- **Shift-based routing.** Route calls to available reps only, skipping reps who are unavailable.
- **Skills-based routing.** Matches callers to the rep best equipped to help them based on expertise.
- **Sequential routing.** Call reps in predetermined batches. When one isn’t available, your call routing software will call the next batch.

**How to choose the best call routing software**


Struggling to choose the right call routing software? To help narrow down your list, consider the following factors:


- **Routing options.** Not all call routing software works the same way. Some providers only offer basic call forwarding. Others support phone menus and visual call flow builders. Make sure the platform supports the routing options your team needs *before* signing on the dotted line.
- **Team size.** A small painting company may only need a shared number and basic ring groups. Larger enterprises may need advanced routing features like call queues and skill-based routing.
- **Pricing.** Pay close attention to which routing features are included in the base plan. Some providers lock features like business hours and voice calls behind higher-tier plans.
- **Growth plans.** Your routing needs will likely become more complex as your business grows. Look for software that can support your growth with[intelligent call routing](https://www.quo.com/blog/intelligent-call-routing/) options.
- **After-hours coverage.** What can your phone system do when nobody’s available to answer? Some providers let you route calls differently during evenings, weekends, and holidays. Others offer AI voice agents that can answer calls, capture leads, and take messages when your team is unavailable.
