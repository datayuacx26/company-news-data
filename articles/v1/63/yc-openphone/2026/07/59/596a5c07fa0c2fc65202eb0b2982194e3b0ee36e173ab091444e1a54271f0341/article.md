# How to forward calls without having the phone on you

If you want to redirect phone calls from anywhere, whether you’re traveling, away from your desk, or your phone is lost or broken, you may be wondering: **Can you forward calls without having the phone on you?**


Yes, you can forward calls without the phone in your hand — but not the way most people assume. Carrier forwarding on Verizon and T-Mobile can only be set from the phone itself, using star or MMI codes, so it’s no help when your phone is lost, dead, or left behind. There’s one narrow exception on certain AT&T landline plans, but for a typical cell phone, a virtual number is the real answer. It routes calls to any device over the internet — no codes, no phone required.


Keep reading to learn how to set up[remote call forwarding](https://www.quo.com/product/call-forwarding/remote-call-forwarding) , or redirecting your calls to another number without physical access to your phone.


##


1. Forwarding calls using AT&T’s landline remote access


Most cell phone carriers don’t give you the option to turn on[call forwarding for an iPhone](https://www.quo.com/blog/call-forwarding-iphone/) or another device unless you have the phone with you. This is for security reasons. The one exception is on some landline phones — by dialing an access number, you can adjust your call settings remotely. For instance, if you use AT&T and have Remote Access to Call Forwarding turned on, you can forward calls by following these steps:


1. Dial the special access number provided when you added[AT&T Remote Call Forwarding](https://www.att.com/support/smallbusiness/article/smb-local-long-distance/KM1194354/) to your monthly plan.
2. Enter the number you want to forward calls from.
3. Enter your remote access PIN.
4. Dial 72#.
5. Enter the number you want to forward calls to.
6. Confirm your forwarding number.


Keep in mind that AT&T’s Remote Access to Call Forwarding is a paid add-on for landlines and business phones — it’s not available for wireless accounts. And like our previous option, this call forwarding method isn’t scalable in a business context. You’ll waste time manually activating and deactivating call forwarding, and you’ll only be able to choose one forwarding number.


If you need to forward calls to several different numbers or devices, there’s a better option.

How to keep remote call forwarding secure


Both carrier and virtual methods put a lock in front of your forwarding settings. Carriers require you to sign in to your account or enter a PIN before anything changes, and a virtual provider like Quo requires you to sign in to a secured account — so a stranger can’t quietly reroute your calls without getting past that gate first.


The risk worth understanding isn’t someone guessing a code — it’s someone getting into the account behind it. Two things to watch for:


- **Compromised login credentials.** If someone gets your password, they can change your settings as easily as you can.
- **SIM swap or port-out fraud.** A scammer convinces your carrier to move your number to a device they control, taking over your calls and texts — including any SMS verification codes tied to your number.


The[FCC now requires](https://www.fcc.gov/consumer-governmental-affairs/fcc-announces-effective-compliance-date-sim-swapping-item) wireless carriers to verify your identity before processing a SIM change or port-out, and to notify you the moment one is requested on your account. If your carrier offers a port-out PIN or number lock, turning it on is a simple, worthwhile safeguard.


A virtual number shifts the exposure in your favor. Because forwarding lives behind your provider account rather than your physical SIM, it isn’t vulnerable to a SIM swap the same way carrier forwarding is. Quo also adds two-factor authentication and centralized admin controls, so an admin can see who has access to a number and revoke it instantly. If a team member’s login is ever compromised, you shut it down in one place instead of hunting through settings on a lost device.

FAQs


****How to forward calls without having the phone for Android?****


There is no exclusive call forwarding method available to Android devices. You can enable remote call forwarding for Android phones using your carrier’s website or with a third-party virtual phone provider like Quo.


****How do you set up remote call forwarding in Quo?****


You can set up remote call forwarding in Quo with our desktop or web app.


If you want to forward all your calls remotely:
1. Sign up for a Quo account.
2. In the desktop or web app, go to the call flow builder in your phone number settings.
3. Click the **Default call flow** dropdown at the top of the call flow builder.
4. Select **Forward all calls.**
5. Enter the destination number, which can be internal or external.
6. Click **Create new call flow.**


If you want to set up conditional call forwarding remotely:
1. Add the **Business hours** call flow step to your default call flow.
2. Add the **Forward call** step to your during-hours or after-hours call flow.


****How do you forward calls without the phone using T-Mobile?****


[T-Mobile’s standard call forwarding](https://www.t-mobile.com/support/plans-features/calling-services) is set from the device using star codes. For forwarding when you don’t have your phone, T-Mobile points customers to DIGITS, its virtual-number product.


****How do you forward calls without the phone using AT&T Wireless?****


For most AT&T wireless customers, you can’t. AT&T requires you to change call forwarding settings directly from your wireless phone — there’s no option to manage it online or through the AT&T app. This is confirmed on[AT&T’s support page](https://www.att.com/support/article/wireless/KM1011513/) .


The exception is AT&T landlines and business phones. If you have an AT&T landline, you can set up remote call forwarding using an access number — but this is a paid add-on, not a default feature. You’ll need to contact AT&T to enable it before you can use it.


**How do I stop remote call forwarding?**


Here’s how to deactivate call forwarding depending on how you set it up:


**If you’re using a carrier’s website or app:**


Call forwarding is turned off from the phone itself, using the code that matches your carrier:


– **Verizon (and other legacy CDMA carriers):** dial *73 to turn off standard forwarding.
– **T-Mobile, AT&T, and other GSM carriers:** dial ##21# to turn off unconditional forwarding, or ##002# to cancel all forwarding types at once, including busy, no answer, and unreachable.


**If you’re using Quo:**


1. Open Quo’s desktop or web app.
2. Go to the call flow builder in your phone number settings.
3. Select **Default call flow** from the dropdown at the top of the call flow builder.
4. Click **Enable this call flow** .


Forwarding stops immediately once you switch back to your default call flow. Note that this applies to the “Forward all calls” flow. If you set up conditional forwarding through a custom call flow, you’ll need to edit that flow directly.


**How do I forward calls if my phone is lost or stolen?**


This is the situation where the difference between carrier forwarding and a virtual number matters most.


**With a carrier, you’re stuck:** forwarding on Verizon and T-Mobile is set from the phone itself, so a lost or stolen device is exactly when you can’t turn it on. Your fastest move is to call your carrier, report the device, and have them suspend service or help you get a replacement SIM.


**With a virtual phone number, a lost phone isn’t really a disruption.** If you’re already using a provider like Quo, just sign in to the app on another device — your computer, a tablet, or a borrowed phone — and you’ll keep receiving calls and texts to your business number automatically. No forwarding setup required.
If you haven’t set up a virtual number yet, this is one of the strongest reasons to consider it. You’re never dependent on a single device to stay reachable.


**Can I remotely forward my landline calls?**


You can in most cases, but your provider usually requires you to add a remote access feature to your plan before you need it, and it may come with an extra monthly fee. You’ll typically receive a special access number and PIN to activate forwarding from any phone. The catch is if you haven’t set it up in advance, you won’t be able to enable it remotely.


With a virtual phone system like Quo, you can skip the landline entirely — just port your number and take calls from any device with an internet connection.


****What happens to my text messages when I forward calls?****


Call forwarding only applies to voice calls. Your text messages will still go to your original phone number and won’t be redirected. If you need both calls and texts accessible from another device, a virtual phone system like Quo lets you manage everything from one app on any device.


****Can I forward my calls to an international number?****


No, most carriers and VoIP providers don’t allow call forwarding to an international number. International forwarding is a well-known vector for toll fraud, where bad actors route calls through expensive premium overseas numbers and run up large charges — so carriers find it simpler to block the feature outright.


If you need to receive calls while traveling or working abroad, a virtual phone system like Quo is the simpler option.
