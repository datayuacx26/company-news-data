# RingCentral review: Features, pricing, pros & cons

In the market for a business phone system? If you are, then you may be considering RingCentral.


Founded in 1999, RingCentral is one of the oldest cloud-based business communications platforms. It offers VoIP calling, video meetings, team events, and business SMS in one place. Its plans start at $20 per user per month. RingCentral is a solid option for mid-market and enterprise teams. For small teams, however, its feature limits and outdated interface make it hard to recommend.


In this article, we conduct a thorough review of RingCentral. We’ll cover its key features, plans and pricing, and its pros and cons. We’ll highlight which teams will benefit from using RingCentral’s platform. Plus, we’ll present a RingCentral alternative that’s better suited to small and fast-growing businesses.


##


How does RingCentral work?


RingCentral uses[VoIP technology](https://www.quo.com/blog/what-is-a-voip-phone/) to let you call and text your customers. VoIP infrastructure sends calls and texts over the internet instead of a carrier network. This enables its platform to provide calls, texts, and video calling tools on the same plan. As long as you’re connected to the internet, you can use your mobile device, laptop, or desktop computer to talk to customers and team members.


##


Key features of RingCentral


RingCentral offers everything teams need for their business communications. Here’s a list of the features RingCentral provides that make it unique:


- **Team messaging.** Collaborate with your team by sending direct messages and files to your colleagues. You can also assign follow-up tasks to your teammates.
- **Voice calling.** Unlimited domestic calling to US and Canadian numbers on all plans. Supports desk phones, softphones, and mobile apps.
- **Video meetings.** Built-in video conferencing on every plan. Supports up to 200 participants in a single meeting.
- **Business SMS/MMS.** Business texting to customers. Capped in each plan; shared texting is only available as an add-on.
- **Multi-level auto attendant and IVR.** Multi-level phone menus for routing callers. Available on all plans.
- **Third-party integrations.** 500+ integrations are available in RingCentral’s platform. You can also build your own RingCentral integrations with its API.
- **AI features.** Automatically take notes and get meeting and SMS thread summaries with[RingCentral’s AI](https://www.quo.com/blog/ringcentral-ai/) assistant.
- **Call and video recording.** RingCentral offers on-demand call recording and automatic video recording on all plans. You’ll have to upgrade to get access to automatic[RingCentral call recording](https://www.quo.com/blog/ringcentral-recording-calls/) .
- **Analytics.** Get call log reports and quality of service dashboards on all plans. Upgrade to get even more analytics features.

Here are a few things to know about RingCentral’s pricing structure:


- **Free trial limitations.** If you want to try RingCentral before you sign up for a paid plan, you can only do so with fewer than five team members. RingCentral doesn’t let you try it with larger teams.
- **Add-ons.** RingCentral has over a dozen different add-ons for teams to choose from. Some of these add-ons are for essential features like shared texting and text templates. Others are for advanced features like conversation intelligence. These add-ons can make it hard to estimate your monthly costs.
- **Hidden fees.** If you exceed your monthly usage for certain RingCentral features, you’ll be subject to overage fees. For example, if you send more than 25 SMS messages in the Core plan, every additional message costs $0.0119. You’ll find similar fees for features like toll-free minutes and additional toll-free numbers.

##


Pros: What RingCentral does well


RingCentral offers dozens of features across its different plans. It can be confusing to know where it excels. Here’s a list of its unique strengths:


1. **Local numbers for global expansion** . RingCentral lets you set up local numbers in 45 countries and territories. You can run a unified phone system for teams based across the globe.
2. **Video conferencing built into every plan** . Every RingCentral plan comes with video conferencing, not just calling and texting. You can host meetings for up to 200 people, record sessions, and rely on end-to-end encryption.
3. **Desk phone rentals for teams in the office** . For teams that haven’t transitioned to[softphones](https://www.quo.com/blog/what-is-a-softphone/) , RingCentral still supports traditional desk phones. You can even rent hardware directly through RingCentral for an extra fee.
4. **Access to 500+ app integrations** . RingCentral connects your call and text data across a library of third-party apps. For teams that want more control over their integrations, you can use its API.
5. **Multiple communication add-ons.** RingCentral lets you extend communication capabilities beyond individual voice and video calls. Host group events, webinars, and hybrid conferencing with its add-ons and extensions.
6. **Multi-level IVR and call routing.** RingCentral supports up to 250 auto-attendant submenus, call queues with callbacks, and ring groups.
7. **AI features.** You can find AI-assisted SMS texting and summary features in RingCentral’s platform. However, you’ll also find additional usage caps here. The AI receptionist add-on, for instance, includes only 100 minutes per month before overage charges of 50 cents per minute kick in.


##


Cons: Where RingCentral falls short


Before you decide whether RingCentral is right for you, take a look at these key drawbacks:


1. **Strict texting limits** . RingCentral caps outgoing texts by plan. If you exceed its limits, each extra message costs $0.0119, which adds up fast for text-heavy teams.
2. **Shared texting costs extra** . Calling comes with shared numbers, but texting doesn’t. You’ll need the Business SMS Booster add-on for team-wide texting, at $25 per month.
3. **Slow texting registration** . Registering your virtual number with[The Campaign Registry through RingCentral](https://www.quo.com/blog/ringcentral-tcr-registration/) can take months. Some users face repeated rejections or long stretches with no update from support. It delays their ability to text customers.
4. **Recordings expire after 90 days** . RingCentral automatically deletes call recordings after 90 days. That’s a problem if you need them for training, compliance, or resolving customer issues down the line.
5. **Toll-free minutes run out quickly** . RingCentral’s base plan includes just 100 toll-free minutes a month for your whole team. If you go over the limit, you’ll be charged for every additional minute you use.
6. **Outdated interface.** RingCentral’s app splits calls, texts, and voicemails into separate folders. That makes it harder to follow a full customer conversation in one place.
7. **Recurring billing complaints** . Users often report being billed after canceling or charged more than expected. It’s a pattern that shows up repeatedly in customer reviews.


For a focused breakdown of the trade-offs, check out our analysis of[RingCentral pros and cons](https://www.quo.com/blog/ringcentral-pros-and-cons/) .


##


Is RingCentral right for your business?


Now that you know where RingCentral excels and where it falls short, how do you decide if it’s right for you? Signing up for RingCentral comes down to the type of business you operate. Here’s a quick decision-making framework:


- **Business size.** If you’re a mid-market or enterprise team, RingCentral may work for you. It offers multiple channels for teams, including phone, text, video, webinars, and events. But if you’re a small team that only needs one or two channels to talk to customers, it may not be the best option.
- **Global presence.** If you have team members based outside the US and Canada, RingCentral can help them stay connected. Located in one domestic city or region? Then RingCentral may not be the most ideal fit.
- **Feature complexity.** Need advanced contact center features like multi-level IVR and workforce management tools? Then RingCentral is a good option. If you want a platform that’s easier to use and less complex, you should look elsewhere.
- **Affordability.** RingCentral offers more features through add-ons, which can cause your monthly phone costs to balloon over time. If you’re looking for a more cost-effective business phone, you should look for a RingCentral alternative.
- **Setup speed.** RingCentral can take months to get your texting registration approved. Businesses that need to text customers immediately should find a service with faster approval times.


Also, if you’re a healthcare business, then you likely need a business phone that’s HIPAA compliant. Check out our article to find out if RingCentral is HIPAA compliant.

FAQs


****What is RingCentral?****


RingCentral is a cloud-based business communications platform. Founded in 1999, it’s one of the oldest platforms available in the VoIP phone market. It offers VoIP calling, texting, video conferencing, and webinars in a single platform.


****What is RingCentral RingEX?****


RingEX is RingCentral’s business phone offering. Formerly known as RingCentral MVP, RingEX provides all of RingCentral’s core business phone features. That includes calling, texting, call recording, call routing, and third-party integrations.


****What is RingCentral’s cancellation policy?****


RingCentral’s discounted pricing requires an annual contract, and plans typically auto-renew. Canceling usually means waiting until the end of your term or paying an early-termination fee.


Some users report issues with billing, even after they’ve canceled their accounts:


*“The contract has an auto-renewal feature for an additional two years, so after two years, I was opted in by contract with no notification and stuck. Then, when the contract was over, I canceled half of my lines and was assured this was done. They continue to charge me for these lines and refuse to respond to my requests for review.”* –[G2 Review](https://www.g2.com/products/ringex/reviews/ringex-review-10366375)


****How does RingCentral compare to Google Voice?****


Google Voice is a more affordable business phone platform for teams just getting started. Pricing starts at $10 per user per month. However, it lacks business phone features like integrations, texting automations, and an AI receptionist. Google Voice suits solo users and very small teams, while RingCentral fits larger teams that need the feature depth. Check out our full[Google Voice vs RingCentral](https://www.quo.com/blog/google-voice-vs-ringcentral/) comparison.


****How do I port my number to RingCentral?****


You can port an existing business number to RingCentral, but only after signing up for a paid plan. Submit a porting request with your current account number, PIN, and a recent bill. Keep your existing service active until the port completes. This typically takes a few business days to a couple of weeks, depending on the carrier.
