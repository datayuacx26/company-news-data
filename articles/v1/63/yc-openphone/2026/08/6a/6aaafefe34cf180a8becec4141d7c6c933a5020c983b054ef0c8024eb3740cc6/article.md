# RingCentral call forwarding: How it works + limitations

Imagine you’re finally clocking out for a well-deserved weekend. You’re driving home, music blasting, thinking about sinking into the couch with a bowl of popcorn. But your business number starts to ring.


With a sigh, you answer. You’re surprised to learn it’s a simple customer support question that your on-call teammate should’ve answered. It’s time to choose a phone system that offers better call forwarding features.


If you’re considering going with RingCentral for your call forwarding needs, you should understand what it can ** and can’t ** do. To access it, your admin has to activate call handling permissions for specific users. You can choose to forward all calls, create scheduling rules, or send callers to voicemail. You can also play a custom audio message if you prefer. The downside? There’s no visual editor for call forwarding. That means you’ll have to manually adjust your settings without seeing your call flows in context.


This guide explores the key features and limitations of RingCentral call forwarding. You’ll learn about the different options available, how to set it up, and a RingCentral alternative that offers modern call forwarding for teams.


##


How to forward calls on RingCentral


RingCentral offers call handling and forwarding settings at two levels: for admins and for users.


- **Admin-level forwarding** lets account administrators set up call forwarding rules for other users. For example, you can set up business hours that affect incoming calls to the main company number.
- **User-level forwarding** is when reps add rules to their own number or user extension. They can adjust when calls come in during specific days and times.


Here’s what these look like in greater detail.


### How to forward RingCentral calls as an admin


As a RingCentral admin, you can manage call forwarding at the account level. This means you can make changes to your own phone number, as well as your entire team.


There are two different types of calls you can forward: company calls and queue calls.


Company calls refer to any incoming call that reaches your main business number. Creating forwarding rules will affect every inbound call to that number. For example, you could send all incoming after-hours calls to a voicemail inbox or an emergency backup number.


Queue calls are groups of callers routed to specific groups. This could be an internal extension or a shared support number where multiple team members work together to answer calls. Forwarding rules for queue calls only affect calls coming to that specific group. For example, you can send all missed callers on your shared support number to RingCentral’s AI receptionist.

##


RingCentral call forwarding options


There are multiple ways to forward your calls on every[RingCentral pricing](https://www.quo.com/blog/ringcentral-pricing/#whats-missing-from-ringcentral-plans) plan. These options can vary depending on the type of call forwarding you choose.


This includes:


### Forward all calls forwarding options


The Forward all calls feature in RingCentral lets you forward every incoming call to another destination when you can’t pick up the phone. This is configurable at the user level. That means you don’t need to be an admin to set this up for your RingCentral number.


You can choose to forward all calls to:


- **A voicemail inbox.** You can also configure a custom greeting.
- **Any extension or phone number.** This includes numbers outside of RingCentral.
- **A custom or general announcement.** Play a recorded message, then end the call.


### Forward all company calls forwarding options


If you’re a RingCentral admin, you can forward all incoming calls to your company’s main numbers. This can be useful during business closures, holidays, or emergencies.


You can forward all company calls in RingCentral to the following destinations:


- **A recorded announcement** . Send calls to an extension that plays a recorded message and then disconnects.
- **Voicemail** . Send callers to a dedicated voicemail inbox so you can follow up later.
- **A specific user** . Send calls to a selected user or extension.
- **Multiple users in a call queue** . Send callers to a queue where multiple team members share responsibility for incoming calls.
- **Company greeting** . Play a default or custom greeting.
- **AI Receptionist** . RingCentral’s AI Receptionist, or AIR, can answer questions, screen leads, and forward calls to other team members.
- **IVR menu** . Send callers to a phone menu.
- **Shared line** . Send calls to a shared number, like for sales or customer support.
- **Limited extension** . Send calls to an extension with limited features that isn’t tied to a specific user. It may be useful when configuring[RingCentral call recording](https://www.quo.com/blog/ringcentral-recording-calls/) , since you won’t have to tie the feature to an individual user’s account.


### Forward all queue calls forwarding options


Forwarding all call queues will send every member of a queue to a separate destination. That way, they can bypass the queue. This is useful for holidays, business closures, or emergencies when your team isn’t available to answer queue calls.


Here’s a breakdown of each queue destination you can choose from:


- **AI Receptionist** . Send callers to a RingCentral AIR extension.
- **Announcement** . Play an audio recording.
- **Voicemail** . Send missed calls to voicemail.
- **External number** . Forward the call to a number outside of RingCentral.
- **A specific user** . Send calls to a specific user.
- **Multiple users – call queue** . Send calls to a shared call queue where multiple team members can answer the phone.
- **IVR menu** . Send calls to a phone menu.
- **Delegated line** . Send calls to multiple *people* on a shared number.
- **Shared line** . Send calls to multiple *devices* on a shared number.
- **Limited extension** . Send calls to a phone in a common area, like an office desk phone.
- **Site** . Send calls to an extension at a different company site. For example, you could send a sales call from your Norfolk division to your Richmond site.

How to set up additional RingCentral call routing features


Need to set up a more complex call flow? You can combine call forwarding with RingCentral’s other call routing features. This includes multi-level IVR, call transfers, and ring groups.


### How to set up RingCentral IVR


RingCentral’s IVR phone menus let callers route themselves to the right person or department. You may have also heard IVR called a virtual receptionist or auto-receptionist.


Here’s how to set up RingCentral’s IVR feature:


1. In the **Admin Portal** on the RingCentral desktop and web app, click **Phone System** .
2. Under **Company** , **** click **IVR Menus (b)** , then **New IVR Menu** .
3. Add keys under the **Add Key** dropdown box.
4. Decide how these keypad dials should route callers. This could be:


- Routing the caller to a specific extension
- Transferring to a voicemail inbox
- Connecting to a dial-by-name directory
- Transferring to an external number


5. Decide what happens if callers enter the wrong number or don’t press anything.
6. Click **Validate Menus** to check for problems with your IVR menu.
7. To finalize your menu, tap **Save.**


Keep in mind there’s also a visual IVR menu builder and an XML Editor if you’d prefer to build your phone menus another way.


### How to transfer calls in RingCentral


You can transfer live calls to any number or voicemail inbox directly from the RingCentral mobile app. Use the following steps to set this up:


1. While on a live call, tap the three-dot **More** icon.
2. Select **Transfer** .
3. Choose your transfer type:


- **Ask first.** Give context to a team member before transferring a call over. Also known as a warm transfer.
- **Transfer now.** Transfer a call without speaking to the recipient first. Also known as a cold transfer.
- **To voicemail.** Send the caller to a specific person’s voicemail without ringing the number.


1. Search for the person, extension, or external number you want to transfer to.
2. For a warm transfer, wait until the recipient answers and brief them on the call before completing the transfer.
3. Tap or click **Complete Transfer** to finish.


### How to set up ring order in RingCentral


RingCentral also lets you control the order in which calls ring your devices and team members. Here are the options available to RingEX users:


- **Ring in order.** Ring numbers or devices one at a time in a sequence you set. If the first person doesn’t answer, the call moves to the next.
- **Ring all together.** Ring all numbers or devices at once until someone picks up the phone.


For call queues, you have additional ring order options:


- **Longest idle.** Ring whichever team member has been inactive the longest. This can help distribute call volume evenly across your team.
- **Fixed order.** Rings team members one at a time in a specific sequence. This is useful when you want to prioritize certain reps before others.


You can adjust ring order settings under **Call handling & forwarding** in your RingCentral phone settings. Or you can make changes at the queue level in the **Admin Portal.**

RingCentral call forwarding limitations


RingCentral seems to offer plenty of routing features at first glance. But once you start building a custom flow for your business, you’ll see its call forwarding limitations firsthand.


Here are four reasons why RingCentral’s call forwarding settings fall short:


### 1. No visual call flow builder


RingCentral doesn’t have a visual interface to set up and manage your call flows. It has one for IVRs, but not for your call flow.


Without a visual interface, it can be hard to see your call flow paths as you set up different ring groups, multi-level IVRs, and working hour schedules.


### 2. Setup complexity


Setting up your call management and routing settings in RingCentral can be tedious. Each setting needs to be toggled on and modified in the admin portal.


As one RingCentral review states:


*“The platform is overly complicated and not intuitive, especially for small business owners who need something simple and reliable. Basic tasks take far more time than they should, and the overall setup process feels unnecessarily complex.”* —[Trustpilot](https://ie.trustpilot.com/reviews/69a62496d22b182066eb4928)


### 3. No backup call forwarding to AI Receptionist


RingCentral’s AI Receptionist add-on can only take incoming calls as they come in. That means you can’t forward unanswered calls to it as a backup call routing option.


You *can* forward calls from the receptionist to a rep, but not vice versa. You can’t put callers back into the AIR queue. This could create problems for businesses with high call volumes. If you route callers to voicemail, they’re more likely to hang up and try reaching out to your competition.


### 4. No call flow scheduling


RingCentral doesn’t let you set up multiple call flows to schedule when needed.


For example, you can’t schedule a special call flow for a holiday and let it automatically take over from your default call flow. You’ll have to manually set up your working hours each time, which can be tricky when you’re moving fast or are out of the office.


You’ll also need to remember to adjust your settings again when you’re back to your regular working hours.

FAQs


****Can RingCentral forward calls to an external number?****


Yes. RingCentral supports forwarding calls to external numbers, including mobile phones and landlines. To do this, go to your admin portal. Then go to the user’s forwarding rules and select **External Number** as the destination.


****What’s the difference between RingCentral call forwarding and call flip?****


Call forwarding routes incoming calls to another number. Call flip moves a call you’re already on from one device to another, like from your desk phone to your cell. You should use call forwarding for unanswered calls and call flip for live calls between devices.


****How do I forward calls only after business hours?****


To forward calls only after business hours with RingCentral, you’ll need to set up after-hours call forwarding. Here’s how:
1. In your RingCentral account, click **your profile picture** at the top left, then click **Incoming call rules.**
2. Toggle on **Forward all calls** . Then choose **Forward the call** under the dropdown box. You can send incoming calls to any forwarding number or extension within the RingCentral app.
3. **Click Add schedule** , then set your call forwarding schedule using the date and time selectors. This rule will forward all after-hours incoming calls to the number or extension of your choice.


****Why is my RingCentral call forwarding not working?****


The most common causes for RingCentral call forwarding to not work are:
• A forwarding rule that wasn’t saved or enabled
• An admin-level setting that’s overriding a user-level rule
• The destination number not including the country code
Don’t forget to check if external call forwarding is permitted on your plan.
