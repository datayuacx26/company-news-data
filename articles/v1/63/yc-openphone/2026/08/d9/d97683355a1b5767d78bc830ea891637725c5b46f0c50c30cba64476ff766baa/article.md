How to write an appointment reminder text


Effective reminder texts are short and clear, but still include all the necessary details:


- **Personalization.** Include the customer’s name and[type of appointment](https://www.quo.com/blog/types-of-appointments/) , such as fixing a leaking pipe.
- **Clear appointment details.** Include the date, time, location, and any parking instructions.
- **Simple confirmation mechanism.** Add a call to action like, “Reply Y to confirm or R to reschedule,” or include a reschedule link.
- **Opt-out option.** Include an opt-out in every text, like “Reply STOP to unsubscribe.”
- **Business identification.** Include your name and your business name in your first message to follow[SMS compliance](https://www.quo.com/blog/sms-compliance/) .


Here’s an example text that follows these effective text reminder writing tips:


Hi Sarah, this is Hot Box Fitness. Reminder: Your boxing class is scheduled for Thursday, Jan 9 at 2:00 p.m. at our downtown location. Reply Y to confirm or R to reschedule. Reply STOP to unsubscribe.


##


When to send appointment reminder texts


Here’s a simple schedule that works for most businesses:


- **Immediate confirmation.** Within five minutes of booking, send a confirmation that says, “We’ve got you down,” to build trust.
- **Week-before reminder.** For appointments scheduled over seven days out, you can send an early reminder. It gives clients a big window to reschedule if needed.
- **24–48-hour reminder.** This is the classic reminder. Send it 24 to 48 hours before the appointment. It’s the perfect window for clients to confirm or reschedule without throwing your whole day into chaos.
- **Day-of nudge.** For some businesses — like home services where you need someone to be there — a quick “we’re on our way” text an hour or two before is a nice touch. For others, it might be overkill. You know your clients best.

8 best practices for sending appointment reminder texts


Be mindful of these best practices when automating SMS appointment reminders:


1. **Obtain consent.** You need to get permission from a contact before sending them automated event reminders so your messages aren’t flagged as spam. You can get[SMS consent](https://www.quo.com/blog/sms-consent/) in a digital or physical form; just be sure to keep the record on file. Also be sure to include clear opt-out language in each text, such as “If you wish to no longer receive texts from us, reply with STOP.”
2. **Get verified with the large US cell carriers.** If you’re using a virtual phone service to send business texts to the US, you need to submit your business profile to[The Campaign Registry](https://www.quo.com/blog/the-campaign-registry/) , a third-party organization selected by the major US cell carriers. Quo users can complete this verification step by filling out the[US carrier registration form](https://support.quo.com/getting-started/carrier-registration/carrier-registration) .
3. **Keep messages short.** Be direct and concise, but not at the expense of important details. You still need to include your name, the appointment time, and other relevant details.
4. **Use a phone number people can reply to.** Make responding to your text reminders easier by not using a short code.
5. **Identify yourself.** Identify yourself along with your business name when you send the first message to avoid being marked as spam.
6. **Use the same number for communicating with a customer.** You should send all texts from your dedicated business number — instead of just[appointment reminder software](https://www.quo.com/blog/appointment-reminder-software/) . This makes it easier to catch up on any conversation because all phone communication appears in the same thread.
7. **Be on brand.** Don’t make[automated texts](https://www.quo.com/product/messaging/automated-text-messages) sound robotic. Your text messages should reflect your brand’s voice.
8. **Schedule text messages based on the recipient’s time zone.** If your client is in Japan and you’re based in New York, it’s best not to schedule a text message for 11 a.m. EST. Instead, determine the recipient’s business hours based on their time zone, convert the time into your local time, and[schedule the text](https://www.quo.com/blog/how-to-schedule-texts/) to send during those hours.

Frequently asked questions about appointment reminder texts


****How many appointment reminders should I send?****


Start with two: an immediate confirmation text right after they book and another 24 hours before the appointment. Some businesses also send a third “we’re on our way” text for house calls, but two is effective for many businesses.


****What if customers don’t respond to my reminder texts?****


A non-response doesn’t always mean a no-show. Depending on your cancellation policy, either assume they’re coming or follow up with a quick phone call or text a few hours before.


****Can I use the same reminder template for all appointment types?****


You can, but personalization helps. New clients might need your full address or a link to intake forms, while regulars just need a quick confirmation. Adjust your templates to be helpful, not robotic.


****How do I avoid having my appointment reminders marked as spam?****


Always get permission to text your clients first, use a dedicated 10-digit business number instead of a short code, and introduce yourself in the first message. Register your number with the US carriers and communicate like a person, not a bot.


****Is it legal to send reminder texts to customers?****


Yes, but you need your customer’s permission before sending reminder texts. If you’re texting US phone numbers, you’ll also need to complete US carrier registration before you can send messages.


Every reminder text should include a clear way for customers to opt out of future messages, such as “Reply STOP to unsubscribe.” It’s also a good idea to use scheduled texts to send reminders during your recipients’ normal business hours, which helps ensure you stay[TCPA compliant](https://www.quo.com/blog/tcpa-compliance-checklist/) .


****Can I send reminder texts from my business landline?****


Yes. You can send reminder texts from your business[landline](https://www.quo.com/blog/what-is-a-landline-phone-number/) by[porting it to Quo](https://www.quo.com/porting) .


****Do customers have to opt in to receive reminder texts?****


Yes. Under the[Telephone Consumer Protection Act](https://www.jdsupra.com/legalnews/what-is-the-tcpa-5195949/) , or TCPA, businesses must get a customer’s express written consent before sending them text messages. Businesses that don’t follow[TCPA rules](https://www.quo.com/blog/tcpa-opt-in/) can face fines issued by the Federal Communications Commission. Keep proof of consent on file, such as the original consent form, the keyword the customer texted to opt in, or the conversation where they agreed to receive messages.


****Are appointment reminder texts HIPAA compliant?****


SMS and MMS aren’t HIPAA compliant by default. Healthcare organizations can still use them in a manner consistent with HIPAA regulations if they get appropriate patient authorization, limit protected health information to the minimum necessary, and use a[HIPAA-compliant phone system](https://www.quo.com/blog/hipaa-compliant-voip/) like Quo that supports calling and texting in a compliant manner. Organizations should also document their decision to use[SMS or MMS](https://www.quo.com/blog/sms-vs-mms/) , along with the associated risks, in their HIPAA policies.


With Quo, once you have an active account, you[can request a BAA](https://openphone.typeform.com/to/vAX6Mdaz) in 15 seconds or less on a Business or Scale plan.


****Why should businesses send reminder texts?****


Here are three ways reminder texts can help your business:
**Spend less time reminding customers about appointments.** Instead of calling every customer yourself, schedule reminder texts to go out automatically. That frees up your team to focus on other work.
**Make it easy for customers to confirm, cancel, or reschedule.** For some customers, it’s easier to reply to a text than call your business. That means you’re more likely to find out sooner if they need to cancel or reschedule, giving your team more time to update your calendar.
**Give yourself more time to fill canceled appointments.** When a customer lets you know they can’t make it, you have more time to offer that appointment to someone else.


****Do you need a text reminder service for your business?****


Not necessarily. Many businesses start by scheduling appointment reminder texts from their personal phones. But once you’re managing more appointments, it’s easy for reminders to slip through the cracks. You can use Quo as a text reminder service with other tools in your tech stack, such as Zapier or Calendly to help you keep up without missing appointments or leaving money on the table.
