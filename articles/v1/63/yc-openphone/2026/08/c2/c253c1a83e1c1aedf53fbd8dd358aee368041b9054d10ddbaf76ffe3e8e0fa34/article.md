##


**Why people look for alternatives to Elevate**


Elevate is a legacy cloud-based VoIP phone system that launched in 2005. It integrates with Office 365. That way, your reps can make and receive business calls without leaving Teams. It also offers features that suit larger businesses, like video conferencing with up to 200 people and call queues.


But if you’re not at an enterprise business or your business doesn’t rely on online meetings, Elevate UC is likely overkill. It also comes with many limitations that could prevent your business from growing in the first place.


Let’s take a look at seven of these below.


### **1. Text messaging is limited**


Elevate doesn’t offer any SMS or MMS messaging on the base plan. Higher plans *do* unlock text messaging, but you’ll still be subject to limits. There’s no unlimited texting available, no matter how much you upgrade.


Tiers include:


- **Essentials:** 25 messages per user per month
- **Pro:** 500 messages per user per month
- **Enterprise:** 1,000 messages per user per month


### **2. Voicemail transcription requires an upgrade**


Voicemail transcription isn’t available on Elevate’s base plan. You’ll have to manually listen back to voicemails to retrieve key details or context. This makes it more difficult to share details with your team. For example, if you want to copy-paste information left in a voicemail, you’ll either have to type it out or convert the audio file to text.


The good news? Many Elevate VoIP alternatives, including Quo, include voicemail transcription and[voicemail-to-email](https://www.quo.com/blog/voicemails-to-email/) on all plans.


### **3. Simultaneous ring requires an upgrade**


Most business phone systems let you notify multiple team members of incoming calls at the same time on the base plan. But that’s not the case with Elevate — you’ll need to upgrade to the Essentials plan, which starts at $24.99 per user per month.


Thankfully, Elevate VoIP alternatives like Quo include[simultaneous ring](https://www.quo.com/product/call-routing/simultaneous-ring) on the base plan. Then, upgrade your plan for custom ring groups whenever you need more specific ring orders.


### **4. Pricing favors larger businesses**


For businesses with more users and longer contracts, Elevate offers a few discount packages. But the best ones require a five-year agreement. This means small and growing businesses will have to pay more to access the same features.


For example, Elevate Pro costs $30.99 per user per month on a one-year contract. But if you pay for a five-year contract, the price drops to $24.99 per user per month. Elevate Enterprise drops from $35.99 to $31.99 per user per month over the same period. This means you’ll pay an extra $48 to $72 per user per year just because you’re a smaller business not ready to lock into a multi-year contract.


### **5. Calling costs can add up**


Elevate’s “unlimited” calling isn’t technically unlimited. If you read the fine print, you’ll see you get 6,000 outbound minutes per user per month. You’ll also pay overage fees that cost $0.0199 per minute.


Keep in mind this doesn’t include toll-free calling. You’ll need to purchase a monthly toll-free minute package starting at $13 per month for 500 minutes. You’ll also have to pay $0.039 per minute for any additional minutes.


### **6. Longer message and call retention costs extra**


Need to store conversations for legal or compliance purposes? You should know Elevate only provides 30-day retention for calls, voice messages, and SMS messages. If you want more than that, you’ll need to purchase a paid add-on for *every* member of your account.


Prices include:


- **One-year retention:** $3.60 per user per month
- **Three-year retention:** $5 per user per month
- **Seven-year retention:** $8.50 per user per month
- **10-year retention:** $10 per user per month


Other Elevate alternatives, including Quo, offer unlimited storage for free on every plan.


### **7. You can’t save time with texting automations**


Even if you upgrade to an Elevate plan with texting, your team still has to send every message manually. That means retyping common replies and keeping track of follow-ups.


Other Elevate VoIP alternatives, including Quo, can take some of that work off your plate. With Quo, you can:


- Save common replies as snippets
- Automatically respond to missed calls and texts with auto-replies
- Schedule texts to send later
- Automate repetitive workflows such as appointment confirmation texts via Quo’s Zapier or Make integration; for more custom workflows, you can use the Quo API
