# 9 Best voice of customer tools for small businesses

You already have customer feedback. It shows up in calls, reviews, and support tickets. The data exists… you just don’t have a system that pulls it together.


That’s the role of Voice of Customer, or VoC, tools. But most information about collecting data and utilizingVoC tools is written for enterprise companies. This can feel complex for a smaller team.


But that’s just not true. There *are* VoC tools purpose-built for smaller teams. You can start with a single channel and grow from there.


Here’s a quick look at what we found:


- The best Voice of Customer tool for insights from your business phone conversations is **Quo** .
- The best Voice of Customer tool for structured surveys is **SurveyMonkey** .
- The best Voice of Customer tool for social listening is **Brandwatch** .
- The best Voice of Customer tool for omnichannel feedback is **Zonka Feedback** .


Below, we review nine Voice of Customer tools across different categories. Some can get you up and running fast. Others are better suited once you have more feedback volume.


##


What are Voice of Customer tools?


Voice of Customer tools collect feedback from channels like phone calls, surveys, and support tickets. These tools can also collect data from public channels like online review sites and social media. Then, they organize the feedback and surface trends, patterns, and themes. To do that, they often rely on AI-powered features like sentiment analysis and[customer intelligence](https://www.quo.com/blog/customer-intelligence/) .


Why do businesses use Voice of Customer software?


When you can identify recurring issues across channels, you can fix problems faster. You can also make better decisions about where to invest your resources.

How do you choose the right VoC tool for your business?


Not sure which Voice of the Customer tool is the right fit for your business? There are certainly a lot of options to consider, even beyond the nine tools we’ve covered. A Google search would show you additional options like InMoment, QuestionPro, SentiSum, CustomerGauge, and GetFeedback. But these products target enterprise companies or large B2B organizations. We chose to dig into tools with the widest appeal to small and growing businesses.


If you’re exploring your options, run through these five questions to narrow your search:


**1. Where does your feedback actually live?** Map your channels first. If most interactions happen over the phone, a tool like Quo that[captures call analytics](https://www.quo.com/blog/call-analytics/) gives you more value than a survey platform. Feedback mostly in tickets? Zendesk is a good fit.


**2. How much feedback volume do you have?** A handful of reviews and a dozen support emails don’t justify an enterprise-grade VoC platform. A CRM-native feature like Jobber Copilot can give you insights into customer satisfaction without a bunch of features you don’t need.


**3. Will your team actually use it?** A tool nobody logs into doesn’t generate insights. Prioritize platforms that fit workflows you already have, such as Usersnap for app or product feedback. If you use kiosks in your business, then choose Zonka Feedback.


**4. Does it integrate with what you already use?** Check whether the tool connects to your CRM, phone system, or helpdesk.[Customer intelligence software](https://www.quo.com/blog/customer-intelligence-platform/) works best when it pushes to and pulls from your daily tools.


**5. Does it meet your data privacy requirements?** Confirm SOC 2 certification, HIPAA compliance, or GDPR compliance if your business or industry requires it.


##


6 Features to look for in a VoC tool


When evaluating VoC software, check whether the tool has your highest priority features for your budget. Consider:


- **Centralized feedback view.** You want one place to see your most important channel sources, whether it’s call data, survey responses, tickets, or reviews. Without it, you’ll miss insights or spend too much time digging for information.
- **AI sentiment and theme detection.** Look for automated tagging that sorts feedback by topic and sentiment. You can quickly spot issues, problems, or things your customers appreciate.
- **Scheduled reporting and alerts.** You want **** regular reports delivered to your inbox or Slack without anyone pulling data manually. You won’t have to remember to review the data.
- **Integrations with tools already in use.** Data moves automatically between systems. Less manual work means your team is more likely to use the tool consistently and make fewer errors.
- **Analytics dashboard.** Visual reporting shows trends over time,[conversation analytics](https://www.quo.com/blog/conversation-analytics/) , and sentiment breakdowns. The data should be easy to understand so you can act on it.
- **Security and compliance.** Look for SOC 2 certification, encryption, role-based access, and industry-relevant regulatory compliance.

FAQs


**Do small businesses actually need a VoC tool?**


Yes. Small businesses collect feedback every day through calls, texts, reviews, and support requests. The difference between acting on that feedback and missing it comes down to having a system. A VoC tool doesn’t have to be complex. Even a phone system with analytics can surface trends that improve the customer experience.


**What channels should a small business start with for VoC?**


Start where your feedback already is. For many service businesses, that’s phone calls and texts. Other businesses may rely on social channels or customer support tickets.


**What are the benefits of Voice of Customer tools for growing businesses?**


VoC tools help you improve customer satisfaction by identifying issues faster. They can also help you see which services your customers value most. You can better target your marketing by uncovering your most engaged customer segments. When you act on VoC data, you can build customer loyalty, improve customer retention, and reduce customer churn.


**What are examples of Voice of the Customer?**


Voice of the Customer data includes any indication of how customers feel. Common Voice of Customer examples include survey responses and feedback forms, NPS, and CES ratings. It may also include call recordings and speech analytics data and support ticket trends. Online reviews and social mentions are another source. Customer interviews and focus groups also count as VoC data.


**What are the best Voice of Customer tools for small businesses?**


Quo is the best VoC tool for small businesses whose interactions happen over calls and texts. SurveyMonkey works well for gathering structured survey feedback. Jobber Copilot suits service businesses already using Jobber as their CRM.


**Are there free or low-cost Voice of Customer tools?**


SurveyMonkey has a free plan with basic survey capabilities to collect direct feedback. Usersnap offers a free tier for up to 20 feedback items. Starting with a free plan is a practical way to start looking at VoC data without a commitment.
