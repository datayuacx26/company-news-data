# Call forwarding to multiple numbers: Why it doesn’t work, and what to use instead

[Forwarding calls](https://www.quo.com/blog/how-to-forward-calls/) to one number at a time doesn’t always meet your business needs. Wondering if you can forward calls to multiple numbers?


You can, but not with your phone’s native call forwarding, which only sends calls to one number at a time. To ring multiple phones from a single number, you need a shared number on a[business phone system](https://www.quo.com/blog/best-small-business-phone-systems/) . It uses simultaneous ring (every phone rings at once and the first to answer takes the call) or sequential ring (phones ring in order) so whoever is available answers first. Shared numbers work on any device with an internet connection and fall back to voicemail or an[AI voice agent](https://www.quo.com/product/ai/voice-agent) when no one picks up.


Below, we’ll show you how shared numbers work, where native call forwarding falls short, and how to set things up so there’s a reliable way to answer every call.


##


Can you set up call forwarding to multiple numbers?


You can set up call forwarding to multiple numbers, but not through native call forwarding, including on major carriers like AT&T and Verizon. Star codes only route calls to a single destination at a time.


Similarly, you can configure[call forwarding on an Android](https://www.quo.com/blog/call-forwarding-android/) , iPhone, or a traditional landline, but it’s also only to *one* phone number at a time — not multiple. That means if the person can’t pick up, the call gets missed — and the customer is left waiting, or they phone a competitor instead.


To truly send customer calls to multiple numbers, you need more than basic call forwarding. That’s where shared numbers come in. With a shared number, multiple people can use a single number to receive and make calls or texts at the same time. It doesn’t matter where they are or what devices they use — all they need is an internet connection.


Let’s take a closer look at how shared numbers help you avoid missed calls.

To reduce the chances of missing a call, Quo provides three different[ring groups](https://support.quo.com/core-concepts/administration/call-flows/ring-groups) . A ring group is simply the set of people assigned to answer a shared number, and the ring order (simultaneous, random, or sequential) decides how that group’s phones ring:


- [Simultaneous ring](https://www.quo.com/product/call-routing/simultaneous-ring) : Every assigned device rings at the same time, so the first available person answers and the others stop ringing. Best for all-hands support or sales teams where speed to answer matters most.
- **Random** : Ring batches of team members in random order to distribute call volume evenly.
- [Sequential ring](https://www.quo.com/product/call-routing/sequential-ring) : Rings people or batches in a set order until someone picks up. Best when a primary owner should get the first ring before it rolls to backup.

### Other ways businesses route calls to multiple people


Native forwarding isn’t the only option before you get to a shared number. A few other approaches show up in the wild:


- **Find me / follow me:** Rings a list of your numbers in sequence — desk, then cell, then home — until you answer. It follows one person across devices, not a team.
- **PBX hunt groups:** Legacy office-phone systems ring a group of extensions in a set pattern. Effective, but tied to on-premise hardware and IT setup.
- **Answering services:** A human or AI service picks up overflow or after-hours calls, then relays messages or transfers to your team.


A modern shared number does the useful part of all three. It reaches multiple people, follows them across devices, and covers after-hours — without the hardware or a separate service. And if whoever’s responsible rotates, it handles that too. If a different person is on call each week, you shouldn’t have to re-point one forward by hand. Scheduled call flows send after-hours calls to whoever is on that week.

There’s always a chance that no one on a shared phone line will be available to pick up. **Can two phones receive calls at the same time?**


Not with native call forwarding, which rings one phone at a time. With a shared number on a business phone system, yes: simultaneous ring makes multiple phones ring at once, and the first person to answer takes the call. The others stop ringing.


****Can I forward calls to both mobile phones and landlines?****


Most devices let you[forward calls](https://www.quo.com/product/call-forwarding) to a mobile phone or landline, but you can’t set up call forwarding to multiple numbers. To set up single-number forwarding, open Settings > Apps > Phone > Call Forwarding on iOS 18 or later. Toggle it on, and enter one number — the toggle only appears if your carrier supports it.
