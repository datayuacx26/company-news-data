# Scaling Your Food Brand: When to Hire a Contract Manufacturer

[Tips](https://thefreshfactory.co/blog-categories/tips/)


April 23, 2026


You just landed every other food brand’s dream: a deal with your perfect retailer. For a moment, you forget your current manufacturing setup isn’t going to be able to service 30 locations.


Or maybe your business is growing faster than you projected — fast enough that production is completely maxed out. Your current copacker is hitting their ceiling, and you know it’s time to find a partner who can actually grow with you.


That’s where The Fresh Factory comes in. Keep reading to learn the key signs it’s time to bring in a co-manufacturer, which mistakes you should avoid, and what to look for in a true manufacturing partner.


## Signs Your Brand Is Ready for Co-Manufacturing


If you’re not sure whether finding a co-manufacturer is the right next step, consider these six signs:


### **1. You’ve outgrown your test kitchen or self-manufacturing setup.**


Much like the scenario above, your brand might be operating out of a test kitchen — or even your home kitchen — but can no longer meet demand. If your production runs are taking all day and you still can’t keep up, that’s your signal to find a co-manufacturer.


### **2. You’re getting into bigger retailers (or looking to do that).**


Working out of a commercial kitchen may have worked for a time, but larger retailers like Whole Foods or Costco require SQF-certified (Safe Quality Food) facilities,[strict compliance standards](https://thefreshfactory.co/certifications/) , and consistent volume. If your current setup doesn’t meet those requirements, partnering with a certified co-manufacturer removes that barrier.


### **3. Your current copacker or contract manufacturer has hit a capacity ceiling.**


You may have already made the move to a larger copacker, but demand has outgrown their capacity, too. If they can’t take on more volume, it’s time to find a co-manufacturer that can grow with you.


### **4. Your product quality is suffering.**


Rapid growth without the right infrastructure will eventually cause your product’s quality to suffer. While growth can be an exciting problem to experience initially, inconsistent production runs and ingredient sourcing issues are common warning signs you need a co-manufacturer with strong quality controls to get things back on track.


### **5. You can’t answer your retail buyers’ questions.**


If questions from retail partners about compliance, production capacity, and lead times feel overwhelming, that’s called a readiness gap. A co-manufacturer can help close that gap so you can respond to buyers with confidence.


### **6. Your growth forecasts have outpaced your production reality.**


If your production can’t realistically support your sales forecasts, your brand may have a structural problem. Part of a co-manufacturer’s job is to align your manufacturing capacity with realistic, achievable growth targets.


## Common Mistakes Brands Make When Looking for a Co-Manufacturer


As a co-manufacturer that has worked with many brands in this space, we’ve seen the same pitfalls come up again and again. Avoid these common mistakes:


### Overestimating Volume and Scale


It’s easy to overestimate future projections when you’re excited about your brand’s momentum. Most brands overestimate their growth timeline, which often leads to unrealistic manufacturing decisions. Base your decisions on where your brand is today rather than where you hope to be by year-end. Setting realistic expectations lets your business grow alongside your co-manufacturer.


### Assuming You Already Know Everything


The learning curve in this space is steeper than most people expect. From food safety and quality to distribution and regulatory details, there’s a lot to take in. Keeping an open mind positions you to make the best decisions for your brand’s growth.


### Searching for a Vendor Rather than a Partner


What business hasn’t done a “copackers near me” Google search? While this isn’t a bad place to start, we recommend refining your search beyond the scope of price, as that rarely ends in a reliable match. Find a partner who is willing to treat your brand as their own: someone you can trust to keep your brand’s best interests at the center of every decision.


## What to Look for in a Contract Manufacturing Partner


Once you’re ready to find the right co-manufacturing partner, here’s what to prioritize:


- [Quality standards and certifications](https://thefreshfactory.co/certifications/) : Most major retailers don’t just prefer SQF certification and strong Food Safety and Quality Assurance practices — they require them.
- **Willingness to work with complex ingredients or formulations** : Not every co-manufacturer is willing to take on unique or challenging formulations. Finding one that will go the extra mile matters.
- **Supplier networks and supply chain capabilities** : A strong co-manufacturer brings[sourcing connections](https://thefreshfactory.co/food-logistics/) and ingredient expertise that most brands couldn’t find or build on their own.
- **Experience that goes beyond a vendor relationship** : The best co-manufacturers bring meaningful knowledge across all aspects of your business. Look for co-manufacturers who don’t gatekeep, but rather work with you to prioritize your brand’s growth.


“Experience matters, and it’s helpful when you’re talking to a potential co-manufacturer to understand what their background has been,” says Donna Duwe, EVP of Sales at The Fresh Factory. “Use them as a consultant to help with aspects like sales strategy and packaging strategy.”
