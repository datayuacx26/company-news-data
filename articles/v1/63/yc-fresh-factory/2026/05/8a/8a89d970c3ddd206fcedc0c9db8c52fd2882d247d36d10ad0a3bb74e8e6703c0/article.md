# What to Expect During the R&D Phase With a Contract Manufacturer

[Manufacturing](https://thefreshfactory.co/blog-categories/manufacturing/) ,


[Tips](https://thefreshfactory.co/blog-categories/tips/)


May 29, 2026


Bringing a product to a contract manufacturer isn’t just a production decision. For many brands, it’s also an R&D decision.


Most co-manufacturers start the conversation at commercialization: you have a formula, they scale it. But what happens before that? What does it look like when your formula needs work before it’s ready for a production line? Or when you’re adding a new SKU and need a manufacturing partner who can help develop it?


At The Fresh Factory, we have in-house R&D capabilities, which isn’t universal in contract manufacturing. That means we’re not just scaling formulas. We’re evaluating them, refining them, and in some cases building them from the ground up, with full-scale production as the target from day one.


Here’s what that process actually looks like.


## What You Need Before R&D Begins


Most brands that come to us for R&D work arrive with a product brief: a clear articulation of what they’re trying to make, what the formula parameters are, what certifications matter, and what the target consumer looks like. That foundation makes everything downstream faster.


If R&D starts without that clarity, it tends to cost more time and money than brands expect. The brief is how both teams stay aligned on what success looks like before any lab work begins.


## Step 1: Initial Feasibility Evaluation


Once a product comes through the door, our R&D team runs an initial evaluation before any scope of work is set. They’re assessing organoleptic properties — pH, texture, flavor profile, appearance — and asking a foundational question: can this product be produced in our facility?


What that evaluation looks like depends on the product category. A beverage is evaluated differently than a condiment or a snack bite. The criteria aren’t the same, the equipment isn’t the same, and the thresholds for what’s commercially viable differ. This step exists to surface fit issues early, before they become expensive problems.


## Step 2: Technical Alignment


If the product passes the initial evaluation, our R&D team and the brand’s team connect for a technical alignment call. The agenda covers what’s already been developed, what parameters govern the formula, and the food safety framework.


That last piece is critical and sometimes underestimated. Every product that goes to retail needs to meet food safety requirements, and those requirements have to be built into the formula, not added at the end. That means establishing kill steps: the specific processes that make a product safe for consumption. Depending on the product, that might be hot fill, pasteurization, high-pressure processing (HPP), or another validated approach.


Understanding the kill steps early, and whether a process authority has already been engaged, shapes everything that follows. A formula that hasn’t accounted for these requirements will need reformulation work. Better to know that now.


## Step 3: Scope of Work & Formulation


If the product needs reformulation, it becomes a formal R&D project. The Fresh Factory assigns a scientist to the project and develops a scope of work that defines exactly what needs to happen to get the formula production-ready.


## Step 4: Benchtop Trials


With the scope agreed upon, work moves into the lab. Our team produces benchtop samples, either with or without the brand’s team on site, and iterates until both parties are satisfied with the result.


The goal isn’t just a formula that tastes right. It’s a formula the team is confident will hold at scale. We try not to take anything to a production trial until we’re reasonably certain it will succeed. Thorough benchtop work is what makes that possible: the more you resolve at this stage, the less you’re spending on trial time later.


## Step 5: Production Trial


A production trial is a full plant run under real production conditions, from inbound ingredients through packaging. At this stage, we validate the operational assumptions made throughout the R&D process, including throughput rates, line speeds, batch consistency, and the cost estimates built on all of the above. If the line needs to run slower than projected to achieve the right consistency, that affects pricing. Production trials surface any issues before they become a surprise.


“There are always learnings and tweaks that might need to be made,” says Donna Duwe, Executive Vice President of Sales at The Fresh Factory. “By and large, if we’re taking it to trial, we’ve run enough benchtop scenarios that we know with a pretty high degree of certainty it’s going to succeed.”


## Why R&D With Your Co-Manufacturer Changes the Math


There’s a practical case for doing R&D with the same partner who will produce your product: you know the formula will work before you commit to scale.


Formulas developed elsewhere (even good ones) carry hidden risks when they move to a new facility. Differences in equipment and processes mean that something that worked in one environment may behave differently in another. When The Fresh Factory develops the formula, those variables are eliminated before the first production run.


If you’re a better-for-you food brand committed to clean-label products, R&D with The Fresh Factory also means you can move much faster.


“We’re figuring out ways to bring products to market that don’t have any artificial preservatives and added sugars,” says Bill Besenhofer, CEO and co-founder of The Fresh Factory. “And we’re able to do it way faster than a traditional CPG development timeline that often takes eighteen months to two years to put a product in market.”
