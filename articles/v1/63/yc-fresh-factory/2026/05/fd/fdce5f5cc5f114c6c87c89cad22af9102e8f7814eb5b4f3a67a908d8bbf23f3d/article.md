# Contract Manufacturing vs. Co-Packing vs. Private Label vs. White Label: A CPG Brand’s Guide

[Tips](https://thefreshfactory.co/blog-categories/tips/)


May 1, 2026


One of the biggest challenges growing and newly established CPG brands encounter is figuring out how to manufacture, package, and scale production as demand increases. The other big challenge? Determining the type of[manufacturing and packaging model](https://thefreshfactory.co/manufacturing/) that makes sense for their brand.


Terms like co-manufacturer, co-packer, private label, and white label are often used interchangeably, even though they represent different approaches to how products are made. Understanding how they differ is key to choosing the right path forward.

## Contract Manufacturing / Co-Manufacturing


[Contract manufacturing](https://thefreshfactory.co/manufacturing/) , which is The Fresh Factory’s specialty, involves handing over the full production process to a partner. Also known as “co-manufacturing,” a co-man sources ingredients, manufactures the products, and packages them for you.


Even though you’re outsourcing production, your recipe, brand standards, and quality expectations should still drive every decision. A co-manufacturer that doesn’t involve you in the process is a red flag. Look for a manufacturer that feels like an extension of your own operations rather than just a vendor.


The co-manufacturing model works best for brands that have a validated product and a clear vision, but don’t have (or want) their own production facility. It allows the brand to focus on sales, marketing, and growth while the co-man handles the operational heavy lifting.


## Co-Packing


With co-packing, finished products produced by the brand are sent to a partner to be bottled, labeled, boxed, or kitted.“You’re just packing it into whatever format they want,” says Donna Duwe, Executive Vice President of Sales at The Fresh Factory. “You’re not actually creating the product.”


Co-packing is a solid option for brands that have the facilities and equipment to make the product itself but lack access to industrial machinery like blow-molders and liquid fillers. You maintain more control, but also take on more complexity. For example, any hiccups from an ingredient supplier are your responsibility to solve.


## Private Label


Rather than bringing their own formula to a manufacturer, a private-label customer chooses a product from the manufacturer’s catalog to put their brand name on.


“Private label is a retailer-owned brand,” Duwe says. “A lot of people who are private label manufacturers will make ketchup, for example, and they will make the same ketchup all day long. All they do at the end of the line is change the label.”


Examples of private labels include Kirkland Signature at Costco, Good & Gather at Target, and 365 at Whole Foods. The benefits of a store brand are customer loyalty, flexible pricing, and higher margins.


## White Label


White label is similar to private label, but more targeted to CPG brands than retailers. Again, brands choose from a product catalog, but in this case, they get to put their brand name on the product and sell it to multiple stores.


White labeling gives brands a faster path to market. You skip the research and development phase. The obvious downside is differentiation. If a manufacturer offers the same base product to ten different brands, what makes yours stand out? The answer has to live entirely in your branding, positioning, and customer relationships.


White label offers a good path to entrepreneurs who want to move fast, test a market, or build a brand around curation and positioning rather than a proprietary formula.


## What Production Model is Best for my Brand?


The right production model depends on your business and its goals. Retailers building out a store brand should find a manufacturer with a trusted and robust private label catalog. A CPG brand looking for speed over differentiation or testing a new category is well suited for the white label route.


If you have your own formula and strong supplier relationships, you can get by with a co-packer. But if you’re building a brand around a truly differentiated product and want a partner to handle manufacturing while keeping you involved, co-manufacturing is the right choice. It provides you with the infrastructure to scale without compromising what makes the product or your brand unique.

## FAQs about Food Production Models


### What is contract manufacturing in the food industry?


Contract manufacturing, also called co-manufacturing, is when a brand partners with a manufacturer to handle the full production process, including ingredient sourcing, manufacturing, and packaging. The brand retains ownership of the formula and sets the quality standards. The co-manufacturer executes production. It’s the right model for brands with a validated product that want a capable production partner without operating their own facility.


### What is co-packing?


Co-packing is when a brand produces its own finished product and sends it to a third party for bottling, labeling, boxing, or kitting. Unlike co-manufacturing, the co-packer doesn’t make the product — they package it. This model works for brands that have their own production facilities but lack industrial packaging equipment.


### What is private label manufacturing?


Private label is when a retailer selects an existing product from a manufacturer’s catalog and sells it under their own store brand. The manufacturer owns the formula and produces the same product for multiple retailers, who differentiate only through branding. Examples include Kirkland Signature at Costco and 365 at Whole Foods.


### What is white label manufacturing?


White label is similar to private label, but targeted to CPG brands rather than retailers. A brand selects a product from a manufacturer’s existing catalog, puts their name on it, and sells it across multiple retail channels. It offers a fast path to market but limited product differentiation, since multiple brands may sell the same base product.


### What is the difference between co-manufacturing and co-packing?


The key difference is scope. A co-manufacturer handles the entire production process, including sourcing ingredients, manufacturing the product, and packaging it. A co-packer only handles packaging of a finished product the brand has already produced. Co-manufacturing is the more comprehensive partnership and is better suited for brands that want a single partner to manage production end to end.


### What is the difference between private label and white label?


Both models involve selecting a product from a manufacturer’s existing catalog rather than developing a proprietary formula. The difference is the buyer: private label is typically used by retailers building a store brand, while white label is used by CPG brands that want to sell a manufacturer’s product under their own name across multiple retail accounts.


### How do I know if co-manufacturing is the right model for my brand?


Co-manufacturing is the right fit if you have a validated product formula, established brand standards, and are ready to scale into retail. It works best for brands that want a production partner deeply involved in quality and execution, not just a facility running a formula. If you’re still developing your product or testing a concept, co-manufacturing may not be the right next step.
