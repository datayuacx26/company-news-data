# How Vertical Integration Lowers Risk for Growing CPG Brands

[Manufacturing](https://thefreshfactory.co/blog-categories/manufacturing/) ,


[Tips](https://thefreshfactory.co/blog-categories/tips/)


June 29, 2026


Most CPG brands don’t set out to build a complicated supply chain. It happens incrementally. A sourcing partner here, a contract manufacturer there, a 3PL to handle warehousing once volume starts climbing. Each decision makes sense in isolation. The problem is that stitching together four separate vendors creates four separate points where something can go wrong, with no single party responsible when it does.


A lead time issue that a sourcing partner knows about doesn’t automatically reach the production schedule. A spec change communicated to the co-manufacturer doesn’t always make it to the warehouse. When an ingredient runs short, someone has to manually connect those dots across company lines. The cumulative cost of that coordination in time, margin, and mistakes rarely shows up cleanly on any invoice.


That’s the case for working with a vertically integrated partner, where your product is managed smoothly and cohesively from start to finish.


## The Real Cost of a Fragmented Stack


It’s easy to think about supply chain risk in terms of catastrophic failures, whether it’s a production shutdown or a missed retailer window. But much of the cost of having multiple partners is less obvious. It’s the hours spent chasing status updates across vendors with different communication systems and different priorities. It’s the margin lost when a problem that should have surfaced in week one doesn’t surface until week three.


“It’s cost, but it’s also just more people to deal with,” says Bill Besenhofer. “More of your time and resources and energy.”


For a brand managing multiple SKUs across multiple retail accounts, expenses get worse with each added vendor. Every handoff between separate companies is a place where accountability gets diluted. The more transitions in the chain, the more moments where something has to be manually re-communicated…and where it can get lost.


## Sourcing: Where Integration Creates an Early Advantage


One of the less visible benefits of a vertically integrated partner appears before a product is ever made.


Because The Fresh Factory works primarily with fresh, natural, and clean-label ingredients, our sourcing team has built supplier relationships in categories that are harder to navigate independently. We have direct relationships with farmers across the country, and we know which suppliers can support the volumes growing brands need.


“Because we’re dealing primarily with fresh and natural ingredients, we have stronger sources of supply,” says Donna Duwe, EVP of Sales at The Fresh Factory. “Our supply chain is more knowledgeable about where to find some of these unique ingredients, and we may already be buying them. That gives us an advantage over the customer having to go find and source that themselves.”


For brands built around clean-label or better-for-you formulations, with natural sweeteners, alternative fats, specialty proteins, this makes a big difference. Starting from scratch on supplier relationships that The Fresh Factory has already built takes time and money that most brands would rather spend elsewhere.


## One Chain of Custody Keeps Finished Goods Moving


Once a product moves into production, the integrated model keeps it moving. Finished goods don’t leave the facility to sit in a third-party warehouse at additional cost. They clear quality control, move into finished goods, and ship, all within the same building and managed by the same team.


For brands running multiple products across categories, there’s an additional operational benefit: outbound shipments can be consolidated across SKUs rather than coordinating separate fulfillment runs from separate manufacturers.


## Vertical Integration Means Faster Time to Market


An integrated supply chain doesn’t just reduce friction; it also compresses timelines. When sourcing, production, and fulfillment operate within a single organization, problems surface inside one communication chain rather than getting discovered late because three different vendors didn’t happen to compare notes.


That speed shows up in time to market. We typically bring products to market in four to eight months, compared to the two or more years it can take a large manufacturer working through a more fragmented process. Part of that is our scale and flexibility. A significant part of it is not losing time at handoffs.
