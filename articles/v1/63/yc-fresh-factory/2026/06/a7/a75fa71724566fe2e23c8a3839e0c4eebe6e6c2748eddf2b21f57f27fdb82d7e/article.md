# How to Commercialize a Food Product With a Contract Manufacturer

[Manufacturing](https://thefreshfactory.co/blog-categories/manufacturing/) ,


[Tips](https://thefreshfactory.co/blog-categories/tips/)


June 18, 2026


Having a recipe is not the same as having a production-ready product. A formula that works in a home kitchen, in a commercial test kitchen, or even on a small-batch run can behave completely differently on a full-scale production line. Ingredients that are easy to source at low volume become complicated at scale. Processing steps that work manually may not translate to automated equipment. What tastes right at 50 pounds may not taste the same at 5,000.


Commercialization is the process of closing that gap: taking a formula that exists and making it viable for consistent, scalable production. If you already have a recipe and you’re ready to bring it to market, commercialization is where the real work begins.


Here’s what that process looks like when you work with The Fresh Factory.


## Commercialization vs. R&D


We’ve covered the[R&D process at The Fresh Factory](https://thefreshfactory.co/blog/research-development-phase/) before — it’s what happens when a brand comes to us with a concept but no formula. Commercialization is the other scenario: you have a recipe, it’s been validated in some form, and now you need to scale it.


Most brands that come to us are in this position. They’ve been producing with an outside formulationist, or they’ve outgrown a previous co-manufacturer’s capacity, or they’re launching a new SKU to an existing line. The formula exists. The question is whether it can be produced at scale, and if so, how.


## What to Bring Before You Start


The more documentation a brand brings into the commercialization process, the faster and less expensive the process tends to be. At minimum, that means a complete formula with ingredient specifications, a target product specification sheet, any existing COAs for ingredients, allergen documentation, and clarity on[certifications](https://thefreshfactory.co/certifications/) the finished product needs to carry, whether it’s USDA Organic, Non-GMO Project Verified, Gluten-Free, or Kosher.


If a process authority has already been engaged and kill steps have been established, bring that documentation, too. If not, that work will need to happen as part of the process.


Brands that arrive with complete documentation move through each phase faster. Brands that arrive without it spend the early stages filling gaps instead of making progress.


## Step 1: Lab Evaluation


Before any commercialization work begins in earnest, our R&D team runs an initial evaluation of the product in our lab. They’re assessing organoleptic properties (pH, texture, flavor profile, appearance) and establishing whether the formula can be produced in our facility as written.


This step also surfaces ingredient issues early. Some formulas work beautifully at small scale using specialty ingredients that are simply not practical at production volume. It could be that they’re difficult to source consistently, cost-prohibitive in large quantities, or challenging to work with on automated equipment.


“Some of our customers have recipes that are just incredibly difficult to produce because they’re making them the way Grandma used to make them,” says Donna Duwe, EVP of Sales at The Fresh Factory. “Using a particular ingredient because it’s so unique just might not be feasible for mass production.”


When that happens, our team identifies alternatives: ingredients that perform similarly, meet the same label requirements, and work within the production environment.


## Step 2: Ingredient Sourcing


Commercialization isn’t just about the formula itself. It’s also about whether you can reliably source what’s in it.


Our team evaluates ingredients for supply chain consistency, lead times, and cost at production volume. Where a brand’s current supplier is workable, we confirm it. Where it isn’t, due to volume minimums, lead time constraints, or certification requirements, we work to identify alternatives that maintain the product’s integrity.


This is one of the practical advantages of working with a co-manufacturer that handles fresh and natural ingredients regularly. At The Fresh Factory, we’ve already established supplier relationships and buying volume in categories that matter to clean-label brands. That means we often already know where to find a specific ingredient, what it costs at scale, and whether the supply is reliable, which is information a brand sourcing independently would have to build from scratch.


## Step 3: Benchtop Trials


The goal of this phase isn’t just a product that tastes right. It’s a formula the team is confident will perform consistently at production volume. Thorough benchtop work is what determines whether a production trial succeeds. Brands that want to rush through this phase tend to spend more money on repeated trials later.


Once the formula has been evaluated and ingredient sourcing is confirmed, the product moves to benchtop trials. Our scientists produce samples in the lab and iterate until the product matches the brand’s expectations for flavor, texture, appearance, and performance against any target specifications.


Brands can be on site for benchtop work or not — we accommodate both. If you’re particular about flavor development or want your team involved in approvals at each iteration, on-site access is available. If you’re comfortable reviewing samples and signing off remotely, that works too.


## Step 4: Production Trial


A production trial is a full plant run under real production conditions, from inbound ingredients through packaging. It’s the final confirmation before a product goes into regular production.


A production trial confirms much more than whether the product tastes right. It’s validating every operational assumption made throughout the process: throughput rates, line speeds, batch consistency, and the cost estimates built on all of those variables.


“Your trials are used for more than just qualifying the product,” says Donna Duwe. “They’re designed to confirm all the assumptions you made getting up to that point.”


If a line needs to run slower than projected to achieve the right consistency, that affects pricing. If a batch size assumption was off, that affects scheduling. Trials surface those realities before they become production surprises.


## Commercialization of Clean-Label Products


At The Fresh Factory, commercializing clean-label, natural foods and beverages is our specialty and area of expertise. We know exactly how to bring products to market that don’t contain artificial preservatives or additives. Want to use alternatives to refined sugar? Our R&D team understands the ingredient interactions and process adjustments to make to avoid affecting texture, moisture, and shelf life.


Most large contract manufacturers are still building that expertise, in part because they’ve spent decades doing it the other way. But The Fresh Factory has been formulating and manufacturing cleaner, better-for-you products long enough that it’s become standard practice rather than a special project, and that translates directly into speed. While large manufacturers can spend two or more years moving a single product from formula to shelf, we typically bring products to market in four to eight months.
