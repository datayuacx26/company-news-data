# Sourdough, Spice, and Everything Nice: Trending Products at Natural Products Expo West

[Innovation](https://thefreshfactory.co/blog-categories/innovation/) ,


[Trends](https://thefreshfactory.co/blog-categories/trends/)


March 11, 2026


Last week, The Fresh Factory team attended the[45th annual Natural Products Expo West](https://www.expowest.com/en/home.html) , an industry event for natural and organic brands. We love attending this event each year — from the exhibitors and networking to the speaker sessions, it never fails to reenergize us about our mission, *Honest Food for All* .


“Retailers go to see what’s new,” says Donna Duwe, Executive Vice President of Sales at The Fresh Factory. “Startups go to try and find interest and get the first foot in the door, and contract manufacturers go to try and figure out how they can help some of these startups get their start.”


For The Fresh Factory, Expo West gives us a glimpse of the future of food. Here are the big takeaways we came back with this year.


## Natural Product Trends


At the event’s keynote address, we learned that 63% of all CPG growth in 2025 came from natural products, and from the looks of the show floor (and our[manufacturing](https://thefreshfactory.co/manufacturing/) facility), this won’t slow down anytime soon.


### Sourdough on the Rise


Sourdough starters were fed consistently during the early days of the pandemic, and consumers never really stopped loving them. What started as a quarantine hobby has quietly become a full-blown category.


This[gut-healthy](https://thefreshfactory.co/blog/functional-foods-2026/) format, which recently received the[Taylor bump](https://www.realsimple.com/taylor-swifts-favorite-homemade-sourdough-bread-11797003) , is expanding well beyond traditional bread to crackers, tortillas, snacks, and other baked goods. Brands are leaning into the fermentation story, clean ingredient lists, and digestive health credentials.


### Fiber Forward


According to the[CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/diabetes/healthy-eating/fiber-helps-diabetes.html) , only half of U.S. adults get their recommended daily intake of fiber. Brands have taken note, and we saw a noticeable increase in fiber-forward products and messaging around digestive health and satiety.


This isn’t just a packaging callout anymore. As the[fibermaxxing](https://www.mskcc.org/news/fiber-and-cancer-risk) trend picks up, fiber is becoming a core formulation strategy.


### Protein Packed


Driven by[GLP-1 use](https://thefreshfactory.co/blog/glp1s-food-brands/) , protein continues to dominate across categories. It showed up in desserts, snacks, and beverages. We even saw it in formats you wouldn’t expect, like protein candy and protein coffee.


In addition, brands positioned products as protein accompaniments. For example, sauces were marketed as pairing with “the original protein” — meat.


### Spice it Up


The appetite for bold, spicy flavors isn’t letting up. Heat is showing up across multiple categories, including snacks, sauces, and beverages.


We’re no longer seeing hot for hot’s sake. Instead, brands are pairing spice with global flavor profiles, functional ingredients, and premium positioning to give consumers something more complex to reach for. Examples of flavors we saw on the floor are Aleppo pepper and Middle Eastern spice blends.


### Functional Beverages


The clean soda and functional beverage category continues to grow quickly, with a wave of fast followers entering the market featuring probiotics, prebiotics, and gut-health positioning. From coconut beverages for better hydration to protein drinks, brands are launching drinks that do more for you.
