# How to Pitch Your Products to Grocery Buyers

[Tips](https://thefreshfactory.co/blog-categories/tips/)


March 6, 2026


Getting a meeting with a retail buyer takes considerable time and effort, from finding the right contact to building connections and often involving a broker. Once you schedule that coveted meeting, you don’t want to blow it.


But how can you wow the buyer and get your product on the shelves? You need a unique, quality product and a strong go-to market (GTM) strategy. At The Fresh Factory, we regularly help food brands prepare pitches, so we’ve learned a lot about what leads to success. Read on to learn more about CPG buyer expectations and how to get your product into grocery stores.


## How to Get a Meeting


Getting in front of a buyer is one of the hardest parts of breaking into retail. The process is relationship-driven, and if you don’t already have those relationships, the fastest path in is through a broker. Brokers come with established connections to buyers across channels and can get you in the room far faster than cold outreach ever will.


## Buyer Meeting Checklist


Have you ever seen someone walk into the Shark Tank without the right preparation? They typically leave without a deal. Don’t let that happen to you. Here’s what you need to know backwards and forwards for a successful pitch.


### Funding


Know exactly where you stand financially before you pitch. Buyers will want to understand that you can actually support the business if they say yes. That means you can fund inventory, cover slotting fees, run promotions, and survive the gap between shipping products and getting paid.


### The Four P’s


Nail down the four P’s:


1. **Product** : Know your product inside and out: ingredients, sourcing,[certifications](https://thefreshfactory.co/certifications/) , shelf life, packaging dimensions, and how it performs at retail. Be ready to speak to quality, consistency, and scalability.
2. **Price** : Your pricing needs to work across the entire supply chain and include your cost of goods, your margin, the retailer’s margin, and any distributor margin in between. Know your price point cold and make sure it’s competitive on shelf without leaving you underwater.
3. **Place** : Understand which channel you’re targeting and why it makes sense for your product. While it might be the best fit for your product, going the natural channel is the most expensive play, according to Donna Duwe, Executive Vice President of Sales at The Fresh Factory. However, independents are more likely to take a chance on new products.
4. **Promotion** : Landing a rotation gets your product on the shelf. What keeps it there is sell-through, and retailers know the difference. Come in with a real promotional plan for trade promotions, demos, digital marketing, and any co-op advertising. “Whatever the strategy is, you’ve got to do the diligence behind what it takes to be successful within each platform,” says Bill Besenhofer, CEO and Co-founder of The Fresh Factory.


### Product Differentiators


What makes your product different from everything else already on that shelf? Be specific. “Better quality” isn’t enough. Know your unique selling proposition, who your target consumer is, and why they’ll choose you over the competition. Bring data if you have it, whether that’s sales velocity, customer reviews, or retail traction you’ve already built.


## What Grocery Buyers Look For


Understanding CPG buyer expectations makes you a better pitcher. Here’s the reality: priorities vary by chain, by category, and even by individual buyer. But there are a few things that tend to matter across the board.


Buyers want to see momentum. If your product is already moving somewhere, that’s proof of concept. At the same time, they love being first on something truly unique, especially if there’s an opportunity for exclusivity.


Beyond your product itself, buyers are often evaluated on how well their category performs, but also on how well they’re meeting broader company goals around sustainability, global sourcing, and supporting women- or minority-owned brands. Knowing where your product fits into those priorities can give you an edge.


Where you start also matters. Independent grocers and regional chains are far more likely to take a chance on a local or emerging brand than a national retailer. National chains tend to make selections based on corporate directives. They also move slower and require more proof of concept.
