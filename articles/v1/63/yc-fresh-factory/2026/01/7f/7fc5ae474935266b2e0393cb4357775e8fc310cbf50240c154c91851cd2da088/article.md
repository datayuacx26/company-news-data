# Unboxing the Food Packaging Trends of 2026

[Packaging](https://thefreshfactory.co/blog-categories/packaging/) ,


[Trends](https://thefreshfactory.co/blog-categories/trends/)


January 30, 2026


Much like judging a book by its cover, consumers look for food packaging that catches their eye. For that reason, startups and innovative brands seek completely unique packaging options to help them stand out on the shelves.


“Think protein balls or bites in pillow packs,” says Donna Duwe, Executive Vice President of Sales at The Fresh Factory.


Wondering what packaging options will go to market in 2026? Here are the upcoming food packaging trends we’re seeing at The Fresh Factory.


## Convenient + Portion-Controlled Formats


Busy consumers continue to prioritize convenience. The packaging world sees this in the prevalence of resealable pouches, single-serve formats, sachets, and portion-controlled packs. With the rising use of GLP-1s, packaging that prioritizes portion control remains a strong growth area, particularly for snacks and functional foods.


We’re also seeing increased interest in modular and mix-and-match formats. Examples include dual-compartment packs, peel-and-pour cups, and snack pairings that support both personalization and mindful eating. Flexible packaging, in particular, is well-positioned to lead this shift thanks to its lightweighting benefits and adaptability.


## Sustainable Food Packaging


Sustainability still matters, especially for consumers of better-for-you brands. When it comes to packaging, the focus is shifting from ideals to execution. Brands are prioritizing solutions that deliver real environmental progress without sacrificing barrier performance, shelf life, or line efficiency.


Environmentally-friendly food packaging includes:


- Compostable, biodegradable food packaging, such as bioplastic films
- Recyclable food packaging, like the[HelloFresh recycled packaging box](https://www.hellofresh.com/about/sustainability/packaging)
- Monomaterial structures that are easier to recycle


**Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR)** : Seven states have now passed[Extended Producer Responsibility laws](https://epr.sustainablepackaging.org/) for packaging. Under EPR, companies that sell packaged products in these states must pay fees to fund recycling programs and infrastructure, with costs based on the amount and type of packaging they use.


Designing with sustainability in mind can lower EPR fees while meeting your brand’s environmental goals. The Fresh Factory partners with vendors that help brands navigate EPR compliance and optimize their packaging strategies.


## A New Look for Wellness Brands


Wellness continues to drive purchasing decisions. But brands must evolve how they communicate that message. Gone are the days when muted colors and earthy cues were the only way to signal “healthy.” In 2026, packaging design for natural and functional foods will feel more confident, contemporary, and emotionally engaging.


Think: clean layouts that clearly communicate benefits — energy, mood, digestion, etc. — paired with bolder colors, playful typography, and sensory details that enhance the experience. The goal isn’t to shout[health claims](https://thefreshfactory.co/certifications/) , but to build trust while making products feel enjoyable, approachable, and relevant.


## A “K-Shaped” Approach to Health Foods


In 2026, wellness isn’t one-size-fits-all. Some consumers are trading up for premium ingredients and elevated experiences, while others are looking for cost-effective solutions that don’t compromise their values. Smart packaging strategies recognize both realities.


Clean-label brands are increasingly deploying tiered packaging systems: recyclable, cost-efficient formats for everyday use alongside premium designs featuring reusable or refillable elements. The most successful brands maintain a cohesive visual identity across price points, ensuring that all consumers feel included in the same wellness movement.


## Versatility to Cross Sales Channels


As e-commerce and omnichannel retail continue to expand, packaging must perform across more environments than ever. Lightweight, durable, and recyclable formats are increasingly important in[food logistics](https://thefreshfactory.co/food-logistics/) , particularly for food brands shipping smaller, more frequent orders.


Corrugated cases will remain dominant, but flexible, mail-ready packaging is gaining traction for suitable SKUs. In all cases, packaging is more consumer-facing than ever. As such, it’s exposed to more scrutiny for both sustainability and brand consistency.
