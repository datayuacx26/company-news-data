# Five business advantages that hinge on your QA strategy

#### Most organizations have quality assurance. Fewer have quality assurance that actively drives business value. The difference lies in where it sits and how seriously it’s treated as a strategic capability rather than a final-stage filter.


Quality assurance (QA) is part of an organization’s overall governance process. It involves testing development code and assuring it’s been built to the required specifications. It’s a critical step in the application life cycle. It enables an organization to detect and fix defects before the final application or feature release, when they are far more costly to resolve. Embedding quality earlier in the development life cycle can reduce costly rework and improve release outcomes before issues reach customers.


Enterprises want faster releases, better customer experiences, improved operational resilience, and greater engineering productivity However, many still treat[quality assurance](https://www.unisys.com/solutions/cai/applications/#qualityEngineeringAndAssurance) as a downstream checkpoint, a function that catches defects rather than prevents them. This disconnect is a strategic blind spot. Closing it opens five meaningful business advantages.


### #1: Protect your brand reputation


When customers use your applications, they expect a seamless, user-friendly experience. If they experience software defects or difficulty navigating its capabilities, they’re less likely to use it and may seek alternatives. The same applies to employees' expectations of internal applications.


Upstream QA catches customer-facing defects before they can impact users and potentially harm your organization’s brand reputation. Reliable applications strengthen customer trust and loyalty.


### #2: Minimize costly rework


Organizations commit a set number of hours to developers, project managers, testers, and other employees involved in application development. Quality failures require costly, unanticipated rework from these team members and the reallocation of budget previously earmarked for other spending.


When QA is embedded earlier in the development cycle and supported by continuous, automated testing, releases go more smoothly and your organization’s developers can spend time working on new capabilities and new applications. This can mean more opportunities to profit, attracting new customers, and gaining additional traction with existing customers.


### #3: Avoid productivity and operational disruptions


Multiple team members are invested in releasing high-quality applications. Software issues disrupt operations and cause churn by forcing employees to redo work, pulling them away from other tasks. Additional people may even need to be pulled in to work on a fix. This decreases overall productivity.


Teams with QA embedded earlier in the development process benefit from more stable releases, increasing employee productivity and keeping operations running smoothly. This minimizes operational and productivity disruptions and delays, so you can keep innovating and achieving your business objectives.


### #4: Accelerate time-to-market


Unstable releases, those that slip through testing with undiscovered defects, undermine an organization’s ability to overcome the pressures of market competition. If competitors have stronger quality assurance practices, their applications might be more reliable and more attractive to consumers who prioritize reliability.


Organizations with mature QA practices, increasingly powered by AI-driven and automated testing, can accelerate time-to-market without compromising the commitment to deliver on schedule. A well-executed quality assurance process helps ensure the product matches expectations within the promised timeframe, increasing customer confidence.


### #5: Build digital resilience


Digital resilience separates market leaders from followers because the former don’t compromise on the quality of their[applications](https://www.unisys.com/solutions/cai/applications/#applicationManagedServices) . They recognize that delivering high-quality applications helps them stay agile and able to adapt and recover when the unexpected occurs.


Strategic QA protects your digital ecosystem by identifying defects and security vulnerabilities early and ensuring seamless performance before issues ripple through your business. If the unexpected happens, such as a spike in users or an emerging security threat, your systems stay responsive and reliable.


### Consider the business benefits of quality assurance


Quality assurance clearly matters. The real question is whether yours is positioned to drive business value or whether it’s still treated as a checkpoint at the end of the line. As automation, autonomous systems, and agentic AI grow, QA must evolve alongside them. That means building quality in earlier, resourcing it properly, and measuring it against business outcomes, such as release velocity, operational stability, customer satisfaction, and resilience.
