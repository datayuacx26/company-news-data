# Connecting Energy Education to Opportunity

February is Career and Technical Education Month. It’s a time to recognize the programs and educators preparing students for skilled careers that power our communities and economy.


Across the country, schools are working to close the gap between what students learn and what employers need. Career and Technical Education (CTE) programs are leading that effort, offering hands-on training in fields like welding, electrical work, heavy equipment operation, information technology, and healthcare. These aren’t fallback options. They’re direct pathways to stable, well-paying careers that don’t require a four-year degree.


## **The Skills Gap is Real**


The energy sector, like many industries, is facing a workforce challenge. Experienced professionals are retiring faster than new workers are entering the field. According to industry reports, nearly half of the current energy workforce will be eligible for retirement within the next decade. That leaves thousands of open positions for technicians, operators, engineers, and tradespeople who keep America’s energy infrastructure running.


The need isn’t limited to energy. Manufacturing, construction, logistics, and healthcare all report similar shortages. What these industries have in common is a reliance on skilled workers who can solve problems, operate complex systems, and adapt to new technologies.


## **Why CTE Works**


CTE programs succeed because they connect learning to real-world application. Students don’t just memorize formulas—they use them. They weld joints, troubleshoot circuits, and operate machinery. They earn industry certifications while still in high school, giving them a head start before they even graduate.


These programs also expose students to careers they might not have considered. Many young people don’t realize that skilled trades offer competitive salaries, job stability, and opportunities for advancement. A diesel mechanic, industrial electrician, or pipeline technician can earn a strong income while staying rooted in their community.


Dual-enrollment partnerships between high schools and community colleges make it even easier for students to gain credentials and enter the workforce quickly. Some students graduate high school with both a diploma and a technical certificate, ready to start earning on day one.


## **Building Stronger Communities**


When students stay local and build careers close to home, everyone benefits. Communities retain talent. Businesses find the skilled workers they need. Families build generational stability.


CTE programs also strengthen connections between schools and employers. When energy companies, manufacturers, and contractors partner with schools, they can shape curriculum to reflect real industry needs. Internships, mentorships, and job shadowing opportunities give students a clearer picture of what these careers actually look like.


## **Looking Ahead**


The future of America’s workforce depends on what we teach today. Career and Technical Education programs are already making a difference, but they need continued support—from funding and equipment to industry partnerships and community awareness.


As we recognize CTE Month this February, let’s celebrate the educators, students, and industry partners who are building the skilled workforce our country depends on. These programs aren’t just about jobs. They’re about creating opportunities, strengthening communities, and ensuring the next generation is ready to meet the challenges ahead.


The pathways are there. Now it’s up to all of us to make sure students know where they lead.
