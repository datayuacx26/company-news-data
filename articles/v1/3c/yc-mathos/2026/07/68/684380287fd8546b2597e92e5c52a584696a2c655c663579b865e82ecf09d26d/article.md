# What Are The Hardest AP Classes

AP (Advanced Placement) classes are college-level classes offered in high school. While all AP classes are challenging, some are consistently ranked as more difficult than others. Based on recent exam pass rates, the hardest AP classes include **Physics 1 (45.6% pass rate)** , **United States History (47.5% pass rate)** , **Latin (56.7% pass rate)** , and **Calculus AB (58% pass rate)** .


However, **determining the hardest AP class is subjective and can vary depending on individual strengths, interests, and preparation.** For example, if you're not good at Math, AP Calculus could be the hardest course. Vice versa, if you're good at Math, it can be the easiest AP course.


That's why when choosing the right AP class for you, you should consider your academic strengths and ability to manage the workload alongside other commitments.


If you’re curious about which AP classes students struggle with the most, looking at exam pass rates can reveal some interesting insights. Let’s dive into the hardest AP classes based on these numbers.

### Hardest AP Class vs Hardest AP Exam: What's the Difference

Note that there is a difference between class difficulty and exam difficulty.


The **hardest AP exam** is the one that most students struggle to pass, meaning the test itself is really tough.


The **hardest AP class** is about the overall difficulty of the course, including homework, projects, and how hard the subject is to learn. Even if the exam isn't the hardest, the class itself is tough because of all the work you have to do.


For example, AP Physics 1 has a low passing rate, so it’s ranked as one of the hardest exams. However, AP Chemistry can be a harder class overall because it has a lot of material to learn and includes tough labs and assignments.


## How to Determine What's The Hardest AP Course: 4 Factors to Consider


Determining the hardest AP course isn't straightforward, as difficulty can vary based on several factors. Here are four key factors to consider when assessing AP course difficulty:


1. **Pass Rates and Score Distribution**


As I mentioned above, there's a difference between class difficulty and exam difficulty. Pass rates reveal course difficulty. AP Physics 1’s low pass rate of 45.6% shows that many students find its concepts and problem-solving highly challenging. In contrast, AP Calculus BC’s 78% pass rate reflects its advanced and complex material but also highlights that students who take the course are typically well-prepared, leading to greater success.


1. **Content Complexity**


Some courses demand more advanced thinking and skills. Even if they have a high passing rate, the course itself is challenging. For instance, AP Physics C: Mechanics combines calculus with complex physics concepts, requiring students to solve intricate problems that test both mathematical and scientific understanding.


1. **Student Background and Preparation**


Individual academic strengths dramatically impact course difficulty. Take the AP Chinese Language as an example. If Chinese is your first or second language, it can be the easiest AP class for you, but it can be incredibly challenging for students without prior language exposure.


1. **Time Management and Workload**


The volume of material and study required can make a course hard. A good example is AP United States History, which demands extensive reading, critical analysis, and essay writing across multiple historical periods. It can be very challenging if you're not prepared for the workload or don't have enough time to study for the exam.


## How to Choose the Easiest AP Course For You


Choosing the right AP courses is like creating a personalized academic roadmap that not only makes you shine in college applications but also gives you a head start on your educational journey by saving time and money, and helping you discover your true academic passions.


**Choose a course based on your academic strengths**


When you are good at a specific subject, it's easier for you to learn more about the topic. Let's say that you're good at numbers and got high scores in regular high school math classes, then AP math should be easy for you to manage. Anyway, talk to your teacher before enrolling in an AP course.


If you're not sure about your strong subject, consider the factors above and see which AP course is harder for you at the moment.


**Consider your learning style and the course format**


Teaching methods and assessment styles can also determine the difficulty of a course depending on your learning style. If you like discussion-based classes, you might find AP English Literature or AP World History more manageable. On the other hand, if you excel with hands-on learning, AP Computer Science or AP Studio Art could be a better fit. It'll be easier for you to succeed in the course if you understand how the course is taught and if it aligns with your preferred learning style, even better.


**Look at the prerequisites and build gradually**


Start with AP courses with fewer prerequisites or build on subjects you've already mastered. For example, if you've done well in biology, AP Biology might be an easier choice than jumping straight into AP Physics without a strong foundation. This approach allows you to gradually increase the difficulty of your AP courses over time, building your confidence and skills along the way. Remember, it's not just about choosing the easiest course, but also about setting yourself up for long-term success in your academic journey.

## FAQs


### How many AP classes should you take?


The number of AP classes you should take depends on your goals, interests, and ability to manage the workload. For instance, most students take 1 to 2 AP classes in 10th grade, 2 to 4 in 11th grade, and 3 to 5 in 12th grade. We suggest you focus on quality over quantity.


### How do AP scores affect college admission?


AP scores can strengthen your college application by showing your ability to handle rigorous coursework. While most colleges don’t require AP scores for admission, high scores can demonstrate academic skills and may help you earn college credit or advanced placement.


### Will college and university accept a 3 on AP exams?


Many colleges accept a 3 on AP exams for credit or placement depending on the school and the subject. Some competitive universities may require a 4 or 5 for credit, so check the policies of the colleges you're applying to.


### What is the easiest AP course?


The easiest AP course depends on your interests and strengths. However, AP Psychology, AP Human Geography, and AP Environmental Science are often considered easier due to lighter workloads and higher pass rates. Choose a course that aligns with your skills and goals.


### What are the benefits of taking a challenging AP course?


Taking a challenging AP course can help you develop critical thinking, time management, and problem-solving skills. It also strengthens your college applications, prepares you for college-level work, and may earn you college credit or advanced placement.
