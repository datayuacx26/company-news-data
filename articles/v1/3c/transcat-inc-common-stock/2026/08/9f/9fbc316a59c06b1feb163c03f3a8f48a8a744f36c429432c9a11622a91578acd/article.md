# How to Choose the Right OGI Camera: Matching Optical Gas Imaging Technology to Your Application

**Why Selecting the Right OGI Camera Matters**


Gas leaks present significant challenges across utilities, renewable energy facilities, industrial plants, and critical infrastructure. Whether operators are searching for methane emissions at a renewable natural gas facility, inspecting utility substations for sulfur hexafluoride (SF₆) leaks, or monitoring industrial process gases, early detection is essential for maintaining safety, environmental compliance, operational efficiency, and asset reliability.


The challenge is that not all gases behave the same, and not all Optical Gas Imaging (OGI) cameras are designed to detect the same compounds. Selecting the right FLIR OGI camera can help engineers and technicians locate leaks faster, reduce product loss, and perform inspections safely from a distance without shutting down operations.


**Understanding OGI Applications Across Industries**


Optical Gas Imaging technology has become increasingly valuable because it allows inspectors to visualize otherwise invisible gas emissions in real time. Unlike traditional leak detection methods that often require close access to equipment, OGI cameras can rapidly scan large areas, inspect hard-to-reach assets, and identify leaks while systems remain operational. This capability is particularly important in utility substations, renewable natural gas facilities, oil and gas operations, industrial manufacturing sites, and contractor-led maintenance projects where safety and uptime are top priorities.


For organizations primarily focused on methane, hydrocarbon, and volatile organic compound (VOC) emissions, the[FLIR Gx320 Optical Gas Imaging Camera](https://www.transcat.com/rental-equipment/thermal-camera-rental/rent-flir-gx320-optical-gas-imaging-camera)


provides a powerful solution. Designed specifically for hydrocarbon detection, the Gx320 delivers gas quantification analytics and high sensitivity for methane and VOC leak detection, making it well suited for natural gas infrastructure, oil and gas operations, renewable natural gas facilities, and environmental compliance programs. Its ability to help inspectors identify both the location and severity of leaks can streamline maintenance planning and support emissions reduction initiatives.


**How Do You Choose the Best Camera for Utility and Power Applications?**


Utility companies face a different challenge when maintaining high-voltage infrastructure. Many substations rely on sulfur hexafluoride (SF₆)-insulated switchgear and circuit breakers, making leak detection a critical reliability and environmental concern. This makes the camera particularly valuable for utility maintenance teams, contractors, and substation asset managers responsible for maintaining grid reliability.


In situations where organizations need greater flexibility across multiple gas types, the[FLIR GF77 OGI Camera with Lenses](https://www.transcat.com/rental-equipment/thermal-camera-rental/flir-gf77-for-rent)

**Specialized OGI Solutions for Industrial Gas Detection**


Industrial manufacturing and process facilities often work with gases beyond methane and SF₆. In these environments, selecting an OGI camera designed for the target gas is essential. The[FLIR G343 Optical Gas Imaging Camera](https://www.transcat.com/rental-equipment/thermal-camera-rental/rent-flir-g343-optical-gas-imaging-camera)

Its non-contact inspection capability allows operators to identify CO₂ leaks without interrupting production or exposing personnel to unnecessary risk.


Facilities working with refrigerants and hydrofluorocarbons can benefit from the[FLIR G304 Optical Gas Imaging Camera](https://www.transcat.com/rental-equipment/thermal-camera-rental/rent-flir-g304-optical-gas-imaging-camera)
