# Why Equipment Goes Out of Calibration

# Why Equipment Goes Out of Calibration


Here’s the hard truth: even the most reliable equipment doesn’t stay accurate forever.


Over time, all test and measurement instruments experience calibration drift. It’s not a matter of if, but when. Since you’re relying on these tools for accuracy, quality, compliance, or safety-critical work, that drift can quietly put your operations at risk.


## The Hidden Drivers Of Calibration Drift


So, why does this happen?


- Normal wear and tear from daily operation and handling
- Environmental factors like[temperature, humidity](https://www.transcat.com/calibration-services/capabilities/disciplines/temperature-humidity/temperature-calibration-labs/rtd-calibration-labs) , and vibration
- Aging of internal components, even during storage
- Electrical or physical stress, such as overloads or drops
- Natural drift inherent to all measurement systems


Individually, each of these factors seems minor. Together, they steadily push instruments out of tolerance.


**Everyday wear adds up.**


Even careful, routine use introduces small stresses over time. Repeated adjustments, handling, and minor impacts slowly shift performance, often without any obvious warning signs.


**Your environment plays a role.**


Precision instruments are sensitive by design and respond to their surroundings. Fluctuating temperatures, excess humidity, airborne contaminants, and vibration can all influence sensitive components and accelerate drift.


**Timing is everything.**


Calibration doesn’t stop aging. Internal components naturally degrade whether equipment is used daily or sits in storage, making time-based calibration just as critical as usage-based schedules.


**Stress and mishandling accelerate failure.**


Overloads, drops,[power surges](https://www.transcat.com/calibration-services/capabilities/disciplines/electronic-calibration-labs/ac-dc-power-supply-calibration-labs) , or improper transport can push instruments out of tolerance instantly, or weaken them enough to fail sooner than expected.


**Drift Is Unavoidable, But Manageable**


Your equipment is an investment that you use every day. Taking care of your tools to ensure they last as long as possible is paramount. Out-of-spec readings lead to inaccurate results, non-compliance, product failures, and costly rework.


No matter how advanced the design, all measurement equipment changes over time. Regular calibration is a preventative measure.[Let us help](https://www.transcat.com/calibration-services/service-types/lab-capabilities) .


## Instrument Care


While calibration drift can’t be eliminated, proper handling and care significantly slow it down. General best practices include:


- Operating instruments in controlled environments whenever possible (typically 20–25 °C and 40–60% relative humidity)
- Avoiding unnecessary exposure to dust, moisture, and vibration
- Using stable work surfaces and proper accessories
- Storing equipment in protective cases when not in use
- Handling instruments carefully during transport and setup


## Instrument-Specific Care


### [Liquid Flow Meters](https://www.transcat.com/liquid-flow-meter-calibration-services)


Liquid flow meters are highly sensitive to contamination, pressure changes, and improper handling.


**Best practices:**


- Flush the meter before and after use to prevent residue buildup
- Avoid operating outside rated flow, pressure, or temperature limits
- Ensure proper orientation and straight-run piping during installation
- Protect meters from freezing temperatures and sudden pressure shocks
- Store dry and capped to prevent corrosion or debris ingress


### Torque Wrenches


Torque wrenches are mechanical precision tools that drift easily if mishandled.


**Best practices:**


- Always return adjustable torque wrenches to their lowest setting after use
- Avoid using torque wrenches as breaker bars or general tools
- Apply force smoothly—no jerking or impact loading
- Do not exceed rated torque limits
- Store in protective cases to prevent drops or misalignment


Consistent handling preserves spring integrity and accuracy over time.
