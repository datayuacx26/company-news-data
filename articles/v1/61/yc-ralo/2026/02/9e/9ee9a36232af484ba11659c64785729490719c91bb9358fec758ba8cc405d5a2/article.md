# You're Almost Home: How to Avoid Mortgage Mayhem Before Closing

## You Are Incredibly Close


If you are reading this, you are incredibly close to the finish line. The home is selected, the paperwork is largely complete, and you can probably already imagine where your furniture will go. This is an exciting stage in the process, and you absolutely deserve to feel that excitement.


However, there is one very important detail that borrowers often do not realize. **Until your loan officially funds, your financial profile is still being reviewed.** This does not mean anyone expects perfection. It simply means this is not the ideal moment for bold financial experiments.


## Why Lenders Continue Verifying Everything


Mortgage approvals follow guidelines established by agencies such as Fannie Mae, Freddie Mac, the Federal Housing Administration, and the U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs. These organizations require lenders to confirm that your financial situation remains consistent through closing and funding.


That confirmation process often includes a final verbal verification of employment, a possible credit refresh, and a review of financial activity if closing timelines shift. Your loan was approved based on a snapshot of your finances. The lender simply needs to confirm that the snapshot still matches reality.


## Please Avoid Changing Jobs Right Now


This is not the ideal week to resign and pursue your dream career as a travel blogger. This is also not the ideal time to switch from salaried employment to commission income or independent contracting.


Even if your new position offers higher pay, lenders may require a documented history of that income type before they can use it to qualify you. **Commission, bonus, and self-employment income typically require a two-year track record** to be considered stable.


Employment is usually re-verified shortly before closing. If the lender discovers a change at that point, the file may need to be re-underwritten. That process can delay closing or, in some cases, affect approval. If a job change is unavoidable, notify your mortgage professional immediately so a strategy can be developed.


## Please Do Not Open New Credit Accounts


The furniture showroom will make very persuasive arguments. The appliance store will promise zero percent financing. The home improvement store will insist that everything is easier with a brand new credit line.


Opening a new credit card, auto loan, or promotional financing account can directly impact your approval. Every new account creates a credit inquiry and introduces a new monthly payment. Even deferred interest or "same as cash" plans count as debt obligations in underwriting calculations.


**Mortgage approvals rely heavily on your debt-to-income ratio.** Adding even one new payment can push your numbers beyond allowable limits. Waiting a few weeks can prevent unnecessary complications.


## Be Careful With Large Purchases


Large purchases on existing credit cards can also create issues. Lenders may pull a final credit report before funding occurs. If your balances increase significantly, your credit utilization ratio rises.


Higher utilization can lower your credit score and increase your minimum required monthly payments. Both of those changes can affect loan eligibility or pricing. It is generally safer to postpone major shopping trips until after the loan has funded.


## Avoid Moving Large Amounts of Money


Lenders carefully review the source of your down payment and closing funds. Large transfers between accounts can trigger additional documentation requests.


Underwriting guidelines require lenders to verify that funds are not undisclosed loans and that they meet federal compliance standards. If significant sums suddenly appear or move between accounts, underwriters may request explanations and supporting paperwork.


If you need to transfer money for any reason, **consult your mortgage professional beforehand.** A brief conversation can prevent a lengthy delay.


## Please Do Not Co-Sign for Anyone


You may be generous and eager to help a family member or friend secure financing. This particular window of time is not ideal for that generosity.


When you co-sign a loan, the new debt becomes part of your financial profile. The monthly payment is typically included in your debt-to-income calculation. Even if someone else promises to make every payment, the liability still affects your approval.


Waiting until after funding is usually the safest course of action.


## Do Not Close Existing Credit Accounts


Closing a credit card may feel like a responsible financial move. Unfortunately, it can lower your credit score in the short term.


When you close an account, your total available credit decreases. This change can increase your credit utilization ratio. It may also affect the average age of your credit history over time.


Unless your lender specifically instructs you to close an account, it is generally safer to leave everything exactly as it is until funding is complete.


## Be Mindful of Large Deposits


Large deposits into your bank account can raise questions during underwriting. Lenders must document the source of significant funds to ensure compliance with mortgage regulations.


Gift funds from family members are often allowed, but they require proper documentation and a formal gift letter. Random large deposits without clear sourcing can create delays while explanations are gathered.


If you are unsure whether a deposit could create an issue, consult your mortgage professional before making it.


## The Safest Strategy Is Financial Consistency


The simplest way to protect your approval is to keep your financial life predictable for a little while longer. Continue working at your current job. Continue paying your bills on time. Avoid new debt and avoid unnecessary account changes.


Think of this phase as financial cruise control during the final stretch of a long trip. You would not suddenly experiment with the steering wheel one mile before your destination. The same principle applies here.


## You Are Almost There


The period between final approval and funding can feel like the slowest part of the journey. In reality, it is simply a brief verification stage designed to protect everyone involved.


If you are considering any financial change, no matter how minor it may seem, **reach out before making a decision.** A short conversation can prevent a long delay. Staying steady for a few more weeks will help ensure that your closing happens smoothly and that you receive your keys without unnecessary surprises.
