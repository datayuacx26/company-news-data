## Using the CEFR Framework to Self-Assess Your Level


The


**[Common European Framework of Reference for Languages](https://www.coe.int/en/web/common-european-framework-reference-languages)**


serves as a clear, standardized map for your language learning journey. By evaluating your proficiency across the A1 to C2 spectrum, you can set specific learning goals and track your growth in listening, reading, and speaking.


Understanding that your English progress is rarely a straight line is completely normal. You might find your reading comprehension at an upper-intermediate level while your conversational skills remain at a basic stage. This is perfectly fine. Using a framework like this helps you identify specific areas that need more attention, ensuring your practice is balanced and intentional.


1. **A1 to A2:** Focus on mastering basic daily vocabulary and functional phrases for simple introductions.


2. **B1 to B2:** Set goals around participating in nuanced workplace discussions and expressing your own opinions clearly.


3. **C1 to C2:** Aim for the ability to manage spontaneous, complex conversations where you think directly in the language.


4. **Cross-skill tracking:** Use the framework to check if your speaking ability matches your written comprehension.


Regularly applying these descriptors to your performance makes it easier to celebrate small wins. When you can comfortably handle a new real-life scenario, you are not just checking a box; you are advancing your everyday communication skills. This method of self-assessment helps you set realistic milestones, turning consistent, bite-sized practice into long-term fluency.
