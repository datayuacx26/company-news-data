# Call monitoring software: what it covers beyond recording

***See how full-coverage monitoring catches quality and compliance issues that live spot-checks miss.***


Your QA team can review perhaps 20 calls a week per agent, if they’re fast. Your contact center handles thousands. That gap is where compliance risk hides, where coaching opportunities go stale, and where a customer experience problem festers until it shows up in a customer satisfaction (CSAT) report or a regulator’s inquiry.


Most teams still run QA manually: a supervisor picks a handful of calls, scores them against a rubric, and moves on. It’s slow, and it only ever touches a sliver of what actually happened on the phones.


Call monitoring software changes that. It analyzes 100% of interactions instead of a sample, flags compliance issues automatically, surfaces coaching opportunities in real time, and separates platforms that just archive audio from ones that help you catch problems before they compound.


## Key takeaways


- Call monitoring software scores quality and compliance signals, not just archived audio.
- Manual review typically covers only a small percentage of your total calls.
- Full-coverage monitoring surfaces patterns sampled reviews structurally can’t catch.
- Evaluate platforms on coverage, workflow fit, and how actionable the output is.
- RingCX’s native AI Quality Management scores 100% of interactions automatically.


## What is call monitoring software?


Call monitoring software evaluates live and recorded calls against defined quality and[call center compliance criteria](https://www.ringcentral.com/call-center-compliance.html) , then surfaces the results for coaching and audit purposes. It’s the analysis layer that sits on top of your calls, not just the storage layer underneath them.


That distinction matters for how you budget and staff QA. A platform that only stores audio still requires a human to listen, score, and log every interaction you want reviewed. A platform built for conversation analytics does that scoring automatically at a scale no QA team could match manually.


### What call monitoring software actually does


At its core, call monitoring software listens to, records, and then uses conversational intelligence to run every call against scoring criteria you define. Did the agent verify identity, disclose required terms, follow the escalation path, hit the tone and empathy markers you coach toward? Most of all, it does this across every interaction, not a sampled subset.


Call monitoring software also flags compliance and procedural gaps automatically. If an agent skips a required disclosure or a call touches a regulated topic without the right script cues, the system catches it the moment the call ends, not weeks later during an audit.


The output feeds coaching, not just record-keeping. Supervisors get real-time dashboards with[key performance indicators](https://www.ringcentral.com/us/en/blog/customer-service-kpi/) (KPIs) and agent performance trends across teams. They can see who is slipping on a specific skill, which scripts underperform, or where compliance risk clusters.


Metrics like average handling time, first-call resolution rate, and call duration become coaching inputs, not just historical logs. That’s fundamentally different from just maintaining a stack of individually scored transcripts.


This also changes what a call center manager’s week looks like. Instead of spending most of their time listening to calls and filling out evaluation forms, they spend it reviewing trend reports and building coaching plans around what the data already flagged. The scoring work moves off their calendar, while the judgment work stays on it.


### Call monitoring versus call recording


Recording captures the call. Monitoring evaluates it. Those are two different jobs, and a lot of platforms only do the first one well.


A recording system gives you an audio file and maybe a transcript. That’s useful for dispute resolution and training reference, but it doesn’t tell you anything until someone listens to it. Monitoring software applies scoring criteria automatically, so you get a quality score, a compliance flag, or a coaching note without a human having to review the file first.


If your current stack only records, you’re still doing QA manually. You’ve just made the manual step easier to schedule.


## Types of call monitoring software


Call monitoring platforms generally fall into three categories. Knowing which one you’re evaluating matters more than any single feature checklist.


### Contact center QA solutions


Native contact center QA tools live inside your existing contact center platform and score calls automatically as part of the same system that routes and records them. Because they’re built into the platform, they don’t require a separate login or data sync, and they typically cover 100% of interactions by default instead of as an add-on.


### Call recording tools


Call recording platforms with analytics add-ons start as a recording and storage tool, then layer scoring or tagging features on top. These work well if you already have a recording system you like, but the analysis layer is often bolted on rather than native to the workflow, so coverage and integration depth vary widely from vendor to vendor.


### Conversation intelligence software


Standalone conversation intelligence platforms sit outside any single contact center or phone system and connect to multiple communications tools at once. They’re built for organizations that want one quality and compliance standard across contact center, sales, and service teams, not a tool scoped to a single department.


## Why manual call monitoring leaves gaps


Manual monitoring is expensive to scale, so most teams never scale it past a handful of reviewers. Live monitoring ties up a supervisor for the length of the call. Manual review after the fact ties up a supervisor’s time and delays coaching by days, often too late to matter. Neither approach covers enough ground.


The result is most teams sample a small fraction of their calls and extrapolate from there. In an article covering AI in customer care,[McKinsey](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/operations/our-insights/operations-blog/ai-mastery-in-customer-care-raising-the-bar-for-quality-assurance) found that manual QA is often limited to less than 5% of total call volume at most contact centers.


Under 5% coverage means the other 95% of your calls go unreviewed. Outliers, edge cases, and slow-building compliance drift don’t show up in a sample that small. They show up later, in an audit finding, a regulatory complaint, or a CSAT dip your team can’t trace back to a root cause. A 5% sample and 100% coverage carry entirely different risk profiles.


Think about how a sample gets picked in most centers: a supervisor grabs whatever calls are easiest to pull, often the ones flagged by a customer complaint or an obvious escalation.


That approach already skews toward the calls most likely to look bad and away from the quiet, slow-building issue, like a script deviation an agent repeats 40 times before anyone notices. A 5% sample built that way tells you about your worst days, not your average ones.


## The business case for full-coverage monitoring


Auditing your QA spend gets a lot more interesting once you look at what automated coverage actually returns. In the same article, McKinsey estimates that companies using AI to monitor customer interactions can see more than 50% savings in QA costs, up to 30% improvement in agent performance, and up to 10% improvement in CSAT.


Run those numbers against your current QA headcount and review backlog. If you’re paying a team to manually score a 5% sample, automating that scoring frees those hours for the coaching conversations that actually move performance, not the data entry that precedes them.


Full coverage also changes when problems surface. Instead of catching a compliance gap three months in during an audit, you catch it on day one because every call gets scored. Instead of coaching an agent on last week’s flagged call, you coach on this morning’s pattern across dozens of calls.


That’s the real shift: coaching moves from reactive, built around whichever calls happened to get sampled, to pattern-based, built around what’s actually happening across the floor.


## What to look for when evaluating call monitoring software


The right platform closes the gap between what happens on a call and what a supervisor can act on. Use these five criteria to separate tools that automate quality assurance from tools that just digitize it.


### 1. Coverage


Ask any vendor directly whether the platform scores 100% of interactions or a sampled subset. A platform that still relies on sampling has only automated part of the old process, and the coverage gap remains.


### 2. Integration with your existing stack


Check integration with the contact center and communications platform you already run. Look for systems that support smart call routing, interactive voice response (IVR) integration, and other core platform features.


Monitoring software that lives outside your existing workflow adds a login, a data sync, and a gap between where calls happen and where they get scored. That is where adoption stalls.


Workforce planning teams feel this friction directly when scheduling and coaching data live in separate systems. This is why it’s worth reviewing your[workforce management approach](https://www.ringcentral.com/us/en/blog/workforce-management-call-center/) alongside any QA platform decision.


### 3. Actionability


A quality score with no context doesn’t help a supervisor coach anyone. Look for output that flags the specific moment in the call that triggered the score, ties it to a coachable skill, and tracks it over time. Raw numbers on a dashboard are a reporting exercise. Contextualized flags tied to specific coaching actions are a performance program.


### 4. Compliance posture


Compliance posture describes how consistently a vendor’s platform, data handling, and internal controls meet the regulatory standards that apply to your industry, not whether the company can produce a certificate on request. A strong compliance posture pairs third-party audited certifications with controls that hold up under an actual audit or regulatory review, not just marketing copy.


Ask any vendor for proof of relevant certifications and attestations before you sign, including:


- **SOC 2 (Type 2):** An independent audit validates security, availability, and confidentiality controls, typically renewed annually.
- **ISO 27001:** Certifies that the vendor’s information security management system meets international standards for confidentiality, integrity, and availability.
- **HIPAA:** Relevant if calls involve protected health information. Look for a signed Business Associate Agreement (BAA) and controls mapped to HIPAA safeguards, not just a mention of the acronym.
- **GDPR:** Relevant if you handle EU resident data. Look for documented data processing agreements and data residency options, since GDPR compliance is a legal obligation, not a certification a vendor can simply hold.
- **PCI DSS:** Relevant if calls or recordings ever touch payment card data.


RingCentral publishes its current[compliance certifications](https://www.ringcentral.com/trust-center/compliance.html) , including ISO 27001, SOC 2+, and PCI attestations for its contact center platform, a useful benchmark for what documentation any vendor should be able to produce.


Confirm the platform tracks adherence against your industry’s specific regulatory requirements, not a generic script-adherence check. Review this against your[call center compliance](https://www.ringcentral.com/call-center-compliance.html) requirements before you commit to a vendor.


### 5. Speed to flag


Catch a compliance gap the same day, and you’ve fixed it. Catch the same gap in a quarterly audit, and you’ve got a finding, and possibly a report you have to file.


Ask any vendor how long it takes between a call ending and a flagged issue reaching a supervisor’s queue. An answer of “during the next review cycle” means the coverage problem is still there, just running on a faster clock.

## FAQs about call monitoring software


### What is call monitoring software?


Call monitoring software is a QA tool that automatically scores live and recorded calls against quality and compliance criteria, producing three outputs: a quality score, a compliance flag, and a coaching insight. It differs from traditional call recording, which only captures and stores audio and still requires a person to listen before any evaluation happens.


### How is call monitoring software different from call recording?


Call recording produces an audio file, while call monitoring software produces an assessment of that file. Recording answers “what was said” and requires manual review to be useful. Monitoring answers “how well the call met your standards,” applying scoring criteria automatically across every interaction so no agent call goes unreviewed for lack of QA staff time.


### Can call monitoring software cover 100% of calls instead of a sample?


Yes. Platforms built with native AI scoring, like[RingCX’s AI Quality Management](https://www.ringcentral.com/ringcx/ai-quality-management.html) , evaluate every interaction automatically instead of relying on a manually sampled subset. That’s a structural difference from tools that still depend on a supervisor listening to a handful of calls each week.


### Does call monitoring software help with compliance, not just quality scoring?


Yes. Automated monitoring flags procedural and regulatory gaps, like missed disclosures or skipped verification steps, at the moment they happen rather than during a retroactive audit. That shortens the window between a compliance miss and a corrective fix.
