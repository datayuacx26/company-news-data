# Before the first shovel hits the ground: How 811 centers can modernize the call that saves lives

## At a glance


- 811 centers can use AI to answer routine questions, collect caller details, and route urgent requests to trained staff.
- The Common Ground Alliance 2024 DIRT Report identifies failure to notify 811 as the top damage root cause, at 24.54% of reported damages.
- AI should support emergency escalation, not replace trained emergency staff.
- RingCentral supports this model through AI Receptionist, RingEX, AI Conversation Expert, and RingCX.


---


When an excavator calls 811 before breaking ground, that call sets off a chain of events measured in hours – not days. Utilities must be notified, lines must be marked, and the excavator must receive a clear response before work can safely begin. In that window, communication is everything.


811 call centers are the operational backbone of this process. They receive millions of inbound dig requests every year, coordinate notifications across dozens of utility operators, and manage a surge in volume every spring when construction season peaks. And they do it with lean teams, aging telecommunications infrastructure, and constituents – both excavators and utility companies – who expect immediate, accurate answers.


The consequences of a missed call or a misrouted inquiry are not abstract. The Common Ground Alliance’s annual DIRT Report consistently finds that failure to notify a One Call center is among the leading causes of underground utility strikes, which result in injuries, fatalities, service outages, and hundreds of millions of dollars in damage each year. As RingCentral’s[Transforming 811 Call Center Operations with AI-Powered Communications](https://assets.ringcentral.com/us/whitepaper/811-call-center-operations-ai.pdf) white paper details, every missed or delayed response to a locate request increases the risk of excavation disasters – gas leaks, electrical hazards, and service disruptions whose consequences can be catastrophic. When the communication system fails, the safety net fails.


Yet many 811 centers still rely on phone infrastructure that cannot scale to seasonal demand, cannot provide 24/7 automated coverage, and cannot give leadership the visibility they need to continuously improve service. A more capable communications approach is not a technology upgrade – it is a public safety imperative.


## What is the main communications challenge for 811 call centers?


The rhythm of a 811 center is unlike almost any other government operation. Call volume is highly predictable in pattern but extreme in peak – a warm spring Monday can bring two to three times the call load of a typical winter day. Staff must handle dig tickets, emergency locate requests, and general inquiries simultaneously, while ensuring that every notification reaches the correct utility within the legally required timeframe.


Several structural pressures compound this challenge:


**Seasonal surges expose staffing limits.** One Call centers cannot hire and train seasonal staff fast enough to absorb the spring construction ramp-up. Every call that goes unanswered, reaches a voicemail, or sits in a queue is a dig request that may proceed without proper notification – a direct safety risk.


**Emergency locates require zero-friction escalation.** When a contractor hits an unexpected obstruction or an excavation is happening near a gas line, they need to reach a live agent immediately. Rigid phone menus and long hold times in those moments are unacceptable. Research shows that[85% of callers who cannot reach a business on the first try will not call back](https://www.ringcentral.com/ai-receptionist.html) – a statistic with real consequences when the caller is reporting an in-progress emergency.


**Routine inquiries consume expert staff time.** A significant share of inbound volume – status checks on existing tickets, questions about locate timeframes, after-hours callback requests – does not require a trained agent. When those calls consume the same queue as urgent new dig requests, resolution times suffer across the board.


**Legacy systems provide no operational visibility.** When calls are handled across disparate systems with no unified reporting, center directors cannot identify peak load patterns, track first-contact resolution rates, or coach staff on the interactions that need improvement.

## What modern 811 constituent engagement looks like in practice


Consider how a spring Monday morning could unfold at a state 811 center with RingCentral in place:


At 6:00 a.m., before the center’s live agents have logged in, inbound calls begin arriving from excavators who plan to break ground at 7:00 a.m. and want to confirm their locate requests are active. AIR answers each call immediately, pulls up the relevant information, confirms ticket status, and provides estimated locate completion times – no hold music, no voicemail, no missed opportunities. Callers who have more complex questions are offered a callback when agents come online at 7:00 a.m., with their inquiry already summarized and ready for the agent.


By 8:30 a.m., a contractor calls in reporting an emergency: they have encountered an unmarked line during excavation near a gas distribution area. AIR immediately recognizes the urgency from the caller’s natural language – “I think I may have hit something” – and routes the call to a live agent with full context. The agent has the caller’s location, the nature of the concern, and the ticket history within seconds of picking up. Response is immediate, coordinated, and documented.


Throughout the day, ACE captures summaries of every interaction. A center director reviewing the morning’s activity can see which ticket status question was asked most frequently – a signal that the utility’s estimated locate time is unclear to excavators – and update AIR’s knowledge base to provide a more precise answer tomorrow. Over time, this feedback loop systematically reduces repeat calls and improves the accuracy of the information excavators receive before they dig.


At the end of the day, the full interaction log – every call, every escalation, every SMS follow-up – is available for audit, training review, or regulatory reporting. Nothing is missing from the record.
