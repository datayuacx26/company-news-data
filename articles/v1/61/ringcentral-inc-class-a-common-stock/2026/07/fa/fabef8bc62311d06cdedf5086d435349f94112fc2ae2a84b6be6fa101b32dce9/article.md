# Competing off the field: How college athletic departments can win with AI-powered communications

College athletic departments now run NIL compliance, donor relations, and international recruiting through communication systems that were never built for it. AI-powered communications can close that gap, answering every call, tracking every conversation, and giving compliance teams a searchable record automatically.


**Here’s what that looks like in practice:**


When a prospective recruit’s family calls an athletic department, they are not just asking about scholarships and practice schedules. They are forming an impression of the institution. If that call goes to voicemail, takes too long to route to the right person, or gets answered inconsistently depending on who picks up – that impression sticks.


College athletic departments have become some of the most complex operations on any campus. Institutions must manage NIL (Name, Image, Likeness) agreements and ensure compliance oversight that influences nearly every interaction a department has with athletes, agents, donors, and collectives. Simultaneously, many departments face the challenge of accomplishing more with stagnant or reduced budgets while competing with peer institutions that are heavily investing in enhancing the student-athlete experience.


The cost of a missed call, a miscommunicated NIL disclosure, or a slow response to a recruit has never been higher. And for most athletic departments, the communication systems in place were not built for the moment they are now in.


## A department under pressure from every direction


The communication challenges facing athletic departments today are not isolated – they compound. Compliance offices are tracking verbal commitments, text confirmations, and phone conversations that now function as potential audit items under the new oversight regime. Ticketing and donor relations teams are fielding questions from fans and major donors who expect the same responsiveness they receive from consumer brands. Recruiting coordinators are trying to make first impressions on international student-athletes and their families, often in languages that their current staff cannot support at scale.


Meanwhile, the operational baseline has eroded. Every unanswered call or slow reply is potential revenue walking away. A recruit who feels unheard, a donor who doesn’t get a callback, a fan who can’t resolve a ticketing question—the cost adds up fast. For a ticketing office missing a season-ticket renewal call, that is direct revenue lost. For a recruiting office missing a family’s inquiry during an official visit window, it may be a scholarship offer that never gets made.


Staff burnout compounds the problem. The transfer portal and compressed recruiting cycles have made athlete movement constant, while support and operations staff report rising workloads from repetitive inquiries, after-hours demand, and manual administrative tasks. Departments face a paradox: the moment they need their best people focused on high-impact relationships, those people are buried in routine call handling.

## What AI-powered communications looks like inside an athletic department


Here is how these capabilities come together during a critical window in the recruiting calendar:


A highly recruited prospect and their family are on campus for an official visit. While the coaching staff is running the on-field portion of the day, the family has questions – about the academic support program, about financial aid timelines, about how the NIL program works. They call the main athletic department number. AIR answers immediately, understands their questions in natural language, provides accurate answers drawn from the department’s own documentation, and routes the family to a senior recruiting coordinator with a full summary of what was asked and answered. The family never has to repeat themselves. The coordinator walks in prepared.


That evening, a major donor calls the development office after hours to ask about their suite renewal and whether the naming rights conversation from last month’s lunch is moving forward. AIR handles the suite renewal inquiry completely and leaves a detailed message for the development officer, including a summary of the naming rights question – to review first thing in the morning. Nothing falls through the cracks.

ACE has automatically captured and organized summaries of every relevant conversation—calls with the athlete’s agent, texts with the collective, internal review discussions—into a searchable, timestamped record. What would have taken a compliance officer hours of manual review is ready in minutes.


Across the department, staff who once spent hours on repetitive call handling are redirected to the work that requires human judgment: building relationships with recruits, stewarding major donors, and supporting student-athletes through the challenges of balancing competition, academics, and personal-brand obligations.
