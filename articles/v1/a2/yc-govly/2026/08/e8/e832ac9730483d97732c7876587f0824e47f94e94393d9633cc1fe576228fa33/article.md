# Cybersecurity and Rural Health Tech Investments Drive This Week's SLED News

## This Week’s Featured Headlines


### **1.**[Pennsylvania Invests $10M in Cybersecurity and $3.7M in Digital Permitting Modernization](https://www.pa.gov/agencies/oa/newsroom/governor-shapiro-secures-new-funding-to-continue-modernizing--se)


**Source:** Pennsylvania Office of Administration Newsroom | August 12, 2026
Governor Shapiro's 2026-27 budget adds $10 million in new cybersecurity funding and $3.7 million for digital permitting modernization through the Commonwealth Office of Digital Experience, which is part of a cybersecurity investment that has grown by $30 million over the past three years.


‍


**What happened**


- The $10 million cybersecurity investment builds on a multi-year strategy to strengthen monitoring across state networks and applications and improve real-time threat detection, with the cumulative three-year total signaling a sustained and growing commitment to cyber resilience that is actively producing procurement activity rather than still in planning stages.
- The $3.7 million for CODE PA will fund continued modernization of permitting processes in collaboration with the Department of Environmental Protection, building on existing digital tools including the PAyback money-back guarantee system and a permit tracking tool while expanding the state's track record of saving $37 million in IT spending through in-house modernization work.


**What it means for you**


- Cybersecurity vendors offering network monitoring, threat detection, and endpoint protection should treat Pennsylvania as an active and repeat buyer, with $10 million flowing into a mature multi-year program that is procuring solutions rather than starting from scratch.
- Digital permitting platform vendors, environmental compliance technology firms, and government UX specialists should monitor procurement activity from CODE PA and DEP as the permitting modernization effort expands into new workflows and agency partnerships.


### **2.**[Massachusetts Provides $2M in Grants to Harden Water Utility Cybersecurity](https://www.govtech.com/security/massachusetts-grants-look-to-boost-cybersecurity-for-water)


**Source:** Government Technology | August 12, 2026
The Massachusetts Clean Water Trust is providing at least $2 million in grant funding to help local water suppliers upgrade cybersecurity defenses, targeting small and disadvantaged communities whose aging infrastructure and limited budgets have left them most exposed to attack.


‍


**What happened**


- Grants of up to $50,000 are available to eligible public water suppliers for cybersecurity risk assessments and operational technology upgrades, with the program created in 2024 and already having distributed $1.3 million to 38 water systems, demonstrating active and ongoing deployment rather than a new initiative still finding its footing.
- State officials identified outdated software, weak network security, vulnerable access controls, and a lack of employee cybersecurity training as the primary threats to water infrastructure, pointing directly to the categories of solutions that funded water systems will be purchasing with grant money.


**What it means for you**


- OT security vendors, cybersecurity assessment firms, and water utility technology providers should prioritize outreach to Massachusetts water suppliers, particularly smaller and rural systems that are newly eligible for funding and actively seeking implementation partners to help them spend grants efficiently before program deadlines.
- The explicit identification of weak access controls, unpatched software, and untrained staff as the primary vulnerabilities means vendors with solutions in identity management, patch management, and cybersecurity workforce training have particularly strong alignment with what funded systems will be buying.


### **3.**[Illinois Awards $50M to Rural Hospital Network for Technology Modernization and Workforce Development](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/icahn-awarded-more-than-50-million-by-the-state-of-illinois-to-advance-rural-healthcare-transformation-across-illinois-302849929.html)


**Source:** PR Newswire | August 12, 2026
The Illinois Critical Access Hospital Network received more than $50 million from the Illinois Department of Healthcare and Family Services to administer rural health transformation grants to 60 small and rural hospitals serving approximately 1.4 million residents across the state.


‍


**What happened**


- The three grant agreements, signed July 31 and effective August 1, will fund technology modernization and infrastructure improvements at rural hospitals, chronic disease prevention programs, and workforce sustainability efforts including recruitment, training, and retention of healthcare professionals in underserved areas.
- ICAHN was selected as the largest sub-awardee of Illinois' $193 million first-year Rural Health Transformation Program allocation, giving it significant authority to direct funds to the 60 hospitals in its network, serving communities where 89 of 102 counties are designated Health Professional Shortage Areas with more than 3.5 million residents lacking adequate provider access.


**What it means for you**


- Health IT vendors, electronic health records firms, and clinical technology providers should move quickly to engage ICAHN and its 60 member hospitals now that grant agreements are executed and funds are active, as technology modernization is one of three named funding priorities and recipients are under pressure to deploy funds before federal deadlines.
- The workforce sustainability initiative creates additional opportunities for healthcare staffing platforms, credentialing software, and rural recruitment technology firms, as Illinois hospitals are actively seeking tools to build long-term provider pipelines in communities where shortages are already severe.


### **4.**[Florida Awards $188M in Rural Health Grants to Organizations Serving 1.2 Million Residents](https://floridaphoenix.com/2026/08/11/188-million-headed-to-organizations-to-improve-rural-health-in-florida/)


**Source:** Florida Phoenix | August 11, 2026
The DeSantis administration announced $188 million in competitive grants to organizations across the state to improve healthcare access for 1.2 million rural Floridians, with technology-enabled care receiving the largest share of funding through a single awardee.


‍


**What happened**


- Community Health IT received approximately $44 million across four regional awards, the largest single recipient in this round, to support preventive care and care-at-home services across Northeast, Southwest, Southeast, and Northwest Florida, with the concentration of funding in one technology-forward organization signaling the state's deliberate prioritization of tech-enabled care delivery models.
- Florida's 31 rural counties were divided into four geographic regions for grant administration, with the Northwest Florida region receiving $81 million as the largest allocation, and grant categories spanning preventive and home-based care, value-based care, rural and satellite clinics, workforce initiatives, and health and lifestyle programs.


**What it means for you**


- Telehealth vendors, remote patient monitoring firms, and care coordination platform providers should monitor procurement activity from Community Health IT and other large awardees as they stand up regional care-at-home programs across Florida's rural communities with funds they must deploy by September 2027.
- Florida's Year Two allocation will equal whatever the state actually spends by September 2027, creating strong financial incentive for all grantees to move quickly on implementation, meaning vendors who can demonstrate deployable solutions and fast onboarding timelines will have a meaningful advantage in winning contracts from organizations under spending pressure.


### **5. Nebraska Awards $136M in Rural Health Grants, Prioritizing Remote Patient Monitoring Technology**


**Source:** Nebraska Public Media | August 11, 2026
Nebraska has committed approximately $136 million of its $218.5 million first-year Rural Health Transformation Program allocation through nearly 200 grant agreements, with remote patient monitoring technology for 22 rural hospitals representing one of the largest and most explicitly tech-focused awards in the state's portfolio.


‍


**What happened**


- The Nebraska High Value Network, a partnership of 22 rural hospitals, received roughly $18 million to deploy remote patient monitoring technology including wearable BioButton devices that track respiration rates, blood pressure, and other patient health measurements from both hospital and home settings, combined with data system improvements that allow participating hospitals to aggregate monitoring data and evaluate its effectiveness in reducing emergency room visits and improving chronic disease management.
- Nebraska must award its remaining first-year funds to grantees by October 30, 2026 or risk losing the unspent balance to redistribution among other states, with DHHS officials confirming that currently available funding opportunities are forecast to cover Nebraska's full $218.5 million allocation, and a few grant opportunities remain open for eligible organizations to apply.


**What it means for you**


- Remote patient monitoring vendors, wearable health device providers, and health data integration firms should treat Nebraska's High Value Network investment as a concrete near-term procurement opportunity, as 22 rural hospitals are now funded and actively building the infrastructure to deploy and evaluate these tools at scale across the state.
- With the October 30 obligation deadline approaching, vendors offering deployable solutions in Nebraska's funded categories including remote monitoring devices, health data integration platforms, VR-based EMS training systems, oral health platforms, and behavioral health tools should prioritize outreach now as remaining grant recipients finalize their implementation plans.
