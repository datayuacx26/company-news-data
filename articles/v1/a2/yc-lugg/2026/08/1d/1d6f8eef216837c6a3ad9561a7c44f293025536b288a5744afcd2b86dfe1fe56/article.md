# How much a local move costs in 22 U.S. cities

Picture a move and you probably picture the big one: a truck packed to the ceiling, a long drive, a new city. It makes for good movies, but it isn't the move most people make. About three in four Americans who move each year stay within the same state, and only about one in five crosses state lines,[according to the U.S. Census Bureau](https://www.census.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2024/geographic-mobility-cps.html?ref=lugg.ghost.io) . The real American move is small and close to home — a couch, a few rooms of furniture, carried a few miles across town, often on short notice.


Summer is the busiest stretch of the year for moving — and August is its single busiest month. To see what that everyday move actually looks like, Lugg analyzed hundreds of thousands of completed local moves across 22 U.S. metro areas over the last 12 months — what they cost, how far they went, when people booked them, and what was in the truck.


## **Key findings**


- **August is the single busiest moving month of the year** — more local moves happen then than in any other month, as summer leases turn over and students head back to school.
- **The couch is America's most-moved item** , named in about one in five moves — ahead of tables, beds, mattresses, and dressers. The trusty **pickup truck is the most-booked vehicle in every metro** studied.
- **The typical local move costs a median of about $145** — a fraction of the four-figure sum a whole-home or long-distance relocation runs.
- **New York City is the costliest metro for a local move at about $155; Salt Lake City is the most affordable at about $95** — nearly half as much.
- **Most moves are small and short** — 96% fit in a van or smaller, and the median trip is about six miles. They're also getting shorter: the median move fell from about 8.5 miles in 2022 to 6.4 in 2026.
- **Moving is an afternoon, spur-of-the-moment habit —** bookings cluster around 2 p.m., and a large share happen the same day they're requested.
- **Whether a move is a shopping trip or a household shuffle depends on the city —** moves in San Antonio, Houston, and Phoenix are the most likely to be hauling home a new purchase, while New York, D.C., and the Bay Area skew toward moving furniture people already own.


## **The move most people make is smaller than they expect**


Most sticker shock comes from picturing the wrong move. National cost guides describe a four-bedroom house with professional packing and weeks of lead time; the move you're far more likely to make is a studio's worth of furniture, a single big-item pickup, or a cross-town apartment hop. The gap is enormous: the same booking data shows a single-item move averages about $93, a studio around $404, and a full home about $1,346 — so where your move falls on that spectrum matters far more than the city you live in.


Most local moves land at the small end, which is why the citywide median sits near $145. It helps to know the alternative: traditional moving companies generally[bill $105 to $165 an hour for two movers](https://lugg.com/blog/moving-company-cost?ref=lugg.ghost.io) , with minimums that keep even a quick job from being cheap. Priced by the minute actually worked rather than a padded hourly block, the short local move has become one of the most affordable — and most bookable — services in the moving world.


## **Why moves are getting shorter**


The clearest change over the past four years is distance: the median local move has shrunk from about 8.5 miles in 2022 to 6.4 in 2026, a trend that holds even when the same kind of job is compared year to year. The housing market explains much of it.


The share of moves that cross state lines has[slipped to about one in five](https://www.redfin.com/blog/how-moving-trends-are-changing/?ref=lugg.ghost.io) , while a nearly 30% jump in rents over five years and tight supply keep people circling within their own metro rather than leaving it. When a move is a few miles instead of a few states, it stops being a months-long project and becomes a same-day errand.


## **A closer look, city by city**


### **1. New York City — $155**


The country's shortest typical move, yet its most costly — in a dense city you pay for the stairs and the street, not the distance. New Yorkers also move into storage more than anyone, no surprise in a city famous for tiny apartments and closets to match, and they're among the country's most reliable tippers.


- **Typical move (all sizes):** $154
- **Median distance:** 4.2 miles
- **Busiest day:** Friday
- **Peak month:** May
- **Most-moved item:** Couch
- **Tip rate:** 95% of moves

## **Why the price changes from city to city**


The differences come down to a few familiar factors. Local labor rates are the biggest, and they follow regional wages, so higher-wage metros sit higher on the list. Building access matters too — walk-ups, elevators, permit-only loading zones, and traffic all add working minutes, and minutes are what a local move is priced on. Distance matters least: because most local moves are short, mileage rarely decides the bill. New York makes the point — the country's shortest typical move, yet its most costly, because the money goes toward the time it takes to carry a couch down five flights and load a truck on a crowded street.


## **Where moving is quickest and shortest**


Distance swings almost as much as price, and it tracks the shape of each city. The shortest moves happen where everything is stacked close together: a median of just 4.2 miles in New York, 4.6 in Portland, and just over five miles in Chicago, D.C., and Boston. The longest turn up in the spread-out Sun Belt, where "across town" covers real ground — 11.4 miles in San Antonio, 9.6 in Houston, 9.4 in Dallas.


Same-day moves are common everywhere but most common out West — Los Angeles and the Bay Area lead the country, while planners in Boston and D.C. are the most likely to book ahead. Short, quick, and spur-of-the-moment is simply how a lot of the country moves now.


## **What it means for your next move**


For most people, moving is a smaller, more predictable expense than the internet would have you believe — a mid-three-figure afternoon, not a four-figure ordeal, and increasingly one that happens just a few miles away and on short notice. The practical takeaway is simply knowing your city's going rate: it turns a vague dread into a number you can plan around, and it makes an out-of-line quote easy to spot.
