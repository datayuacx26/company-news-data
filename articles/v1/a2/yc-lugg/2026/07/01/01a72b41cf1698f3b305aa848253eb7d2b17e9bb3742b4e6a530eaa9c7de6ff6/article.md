# The best places to buy furniture in Boston: outlets, flea markets, and curb finds

In Boston, the best furniture turns up on the curbs, at the Sunday flea markets, and in a cluster of outlets about 35 minutes south of the city. Boston moves on furniture turnover: roughly 70% of the city's apartment leases turn over on the very same day, September 1 ([City of Boston](https://www.boston.gov/departments/new-urban-mechanics/coordinated-furniture-pick-pilot?ref=lugg.ghost.io) ), which sends a constant churn of couches, dressers, and dining tables through move-outs, vintage markets, and Facebook Marketplace. Whether you just signed a lease in Allston or you're furnishing a place in the Seaport, here's where locals actually find furniture — new, vintage, and free.

## Allston Christmas: Boston's furniture free-for-all


The single best day to find free furniture in Boston is September 1. It's known locally as "Allston Christmas" — the day roughly 70% of the city's leases turn over at once and the sidewalks fill with couches, dressers, desks, bookshelves, and lamps left behind by everyone moving out. It's been happening for over a century, and it's the closest thing Boston has to a citywide furniture giveaway.


Items start hitting the curb in mid-August and peak around September 1, when more than 150,000 college students are moving at the same time. The neighborhoods to walk: Allston, Brighton, Fenway, Mission Hill, and parts of South Boston and the South End — anywhere with a dense mix of students and renters. The good pieces go fast, so the best finds happen early in the morning.


A few ground rules. Once furniture is at the curb for trash, it's fair game — but check every piece for bed bugs before it goes anywhere near your car ([EPA](https://www.epa.gov/bedbugs/how-find-bed-bugs?ref=lugg.ghost.io) ), and don't grab anything that clearly still belongs to someone mid-move. The city has also started a[coordinated furniture pickup and donation program](https://www.boston.gov/departments/new-urban-mechanics/coordinated-furniture-pick-pilot?ref=lugg.ghost.io) to keep usable pieces out of landfills, so if you're the one moving out, donating is easier than it used to be — and furniture banks like Household Goods in Acton will take usable pieces too. For the full story behind the tradition, see our guide to[the September 1 moving-day chaos](https://lugg.com/blog/what-is-allston-christmas?ref=lugg.ghost.io) .


## Vintage markets and the flea circuit


Boston's vintage scene is one of the strongest in the Northeast, and most of it runs on a weekly or seasonal schedule you can plan around.


**SoWa Vintage Market (South End).** The anchor of the local scene. Held downstairs at 450 Harrison Ave every Sunday, year-round, from 11 a.m. to 4 p.m., SoWa brings together dozens of curated vendors selling furniture, antiques, art, and vintage goods. Admission is free, and it's part of the larger SoWa Arts + Design District, so you can make a full afternoon of it.


**The Somerville Flea (Davis Square).** An open-air vintage and artisan market that runs seasonally, roughly August through October, in the heart of Davis Square. Great for mid-century furniture, vinyl, and handmade goods, with a college-town energy that keeps prices reasonable.


**Cambridge Antique Market.** Five floors and more than 150 dealers packed into a brick building at 201 Msgr. O'Brien Hwy, open year-round. The basement is the spot for mid-century modern furniture, and because it's indoors, it's a reliable rainy-day option when the outdoor markets are closed.


**Brimfield Antique Show.** The big one. Brimfield is the largest outdoor antique show in the country, and it runs three times a year —[May 12–17, July 14–19, and September 8–13 in 2026](https://www.brimfieldantiqueweek.com/?ref=lugg.ghost.io) — about 90 minutes west of Boston. Thousands of vendors line Route 20 with antique furniture, and plenty of Boston shoppers make the drive. Bring cash, comfortable shoes, and a way to get large pieces home.


## The outlet run: Wrentham and Patriot Place


If you want brand-name furniture at a discount, the outlet corridor south of the city is worth the trip. Most of it clusters around Wrentham and Foxborough, about 35 minutes down I-495.


**Wrentham Village Premium Outlets.** New England's largest outlet center, with a Pottery Barn Outlet and West Elm Outlet both on site at 1 Premium Outlets Blvd. Expect full sofas, dining sets, bed frames, and bedding — overstock and floor samples at a fraction of showroom prices.


**RH Outlet (Patriot Place, Foxborough).** Restoration Hardware pieces at deep discounts, right next to the Wrentham cluster at 370 Patriot Place. Some of the heaviest, highest-end furniture in the region ends up here.


**IKEA (Stoughton).** The only IKEA in the Boston area, with an in-store as-is section for floor models and discontinued items at clearance prices. A rite of passage for anyone furnishing a first apartment.


**Bob's Discount Furniture & Circle Furniture.** For everyday budgets, Bob's has high-volume outlet locations in Dedham and Dorchester, and Circle Furniture is a New England chain (nearly 70 years in business) with a made-to-last reputation and showrooms in the Seaport and Cambridge. Boston Interiors also runs a clearance floor in Stoughton with periodic warehouse sales.


Timing matters at the outlets: the deepest markdowns tend to land during seasonal clearance windows, so it's worth knowing[the best times of year to buy furniture](https://lugg.com/blog/best-time-to-buy-furniture?ref=lugg.ghost.io) before you go.


## Secondhand shops and resale worth the trip


Beyond the flea markets, a handful of resale shops keep a steady rotation of gently used furniture year-round.


**Boomerangs (Jamaica Plain).** A local thrift chain whose JP location is the best for furniture. Stock changes constantly and conditions range from nearly new to well-loved, so it rewards patience — and proceeds support community programs.


**Habitat for Humanity ReStore (West Roxbury).** Secondhand furniture, mirrors, tables, and dressers at 50–70% below retail, with proceeds funding local home builds. One of the best values in the city if you're willing to hunt.


**CORT Furniture Outlet.** Previously rented furniture, graded and sold at a discount, with locations serving South and East Boston. Because much of it was staged in model homes, the pieces often look far newer than the price suggests.


## Where to buy new furniture in Boston


If you'd rather buy new, Boston's showrooms cluster in a few walkable pockets. The Seaport has become the design hub — Blu Dot, Circle Furniture, and a Herman Miller store are all near the waterfront. On and around Newbury Street in Back Bay, Room & Board anchors a five-floor modern showroom. And in Cambridge, Design Within Reach occupies three floors in Harvard Square, while the South End's Lekker Home brings Scandinavian design to a smaller, curated space. These are the spots for made-to-last pieces you'll move from apartment to apartment for years.


## Buying furniture online in Boston


Boston is one of the most active Facebook Marketplace cities in the Northeast, and it's where a huge share of secondhand furniture actually changes hands. Among Boston shoppers who mention where a piece came from, Facebook Marketplace comes up far more often than Craigslist or OfferUp, based on Lugg data. Apps like AptDeco and Chairish are also good for Boston — both handle vintage and gently used furniture, with delivery options built in.


The catch is always the same: you found the perfect couch, but it's on the third floor of a walk-up in Southie and your car is a Honda Civic. The most common item Bostonians book movers to haul is, in fact, a couch (Lugg data).
