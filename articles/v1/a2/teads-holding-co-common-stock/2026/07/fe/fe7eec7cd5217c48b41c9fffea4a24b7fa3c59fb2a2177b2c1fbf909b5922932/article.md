# How Omnichannel Advertising Delivers Better Full-Funnel Results

## The Case for Omnichannel Advertising


Consumers don’t move through a purchase journey one channel at a time.


They discover brands on streaming TV, compare products on mobile, read reviews on premium publisher sites, receive recommendations on social media, and often convert days later from another device or in a physical store.


Marketing, however, often remains fragmented.[Nielsen’s 2025 Annual Marketing Report](https://www.nielsen.com/insights/2025/annual-marketing-report-2025-chaos-to-clarity/) found that only 32% of marketers globally say they measure their media spending holistically across both digital and traditional channels today.


Many campaigns are still planned and measured channel by channel, leading to fragmentation, duplicated reach, and inconsistent messaging. The result is wasted impressions, inefficient frequency, and an incomplete view of campaign performance. More importantly, it no longer reflects how consumers actually discover, evaluate, and purchase products today.


This disconnect is being accelerated by broader changes in the media landscape. Streaming has become mainstream, connected TV continues to grow, privacy regulations have reshaped audience targeting, and advertisers face increasing pressure to demonstrate measurable business outcomes, not just media delivery.


As audiences continue to fragment across connected TV, mobile, desktop, and other moments, creating connected experiences has become essential for maximizing media investment.


That’s where omnichannel advertising comes in. Unlike multichannel marketing, which simply distributes campaigns across multiple platforms, an omnichannel approach coordinates messaging, sequencing, frequency, and measurement around the consumer rather than the channel. The goal is to create a connected experience that reflects how people actually engage with brands while maximizing the effectiveness of every media investment.


## Why Omnichannel Advertising Performs Better


Adding more channels doesn’t automatically improve campaign performance. What drives better results is ensuring those channels work together as a coordinated experience.


Each environment plays a distinct role within the consumer journey. Connected TV captures attention in high-impact, lean-back viewing moments, premium publisher environments encourage deeper engagement while audiences actively consume content, and mobile and desktop create opportunities for continued interaction and conversion.


Together, these touchpoints reinforce a consistent brand story instead of delivering isolated impressions across disconnected channels.


Industry researches, including[WARC’s reports](https://www.warc.com/en/article/cross-media-campaigns-are-more-effective-12526724e5824db7b646bb94e4ba3581) , has consistently shown that coordinated cross-screen campaigns outperform siloed media strategies by improving incremental reach, strengthening message recall, and creating more meaningful customer experiences.


Teads’ own research demonstrates this in practice. Compared with audiences exposed through only one medium, consumers reached across multiple screens generated stronger improvements in key brand metrics, including opinion, perception, and consideration.


Additional brand lift research showed similar results when audiences experienced coordinated campaigns across multiple media instead of a single environment.


- Brand awareness increased from **+8% to +13%**
- Ad recall improved from **+37% to +50%**
- Purchase intent rose from **+13% to +32%**


Delivering an omnichannel strategy, however, requires more than simply buying inventory across multiple channels. It depends on consistent creative, coordinated audience management, frequency optimization, and cross-screen measurement that connects every touchpoint to business outcomes. The following campaigns illustrate how brands have applied these principles across different objectives and industries.

## The Future of Full-Funnel Advertising Is Omnichannel


Marketing success is becoming less about optimizing individual channels and more about orchestrating connected consumer experiences.


The strongest campaigns aren’t built around connected TV alone, premium publishers alone, or mobile alone. They combine each environment to maximize attention, reinforce messaging, improve measurement, and guide consumers naturally from awareness through consideration to conversion.


As these success stories demonstrate,[omnichannel advertising enables brands to move beyond isolated media performance](https://www.teads.com/omnichannel/) and measure what matters most: business outcomes.


Whether the objective is increasing brand awareness, improving recall, generating qualified website visits, or driving incremental conversions, coordinated campaigns consistently outperform disconnected ones.


For advertisers planning their next campaign, the question is no longer *which channel works best.* It’s *how effectively those channels work together* .
