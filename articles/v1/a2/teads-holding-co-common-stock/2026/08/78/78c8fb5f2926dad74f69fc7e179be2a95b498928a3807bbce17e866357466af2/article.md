# What Makes a High-Quality Connected TV Advertising Experience

The connected TV industry has spent years debating what qualifies as premium inventory.


Initially, the answer was straightforward: premium meant advertising inside well-known streaming platforms. As the ecosystem expanded, advertisers added new evaluation criteria – completion rates, viewability, audience targeting, and more sophisticated measurement.


Those signals remain valuable. But they no longer explain why campaigns with similar reach and completion rates often produce different business results.


As connected TV matures, the conversation is shifting from where ads appear to how campaigns are designed, optimized, and measured. The highest-performing campaigns consistently combine four characteristics:


**Premium Supply**


Trusted, high-attention media environments that maximize visibility and brand impact.


**Creative Intelligence**


Creative built specifically for connected TV to maximize attention and effectiveness.


**Unique Data Solutions**


Rich audience signals that go beyond demographics to reach consumers in relevant moments.


**AI Optimization**


Optimization focused on measurable business outcomes across the full funnel.


Together, they provide a more complete framework for evaluating CTV quality than inventory alone.


## **Premium Supply Creates the Conditions for Attention**


Premium supply remains the foundation of connected TV advertising.


Where an ad appears continues to influence how viewers perceive both the message and the brand itself. Premium streaming environments and high-attention placements consistently outperform lower-quality inventory across recall, consideration, and purchase intent.


The[MMA 2025 State of Video & CTV study](https://www.teads.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/Teads-MMA-2025-State-of-CTV-Video-eBook-Study.pdf) found that:


55%


of marketers believe attention will become the standard for CTV measurement and media buying.


40%


already treat attention as a core KPI for evaluating campaign performance.


47%


are testing alternative measurement currencies that incorporate attention.


Rather than treating premium as a label attached to a streaming app, advertisers increasingly evaluate the viewing context itself – how engaged audiences are, what they’re watching, and the moment in which the ad appears.


That’s a more useful definition of quality because it reflects how consumers actually experience advertising.


## **Creative Quality Has Become a Competitive Advantage**


Attention creates opportunity. Creative determines how much value brands capture from it.


This has become particularly important as connected TV expands beyond traditional InStream video into environments such as HomeScreen and interactive formats.


Each requires creative designed specifically for that viewing experience.


An study developed in collaboration with[Heineken and Strat7](https://view-su2.highspot.com/viewer/99bc3a96f46b7a8a79aec77d4c657453) illustrates this well.


Rather than comparing CTV formats, the study examined how creative decisions within the HomeScreen environment influenced brand outcomes across multiple European markets. Four findings emerged:


**2.3× Higher Ad Awareness**


Heineken’s CTV HomeScreen activity delivered a 2.3× uplift in ad awareness versus brand norms.


**Longer Creative Wins**


10–15 second videos consistently outperformed 6-second executions across engagement and upper-funnel brand metrics.


**Keep Split-Screen Simple**


Simple split-screen creative with one clear message and minimal visual clutter significantly strengthened message association.


**Full-Screen Performs Best**


Full-screen video delivered the strongest overall upper-funnel performance across recall, message delivery, and brand consideration.


Creative adaptation is becoming a meaningful driver of campaign effectiveness, which explains why advertisers increasingly invest in creative optimization alongside media buying.

## **Audience Intelligence Creates More Relevant Experiences**


Audience targeting has become standard practice in connected TV. Today, most advertisers activate first-party audiences, behavioral segments, and contextual signals.


The next evolution is making those audiences work consistently across screens. Rather than creating separate audience strategies for connected TV, mobile, and desktop, advertisers increasingly want one audience framework that follows consumers throughout the journey.


Capabilities such as household graphs, life-stage targeting, intent signals, and program-level contextual targeting help extend campaigns beyond demographic reach into more relevant consumer moments.


Audience intelligence doesn’t replace creative or premium media. It amplifies both.
