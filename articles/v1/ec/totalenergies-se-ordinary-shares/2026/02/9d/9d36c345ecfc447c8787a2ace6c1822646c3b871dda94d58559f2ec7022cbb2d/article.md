# Summary of TotalEnergies’ position on the French Duty of Vigilance Law

**1. TotalEnergies will show that this case is misconceived and has no legitimacy** , **for the following reasons** **:**


- The claimants are in reality attempting to impose upon a single oil and gas producing company - representing less than 2% of the oil and gas sector and not at all involved in coal -the alleged responsibility for the or the functioning of the entire European and global energy system. This system includes thermal power plants supplying electricity to households, industries and public services; cars, trucks, ships and planes fueled with petroleum products and industrial and agricultural sectors relying on oil and gas to manufacture goods and foodstuffs.
- However, this energy system has been designed, developed and governed for more than a century by States and by a constellation of public and private actors. It has delivered undeniable benefits that have driven social, economic and human progress. Access to energy remains, to this day, a prerequisite for development.
- No-one can deny that the system's burning of fossil fuels has effectively generated and continues to generate greenhouse gas emissions that the energy transition is attempting to reduce. These efforts are part of an international framework and national frameworks defined by governments and implemented by companies like TotalEnergies.
- The energy transition requires coordinated management of global supply and demand for energy across all sectors:


- **Power generation, which generates 14 billion tons of CO2 a year, must shift primarily by reducing coal use** which alone accounts for three quarters of the sector's CO2 It is important to note that TotalEnergies does not produce coal and is developing a power activity based on renewables and flexible assets (gas-fired power stations and batteries).
- **Road, maritime and air transport, which generate 8 billion tons of CO2 a year, need to decarbonize** . That will require technical, technological and financial changes to the production of cars (hybrid cars, electric cars, cars running on low-carbon liquid fuels), ships (dual fuel vessels that can run on LNG, hybridization) and planes. The price of these technological changes will also have to be made acceptable to customers. TotalEnergies supports this transition by deploying EV charging infrastructure, producing biofuels and marine LNG and building an integrated electricity business. But no operator – TotalEnergies included – can dictate the pace at which manufacturers, shipyards or consumers adopt low‑carbon technologies.
- **Industrial activities, which generate 6 billion tons of CO2 a year, require electrification and the use of lower-carbon fuels** (replacing coal with gas, low-carbon hydrogen) meaning that industrial groups will need to invest in furnaces, boilers and different industrial processes. Yet these transformations depend on investment decisions by industrial operators themselves, not by TotalEnergies.


- It makes no sense at all to prevent TotalEnergies producing oil and gas that the global energy system still uses today: demand for these energies is still rising, while the production of oil and gas declines naturally by 6 to 7% a year absent new investment, according to the IEA's report of September 2025.
- The courtroom is not the right place to advance the energy transition. This transition requires reconciling complex, interdependent challenges: energy security, affordability, consumer acceptance, technological maturity, etc. What is appropriate is targeted, sector-specific regulation, such as CO2 pricing, supported by TotalEnergies, vehicle emissions standards and sustainable aviation fuel blends, public grants and incentives for electrification—all fields to which TotalEnergies is already actively contributing—and increased international cooperation allowing the developed world to fund the decarbonization of emerging countries using the Paris Agreement's carbon credit system.


**2.** **TotalEnergies will further demonstrate that it is already implementing an integrated multi-energy strategy:**


- This strategy rests on two pillars: oil and gas, particularly liquefied natural gas, and power, the energy at the heart of the transition.
- TotalEnergies has invested over €20 billion worldwide in low-carbon energies since 2020. These investments are made possible by revenues from its oil and gas activities, which the claimants paradoxically ask the Company to discontinue.
- From a starting point close to zero in 2020, TotalEnergies is now producing almost 50 TWh of electricity worldwide (2025), supported largely by 34 GW of gross installed renewables capacity, equivalent to roughly fifteen nuclear reactors.
- Greenhouse gas emissions from TotalEnergies' oil and gas facilities fell by 38% between 2015 and 2025, and methane emissions fell by 65% between 2020 and 2025.
- TotalEnergies has reduced the carbon intensity of its operated oil and gas production from 21 kg in 2015 to 17 kg CO2e per barrel produced in 2024, a drop of nearly 20%, compared to industry averages of around 60 kg CO2e for oil and 43 kg CO2e for gas.


**3. TotalEnergies will finally demonstrate that the case has no solid legal basis.**


- TotalEnergies' activities are lawful. The Company complies fully with applicable national and European regulations.
- The claimants' reliance on the French Duty of Vigilance Law is erroneous: the statute does not govern climate‑change. Likewise, the Paris Agreement imposes obligations exclusively on States, not on corporations. The action therefore misconstrues the legal framework and must be dismissed.
