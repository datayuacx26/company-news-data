# Q&A setting out the position of TotalEnergies on the duty of vigilance

*This Q&A was published prior to the court decision of 25/06/2026.*


A lawsuit has been brought against TotalEnergies in France by a number of associations seeking an order from the court requiring the Company to amend its vigilance plan so as to add climate-related risks and reduce its exploration and production activities for oil and gas.


Below, we set out the details of the case and the reasons why TotalEnergies considers that these claims should be dismissed. This is not to suggest that TotalEnergies considers that climate change and the energy transition are not real issues. Nor does it imply that courts have no role to play in, for example, examining appeals against administrative decisions authorizing the Company's energy projects. However, decisions that concern the transition strategy generally – which require consideration of climate issues, energy security and sovereignty, the need to maintain affordable energy cost and the economic and social acceptability of transition pathways – fall within the remit of governments and legislators, who are responsible for balancing these sometimes competing imperatives and oversee the coordinated evolution of energy's supply and demand.


**1- How TotalEnergies addresses climate change in its vigilance plan**


TotalEnergies has indeed integrated climate issues into the identification of risks in its vigilance plan:


Climate change is a global risk for the planet and results from various human actions such as energy consumption. As an energy producer, TotalEnergies seeks to reduce direct greenhouse gas emissions resulting from its operated Activities. In 2025, worldwide greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions from the facilities operated by TotalEnergies amounted to 33.1 Mt CO2 e, less than 0.1% of total worldwide emissions, which amounted to 57.7 Gt CO2 e for the year of 2024(4). In addition, TotalEnergies implements a strategy to tackle climate change challenges and reports on this in detail, notably in its Sustainability Report (refer to point 5.2.2.1 of chapter 5), in accordance with Articles L. 232-6-3 and L. 233-28-4 of the French Commercial Code.


([2025 Universal Registration Document](https://totalenergies.com/system/files/documents/totalenergies_universal-registration-document-2025_2026_en.pdf) , p.152. See also pp. 173-185 for details of TotalEnergies' ambition, action taken to reduce greenhouse gas emissions in Scopes 1 and 2 and the orderly energy transition strategy.)


TotalEnergies' vigilance plan describes in detail the action taken to reduce greenhouse gas emissions from its operations, precise targets for 2030 and its ambition for carbon neutrality , together with society. It presents TotalEnergies' orderly energy transition strategy, based on an oil and gas pillar under which the Company will continue to pursue the reduction in greenhouse gas emissions from its operations, and a pillar under which it produces and sells low-carbon electricity.


**2- What is the timeline of the case ruled on by the Paris Judicial Court on June 25, 2026?**


**In January 2020,** a coalition of six NGOs and 17 local authorities brought a case against TotalEnergies SE alleging failure to comply with its “climate” obligations under the French duty of vigilance law and citing the provisions of the French Civil Code relating to ecological damage.


Following a debate on jurisdiction, and after the entry into force in December 2021 of a new procedural statute conferring jurisdiction on the Paris Judicial Court for disputes relating to the Duty of Vigilance Law, that court held, on 6 July 2023, that the claims brought by all the claimants were inadmissible.


**On June 18, 2024** , the Paris Court of Appeal found the case admissible in principle on both grounds (duty of vigilance and damage to the environment) and then examined whether each of the plaintiffs had the standing and interest to bring the case. Four NGOs and the City of Paris (which is supporting the claims on a voluntary basis) were given leave to proceed; the other local authorities, including the City of New York, were not.


The case returned to the Paris Judicial Court to be judged on merits. **In February 2026** , the attorney general intervened as an additional party to give its opinion on the question of the scope of the French duty of vigilance. It considered that the law on the duty of vigilance did not extend to climate change, calling the attention of the court to the definition of the environmental content of the vigilance plan.


The Paris Judicial Court is expected to rule on **June 25, 2026** , following the hearings on February 19 and 20, 2026.


The ruling of the Paris Judicial Court may be appealed at the Paris Appeal Court.


**3- What are the plaintiffs asking** **for?**


The court is being asked to rule on two types of issue, depending on the legal grounds (the law on the duty of vigilance or the Civil Code on the prevention of ecological harm) :


**The first category** is based solely on the duty of vigilance law. The plaintiffs are asking for TotalEnergies to be required to supplement and clarify the wording of its vigilance plan, in particular to include, in its mapping of risks of serious harm, climate-related risks associated with global warming above 1.5°C, as well as the alleged contribution of TotalEnergies—through its activities and those of its clients—via direct and indirect emissions (Scopes 1, 2 and 3).


**The second category** is common to both grounds and consists of a series of injunctions: (1) reducing oil and gas production or emissions from Scopes 1, 2 and 3 by 2050, in line with three pathways (including the 2018 IPCC's P1 and the IEA's 2023 NZE), all aiming for 1.5°C, and (2) the discontinuation of the exploration and exploitation of new oil and gas projects that have not been subject to a final investment decision.


**4- What is the French law on the duty of vigilance?**


Under Article L. 225-102-1 of the French Commercial Code, certain large companies **meeting specific criteria are required to** establish, publish and implement **a vigilance plan.** This framework is designed to identify and prevent the risk of severe harm to human rights, health, safety and the environment resulting from the parent company's activities, its controlled affiliates, and some of its suppliers or subcontractors connected by an established business relationship.


**The duty of vigilance law does not, however, cover the activities of a company's clients.** This is one of the reasons why TotalEnergies argues that Scope 3 emissions (Scope 3 category 11), i.e. those resulting from the use by customers of the products sold by TotalEnergies do not fall within the scope of this law.


**Passed in the aftermath of the Rana Plaza disaster the Duty of Vigilance law is not a “climate” law.** It requires the identification and implementation of appropriate measures to address the risks listed above.


**5- What is TotalEnergies' position on its obligations under the law?**


**It is the Company's view that it is completely fulfilling its obligations under French law on the duty of vigilance.** This legal framework requires that we identify the risks related to our activities, those of our affiliates and certain of our suppliers and subcontractors, and that we take measures to deal with these risks. The law ensures reasonable vigilance on the conduct of our activities in the field.


As set out in paragraph 1 above, TotalEnergies' vigilance plan integrates climate issues into the identification of risks in its vigilance plan and details action taken to reduce its greenhouse gas emissions from its operations.


**6- Why** **does TotalEnergies consider that this action is not legitimate?**


Essentially, the purpose of the case brought by the NGOs is to bring down the Scope 3 emissions connected to the use by customers of the energy products sold by TotalEnergies. The NGOs argue that by reducing TotalEnergies' Scope 3 emissions, and by requiring the Company to reduce its oil and gas production, this would in turn lead to a reduction in its customers' emissions.


However, setting an absolute reduction target for Scope 3 for a company like TotalEnergies, without customers themselves having targets for reducing their Scope 1 related to the use of energy products, is not an effective approach in reducing global GHG emissions.


**By focusing on indirect greenhouse gases emissions related to the use of energy products that TotalEnergies sells to its customers (Scope 3), the action brought by the associations seeks to hold the Company responsible for emissions whose generation lies with its customers. Yet the use of these products is determined by customers' own decisions.** TotalEnergies is not involved in production and sale chain of the goods and equipment that consume energy or require energy for their making: TotalEnergies does not make airplanes, cars, cement or steel. The Company therefore cannot be held responsible for the reduction of emissions resulting from the use of its products by its customers. Obviously, TotalEnergies acts to contribute to the transformation of its customers' energy demand and thus to help its customers reduce their scope 1. Through its multi-energy strategy, TotalEnergies is adapting its offer to provide a wider range of energy products, including low-carbon solutions, for example by developing sustainable aviation fuels, and supports its customers in their decarbonization plans. However, only coordinated actions of all the players in the energy value chain can have a concrete impact on the global GHG emissions and drive changes in energy consumption patterns: through public policies aimed at orienting energy consumption, sectoral initiatives of energy-consuming companies, technical and technological developments.


Drastically reducing the global Scope 3 emissions in absolute value of a company like TotalEnergies, as the proposed resolution underpins, without an evolution of the overall structure of energy demand, would lead to directing that energy demand toward other suppliers, notably the national oil companies of producing countries. This strategy would have no effect on lowering global greenhouse gas emissions, and therefore no positive impact on climate. Its implementation of this strategy would be detrimental to the Company which would be compelled to divest its downstream oil and gas marketing activities to other operators, thereby reducing its financial capacity to finance the energy transition. The strategy would also be dangerous for our customers, insofar as it would not integrate the necessity to ensure a secure energy supply to meet the existing demand. Too abrupt a transition could destabilize the availability and the prices of oil and gas petroleum products.


**7- Does the law on the duty of vigilance apply to the climate?**


**The public prosecutor has expressed its position on this question and has indicated that climate change does not fall within the scope of this law.**


Neither the duty of vigilance law nor the Civil Code on the prevention of ecological damage were intended to govern the development of energy projects. In reality, TotalEnergies' oil and gas activities—just like its power activities—are regulated by specialized laws and regulations in the countries where it produces, processes and distributes this energy. Those regulations, including for example environmental permitting regimes or administrative authorisations to operate, allow oil and gas resources to be developed and produced, refined and sold under certain conditions. The courts of the countries where TotalEnergies operates are responsible for ensuring compliance with the regulations during the administrative and legal procedures applicable within their jurisdiction.


**Neither the Paris Agreement, which applies to nation states and not to private parties, nor the law of the European Union, nor the scenarios developed by the IPCC or the International Energy Agency impose on private companies any legally binding, quantified obligation to reduce their emissions, their production, their projects or their asset disposals in accordance with a predetermined trajectory over the next twenty-five years** . Those pathways are established by national or regional governments, (e.g. the European Union) or by the international community at conferences like the Conferences of the Parties.


**8- Why are Scope 3 emissions not covered by the vigilance plan?**


Climate change is a planetary risk resulting from various human activities including energy use.


For TotalEnergies, **Scope 3 emissions are indirect emissions that mostly correspond to emissions generated by its customers when they use the oil and gas products sold by the Company. As mentioned above, customers' activities do not fall within the scope of the duty of vigilance law.**


The Scope 3 emissions published by TotalEnergies under its sustainability reporting for the Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive correspond to Scope 1 emissions of its customers (airlines, shipping companies, road transport operators etc). They are neither controlled nor managed by the Company.


**These emissions result directly from customers' activities: their evolution depends exclusively on the investment and energy choices made by TotalEnergies' customers,** and more generally on changing energy practices like the shift from internal combustion vehicles to electric vehicles for road transport, and the use of heat pumps instead of fuel oil or gas boilers in buildings.


TotalEnergies is guiding its customers, through its multi-energy strategy that offers a range of energies and solutions that contribute to the energy transition.


**9- What is wrong with the idea of an injunction based on the vigilance plan?**


TotalEnergies is arguing that compelling, through an injunction based on the vigilance plan, a transformation of the Company's global strategy would go beyond the scope of what the law requires. And it would also be ineffective in reducing global emissions insofar as the demand for energy would then be met by other actors, that may potentially have less stringent standards. It would also run counter to the public interest by jeopardizing France's and Europe's energy security, price stability and the ability to fund the development of low-carbon energies.


**10- What is TotalEnergies' position on the reality of climate change and the energy transition?**


**TotalEnergies has acknowledged the reality of climate change** for many years and is committed to supporting the global energy transition within the national frameworks defined by States to achieve the objectives of the Paris Agreement. While continuing to meet society's current energy needs, **the Company is consistently investing in the energies of tomorrow.** This momentum enables TotalEnergies to position itself **among the** **world's leading renewables developers.** **By 2030, the Company is aiming to achieve a 25% reduction in the carbon intensity of all energy products sold compared to the 2015 baseline.**


**11- Why** **can the energy transition not rest solely on a single company?**


The transition of energy systems requires a coordinated shift in energy supply and demand at every level. Power generation, which amounts to 14 billion tons of CO2 a year, must evolve in order to reduce the use of coal, which alone accounts for three quarters of the sector's emissions. The Transport sector, which emits 8 billion tons of CO2 a year, has to change so that land, sea and air travel can use increasingly lower-carbon energies (EVs, biofuels, liquefied natural gas as a marine fuel, biogas). Heat production for buildings and industry also has to change towards increased electrification or hybridization, combining thermal and electric heat production. TotalEnergies does not manufacture the equipment that uses energy; the energy transition requires a coordinated effort by all actors in the energy system – States, businesses and customers.


**12- Why** **would banning a company from producing oil and gas make no sense?**


Oil and gas production primarily meets the concrete energy needs of the global economy, which still relies largely on those resources to function. TotalEnergies continues to invest in existing fields and new oil and gas projects to meet growing energy demand and to offset the natural decline of oil and gas fields (averaging 6% to 7% per year for the industry, according to the IEA's 2025 report). These investment decisions are guided by selectivity criteria to ensure both profitability and reduced operated emissions across our portfolio.


TotalEnergies conducts its exploration and production activities in strict compliance with the legal frameworks and national regulations in force in each country where it operates. Defining energy trajectories, determining the pace of the transition and decline of fossil fuels and setting market rules are the sovereign responsibility of individual States. No country in the world, including France, bans the consumption of fossil fuels within its territory.


**13- How** **would an adverse ruling against TotalEnergies affect France's energy security and sovereignty?**


In the context of recent crises (Russia/Ukraine and the Middle East) or other crises that may arise, a ruling that would limit production or halt future oil and gas exploration and production projects worldwide by a French company such as TotalEnergies would affect the security of the energy supply to France and Europe. It is precisely because of the global diversity of TotalEnergies' portfolio (operations in 27 countries for oil and gas and 11 countries for LNG) and the integration of its refining, trading and distribution activities that, despite the closure of the Strait of Hormuz, TotalEnergies can guarantee supply security for its customers, including those in France, and maintain a unique commercial policy in France designed to protect purchasing power.


A court injunction that lessened this diversification, disrupted projects or reduced supply capacities would undermine the resilience of an industrial model that contributes directly to the energy security of France and Europe, most likely to the benefit of other foreign operators, without any positive impact on the climate, since such an injunction would have no effect on the structure of energy demand in France, Europe or globally.


**14- How much electricity does TotalEnergies produce?**


Starting from nearly zero in 2020, **TotalEnergies produced close to 50 TWh of electricity worldwide in 2025** , notably thanks to its **35 GW of gross installed renewable energy capacity** , which is equivalent to the capacity of about fifteen nuclear reactors. **The Company plans to increase electricity production to more than 100 TWh by 2030.**


**15- What emission reductions has TotalEnergies already achieved?**


TotalEnergies has a long-standing commitment to reducing direct emissions resulting from its operated industrial activities.


The Company is continuing to reduce emissions related to its operations (operated Scopes 1 and 2). TotalEnergies has already reduced its operated greenhouse gas emissions by 28% since 2015, and we are aiming for a net reduction of 40% by 2030, as well as an 80% reduction in methane emissions (compared to 2020), which were already 65% lower as of 2025. In addition, the Company continues to reduce the lifecycle carbon intensity (Scopes 1, 2 and 3) of the energy products we sell to our customers, with an 18.6% decrease in 2025 compared to 2015 and a target reduction of 25% by 2030.


**16- What is the trend in the carbon intensity of TotalEnergies' production?**


TotalEnergies has reduced the carbon intensity of its operated oil and gas production, which fell from 21 kg of CO2e per barrel of oil equivalent — boe —produced in 2015 to 16 kg in 2025. This is approximately three times lower than the industry average (source: IEA 2023, *The Oil and Gas Industry in Net Zero Transitions* ).


**17- What role does TotalEnergies play in decarbonizing transportation?**


Land, maritime and air transport account for approximately 8 billion tons of CO2 per year. That carbon intensity can be reduced through technical enhancements (engine efficiency) and technological developments (e.g. electric vehicles) in both the production of vehicles, ships and aircraft and the type of energy used (e.g. biofuels to supplement petroleum products, or liquefied natural gas instead of petroleum products for ships). TotalEnergies is supporting that transformation by developing an integrated electricity business, deploying charging stations for electric vehicles and producing biofuels as well as marine LNG. However, TotalEnergies cannot determine the pace at which electric vehicles or other low-carbon technologies are adopted by the various market participants.
