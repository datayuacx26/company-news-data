# Q&A: Everything you need to know about TotalEnergies’ taxation

**How much does TotaEnergies pay in taxes worldwide? And in France?**


TotalEnergies (TTE) pays far more in corporate income tax than any other French company. Since 2022, the Company has paid $100 billion in corporate income tax and production royalties.


Worldwide, TotalEnergies' tax rate averages at 43% since 2022.


In 2025, the tax rate came to 40%, or $19 billion.


These average rates clearly show that TotalEnergies is not relocating its profits to low-tax countries.


When our profits rise, our tax bill rises too. Our revenues are taxed according to their amount and the country they were generated in.


In 2022, when profits were high, our current income tax amounted to almost $20 billion, representing an effective tax rate of over 51%. If we include production royalties, the total tax bill for 2022 amounted to over $35 billion.


**How much does TotaEnergies pay in taxes in France?**


Our total contribution to French public finances (tax and employer social security contributions) **in 2025 came to over $2 billion** .


This figure puts France in the top five countries (out of 130) where the Company pays the most in tax of all kinds, despite producing no oil or gas there.


Paying corporate income tax in France would mean that the Company is profitable there, that its refining is making money and that its revenues could be taxed. Unfortunately, our refining and petrochemical activities are, overall, structurally loss-making.


That's why closures and bankruptcies have halved refining capacity in France since the 1990s. As has been covered in the press recently, the other oil majors have left France or intend to do so. If the business was profitable, they would remain.


**Why can't TotalEnergies' overseas profits be taxed in France?**


France's tax regime is based on territoriality, meaning that only those profits that have been generated in a country can be taxed in that country.


Almost all international tax treaties signed by France prevent its taxing the Company's overseas profits again.


Taxing this income again in France, after the Company has already been taxed at high rates in the country of production (43% average effective tax rate since 2022), amounts to double taxation, in violation of all internationally recognized tax principles and international tax treaties signed by France.


**Why does TotalEnergies voluntarily issue a tax report by country?**


TotalEnergies is the first, and remains one of the only, French companies to voluntarily release its results and corporate income tax by country (country by country reporting or CBCR), despite having no obligation to do so, in the interests of transparency for its stakeholders in our 120 host countries.


This report has been published since 2022, and includes data as far back as 2019. The reporting began six years before the EU CBCR directive (which begins at the end of 2026 for the 2025 figures) and covers more countries than required.


We chose countries according to specific criteria:
- Countries that come under the EU public CBCR directive: EU Member States, members of the European Economic Area and countries on the list of non-cooperative jurisdictions;
- Countries covered by the directive on transparency in extractive industries: i.e. countries where the Company has extractive activities;
- We decided to voluntarily add countries generally considered to be tax havens, regardless of whether they were included in the list of non-cooperative jurisdictions (Bermuda, the Bahamas, the Cayman Islands). This list is subjective in nature, given that there is no consensus on what constitutes a tax haven nor any official list.


In total, our report covers some 70 countries that account for over 90% of our corporate income tax worldwide.


**Is Switzerland really a tax haven for TotalEnergies?**


Switzerland is not a tax haven. Its corporate income tax is 15%, which is higher than some EU Member States, such as Hungary (9%), Bulgaria (10%), and Ireland (12.5%). Globe Pillar 2, the initiative for a minimum global tax rate, sets 15% as standard. This reform was negotiated and approved by over 140 countries, led by the OECD, the G7 and the G20. France helped drive these negotiations.


TOTSA is a genuine Swiss-based activity rather than a tax-motivated location: Switzerland is a global trade center for oil and gas, and commodities, by virtue of its expertise in international trade and financing and the speed of its administration, all of which establish it as a leading international marketplace.


We employ over 1,000 people in Switzerland in a range of businesses: retail, international trade, insurance.


**Does TotalEnergies still have affiliates in tax havens?**


The Company has pledged not to create affiliates in countries generally considered to be tax havens and to move or liquidate existing entities wherever possible.


Consequently, 27 consolidated entities have been liquidated or transferred to Europe since 2012, from Bermuda, the Bahamas, the Cayman Islands and the British Virgin Islands.


At the end of 2025, there remain seven consolidated affiliates in these countries (six in Bermuda and one in the Bahamas), and just two of these are more than 50% owned by the Company:
- The Bahamas entity came from the acquisition of Sapura OMW from a third party at the end of 2024; the transfer of its headquarters outside of the Bahamas is under examination and will be carried out.
- The other entity is a minority interest in the liquefaction plant in Yemen; given the current conflict in Yemen it cannot currently be relocated.


The five other consolidated affiliates are very small minority interests. These are the Company's minority interests in liquefaction projects in Nigeria, Angola and Oman; as a minority shareholder, the Company did not choose the location and cannot decide to relocate them.


There is no tax advantage to locating these entities' headquarters in Bermuda or the Bahamas, however: their activities and profits are conducted and taxed in the relevant producing countries. No revenues are generated in the country where they are headquartered. The location was chosen for practical reasons, particularly the holding of accounts in dollars. The entities concerned, most of which were created before 2010, were also declared and taxed in France under the consolidated profit system until it was scrapped by the legislator.


**Does TotalEnergies use transfer pricing to optimize its taxes?**


Transfer prices are poorly understood and the subject of false information that misleads the public.


Transfer prices cannot be used to optimize or evade taxes. They are simply internal prices at which products and services are sold within a group of companies.


Transfer prices are not an issue at TotalEnergies: the idea of an “internal arrangement” on prices betrays a complete misunderstanding of how the business operates. Every entity in the Company buys from and sells to other entities at prices that are internationally listed and overseen by government. Over 90% of intra-group business is based on international listings. Prices are transparent. Market prices are available to anyone, unlike other fields of activity. It is absolutely impossible for our trading affiliates to buy oil from our production affiliates at $50 and then sell it to our refineries at $100 in order to pocket the difference, as some have claimed.


It may be widely believed, but we do not refine products from the crude oil that we produce and then sell it through our service stations. Most of the crude oil produced by our production affiliates is sold by Trading to third parties on markets at international prices, and Trading buys most by far of the crude oil we refine on the market from third parties. The model described by many self-proclaimed experts, in which oil companies refine the crude oil they produce, disappeared at the end of the 1970s.


Our transfer prices are very strictly and frequently checked by tax authorities in producing countries and importing countries.


**How are TotalEnergies' dividends taxed in France?**


These dividends are taxed: in France, that includes a withholding tax on dividends paid to our foreign shareholders. The average rate is 15%. By comparison, the UK, home to our major European rivals Shell and BP, has no withholding tax.


Our French shareholders are also taxed, either under income tax (31.4% PFU) or corporate income tax.
