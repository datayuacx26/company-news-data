# Response on TotalEnergies’ Alleged “Superprofits”

**1 - Oil and gas profits are generated and taxed in producing countries**


TotalEnergies' results in oil and gas are not generated in France, but overwhelmingly in the countries where hydrocarbons are produced (Africa, the Middle East, the Americas, Asia-Pacific).


In these countries, profits are subject to specific tax regimes, including royalties, production taxes and corporate income tax. The tax burden increases mechanically when oil and gas prices are high, to the benefit of host countries.


The Group is already one of the most highly taxed in the world, with an average global tax rate of 43% since 2022 (while the corporate tax rate in France is 25%). This represents tens of billions of dollars in taxes and levies paid each year by TotalEnergies worldwide: $25 billion on average per year since 2022, i.e. $100 billion over the past four years.


Seeking to tax these same profits a second time at the level of consuming countries


amounts to ignoring the reality of international oil taxation and introduces double taxation, contrary to the principle of tax territoriality, a principle that the European Union and France consistently uphold in the international order.


In France, TotalEnergies is subject to corporate income tax based on its activities within the national territory, in particular on the results of its refining activities.


**2 - Refining in Europe, a structurally fragile activity**


Refining activities in Europe – including in France – are cyclical, highly volatile and often loss-making over the long term, due to European overcapacity, more stringent regulations than abroad, and international competition.


In this context, the rare years in which refining results are positive only partially offset years of losses. Specifically taxing these profits amounts to penalizing an activity that remains globally fragile, at the risk of accelerating the closure of industrial assets in Europe.


It would be economically inconsistent to overtax only the favorable years of a structurally loss-making activity, while Europe is seeking to preserve its security of supply.


In 2026, if the results of the refining activity remain positive over the full year, as they were in March (but not in January or February), TotalEnergies will pay corporate income tax as well as


the additional exceptional corporate tax contribution.


**3 - TotalEnergies' profitability is the result of its cost control and the work of its teams**


For more than 10 years, TotalEnergies has undertaken a continuous effort to reduce its production costs (and its greenhouse gas emissions), significantly lower its breakeven point, and adjust the selectivity of its investments.


This effort is essential because oil and gas prices are determined by international markets dependent on geopolitics, global supply and demand—particularly in Asia—and technological and regulatory disruptions. It is therefore essential for a producer to remain profitable when prices are low.


It should be recalled that TotalEnergies did not benefit from any public support during the collapse in prices in 2020 during the Covid crisis, and that the losses and impairments of that period were entirely absorbed by the company and its shareholders.


It would be paradoxical to penalize today a discipline in management that precisely ensures the profitability of TotalEnergies' activities regardless of price levels, while this discipline benefits its employees, its suppliers and customers, its shareholders, and the States concerned with preserving security of energy supply under all market conditions.
