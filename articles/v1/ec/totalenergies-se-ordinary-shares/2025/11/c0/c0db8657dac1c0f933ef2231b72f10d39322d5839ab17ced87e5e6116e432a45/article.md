# Rwanda: TotalEnergies and DelAgua join forces to provide access to clean cooking for 200,000 households

**Paris, November 13, 2025** – TotalEnergies has entered into a partnership with DelAgua to distribute improved cooking stoves to hundreds of thousands of Rwandans. This initiative supports Rwanda's ambition to provide universal access to clean cooking by 2030 and is in line with TotalEnergies' ambition to provide more affordable, more available, more sustainable energy accessible to as many people as possible.


Thanks to funding from TotalEnergies, DelAgua will supply 200,000 high-performance cooking stoves over the course of a year, benefiting more than 800,000 Rwandans living in rural areas. Their use reduces harmful smoke emissions by 81% compared to traditional open fires and cuts wood consumption by 71%. The project is expected to avoid the emission of more than 2.5 million tons of CO2 equivalent over the next 10 years.


**The benefits of clean cooking**


According to the IEA, more than 2.3 billion people worldwide do not have access to clean cooking and cook in traditional stoves using wood, charcoal, kerosene, coal, and even animal waste. Clean cooking makes it possible to:


- **improve people's health** with better air quality, limiting the risk of respiratory complications and cardiovascular disease.
- **reduce gender inequality** by facilitating access to education, employment, or entrepreneurship and, ultimately, financial independence for women. Access to clean cooking saves a considerable amount of time for people who can spend up to 20 hours a week collecting wood for cooking.
- **reduce CO2 emissions and deforestation.** Universal access to clean cooking would prevent the emission of 1.5 gigatons of CO2 equivalent by 2030 (including 900 million tons in Africa), equivalent to the CO2 emissions produced by air and sea transport in 2022 or the disappearance of forests covering an area equivalent to that of Ireland each year.


**Carbon credits recognized by the Paris Agreement**


The carbon credits generated by the project will be acquired by TotalEnergies. They will initially be certified by the international organization VERRA, pending their approval under Article 6.4 of the Paris Agreement's Credit Mechanism as soon as it becomes operational.


After prioritizing emissions avoidance and reduction, the Company will use these credits from 2030 onwards for the voluntary offsetting of part of its residual direct Scope 1 and 2 emissions.


"TotalEnergies is proud to be part of this project in Rwanda, which will give more than 800,000 people access to clean cooking, illustrating the Company's purpose of providing more affordable, more available and more sustainable energy to as many people as possible. By developing access to these more sustainable cooking methods, TotalEnergies aims to have a positive impact on the environment and people's health, while helping to reduce gender inequalities. Clean cooking contributes to economic, social and human development in a more sustainable way," said **Arnaud Le Foll, Director of New Business – Carbon Neutrality at TotalEnergies.**


"This partnership demonstrates what can be achieved when innovation and collaboration come together. By combining DelAgua's proven track record of large-scale impact with TotalEnergies' commitment to sustainable development, we are transforming lives in Rwanda while setting a new benchmark for high-quality carbon projects globally," said **Euan McDougall, President and CEO of DelAgua.**
