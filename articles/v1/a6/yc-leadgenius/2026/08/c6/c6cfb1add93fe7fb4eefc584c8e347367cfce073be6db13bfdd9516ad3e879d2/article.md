# The idea of an AI SDR is showing some real weakness

The Wolf of Wall Street as the face of "AI will replace your sales team."


When Artisan announced in early April that Jordan Belfort was joining as VP of Sales, my first reaction was: who approved this?
‍


Not because the movie isn't entertaining. It is. But the moral of that story was never "aspire to this man." Jordan Belfort spent years lying to people, stealing their money, and running a fraud operation that wrecked ordinary investors' finances before he did his time for it. He's a great character. He is a strange choice to be the human face of a technology you're asking businesses to trust with their pipeline and their customer relationships.


Hiring the guy most famous for teaching people how to manipulate strangers into buying things they didn't need, as the spokesperson for "the AI will handle your outreach so it's more honest and efficient" pitch, is somewhat comedic. Sarcastically: great pick.


Now, a few months later, Artisan is hiring human BDRs and retiring the "stop hiring humans" slogan that made them famous. Growth has been modest, not the hockey stick the billboards implied. Draw your own conclusions about what that tells you about the AI SDR category.


Here's the part that actually matters, though. This isn't really about Artisan. It's about outbound.


Cold email reply rates dropped from roughly 5% in 2025 to about 3.4% in 2026, per Instantly's benchmark data, continuing a decline that's been running for years as inboxes get more crowded and spam filters get smarter. Cold calling isn't spared either. AI call screeners and spam-labeling on phone numbers are quietly killing connect rates the same way filters killed email.


The pattern is consistent: the more the entire industry tries to automate volume, the worse the results get. Every company automating outreach at scale is fighting for attention in the same saturated channel, using tools that make everyone's messages look and sound the same. AI didn't fix the noise problem. It made more noise.


The way to stand out isn't more volume, it's less of it, done better. A handwritten note. A physical piece of mail that isn't a bill or a catalog. A short video made for one specific person, not a template with a merge field. Spamming the world faster isn't a strategy. It's the reason the world stopped listening.
