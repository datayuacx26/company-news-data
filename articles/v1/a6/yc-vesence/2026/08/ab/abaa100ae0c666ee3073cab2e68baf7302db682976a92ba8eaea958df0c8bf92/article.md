# What's New in Vesence: July 2026

## The Web App


Almost everything we shipped in July points in one direction: the web app. Vesence began as an intelligence layer inside Microsoft Office, and Word, Outlook, Excel, and PowerPoint are still where the work gets finished. But the place people direct that work from has become the web.


So July went into building the most powerful agentic workspace on the market: one intuitive place to brief agents, watch them work, and take over the moment you want to. Five things stand out.


## 1. Create and Edit Directly in the Web


You can now create and manually edit emails, Teams messages, documents, spreadsheets, and PowerPoint presentations directly within the chat. Each surface has a real editor rather than a preview, so changing a sentence no longer means downloading a file.


Everything an agent proposes arrives as tracked changes. Step through them, accept, reject, or type over them yourself, and the formatting holds throughout. More in[Introducing Vesence Editor](https://www.vesence.com/blog/vesence-editor) .


## 2. Vesence Projects


Most professional work isn't a single question. It's a matter that runs for weeks around the same files, the same colleagues, and the same standing instructions. Projects give that work a home: files, chats, and drafts in one place, shared with your team.


Data, files, context, and instructions live on the project, and every chat inside it inherits them, so users and agents can work continuously around the same matter instead of restarting each session. It's an addition to SharePoint or your DMS, not a replacement. More in[Introducing Vesence Projects](https://www.vesence.com/blog/vesence-projects) .


## 3. A New Task and Chat Hierarchy


A task can now contain multiple chats. Complex work divides into separate conversations that stay organized under the same objective — one chat drafting the agreement, another checking cross-references, a third working through the counterparty's comments.


This is structure more than it is a feature, and it's what makes long-running work across many agents legible. You can see what's running, what finished, and what still needs you.


## 4. An App Store for Every Organization


Users can build their own apps in Vesence and share them across the organization. Describe the tool your team needs and the agent builds it: a chronology tracker, a cap table reconciler, a conditions precedent monitor.


Publish an app to your firm's Library and it appears in everyone's launchpad. In effect, each organization ends up with a private app store tailored to its own processes, built by the people who actually know the work. More in[Introducing Vesence Apps](https://www.vesence.com/blog/introducing-vesence-apps) .


## 5. Custom CLIs and Third-Party Integrations


Organizations can now integrate virtually any third-party system into Vesence: ERP systems, timekeeping tools, document templating software, and accounting platforms.


It works through custom CLIs. Rather than a bespoke connector per system, agents get a command-line interface to yours — the same mechanism they already use to read and write Office files. Adding a system becomes a matter of describing it.


## What This Adds Up To


Taken together, July moves Vesence past being an AI chat interface. People, agents, data, applications, and enterprise systems now work together in one place: you direct the work, and it gets done in the tools you already use.
