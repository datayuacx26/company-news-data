# Best Shopify Alternatives: When You Need a Site, Not a Store

## How we checked these numbers


We read every price, fee and limit off the vendor's own pricing page or help centre where those render to a fetch, and off dated independent trackers where they do not. Two we could not read: **neither Squarespace's nor Elementor's pricing page served dollar figures** , so those are third-party-sourced and attributed by name. Shopify, Ghost and Wix's plan ladder were checked **18 August 2026** ; Blink and Webflow across **14–18 August** ; Wix's fee and CMS-ceiling figures, WordPress and Squarespace **14 August** . "Not stated" means the vendor does not publish it and we have not guessed.


**We have run no speed tests, no Lighthouse audits and no migration timings of our own.**


**We publish no dollar figure for Shopify app spend, because no sourced one exists.** The year-one totals for Wix, Webflow, Squarespace and WordPress all carry cited add-on spend; Shopify's is subscription plus domain only, which flatters it on exactly the axis its reviewers complain about most.


**Disclosure: Blink is our product, and we rank it first.** Every page competing for this term is written by a vendor, an affiliate or both — BigCommerce ranks BigCommerce first, Wix takes slots one and two, Salesforce puts two CRM products above any store builder — and most do not say so. What should decide your trust here is that the numbers link to their source.


## What Shopify actually costs, and what it locks


Shopify's homepage sells the ambition, not the machinery — "the world's best commerce platform", selling "everywhere people shop. Online and in person"


Blink


The[published ladder](https://www.shopify.com/pricing) , read 18 August 2026, runs **Basic $29/mo, Grow $79/mo, Advanced $299/mo billed annually** , with Plus from a negotiated **$2,300/mo** floor. There is **no free plan** — a 3-day trial, then **$1/month for three months** , then full price. The real cost is invisible for a quarter.


**Basic includes zero additional staff accounts** , so hiring one person forces the step to Grow: a **172% subscription increase** . Advanced to Plus is a **7.7× step** . Our arithmetic over Shopify's list prices puts year one at **$363 to $3,603** , app spend and processing excluded.


The reviewers are precise about where the pain sits, and it is not the sticker. On[Capterra's Shopify listing](https://www.capterra.com/p/83891/Shopify/) , read 18 August 2026 across 6,706 reviews, **transaction fees are the most negative theme at 78% negative across 250 reviews** , "combined monthly costs plus required paid apps create high ongoing expenses" runs **65% negative across 248** , and **Value for Money is the lowest sub-score at 4.3 across 5,111 raters** .


The largest negative pool goes further: "frequent technical glitches and delays" runs **65% negative across 554 reviews** , and those reviews name **unreliable support** as part of what disrupts trading. That sits awkwardly against Customer Service scoring 4.4 across 4,891 raters. The likeliest reading is that routine support is fine and support during an incident is not — but that is a reading of two differently-built measurements, not a finding, and both are printed rather than reconciled.


Note the word reviewers use: **required** . Not optional.


In a July 2026 thread titled["Which Shopify app do you pay for but only use one feature of?"](https://community.shopify.com/t/which-shopify-app-do-you-pay-for-but-only-use-one-feature-of/659021) , the agency Weaverse described auditing one client: *"they were paying around $80/month across 4 different micro-apps just for basic visual sections on product pages"* — an FAQ accordion, product tabs, a countdown bar, a testimonial grid. One merchant, one store, and it names the category precisely: **those four line items are page layout, not commerce.**


The same pattern runs through a forum thread titled["Why is the Blog feature so bad?"](https://community.shopify.com/t/why-is-the-blog-feature-so-bad-are-they-looking-to-improve-this-feature-tips-from-anyone/367988) , open since October 2024 and still collecting replies in January 2026, where a Shopify community moderator answers: *"shopify is a starting point it can't do everything it is an ecommerce system first… When a blogging/content-management-systems is the priority see wordpress etc."*


That is the whole argument on this page, written by Shopify's own community.


Then the export. Shopify does something Wix and Squarespace do not: **you can download your theme** , as real Liquid, HTML, CSS and JavaScript. But[its own documentation](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/online-store/themes/managing-themes/downloading-themes) states the download "does not include your store's products, collections, menus, pages, blog posts, images, or other files", nothing exports the checkout or the admin, and a Theme Store theme is "licensed only to the store that you originally bought it for."


Now give Shopify its due, because the case for it is strong: **96% positive across 859 Capterra reviews** on being a comprehensive ecommerce builder, **$292.3B processed in 2024** , **unlimited product listings on every plan** where Wix, Squarespace and Webflow cap items, and a **POS** product nothing here approaches — in-person rates from 2.6% + 10¢, up to 200 locations on Plus.


None of that is why you are here. You are here because your store has fewer SKUs than your site has pages.


## The store platforms, and when one of them is your answer


Five platforms recur across the pages ranking for this term. We did not price them — they sit outside this comparison and we carry no vendor data for them — but we can say which buyer each serves.


**BigCommerce** is the answer the internet actually gives — the[r/ecommerce thread ranking first for this search](https://www.reddit.com/r/ecommerce/comments/rrf1d1/which_sites_do_you_recommend_as_an_alternative_to/) answers in one line, *"BigCommerce is better than shopify in almost every way."* It is the shortlist entry for a B2B or high-SKU seller who has hit Shopify's ceilings.


**Adobe Commerce (Magento)** is the enterprise answer, and belongs on your list only with a development team, a catalogue needing ERP sync, and a multi-month window.


**Square Online** is right for a business with a till, because the store syncs against the register with no double entry. Nothing here, us included, beats it for a bakery.


**Ecwid** embeds a cart into a site you already have — which every ranking page frames as a downgrade, and isn't: if your site already works and ranks, adding a checkout is the right architecture.


**Big Cartel** serves an artist or maker with a few dozen products who wants a shop, not a platform.


Every option above is a store platform: the catalogue is the architecture, the site a theme around it. That is the right trade when the catalogue *is* the business.


### Answer four questions and the shortlist picks itself


1. **How many products?** Under 50 and the store is a section of a site. 50 to 500, and Squarespace, BigCommerce or WooCommerce. Over 500 and you want Shopify or BigCommerce.
2. **Does the software have to track inventory?** Stock across locations, a till, fulfilment — **Shopify or BigCommerce** , and no migration plan substitutes for it.
3. **Where does traffic come from?** Paid ads into product pages, and a store platform is fine. Search, content, a newsletter — the site *is* the business.
4. **Who changes the site next month?** A developer on retainer, and Webflow or WooCommerce work. Nobody, or you at 11pm, and you want the tool that changes by being described.


Two answers pointing at the catalogue and you should stay where you are. Two the other way and this page is about you.

## 2. Squarespace — the best template catalogue, with a real small store in it


Squarespace leads on template-driven design and a "Start for free. No credit card required." button — wording that reads like a free plan but buys a trial, because there is no free tier


Blink


**Price:** Basic $19/mo · Core $29/mo · Plus $49/mo · Advanced $99/mo, billed annually. No free plan — a 14-day trial. Third-party-sourced from[WebsiteBuilderExpert](https://www.websitebuilderexpert.com/website-builders/squarespace-pricing/) and tech.co, because Squarespace's pricing page served no dollar figures. **Best for:** a boutique brand or studio whose site is the sales pitch and whose catalogue is small


Nobody has a better catalogue of pre-made templates. Roughly 200 designer-made options let someone with no design skill ship something professional on day one, and Capterra rates it[4.6 across 3,415 reviews](https://www.capterra.com/p/143461/Squarespace/reviews/) .


Its commerce terms are cleaner than Shopify's on the axis this article cares about: a **2% commerce transaction fee on Basic only — 0% from Core upward** — with card rates stepping 2.9% + 30¢ to 2.5% on Advanced. Digital products carry a separate fee of 7% on Basic falling to 0% on Advanced, and ceilings are generous: 10,000 products site-wide on 7.1, **no limit on blog posts** .


The ceiling arrives fast if the store grows: **no multi-currency selling, gateways limited to Stripe, PayPal and Squarespace Payments, a POS that is US and UK only** , and an Extensions marketplace of roughly 48 items against thousands for Shopify.


Costs also moved against customers: on **17 July 2026 Squarespace raised prices by up to 26%** , annual Core from $23 to $29 and Plus from $39 to $49, notified by email with no public memo,[as PetaPixel reported](https://petapixel.com/2026/07/17/squarespace-is-increasing-prices-by-up-to-26/) . The export is a partial, one-way WordPress-oriented XML file — layout pages, *one* blog page and its posts, text and image blocks, with no style settings, no custom CSS and no store pages. **A store exports essentially nothing structural.**


**Verdict:** right if the site is a brochure with a small shop attached and you will not restructure it. If it has to keep changing, you will meet the frame. See[Blink vs Squarespace](https://blink.new/vs/squarespace) .

## Moving off Shopify without losing the orders


You cannot export a running Shopify store, so the order matters more than the tooling.


1


#### Export your data while the store is still live


Products, collections, customers and orders export as CSV. Download the theme as a design reference — it carries no products, pages, posts or images.


2


#### Rebuild the site, not the store


Describe the business and what it sells. You are not recreating a commerce platform — you are building the site that sells, with a checkout in it.


3


#### Wire the payments before you cut over


Test checkout end to end on the preview URL while Shopify is still taking orders, and decide who owns tax, shipping rates and refunds.


4


#### Keep the URLs that earn traffic


Map every page with rankings or backlinks and keep the path identical where you can. Redirect the rest. This is the step people skip, and the one that costs revenue.


5


#### Point the domain, then cancel


CNAME to` cname.blink.new` ; SSL is issued within 5–10 minutes of DNS verification. Watch orders for a billing cycle before cancelling Shopify.


Do not start this if your catalogue needs real inventory management, multi-location stock or in-person selling. Those are the things Shopify does that a website does not, and no amount of migration planning replaces them.
