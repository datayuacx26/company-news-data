# Best Squarespace Alternatives: What All-in-One Leaves Out

## How we checked these numbers


**Disclosure: Blink is our product, and we rank it first.** A vendor, an affiliate or both wrote every competing article on this term, and most do not say so — one of the six ranking pages is a page-builder vendor ranking its own product fourth in its own roundup, and another's six recommendations are also its six advertising partners.


Prices and limits were read off each vendor's own pricing page or help centre where those render to a fetch, and from dated independent write-ups where they do not — Squarespace falls in that second group, because its pricing page draws dollar figures client-side.


Squarespace was checked **14 August 2026** ; Webflow's prices 18 August and limits 14 August; Wix's plan ladder was read off[Wix's own plans page](https://www.wix.com/plans) on **18 August 2026** with its other limits checked 14 August; Framer, Ghost, Shopify and Carrd **18 August 2026** . Blink's prices and template gallery were re-derived 18 August 2026, its capability facts checked 14 August 2026. Claims about the ranking pages come from our own audit of them, read 18 August 2026.


**We have run no speed tests, Lighthouse audits or migration timings of our own.** Every performance figure is someone else's, attributed to them, and every "realistic year one" is our arithmetic over published list prices. Where a vendor's own pages contradict each other, we say so and name both. That happens six times below. **Four of them are ours.**


**Why WordPress is not on this list.** It is the answer four of the six ranking pages give, so it deserves a reason rather than a silence. WordPress is not a product you buy, it is a project you run: hosting, updates, plugin security and backups become your job, and every roundup pricing it at "free" omits the hosting bill and the maintenance hours. If you left Squarespace because you did not want to be a webmaster, WordPress is a lateral move — Ghost, in section five, is the managed version of the same philosophy.


## What Squarespace actually costs, and what it locks


Squarespace's homepage, selling the outcome — "A website makes it real" — over three template mockups, with a 20% intro code in the top bar; the "start for free" line is a 14-day trial, not a permanent free plan


Blink


Squarespace's advertised entry price is real. It is also the smallest part of what a working business site costs there.


The ladder runs **Basic $19/mo, Core $29/mo, Plus $49/mo and Advanced $99/mo billed annually** — $228, $348, $588 and $1,188 a year, or $25, $39, $65 and $139 paid monthly. There is no free plan at any point, only a 14-day trial.


Those numbers moved recently, and against existing customers. On **17 July 2026 Squarespace raised prices by up to 26%** — annual Core from $23 to $29, Plus from $39 to $49, Basic from $16 to $19 — notified by email with no public memo,[as PetaPixel reported](https://petapixel.com/2026/07/17/squarespace-is-increasing-prices-by-up-to-26/) . Most pages ranking for this term still quote the old numbers.


Our arithmetic puts year one at **$228 bare minimum, about $444 on Core with Email Campaigns, and $2,196** on Advanced with Acuity Scheduling and Member Areas.


The structural limits matter more than the money.


- **No code export, ever.** The[export is a partial, one-way WordPress-oriented XML file](https://support.squarespace.com/hc/en-us/articles/206566687-Exporting-your-site) — layout pages, *one* blog page with its posts, text and image blocks, and none of your style settings, custom CSS, store, portfolio, event, index or calendar pages.
- **No template switching on 7.1** , and developer mode is 7.0 only. Changing the look means building a new site.
- **No autosave and no version history** for content.
- **You cannot edit robots.txt or the sitemap** , canonical tags and advanced schema are not directly controllable, and redirects are 301-only with no bulk upload and no regex.
- **Refunds are narrow.** Annual subscriptions within 14 days only, monthly plans not at all, Email Campaigns explicitly non-refundable.
- **AI is metered** at **10 to 120 credits per month depending on plan** .


**Nobody has a better catalogue of templates.** Roughly 200 designer-made, fully responsive templates let someone with no design skill ship something professional on day one. That is the reason to choose it, and it is not a small one. Capterra rates Squarespace[4.6 out of 5 across 3,415 reviews](https://www.capterra.com/p/143461/Squarespace/reviews/) , Ease of Use 4.5, and Tooltester measured 99.96% uptime.


The two lowest sub-scores tell you where this article comes from: Features 4.3 and Value for Money 4.3, the lowest two of six.

## Still buyable in 2026?


Three of the six pages ranking for this term recommend **Weebly** , one telling readers to buy the Professional plan. Square is winding Weebly down in[67 countries on a published timeline](https://www.weebly.com/app/help/us/en/topics/changes-to-weebly-global-operations) : announced 20 June 2026, no new pages publishable from 29 June, **sites unpublished 27 September 2026** , account access ending 26 December 2026. Weebly's own help page renders client-side and returned an empty body to us, so we confirmed all four dates against[Khaleej Times, 5 July 2026](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/business/tech/weebly-websites-shut-down-uae-66-more-countries-september) , which works from the customer email and publishes the full country list.


Read the country list before you panic or relax. The US, UK, Canada, Australia and most of the EU are **not** on it — the 67 are largely the Middle East, Africa, South and Southeast Asia and Latin America, plus Russia, Ukraine, Turkey, Singapore, South Korea and Iceland.


If you are in one of them, Weebly is closing under you. If you are in ours it is still sold — but a vendor executing a 67-country withdrawal is not where you put a five-year site. That is the concrete version of platform risk: your site exists at the vendor's discretion, and Squarespace's export would not let you rebuild it.


## 1. Blink — the site is designed for you, and it keeps growing with the business


Blink's Architecture Studio template — a finished monochrome studio site, "Silent Space. Bold Truth.", built with React and Tailwind and changed either by describing what you want or by opening the code directly


Blink


**Price:** Free ($0 — 5 refresh credits a day, capped at 30/month, preview URLs only) · Starter $25/mo or $150/yr · Pro $50/mo or $300/yr · Max from $200/mo or $1,200/yr · Team $40/seat/mo with a 3-seat minimum, so $120/mo real entry. Annual is 50% off, shown on[the pricing page](https://blink.new/pricing) as $13, $25 and $100 a month. **Best for:** the owner, designer or agency who wants a site built to a brief that can absorb bookings, members, courses or a blog without four more subscriptions


Blink does not hand you a catalogue. You describe the business, the site is designed and built for it, and it is hosted on your own domain with SSL issued automatically within 5–10 minutes of DNS verification.


**Design that is not a template.** The[42+ production-ready templates](https://blink.new/templates) , read 18 August 2026, span landing pages, portfolios, online stores, dashboards, SaaS and AI apps, and they are optional starting points. There is no equivalent of the 7.1 template lock, because there is no template to be locked into. The Visual Editor, shipped 6 June 2026, then lets you point and click on an element and edit it without writing a prompt at all — which is what lets an agency hand a site over and stop being the client's webmaster.


**The things Squarespace bills separately are in the plan.** A managed SQL database per project with row-level security built in,[built-in authentication](https://blink.new/docs/extend/authentication) across Google, GitHub, Apple, Microsoft, email/password and magic links, plus file storage, queues, real-time and email from your own domain. Blink takes no percentage of anything you sell. Hosting is metered in credits rather than billed per site — 10,000 visits costs 50 credits on a rolling 30-day cycle, 1,000,000 costs 500.


**SEO is a panel, not a project.** The SEO & AI Search panel, shipped 15 June 2026, scans your live site, scores it 0–100 across five levers and applies fixes in one click, with robots.txt, sitemap, JSON-LD and Open Graph controlled in-product. Squarespace lets you edit neither robots.txt nor the sitemap.


**The economics, for the agency.** Squarespace bills per site, so your book of business is your bill. Ten client sites on Core is **$3,480 a year before a single hour of work** , growing $348 with every client you win. Give Squarespace its due: contributors on Core are unlimited, so the meter is sites, not seats.


The percentage is the part you cannot bill for. A client selling courses on Core pays Squarespace 5% of every sale, and the only route to zero is Advanced at $1,188/yr — $11,880 if you move all ten.


On Blink there is no per-site plan, viewer seats are unlimited and free, and Blink takes no cut of what your clients sell. Ship in days rather than weeks, keep the margin, and hand over a site the client edits themselves instead of a retainer you had to sell. (Ten Webflow Premium site plans, a Core workspace and one extra Full Seat is $3,696/yr on our arithmetic over Webflow's list prices.)


**The exit is written down.** Blink's terms state you "retain full ownership of any code, applications, or other content" you create and may "use, modify, distribute, publish, sell, or open-source" it: a code download, two-way GitHub sync that pulls back edits from VS Code or Cursor, and a Dockerfile template in the export docs naming Vercel, Netlify, Railway and Render.


**The free tier is a preview, not a site.** Production hosting, a custom domain, code download and badge removal all start at Starter, and free-plan projects are public by default. **Credits do not roll over on Starter or Pro** , hosting traffic draws on the same pool as building, and all fees are non-refundable including partially used billing periods.


**Our own pages disagree in four places, and we would rather print that than pick the flattering one.**` /faq` claims every Blink app includes free hosting and a custom domain, against the Starter-and-above gate on` /docs/launch/hosting` .` /docs/faq` says Max credits roll over;` /faq` says rollover applies only to teams on 800+ credit tiers.` /docs/faq` puts Team at $50/seat with a 2-seat minimum against $40/seat and a 3-seat minimum in the billing catalogue. And the Starter database ceiling is documented at 5 GB but enforced at 2 GB.


**What Blink is not.** No native store, no checkout, no inventory, no POS — if your revenue is a product catalogue, Shopify is the answer and it is section six. Blink is also a client-rendered React and Vite app, and no public page states whether server-side rendering or prerendering is available, a material unknown if organic search is your acquisition channel.


**Verdict:** if the site has to do more than look good, and you would rather not pay a percentage every time it does, this is the one. **[Start free at blink.new](https://blink.new/) .** Full breakdown:[Blink vs Squarespace](https://blink.new/vs/squarespace) .


## 2. Webflow — control you operate yourself, priced per axis


Webflow's homepage, pitching the site as a "growth engine" for design and marketing teams — Enterprise, AEO and MCP cards above a logo wall of Spotify, TED and Dropbox, not a solo owner in sight


Blink


**Price:** Site Basic $15/mo · Site Premium $25/mo · Workspace Core $19/mo (required for code export) · each extra Full Seat $39/mo, each Limited Seat $15/mo, billed annually. Reviewer seats are free, up to 100. **Best for:** an in-house design team, or an agency with a dedicated Webflow specialist


Webflow gives you the most direct design control of any visual tool here — box model, flexbox, grid, breakpoints and z-index all exposed and operable, scored[4.6/5 on design capability](https://www.websitebuilderexpert.com/website-builders/webflow-review/) by WebsiteBuilderExpert. Output is clean semantic HTML on a CDN, with editable robots.txt, an auto-generated sitemap and 301 redirects.


**Everything Squarespace withholds.**


That design control is a skill purchase, not a design outcome. The same review scored **ease of use 2.1 out of 5, the lowest of any builder they tested** , and explicitly redirects beginners elsewhere.


The pricing bills on three axes at once: site plans **per website** , Workspace plans separate and gating code export at Core, and seats separate again at $39/mo after the first. Basic has **no CMS at all** , so any site with a blog starts at Premium. A realistic year one is **$540 low, about $1,020 typical and $3,876** for a small team.


**Three bills, one website.**


The May 2026 restructure **cut Premium's included bandwidth to 50 GB** from the old Business plan's 100 GB, bots count toward it, and two consecutive overage months automatically upgrade your plan. Webflow also **discontinued native User Accounts on 29 January 2026** , after disabling Logic on 27 June 2025 — gated content now needs a third-party service. (Both dates rest on a single source; Webflow's announcements return 403 to a fetch.)


The export is narrower than buyers assume: static HTML, CSS, JS and assets, but no working CMS content, forms, site search, ecommerce or memberships.


**Verdict:** right when a designer owns the site as their job and sells the craft. For an owner who wants the outcome, the 2.1 ease-of-use score and the three-axis bill are the whole story. See[Blink vs Webflow](https://blink.new/vs/webflow) .


## 3. Wix — the widest feature surface, and no way out


Wix leads with "the leading website builder" and puts Harmony in the subhead — the Ask Aria panel below is taking a plain-English brief for a cycling club, wrapped around the widest all-in-one feature surface here, and the one with no export at any price


Blink


**Price:** Light $17/mo · Core $29/mo · Business $39/mo · Business Elite $159/mo billed annually. A permanent free plan exists, with Wix ads, a wixsite.com subdomain, no ecommerce and no analytics. **Best for:** a small business that wants bookings, payments, ecommerce and AI in one dashboard and will never leave


Wix does more things than anything else here, and one is a genuine structural cost advantage: **0% platform transaction fees on every plan that sells** , across 20+ payment gateways. (Wix's own plans page, read 18 August 2026, shows ecommerce starting at Core, not on the $17 Light plan.) Against Squarespace's 2% commerce fee and 7/5/1% digital ladder, that is the cleanest reason for a seller to move. Our source for the 0% figure returned a 403 to a direct read, so it is snippet-level rather than page-level evidence; confirm it at Wix first.


Bookings are native on Wix's plans, where Squarespace routes you to Acuity as a separate $16–$49/month subscription.


Capterra rates Wix 4.4 across 10,755 reviews, Ease of Use 4.5 from 10,702 of them. The SEO controls are substantive: editable robots.txt, per-page and bulk robots meta tags, editable slugs, a redirects manager, sitemaps and structured-data presets. G2 reviewers still describe an SEO ceiling on ambitious sites, and that gap between shipped and perceived capability is itself worth knowing.


Wix Harmony, launched 21 January 2026, is the AI builder, driven by an agent called Aria on a credit allowance reported at 500/month on Core — a third-party figure, not a Wix page. There is **no migration path from existing Wix Editor sites** into it.


Then the trade. Wix has **no site or source-code export of any kind** — its help centre states a Wix site "needs to be hosted and operated on Wix's servers" because the architecture "does not support external hosting". You also[cannot switch template after building](https://support.wix.com/en/article/switching-your-site-template) , the same restriction as Squarespace 7.1.


That this is a product decision rather than a technical limit shows in **Base44, which Wix owns** — it does offer real code export on paid plans.


**Verdict:** the strongest pick if you want every business function in one dashboard, you sell physical goods and want 0% platform fees — a figure we could only read at snippet level, so confirm it at Wix first — and you are comfortable never moving. See[Blink vs Wix](https://blink.new/vs/wix) , or the[best Wix alternatives](https://blink.new/blog/best-wix-alternatives) .


## 4. Framer — design-led, fast, priced per editor


Framer's homepage, pitching itself as a "design agent for every step from idea to launch" — a CMS table resolving into a finished page, aimed at designers who want the output to look art-directed


Blink


**Price:** Free (1 GB bandwidth, Framer subdomain, no custom domain, 500 one-time AI credits per Framer's pricing page) · Basic $10/mo yearly, $120/yr · Pro $30/mo yearly, $360/yr · editor seats $20/editor/month, content-editor seats $10. **Best for:** a designer producing marketing sites who wants AI on the canvas rather than in a chat panel


Framer solves the thing Squarespace is weakest at, architecturally. **Framer sites are server-rendered React** — most-visited pages pre-rendered at publish, the long tail on first request, everything cached globally, so crawlers get HTML. It auto-generates sitemap.xml and robots.txt, and its redirects manager does wildcard matching with capture groups.


Framer 3.0, published 16 June 2026, added AI agents working directly on the canvas, plus External Agents in preview connecting Claude Code, Codex, Cursor or Gemini CLI. Credits are published: 500 one-time on Free, 1,000/month on Basic, 3,000/month on Pro, with Framer's own examples putting a landing page at roughly 300.


Do the division. Basic's 1,000 monthly credits is three landing pages.


**A custom robots.txt requires Pro or Enterprise** , through the Pro-gated Static Files feature — and so does` llms.txt` , which Framer does not auto-generate at all. There is no native structured-data UI; Framer's own SEO guide suggests you write the JSON-LD yourself. **Editor seats are a second billing axis** , billed monthly even on an annual site plan and capped at 10. One extra editor is $240/yr against a $120/yr site plan.


On export Framer is unambiguous on its own help centre:["Framer does not offer HTML export for self-hosting"](https://www.framer.com/help/articles/can-i-export-my-website-to-html-and-self-host-it/) . Its terms say all fees are non-cancellable and non-refundable, though Framer publishes a 7-day refund window worldwide and 14 days in the EU and Turkey — two statements that do not sit easily together, and we are not resolving them for it. There is no native ecommerce and no native end-user authentication either, the latter inferred from the third-party auth plugins in Framer's own marketplace.


**Verdict:** use Framer if you are a designer who works on a canvas and one person publishes the site. The moment a second editor touches it the per-editor axis costs more than the Basic site plan, and a custom robots.txt needs Pro. See[Blink vs Framer](https://blink.new/vs/framer) .


## 5. Ghost™ — the right answer if the business is the audience


Ghost's homepage — "Turn your audience into a business" over a members dashboard showing 3,208 paid of 13,042 total; memberships and newsletters are the product, not an add-on with its own percentage cut


Blink


**Price:** Self-hosted is free MIT-licensed software plus your own infrastructure · Ghost(Pro) Starter $18/mo billed yearly · Publisher $29/mo · Business $199/mo. Those are yearly-billed monthly rates; the monthly-billed rates were not exposed on the page we read. **Best for:** a writer or publisher whose revenue is subscriptions, not products


Ghost is run by a non-profit foundation whose constitution forbids its own sale, publishes live financials — $11,055,673 ARR and 30,557 active customers as read 18 August 2026 — and ships MIT-licensed software developed in the open.


If you are on Squarespace running a newsletter and paying a separate tool to send it, Ghost is the answer. Memberships, paid subscriptions and newsletters are the product rather than a $9-to-$35-a-month add-on with its own transaction fee, and all Ghost(Pro) plans include unlimited emails with no per-send charge (independent pricing guide, 24 Oct 2025). **There is no published limit on posts or pages** , against Squarespace's 1,000-page ceiling, and **Ghost 6.0, shipped 5 August 2025** , took ActivityPub federation out of beta.


Now the counterweights. **The bill tracks your subscriber count, not your feature use** — on Publisher, roughly $29/mo at 1,000 members, $88 at 10,000 and $274 at 100,000. (Same 24 Oct 2025 guide: the shape is current, the steps are as of that date.)


**Starter is a dead end rather than a starting point:** 1,000 members, one staff user, official themes only, no paid subscriptions. And **it is a worse design tool than Squarespace** — Capterra's cons cluster on limited customization and advanced changes requiring code. Capterra rates it 4.7 on 53 reviews against Squarespace's 3,415; G2 puts it lower at 4.1, and we are not picking between them.


The export is real but not one button: the JSON carries posts and settings, while images, themes, routes and redirects come out separately, and the Ghost(Pro) to self-hosted direction is undocumented. There is no commerce, and self-hosting adds Node.js, MySQL, backups, upgrades, security and an email provider to your job description.


**Verdict:** if the business is the audience and the revenue is subscriptions, go to Ghost. If the newsletter is one thing your business does among five, you are buying a publishing platform to solve a website problem.


## 6. Shopify — the right answer when the catalogue is the business


Shopify's homepage, selling ambition over site design — "the world's best commerce platform", with "sell everywhere people shop, online and in person" as the first thing below the fold


Blink


**Price:** Basic $29/mo · Grow $79/mo · Advanced $299/mo billed annually ($39/$105/$399 monthly) · Plus from $2,300/mo negotiated. No free plan — a 3-day trial, then $1/month for three months. **Best for:** a real product catalogue with inventory, fulfilment and in-person sales


If you are leaving Squarespace because the store outgrew it, this is the answer and we are not going to pretend otherwise. **Product listings are unlimited on every plan including Basic.**


**Squarespace stops at 10,000.**


Card rates step down as you climb: 2.9% + 30¢ on Basic, 2.7% on Grow, 2.5% on Advanced. Themes are Liquid, HTML, CSS and JavaScript you can read and edit in place, downloadable from the admin — which Wix and Squarespace do not offer at any price.


**The third-party gateway fee is the number buyers skip.** Shopify charges 2% on Basic, 1% on Grow, 0.6% on Advanced and 0.2% on Plus of every sale, **on top of whatever the other processor charges** , purely for not using Shopify Payments. On Basic that 2% exceeds the entire $348 annual subscription at about $17,400 of sales — our arithmetic over Shopify's own two figures. Squarespace's commerce fee disappears at Core; Shopify's only shrinks.


**Basic includes zero additional staff accounts** , so hiring one person forces the jump to Grow — a 172% subscription increase. Reviewers also describe the apps as required, not optional: Capterra records that across 248 reviews at 65% negative, and transaction fees as the most negative theme at 78% negative across 250 reviews.


Theme code is genuinely yours, but the download excludes your products, collections, menus, pages, blog posts and images, and a Theme Store theme is licensed only to the store you bought it for. The storefront design is portable; the store is not.


**Verdict:** if the catalogue is the business, use Shopify. If the store is one section of a site whose job is to explain, rank and convert, you are buying a commerce platform to solve a website problem.


## 7. Carrd — the answer if one page is genuinely all you need


Carrd's homepage — "simple, free, fully responsive one-page sites" — the only tool here whose marketing admits up front exactly where it stops


Blink


**Price:** Free (3 sites, carrd.co subdomain, "Made with Carrd" footer) · Pro Lite $9/yr · Pro Standard $19/yr (first tier with a custom domain) · Pro Plus $49/yr (first tier with site download). Annual billing only. **Best for:** a link-in-bio, a launch page, an email-capture page — one page, and you know it


A large share of "I need a Squarespace site" briefs are actually one page, and for those Squarespace is overbuying by an order of magnitude. Carrd's most expensive realistic year one is **$64** including a domain, against Squarespace's cheapest at $228 without a second year of domain.


Capterra rates Carrd 4.6 across 28 reviews, and Value for Money is its *highest* sub-score at 4.7, rated by 23 of them. It offers **real source export** — a ZIP of unminified HTML, CSS, JS and images from Pro Plus at $49/yr, which neither Wix nor Squarespace offers at any price.


Read the gates carefully. The **$9 Pro Lite tier removes the branding but still publishes on a carrd.co subdomain** ; the **free plan cannot capture an email address** , because forms are Pro-only; and **export is gated by ladder, not by price** — five Pro Standard tiers costing more than Pro Plus, up to $599, have no export at all.


**Pay more, get less.**


The ceiling is the point. No CMS, no blog, no catalogue, no second page — Capterra's top con is "not suitable for complex or interactive websites". Carrd is run by a single pseudonymous founder with a reported team of about two and no published SLA.


**Verdict:** if one page genuinely is the whole job, this is the cheapest good answer on the internet and Squarespace is a mistake. The moment you need a second page it is not an upgrade — it is a different product.

## Moving off Squarespace without losing the design


You cannot export a Squarespace site, so you rebuild. The point is not to rebuild by hand.


First, the trap that costs money rather than time. **Acuity Scheduling, Email Campaigns, Member Areas, Extensions, Domains and Google Workspace are separate subscriptions** , not parts of the site, and cancelling the site plan does not cancel them. List them before you start, or you will pay for two stacks in month one.


Then: export the XML for what it carries, screenshot the pages you like (style settings and custom CSS do not export, so this is your only record of them), rebuild in Blink by describing the business rather than the old site, and point your domain by CNAME to` cname.blink.new` — root domains need CNAME flattening or ALIAS records, which Cloudflare and Route 53 both support. Keep URL paths matching wherever they were earning traffic; Squarespace forces a` /blog/` prefix on posts.


Cancel last, and check the date first. Annual subscriptions are refundable within 14 days only, monthly plans not at all, and if a free first-year domain is involved and you cancel inside the refund window, the domain cost is deducted from the refund. If the domain is registered at Squarespace, you can transfer it out after unlocking, or leave it there and point the DNS.
