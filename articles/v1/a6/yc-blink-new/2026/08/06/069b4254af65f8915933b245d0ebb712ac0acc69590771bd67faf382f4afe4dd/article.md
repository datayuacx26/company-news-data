# Best WordPress Alternatives: No Plugins, No Patching

## How we checked these numbers


Every price, limit and quota below was read off the vendor's own pricing page or help centre where those render to a fetch, and from dated independent write-ups cross-checked against each other where they do not. Webflow and Squarespace fall in that second group, because neither renders prices to a fetch. The load-bearing figures link to their source. WordPress, Wix, Squarespace and Webflow figures were checked on **14 August 2026** ; Shopify, Framer and Blink on **18 August 2026** . Where a vendor's own pages contradict each other, we say so rather than quoting the flattering one.


We have run no speed tests, Lighthouse audits or migration timings of our own, and none are invented here. Where a performance figure appears it is someone else's published measurement, attributed to them.


One consequence worth stating: you will not find a "WordPress loads in 13.25 seconds" claim on this page. That figure appears on competing articles with no source attached, so we are not repeating it.


## What leaving WordPress actually saves you


Leaving WordPress saves you more in labour than in licence fees. Here are the three costs, and only one of them is money.


**The maintenance cost.** 91% of those 11,334 ecosystem vulnerabilities were in plugins, 17% were rated high severity, and **46% were publicly disclosed while still unpatched** . The practical consequence is not a breach; it is that keeping up is now a standing job. The standard remedy for a broken update — deactivate everything, reactivate one plugin at a time — is a diagnostic burden a hosted platform simply does not impose.


**The real money.** Self-hosted WordPress looks cheap and reprices at renewal. Bluehost's Basic promo runs $2.95/mo and renews near $9.99/mo; SiteGround's StartUp goes from $2.99 to $17.99/mo — a jump of up to 6×. The standard non-developer stack is annually recurring, not one-off: Elementor Pro $59/yr, WP Rocket $59/yr, Wordfence Premium $149/yr — and Wordfence's free tier withholds new threat signatures for roughly 30 days, which is what pushes security-conscious sites onto the paid tier. A realistic first year runs **$51 bare-bones, $229 typical, $462** once you are paying renewal rates with security included.


On the hosted side,[WordPress.com](https://wordpress.com/pricing/) runs Personal $4/mo, Premium $8/mo, Business $25/mo and Commerce $45/mo billed annually. Business is where SFTP, SSH, WP-CLI, staging and database access live — a 6.25× jump from Personal.


**The governance risk, which is new.** The Automattic–WP Engine dispute that began in September 2024 became a federal case; by December 2025 a California judge had **ordered Automattic to restore WP Engine's WordPress.org access within 72 hours** , with trial expected late 2026 or early 2027. Along the way Automattic cut its core contribution from 2,560 to 45 hours per week in January 2025 and **[laid off 16% of staff, roughly 280 people, in April 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/04/02/wordpress-maker-automattic-lays-off-16-of-staff)** . WordPress also recorded **six consecutive months of market-share decline** through May 2026, from 43.20% to 41.90% — the first sustained decline in a decade. Where it went is the useful part: Shopify absorbed roughly 59% of the losses, and Framer, Webflow and Duda took about 16% between them.


Now the credit WordPress has earned, because it is real and it is why the platform is still the default. Ownership is unmatched: GPLv2 software you can self-host means no vendor can lock you out, ever. The plugin directory carries 50,000+ options. And by review volume the praise outweighs any single complaint — 3,172 Capterra reviews on publishing and content management run **95% positive** , inside a 4.6/5 lifetime rating across 14,989 reviews. That score is about the editor, and it is deserved. It is not about what surrounds the editor: the same review corpus runs 74% negative on plugin conflicts and 77% negative on speed. The publisher's complaint was never writing posts.


If ownership is your highest value and you have a developer, stop reading and stay. If you want the outcome without the operations, continue.

## 2. Webflow — the designer's answer, priced per axis


Webflow's homepage, positioning itself as a website experience platform for design and marketing teams


Blink


**Price:** Site Basic $15/mo · Site Premium $25/mo · Workspace Core $19/mo (required for code export) · each additional Full Seat $39/mo — billed annually **Best for:** an agency or in-house team with a dedicated Webflow specialist


Webflow is the most common destination for WordPress agencies, and for a real reason: WebsiteBuilderExpert scored its design capability 4.6/5, and reviewers credit its clean semantic HTML and CSS output for both SEO and long-term maintainability. That is a fair point about hand-built markup, and the right question to ask of any tool. The answer that matters commercially is whether you can inspect and fix what ships: on Blink the sitemap, robots.txt, canonical tags, JSON-LD and Open Graph tags are all controlled in-product, and the SEO & AI Search panel scores your live URL 0–100 across five levers and applies fixes — so the output is auditable rather than taken on trust.


The same review scored its **ease of use 2.1 out of 5, the lowest of any builder they tested** . That is the deliberate trade, and it means Webflow replaces your plugin maintenance job with a specialist headcount.


The billing is the thing WordPress refugees underestimate, because it runs on three axes at once. Site plans are **per website** — an agency with three client sites pays three times. Workspace plans are separate, and code export requires at least Core at $19/mo. Seats are separate again: one Full Seat is included, the next costs $39/mo, so seat spend commonly exceeds plan spend for teams of three or more. Basic at $15/mo has **no CMS at all** , so any content-driven site starts at Premium — and Premium tops out at 20,000 CMS items, with per-item add-ons removed in May 2026, so exceeding that ceiling means jumping to the Team plan at $2,500/mo or to Enterprise. Realistic year one: **$540 low, $1,020 typical, $3,876** for a small team.


Two 2026 changes matter. The May 2026 restructure **cut included bandwidth on the Business path from 100 GB to 50 GB** — bots count toward it, and two consecutive overage months automatically upgrade your plan. And Webflow **discontinued native User Accounts (memberships) on 29 January 2026** , having disabled its Logic automation product in June 2025. Both were capabilities Webflow removed, stranding sites that used them, and gated content now needs Memberstack-class tooling at roughly $25/mo.


On export, be precise: you get static HTML, CSS, JS and assets, but **CMS content, forms, site search, ecommerce and memberships all stop working** . Collection lists render empty and forms submit nowhere. If you left WordPress for portability, read that twice.


**Verdict:** the right move for an agency that employs a Webflow specialist and sells design craft. For everyone else it trades one specialist dependency for another.


## 3. Framer — the gentler design tool, with a genuine export question


Framer's homepage, pitching design-led site building with AI agents on the canvas


Blink


**Price:** Free (500 credits, Framer subdomain, 1 GB bandwidth) · Basic $10/mo · Pro $30/mo, both billed yearly · Enterprise custom **Best for:** a freelance designer who wants Webflow-grade output with a shorter learning curve


Framer is the most credible design-first alternative for someone who found Webflow punishing. It ships server-side rendering and page pre-rendering, auto-generates robots.txt and sitemap.xml on every published site, and supports per-page and per-CMS-page meta titles and descriptions, semantic tags, alt text and redirects. Framer 3.0, published 16 June 2026, added **Framer Agents** — canvas-level AI that generates pages, handles responsive breakpoints, writes code components, generates SEO metadata and audits broken links.


Watch three things.


**Credits are a real ceiling.** Agents run on a shared monthly workspace allowance — 500 on Free, 1,000 on Basic, 3,000 on Pro. Credits reset on the 1st of each calendar month and **do not roll over** . At 100% usage, credit-using features pause.


**Plan gating bites at the SEO layer.** robots.txt is auto-generated but editable only by uploading a custom static file, which is **Pro and Enterprise only** — not Basic, not Free. Site redirects are also a Pro feature. Structured data is manual JSON-LD; there is no generator.


**Export is genuinely unclear, and that is not our opinion.** Two Framer help articles, both updated 7 August 2026, say opposite things. One states flatly: "Framer does not offer HTML export for self-hosting." The other states: "Every Framer site is published as standard HTML, CSS, JavaScript, and static assets. You can download these files at any time." They cross-link to each other. We checked both on 18 August 2026 and are not going to resolve it for them — if portability is why you are leaving WordPress, test this on a trial account before committing.


**Verdict:** a strong pick for a designer delivering marketing sites, with a cheaper entry than Webflow. Not a pick for anyone whose exit plan needs to be certain. See[Blink vs Framer](https://blink.new/vs/framer) .

Wix earns two genuine points. Ease of use is its best-evidenced strength — Capterra's Ease of Use sub-score is[4.5 out of 5 across 10,702 reviewers](https://www.capterra.com/p/169007/Wix/) , its highest of four. If you are leaving WordPress specifically because you disliked being dependent on someone else's stack, moving to the one platform in this comparison with zero export is a strange trade. Cost creep is documented too: custom email is a separate ~$6/mo charge, App Market apps run $3–$20/mo each, the free first-year domain renews near $13.35/yr, and a native mobile app is a separate $99/mo Branded App plan plus $99/yr to Apple and $25 to Google. ## Moving a WordPress site without a rebuild week


Leaving WordPress is the easy migration in this category, because you already own everything. The order is what protects your rankings.


Start with the content: export from **Tools → Export** in wp-admin for a WXR file of posts, pages, categories and tags. Note that WordPress.com's standard export deliberately excludes theme design, customisations, plugins and the media *files* themselves — a full-site move needs a migration plugin such as All-in-One WP Migration, which requires a paid plan.


Then rebuild the front end. In Blink, type` /clone-url` followed by your live WordPress URL. Blink reads the published site and rebuilds it as a starting point, so you are editing a version of your own site rather than starting from a blank prompt.


Then the parts that protect your traffic, in order: keep your URL structure identical wherever pages are already ranking · map redirects for every path you do change · move your media and check the paths resolve · point your domain by CNAME to` cname.blink.new` , with SSL issued automatically within 5–10 minutes of DNS verification · verify the sitemap and robots.txt on the new site before you switch · **then** cancel the hosting.


Do not cancel hosting first. On self-hosted WordPress your site and database live there, and once it lapses, restoring is your problem alone.
