# Best Wix Alternatives: Custom Design Without an Agency

## How we checked these numbers


Every price, limit and quota below was read off the vendor's own pricing page or help centre where those render to a fetch, and from dated independent write-ups cross-checked against each other where they do not. Webflow and Squarespace fall in that second group, because neither renders prices to a fetch. The load-bearing figures link to the page they came from. Wix, Squarespace, Webflow and WordPress figures were checked on **14 August 2026** ; Shopify, Hostinger and Blink on **18 August 2026** . Where a vendor's own pages contradict each other, we say so instead of picking the flattering one — that happened three times, and one of them was us.


We have run no speed tests, Lighthouse audits or migration timings of our own, and none are invented here. Where a performance figure appears, it is someone else's published measurement, attributed to them.


Most articles on this topic quote a "starting price" and stop. The numbers below are year-one totals including the add-ons that are not optional in practice.


## What Wix actually costs, and what it locks


Wix's advertised entry price is not the price of a working business site.


The[published ladder](https://www.websitebuilderexpert.com/website-builders/wix-pricing/) runs Light $17/mo, Core $29/mo, Business $39/mo and Business Elite $159/mo billed annually. The free plan forces Wix ads and a wixsite.com subdomain, with no ecommerce and no analytics.


Then the additions arrive. Custom business email is a separate ~$6/mo Google Workspace charge. App Market apps run $3–$20/mo each. The domain is free for year one, then renews around $13.35/yr. A native mobile app is a separate[$99/mo Branded App plan](https://www.wix.com/app-builder) , plus $99/yr to Apple and $25 one-time to Google.


Twelve months in, the honest range is **$204 bare minimum, about $540 for a typical small business on Core with email and one app, and $3,292** for a business on Business Elite that also wants the mobile app. That is arithmetic over Wix's own list prices.


The structural limits matter more than the money:


- **No export, no self-hosting.** Content exports — products, contacts, orders, blog posts. The site does not. Worth knowing:[Base44, which Wix owns](https://support.wix.com/en/article/exporting-or-embedding-your-wix-site-elsewhere) , *does* offer real code export — ZIP download and GitHub integration — on paid plans. So this is a product decision, not a technical limit.
- **No template switching.** Rebuild from scratch to change the look.
- **CMS ceilings force upgrades** :[1,500 items on Light, 4,000 on Core, 20,000 on Business](https://support.wix.com/en/article/cms-understanding-collection-storage-limits-and-quotas) , with dedicated database infrastructure only at Business Elite.
- **Wix Harmony runs on credits.** Wix's AI builder, launched 21 January 2026, is metered — Core reportedly carries 500 AI credits a month. Run out and you wait for the next cycle or buy a top-up.


Give Wix its due on two real things. It charges **0% platform transaction fees on every plan** across 20+ payment gateways, better than several rivals. And ease of use is its best-evidenced strength: Capterra's Ease of Use sub-score is[4.5 out of 5 across 10,702 reviewers](https://www.capterra.com/p/169007/Wix/) , its highest of four sub-scores.


Neither is why you are leaving. You are leaving because you cannot make it look like you, and you cannot take it with you.

## 2. Squarespace — the best template catalogue, and you are renting the design


Squarespace's homepage, leading with template-driven design and a 14-day trial — there is no permanent free plan


Blink


**Price:** Basic $19/mo · Core $29/mo · Plus $49/mo · Advanced $99/mo, all billed annually. No free plan — a 14-day trial only. **Best for:** a portfolio, restaurant or boutique brand that wants to look excellent immediately and will not restructure later


Nobody has a better catalogue of pre-made templates. Roughly 200 designer-made options let someone with no design skill ship something professional on day one, and Capterra rates it[4.6 out of 5 across 3,415 reviews](https://www.capterra.com/p/143461/Squarespace/reviews/) . Tooltester measured 99.96% uptime in its own monitoring.


That is a different thing from a site designed around your business. The ceiling on a Squarespace site is how close your business happens to sit to one of those 200 templates, and the same opinionated system that makes it look good is the wall you meet: rigid page layouts, block-sizing constraints, only one navigation level below the main menu — and on version 7.1 you **cannot switch templates after launch** .


The export is the harder problem. Squarespace's documentation is explicit that the export is a partial, one-way WordPress-oriented XML file carrying layout pages, *one* blog page and its posts, and text and image blocks. It does **not** carry style settings, custom CSS, store pages, portfolio pages, event pages, index pages, or any blog page beyond the first. Squarespace also states you cannot export from one Squarespace site and import into another.


Costs moved recently, and against customers. On **17 July 2026 Squarespace raised prices by up to 26%** — annual Core from $23 to $29, Plus from $39 to $49 — notified by email with no public memo,[as reported by PetaPixel](https://petapixel.com/2026/07/17/squarespace-is-increasing-prices-by-up-to-26/) . Scheduling (Acuity, $16–$49/mo), Member Areas ($9–$35/mo) and Email Campaigns (from $8/mo) are separate subscriptions, not plan features.


Two limits matter if organic traffic pays your bills: you cannot edit robots.txt or the sitemap, and Tooltester measured Squarespace's mobile page-speed score at 28.94 — last among the twelve builders they tested.


**Verdict:** the right pick if your site is a beautiful brochure that will not change much and you accept you are renting the design. If the site must evolve with the business, you will meet the frame. See[Blink vs Squarespace](https://blink.new/vs/squarespace) .


## 3. Webflow — control you operate yourself, priced per axis


Webflow's homepage, positioning itself as a website experience platform for design and marketing teams rather than solo owners


Blink


**Price:** Site Basic $15/mo · Site Premium $25/mo · Workspace Core $19/mo (required for code export) · each additional Full Seat $39/mo — all billed annually **Best for:** an in-house design team, or an agency with a dedicated Webflow specialist


Webflow gives you the most direct control of any visual tool here — box model, flexbox, breakpoints and z-index, exposed. That is a skill purchase, not a design outcome. WebsiteBuilderExpert scored its design capability 4.6/5 and its **ease of use 2.1 out of 5 — the lowest of any builder they tested** — and explicitly redirects beginners elsewhere.


The pricing catches business owners because it bills on three axes at once. Site plans are **per website** . Workspace plans are separate, and code export requires at least Core at $19/mo. Seats are separate again: one Full Seat included, the next at $39/mo. Basic, at $15/mo, has **no CMS at all** , so any site with a blog starts at Premium.


A realistic year one is **$540 at the low end** (Premium site + Core workspace + domain), about **$1,020 typical** with a second editor and one gap-filler tool, and **$3,876** for a small team with extra seats, bandwidth and third-party tooling.


If you are an agency, run the multiplication before you commit. Ten client sites for a year is 10 × Premium ($300/yr each) = **$3,000** , plus a Core workspace at $228/yr, plus one extra Full Seat at $468/yr — **$3,696 before a single hour of work** , and every new client adds another $300.


Two 2026 changes matter before you commit. The May 2026 restructure **cut included bandwidth on the Business path from 100 GB to 50 GB** , and two consecutive overage months automatically upgrade your plan. And Webflow **discontinued its native User Accounts (memberships) feature on 29 January 2026** , after disabling its Logic automation product in June 2025 — gated content now needs a third-party service such as Memberstack, around $25/mo.


The export is narrower than buyers assume: static HTML, CSS, JS and assets, but no working CMS content, forms, site search, ecommerce or memberships. Collection lists render empty; forms submit nowhere. It is a design artifact, not a running site.


**Verdict:** right when a designer owns the site as their job. For an owner who wants the outcome rather than the craft, the curve and the three-axis bill are the whole story.


## 4. WordPress — maximum ownership, maximum maintenance


WordPress.com's homepage — the managed face of the open-source software that still runs roughly 41.9% of the web


Blink


**Price:** WordPress.com Personal $4/mo · Premium $8/mo · Business $25/mo · Commerce $45/mo, billed annually. Self-hosted WordPress.org is free software plus your own hosting and domain. **Best for:** someone with a developer, who wants nobody to be able to lock them out, ever


On one axis WordPress is unbeatable, and it is worth saying plainly: because the software is GPLv2 and you can self-host it, no vendor can hold your site hostage. You own the files and the database outright. That is why it still runs roughly 41.9% of all websites.


The cost of that ownership is that you become the operations team.


The security surface is the clearest number. Patchstack recorded **[11,334 new vulnerabilities across the WordPress ecosystem in 2025, up 42% year over year](https://patchstack.com/whitepaper/state-of-wordpress-security-in-2026/)** — 91% in plugins, 17% rated high severity, and 46% publicly disclosed while still unpatched. Capterra's review-topic tallies agree: 1,255 reviews mention bugs and plugin conflicts (74% negative), 1,222 mention security (75% negative), 765 mention speed (77% negative).


Pricing is less simple than the sticker, too. Self-hosted looks cheap until renewal: Bluehost's Basic promo runs $2.95/mo and renews near $9.99/mo; SiteGround's StartUp goes from $2.99 to $17.99/mo. The standard plugin stack recurs annually — Elementor Pro $59/yr, WP Rocket $59/yr, Wordfence Premium $149/yr, with Wordfence's free tier withholding new threat signatures for around 30 days. A realistic first year is **$51 bare-bones, $229 typical, $462** at renewal rates with a security plugin.


Credit where due: Automattic **opened plugin installation to every paid plan on 2 April 2026** , including the $4/mo Personal tier, removing a long-standing $252/yr jump.


**Verdict:** correct if ownership beats everything and you have — or are — a developer. If your actual goal is a site that looks good and changes easily, you are buying a maintenance job to get there.


## 5. Shopify — the right answer when the catalogue is the business


Shopify's homepage, built around commerce infrastructure — checkout, POS and fulfilment rather than general-purpose site design


Blink


**Price:** Basic $29/mo · Grow $79/mo · Advanced $299/mo billed yearly ($39/$105/$399 monthly) · Plus from $2,300/mo. No free plan — a 3-day trial, then $1/month for three months. **Best for:** a real product catalogue with inventory, fulfilment and in-person sales


If you are leaving Wix because the store outgrew it, Shopify is the honest answer and we are not going to pretend otherwise. Card rates step down as you climb — 2.9% + 30¢ on Basic, 2.7% on Grow, 2.5% on Advanced, 2.25% on Plus — and third-party gateway fees fall from 2% to 1% to 0.6% to 0.2%. Bandwidth is unlimited on every plan with no overage fees, and SSL is free on any domain you add.


Two things to price in properly. **No domain is bundled** — domains start around $9/yr, and Shopify states outright that "renewal pricing may differ from initial purchase and can change over time," with the renewal figure shown only in-admin. And **annual billing is effectively mandatory** to hit the advertised numbers: $39/$105/$399 monthly against $29/$79/$299 yearly is about a third more.


The portability picture is mixed. Theme code is genuinely editable, with GitHub integration, a CLI and a VS Code extension. But a Shopify theme is Liquid, which needs a Shopify backend to render — the theme is not a site you can host elsewhere, and your products, orders, customers and checkout do not live in it.


**Verdict:** if the catalogue *is* the business, use Shopify. If the store is one section of a site whose job is to explain, rank and convert, you are buying a commerce platform to solve a website problem.


## 6. Hostinger — the cheapest sticker, once you read the billing

Hostinger genuinely is the cheapest entry on this page, and it bundles a custom domain free for the first year. It is also the entry where the headline number needs the most translation.


**That $2.99/mo is not a monthly price.** Hostinger states plainly: "All plans are paid upfront. The monthly rate reflects the total plan price divided by the number of months in your plan." The $2.99 rate is $143.52 charged upfront for 48 months, and it renews at $10.99/mo — **$131.88/yr** thereafter. Unlimited renews at $203.88/yr, Cloud Startup at $311.88/yr. Prices are listed excluding VAT. The bundled .com renews at $19.99/yr after its $0.01 first year, and the free-domain offer excludes monthly plans, upgrades and renewals.


The AI product, Hostinger Horizons, is metered twice over: "AI credits for website and app creation" (5 on Premium, 15 on Unlimited) and a separate pool of "credits for AI agents" (none on Premium, 500 on Unlimited, 1000 on Cloud Startup). Reverting with Undo does not refund consumed credits.


Export deserves a flag. Horizons does offer a code export, but it is **one-way** — Hostinger states "there is no way to import the edited version back to Horizons and continue with prompting." Hostinger's own documentation also contradicts itself on the format, with the export article describing a Node.js/React/Vite project and the technical-specifications article describing a static website. Both were current when we checked on 18 August 2026.


**Verdict:** genuinely the cheapest way to put something simple online, provided you understand you are prepaying four years and that the AI path is one-way. It is a hosting company that added a builder, and it prices like one.

## Moving off Wix without rebuilding it by hand


You cannot export a Wix site, so you rebuild — but you do not rebuild by hand.


Wix gives you no site export, and that means no tool can properly "import" a Wix site. What it does give you is a live, public URL, and that is enough. In Blink, type` /clone-url` followed by your Wix address. Blink reads the live site and rebuilds it as a starting point, so you begin by editing a version of your own site instead of staring at a blank prompt. From there you describe what you always wanted it to be.


Then the mechanical parts, in order: export your Wix **data** — products, contacts, orders, blog posts as XML — since that is what Wix does let you take · rebuild and review on the preview URL · point your domain by CNAME to` cname.blink.new` , with SSL issued automatically within 5–10 minutes of DNS verification · keep URL paths matching the old ones wherever they were earning traffic, and redirect the ones that changed · **then** cancel Wix.


One timing note that costs people money: Wix subscriptions auto-renew, and billing is the most consistent hard complaint in the public record — the Better Business Bureau lists 920 complaints over three years, 416 closed in the last twelve months, with documented disputes of $324, $1,116 and £360. The honest counterweight is that BBB still accredits Wix and rates it A+, so this is a friction pattern rather than a scandal. Check the renewal date before you start, and cancel deliberately.
