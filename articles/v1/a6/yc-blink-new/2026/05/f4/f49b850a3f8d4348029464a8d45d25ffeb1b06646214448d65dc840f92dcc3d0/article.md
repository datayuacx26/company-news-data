# How to Build a Landing Page with AI (That Actually Converts)

A landing page has one job: convert a visitor into a lead, signup, or customer.


Not impress them. Not tell them your whole story. Convert.


The median landing page converts at 6.6% across all industries — based on Unbounce's Q4 2024 analysis of 464 million visits. The top quartile converts at 10–15% or higher. The gap between those two numbers is almost never about design. It's about message match, a clear offer, and a form that actually captures leads.


AI changes how fast you can get to a first version. But most AI landing page tools stop at the frontend. You still have to wire up a database for the form submissions, configure auth if you want a waitlist, and sort out hosting separately. That's the gap this guide closes.

## The 5 elements every high-converting landing page needs


Before you prompt any AI, know what you're building. A landing page that converts has five components. All five must be present.


**1. Hero section.** The first thing a visitor sees. One headline that states the value. One subheadline that explains who it's for. One primary call-to-action button. Nothing else.


**2. Value proposition.** Why this? Why now? Three to five bullets, each one describing an outcome — not a feature. "Save 2 hours per week" beats "time-saving automation."


**3. Social proof.** A testimonial, a logo row, a user count, a G2 rating. Something that tells a first-time visitor someone else already trusted this.


**4. Lead capture form.** The whole point. Name and email for most use cases. Keep it short — every extra field you add drops conversion rates by roughly 11% per field, according to Formstack's form conversion data.


**5. A single CTA repeated.** Top of page, bottom of page, inside the hero. One action, not three.
