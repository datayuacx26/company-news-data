# Build vs Buy Software in 2026: When AI-Built Custom Tools Beat SaaS

## When Buying Still Wins


Be honest about this. Building isn't always right.


**Enterprise compliance requirements.** SOC 2 Type II, HIPAA, PCI-DSS, FedRAMP — mature SaaS vendors have invested years and millions into compliance certifications. Building a custom HIPAA-compliant patient data system from scratch is not an afternoon project. Buy the compliant tool; build around it for workflow customization.


**Network effects and marketplace ecosystems.** Some SaaS tools are valuable because everyone else uses them. Salesforce integrates with 4,000+ tools because it's the standard. A custom CRM doesn't have that ecosystem on day one. If third-party integrations and partner compatibility matter to your business, buy the platform, not the point solution.


**Complex integrations built over years.** Enterprise resource planning, payroll, financial compliance tools — these have integrations, audit trails, and compliance workflows that took years to build. Don't reinvent them. Where building wins is at the edges: the internal tools, dashboards, and workflow apps that sit on top of those systems.


**Your core technical advantage.** If your software product *is* your business, hire engineers and build it properly. A fintech building its own financial infrastructure should invest in engineering. A law firm tracking client matter workflows should consider Blink.


## When Building Wins in 2026


The clearest build cases are internal and workflow-specific tools:


**Internal dashboards and operations tools.** Your team has a Notion doc that gets copy-pasted into a Google Sheet that feeds a Zapier automation every Monday. That's a custom app waiting to exist. Blink can build that entire workflow — database, input forms, automated processing — in one afternoon.


**Custom CRMs and pipeline trackers.** A generic CRM has deal stages designed for generic sales teams. Your sales process isn't generic. The fields matter. The views matter. The automated actions matter. CRMs are one of the most-replaced SaaS categories in 2026. See[how to replace Salesforce with a custom CRM](https://blink.new/blog/replace-salesforce-with-custom-crm) .


**Team-specific workflow apps.** Onboarding trackers. Content approval workflows. Vendor management systems. Anything where your team says "we use \[SaaS tool\], but we really just need it to do these three specific things" — that's a build.


**Single-purpose data tools.** Weekly reporting tools. Client-facing portals. Inventory trackers with your team's specific SKU logic. These are narrow enough that a custom build covers 100% of the use case and nothing more.


For more examples, see[what sales teams build with AI](https://blink.new/blog/what-sales-teams-build-with-ai) and[how to build an internal tool without code](https://blink.new/blog/how-to-build-internal-tool-without-code) .
