Your agent doesn't find it awkward.

## Workflow 3: Meeting Prep Briefs


You have a client call in 45 minutes. When did you last speak? What did they ask for? Are there any open deliverables?


Your agent generates a brief automatically, 30 minutes before every scheduled call: last 3 CRM notes, open action items, recent invoices, any new support tickets they've submitted. Delivered to your Telegram.


This is one of the workflows teams report being most surprised by. It doesn't sound like much until you've had three consecutive calls where you walked in prepared — and realized how often you'd been winging it before.

## Workflow 5: Customer Support Ticket Routing


First response time matters in support. So does getting the ticket to the right person on the first try.


Your agent reads incoming requests — via email, web form, or chat — categorizes them (billing question, technical issue, feature request, general inquiry), and routes them to the right team member with a one-paragraph summary. Simple FAQs it can answer directly from a knowledge base you've set up.


Your team sees pre-triaged tickets. The billing question goes to accounts, not to the developer handling bugs. Nobody reads and re-routes the same email three times.


For a team handling 20+ support requests a week, triage time alone is 2-3 hours. The agent does it in seconds per ticket.


## Workflow 6: Competitor Monitoring


Most small businesses don't have a dedicated person tracking what competitors are doing. They find out late — when a client mentions it, or when the announcement shows up in their LinkedIn feed.


Your agent runs a weekly scan: competitor websites, their pricing pages, social activity, relevant news. It sends a short summary of anything that changed. You define what to watch; it does the checking.


At $22/mo all-in — less than a single hour of employee admin time — this is the cheapest competitive intelligence your business will ever add.


## Workflow 7: New Client Onboarding Checklist


When a new client signs, 8 to 12 things need to happen: welcome email, contract sent, access provisioned, team introduced, kickoff scheduled, billing configured, files shared, internal brief written.


One person usually "owns" this. They usually miss at least two steps.


Your agent triggers an onboarding sequence automatically when a new client is added to your system. It creates a task list, sends Slack assignments to the right team members, fires the welcome email from your templates, and follows up on incomplete steps at 48 hours.


Every client gets the same first impression. Nothing slips through the cracks between teammates.
