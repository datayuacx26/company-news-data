# Delaware C-Corp Tax Requirements: A 2026 Guide

## What Is a Delaware C-Corp?


Every Delaware C-corp annual tax filing obligation traces back to two documents: the certificate of incorporation and Subchapter C of the Internal Revenue Code. The corporation is formed under the Delaware General Corporation Law, Title 8 of the Delaware Code, and taxed as a separate entity paying a flat 21% federal rate under IRC Section 11(b).


Founders pick Delaware for reasons that have little to do with tax. The DGCL is the most heavily litigated corporate statute in the country, the Court of Chancery hears business disputes without a jury, and investors know exactly what they’re buying. Predictability is the product.


Here’s what actually defines the entity:


- **Separate taxpayer.** The corporation pays 21% on its own income. Shareholders pay again on dividends and on gain at exit. That second layer is the double-taxation tradeoff.
- **Continuous registered agent.** Under 8 Del. C. Section 132, the corporation maintains a registered agent with a physical Delaware street address from incorporation until formal dissolution. A P.O. box doesn’t qualify.
- **A specific annual report.** Under 8 Del. C. Section 502, the report lists the registered office, the nature of the business, the principal place of business, every director, at least one officer, and authorized stock detail.
- **Tighter agent rules since 2025.** Delaware Senate Bill 95, generally effective August 1, 2025, ended virtual-office and mail-forwarding-only registered agents and made nature of business a required annual report field.
- **QSBS eligibility.** Domestic C-corp status is a hard prerequisite for the IRC Section 1202 gain exclusion, the biggest planning reason founders accept double taxation.


A Delaware charter is a default, not a requirement. Plenty of healthy companies are LLCs taxed as partnerships filing Form 1065 with Schedules K-1, or S-corps filing Form 1120-S under a 100-shareholder cap and a single-class-of-stock rule that a priced preferred round would break immediately. Delaware LLCs and LPs pay a flat $300 annual tax due June 1 and file no annual report at all. If you’re still weighing the choice, start with[the main types of corporations](https://www.indinero.com/blog/types-of-corporations/) and[how to choose your small business entity](https://www.indinero.com/blog/how-to-choose-your-small-business-entity/) . One note for converters: the QSBS holding period generally starts when C-corp stock is issued, not when the LLC was formed.


## Federal Tax Obligations


Federal C-corp tax obligations start with one return. Form 1120, U.S. Corporation Income Tax Return, is filed every year the corporation exists, including a year with zero revenue and zero activity. There’s no “we had no income so we skipped it” exception.


### Form 1120, deadlines, and the extension that doesn’t extend payment


The[IRS Instructions for Form 1120](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1120) set the deadline at the 15th day of the 4th month after the tax year ends, so April 15 for a calendar-year company. A corporation with a June 30 fiscal year end files by the 15th day of the third month instead, and a short year ending anytime in June is treated as ending June 30.


Form 7004 buys an automatic 6-month extension to October 15. It extends time to file, not time to pay. Tax owed is still due on the original date, with interest and the failure-to-pay addition running from there. The 7-month extension June 30 filers used to receive applies only to tax years beginning before January 1, 2026.


Penalties are where founders get hurt. Late filing runs 5% of unpaid tax per month, capped at 25%, and for a 2026 return filed more than 60 days late the minimum is the smaller of the tax due or $525. Late payment runs 0.5% per month, also capped at 25%. Founders asking how to file 1120 for startup entities with no revenue get the same answer as everyone else. File on time anyway, because the return preserves net operating losses and keeps the R&D credit election alive.


### Estimated tax, and why Form 1120-W is no longer the answer


A corporation pays estimated tax in installments when it expects total tax less credits of $500 or more. Installments fall on the 15th day of the 4th, 6th, 9th, and 12th months, so April 15, June 15, September 15, and December 15 at 25% each for a calendar-year filer.


Now the correction most published checklists still miss. The IRS designated[Form 1120-W and its instructions historical](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/form-1120-w-estimated-tax-for-corporations-and-the-instructions-for-form-1120-w-are-now-historical) after the 2022 revision, and nothing replaced it as a filed form. Corporations now compute estimated tax on the Publication 542 worksheet, keep the workpaper internally, and reconcile any shortfall on Form 2220.


The obligation under IRC Section 6655 is fully intact. The underpayment addition applies unless the corporation paid at least the lesser of 100% of current-year tax or 100% of prior-year tax, and that safe harbor isn’t available to a large corporation or to one with no prior-year liability. Workpapers that still reference a Form 1120-W filing need a refresh alongside the rest of your[year-round tax planning](https://www.indinero.com/blog/small-business-tax-planning/) .


### Payroll and information returns


These filings quietly generate the most penalty notices at early-stage companies, because they’re frequent and calendar-driven rather than annual and accountant-driven.


- **Form 941** , quarterly federal payroll, due April 30, July 31, October 31, and January 31.
- **Form 940** , the annual FUTA return, due January 31.
- **Forms W-2 and W-3** , due to employees and the Social Security Administration by January 31.
- **Form 1099-NEC** , due to recipients and the IRS by January 31.
- **Form 3921** , filed per incentive stock option exercise. Employee statement January 31, IRS copy February 28 on paper or March 31 electronically.


The e-file threshold is 10 information returns in aggregate, not 10 of any single type. W-2s, 1099s, and 3921s are added together. Penalties for returns required in 2026 run $60 each if corrected within 30 days, $130 if corrected by August 1, and $340 after that.


### International reporting


Form 5472 applies when a domestic corporation is at least 25% foreign-owned and had a reportable transaction with a related party. The penalty under IRC Section 6038A(d)(1) is $25,000 per form per year, plus another $25,000 for each 30-day period the failure continues after IRS notice, and a substantially incomplete form counts as a failure to file. This catches more Delaware startups than founders expect, because one foreign-parent capital contribution is a reportable transaction.


Form 5471 applies when the corporation owns or controls a foreign subsidiary. The base penalty under IRC Section 6038(b) is $10,000 per foreign corporation per year, with continuation penalties up to $50,000 more. Form 8938 can pull a closely held corporation with substantially passive assets into FATCA asset reporting.


### Beneficial ownership reporting is off the calendar


Delete the BOI filing from your 2026 list if your corporation was formed in the United States. On August 11, 2026, FinCEN issued a final rule that[permanently removes beneficial ownership reporting](https://www.fincen.gov/news/news-releases/fincen-permanently-ends-beneficial-ownership-reporting-requirements-millions) for US companies and US persons under the Corporate Transparency Act. It makes permanent the March 26, 2025 interim rule narrowing “reporting company” to foreign-formed entities registered in a US state, and FinCEN said it will delete previously reported US-person information. Foreign entities registered here still report for foreign individual beneficial owners, but not for US-person owners or company applicants.

## State Tax Obligations


The state layer of Delaware C-corp tax requirements is where the confusion lives. Incorporating in Delaware doesn’t create a Delaware income tax return. It creates a Delaware franchise tax bill, and those are different taxes.


### Delaware franchise tax and the March 1 annual report


Every Delaware corporation owes the annual report and franchise tax by March 1, regardless of fiscal year end, revenue, or whether it ever did a dollar of business in the state. The report fee for a non-exempt domestic corporation is $50. Delaware allows two calculation methods and lets you pay the lower of the two, per the[Delaware Division of Corporations franchise tax guidance](https://corp.delaware.gov/frtaxcalc/) .


- **Authorized Shares Method.** The default. It looks only at authorized shares in the certificate of incorporation, ignoring issued shares and company assets entirely. Minimum $175.
- **Assumed Par Value Capital Method.** Uses total gross assets from Form 1120 Schedule L with total issued shares, at $400 per million of assumed par value capital. Minimum $400.


Maximum tax under either method is $200,000, rising to $250,000 for a large corporate filer, a designation that under 8 Del. C. Section 503 requires exchange-listed stock and consolidated revenue and assets in the hundreds of millions. It effectively never touches a private company.


The Delaware franchise tax startup founders actually owe usually bears no resemblance to the number on the notice. A typical venture-backed company authorizes 10,000,000 or more shares at $0.00001 par to make room for an option pool. Under the Authorized Shares Method, that share count produces a five-figure bill. Under Assumed Par Value Capital, the same asset-light company frequently lands at or near the $400 floor. Delaware mails the notice using the higher default figure, and nothing on it says in plain language that you can recalculate.


Delaware bills the wrong number first.


Corporations owing $5,000 or more prepay at 40% by June 1, 20% by September 1, 20% by December 1, and the balance by March 1. Miss March 1 and you get a $200 penalty plus 1.5% interest per month under 8 Del. C. Sections 502 and 504, and no certificate of good standing until it’s cured. After a year of nonpayment, Section 510 voids the charter. Delaware sends a warning notice by November 30, and revival costs $169 plus all back tax, penalty, and interest. For the plain-English version, see[what a franchise tax actually is](https://www.indinero.com/faq/what-is-franchise-tax/) and our[Delaware tax rate reference](https://www.indinero.com/state-tax-guides/delaware-tax-rates/) .


### The Delaware income tax return most Delaware corporations never file


Delaware’s corporate income tax rate is 8.7% on federal taxable income allocated and apportioned to Delaware. The return is now Form CIT-TAX, the modernized replacement for the long-familiar Form 1100, due April 15 for calendar-year filers. A federal extension automatically extends the Delaware deadline, and estimated tax is prepaid at 50%, 20%, 20%, and 10%.


Here’s the part almost everyone gets wrong. A corporation that maintains a statutory corporate office in Delaware but doesn’t actually do business there is exempt from Delaware corporate income tax and from filing the return, and it doesn’t have to establish that exemption through an information return. The citation is 30 Del. C. Section 1902(b)(6), in[Title 30, Chapter 19 of the Delaware Code](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title30/c019/index.html) . Section 1902(b)(8) is a different exemption covering corporations whose Delaware activity is confined to managing intangible investments, and those corporations do file, via an Annual Information Return under Section 1904(f). Plenty of published guides cite the wrong subsection.


If you genuinely operate in Delaware, two more obligations attach. Delaware has no sales tax but does impose a gross receipts tax, with rates running roughly 0.0945% to 1.9914% by activity. Conducting a trade or business in the state also requires a Delaware business license, generally about $75 per year for a first location.


### Where you actually owe tax


Delaware incorporation says nothing about where the company owes tax. Nexus does. Incorporating in Delaware isn’t a tax strategy. It’s a corporate-law choice with a March 1 bill attached.


- **Corporate income tax.** You file wherever nexus exists, generally created by employees, offices, property, or economic thresholds. Over 30 states use single sales factor apportionment with market-based sourcing, assigning revenue to the customer’s location rather than to where engineering sits. A fully remote 20-person company can generate a wider filing footprint than a 200-person company with one office. Our guide to[startup tax nexus](https://www.indinero.com/blog/startup-tax-nexus/) covers the triggers.
- **P.L. 86-272 won’t save a SaaS company.** The shield covers soliciting orders for tangible personal property, and software delivered over the internet generally isn’t that. The Multistate Tax Commission’s revised Statement of Information also treats common website and app interactions as unprotected.
- **Sales tax.** After South Dakota v. Wayfair, economic nexus is the norm. As of January 1, 2026, 16 states have eliminated the 200-transaction threshold, and Kentucky drops its transaction test on August 1, 2026. Product taxability is a separate question from nexus, which is why[SaaS sales tax](https://www.indinero.com/blog/saas-sales-tax/) is a two-step analysis.
- **Payroll registration.** One remote employee generally means registering for state withholding and unemployment insurance in that employee’s state, often within a short window after the first payroll.

## Entity-Specific Considerations


C-corp status carries planning consequences pass-through entities don’t have. Some are worth real money. Some evaporate quietly when the paperwork isn’t there.


### QSBS under Section 1202, now two regimes at once


The One Big Beautiful Bill Act, P.L. 119-21, enacted July 4, 2025, rewrote Section 1202 through Section 70431. Two regimes now run side by side, and which applies depends on when the stock was issued. Most cap tables have both.


- **Stock issued on or before July 4, 2025.** Full 100% exclusion only after a 5-year hold. Per-issuer, per-taxpayer cap of the greater of $10,000,000 or 10 times aggregate adjusted basis. Aggregate gross assets could not exceed $50,000,000 before and immediately after issuance.
- **Stock issued after July 4, 2025.** Tiered exclusion by holding period, 50% at 3 years, 75% at 4 years, 100% at 5 years. Cap raised to the greater of $15,000,000 or 10 times basis. Gross asset ceiling raised to $75,000,000. Both indexed for inflation for tax years beginning after 2026.


The rest of the machinery is unchanged, and it’s where deals break. The issuer must be a domestic C corporation at issuance and generally throughout. Stock must come at original issuance directly from the corporation, so secondary purchases don’t qualify. At least 80% of assets by value must be used in an active qualified trade or business throughout, a continuing test a company sitting on an oversized round can fail. Excluded fields under Section 1202(e)(3) sweep in consulting, financial services, health, and law. Redemptions under Section 1202(c)(3) are the silent killer, because routine repurchases from departing employees have tainted whole tranches. Verify the text at[26 U.S.C. Section 1202](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/1202) and read our[QSBS exemption overview](https://www.indinero.com/blog/qsbs-exemption/) .


### Section 174A and the R&D credit


Skip the old five-year domestic amortization rule. New IRC Section 174A permanently allows full deduction of domestic research or experimental expenditures in the year paid or incurred, effective for tax years beginning after December 31, 2024, with an election to capitalize over a minimum 60 months. Foreign R&E stays capitalized over 15 years. Rev. Proc. 2025-28 is the implementation guidance, and any 2026 workpaper still amortizing domestic R&E over five years is applying repealed law.


The credit under IRC Section 41 is separate from the deduction. A pre-revenue Delaware C-corp with no income tax liability can still turn it into cash. A qualified small business under Section 41(h)(3) may elect on Form 6765, Section D to apply up to $500,000 of credit against payroll taxes, then claim it quarterly on Form 8974. The election must ride on an originally filed, timely return including extensions, which is why a late Form 1120 costs real cash and not just penalties. Here’s how the[R&D credit works against payroll taxes](https://www.indinero.com/blog/rd-tax-credit-against-payroll-taxes/) .


### Equity, 409A, and the grant that ages badly


A C-corp granting options needs a defensible common stock fair market value. Treas. Reg. Section 1.409A-1(b)(5)(iv)(B) provides a rebuttable presumption of reasonableness through three safe harbors, the common one being an independent appraisal dated no more than 12 months before the grant by an appraiser with significant relevant experience. The presumption shifts the burden to the IRS.


Price grants off a stale valuation and the holder can face Section 409A income inclusion plus a 20% additional tax, while the company carries an ASC 718 expense that won’t survive audit. The clock resets on material events, not just the calendar. Indinero’s[409A valuation services](https://www.indinero.com/services/409a-valuation-services/) sit inside the same engagement as the tax work, so the grant price and the tax position come from the same numbers.


### Double taxation and fundraising flexibility


The 21% entity-level tax plus shareholder tax on distributions is the cost of the structure. Most venture-backed companies never feel it directly, because they don’t distribute earnings. What they get in exchange is the ability to issue multiple classes of stock, admit entity and non-US investors, and hold capital from funds with tax-exempt limited partners that won’t touch pass-through equity. An S-corp can’t do any of that without terminating its election, and it gets no QSBS. A multi-entity group with a Delaware parent also chooses between a consolidated Form 1120 and separate returns, and that choice ripples into apportionment in combined-reporting states.


## Common Pitfalls


Most of these are calendar and documentation failures, not judgment failures. That’s what makes them avoidable.


1. **Paying the Authorized Shares default without recalculating.** Delaware’s notice uses the default method, so a company with 10,000,000 authorized shares that never recalculates under Assumed Par Value Capital can pay five figures instead of a few hundred dollars. The recalculation is free and takes minutes. It just has to happen before March 1.
2. **Assuming Delaware incorporation removes the home-state filing obligation.** Incorporating in Delaware creates a franchise tax obligation, not an income tax shelter. You still file corporate income tax wherever nexus exists, register payroll wherever employees live, and collect sales tax wherever economic thresholds are met. California-based Delaware corporations get caught here constantly, which is why we wrote about[Delaware startups and California compliance](https://www.indinero.com/blog/delaware-startups-california-compliance/) .
3. **Still filing Form 1120-W, or still budgeting for a BOI report.** Both are gone. Form 1120-W has been historical since the 2022 revision, replaced by the Publication 542 worksheet and Form 2220, and BOI reporting for US-formed entities ended permanently with FinCEN’s August 11, 2026 final rule.
4. **Missing the R&D credit or mishandling Section 174A.** The payroll offset election has to be on a timely filed original return, so a late Form 1120 can wipe out up to $500,000 of usable cash benefit for that year. Domestic research is immediately deductible under Section 174A, and amortizing it over five years overstates taxable income.
5. **A stale 409A.** More than 12 months old, or issued before a priced round, a large secondary, or a material business change. Every grant priced off it inherits the problem, and it surfaces during diligence when it’s most expensive to fix.
6. **Missing QSBS issuance documentation.** You need gross-asset values at each issuance date, active-business test support, original-issuance records, and a clean redemption log. Nobody reconstructs that seven years later. Without the paper, the exclusion is a claim rather than a position.
7. **Treating the registered agent as a formality.** Delaware SB 95, effective August 1, 2025, ended virtual-office and mail-forwarding-only agent arrangements and barred listing the agent’s address as the principal place of business unless the corporation genuinely maintains it there.
8. **Letting the charter go void.** One year of nonpayment or an incomplete annual report voids the charter under 8 Del. C. Section 510. Revival costs $169 plus all back taxes, penalties, and interest, and until it’s cured there’s no certificate of good standing. A void charter can freeze a wire from a fund.

## Frequently asked questions


Short answers to what founders and finance leads ask most often about Delaware C-corp filings, deadlines, and penalties. For anything specific to your cap table or your state footprint, talk to a tax advisor who can see the whole picture.


### What annual tax filings does a Delaware C-Corp need to make?


A Delaware C-corp files Form 1120, quarterly estimated tax installments, payroll and information returns, and the Delaware annual report with franchise tax by March 1. Add Form 7004 if you extend, and Forms 5472, 5471, or 8938 where foreign ownership or foreign assets apply. Form 1120-W is obsolete, so estimated tax now runs off the Publication 542 worksheet and reconciles on Form 2220. At indinero, the team that closes your books files all of it inside one monthly engagement.


### When is the Delaware franchise tax due and how is it calculated?


Delaware franchise tax is due March 1 each year for the prior calendar year, along with the annual report and its $50 fee. Delaware allows two calculation methods and you pay the lower. Authorized Shares starts at $175, and Assumed Par Value Capital runs $400 per $1,000,000 of assumed par value capital with a $400 minimum. Either method caps at $200,000. Corporations owing $5,000 or more prepay in installments, and indinero runs that recalculation off the gross assets already on your balance sheet.


### What is the difference between Authorized Shares Method and Assumed Par Value Capital Method?


Delaware’s Authorized Shares Method taxes authorized share count alone, while the Assumed Par Value Capital Method uses gross assets and issued shares. Authorized Shares runs $175 for 5,000 shares or fewer, $250 up to 10,000, then $85 per additional 10,000 or fraction. Assumed Par Value Capital is $400 per $1,000,000 of assumed par value capital or fraction, minimum $400. Delaware bills the Authorized Shares figure by default, because authorized shares are the only input it holds. Indinero runs both methods off your closed books before the deadline.


### When does Form 1120 need to be filed?


Form 1120 is due the 15th day of the 4th month after the tax year ends, so April 15 for calendar-year corporations. Form 7004 buys an automatic six-month extension to October 15, but it extends time to file, not time to pay. Interest and the failure-to-pay addition run from the original due date on any unpaid tax. The R&D payroll offset election only rides on a timely filed return, which is why indinero keeps the close on schedule.


### What multi-state filings apply if the company operates outside Delaware?


A Delaware C-corp operating elsewhere owes corporate income tax where it has nexus, sales tax after Wayfair, and payroll registration in every employee’s state. Most states now apportion on a single sales factor, so revenue follows the customer’s location rather than where your engineers sit. A Delaware charter alone usually creates no Delaware corporate income tax return under 30 Del. C. Section 1902(b)(6), though foreign qualification applies in states where you actually operate. Indinero tracks that footprint inside one monthly engagement as headcount moves.


### How does QSBS planning work for a Delaware C-Corp?


QSBS planning for a Delaware C-corp now runs on two regimes, and the stock’s issuance date decides which one applies. Stock issued on or before July 4, 2025 keeps the five-year hold, the $10,000,000 cap, and the $50,000,000 gross-assets ceiling. Stock issued after that date gets 50% at three years, 75% at four, 100% at five, a $15,000,000 cap, and a $75,000,000 ceiling. Most cap tables hold both, so indinero captures gross-asset values at each issuance date while the books are still current.


### What happens if I miss the Delaware franchise tax deadline?


Missing the March 1 Delaware franchise tax deadline triggers a $200 penalty plus 1.5% interest per month on the unpaid tax and penalty. You also lose the Certificate of Good Standing, which stalls a financing, an acquisition, or a bank facility. Neglect it for a year and 8 Del. C. Section 510 lets Delaware void the charter and declare corporate powers inoperative. Revival under 8 Del. C. Section 312 requires all back taxes, penalties, and interest.
