# Bench Accounting Alternatives in 2026: Where to Go Next

## What happened to Bench, and why migration is urgent


Bench Accounting, founded in 2012 and once self-described as North America’s largest bookkeeping service for small businesses, abruptly ceased operations on Friday, December 27, 2024, locking roughly 12,000 customers out of their books days before tax season. The shutdown carried no advance notice. Three days later, on December 30, 2024,[Employer.com announced its acquisition of Bench out of insolvency](https://techcrunch.com/2024/12/30/bench-to-be-acquired-after-abruptly-shutting-down/) , a deal stitched together by a company whose CEO had only acquired the` employer.com` domain weeks earlier for around $450,000.


The financial picture behind the collapse was severe. On January 7, 2025, Bench Accounting and its subsidiary 10Sheet Services Inc. filed an assignment in bankruptcy in Canada. Filings revealed $2.8 million in cash against $65.4 million in liabilities, with $51 million owed to the National Bank of Canada, more than 85% of which was unsecured.


On August 11, 2025, Employer.com officially rebranded Bench as part of[Mainstreet](https://www.mainstreet.com/rebrand) , folding it together with two other recent acquisitions. The Bench-branded site now redirects most flows under Mainstreet’s umbrella. As of May 2026,[Bench Accounting holds a D- rating from the Better Business Bureau](https://www.bbb.org/ca/bc/vancouver/profile/bookkeeping-services/bench-accounting-0037-1279597) and is not BBB-accredited, with recurring 2026 complaints citing late tax filings, missed-deadline penalties, unresponsive support, and forced tier upgrades.


Why migration is urgent. Any business still on the platform is now relying on a brand that has been shut down once, sold once, and rebranded once, all inside nine months. Tax season 2026 filings, year-end 2025 closings, R&D credit claims, and audit prep all depend on a provider that may not be the same entity six months from now. Indinero has maintained continuous operations since 2009 with stable ownership and a 5-star Clutch rating, the kind of operational stability that matters most when your books underwrite every other decision your business makes.


## What Bench customers actually need in a replacement


Bench’s value proposition was simple: hand off bookkeeping to a real human team and stop thinking about it. The replacement must match that simplicity while fixing the structural problems Bench customers are migrating away from.


**Data portability.** Bench used a proprietary bookkeeping platform. Customers can’t export a clean QuickBooks Online or Xero file without manual rebuilding. Pilot publicly built a free Bench-to-QuickBooks migration service to capture stranded customers, which underscores how non-portable the data is. Any replacement should put your books inside a tool you own, QuickBooks Online, Xero, or NetSuite, so a future provider change is a vendor swap, not a data-recovery project.


**GAAP-readiness.** Bench was historically cash-basis-only. Growing companies above roughly $1M in revenue, and any company headed for an audit or institutional financing, need accrual-basis GAAP books. The migration is the right moment to convert. The[IRS Publication 538 guidance on accounting methods](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p538) is the technical reference.


**Catch-up bookkeeping.** Many Bench customers experienced months of missed or delayed reconciliations across the shutdown and the post-acquisition transition. A replacement provider should price catch-up cleanly, not bury it inside an ambiguous onboarding fee.


**Tax filing continuity.** Bench’s combined bookkeeping plus tax offering was its most cited feature. A replacement must either include tax filing or partner cleanly with a tax provider, especially mid-year when partial-year filings, extension management, and R&D credit windows are all live.


**Multi-state nexus exposure.** Companies that grew on Bench may have crossed economic-nexus thresholds in additional states post- *Wayfair* without registering or filing. Any catch-up engagement needs a nexus exposure check baked in. For background, see[Indinero’s sales tax expertise](https://www.indinero.com/blog/sales-tax-experts/) .


**R&D credit capture.** Under the post-2022 Inflation Reduction Act rules, qualified small businesses can now offset up to $500,000 per year in payroll taxes using the Section 41 R&D credit on Form 6765, doubled from the prior $250,000 cap. Companies that skipped the credit while on Bench may be able to claim retrospectively.


**Operational stability of the new provider.** The whole point of migration is to land somewhere that will still exist in 24 months. Track record, ownership stability, and provider scale all matter more now than they did before December 2024.

## A clean migration checklist from Bench


A 30/60/90-day migration from Bench, framed as the sequence any new provider should be willing to walk with you. None of this is exotic, it’s the discipline most ex-Bench customers wish their old provider had brought.


**Week 1: Recover and inventory.**
1. Log into the Bench platform (now under Mainstreet) and export every available report, profit and loss statements, balance sheets, general ledger details, transaction registers, payroll exports if applicable.
2. Save copies of any tax returns Bench prepared.
3. Inventory which months are reconciled vs unreconciled. Note any open items.
4. Pull historical bank, credit card, and merchant processor statements directly from the source institutions for at least the prior 24 months as a backstop.


**Weeks 2 to 4: Provider selection and onboarding.**
1. Shortlist providers against the cleavage lines in the prior section.
2. Run a fit conversation with two or three providers. Ask each: pricing transparency, GAAP posture, data portability, multi-state handling, R&D credit workflow, and how they handle catch-up work.
3. Make sure the new provider will own the migration, not just receive the export.


**Days 30 to 60: Catch-up and conversion.**
1. The new provider rebuilds books inside QuickBooks Online, Xero, or NetSuite, avoid any provider that wants you on a proprietary platform.
2. If Bench was cash-basis and the new engagement is accrual, schedule the cash-to-accrual conversion explicitly. Per the[AICPA’s audit and accounting guidance](https://www.aicpa-cima.com/topic/audit-assurance) , this conversion requires recognition of accruals, deferrals, prepayments, and inventory adjustments at the conversion date.
3. Reconcile every account against the source-institution statements pulled in Week 1.
4. Identify any missed tax filings, federal, state income, state sales, and payroll. File extensions where necessary.


**Days 60 to 90: Forward-state hygiene.**
1. Confirm the monthly close cadence with the new provider. A reasonable mid-market expectation is books closed within 10 to 15 business days of month-end.
2. Verify state nexus posture, especially for any state where you crossed the post- *Wayfair* economic-nexus threshold while on Bench.
3. If R&D-eligible, capture any prior-year credit you can claim via amended return.
4. Lock the new tax-prep workflow for the current tax year and confirm responsibility for federal and state filings.


The faster you move through Week 1, the more options you keep. The Mainstreet portal export window will not stay open forever.
