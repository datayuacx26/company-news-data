# 409A Valuation Cost in 2026: What to Expect

## What Is a 409A Valuation?


A 409A valuation is an independent appraisal of a private company’s common-stock fair market value under IRC Section 409A, used to set option strike prices. The name comes from the statute itself.[IRC Section 409A](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/409A) was enacted in 2004 through the American Jobs Creation Act to govern nonqualified deferred compensation.


Options granted at or above fair market value (FMV) are exempt from Section 409A. Options granted below FMV without a defensible valuation are treated as deferred compensation and lose that exemption. The consequences fall on the employee, not the company. The discounted spread becomes taxable income, plus interest at the IRS underpayment rate plus one point, plus a 20 percent federal penalty. That’s why a defensible 409A is a hiring and retention issue, not just a tax formality.


A few elements define a compliant report:


- **Independent appraiser.** A qualified, arms-length preparer, not an internal estimate.
- **Written report.** Documented methodology and conclusions, dated to the grant.
- **12-month shelf life.** Valid for a year or until a material event, whichever comes first.
- **Safe harbor path.** Structured to earn the IRS presumption of reasonableness.
- **ASC 718 downstream.** The same FMV feeds your stock-comp expense.


The 409A number is not the price investors pay for preferred stock. Common stock sits behind preferred in the liquidation stack, so its FMV is usually a fraction of the latest preferred price per share. For the full context, see our[essential guide to 409A valuations](https://www.indinero.com/resources/the-essential-guide-for-409a-valuations/) .


## Who Needs a 409A


Any private company that grants stock options, restricted stock, or other equity compensation needs a 409A valuation before its first grant. This is broader than the common assumption. It isn’t only VC-backed Delaware C-Corps.


Bootstrapped companies, PE-backed businesses, LLCs issuing profits interests, S-Corps with equity-linked awards, and multi-entity growth companies all fall inside the requirement once they issue equity to employees or contractors. Founders typically commission their first 409A ahead of an initial option pool or a priced round. Controllers own the refresh cadence after that.


The trigger is grant-driven, not stage-driven. If you’re issuing equity, you need a current valuation on file the day the board approves the grant. Common moments that call for one:


- **First option grant** to employees or contractors.
- **A priced funding round** that creates a new preferred class.
- **M&A discussions** or a signed letter of intent.
- **IPO preparation** and pre-offering diligence.


Skip it and the penalty is steep. Under Treasury regulation, a below-FMV grant without a defensible valuation triggers immediate income tax on the option spread, a 20 percent additional tax, and interest on the underpayment, all landing on the option holder. Because option grants also carry their own tax elections, it’s worth reading how the[83(b) election works for startup equity](https://www.indinero.com/blog/83b-startup-taxes/) alongside your first 409A.


## The Safe Harbor Framework


The safe harbor is a rebuttable presumption of reasonableness under Treasury Regulation 1.409A-1(b)(5)(iv)(B) that shifts the burden of proof to the IRS. When you set FMV with a qualifying method, the IRS can only overturn it by proving the method or its application was grossly unreasonable. That protection is what you’re actually paying for.


The regulation recognizes three qualifying paths:


1. **Independent appraisal.** A written report by a qualified appraiser, dated within 12 months of the grant. This is the standard path for VC-backed and growth-stage companies, and it’s what nearly every provider in the cost table below delivers.
2. **Illiquid startup formula method.** A predetermined non-lapse-restriction formula applied consistently to every transfer. Rarely used in practice.
3. **Written valuation by a qualified individual.** Available to companies under 10 years old with no readily tradable stock and no near-term liquidity event, where the preparer has at least five years of relevant valuation experience.


Here’s the pricing insight that matters most. A more expensive 409A does not produce a better safe harbor outcome. The presumption is binary. Either the report meets the qualified-appraiser and written-report tests inside the 12-month window, or it doesn’t. A $2,000 platform report and a $15,000 Big 4 report clear the identical legal bar. What the higher price buys is deeper documentation that withstands a Big 4 audit, not a stronger statutory presumption. The bar is binary. For the qualifying-method language, see the[full text of Treasury Reg 1.409A-1](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/26/1.409A-1) , and for a plain-English breakdown, our[409A essentials primer](https://www.indinero.com/blog/irs-409a/) .


## The Valuation Methodology


A 409A analyst first estimates total company value, then allocates it across the cap table to isolate common-stock fair market value. Methodology sits at the center of what drives cost, because more complex cap tables demand more complex allocation work.


Three approaches establish total value. The **income approach** discounts projected cash flows. The **market approach** benchmarks comparable public companies and transactions. The **asset approach** uses net asset value, and is rarely used for a going concern.


Three allocation methods then split that value across share classes:


- **Option Pricing Method (OPM).** Treats each equity class as a call option on total equity value, priced with a Black-Scholes model. Per the AICPA’s[valuation guide for equity issued as compensation](https://www.aicpa-cima.com/cpe-learning/publication/valuation-of-privately-held-company-equity-securities-issued-as-compensation-accounting-and-valuation-guide-OPL) , OPM is the most common method for growth-stage SaaS with a single common class.
- **Probability-Weighted Expected Return Method (PWERM).** Models discrete exit scenarios such as an IPO or acquisition, weighted by probability. Fits when a near-term liquidity event is defined.
- **Hybrid method.** Combines OPM and PWERM for companies with both a defined exit path and ongoing uncertainty.


The practical takeaway is about cost. A single-common-class seed company priced with a straight OPM is cheap to value. A Series C company with five preferred classes, participating preferred, ratchets, and a defined IPO scenario needs a hybrid model and far more analyst hours. Same statute, very different price.

## When to Refresh


A 409A is valid for 12 months under the safe harbor, or until a material event, whichever comes first. Granting options on a stale 409A after a material event is the single most common compliance failure, and it reopens the full Section 409A penalty exposure described above.


Material events that trigger an early refresh include:


1. A priced funding round that creates a new preferred class.
2. A secondary tender offer.
3. An M&A discussion or a signed letter of intent.
4. A major change to the business model.
5. A material change in financial condition, such as a large revenue swing or a lost anchor customer.
6. Active IPO preparation.


SAFE and convertible-note rounds usually don’t trigger a refresh on their own, because they don’t create a new priced preferred class until they convert. The refresh cadence is a recurring cost line, not a one-time purchase. Most active companies refresh annually at minimum, and often two to three times a year around funding events. Refresh before, not after.

## The 409A and ASC 718 Connection


The 409A fair market value feeds directly into ASC 718, the FASB standard governing stock-based compensation expense. Under[ASC 718](https://storage.fasb.org/ASU_2021-07.pdf) , equity awards are measured at grant-date fair value and expensed over the requisite service period. The 409A common-stock FMV becomes the strike-price input and a core anchor for the Black-Scholes fair-value calculation.


The other Black-Scholes inputs follow from there. Expected term (the SAB 107 simplified method uses roughly 6.25 years for standard options), expected volatility (derived from a public-company peer set), the risk-free rate (a Treasury rate matching the expected term), and a dividend yield (near zero for growth-stage companies).


This is the coordination point that makes the bundled model efficient. The 409A produces the FMV, and the same peer set and cap-table facts drive the ASC 718 expense schedule. Buy them separately and you pay to build the same peer analysis twice. The expense also flows through your P&L, so it touches EBITDA, board reporting, and the wage portion of your R&D credit.
