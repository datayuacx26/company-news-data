# Powering the Future: The Growth of New Energy Storage

Sustainable energy sources are increasingly meeting energy demands. In the United States, for example, renewable energy use[grew by 8.8%](https://www.eia.gov/electricity/monthly/epm_table_grapher.php?t=table_es1a) from 2024 to 2025 and now generates[almost a quarter](https://www.statista.com/statistics/183396/proportion-of-renewables-in-us-electricity-generation-since-2000/) of the country’s electricity.


Further scaling new energy sources, such as solar, wind, and wave power, requires investing in battery energy storage systems (BESS). These units can store energy and discharge electricity as needed, providing a reliable supply for widespread use.


Adhesives bond, cool, and insulate BESS components, helping ensure the systems produce power efficiently over their lifespan. In the battery management systems (BMS) that monitor and control battery cell use, adhesives provide similar, critical functions. As BESS design evolves to meet new energy growth, the industry will need innovative adhesive solutions to meet new requirements.


### The World Needs Smarter, Safer Energy Storage Solutions


According to the International Energy Agency, renewable energy sources are on track to meet[nearly half of global energy](https://www.iea.org/news/massive-global-growth-of-renewables-to-2030-is-set-to-match-entire-power-capacity-of-major-economies-today-moving-world-closer-to-tripling-goal) demand by 2030. Global energy storage capacity is expected to[reach the terawatt-hour mark](https://about.bnef.com/insights/clean-energy/global-energy-storage-market-set-to-hit-one-terawatt-hour-by-2030/) in the coming years—a benchmark for describing massive amounts of energy.


Energy storage solutions are essential for scaling new energy use because renewable energy sources' production rates can vary.


“Solar and wind power are not always ‘on,’” explains Florian Schloegl, Business Manager ePower/Storage and Clean Energy at H.B. Fuller. “Battery energy storage systems can be used to help with supply and demand. Excess energy is stored and then discharged when production is low or when demand increases.”


BESS support new energy growth through:


- **Peak shaving:** BESS can discharge energy during high periods of demand so that customers do not need to draw from the grid during that time. This reduces the strain on the grid.
- **Grid stabilization:** Storing energy in BESS helps ensure electricity is available in times of peak demand.
- **Decentralization:** Drawing power from multiple sources decreases a community’s dependence on a single plant. If a local power plant has an emergency shutdown, customers with a grid that also draws from BESS will still have electricity to power essential items, such as medical devices.
- **Optimizing upcoming projects:** Large clean energy projects currently in process will require BESS to make the most of all the energy they produce.


### Inside Today’s Battery Energy Storage Systems


A BESS unit typically includes battery modules, a BMS, and an inverter that converts direct current to alternating current to enable the transition of the energy from batteries into the grid or vice versa. All these components are housed in a single structure, typically similar to a storage container. The structure's size, number of batteries, and battery arrangement vary depending on its use.


Although BESS designs vary, all systems[rely on adhesives and sealants](https://www.hbfuller.com/en/blog/thegluetalkblog/2025/october/the-role-of-adhesives-in-next-gen-renewable-energy-storage-solutions) . Adhesives and sealants provide:


- **Structural support and bonding:** Adhesives bond BESS components together, such as the cooling plates and battery cells within battery modules. Adhesive manufacturers can formulate adhesives for different levels of structural support, depending on their use.
- **Thermal management:** Heat generated by powerful batteries must have somewhere to go. Adhesives and gap fillers pass heat from cells to heatsinks so batteries perform as intended for as long as possible. Encapsulants help prevent thermal runaway, which can lead to fires. If fires do break out in individual cells, encapsulants isolate the cell so the fire doesn’t spread.
- **Protection:** Sealants and gaskets protect components from moisture, humidity, and environmental factors that could damage the equipment or decrease its durability.
- **Electrical insulation:** Dielectric coatings prevent electricity from arcing between battery components, causing short circuits.


Exactly how each sealant or adhesive is used in a BESS depends on the final application. For example, a BESS set on a hard-packed gravel pad in a rural area would not require highly structural adhesives for vibration protection. If this BESS held a large number of extremely dense battery packs that generated substantial heat, however, the batteries would require encapsulant to prevent fires.


### BESS and BMS: New Energy BFFs


Every BESS needs a highly intelligent BMS to monitor battery performance. BMS are electronic systems that track each battery’s state of charge (energy movement in and out of battery cells), record battery capacity, and protect cells from overcharging and overuse. A BMS tracks these metrics throughout each battery's lifetime to determine its state of health, helping operators identify which batteries are ready for replacement. BMS help BESS function better for longer, and the smarter the BMS, the more efficient the BESS.


BMS manufacturers rely on adhesives to mount printed circuit board (PCB) components, house PCBs, seal high-voltage connectors and enclosures, and pass heat from PCBs to heatsinks. As BMS become more powerful, they generate more heat, requiring[more sophisticated thermal interface materials (TIMs)](https://www.hbfuller.com/en/blog/thegluetalkblog/2025/may/benefits-of-using-thermally-conductive-adhesives) , such as liquid encapsulants, to spread heat away from the system.


### Managing New Energy Storage Risks


Many BESS systems use lithium-ion batteries, which can overheat during use. Although rare, battery fires can be difficult to extinguish and dangerous. A BMS helps mitigate fire risks by limiting charging to overused cells. In addition, incorporating flame-retardant encapsulants into battery design reduces the risk of a damaging fire.


BESS must meet regulatory requirements before use. Common regulations include:


- **UL 9540 – Safety Standard for Energy Storage:** Lists criteria for assessing energy storage system hazards, requires communication systems that alert operators of safety issues, and more.
- **UL 9540A – Test Method for Evaluating Thermal Runaway Fire Propagation in Battery Energy Storage Systems:** Ensures BESS meet fire safety and building code requirements.
- **NFPA 855 – Standard for the Installation of Stationary Energy Storage Systems:** Covers the minimum requirements for mitigating energy storage system risks.


Depending on where the BESS is sold, it will likely need to meet additional or different requirements. BESS components undergo testing to ensure they comply with regulations.


### What’s Next in New Energy Storage Systems


Since BESS are not limited by weight or size, they present an exciting opportunity to experiment with emerging materials and battery cell technology. The innovations are endless—and necessary to scale new energy growth.


“BESS are quite impressive beasts,” says Michael Cooper, Global Business Development Manager at H.B. Fuller. “There’s a whole different approach to these types of designs and these batteries with many different cell and electrolyte technologies. The technologies pose their own set of problems, but also have their own set of needs.”


For years, industry stakeholders have been exploring alternatives to lithium-ion cells. Not only are lithium-ion batteries combustible, but they are expensive to recycle, and[extracting lithium is resource-heavy](https://www.bbc.com/future/article/20240319-the-most-sustainable-alternatives-to-lithium-batteries) . Other options include sodium-ion cells and solid-state cells.


Lithium iron phosphate (LFP) cells present another alternative to lithium-ion cells. Although LFP cells also contain lithium, they have an iron-phosphate cathode rather than the typical nickel, cobalt, and manganese cathode. LFP cell design[decreases energy density but improves safety](https://electrifynews.com/news/batteries/what-is-an-lfp-battery-and-what-does-it-mean-for-evs/) . Since energy density is less critical to BESS design than to other types of electricity storage systems, LFP shows significant promise as a cost-effective, thermally stable, and durable alternative with[enhanced discharge and charge efficiency](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11648842/) .


In addition to adopting new cell technology, BESS can also provide a home for second-life electric vehicle (EV) batteries. These batteries continue to produce energy long after their automotive lifespan has ended. As the number of EVs increases, the number of retired batteries will also increase. Incorporating used EV batteries into BESS rather than disposing of them maximizes their use.


In future years, modular BESS systems and AI-driven BMS may become increasingly common. Modular systems provide easier access to cell repair and replacement. Adding modules to BESS to boost capacity is also a cost-effective way to scale compared to remodeling the units. BMS equipped with AI algorithms custom-built to optimize cell use can further extend battery life.


### How Adhesives Support Next-Generation BESS


Advancements in BESS and BMS will require innovative adhesive products. For example, some types of alternative cell technology expand and contract more than lithium-ion cells. Adhesives bonding these cells must be capable of elongating (stretching) without breaking.


Although alternative cell technology can reduce fire risks, high temperatures can still prematurely age batteries. Incorporating TIMs into BESS design to remove heat will remain critical to their performance. Adhesive manufacturers can work with customers to either create new products or modify existing formulations.


“That’s part and parcel of the solution-oriented approach that we offer to battery manufacturers,” explains Cooper. “We can modify a lot of our materials to facilitate stress management, torsional compression, torque, or even three-dimensional stresses. Having discussions with engineers about what is needed helps us generate and innovate the products that will be used in next-generation applications.”
