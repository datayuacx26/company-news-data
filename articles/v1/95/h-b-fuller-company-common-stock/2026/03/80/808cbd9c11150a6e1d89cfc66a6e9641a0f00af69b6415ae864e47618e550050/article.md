# Adhesives and Recyclability: Why They Matter

Adhesives are substances that bond materials together, playing a critical role in the manufacturing and processing of countless products. Whether industrial production, craft applications, or everyday consumer goods, adhesive bonding technology is essential for modern product design.


In packaging, adhesives serve multiple purposes: securing labels, sealing cartons, and laminating flexible packaging structures. Despite their small proportion, adhesives have an impact on the overall performance of the packaging and recyclability of packaging materials. Their chemical composition and performance characteristics can determine whether a package can be efficiently processed in recycling streams or could become a foreign substance that may reduce the quality of recovered materials.


Choosing the right adhesive is not just a technical decision: it is a sustainability decision! Adhesives compatible with recycling processes help ensure that valuable materials can be recovered and reused, supporting the broader goals of a circular economy.


### Adhesives and the Circular Economy


The circular economy aims to keep resources in use for as long as possible, minimizing waste and reducing the need for virgin materials. Recycling is a cornerstone of this approach, but achieving high-quality recycling depends on packaging design. When different materials—such as paper, plastics, and adhesives—are difficult to separate or process, recycling becomes inefficient. This often results in low-grade recycled outputs or, worse, packaging waste ending up in landfills or incineration.


Adhesives play a pivotal role in this challenge. While they represent a small fraction of packaging weight, their properties can make or break recyclability. Specially designed adhesives are formulated to be compatible with the recycling processes of the substrates they bond. They maintain strong adhesion during use but can be removed or processed without damaging the packaging material. This compatibility is essential for producing high-quality recycled material.


Adhesives designed for recycling are now widely used in corrugated boxes, cartons, labels, and mono-material flexible packaging, for example. This permits brands and producers to meet their extended producer responsibility (EPR) obligations under the European Packaging and Packaging Waste Regulation (PPWR) and US state-based EPR legislation.


By selecting adhesives that enable material recovery, companies contribute directly to circular economy goals. Packaging designed for recyclability and supported by the right adhesive technology ensures that resources stay within the economic cycle, reducing waste and environmental impact.


### Challenges in Recycling


When evaluating the recyclability of packaging, every component matters, including adhesives. While adhesives typically account for a small fraction of the packaging weight, their impact on recycling efficiency can be important. If adhesives are not compatible with recycling technologies, they may cause contamination or reduce material quality.


- **Paper Recycling:** Adhesives follow one of two paths: removal or incorporation into recycled paper. Adhesives that do not break down or separate out properly can create stickies: small, tacky particles that contaminate recycled pulp and compromise the quality of new paper products. These stickies can cause defects in paper reels, may lead to reel breakages during processing, or accumulate on machinery, resulting in production interruptions and increased maintenance costs. Therefore, proper adhesive selection and recyclability testing according to international standards is key to ensuring paper and board recycling is as efficient as possible.
- **Plastic Recycling:** Labels and their adhesives pose unique challenges in plastic recycling streams. Adhesives must have effective wash-off or release properties. Those that release in water or alkali solutions ensure clean separation during the washing stage, leaving no residue on the plastic. When adhesives fail to wash off or release, they contaminate the recycled material, reducing purity, and limiting reuse in high-quality applications.


For flexible packaging materials' recycling is rather more challenging as the recycling technology is still evolving. For instance, mechanical recycling works well for mono-material structures if sorted to a high degree of purity of the plastic type. Specifically formulated laminating adhesives, of which H.B. Fuller has several approved grades, are compatible with this mechanical process to produce new PE (polyethylene) films or rigid PP (polypropylene) packaging. Regarding advanced or chemical recycling the technology is still evolving and the capital investments can be substantial, two reasons why this avenue for flexible packaging recycling is still comparatively small in scale.
