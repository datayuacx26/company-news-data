# Winter Tips for Using Roofing Adhesives

Cold weather brings unique challenges for[roofing adhesives](https://www.hbfuller.com/en/markets/commercial-roofing) . Lower temperatures can thicken products, slow cure times, and affect overall performance—but with the right storage, preparation, and techniques, contractors can still achieve reliable, high performance bonds during winter months.


Below are our essential cold weather best practices to help ensure consistent results on every project.


### How to store adhesive products in cold weather


Proper storage is critical during winter months, especially overnight. Adhesives should typically be kept between 60°F and 80°F (16°C–27°C) in a protected area. Always check the recommended temperature storage range for the specific product you’re using.


Best practices:


- Do not leave product outside exposed to the elements.
- Avoid placing[canister products](https://www.hbfuller.com/en/blog/thegluetalkblog/2025/august/mastering-spray-applied-canister-adhesives-tips-safety-best-practices) directly onto cold surfaces, particularly concrete—use a wooden pallet or insulation barrier to prevent heat loss.
- If products have been stored above or below the ideal temperature range, allow them to return to ambient room temperature before use to maintain expected coverage rates and performance.


### How cold temperatures affect adhesive properties


As temperatures drop, there will be noticeable changes in the flow properties of adhesives. Adhesives become more viscous—thicker and slower flowing—which can affect performance, application quality and coverage.


To minimize these issues, keep products in a protected area, ideally above 60°F (16°C).


Storing canister products at low temperatures for prolonged periods can affect how easily they can be sprayed. The reduced internal pressure makes the thickened adhesive harder to dispense. Best practices to avoid this:


- Bring canisters up to 70°F (21°C) before spraying.
- Keep canisters warm with canister warmers or blankets during use.
- Shake or agitate canisters before application to remix adhesive and propellant for a consistent spray pattern.


### How cold temperatures affect adhesive cure-times


Adhesive cure time—the period required for a full, reliable bond—is strongly influenced by outside air temperature. Cold slows the chemical reaction, meaning the product will cure more slowly. Allow extra time for adhesives to set and cure.


To ensure proper curing:


- Apply products during the warmest part of the day whenever possible.
- Avoid application during freezing conditions.
- Many adhesives require application temperatures above 40°F (4°C) for proper curing. Always follow the guidance in the technical data sheet for your specific product.

### How to prepare surfaces in winter


Colder months bring increased moisture risks—rain, snow, frost, dew, and condensation that can compromise bond integrity.


Before applying any adhesive:


- Ensure the substrate is completely dry, clean, and free of contaminants.
- Remove frost, ice, or moisture from all surfaces.
- Confirm weather conditions will remain dry throughout the bonding and curing/drying periods.


Proper surface preparation is one of the most important steps in securing strong winter bonds.


### Winter roofing doesn’t have to mean compromise


With the right storage, preparation, and product selection, contractors can work efficiently and confidently during winter months. Plan ahead, follow temperature specific guidelines, and choose products formulated for the conditions you’re working in.
