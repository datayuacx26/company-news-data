# How to Get the Benefits of Restaurant Automation—Without Robots

Restaurant kitchen automation has advanced far beyond the original burger-flipping arm, Flippy. Robots can make fried rice, dice avocados, and more. But restaurants haven’t yet scaled up their use of AI-powered robotics in the kitchen. Why?


In a word: Value.


Even as restaurant robotics become more technically advanced, and even as restaurants seek ways to reduce their labor costs, AI-powered kitchen automation remains expensive to install and maintain, particularly at scale.


Also, it’s difficult to add a robotic element to an existing system. Restaurant kitchens are designed to maximize available space and improve human efficiency. Installing a robotic arm at the deep fryer may speed up French fry production but[hinder workers](https://fortune.com/2026/02/26/robot-disruption-fast-food-short-order-cook-flippy-labor-shortage/) from doing their other tasks.


What restaurant robots can and can’t do


Commercial kitchen robots are built for simple, repetitive, and unchanging tasks. For example:


-


Flipping burgers or pancakes


-


Deep-frying certain foods


-


Prep work


-


Measuring uniform ingredient portions


Foodservice robots[don’t perform well](https://www.webstaurantstore.com/blog/2548/restaurant-automation.html?srsltid=AfmBOooHy16vJH88jIaa3n1TugESsmn56geiQpuiJMizXUr2oFgvZgmm) when tasks require troubleshooting or judgment calls. Currently, robots aren’t able to detect when food is blemished, bruised, expired, or discolored.


Another challenge is articulation. Robots aren’t yet capable of imitating the precise motor skills of the human hand. Add in the challenges presented by hot, humid, and sometimes messy restaurant environments, and it may be a while before robots are baking cakes or crafting[beautifully rolled burritos.](https://www.theatlantic.com/technology/archive/2023/10/chipotle-fast-food-preparation-robots/675559/)
