## What is Tea Tree Oil?


Tea tree oil is made from the tea tree plant, also known as *Melaleuca alternifolia \[1\]* . Native to Australia, the oil from this plant has been used for centuries by indigenous communities for its medicinal properties, particularly in skincare.


So, what makes tea tree oil so special?


This essential oil has potent antibacterial, antifungal, and anti-inflammatory properties, making it a popular natural remedy for various skin conditions. While research is limited, tea tree oil is currently used as a treatment for \[2\]:


- Acne
- Athlete’s foot
- Lice
- Nail fungus
- Cuts
- Mite infections on the eyelids
- Insect bites


## Can Tea Tree Oil Help Acne-Prone Skin?


From salicylic acid to sulfur, there are countless ingredient options to choose from in the fight against acne. Is tea tree oil a strong contender?


In short, tea tree oil *could* be useful for acne-prone skin. While minimal, research points to topical tea tree oil as a treatment for mild to moderate acne:


- In one study, a 5% tea tree oil gel reduced mild to moderate acne when compared to a placebo \[3\].
- Similarly, another study found that a twice-daily application of tea tree oil products reduced mild to moderate acne \[4\].
- When compared to a 5% benzoyl peroxide solution, a 5% tea tree oil solution equally reduced acne lesions and severity \[5\].


Like any acne treatment, tea tree oil isn’t an overnight success. It may take a few months, or at least a few weeks, to see a visible difference. However, the research points to potential for this natural acne solution.


## How Does Tea Tree Oil Help Acne-Prone Skin?


Acne isn’t a simple equation. Pimples arise from any chaotic mix of inflammation, bacteria, dead skin, and sebum (the skin’s natural oils) \[6\]. Luckily, tea tree oil attacks more than one of these causes.


When applied to the skin, tea tree oil can reduce acne lesions and severity through these special properties \[7\]:


- **Antimicrobial** – Bacterial overgrowth is a common cause of acne—particularly the overgrowth of *Propionibacterium acnes* . Tea tree oil has been shown to possess antimicrobial properties, making it effective in killing bacteria and reducing the risk of infection.
- **Anti-inflammation** – In addition to its antimicrobial action, tea tree oil also exhibits anti-inflammatory properties. Inflammation is the primary aggravator of acne, resulting in red, swollen, and uncomfortable pimples. By reducing inflammation, tea tree oil can help soothe the skin, alleviate the visible signs of acne, and promote the healing process.
- **Sebum control** – While healthy sebum levels are crucial, too much skin oil can lead to clogged pores and acne. Research shows that tea tree oil could possibly balance excess sebum production in those with oily skin \[8\].

## Side Effects of Tea Tree Oil for Skin


So, tea tree oil may be a *superhero-to-be* in your skincare routine. However, like any skincare ingredient, it comes with potential adverse reactions.


Before adding any tea tree oil product to your regimen, it's important to know the potential side effects, especially for more concentrated forms. In certain skin types, tea tree oil use may lead to any of these less-than-desired outcomes \[12\]\[13\]:,


- **Skin irritation** – While often anti-inflammatory, a high concentration of tea tree oil can be too harsh for some individuals, particularly those with sensitive skin. Undiluted tea tree oil may leave you with redness, itching, or a burning sensation. In severe cases, certain skin types may even develop contact dermatitis—an itchy, red, and burning rash.
- **Photosensitivity** – Like other acne-fighting ingredients, tea tree oil could increase your skin’s sensitivity to sunlight. Using a[daily moisturizer with SPF](https://www.provenskincare.com/personalized-day-moisturizer/) is therefore heavily recommended, whether you’re using tea tree oil or not.
- **Allergic reactions** – Although rare, some individuals may be allergic to tea tree oil. An allergic reaction can manifest as redness, swelling, hives, or even severe reactions like difficulty breathing or anaphylaxis. If you have a known allergy to tea tree oil or any other[natural skincare ingredients](https://www.provenskincare.com/blog/the-best-natural-skincare-ingredients-proven-by-science/) commonly found in essential oils, it's important to avoid using tea tree oil on your skin.


## How to Use Tea Tree Oil in Your Skincare


Every skincare ingredient comes with “do’s” and “dont’s”—and tea tree oil is no exception. To bring this natural solution into your routine, you’ll need to apply it with care and caution.


Above all, tea tree oil has one rule—you *must* dilute it. Tea tree is an essential oil, which makes it highly potent and irritating in its undiluted form \[14\]. To use it on your skin, you must use a solution that’s diluted (usually around 5% to 10% tea tree oil).


Once you have the right concentration, you can incorporate tea tree oil into your regimen by following these steps:


1. **Cleanse** – Use a gentle[facial cleanser](https://www.provenskincare.com/personalized-face-cleanser/) to rid your face of any dirt or impurities.
2. **Tone** – If toner is part of your regimen, apply it to a cotton pad and softly swipe it across your face.
3. **Apply tea tree oil product** – As a gel or tincture, apply your tea tree oil product across the entirety of your face or to any acne-prone areas ( T-zone, chin, jaw-line, etc.).
4. **Moisturize** – Apply a daytime moisturizer or a[night cream](https://www.provenskincare.com/personalized-night-cream/) to your face and neck.
5. **Protect** – During your morning routine, make sure to use a[daily moisturizer with SPF](https://www.provenskincare.com/personalized-day-moisturizer/) to protect your skin from UV rays.
