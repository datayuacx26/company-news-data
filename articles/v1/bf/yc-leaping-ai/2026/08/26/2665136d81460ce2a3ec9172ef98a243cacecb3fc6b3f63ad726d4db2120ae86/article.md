# Why One-Day Bath Companies Are Losing Jobs to a Missed Phone Call

By[Pam Torrey](https://www.linkedin.com/in/pam-torrey-920175b4/) , Senior Director of Marketing at Leaping AI


It's 7:30 on a Tuesday evening, right after the kids are in bed, and a homeowner finally has a minute to fill out the online quote forms she bookmarked earlier for two bathroom remodelers. One calls her back within a few minutes to book the in-home consultation. The other doesn't call until Wednesday morning. By the time that second call comes in, she's already talked to the first contractor, gotten her questions answered, and booked the appointment. The second contractor is now playing catch-up, and it's not because they're more expensive, have a worse reputation, or don't sell the right product. It's because they weren't quick enough on the phone.


Most one-day bath companies understand the importance of speed to lead and may have a system in place to handle inbound calls during normal business hours. But leads don’t always adhere to business hours, or they may come in when the call center reps are already on the phone. And that’s something you need a system to handle.


## The math on speed to lead is not new, and it's not close


The data on response times isn’t new and it hasn’t changed in more than a decade. In 2011,[Harvard Business Review](https://hbr.org/2011/03/the-short-life-of-online-sales-leads) analyzed response times across more than a million sales leads and found that companies trying to make contact within an hour were about 7 times more likely to qualify a lead than companies that waited longer than an hour. And they were 60 times more likely than companies that waited a day or more.


Bathroom remodels are no exception to this pattern. If anything, a project that can run $15,000 to $30,000 is exactly the kind where the decision maker is calling more than one company and the speed with which you get to that lead matters. The first company to connect with the homeowner is more likely to win.


## Why a bathroom remodeling call is not like other home service calls


A bath remodeling lead isn’t usually an emergency call where the homeowner will take whoever shows up first. It is a substantial purchase. The homeowner has done research on products, the demolition and installation processes, and payment options before they reach out to contractors. At that point, much of the buying process has already happened and it becomes about who the homeowner trusts first to work on their home. That’s where answering the phone quickly and getting an appointment scheduled before the competition becomes critical.


Knowing all of this, the reality for bath contractors is:


-


**The ticket size raises the stakes.** A single in-home consultation can be worth anywhere from $5,000 to $30,000 or more for a premium remodel, once it closes. That's not a number worth losing to a call that went to voicemail.


-


**Homeowners research on their own schedules,** and a lot of that research happens in the evening, after work, or over the weekend, when the call center isn't staffed.


-


**Comparison shopping is the default.** Most homeowners aren't calling just one company. They're calling around, and going with whoever answers, explains the process clearly, and gets something on the calendar.


Put those three together and the phone stops being a nice-to-have and becomes the actual point of competition for bath remodelers.


## Where the leads are actually leaking out


Most one-day bath companies are losing jobs before their sales teams ever get involved. The leak is further upstream. A few patterns are consistent across the industry:


-


**Staffing doesn't match call volume.** A small, core call center team can handle steady call flow, but bath remodeling doesn't come in steady. It spikes around marketing pushes, home shows, and seasonal demand, and that's when calls start going unanswered.


-


**After-hours is a black hole.** Evening and weekend calls, which is when a lot of homeowners make the decision to call, often go straight to voicemail and aren’t returned until Monday.


-


**Fresh leads sit longer than they should.** Even with a good call center team, a lead that comes in at 4:45 on a Friday isn't getting a callback until Monday morning, and by then a competitor already picked up the phone.


The problem is clearly capacity and staffing, not team discipline. A team of any size will run out of work hours before your homeowners run out of questions.
