# Choosing the right IPMS for trademarks: Why risk control needs more than recordkeeping

Trademark risk is rarely caused by one missed deadline or one incomplete record. More often, it builds when decisions, business context and portfolio activity are disconnected. For trademark leaders, the right IP management system should do more than store information. It should help teams identify risk earlier, manage decisions consistently and make data-driven portfolio decisions that minimize risk and optimize revenue strategies with confidence.


In this blog, Jill Clark, Lead Product Consultant at Clarivate and former corporate and law firm trademark paralegal, explores why effective trademark risk control depends on connected data, structured workflows and practical visibility across the portfolio.


Drawing on real-world trademark experience, Jill explains how[IPfolio](https://clarivate.com/DiscoverIPfolio) helps trademark teams move beyond simple recordkeeping to advanced decision-making that manages risk and optimizes value.


### Why trademark risk control starts with connected information


In my[last post](https://clarivate.com/intellectual-property/blog/what-trademark-leaders-really-need-from-their-ip-management-system/) , I talked about the difference between information and insight. Trademark teams may have the records and dates they need, and even the reports leadership asks for, but still struggle to answer questions with confidence.


Risk control is the next part of that conversation.


When I worked in trademark prosecution, risk did not always look dramatic. Sometimes it looked like a renewal notice for a mark tied to a product no longer sold. Sometimes it was an ownership update handled on a single record but never carried over to the rest of the portfolio. Sometimes it was a clearance discussion that happened over email, with the reasoning never tied back to the trademark record.


None of those issues may feel urgent in the moment. Across a large portfolio, they add up. They create uncertainty. They make it harder to know what has been reviewed, what still needs action and where the business may be carrying avoidable risk.


That is why choosing the right IP management system matters. For trademark teams, an IP Management System (IPMS) should do more than store marks and documents or track important dates. It should help the team see risk earlier, manage decisions consistently and explain what happened later.


### Trademark risk can hide in plain sight


A deadline may be docketed correctly. Outside counsel may send a reminder on time. The record may look complete. But that does not mean the team has the full picture.


A trademark portfolio changes constantly. Brands expand. Products are retired. Companies reorganize. Markets shift. New conflicts appear. In-use requirements come due. Renewal decisions need current business input.


If the IP management system only stores records, the team still has to build the risk picture manually. That usually means moving back and forth between emails, spreadsheets and docket reports, then trying to confirm the business context before anyone feels confident.


IPfolio helps reduce that manual work by bringing more of that context into one place. The trademark record can show more than the mark itself. It can connect to ownership, prosecution activity, key dates, related products, business units, documents, outside counsel updates and portfolio status. That gives the team a clearer view of what needs attention and why.


That matters at every level. A paralegal needs to know what needs action. An attorney needs the context behind the legal decision. A manager needs to see workload and open issues. A leader needs confidence that risk is being monitored and not buried in someone’s inbox.


### Why disconnected trademark decisions create risk


Trademark work includes a steady stream of judgment calls.


- Should the team oppose a similar mark?
- Should it renew a registration tied to an older product?
- Should it keep protection in a country where the business has scaled back?
- Should it clear a new brand in one market but hold off in another?


The risk is not only whether the team makes the right decision. It is whether the decision is based on the right information and captured where others can find it later.


When decisions live in email threads or separate files, teams lose institutional memory. A new team member may not know why a mark was maintained. A business leader may question renewal spend without seeing the reason behind it. A future clearance review may miss prior concerns because the earlier analysis was never tied to the record.


This is where an IPMS needs to support the work around the record. For trademark teams, the system should connect the legal record to the activity and decisions surrounding it. That includes prosecution history, ownership questions, watch activity, renewal decisions and outside counsel input.


In IPfolio, that connected structure helps the team avoid treating each trademark as an isolated record. The mark can sit within the broader business and legal context. That makes it easier to see what has happened, what is still open and what may need to be escalated.


### What practical trademark risk control looks like


Good risk control does not mean every risk disappears. It means the team can see, track and explain the risk.


That sounds simple, but it is often where systems fall short.


A trademark team may need to know which marks have upcoming deadlines that require business input. They may also need to identify registrations tied to inactive products, monitor open conflicts or understand which decisions are waiting on outside counsel, internal review, or budget approval.


Those are not merely administrative considerations. They can influence costs, revenue, portfolio strategy, brand reputation and overall legal risk.


IPfolio can support this through structured data, workflows and dashboards that surface the work needing attention. Instead of relying on one-off spreadsheets, teams can use current portfolio information to review renewals, monitor open items and prepare for leadership conversations.


This becomes even more important for global trademark portfolios. One deadline may be easy to manage. Hundreds or thousands of deadlines across jurisdictions are different. Without reliable context, everything can start to look equally important. With the right IPMS structure, teams can separate routine maintenance from decisions that require review.


### How IPMS workflows help reduce gaps


One of the most practical ways to reduce trademark risk is through better workflows.


Trademark work depends on handoffs. A business team may request clearance. An attorney may assess the risk. Outside counsel may file a response. A paralegal may manage the date and supporting documents. Leadership may need to approve the cost or strategy.


When those handoffs happen informally, the work may still get done, but the process depends too much on individual follow-up. That creates risk when people are busy, roles change or the portfolio grows.


IPfolio can help teams define workflows around these moments. A clearance request can follow a consistent review path. A renewal decision can require business input before action is taken. A watch notice can be reviewed, escalated and documented. A prosecution task can be assigned, tracked and tied back to the correct record.


The system does not replace legal and business judgment. Trademark professionals still make the decisions. The value is that the process around those decisions becomes more visible and consistent.


### How to choose an IPMS with risk control in mind


When organizations evaluate an IPMS, risk control should be part of the conversation from the beginning.


It is easy to focus on recordkeeping, deadline tracking and reporting. Those matter. But they do not tell the full story.


The better question is whether the system helps the team manage the decisions and exceptions that create risk.


- Can the team see what needs attention without relying on manual follow-up?
- Can legal records connect to business context?
- Can workflows reflect how issues are reviewed, escalated and resolved?
- Can leadership see risk clearly enough to act on it?


These questions matter because trademark risk is not controlled by data alone. It is controlled when the right data is connected to the right workflow at the right time.


For trademark teams, IPfolio is strongest when it is used for that broader purpose. It brings together structured records, workflow management, reporting and portfolio visibility so teams can move from reacting to issues to managing them with more confidence.


The right IPMS should do more than tell a team what it owns. It should help teams spot emerging risks early, understand their potential impact and act with confidence before those risks turn into costly problems or missed opportunities.
