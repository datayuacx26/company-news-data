# The promise – and limitations – of AI in JCA PICO planning for pharmas and medtechs

The central challenge of Joint Clinical Assessment (JCA) is building a dossier before knowing precisely what evidence will be requested by the HTA Coordination Group – effectively, preparing an answer to a question that has not yet been asked.


If outcomes aligned with JCA-defined PICO components (Problem or Population, Intervention, Comparison, Outcome) are not anticipated early, health technology developers (HTDs) can face a last-minute rush to generate evidence within a short submission window. To reduce this risk, HTDs are increasingly turning to artificial intelligence (AI) tools to improve both the speed and quality of PICO planning.


AI can be useful for rapidly synthesizing information, and is effective at surfacing precedent, but it cannot determine what will be considered decision-relevant in a future JCA. Translating these insights into robust and defensible PICO assumptions requires expert interpretation, informed by a deep understanding of how the reimbursement landscape is evolving. Here, we look at some of the ways it can support PICO planning, as well as its limitations.


### How Clarivate leverages AI to strengthen PICO planning


Developing robust PICO assumptions requires input from a variety of sources, including HTA decisions, JCA documentation, and clinical guidelines. However, identifying and generating this information via traditional desk research can be a time-consuming and resource-intensive process.


AI can help teams quickly identify, retrieve, and summarize large volumes of information, including:


- Prior HTA assessments and clinical guidelines in the indication, highlighting relevant comparators and outcomes of interest
- Clinical trial data for the product of interest and key competitors
- Methodological guidance and scoping documents from the HTA Coordination Group outlining requirements for dossier development


However, identifying relevant data is only the first step. Filtering and interpreting what that evidence means for PICO planning requires the experience and judgement of seasoned systematic review specialists and HTA experts who help contextualise findings, ensure alignment with reimbursement expectations and validate assumptions. A deep understanding of policy and HTA evidence requirements is important for predicting how decision-makers may prioritize comparators and endpoints within the JCA framework. Together, these perspectives help ensure PICO assumptions are not only grounded in high-quality evidence but are also relevant to how decisions will be made in practice.


Early PICO planning can also be informed by primary research with appropriate clinicians and payers. Insights from primary research can inform clear recommendations that align with HTA expectations. For example, obtaining clinicians’ perspectives on real-world practice, including evolving standards of care, treatment pathways, unmet need and clinically meaningful outcomes, can provide important context that cannot be derived from published evidence alone. This is important because JCA assessments are expected to focus on outcomes considered clinically meaningful in practice, not simply those captured in existing evidence.


Ultimately, while AI can streamline evidence gathering and highlight historical precedent, human expertise is needed to interpret these insights, along with inputs from clinicians and payers, and apply them to the current landscape.
