# Industry-Changing 21st Century ROAD to Housing Act Becomes Law

# Industry-Changing 21st Century ROAD to Housing Act Becomes Law


Published On: July 13, 2026


•


3 min read


•


***Landmark bipartisan legislation takes effect after president declines to sign or veto***


**WASHINGTON, D.C.** **,** **July 11,** **2026** — The 21st Century ROAD to Housing Act automatically became law on Saturday


, July 11,


without President Donald Trump's signature, clearing a constitutional review period after the president announced he would neither sign nor veto the most sweeping federal housing legislation in decades.


The bill passed both chambers by wide margins: 85-5 in the Senate on June 22 and 358-32 in the House on June 23.


---


### **What the Law Does**


- **Allows qualifying manufactured homes to be built without a permanent chassis**


- **Modernizes FHA financing**


- **Exempts manufactured homes from restrictions aimed at large institutional investors**


- **Streamlines permitting and environmental reviews**


- **Directs federal regulators to study small-dollar mortgage barriers**


### **What This Means for Homebuyers**


- **More affordable housing options.** Chassis-free designs can fit communities where manufactured homes were previously harder to place


- **Potentially easier financing.** Updated FHA loan limits and a study of lending barriers are designed to help more buyers qualify


- **Possibility of faster, less costly approvals.** Streamlined permitting and pre-approved building plans reduce time and expense before construction begins


- **Fewer corporate competitors for starter homes.** The provision applies to qualifying large institutional investors and restricts certain future purchases of single-family homes.

### Boor: Real Change Happens at the State Level


Bill Boor, President and CEO of Cavco Industries, applauded the bill’s passage into law, with an open take about both the opportunity and its limits. On the chassis reform provision, he said earlier this year:


> “Just removing that from the definition of a manufactured home opens up innovation opportunities for our industry: multi-story construction, ground-level installation, new placement options in urban and suburban markets. You start to see the opportunity for product innovation. And that opens up a whole new market opportunity for this industry.”


He was equally consistent about what federal action alone cannot accomplish.


“I always caution that a federal change won’t open up opportunities overnight. There’s a lot of work that has to happen at the state level once the definition is improved.”


In this arena, Boor speaks from experience. He has testified mutliple times in front of Congressional committees


advocating for


the need to expand


manufactured housing as a solution to the nation’s affordable housing crisis.


In a LinkedIn post published the day Congress passed the legislation, Boor identified the law’s broader significance:


> “For the first time, Congress has explicitly recognized manufactured housing as part of the nation’s housing supply solution.” But he argued the harder work lies ahead – in state capitols, county offices and local planning meetings. “That is where housing opportunity becomes housing reality.”


The nation’s housing shortage is estimated at approximately 4 million homes. The median price of an existing U.S. home reached $440,600 in June, according to the National Association of Realtors. As Boor put it:


> “All of these things are aimed toward more supply of factory-built housing – which is a supply the country needs at the lower end of the affordability discussion. They create real momentum. Not just for our company, but for the industry as a whole and hopeful homebuyers across America.”
