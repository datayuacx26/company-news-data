# Manufactured homebuilder expands in Phoenix to address US housing shortage

# Manufactured homebuilder expands in Phoenix to address US housing shortage


Published On: May 29, 2026


•


2 min read


•


A conceptual rendering of Cavco Industries Inc.'s 616,000-square-foot home manufacturing plant in El Mirage, Arizona. (Cavco Industries)


**Factory-built homes, Cavco Industries expansion, and new manufactured housing production in Arizona are driving a 616,000-square-foot manufacturing facility in El Mirage, with operations expected to begin in mid-2027.**


Cavco Industries is expanding its factory-built housing production with a new manufacturing facility in west Phoenix, Arizona. The El Mirage plant will add 616,000 square feet of production capacity dedicated to single-family homes built to federal construction standards and distributed nationwide.


The facility is designed to scale with demand, initially launching with one production line and reserving additional space for future expansion. The project reflects continued investment in increasing the supply of attainable housing across key U.S. growth markets.


Factory-built and manufactured homes are increasingly recognized as an important part of addressing the national housing shortage. Compared to traditional site-built homes, they typically offer shorter construction timelines and more controlled building environments, which can help reduce costs and delays tied to weather and labor constraints.


“The shortage of affordable housing in the United States is real, and the El Mirage project will expand Cavco’s capacity to provide quality homes for deserving families in Arizona and surrounding states,” said Bill Boor, president and CEO of Cavco Industries.


The El Mirage facility is part of Cavco Industries’ broader strategy to expand manufacturing capacity in high-demand regions across the Southwest. The company operates more than 30 manufacturing plants nationwide, including multiple facilities in the Phoenix area.


As housing supply remains tight in many parts of the South and Mountain West, increased production of factory-built homes is helping expand access to more attainable housing options for buyers.
