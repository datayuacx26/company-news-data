# Chase Explores $750 Billion in Housing Financing That Could Include Manufactured Homes

# Chase Explores $750 Billion in Housing Financing That Could Include Manufactured Homes


Published On: August 10, 2026


•


3 min read


•


***O*** ***ne of the nation’s largest bank mortgage lenders is looking at loans for factory-built homes as part of a massive housing push running through 2035.***


[JPMorganChase](https://www.jpmorganchase.com/newsroom/press-releases/2026/american-dream-initiative-housing-supply-support) is exploring financing options for manufactured and modular homes – a move that could open new lending avenues in a sector where it already has a foothold, but where mainstream mortgage access has never been widely available.


The idea is part of a bigger commitment: more than $750 billion the bank plans to deploy through 2035 to boost housing supply and support homeownership across the U.S. The announcement, made Aug. 3, 2026, expands on JPMorganChase’s[American Dream Initiative](https://www.jpmorganchase.com/impact/housing) , which launched in March.


## **This Isn’t JPMorganChase’s First Move in Factory-Built Housing**


JPMorganChase doesn’t currently offer mortgage loans for manufactured or modular homes – but the company has said publicly it’s considering them as possible future loan products.


What’s less widely known is that JPMorganChase already has skin in the game. The firm has committed more than $20 million in philanthropic capital and mission-driven loans to ROC USA, a nonprofit that helps manufactured home community residents purchase the land beneath their homes. It has also supported modular home construction programs in partnership with nonprofit developers, according to the company’s own published impact pages.


So


,


while a


JPMorganChase


mortgage product for factory-built homes would be new territory for the firm’s consumer lending operation, the underlying relationships with the sector are already in place.


## **The Numbers Behind the $750 Billion Plan**


The initiative breaks into three goals:


1. Build or preserve 1 million affordable housing units – defined as housing for households earning no more than 120% of the area median income, a benchmark HUD sets annually by county and metro area


2. Help 500,000 people buy homes, including 200,000 first-time buyers, by increasing mortgage lending more than 40% and hiring 850 new Home Lending Advisors


3. Advance housing policy through the JPMorganChase PolicyCenter and Institute, as well as by chairing the U.S. Chamber of Commerce’s newly formed Housing Advisory Council


The commitment represents nearly a 40% increase in housing capital compared with what the bank deployed over the previous decade. In 2025,


JPMorganChase


produced over $48 billion in originations across more than 90,000 transactions, according to IEmergent’s analysis of federal Home Mortgage Disclosure Act data – finishing as the top depository lender and third overall by dollar volume.


“An affordable and resilient housing market is essential to driving economic growth and increasing opportunity,” said Michelle Herrick, Head of Commercial Real Estate for


J.P. Morgan


.


## **New Federal Housing Law Adds Momentum**


The timing lines up with a significant piece of federal legislation. The 21st Century ROAD to Housing Act became law on July 11, 2026, after the Senate passed it 85-5 on June 22 and the House followed 358-32 on June 23. Among its provisions, the law raises FHA loan limits for manufactured homes and directs HUD to review construction financing programs for barriers facing modular housing developers.


JPMorganChase publicly backed several provisions that fed into the final package, including the Accelerating Home Building Act, the Housing Supply Expansion Act, and the Helping More Families Save Act.


## **What This Could Mean for the Manufactured Housing Industry**


Manufactured home buyers have long been at a financing disadvantage. Most rely on chattel loans – personal property loans that can carry higher interest rates and shorter terms than traditional mortgages. That gap has constrained both affordability and demand in the sector for decades.


A


JPMorganChase


mortgage product for factory-built homes would bring the nation’s largest bank lender into a space where ma


instream options are often limited. And with the ROAD to Housing Act now expanding FHA access and directing HUD to remove barriers for modular developers, federal policy and private capital are moving in the same direction at the same time.


Whether a dedicated


JPMorganChase


product will materialize – and when – is still an open question. But for an industry that has spent years waiting for mainstream lending to catch up, this is the most encouraging set of signals in a long time.
