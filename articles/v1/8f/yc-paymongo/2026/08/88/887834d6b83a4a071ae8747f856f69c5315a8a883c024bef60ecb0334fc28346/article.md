# Online Selling Terms in the Philippines: What UP, LF, MOP and 40+ More Actually Mean

You post a pair of sneakers online. Within minutes, the comments start rolling in.


*"UP."* *"LP?"* *"MOP?"* *"RFS?"*


If you're new to online selling in the Philippines, it can feel like everyone is speaking a different language. In a way, they are.


Over the years, Filipino sellers have developed their own shorthand across Facebook Marketplace, buy-and-sell groups, Instagram, TikTok Shop, and live selling. Knowing what these terms mean helps you respond faster, avoid misunderstandings, and keep buyers from dropping off before they complete a purchase.


This guide breaks down the online selling terms you'll see most often, starting with one of the most common.


## **What Does "UP" Mean in Online Selling?**


Quick answer: In Philippine online selling, **"UP"** means **"under post."** It's a comment used to bump a listing higher in Facebook's feed. Every new comment signals activity, making the post more likely to appear in front of other buyers.


Sellers use it to keep listings visible. Buyers use it to save a post they want to revisit later.


Here's why it works.


Facebook groups prioritize recent activity. As older posts get pushed down the feed, even a single comment can make a listing appear active again. Instead of creating a new post, sellers simply comment "UP" to bring it back into circulation.


You'll also come across a few variations:


- **UP** — the standard bump.
- **Pa-up** / **Up mo** — asking someone to bump the post.
- **UPP** / **UUP** — the same thing, just with extra emphasis.
- **Up for grabs** — different meaning entirely. It simply means the item is still available.


## **Why Buyers Comment "UP"**


It's not just sellers.


Many buyers comment "UP" to keep track of a listing. Once they've commented, Facebook sends them notifications whenever the seller replies or updates the post, making it easy to check back without searching for it again.


Some buyers also bump listings so friends or family can see them.


## **How to Reply to "UP"**


Don't waste the opportunity with a simple "Thanks."


Someone just helped put your listing back in front of more buyers. Use your reply to answer the questions people usually ask before they buy.


*“Thanks for the up! Still available. LP is ₱2,800. MOP: GCash, Maya, card, or bank transfer. Ships nationwide. You can pay here: \[payment link\]”*


In one reply, you've answered the three questions most buyers ask first and given them a way to check out immediately.


The fewer messages buyers need to send before paying, the more likely they are to complete the purchase.

## **MOP Is the Term That Decides Whether You Get Paid**


Scroll through any buy-and-sell group and you'll see the same question again and again:


"MOP?"


It stands for Method of Payment. By the time a buyer asks, they've usually decided they want the item. They're asking how to pay.


This is where many online sellers lose the sale. The typical reply looks something like this: a GCash number, a bank account, instructions to send a screenshot, then a wait while the seller checks if the payment arrived.


It works. Until you're handling dozens of orders. Every extra message slows the checkout. Every extra step gives buyers another chance to change their mind.


There's also the trust problem. Sending money to a stranger's personal account isn't something every buyer is comfortable with. Many would rather buy from someone who offers a proper checkout. A checkout page removes the back and forth.


The buyer opens a link, chooses GCash, Maya, cards, QR Ph, or online banking, and pays. You receive the payment automatically. No screenshots to verify, no receipts to match.

## **Frequently Asked Questions**


#### ****What is the difference between LP and SRP?****


LP is Lowest Price — the least the seller will accept, usually asked by buyers negotiating. SRP is Suggested Retail Price, the original store price, which sellers quote to show how big a discount they're offering.


#### ****What does RFS mean and why do buyers ask it?****


RFS means Reason For Selling. Buyers ask because the answer often reveals problems — a phone sold because of battery issues, or shoes sold because they don't fit. A vague RFS is a common warning sign in secondhand deals.
