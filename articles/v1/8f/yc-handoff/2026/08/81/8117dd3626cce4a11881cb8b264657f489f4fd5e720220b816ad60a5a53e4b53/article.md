# 9 Questions Contractors Ask Us Before They Buy, Answered Straight

Before a remodeler signs up for[AI estimating software](https://handoff.ai/blog/5-questions-to-consider-before-buying-an-estimating-software) , the same nine questions come up almost every time: will it work for my jobs, which plan do I need, what happens to my data, can it track my crew, and a handful more. Here are straight answers to all nine — no demo required, no dodging the ones where the honest answer is "not yet." If you're weighing whether[construction estimating software](https://handoff.ai/blog/ai-estimating-software-the-ultimate-guide-for-contractors-2026) fits a two-person shop, this is the list to read first.


## Which plan do I need, and what counts as an AI interaction?


One contractor thought his plan gave him 50 full jobs and found out it was 50 AI interactions which is a real gap worth clearing up before you sign.


- An interaction is a request to the AI, not a whole project. Building one estimate uses several.
- Low-volume shops bidding a handful of jobs a month fit a lighter plan. Daily users need room to run.
- Plan-set takeoffs sit on the top tier; lighter estimating and proposals sit lower.
- Match the plan to how often you actually bid, not to the longest feature list.


Check the current[plan breakdown and pricing](https://handoff.ai/pricing) before you decide. Handoff is built for contractors doing $500K to $3M a year, so there's a tier that fits without overpaying.


## What happens to my data if I switch software?


Contractors who've been burned by a five-CRM history ask this first, and they're right to.[SCORE](https://www.score.org/) has long advised small businesses to confirm data portability before committing to any platform.


- Your clients, projects, pricing, and history come with you. Migration is part of onboarding, not a DIY project.
- You can run your current tool and a new one side by side for a trial before moving anything for real.
- Ask any vendor the exit question too: if you ever leave, can you take your data out? A straight answer there tells you who to trust.


[Your AI Teammate](https://handoff.ai/ai-teammate) is meant to be the place everything finally lives in one spot, not another silo you have to escape later. Handoff keeps your business data yours.


## Will AI takeoffs work for my type of jobs?


- If it's drawn or written on the plans — dimensions, symbols, schedules, notes — AI reads it and carries it into the takeoff.
- Engineering calls that aren't on the prints, like snow loads or member sizing, stay with your engineer. Good software doesn't pretend to stamp drawings.
- One-of-a-kind custom work still needs your pricing. AI gets the measurements and base scope fast; your specialty numbers come from you.
- Residential plans up to 5,000 sqft are the sweet spot, which covers nearly every kitchen, bath, addition, deck, and custom home a small remodeler bids.


Knowing the limits is what makes the rest trustworthy. See the full breakdown in[what AI can and can't read on your plans](https://handoff.ai/ai-takeoffs) .


## Can it track my crew's hours?


Several contractors asked for geofenced clock-in — auto clock-in when a crew member hits the site, auto clock-out when they leave — to stop paying for early clocks and long lunches. Right now, dedicated time tracking isn't a Handoff feature. But not for long…


- If automated time tracking is a must-have today, you'll still need a standalone tool for it.
- It's on the roadmap because enough contractors have asked. You can track what's shipping on the[public roadmap](https://handoff.featurebase.app/roadmap) .
- What Handoff does capture is crew and material notes inside[daily logs](https://handoff.ai/daily-logs) , tied to the project.


We'd rather tell you plainly than oversell it. That's the same reason the estimates you do get are ones you can trust.


## How do I stop subs from nickel-and-diming me?


- A complete scope of work, so "that wasn't included" stops being a daily conversation.
- A material list attached to the agreement, so the one-screw mobilization charge has nowhere to hide.
- A bid request in one format, so three sub quotes actually compare.
- A running payment record per sub, so 1099 season is a print job.


[The National Association of Home Builders](https://www.nahb.org/nahb-community/member-benefits/savings/nahb-contracts) treats written agreements as the baseline with trade partners for exactly this reason. Full playbook here:[four documents that stop subs from nickel-and-diming you](https://handoff.ai/ai-documents) . Complete paperwork protects your margin from the first handshake.

## Can I give clients options without redoing the whole estimate?


Clients almost always ask to add the pocket door or price a nicer finish. Redoing the estimate three times kills the momentum. One remodeler runs every proposal as a three-column choice: as-drawn, value-engineered, and within-budget.


- Options turn a price objection into an upsell conversation instead of a discount.
- Generating variants from one estimate keeps your numbers consistent across all three.
- The client picks; you don't rebuild.


Contractors who present options close more jobs without lowering their base price.[Your AI Teammate builds the proposal](https://handoff.ai/winning-ai-proposals) once and lets the client choose from it.


## I have more work than crews — can software fix that?


Software won't hire for you, but it changes what your crew can carry. The hardest problem some remodelers face isn't leads. It's labor. No app hires a framer. What software does is take the office load off the owner so more of the day goes to the work only people can do.


- Every hour not spent estimating at night is an hour back for running jobs and finding crew.
- Systematized bids and follow-ups mean you can take on more without a bigger office team.
- [Harvard’s Joint Center for Housing Studies](https://www.jchs.harvard.edu/research-areas/remodeling/lira) tracks remodeling demand at over $500 billion a year — which means the constraint for most small shops is capacity, not work.


Handoff won't swing a hammer, but it frees the owner who's swinging too many.
