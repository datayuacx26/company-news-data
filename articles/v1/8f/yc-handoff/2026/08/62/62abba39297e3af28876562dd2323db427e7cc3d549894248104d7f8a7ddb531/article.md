## Buildertrend takeoffs vs Handoff AI Takeoffs comparison


## Buildertrend Takeoffs: What It Is


Buildertrend's takeoff module lets you upload blueprint PDFs into the platform, then use on-screen measurement tools to manually calculate material quantities. Once you've done the measuring, it generates a material list that connects directly to your estimates and purchase orders.


For teams already running Buildertrend across their projects, it's a logical place to handle takeoffs. Everything stays in one system, and the data flows downstream to procurement.


Buildertrend's own site makes the pitch plainly: takeoffs 33% faster than manual or paper-based methods. Take that claim at face value and notice what it tells you. The ceiling of this approach is measuring faster. Someone on your team still sits down with the plans and measures everything. The software digitizes that process. It doesn't replace it.


## When Buildertrend Takeoffs Make Sense


If you're running a mid-to-large residential construction company with a full Buildertrend implementation, a dedicated estimating team, and complex project flows that need tight integration between takeoff, procurement, and scheduling then Buildertrend's takeoff module makes sense. You're already paying for the platform.


It's a reasonable choice when you have the team to support it.


### The Gap: What Manual Still Costs You


For small-to-mid contractors, the[remodelers and GCs](https://www.handoff.ai/remodelers) doing 5 to 30 jobs a year, manual takeoff is a margin problem disguised as a workflow problem.


Think about what it actually costs:


- [8 to 16 hours](https://handoff.ai/ai-takeoffs) of skilled labor per takeoff, or your own evenings if you don't have staff
- Outsourced takeoff services typically run $700 to $1,000 per plan set, with a 1 to 2 week wait
- Slow turnaround means slower bids, which means[losing jobs to faster competitors](https://www.handoff.ai/blog/how-to-win-contracting-bids-everytime)
- Measurement errors mean scope gaps, which means losing margin mid-project


Measuring 33% faster shrinks the first number. It doesn't even touch the other three.
