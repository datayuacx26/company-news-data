# 6 Steps to Switch Construction Software Without Losing Your Data

*Built from Q2–Q3 2026 discovery calls with contractors evaluating a switch.*


One remodeler we talked to had switched software[five times](https://handoff.ai/blog/5-questions-to-consider-before-buying-an-estimating-software) . Each move cost him something a client list here and two years of pricing there, so by the sixth tool he'd stopped trusting any of them. It doesn't have to go that way: you can switch construction software without losing your data by exporting your records first, running the new tool alongside the old one before you commit, and confirming you can get your data back out if you ever leave. Do it in that order and you keep every client, every project, and every price, with no gap in the jobs you're running today.


## Why switching construction software feels risky


For another customer, he put it plainly: everything he'd built lived inside his current software, and the thought of moving it — or losing it — was the only thing keeping him from a tool he liked better. That scar tissue is common. Here’s why.


- Years of clients, pricing, and job history feel too heavy to move
- A bad migration could stall the jobs you're running right now
- Nobody wants to learn a sixth platform after five let them down
- The last vendor made leaving hard on purpose


[Capterra's software buying guide](https://www.capterra.com/resources/software-buyers-guide/) advises small-business owners to ask about data migration support and portability before committing to any platform. The goal is to make sure that if you make a switch, your data survives it.


## The 6 steps to switching platforms without losing all your data


### Step 1: inventory what you actually need to move


Before you export a thing, list what matters. Most of what clutters an old platform is noise you'll be glad to leave.


- [Clients and contacts](https://handoff.ai/blog/CRM--Save-your-Clients-in-Handoff) — names, phones, emails, addresses
- Active and recent projects, with their estimates and status
- Your pricing: rates, markups, templates, and any catalogs you've built
- Financial records tied to open jobs — invoices, payments, what's still owed


That short list is the[spine of your business](https://handoff.ai/blog/5-best-software-for-residential-remodeling-business) . Everything else is usually a fresh start you'll appreciate. Knowing what to move keeps the migration from becoming its own project.


### Step 2: export your data before you cancel anything


Never cancel the old subscription until your data is exported and confirmed. This is the step contractors skip when they're frustrated, and it bites.


- Pull contacts and clients to a spreadsheet — most tools export to CSV
- Export or download estimates, proposals, and invoices as files you keep
- Screenshot or save your pricing setup, markups, and templates so nothing's rebuilt from memory
- Back it all up somewhere that isn't the old platform


One contractor described his workaround for years: print it, scan it, save it to the iPad, lose it on the desktop, rebuild it in a spreadsheet. Exporting cleanly, once, ends that loop. Your data belongs to you before it belongs to any software.


### Step 3: run the new tool alongside the old one


The safest switch is an overlap. Keep the old system running the jobs already in it and start new bids in the new tool. A[free trial](https://app.handoff.ai/sign-up/) is built exactly for this.


- Start your next new estimate in the new platform and see how it feels on a real job
- Leave in-progress jobs where they are until they close
- Compare the new estimate against numbers you already know are right
- Move fully only once the new tool has earned it


Contractors who overlap for a few weeks switch with zero downtime, because nothing ever went dark.[Your AI Teammate](https://handoff.ai/ai-teammate) can be running your next bid before you've closed your last job in the old system.


### Step 4: rebuild your pricing so it comes out in your format


The reason estimates feel "off" in a new tool is almost always setup, not the software. One remodeler nearly walked away over outputs that didn't match his format until a setup session fixed it in minutes. Load your rules once and every estimate after that looks like your business.


- Enter your labor rates and markup rules so margins are protected automatically
- Upload your own[catalogs and price books](https://handoff.ai/creating-estimates-from-files) so[materials price at what you actually pay](https://handoff.ai/blog/announcing-handoff-home-depot-live-store-pricing-in-every-estimate)
- Save your templates so proposals come out in your layout, not a generic one
- Set your defaults so the AI stops guessing and starts matching how you work


[QuickBooks integration](https://handoff.ai/blog/handoff-now-integrates-with-quickbooks) keeps your accounting connected through the move, so the financial side doesn't fracture. You can even[track budget vs. actuals](https://handoff.ai/ai-budget-tracking) from inside the app. Handoff is meant to be the one place your project knowledge finally lives, instead of scattered across tools.


### Step 5: connect the tools you're keeping


Switching your estimating and project hub doesn't mean abandoning every other app the same week. \[Bridges\]([https://handoff.ai/blog/connect-handoff-to-every-tool-you-already-use.)](https://handoff.ai/blog/connect-handoff-to-every-tool-you-already-use.)) let you move at your own pace.


- Keep the accounting, calendar, or lead source you like and connect them
- Use[connected workflows](https://handoff.ai/zapier-integration) to pass leads and data between tools automatically
- Let a website form or lead source create a project on its own, so nothing's typed twice
- Retire the redundant tools only as the new hub proves it covers them
- Finally, if you have any agents you want to integrate, follow the steps for integrating it[through API Access](https://handoff.ai/api-access) .


Research published by[Construction Dive](https://www.constructiondive.com/spons/the-future-of-preconstruction-isnt-more-software-its-a-connected-platfor/812540/) shows the average contractor operates with 5–7 software systems across estimating and project workflows — the win is fewer tools that finally talk. A connected setup means less double entry and one place to look.


### Step 6: confirm you can get your data back out


The last step protects you from repeating the whole cycle later. Before you commit, ask any vendor the question the last one hoped you wouldn't: if I leave, can I take my data with me?


- Confirm you can export clients, projects, and financials on your own, any time
- Make sure migration help is part of onboarding, not a paid add-on you discover later
- A vendor that answers this straight is telling you how they'll treat you as a customer
