# Selling Professional Services on AWS Marketplace

*A professional services listing on AWS Marketplace is an offering for human-delivered work — assessments, implementation, training, or managed services — scoped and priced per engagement and transacted through a private offer, rather than provisioned self-serve like a SaaS or AMI product.*


---


Most people picture AWS Marketplace as a catalog of software you subscribe to and provision yourself. That’s most of it — but it isn’t all of it. The same marketplace that sells a SaaS contract can also sell the implementation project, the migration, the training program, and the managed service that surrounds it.


For ISVs and consulting partners, that opens a second motion: the work you already deliver off-platform can be transacted on the marketplace your customer already buys software through, against the cloud budget they’ve already committed to spend.


Here’s how professional services listings work, how they differ from software, and when they’re worth the effort.


---


## **What a professional services listing is**


**A professional services listing** is a marketplace offering for human-delivered work rather than software a buyer provisions themselves. It covers the engagements that surround a software purchase, and it’s transacted through the marketplace’s billing and agreement rails instead of a separate contract and invoice.


Services listings generally fall into four categories:


- **Assessments** : Scoped diagnostic engagements — a readiness review, an architecture assessment, a security or cost audit — that produce findings and a recommendation.
- **Implementation and professional services** : Deployment, configuration, integration and migration work that stands up a product or platform for the customer.
- **Training and enablement** : Structured programs that get a customer’s team productive on a product or technology.
- **Managed and support services** : Ongoing operation, monitoring or support delivered on a recurring basis rather than a one-time project.


Each is quote-based and scoped to a specific customer. That single fact is what makes services listings behave differently from everything else in the catalog.


---


## **How professional services differ from SaaS and AMI listings**


Professional services listings differ from SaaS or AMI listings in one structural way: they are custom-scoped and quote-based, so they’re delivered through a private offer rather than a self-serve public price. A buyer can’t click “subscribe” on a migration — the scope, timeline and price are negotiated first, then transacted.


That difference cascades into how the listing is built, priced and fulfilled.


Professional services listing SaaS / AMI listing


**What’s sold** Human-delivered work — a project, program or ongoing service Software the buyer provisions and runs themselves


**Pricing** Custom, per-engagement, quote-based Public price, or private offer on top of it


**Purchase path** Private offer, negotiated then accepted Self-serve subscribe, or private offer


**Fulfillment** Delivered by the seller off-platform; the marketplace handles billing and agreement Provisioned automatically through the platform


**Metering** Fixed scope or milestone-based Usage- or subscription-metered


The through-line: software is productized and self-serve; services are scoped and quote-based. The marketplace gives both the same billing, agreement and procurement rails — the fulfillment is what differs.


---


## **Who can list professional services**


Two kinds of sellers list professional services: consulting and services partners whose core business is delivery, and ISVs that bundle services around their own software. Eligibility runs through the AWS seller and partner programs, and services listings sit alongside your other offerings once you’re registered to sell.


For a consulting partner, the services listing *is* the product — the assessment, the migration, the managed service is what the customer is buying. For an ISV, services are usually the layer that makes the software succeed: the implementation that gets a buyer live, or the managed service that keeps them there. Both list through the same marketplace mechanics covered in our guide to[AWS Marketplace for sellers](https://www.suger.io/resources/blog/aws-marketplace-for-sellers-complete-guide/) , and both benefit from the same billing and procurement rails as software. The difference is only what gets delivered after the offer is accepted.


---


## **How buyers purchase professional services**


Buyers purchase professional services through a private offer: the seller scopes the engagement, sends a custom offer with the agreed price and terms, and the buyer accepts it through their AWS account. From that point the transaction behaves like any other marketplace purchase — it’s billed through AWS and appears on the customer’s consolidated cloud bill.


The reason this matters is the budget it draws against. Because the engagement transacts through AWS, it can count toward the customer’s committed cloud spend — including drawdown against an Enterprise Discount Program (EDP) commitment. A buyer with a large multi-year AWS commitment can apply your services engagement toward that number instead of finding fresh budget, which is often the difference between a project that gets approved this quarter and one that waits.


Setting this up is the same workflow as software: you build the offering as one of your[marketplace listings](https://www.suger.io/platform/product-listing/) , then extend[private offers for services](https://www.suger.io/platform/private-offers/) to named buyers with the scope and price you negotiated.

## **Use cases for services listings**


The strongest use cases pair a services listing with something the customer is already buying or committed to:


- **Bundle implementation with your software** : Sell the SaaS subscription and the deployment project as one marketplace transaction, so the buyer procures the outcome — software that’s live — not just the license.
- **Migrations and modernization** : Transact a cloud migration or re-platforming engagement against the customer’s AWS commitment, where the work and the resulting usage land on the same bill.
- **Managed services** : Offer ongoing operation or support as a recurring services engagement, giving the customer a single marketplace relationship for both the software and the team that runs it.
- **Training and enablement** : Attach a structured onboarding or certification program to a software deal so adoption is procured, not left to chance.


In each case, the services listing removes a second procurement cycle. The customer signs once, through a vendor they’ve already onboarded.


---


## **Benefits of routing services through the marketplace**


Routing professional services through AWS Marketplace gives you three things a separate contract can’t: access to committed cloud budget, a procurement path the customer has already approved, and consolidated billing that puts your engagement on the same invoice as their software. Together they shorten the path from scoped to signed.


For the buyer, the engagement draws on budget they’ve already set aside and clears procurement they’ve already run — no new vendor onboarding, no fresh purchase order. For the seller, the deal moves at marketplace speed instead of legal-and-invoicing speed, and it strengthens the same[AWS Marketplace](https://www.suger.io/solutions/aws-marketplace/) relationship your software already runs through. The services aren’t a separate business; they’re the same customer, transacted the same way.

## **Frequently asked questions**


**Can you sell professional services on AWS Marketplace?** Yes. AWS Marketplace supports professional services listings for assessments, implementation, training, and managed or support services. Unlike software, these are custom-scoped and quote-based, so they’re transacted through a private offer the buyer accepts rather than a self-serve subscription.


**How is a services listing different from a SaaS listing?** A SaaS listing sells software the buyer provisions themselves at a public or negotiated price. A services listing sells human-delivered work — scoped, quoted, and delivered by the seller off-platform. Both are billed through the marketplace, but only software is self-serve.


**Do professional services count toward committed cloud spend?** They can. Because the engagement transacts through AWS, it can draw down against the customer’s committed spend, including an Enterprise Discount Program (EDP) commitment. That’s often why buyers prefer to procure services through the marketplace rather than a separate contract.


**How do buyers purchase professional services?** Through a private offer. The seller scopes the engagement and sends a custom offer with agreed price and terms; the buyer accepts it through their AWS account. It then bills through AWS and appears on the customer’s consolidated cloud bill.


**Can ISVs bundle services with their software?** Yes. An ISV can sell a software subscription and its implementation, migration or managed service as marketplace transactions to the same buyer — so the customer procures a working outcome, not just a license, through one already-approved vendor relationship.
