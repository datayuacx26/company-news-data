# AWS Competencies: Worth the Work?

*The AWS Competency program is an AWS validation that recognizes partners for proven expertise in a specific industry, use case, or workload. It is worth the work when you have a repeatable motion in one of those areas and enough shippable customer evidence to pass the technical assessment — and it is a distraction when you are chasing the badge instead of the buyers it is meant to reach.*


---


Every alliances leader eventually gets the same question from a founder: “Should we go for an AWS Competency?” The honest answer is *it depends on what you already have* , but that answer never survives a board slide. So the badge gets added to a roadmap, a solutions architect gets pulled off the product for a quarter to write the application, and the return is assumed rather than reasoned.


The application is real work. The differentiation is real too — but only for the right company at the right stage. This is a decision framework for telling those apart before you commit the headcount, built from what AWS itself says the program requires and unlocks, so you are weighing effort against a documented benefit rather than a rumor.


---


## What is an AWS Competency?


**An AWS Competency is an AWS validation that “validates Partners who leverage AWS technology to solve complex industry and use-case specific challenges.”** AWS now presents these under the banner of **Specializations** — its own current framing describes them as a way for partners “to validate their expertise in building solutions or delivering services across industry, use cases or workloads.” Competency and Specialization refer to the same recognition; the newer word is the one on the program page.


The key phrase is *specific* . A Competency is not a general “we’re good at AWS” stamp — it names a domain (a security use case, a data-and-analytics workload, an industry vertical) and asserts you have demonstrated repeated success there. That specificity is exactly what makes it valuable when it fits and hollow when it doesn’t.


It also sits at a different layer from the AWS Partner Path. The Path is your engagement track and its stages — the[AWS ISV Partner Path, now the Software Path](https://www.suger.io/resources/blog/aws-isv-partner-path/) , progresses Registered to Validated to Differentiated. A Competency is a separate, domain-specific credential you can pursue *on top of* that progression. Confusing the two is the first mistake: reaching a Path stage is not the same as earning a Competency, and neither is a substitute for the other.


---


## What earning one actually costs


**The cost of an AWS Competency is a technical assessment plus documented customer evidence — real engineering and customer-success effort, not a form.** AWS states that partners complete “rigorous technical assessments including reviews of multiple customer success examples aligned to the Specialization they wish to be recognized for.” Read that literally, because each clause is a line item on your effort estimate.


- **A technical assessment.** AWS reviews your architecture and practices against the validation criteria for the specific Specialization. That means a solutions architect’s time to prepare evidence, and usually to remediate gaps the criteria surface before you can pass.
- **Multiple customer success examples.** Not one reference — *multiple* , and each aligned to the domain you are claiming. You need customers who used your product for that specific use case, who are willing to be written up, and whose deployments actually demonstrate the pattern AWS is validating.
- **Alignment work.** The examples and the architecture both have to map to *the Specialization* . Evidence that is impressive but off-domain does not count, which is why a company with lots of happy customers can still fail if none of them fit the category cleanly.


AWS does not publish a fixed dollar cost or a guaranteed timeline for a Competency application on the program page, so do not put an invented number in your business case. Estimate it the way you would any internal project: the SA hours to assemble and remediate, the customer-marketing hours to produce the write-ups, and the elapsed calendar time to schedule the review. The honest input is effort, not a fee.

## What AWS says it unlocks


**A Competency unlocks recognition and co-sell standing — badges, better placement, a stronger co-sell score, AWS Sales access, and funding eligibility — but no guaranteed revenue.** Here is the benefit list exactly as AWS frames it, separated from anything you might wish it also did.


What AWS says you get What it actually means for pipeline


**AWS Partner badges** A trust signal on your site and in AWS-facing materials that buyers and AWS sellers recognize


**Priority placement in Partner Solutions Finder** You surface higher when a buyer or AWS seller filters for that specific domain


**Improved Partner co-sell score** You look stronger to the AWS field, which affects whether AWS engages on your deals


**Direct AWS Sales team access** A path to AWS sellers and their pipeline, rather than cold outreach


**MDF and MAP funding eligibility** You become *eligible* to apply for Marketing Development Funds and Migration Acceleration Program funding — eligibility, not an allocation


Two cautions keep this honest. First, every one of these is a *better position* , not a booked outcome. A Competency improves how AWS’s field and buyers perceive you; it does not deposit revenue. Second, the funding benefit is eligibility — the money still runs through a separate request and approval, the same way the[AWS partner funding programs](https://www.suger.io/resources/blog/aws-partner-funding-programs/) do for any qualifying partner. AWS does not attach a dollar amount to the Competency itself, so treat funding as a door that opens, not a check that clears.


The reason to want these is co-sell. The badge and the Solutions Finder placement matter because they feed the AWS field motion — the same motion an effective[AWS co-sell program](https://www.suger.io/platform/cosell/) is built to accelerate. A Competency is worth most to a company that already has, or is building, a real co-sell habit for the field to plug into.

## The decision framework


**A Competency is worth the work when the domain is your actual motion and you can already evidence it; it is not when you are chasing the badge to look credible.** Run your situation through these five questions before you commit an SA-quarter. The more you answer “yes,” the stronger the case.


1. **Do you have a repeatable motion in this exact domain?** A Competency validates a *specific* industry, use case, or workload. If your wins are scattered across unrelated categories, you will struggle to assemble aligned evidence — and even a successful badge will point buyers at a domain you don’t consistently deliver.
2. **Can you produce multiple aligned customer examples today?** The requirement is real customer-success stories in the domain, ready to document. If you would have to manufacture references or wait for deployments to mature, you are not ready — you are early, and the effort will stall on evidence you don’t yet have.
3. **Will the AWS field motion actually reach your buyers?** The core benefit is co-sell standing: Solutions Finder placement, co-sell score, AWS Sales access. If your buyers don’t come through AWS sellers or search that catalog, the differentiation lands where your demand isn’t.
4. **Can you spare the technical assessment effort without stalling the product?** The assessment pulls senior engineering time for preparation and remediation. If that same SA is the only person who can ship your roadmap this quarter, the opportunity cost may exceed the badge’s value right now.
5. **Are you doing this for buyers, or for the slide?** The failure mode is pursuing a Competency as a credibility trophy rather than to unlock a motion. If you cannot name the specific deals the badge would help you win, that is a signal to wait.


If you answered “yes” to the first three, a Competency is likely worth it and the remaining two are about timing. If you are stuck on questions 1 and 2, the right move is not to apply harder — it is to build the repeatable domain motion and the customer evidence first, and let the Competency follow the traction rather than precede it.

## Frequently asked questions


**What is the AWS Competency program?** The AWS Competency program is an AWS validation that recognizes partners for proven expertise in a specific industry, use case, or workload. AWS now presents these as Specializations. Earning one requires a technical assessment and multiple aligned customer-success examples.


**Is an AWS Competency worth the effort?** It is worth it when you have a repeatable motion in the exact domain and enough customer evidence to pass the assessment, and when your buyers come through the AWS field. It is a distraction when you are pursuing the badge for credibility rather than a specific co-sell motion.


**What does an AWS Competency require?** AWS states partners complete rigorous technical assessments, including reviews of multiple customer-success examples aligned to the Specialization they want to be recognized for. That means solutions-architect time for the assessment and remediation, plus documented customers in the specific domain.


**What does an AWS Competency unlock?** AWS names Partner badges, priority placement in Partner Solutions Finder, an improved co-sell score, direct AWS Sales team access, and eligibility for MDF and MAP funding. Each is a stronger position with the AWS field, not guaranteed revenue, and funding is eligibility rather than an allocation.


**How is a Competency different from an AWS Partner tier?** A Partner Path and its tiers describe your engagement track and progression, such as Registered, Validated, and Differentiated on the Software Path. A Competency is a separate, domain-specific credential you pursue on top of that progression, and reaching a tier does not earn one.


**Does an AWS Competency come with funding?** It makes you eligible to apply for Marketing Development Funds and Migration Acceleration Program funding, but AWS attaches no dollar amount to the Competency itself. The funding still runs through a separate request and approval process, so treat it as a door that opens rather than a check that clears.


## Takeaways


- An AWS Competency (now called a Specialization) validates expertise in a **specific** industry, use case, or workload — it is not a general “good at AWS” stamp.
- The cost is real: a **technical assessment** plus **multiple aligned customer-success examples** , which is senior engineering and customer-marketing effort, not a form. AWS publishes no fixed fee or timeline, so don’t invent one.
- What it unlocks is **position, not revenue** : badges, Partner Solutions Finder placement, a better co-sell score, AWS Sales access, and funding *eligibility* — the money still runs through a separate request.
- It sits **on top of** your Partner Path stage, not instead of it; reaching a tier does not earn a Competency.
- Apply when you have the domain motion and the evidence *today* , and when your buyers come through the AWS field. If you’re chasing the badge for the slide, build the traction first and let the Competency follow.
