# From Benefit to Employee Strategy: Purpose Brands’ Story

Crisis, change, and stress are part of every employee’s life. What sets organizations apart is how they approach mental wellness—because the right strategy can make all the difference.


Jess Schneider knows this well. After her organization partnered with Modern Health, she found herself asking a question every HR leader eventually asks: We have a great benefit, but are we maximizing our investment?


That question became the catalyst for a much bigger shift in how she and her team think about wellbeing at Purpose Brands—moving mental health support from a line item on a budget to a true part of how people live and work.


"As a people leader, it's not just our job to provide perks," Schneider[explained in a recent webinar](https://join.modernhealth.com/from-benefits-to-workforce-od.html) with Mary Odafe, Ph.D., Clinical Psychologist, Clinical and Scientific Affairs Liaison at Modern Health. "It's to help people through some of the most important moments in their lives."


‍


## **Getting to Know Purpose Brands**


‍


Jess Schneider is the Senior Director of People Operations at Purpose Brands, the parent company of fitness and wellness franchises including Orangetheory Fitness, Anytime Fitness, Waxing the City, and The Bar Method.


Many Purpose Brands employees work remotely, while others are based in the Twin Cities, Boca Raton, and Seattle. Most are Millennials who want their work to matter. When their communities face a crisis, they feel it—and it shows up at work.


Purpose Brands has had plenty to navigate over recent years: COVID, the murder of George Floyd (just miles from the company’s Twin Cities headquarters), and a major merger. The company’s more than five-year partnership with Modern Health has seen its employees through it all, and that relationship has steadily reshaped how Schneider and her team think about well-being.


‍


## **From Benefit to Strategy: Making the Shift**


‍


Helping employees become the best version of themselves is a priority at Purpose Brands. But turning that value into action is not always straightforward.


“I’m sure many of you sitting here listening are like, ‘Jess, you’re a fitness and wellness company. How do you not have this figured out? This is in your DNA,’” Schneider says. “But honestly, we didn’t.”


Schneider is not alone. Even organizations that prioritize wellbeing and offer strong health benefits often struggle to connect the dots between intention and impact.


For Schneider, the turning point was realizing that treating Modern Health as a standalone benefit limited its impact. When Purpose Brands added a new engagement survey question—”Purpose Brands cares about my wellbeing”—it quickly became one of the strongest predictors of employee engagement and advocacy.


** At the same time, Schneider imagined the question any CFO would ask: *it’s a great benefit, but are we maximizing the investment?*


That question was the catalyst. Schneider began mapping Modern Health’s support to the real-life moments of change and tension employees face, uncovering new ways to serve her people.


Several key changes followed. One of them was extending coverage to dependents. “This has been a game-changer for our employees,” she says. “They still don't believe \[me\] when I tell them, ‘Your dependents have access too.’”


Since then, Schneider and her team have focused on weaving mental health into the fabric of company culture—moving from a checkbox to a true employee experience strategy.


‍


## **What This Looks Like in Practice**


‍


When leaders shift their mindset from offering benefits to shaping the employee experience, it becomes clear where support matters most. As Dr. Odafe notes, it is not that mental health issues suddenly appear, but that moments of change and growth amplify stress and uncertainty.


Here are a few ways Purpose Brands has made support feel less clinical and more like a natural part of everyday culture.

## **Vendor or Partner? Leveraging Benefits Under Pressure**


‍


The real test of any benefit is how it performs when things get tough. All the features in the world will not matter if HR leaders are left to handle the hardest moments alone.


The right partner can make a crisis more manageable.


Schneider has found this to be true, and two incidents in particular stand out in her mind. The first was a school shooting that shook her local community. The second was the unexpected loss of an employee. In both cases, Schneider's client success manager, Sara, responded with pre-drafted resources, tools, and emails Schneider could use to support her employees, then helped her think through ongoing support in the weeks that followed.


To Schneider, those actions represented the difference between hiring a vendor and working with a partner.


“One of the things that I value the most about our partnership with Modern Health is that I'm not alone,” she says. “Having this partnership allows me to be an HR leader and support our employees, but it also allows me to be a human, and you can't ask for anything more than that.”

## **Purpose Brands’ 6-Step Mental Health Playbook**


‍


You do not need a huge budget or dozens of new programs to create real change in your wellness strategy. No matter your team’s size, you can start with these simple steps Schneider recommends:


1. **Become obsessed:** Immerse yourself in research; find out what your employees are asking for, what your benefit offers, and how closely the two align.
2. **Look at the data:** Analyze what services your employees use most and when, as well as the problems they’re dealing with.
3. **Map mental health across the employee lifecycle:** Examine the employee journey from start to finish, to identify lifecycle milestones that warrant support.
4. **Integrate wellbeing into learning and development:** Weave resources into goal-setting, performance reviews, leadership training, and coaching conversations.
5. **Build a communications strategy:** Plan ahead to promote benefits year-round through events, activities, and companywide comms.
6. **Find your champions:** Look for employees and leaders who can share real-life stories about how their benefit has impacted them or their loved ones.


“Embedding mental health into the employee experience isn't necessarily about adding more because I know that that stresses folks out, right?” Dr. Odafe says. “Really, it's just about being more intentional about where that support shows up and how.”


Start small and focus on what you can actually achieve. Stay curious about the benefits you already have and build a real partnership with your vendor.

‍


It is easy to look at other organizations and feel behind. The reality is, making work safer and more supportive is ongoing. There is always room to grow.
