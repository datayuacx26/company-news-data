# 7 Signs Your EAP Needs a Modern Upgrade

Although **98% of large employers in the U.S. offer an EAP** , only about[4%](https://mhanational.org/resources/how-can-we-promote-our-eap-to-increase-its-usage/) **of employees use it** —and at some organizations, utilization falls as low as[1%](https://www.businessgrouphealth.org/-/media/bgh/documents/download-pdf/eap-survey-2022-ppt---final.pdf) .


Why do so few employees engage with a benefit that’s nearly universal?


**The reality is, the workforce has evolved, but traditional EAPs largely haven't.**


EAPs became the foundation of employer-sponsored mental health support in the 1970s, typically offering short-term counseling for specific, acute issues. For decades, that model worked—particularly for employees navigating substance use concerns or clinical crises.


But today’s employees face sustained pressure, blurred work-life boundaries, and ongoing stress that doesn’t always rise to the level of crisis. When they turn to their EAP for support, they often encounter complex phone trees, administrative hurdles, long wait times, and limited options for non-urgent care. As a result, only[36% of employees](https://explore.modernhealth.com/2025-trends/elevate-insights-march-2025?utm_source=why-its-time-to-rethink-the-traditional-eap&utm_medium=blog&utm_campaign=flex-eap&utm_content=elevate-insights-march-2025&utm_term=in-text-link&_gl=1*1ejzkuf*_gcl_au*MjQwMjQzNjk5LjE3NjIyMDQ4MTU.) **believe their workplace benefits provide adequate mental health support** .


While EAPs still play an important role, employers who want to meet employee needs—and demonstrate real value—must be willing to reassess whether their current model is still fit for purpose.


## **7 Signs It May Be Time to Reevaluate Your EAP**


Despite these challenges, more than[80% of U.S. employers](https://www.businessgrouphealth.org/-/media/bgh/documents/download-pdf/eap-survey-2022-ppt---final.pdf) **still offer a traditional EAP** , and[30% plan to switch](https://www.businessgrouphealth.org/-/media/bgh/documents/download-pdf/eap-survey-2022-ppt---final.pdf) **EAP vendors within the next two years** .


If you’re unsure whether change is necessary for your organization, the signals below can help identify where risk, inefficiency, or missed value may exist.


### **1. Utilization remains consistently low**


Low utilization is often attributed to stigma or lack of awareness—factors employers can address through education and culture. But when utilization stays low despite sustained communication, it often signals deeper structural issues.


Complicated enrollment processes, outdated care modalities, and misalignment with employee preferences are difficult to fix without modernizing the program itself. Younger employees, in particular, often prefer digital-first care such as virtual sessions, asynchronous messaging, or self-guided tools.


Yet few traditional EAPs support these expectations. Only **37% allow employees to text their provider** , and **38% offer in-app messaging** , according to[Business Group on Health research](https://www.businessgrouphealth.org/-/media/bgh/documents/download-pdf/eap-survey-2022-ppt---final.pdf#:~:text=Average%20number%20of%20visits%20per,that%20used%20the%20EAP%20at) .


No matter how well an EAP is promoted, outdated systems will continue to suppress engagement.


### **2. Employees struggle to access timely care**


Employees wait an average of[4.9 days](https://www.businessgrouphealth.org/-/media/bgh/documents/download-pdf/eap-survey-2022-ppt---final.pdf#:~:text=Average%20number%20of%20visits%20per,that%20used%20the%20EAP%20at) for an EAP appointment, with some waiting up to[three weeks](https://www.businessgrouphealth.org/-/media/bgh/documents/download-pdf/eap-survey-2022-ppt---final.pdf#:~:text=Average%20number%20of%20visits%20per,that%20used%20the%20EAP%20at) . The longer the delay, the greater the risk that mental health concerns worsen—particularly in high-stress or crisis situations.


While long wait times are often framed as unavoidable, they’re typically the result of legacy models that rely on phone-based intake, limited provider availability, and manual clinician matching.


For employers, slow access doesn’t just affect employee experience—it increases the likelihood of escalation, disengagement, and downstream costs.


### **3. Limited insight into outcomes or impact**


Disjointed systems and limited reporting make it difficult for many EAPs to demonstrate effectiveness. As a result, only[39% of employers](https://production-askearn-org.s3.amazonaws.com/FINAL_EAP_report_2008highres_3586beb22f.pdf) **analyze ROI for their EAP** , leaving many leaders unsure whether their investment is driving meaningful outcomes.


While employers can track metrics like absenteeism or turnover internally, an EAP should be able to report on:


- Clinical outcomes
- Engagement and utilization trends
- Service mix and care journeys
- Breakdowns by region, role, or demographic


Without this visibility, it’s nearly impossible to refine strategy or prove value.


### **4. Provider access varies widely by geography**


Few traditional EAPs offer consistent coverage across all regions. Global employers often rely on multiple vendors, creating fragmented data and uneven care experiences.


This patchwork approach can result in inequity—employees in certain countries may face longer waits, fewer modality options, or lower-quality care. Over time, these inconsistencies undermine employer commitments to equity and global workforce standards.


For organizations competing for talent worldwide, consistency in mental health support is no longer optional.


### **5. Care isn’t culturally aligned**


Research shows therapy is[twice as effective](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11119503/) **when delivered in a person’s native language** , and cultural norms strongly influence how individuals seek and experience care, according to[the U.S. Surgeon General](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK44249/) .


Yet[fewer than 60%](https://www.businessgrouphealth.org/-/media/bgh/documents/download-pdf/eap-survey-2022-ppt---final.pdf) **of EAPs allow employees to choose providers based on identity factors** such as language, gender, or race.[Only 43%](https://www.businessgrouphealth.org/-/media/bgh/documents/download-pdf/eap-survey-2022-ppt---final.pdf) **offer anti-stigma training** , and[just 31%](https://www.businessgrouphealth.org/-/media/bgh/documents/download-pdf/eap-survey-2022-ppt---final.pdf) **require cultural competency training** for providers.


Without culturally aligned care, access alone isn’t enough to drive meaningful outcomes.


### **6. Care ends after a fixed number of sessions**


Most EAPs cap support at three to 12 sessions per incident, with six being[the average](https://www.businessgrouphealth.org/-/media/bgh/documents/download-pdf/eap-survey-2022-ppt---final.pdf) . While this may work for some needs, it leaves employees requiring longer-term support navigating fragmented referrals or entirely new benefit systems.


When care stops abruptly, employees often disengage altogether—continuing to struggle at work while costs quietly accumulate.


### **7. Employees need more than crisis intervention**


Traditional EAPs are designed for crisis response, but they’re rarely designed for prevention. While nearly all offer crisis counseling and hotlines, far fewer provide coaching ([63%](https://www.businessgrouphealth.org/-/media/bgh/documents/download-pdf/eap-survey-2022-ppt---final.pdf) ) or support groups ([38%](https://www.businessgrouphealth.org/-/media/bgh/documents/download-pdf/eap-survey-2022-ppt---final.pdf) ).


By the time challenges reach crisis levels, they’re harder—and more expensive—to address. Preventive models that offer varied pathways can support employees earlier and reduce escalation over time.


## **What It Means to “Modernize an EAP”**


Modernizing an EAP doesn’t mean abandoning the model entirely. It means preserving what works while evolving what no longer serves today’s workforce or business realities.


A modernized EAP typically:


### **1. Preserves valuable EAP fundamentals**


Crisis support and therapy remain critical, particularly for cost-conscious employers. Modernization focuses on *how* care is delivered—not eliminating these foundations.


### **2. Expands care options and equity, globally**


Beyond crisis care, modern solutions support a range of needs and preferences, including:


- Clinical therapy
- Mental health and career coaching
- Support groups tailored to life stages or shared experiences
- Self-guided digital resources across mental, physical, relational, and financial well-being


Access should be consistent globally, with culturally competent providers available across regions.


### **3. Integrates reporting and consultative guidance**


Modernized EAPs offer real-time, global reporting and expert interpretation—helping employers understand trends, identify emerging needs, and optimize benefits strategy over time.
