# Application Spotlight: Meeting the growing and diverse market demand for e-bikes with accurate sensing

## Background


Demand for electric bicycles – e-bikes – has skyrocketed, and all indications are that it will continue to be a fast-growing market. In many regions, consumers began adopting ebikes as an affordable, sustainable and convenient alternative for short car or public transportation journeys. Then came the pandemic and the worldwide explosion of demand for leisure and sports equipment. And in 2022, with the ongoing war in Ukraine, gas prices rose dramatically, adding more demand for less expensive, less-polluting ways to get around. All this has coincided with falling prices for Li-ion batteries. These trends have created new opportunities for e-bike manufacturers worldwide and especially in Europe, where they are commonly used for delivery in addition to personal transport and hobbies like mountain biking.


As the market expands, e-bikes with varied types of motors and capabilities are needed to meet various consumer needs and price ranges. E-bikes based on pedal assist (also called pedelec) technology are the most common. Pedal assist motors are light and compact, sitting within the hollow bicycle shaft. They include both electronics (PCBAs) and torque sensors for sensing how much force the rider is contributing and how much needs to be added.


The torque sensor can use strain gauge or magneto-restrictive technology. With VPG’s leadership in strain gauge technology, we offer e-bike manufacturers numerous advantages in terms of both performance and logistics.


## The challenge


Since e-bikes are battery operated, low power consumption is a key requirement for the torque sensor, along with high accuracy, ease of calibration, and low susceptibility to noise. This, in turn, impacts the impedance and resistance requirements for the torque sensor strain gauges.


E-bikes usually require four bonded strain gauges weighing mere milligrams each, although high-end bikes often have eight, to enable higher levels of accuracy. The strain gauges in an e-bike meet a variety of needs and each type has unique specifications.


E-bike manufacturers often use different suppliers for each component in the motor—the PCBA, the strain gauges, communication technology, and cables—using 5-6 suppliers to source what can be 50+ parts. Integrating the various parts from different suppliers can be a challenge. Furthermore, severe supply chain problems mean that it isn’t always possible to source all the parts needed on demand, especially parts that are produced internationally.
