# Tips for Rep Payees to Help Manage Client Spending Objections

In serving as a Representative Payee (Rep Payee),[your primary role](https://www.truelinkfinancial.com/blog/rep-payee-101-what-they-do-who-they-help-and-how-to-become-one) is dictated by the[Social Security Administration](https://www.ssa.gov/payee/) (SSA) to ensure a client’s basic needs—shelter, food, clothing, and medical care—are met first. However, your clients may not always share your budget-first philosophy.


Whether it’s a request for an electronics purchase when the rent is due or perhaps frustration over the $2,000 Supplemental Security Income (SSI) resource limit, objections are a natural part of the job.


Let’s walk through tips for how best to handle these difficult situations with your clients.


## **1. Start with transparency**


Most client objections stem from a feeling of powerlessness in their decisions. If a beneficiary feels their money is being controlled without their say, they may be more likely to push back or become frustrated.


- **Show the math:** Sit down with your client and have a conversation, walking them through the numbers and reasoning why.
- **The bucket visual:** Talk them through the priority of where money needs to go first:


- **Bucket 1:** Housing and Utilities (Non-negotiable)
- **Bucket 2:** Food and Medical
- **Bucket 3:** Personal Comfort and Discretionary.


- **The paper trail:** Regularly share bank statements or simple spending reports with your clients to build trust. When a client sees that **$800** went to rent and **$300** to groceries, the amount of money available becomes more real, which can help alleviate any suspicion. ## **2. Practice reframing**


When a client asks for something outside the budget, often just saying no could make them feel left out or even trigger a defensive response. Using the "Yes, if" strategy instead of a "No, because" could lead to a more understanding outcome.


***Example:*** *"I understand how much you want that new gaming console.* ***Yes*** *, we can definitely look at getting that,* ***if*** *we can save about $50 a month from your personal spending budget over the next few months."*


By validating their desire for a purchase instead of disregarding it, you can shift from being a gatekeeper of what they want to being more of a financial coach helping them reach an important goal of theirs.


## **3. Rely on the SSA as a backstop**


Sometimes being a Rep Payee means that you may run into situations where you have to deliver news a client doesn’t want to hear. In these cases, it’s helpful to remind your client that you are bound by federal law in how you manage their benefits.


If a client is looking to spend their savings in a way that risks their benefits eligibility, reiterating to them that the[SSA has strict rules](https://www.truelinkfinancial.com/blog/important-changes-to-ssi-benefits-every-rep-payee-should-know) you have to follow for spending decisions could help clear the air of any lingering frustration. (Navigating the SSA itself can be a complicated process, but[these tips](https://www.truelinkfinancial.com/blog/how-rep-payees-can-work-effectively-social-security-administration?_gl=1*rdbp1n*_up*MQ..&gclid=CjwKCAjwnZfPBhAGEiwAzg-VzC2iWEf8okavAOSqOABCH0S7ljclmX-AR0brCHKZoujXbJkI2uKu7BoC4KwQAvD_BwE&gbraid=0AAAAAB0m6mj33dfezPk6W0DYJLJGe_mah) could help you work more effectively with them.)


## **4. Create small wins with discretionary funds**


Lack of independence or control is often the main root of a client’s frustration. To help mitigate this, ensure your client has control of a set amount of discretionary money they can use as they please, provided their bills are paid. Even if it’s only $20 or $50 a week, having this type of[autonomy over a portion of their funds](https://www.truelinkfinancial.com/blog/enabling-an-adult-with-a-disability-to-spend-their-own-money-and-increase-their-independence-with-true-link) can help significantly reduce friction regarding the larger, managed portion of funds, and improve your relationship with them in the long term. ## **5. Document each interaction**


Be sure to always keep written documentation on hand. This documentation helps keep memories fresh and will aid you in the event a dispute between you and your client does arise. It is best to keep notes contemporaneously with the meeting instead of saving the note-taking to some later date. Some important information to keep track of is:


- The date of the request
- The specific nature of the objection
- The reason for your denial (e.g., "Rent was overdue")
- The alternative solution you offered


This doesn't just help you stay organized, it helps protect you and your client if questions come up later with the SSA.
