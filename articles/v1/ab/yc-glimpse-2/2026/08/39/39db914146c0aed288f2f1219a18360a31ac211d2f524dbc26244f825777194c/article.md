## What Are Chewy Vendor Deductions?


Chewy vendor deductions are amounts Chewy withholds directly from a vendor payment when a shipment, document, or compliance requirement falls short of their routing guide. If your invoice is $10,000 and Chewy's remittance comes in at $9,200, that $800 gap is a deduction.


These are B2B financial penalties, not consumer chargebacks. There's no shopper complaint and no credit card network involved. The deductions are built into your vendor agreement and applied automatically, often before you know something went wrong.


By the time a short-pay hits your AR, Chewy has already made its call and moved on.


### The Most Common Deduction Types Pet Brands Face


Most Chewy deductions fall into a handful of recurring categories. Knowing which type you're dealing with determines whether you have a legitimate dispute or an internal process to fix.


- Shortage deductions occur when Chewy claims they received fewer units than your invoice reflects. These are among the most frequently invalid deductions and typically require proof of delivery and carrier documentation to dispute.
- Compliance chargebacks are assessed when a shipment violates Chewy's routing guide: a labeling issue, late delivery, or incorrect carton configuration. Other major retailers follow a similar model; see how[disputing Walmart deductions](https://www.tryglimpse.com/post/disputing-walmart-deductions-step-by-step) compares.
- Price discrepancy deductions happen when Chewy's purchase order doesn't match your invoice, often due to promotional pricing or cost change timing gaps.
- Dating and terms deductions arise from disputes over payment terms or invoice dating, and they tend to surface in high-volume periods when paperwork volume outpaces review.


## How Chewy's Vendor Model Creates Deductions


Chewy operates as a first-party retailer, which means when you sell to Chewy, you're selling to Chewy directly. They buy your inventory, set the retail price, and manage the customer relationship. That structure gives Chewy real pricing power over vendors, and it shows up in the deductions they take.


Chewy's vendor model generates deductions across a few recurring categories:


- [Freight and routing guide violations](https://www.tryglimpse.com/post/freight-charge-forensics-an-expert-guide-to-reclaiming-lost-revenue) occur when shipments don't meet Chewy's carrier, labeling, or delivery window requirements. Even minor deviations from their routing guide can trigger a chargeback.
- Shortage deductions are taken when Chewy claims they received fewer units than were invoiced. These are among the most disputed deduction types across all retailers, and Chewy is no exception.
- Co-op and marketing fees are often billed as deductions instead of separate invoices. That makes them easy to miss in standard AP reconciliation, and easy to let expire uncontested.
- New item setup and compliance fees get assessed when product data, packaging specs, or labeling don't meet Chewy's requirements at time of setup or during routine audits.


The volume of these deductions scales with your revenue. A brand doing $2M annually on Chewy can absorb them manually. A brand doing $20M will see them compound fast, and the window to recover closes whether or not your team is keeping up.


## The Main Types of Chewy Vendor Deductions


Chewy runs a deduction-heavy vendor program, and the charges fall into a few distinct buckets. Knowing which type you're dealing with shapes how you dispute it and whether you have a realistic shot at recovery.


### Shortage Deductions


These are the most common and the most contested. Chewy claims it received fewer units than invoiced and deducts the difference. Industry data puts the invalid or preventable rate for shortage deductions at[40 to 60% across retail](https://packagex.io/blog/how-to-prevent-retail-chargebacks) , typically tied to receiving errors or mismatched documentation instead of product that was actually missing.


### Advertising and Marketing Deductions


Co-op fees, promotional allowances, and sponsored placement charges. Some are contractually agreed upon. Others get applied without clear authorization, and those unauthorized deductions are recoverable.


### Compliance Deductions


Chargebacks for labeling issues, late shipments, fill rate misses, or EDI errors. These carry some of the stricter deadlines and often require backup documentation to contest effectively.


### Price Discrepancy Deductions


Chewy deducts when its purchase order price and your invoice price don't match. These can stem from outdated POs or internal pricing errors on either side.

## Shortage Deductions on Chewy: How They Work and Why They Stick


Chewy's shortage process works like this: they receive a shipment, count the units, and file a claim if their count falls below your invoiced quantity. For pet food brands, heavy items and multi-carton pallets see a higher rate of discrepancy claims because of how product moves in transit: weight shifts, cartons get miscounted, and receiving errors happen.


The documentation is what decides these disputes. A signed Bill of Lading from your carrier, matched against the corresponding invoice, gives you a concrete basis to push back. Without both documents aligned, the deduction holds regardless of what you actually shipped.

## Spoilage and Expiration Claims on Pet Food


Spoilage and expiration claims are one of the more frustrating deduction categories pet brands face, because the charges often arrive months after the product left your warehouse.


Chewy issues these deductions when pet food or treats approach or exceed their best-by dates while sitting in Chewy's fulfillment network. The problem is that responsibility for that expiration is rarely clear-cut. If the product arrived at Chewy with adequate shelf life remaining and sat in their warehouse too long, the deduction is invalid.


When these claims hit your account, track three things:


- Confirm the manufacture and best-by dates on the specific lot numbers Chewy references. If the product shipped with sufficient shelf life and expired in their custody, dispute it because you have grounds.
- Pull your shipping documentation and match it against the claim date. A long gap between receipt and the deduction posting is a clear signal the product sat in Chewy's network longer than your agreed shelf life window.
- Check your vendor agreement for any shelf life requirements Chewy imposes at the time of shipment. If you met those requirements, document it.


These disputes require lot-level traceability and clean shipping records. Brands that manage inventory documentation loosely write these off as unavoidable costs. They aren't: the dollars are recoverable, and they add up fast in a category where product turns can be slower on certain SKUs.

## The Documentation Stack That Wins Chewy Deductions Disputes


Each Chewy deduction dispute lives or dies on the backup you attach. Without the right documents, even a legitimate dispute gets denied, and you're left writing off revenue that was never actually owed.


Here's what a winning documentation stack looks like:


- The original purchase order and invoice, matched line by line to what Chewy received and what they're now claiming as a shortage or pricing discrepancy.
- Your proof of delivery, including carrier confirmation and pallet counts, which directly counters shortage deductions where the claim doesn't align with what was actually shipped.
- Any promotional agreements or deal sheets that confirm the pricing Chewy was charged, covering you against unauthorized deduction claims tied to pricing disputes.

One thing brands consistently miss: Chewy's dispute portal requires documents in specific formats. Submissions with the wrong file type or incomplete attachments get rejected outright without any notice. That rejection does not reset your dispute clock.
