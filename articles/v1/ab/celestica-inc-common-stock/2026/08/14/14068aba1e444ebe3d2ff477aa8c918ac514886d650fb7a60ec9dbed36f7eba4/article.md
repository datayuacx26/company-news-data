# Intelligent Infrastructure: Integrating Security Scanning into Building Controls

WRITTEN BY


Celestica


share


- LinkedIn


2026-08-13


As governments and critical infrastructure operators respond to growing geopolitical uncertainty, industrial security has become a boardroom critical priority. Around the world, investments in physical security, infrastructure resilience, and operational continuity are accelerating as organizations seek to protect facilities, assets, personnel, and supply chains from an increasingly complex threat landscape.


Industrial facilities are becoming smarter, more connected, and more automated. Building management systems, industrial automation platforms, security technologies, and operational technology (OT) environments are generating vast amounts of data that can be used to improve both safety and performance.


The scale of this transformation is significant. The[global building automation system market](https://www.psmarketresearch.com/market-analysis/building-automation-system-market) is projected to grow from approximately $115 billion in 2025 to $220 billion by 2030, driven by increasing adoption of intelligent controls, connected devices, analytics platforms, and integrated infrastructure technologies.


These trends are converging in a powerful way.


What was once a clear distinction between facility management and security operations is beginning to disappear. Modern industrial facilities are evolving toward intelligent infrastructure environments where AI-powered monitoring, advanced sensing technologies, and building controls work together to provide continuous visibility across people, assets, and operations.


The result is a new generation of facilities designed to operate efficiently and continuously verify, assess, and respond to potential risks in real time.


## The Evolution of Building Controls


Building management systems have traditionally focused on environmental and operational functions such as:


- HVAC control
- Fire and life safety systems
- Power management
- Lighting systems
- Occupancy management
-


Access control


These systems were designed to optimize comfort, efficiency, and facility performance.


Today, however, building controls are becoming much more than operational management tools. Advances in connectivity, edge computing, AI, and industrial sensing are transforming building management systems into centralized intelligence platforms that integrate information across an entire facility.


As organizations invest in intelligent infrastructure, building controls are serving as the foundation for broader operational awareness, connecting infrastructure, equipment, security systems, and personnel monitoring into a unified environment. The rapid growth of the building automation market reflects this shift, as organizations look to leverage connected technologies for efficiency gains and improved resilience, visibility, and risk management.


Modern smart buildings serve as the foundation for broader operational awareness, connecting infrastructure, equipment, security systems, and personnel monitoring into a unified environment.


For industrial organizations, this convergence creates new opportunities to improve both operational resilience and physical security.


## Security Scanning Becomes Part of the Operational Environment


Security technologies have traditionally operated independently from facility management systems.


Video surveillance, access controls, inspection equipment, and security personnel often functioned within separate operational silos, limiting the ability to correlate security data with broader facility operations.


That model is rapidly changing.


Today's industrial facilities are deploying AI-enabled monitoring technologies such as:


- Intelligent video analytics
- Thermal imaging systems
- X-ray inspection equipment
- Drone-based inspection platforms
- Autonomous monitoring systems
- Environmental sensing networks


Rather than serving solely as security tools, these technologies are becoming integrated data sources within the broader building management ecosystem.


A drone inspecting a rooftop asset, for example, may simultaneously support security monitoring, maintenance planning, compliance reporting, and operational risk assessment. Similarly, AI-enabled cameras can detect unauthorized access and monitor occupancy patterns, equipment utilization, and workplace safety conditions.


By integrating these technologies into building controls, organizations gain a more comprehensive understanding of facility operations and potential risks.


## Test & Measurement Expands Beyond Traditional Applications


Test & Measurement technologies have long played a critical role in validating performance, reliability, and quality across industrial environments.


Today, many of those same sensing and monitoring capabilities are being integrated directly into infrastructure systems.


Advanced sensors can continuously monitor:


- Electrical systems
- Power quality
- Thermal performance
- RF signals
- Environmental conditions
- Mechanical vibration
- Equipment health indicators


When integrated into building management systems, these sensors create a continuous stream of operational intelligence.


Rather than waiting for scheduled inspections or maintenance intervals, facility operators gain real-time visibility into infrastructure performance and equipment condition. This allows organizations to identify anomalies earlier, reduce downtime, improve safety, and strengthen operational resilience.


More importantly, these systems help establish a common operational picture where security, safety, and performance data can be evaluated together rather than independently.


## Moving Toward Zero-Trust Infrastructure


The concept of Zero Trust originated within cybersecurity, based on the principle that no user, device, or connection should be automatically trusted.


Industrial organizations are applying this philosophy to physical infrastructure.


- In a Zero-Trust infrastructure model:
- Personnel access is continuously verified.
- Facility activity is continuously monitored.
- Equipment health is continuously assessed.
- Environmental conditions are continuously measured.
- Operational anomalies are continuously analyzed.


Rather than relying on periodic inspections or isolated security checkpoints, organizations create environments in which verification is continuous.


This approach is particularly valuable for facilities operating critical infrastructure, advanced manufacturing environments, transportation systems, energy assets, and high-value industrial operations.


As threats become more sophisticated and operational environments become more complex, continuous validation provides an additional layer of resilience that traditional security models cannot achieve alone.


## The Future of Intelligent Infrastructure


The future industrial facility will not separate building operations from security operations.


Instead, intelligent infrastructure will combine the following components into a unified environment that provides real-time awareness across the entire facility:


- Building automation
- Security scanning
- AI-powered analytics
- Industrial automation
- Test & Measurement technologies
- Operational monitoring


This convergence creates significant opportunities to improve safety, reduce operational risk, strengthen resilience, and support faster decision-making.


Organizations that embrace this integrated approach will be better positioned to navigate evolving security challenges while maximizing the value of their infrastructure investments.
