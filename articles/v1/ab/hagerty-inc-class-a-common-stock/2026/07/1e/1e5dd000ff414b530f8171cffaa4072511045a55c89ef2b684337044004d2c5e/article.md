# 2026 Lyriq-V: The Sharper, Quicker, Cadillac Crossover

The mid-size Lyriq was Cadillac’s first EV offering, giving the luxury market some red meat with a beautifully designed silhouette of a sleek, tall wagon and plenty of power to boot. When we attended the launch, we were impressed with aspects of its design and engineering, although there[were some quibbles with the ride quality](https://www.hagerty.com/media/new-car-reviews/first-look-review-2024-cadillac-lyriq-600e-awd/) on the stiff, skinny sidewalls needed to handle the EV’s noticeable bulk. A few years later, Cadillac has made some changes to the Lyriq lineup, including expanding to include the sporty Lyriq-V. We spent a week in and around Ann Arbor and Kalamazoo, Michigan, to chart the updates to the 2026 Cadillac Lyriq-V and try out the quickest Cadillac ever built.


In its normal drive modes, the Lyriq-V feels much like the standard AWD Lyriq, which already comes with 515 hp and 450 lb-ft of torque. As with other electric vehicles, the immediate torque on demand makes it easy to merge onto highways and pass semi-trucks, but the Lyriq V has a bit of mechanical advantage over the standard Lyriq, with lower gears providing even more torque multiplication for improved acceleration. The Lyriq V also benefits from recalibrated suspension that includes a stiffer rear anti-roll bar, a quicker steering rack, and Sachs adaptive dampers, which are also used on the Optiq-V, but nowhere else in the Lyriq lineup. Everything is firmer and more responsive, yet we never felt like it was too much. This go-round yielded a more refined experience than some of our earlier interactions with the Lyriq, where every expansion joint was transmitted through to the driver.


Most of our driving time was spent in either urban or highway driving, although the two-lane roads through Southern Michigan provided dozens of miles of curves. We enjoyed the Lyriq-V through the twisting forested roads while keeping a close lookout for whitetail deer. Another impressive aspect that we took advantage of during our drive was Cadillac’s Super Cruise, which comes standard on all Lyriq-Vs. We engaged the hands-free driving mode on a stretch of Michigan highway and let it handle everything, including lane changes. It remains an excellent and nearly fault-free bit of tech.

## Specs: 2026 Cadillac Lyriq-V Premium


**Starting Price:** $85,590 Price as Tested: $86,560
**Powertrain:** Liquid-cooled permanent-magnet synchronous motors front and rear; 102-kWh lithium-ion battery
**Output:** 615 horsepower, 650 pound-feet of torque
**Layout:** Four-door, two-row, five-passenger, all-wheel-drive SUV
**Weight:** 5980 pounds
**0–60 mph:** 3.3 seconds
**Range:** 285 miles (OEM estimate)
**Competitors:** BMW iX, Mercedes-Benz EQE SUV, Rivian R1S


Accessing the Lyriq V’s full 615 hp requires a press of the steering-wheel-mounted V button. A long press of the button goes straight to Velocity Max mode, signaling the change by outlining the instrument panel with a red glow and cueing up a rather ominous sound effect to let you know things are getting serious. Alternatively, a quick tap of the same steering wheel button brings up the V-mode menu on the center screen, allowing the driver to tailor the vehicle’s response. Steering, suspension, and brake settings can be placed in either Sport or Tour modes. The latter suspension choice relaxes the Sachs dampers for a smoother ride. Acceleration Feel can be set to either Sport, Tour, or Relaxed. From the same screen, tapping on Competition Mode also opens up the full potential of the Lyriq V’s power and tells the stability control to lengthen the leash and let the rear end get a bit loose. The Lyriq-V is clearly sportier than the standard Lyriq trims, although it’s no Blackwing, and thus wasn’t built to wring out every bit of lap time on the track. All-wheel-drive acceleration in Velocity Max mode is impressive, with all four tires giving their all on zero-to-60 sprints without a hint of wheel slip.


Painted red calipers ($595), performance brake pads ($100), and the NACS charging adapter ($275) were the only options added to the Lyriq-V Premium we tested.

Cadillac pairs the Velocity Max mode with enhanced motor sounds to fill in some of the sensory engagement lost in an EV. It is a bit of a novelty, although in our short time with the car, it never wore off. You can turn it off if it’s not your thing. The Lyriq is practical; the Lyriq-V just adds an extra layer of sportiness. While most of it comes through the steering wheel and the seat of the pants, the sound does add to the fun.


After debuting in the compact Optiq, Cadillac has given the Lyriq’s AKQ Studio 23-speaker audio system the Dolby Atmos treatment. Atmos gives occupants an immersive experience when playing tracks mixed with the 3D soundscape in mind, and it left us just as impressed as it did[when we first experienced it](https://www.hagerty.com/media/new-car-reviews/first-drive-the-2025-optiq-is-cadillacs-feature-packed-300-mile-range-entry-ev/) . Listening to some of our favorite tracks for comparison, we did find the Lyriq’s audio system to be a bit less vibrant and clear on the top end than some of our favorite systems, specifically the Bowers & Wilkins available in top-trim Volvos. For tracks recorded in Atmos, the effect is surprising, especially when some of our familiar favorites are given new life.


The Lyriq-V’s interior adds a bit of color with its blue ambient lighting, and the V trim adds hints of extra detail in the upholstery’s contrasting stitching and a welcome shift from plastic to aluminum for the regenerative braking paddle behind the steering wheel. The touches on the outside are even more subtle, with just a couple of badges, a new rear fascia, and a new rocker panel treatment to identify the more powerful iteration. That’s fine by us; the Lyriq didn’t need much in the way of tailoring.

Although GM made waves by ditching Apple CarPlay and Android Auto in most of its EVs, opting instead for its in-house software solution, the Lyriq keeps wireless versions of both popular phone-mirroring software options across its trim levels. CarPlay worked flawlessly during our few days with the Lyriq-V and allowed the map to project in the right portion of the wide dash screen, above the center stack, or into the driver’s information center area behind the steering wheel. It also worked with the Augmented Reality head-up display (HUD) that can project an animated directional indicator in front of the driver. Adjustable for position and brightness, we found the HUD helped navigate new roads while keeping our attention in front of us. Cadillac’s built-in navigation can offer the same guidance, and also integrates the vehicle’s state of charge to predict the battery level and range at your destination, or suggest routes that include chargers if necessary.


Cadillac made clear that this isn’t a Blackwing, although it would make for a great garage-mate for one of the brand’s super sedans. Even if the Lyriq-V hadn’t increased the straight-line performance by adding 100 horsepower, buyers might have been lured by the suspension improvements alone. The Lyriq-V buttons down an already solid offering without any compromises that often come with making something more sporting.


**Highs:** Good looks, inside and out. Impressive acceleration. Added grip and handling sharpness came without increased harshness.


**Lows:** Velocity Max mode must be turned on after every key-off. A few interior trim pieces are a bit too flashy for our liking.


**Takeaway:** Starting with the great lines of the Lyriq, the Lyriq-V elevates the handling and fun of an already practical daily driver and irons out some of its kinks.
