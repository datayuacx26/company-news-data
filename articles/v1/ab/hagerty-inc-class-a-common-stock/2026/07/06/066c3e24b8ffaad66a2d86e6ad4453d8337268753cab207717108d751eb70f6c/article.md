# Carini: Of Men and Museums

I love museums and I love cars, so it makes sense that I love car museums. Recently, two of my good friends, Jon Oscher and George Barber, passed away. Both founded terrific car museums that are a little off the beaten path but[very much worth a visit](https://www.hagerty.com/media/video/tom-cotter-barber-vintage-motorcycle-museum/) . If you’re planning on a road trip this summer, maybe add them to your itinerary.


Jon Oscher opened the[Savoy Automobile Museum](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gvl_CAW953U) in Cartersville, Georgia, in December 2021. When they were starting work on the building, they came across a car in the middle of the woods with a tree growing up through the hood. It was a 1954 Plymouth Savoy, so that’s where they got the name for the museum. The car is still on display there outside.


Facebook/Savoy Automobile Museum


The museum is part of a 37-acre campus that includes the main museum building, a storage garage, and an outdoor pavilion. There’s a theater where they give seminars called “Tireside Chats.” It has a turntable built into the stage where they can place a car. When they’re having a chat about that car, it slowly rotates so the audience can see it.


Courtesy Savoy Automobile Museum


Courtesy Savoy Automobile Museum


Jon had the noted automotive historian and author Ken Gross help him curate the collection. Ken, who is a good friend of mine, called me. “We’re opening this museum, and Jon really enjoys your TV show,” Ken said. “He wants you to come down and do a seminar with me for the grand opening.” I made the trip to Cartersville, and Jon drove Ken and me to the museum. Ken and I got out of the car, but Jon stayed behind. I said, “Jon, are you coming in?” He said to me, “No, I’m going to dinner with friends. You guys have a good time, I’ll see you afterwards.” He was so humble. He didn’t want to be the center of attention, even at the grand opening of his own museum.


George Barber[was another great guy](https://www.hagerty.com/media/motorsports/regarding-george-barber-and-a-life-well-lived/) who established an amazing museum. He founded the Barber Vintage Motorsports Museum in 1995, starting with a group of motorcycles that included a rare 1952 Victoria Bergmeister. George decided that to stand out from other car-oriented museums, he would build the world’s best and largest collection of motorcycles. Eventually the museum evolved into a motorsports complex encompassing 880 acres with a 2.4-mile racetrack. The museum has more than 1800 motorcycles,[with over 1000 on display on any given day](https://www.hagerty.com/media/video/tom-cotter-barber-vintage-motorcycle-museum/) .

I remember the first time I met George. He was the nicest guy, larger than life. The museum was everything to him, and he wanted it to be perfect. One morning, we were heading into the theater to give a seminar, and George was outside picking up cigarette butts from the ground. Another year, I took down six of my motorcycles, including my all-original 1928 Indian Scout. I gave a seminar about my motorcycles and then I rode the Indian around the track. George was watching all this, then I came in and he said to me, “Now I have to show you how I drive it.” We got in his minivan and started tearing around. I thought we’d do one lap then come in, because other motorcycles were waiting to get out on the track. Ten laps later, we’re still out there. Finally, he said to me, “How many laps did we do, Wayne?” I said, “I don’t know, eight or 10.” He said, “Oh jeez, we gotta get off the track!”


It was my honor to have known Jon and George. They were generous gentlemen who gave so much to the automotive world. Each left behind a legacy that future generations of car and motorcycle enthusiasts will enjoy. The world is a better place because of these two.
