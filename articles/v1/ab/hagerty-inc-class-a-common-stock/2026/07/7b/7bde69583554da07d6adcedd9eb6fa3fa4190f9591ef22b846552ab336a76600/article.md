# 2027 Maserati GranCabrio Trofeo First Drive: Rational Irrationality

There’s no shortage of supercars, and the Maserati GranCabrio intentionally steers clear of them all. Despite the numbers on the spec sheet, it’s more of a grand tourer that’s at home speeding through the countryside than a supercar built to set lap records on a track. It’s low, but not impossibly so. It’s loud, but it’s not over-the-top. It even has a usable trunk that can fit carry-ons. It’s almost rational.


Can rationality stand out in a price bracket dominated by the irrationality of superlatives? We took a 2027 Maserati GranCabrio Trofeo for a drive in Italy to find out. This is the facelifted version of the second generation of the model, and has received a round of mid-cycle updates including new-look styling and additional power.


We say “despite the numbers on the spec sheet” because they’re edging into supercar territory. Get the base Modena trim and the 3.0-liter, twin-turbocharged Nettuno V-6 has 483 horsepower and 442 pound-feet of torque. This is the same six that powers[the MCPura](https://www.hagerty.com/media/news/goodbye-maserati-mc20-hello-mcpura/) , among others. Step up to the range-topping Trofeo, which is the model that we drove, and you get an evolution of the V-6 that posts figures of 590 and 480, respectively. That’s an increase of about 40 horsepower compared to the pre-facelift car.


The act of downsizing what’s under the hood is often vilified as the enemy of fun, but the GranCabrio makes an excellent case for it. Sure, the last-generation model used a naturally aspirated V-8 that traced its roots to Ferrari. It sounded great, it had a massive amount of character, and an eight-cylinder is more closely aligned with the definition of an exotic car than a six, but the Nettuno brings with it a long list of benefits. For starters, it’s smaller than the V-8, so Maserati stuffed it almost entirely behind the front axle for a front-mid-engine layout. In turn, this evens out the weight distribution to 48% front and 52% rear. The V-6 is also lighter than the old V-8.


Its power travels through a ZF-sourced eight-speed automatic transmission and reaches all four wheels instead of the rear wheels only. All-wheel drive is new for the current generation, and it’s a sign that the GranCabrio and the GranTurismo were designed with everyday usability in mind.

The revised GranCabrio falls in line with the proportions that have defined GT cars for decades. It’s characterized by a long hood, a short trunk, and, with the top up, a sloping roof line—put another way, it’s the exact opposite of a car with a mid-mounted engine, like the MCPura, and it looks like the direct descendant of cars like[the elegant 3500 GT](https://www.hagerty.com/media/news/superleggera-maserati-3500-gt-retromobile-2025-pictures/) .


Most of the changes that set the 2027 car apart from the pre-facelift model are found on the front end, which Quentin Amore, the head of exterior design for Maserati, tells us was designed from the grille out. The new look, penned to give the GranCabrio a more muscular-looking stance, was directly inspired by the track-only MCXtrema. Out back, the biggest change is a set of clear lights. Buyers also have additional customization options, including a head-turning new paint color called Green Jupiter Matte (pictured).


The attention to detail extends beyond the sheetmetal. Fit and finish are both excellent throughout the GranCabrio. The materials used inside are at least on par with what you’d expect to find in a luxury car with a six-digit price tag, regardless of the segment it competes in, and there’s nothing inside that blatantly looks like it was sourced from a more mainstream-oriented carmaker’s parts bin.

Maserati is rooted in tradition, but technology is the new measuring stick in the automotive industry, so even enthusiasts who buy a car for its pedigree now expect screens and connectivity. Maserati took its own approach to in-car tech rather than chasing trends. You’re in the wrong showroom if you want a screen that your front passenger can watch movies on while an armada of LiDAR sensors maps the road to let you read while cruising. The technology in the GranCabrio is largely subtle rather than in-your-face.


The 12-inch, saddle-shaped digital instrument cluster straddles the steering column, allowing Maserati to mount the display lower than a conventional rectangular screen. That made it possible to lower the seating position. There’s also a touchscreen for the infotainment system on the center console, and a third screen shaped like a big watch that’s positioned on top of the dashboard. All of these displays are configurable. They’re not going to blow you away, but they offer sharp graphics, they’re straightforward to use, and, above all, they don’t take anything away from the driving experience.


And that’s *molto bene* , because the driving experience is one of the highlights here. By bringing the grand tourer formula deeper into the 21st century, Maserati engineered a car that’s comfortable, engaging to drive, and that feels special in that nebulous “I’m driving something really cool” way. It’s effortlessly quick with little turbo lag, even in the standard Comfort mode. It sounds amazing as it approaches redline, especially with the top down—the Nettuno will be remembered as one of the most enthusiast-friendly V-6s of its era.


The direct, well-weighted steering complements the all-wheel-drive system’s grip, inspiring confidence in fast corners, and the brakes are reassuringly strong. Select the GT, Sport, or Corsa mode, and the spread between comfort and performance becomes gradually wider. Sport notably makes the air suspension system firmer to reduce body roll, and brings with it a launch control function. We elected not to try that during our journey, lest we summon a dark cloud of disapproval from the Italian *polizia* . Still, the amount of versatility that Maserati packed into this convertible is impressive, and makes the GranCabrio a thoroughly convincing GT.


## Specs: 2027 Maserati GranCabrio Trofeo


- **Price** : $158,000 (Modena), $176,200 (Trofeo)
- **Powertrain:** 3.0-liter twin-turbocharged V-6, eight-speed automatic
- **Horsepower:** 590 hp @ 6500 rpm
- **Torque:** 480 lb-ft @ 3000 rpm
- **Drivetrain:** All-wheel-drive
- **Layout:** Front-engine, two-door, four-passenger coupe
- **0–60 mph:** 3.7 seconds
- **Competitors:** Bentley Continental GT, Mercedes-AMG SL, Porsche 911 Cabriolet

It’s not just the chassis that strikes a balance between comfort and performance. In regular driving, such as on the highway or around town, the GranCabrio stands out as a pleasant place to travel in with well-bolstered seats and ample space for the front passengers. You’re not cramped, and you’re not left wondering if the team in charge of ergonomics was on vacation when the project was signed off. Even minutiae like the gear selector buttons, which were redesigned for 2027, are more intuitive to operate. The rear seats are a different story: they’re there, but we wouldn’t put two adults back there for a long trip. Not unless we wanted to make sure that they didn’t ride with us on the way back.


With our Italian jaunt complete, the 2027 Maserati GranCabrio reminds us there’s still a case to be made for the four-seater convertible, and for cars defined by the driving experience rather than by numbers. It’s plenty quick, well-built, and remains comfortable in what it is without trying to be something it’s not. You’re never going to hear Maserati brag about the GranCabrio’s ‘Ring time, and that’s perfectly fine. This is a true *gran turismo* .


## 2027 Maserati GranCabrio Trofeo


**Highs:** Comfortable in its skin; powerful and poised without being overly sporty.


**Lows:** Rear seats mainly for show or luggage; not for folks seeking bleeding-edge tech.


**Takeaway:** Grand touring well executed.
