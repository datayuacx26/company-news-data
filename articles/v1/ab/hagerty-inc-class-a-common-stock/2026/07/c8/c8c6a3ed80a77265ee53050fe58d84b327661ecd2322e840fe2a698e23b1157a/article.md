# Piston Slap: A Historical Boost From A Booster Plate?

*Adam* writes:


Sajeev,


I’ll be the first to admit that whenever I buy a car from a dealership, the first thing I do when I get home is rip off the dealer license plate frames.


However, after reading a[recent Hagerty article](https://www.hagerty.com/media/automobilia/vintage-dealership-license-plate-frames-and-badges-are-the-ultimate-chefs-kiss/) on the charm of vintage dealership badges, I realized I should probably trace the origin of my “question mark” car. Since my Buick will eventually age into the AACA, I want to gather as much history and documentation as possible for display with the car at shows and help tell its story.


I bought[this Buick that all Piston Slap readers know](https://www.hagerty.com/media/advice/piston-slap/piston-slap-quick-strutting-or-restoring-a-performance-gm-suspension/) on Craigslist. The seller told me it belonged to a 99-year-old former Olympian who wore a leg brace (explaining the wear mark on the transmission tunnel) and used a mobility scooter (explaining the unwired trailer hitch on the back). A Carfax report and a “Twin Lakes Retirement Community” sticker in the back window confirmed it spent its life in Pittsboro and Elon, NC, before coming to me in Raleigh. But I still had no clue who the original selling dealer was. Calling around to local Buick dealerships went nowhere—mostly, I heard, “I wasn’t working here 20 years ago.” Then I had an idea: the GM Heritage Center.


I reached out, and they provided the original dealer invoice in a PDF. As it turns out, the car was sold new at a dealership just five miles from where I live now. In 2005, it was Leith Buick Pontiac GMC Truck (today, it’s just a GMC dealer). I want to find period-correct photos of the dealership and advertisements. I went to a local museum, and all their newspapers had been digitized by newspapers.com, and I would have to pay a subscription fee to access any information. Then I had an epiphany: In 2005, the internet was a thing!


Wayback Machine


For those of us whose collector cars were built during the dawn of the internet age, we can actually digitally sleuth for this stuff. I used the “Way Back Machine” and played around with different web addresses until I finally got a hit: the dealership’s actual homepage from 2005. Now that I have an idea what the dealership’s branding looked like back then, I’m hunting down the proper dealership plate and plate frames.


I’ve always said I’ve made a career out of “I’ll Never’s” and here I am actually spending money on a Dealership License Plate! But my trip down the rabbit hole leaves me with one final question for you:


How can I get someone to faithfully reproduce my original window sticker on a 21-year-old GM car?


*Sajeev* answers:


You’re doing a fantastic job being an Internet sleuth, Adam! Regarding the window sticker, it’s easy for Ford guys, as they have readily available[Marti reports](https://www.hagerty.com/media/market-trends/hagerty-insider/kevin-marti-has-been-driving-fomoco-history-for-50-years/) for most applications. But I found something similar for GM products, so check out[https://www.winvoices.com/](https://nam12.safelinks.protection.outlook.com/?url=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.winvoices.com%2F&data=05%7C02%7Cpistonslap%40hagerty.com%7C2d6efb72fe0045f1d33708deb123a6ed%7C757cbf3a0ef641968163947401a97de4%7C0%7C0%7C639142964032318143%7CUnknown%7CTWFpbGZsb3d8eyJFbXB0eU1hcGkiOnRydWUsIlYiOiIwLjAuMDAwMCIsIlAiOiJXaW4zMiIsIkFOIjoiTWFpbCIsIldUIjoyfQ%3D%3D%7C0%7C%7C%7C&sdata=BLYgN%2BmHw24NAdx4BrFZiv2XmBZyJy7JVOHUiu2zbxw%3D&reserved=0) .


In my quest to motivate/inspire you, here’s a personal admission: While I severely dislike dealership emblems on painted surfaces, I’m addicted to vintage car dealership swag. These bits tell a story that factory sheetmetal cannot by itself, especially the “booster” license plates they regularly attach to the front bumper.


The booster license plate in the lead photo (top) proves that history doesn’t stop at the assembly line. Ford’s factory-blessed certification program for any used vehicle on their lot—that car is a Hyundai, believe it or not—tells the story of a society where used cars are more important than they ever were in decades past. And I bet that will be an entertaining story 30 years from now at a car meet!


Here’s an eBay score from 2025.


[Sajeev Mehta](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/sajeev-mehta/)


So get the booster plates and plate frame that tell your car’s story, and here’s how you do it. Start the hunt with saved searches on eBay. I look for keywords like “\[Dealer Name/Make/Location\] frame,” and “\[Dealer Name/Make/Location\] booster plate.” Also do a simple high-level query of the dealership’s name to hunt for promotional materials like pens, shirts, matchbooks, keychains, and newspaper advertisements.


But sometimes telling your car’s story requires creativity and personal initiative. I’ve made my own booster plate for the beloved dealership that got me hooked on cars at a young age using online vendors for custom license plates.


*(Pro Tip: Date a graphic designer, because they’ll make a period-correct graphic that you can upload to a plate-making website. If you’re a good partner, they’ll probably do it for free!)*


Look vintage, but isn’t.


[Sajeev Mehta](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/sajeev-mehta/)


No, I don’t have a problem. ***You have a problem.***


So anyway, my descent into madness experiences with vintage dealership booster swag (as it were) suggests that patience, persistence, and a little creativity pay dividends. My custom booster plate has drawn numerous comments at car shows, as locals vividly remember this dealership from its Las Vegas-style lighted sign overlooking the freeway. I am lucky I had a graphic designer to help me tell that story, and to get the colors right for my specific application.


Back to your outstanding query: Hopefully Winvoices can help with your quest for the original sticker!


Days later, *Adam* replies:


Yet again, the “common” Buick LeSabre isn’t so common. I reached out to Winvoices and chatted with Paul. He said that in 2005, GM changed the size of the window sticker, so he’s not able to produce one as a result. *(Oh no! — SM)*


Adam the OP


On the plus side, though, I found a dealership license plate on eBay. Maybe a plate frame will come along sometime.


Heavy sigh, any other window sticker reproduction companies you can think of? Thank you in advance for your help!


*Sajeev* concludes:


You will find a booster frame to go with the booster plate;[it just takes patience](https://www.hagerty.com/media/advice/piston-slap/piston-slap-on-a-long-enough-timeline-unobtainium-is-obtainium/) . To help with that, I checked out the Old[Dealership Pictures group](https://www.facebook.com/groups/2120630181518995) on Facebook. It appears that Leith sold more than just Buicks and GMCs in their PMA (Primary Market Area). If your eBay search is restricted by the Buick or GMC criteria, consider replacing it with the city or state where the dealership does business.


Unfortunately, Winvoices was the only place I knew about for GM window stickers, but[I’ve googled](https://www.google.com/search?q=2008+general+motors+window+sticker+print&rlz=1C5GCCM_en&oq=2008+general+motors+window+sticker+print&gs_lcrp=EgZjaHJvbWUyBggAEEUYOTIHCAEQIRigATIHCAIQIRigATIHCAMQIRigATIHCAQQIRigATIHCAUQIRirAtIBCTE1NjUyajBqN6gCALACAA&sourceid=chrome&source=chrome.ob&ie=UTF-8&safe=active&ssui=on) a couple of more generic websites that might provide a similar service. I cannot tell if they are legit, so I am not mentioning them by name.


Maybe a *Piston Slap* reader can assist? I am keeping my fingers crossed!
