# Reputation Management: Tech Disruption Isn’t Just For Used Cars

*Welcome back to[Reputation Management](https://www.hagerty.com/media/category/advice/reputation-management/) ! We are again revealing the underbelly of local automotive retailers—places like car dealers and service shops—with fictional service tales inspired by real customer reviews. How do we make these stories seem like they could really happen? Because of my years of experience with a Fortune 500 automotive retailer as a—you guessed it—reputation manager.* *—Sajeev*


Loathsome has always been a good word for the automotive retail space, and not just for the customer. Just ask around; even if salesfolk are good at remaining positive and will likely sugarcoat their feelings, the sentiment is still there. Happiness and modesty are a great path to strong commissions and big bonuses, so for a more direct answer, ask a parts manager. Or a master technician. Or the guy working the service bay that has the wheel alignment machine. But it can get worse, and the folks working at a car dealership that is transitioning to new ownership experience a unique form of turmoil.


Addressing this directly is my life as a Reputation Manager. I am one of CarCountry’s corporate office front-line workers who gets[dispatched to a dealership](https://www.hagerty.com/media/advice/reputation-management/reputation-management-a-new-dawn-in-the-deep-south/) shortly after its acquisition. Getting individuals to operate *the CarCountry way* was easy for me, because my realm, Online Reputation Management (and Social Media in general), is a nebulous endeavor. The suits who implement overhauls (software and hardware, or the culture change of a business that’s been purchased) bear the real brunt of loathsome employees. Sometimes the hatred is justified, but injecting customer transparency and financing integrity into the system is the way business must be done.


I once walked into a recently acquired Honda dealership that had a singular computer, and it was very, very beige. Every salesperson did deals[on a four square](https://mraa.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/foursquareclosingworksheet.pdf) . This was in the year 2014, but it sure as heck felt like I traveled back to 1994. So believe me when I say that I’m impressed with what Carvana’s accomplished in their buying spree of Stellantis’ franchises. The well-known “tech disrupter” in the car business is doing what my team at CarCountry does, but with turbocharging. And a 150-shot of nitrous oxide, if I am being honest.


Carvana


Carvana


I would kill to be a Reputation Manager in a dealership acquisition like this. (Don’t tell my boss!) You can see why with a series of clicks on their website. Your average non-luxury franchised dealership website generally screams “SPECIAL DEALS CLICK HERE” on their home page, with scrolling banners for everything from new tires to special financing. In contrast, Carvana took a page from their used-car operation, focusing on minimal words and maximum graphics. The feel is premium and the viewer is encouraged to check out their inventory. Inventory pages are similar to how Carvana sells used cars, and that’s worthy of jealousy in my book.


*******


MotorMantra is another tech disruptor in the car business. They are ruffling a lot of feathers here at CarCountry, as Wall Street loves them best. Analysts compare us to them every quarter, and their tone isn’t exactly warm. But MotorMantra’s disruption is fueled by billions in loans and Reddit threads telling everyone to “hold onto their stonks.” *Nice work if you can get it, if you ask me.*


My boss is Greg Green, a well-known Kingmaker in the digital marketing space. I played my cards right with him, earning the title I have today. Digital marketing companies suck up to Greg with impunity, as they know his business unleashes opportunities with every other dealership in the country. Greg doesn’t let the fame go to his head. He sees opportunities and threats, hence why he rang me up with a request.


> “Sajeev, the CarCountry executives are asking me for my thoughts about MotorMantra. This is a complete waste of my time, just like its a waste of money for anyone investing in their high technology grift. But I still need you to do an online scan of their operation, because I need more proof that their business isn’t a unique threat. They are just another large dealer group in my book!”


While I am drooling over MotorMantra’s well-financed user interface, he has a point: Technology doesn’t convince someone to spring for a (chronically underappreciated) transmission fluid exchange. It cannot torque wheels to the correct spec, or hand them the keys to a[loaner car](https://www.hagerty.com/media/advice/reputation-management/reputation-management-a-schism-in-loaner-car-lending/) worthy of usage. Reconditioning used cars, servicing new cars, and performing warranty work with precision is how you keep local customers loyal. Their recurring revenue keeps profit margins fat. It’s not about monthly sales transactions; it’s about the customer lifecycle and keeping them loyal to you.

After scanning recent reviews on their recent acquisitions, MotorMantra has two problems on their hands. The first is their inability to manage Fixed Operations (Service and Parts departments) with the same level of precision as they do IT infrastructure. The second is a compounding factor to the first, as they entered this market with a trouble-prone franchise.


Perhaps you don’t need the Reputation Manager to tell you this, but this manufacturer is having a bit of an issue with warranty costs at the moment. No disrespect intended to the hard-working corporate and franchise employees, but[last time I checked](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/02/06/stellantis-stla-ceo-antonio-filosa.html) , this issue tallied up to 4.1 billion euros in liabilities on the balance sheet. The Internet is also[quite merciless](https://www.tiktok.com/discover/stellantis) in its assessment, so the email to my boss went something like this:


> “Hey Greg, please see the attached reviews. You are on the right track, just hammer the fact that MotorMantra is a newbie to providing service after the sale for cars with factory warranties. Odds are the employees are not happy about being disrupted from their traditional careers and being forced to pivot to that[of a tech employee](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/carvana-layoffs-2500-workers-zoom-email/) . Maybe they will be a threat to CarCountry if they convince Toyota to sell them a franchise?”


The email worked like a charm, but most of them do. The Reputation Manager knows many a universal truth in the car business. Tech disruption only goes so far in retail, because this world is a delicate balancing act that can’t be sorted with a query to Claude or ChatGPT. Just ask Pizza Hut franchises[for their opinion](https://finance.yahoo.com/sectors/technology/articles/pizza-hut-ai-delivery-failure-191837668.html) on the matter. That said, if MotorMantra wants to get serious about their reputation, they know where to find me.


*The Reputation Manager will return…*
