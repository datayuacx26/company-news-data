# 7 Cars We’re Watching from the 2026 Iconic Auctioneers BRDC Classic Sale

British auction house Iconic Auctioneers is hosting a huge sale at the Silverstone Circuit on July 25, 2026. Browsing the auction catalog is a little like flipping through one of those “100 Greatest Cars of All Time” coffee table books. Whether you’re into hot hatches, supercars, bubble cars, or SUVs, there’s something in the catalog that will catch your eye. The vast majority of the cars are old enough to legally import into the United States, and some come from the collection of Anthony Hamilton, who is Lewis Hamilton’s father.


Here are seven highlights from[the upcoming Iconic Auctioneers sale](https://www.iconicauctioneers.com/the-iconic-sale-at-the-brdc-classic-2026-collectors-cars/2026-07-25/page-1/session-259/ipp-100?lo=ASC&search=) . What’s on your to-watch list?


# [1960 Triumph Italia 2000 Coupé](https://www.iconicauctioneers.com/1960-triumph-italia-2000-rec17102-28-silverston-0726?pn=1&el=37117&pp=100)

**Lot 283**


You can find a classic Triumph convertible at just about every auction, but this isn’t your average Spitfire. It’s a rare, TR3-based coupe designed by Giovanni Michelotti and built by Carrozzeria Vignale in Italy. The coachbuilt nature of this coupe made it 26% more expensive than the TR3 it was based on, and as a result, only about 330 units were built between 1959 and 1962. In contrast, Triumph made roughly 75,000 examples of the TR3.


This left-hand-drive example spent most of its early life in the United States. It was then purchased by an enthusiast in Germany who had it fully restored, and it later joined the Anthony Hamilton Collection. The steel wheels that were fitted when it left the factory were replaced by wire wheels, and we’re told that Hamilton had numerous repairs made, including rebuilding the suspension and replacing the water pump.


Iconic Auctioneers expects to sell this coachbuilt Triumph for £80,000 to £100,000 ($107,000 to $134,000).


# [1973 Lamborghini Jarama S](https://www.iconicauctioneers.com/1973-lamborghini-jarama-s-rec16935-1-silverston-0726?pn=1&el=36796&pp=100)

**Lot 213**


In 2026, Lamborghini is known for offering[a wide palette of paint colors](https://www.hagerty.com/media/news/lamborghini-customization-options-studio-pictures/) ranging from subtle to head-turning. While buyers have more color choices today than in the 1970s, this Jarama S proves that Lamborghini has never been shy when it comes to experimenting with distinctive colors. It’s finished in a shade of green called Verde Pistachio that you’ll either love or hate. We’d argue that it works pretty well.


Beyond the unusual color, this[Jarama](https://www.hagerty.com/media/car-profiles/driving-a-jarama-the-unsung-great-lamborghini/) is a sought-after S model powered by a 365-horsepower V-12, and it has been owned by British race car driver Alex Postan since 1984. He later had the car fully restored and repainted (Verde Pistachio is period-correct but not original to the car) over the course of nearly 30 years. The V-12 engine was notably rebuilt, though the interior was intentionally left as-is to preserve its patina.


The pre-sale estimate is set at £35,000 to £45,000 (about $47,000 to $60,000).


# [1982 Aston Martin Lagonda Series 2](https://www.iconicauctioneers.com/1982-aston-martin-lagonda-rec17102-4-silverston-0726?pn=1&el=37005&pp=100)

**Lot 274**


For better or worse, the odds of seeing another Aston Martin sedan like[the Lagonda](https://www.hagerty.com/media/car-profiles/aston-martins-outlandish-lagonda-turns-50/) are low. Long, wide, and boxy, the big sedan represented an attempt to lure buyers away from the Maserati Quattroporte, among others, while branching out of the sports car segment. In a way, the Lagonda was to Aston Martin in the 1970s and 1980s what[the DBX](https://www.hagerty.com/media/news/aston-martin-dbx-s-pictures-specs/) is to the brand today, with a few extra servings of outlandishness.


Painted in Kent Green over a beige interior, the example in the Iconic Auctioneers sale catalog is powered by a 5.3-liter V-8 fed with four Weber carburetors and rated at 280 horsepower. That’s the more exotic side of the spec sheet. Follow the power out of the crankshaft, and you’ll see it travel through a Chrysler-sourced three-speed TorqueFlite automatic transmission. Put another way, you can probably pick up a transmission pan gasket for this rare, 17-foot-long British luxury sedan from your local AutoZone.


Beyond its rarity, one of this Lagonda’s main selling points is that it’s well-documented. It was sold new in London and it remained with its original owner until 2002, when it had about 59,000 miles. The thick stack of service records that come with the car detail thousands spent on maintenance and modifications. This car was notably sent back to the factory and repainted in its current shade of green in October 1988, and the owner commissioned Aston Martin to install a center console with a cocktail cabinet and a television.


Anthony Hamilton,[Lewis Hamilton](https://www.hagerty.com/media/news/lewis-hamilton-wins-seventh-world-title-is-he-the-goat/) ‘s dad, added this car to his collection in 2022 and had it fully restored. It’s expected to sell for between £80,000 and £100,000, or between $107,000 and $134,000.


# [1985 BMW Alpina B10 3.5](https://www.iconicauctioneers.com/1985-bmw-allpina-b10-rec17117-1-silverston-0726?pn=1&el=37043&pp=100)

**Lot 298**


Alpina has been[part of the BMW Group](https://www.hagerty.com/media/news/new-alpina-logo-future-models/) since January 2026, and it will be interesting to see if its early cars go up in value as collectors begin to differentiate pre-merger and post-merger cars. Pre-merger cars were largely built-to-order, so finding two totally identical cars is difficult, and they were relatively rare.


The B10 that’s crossing the block during the Iconic Sale perfectly embodies what makes pre-merger cars special. Delivered new in the United Kingdom, it’s an automatic, 535i-based sedan with an Alpina-specific 3.5-liter straight-six, suspension updates, and a host of trim pieces inside and out that differentiate it from a stock E28. The metal plaque on the passenger’s side of the dashboard confirms its authenticity.


Iconic Auctioneers notes that this B10 has received a long list of repairs and maintenance over the past 20 years. It was fully repainted in the 2000s, the interior was overhauled, and recent work includes replacing the exhaust system in November 2024. While this isn’t a low-mile E28 that has never been out of a heated garage, it looks great and it has been featured in several enthusiast magazines in the United Kingdom.


The pre-sale estimate is set at £45,000 to £50,000 (about $60,000 to $67,000). The sale includes the original bill of sale dated March 27, 1985, and the “B10 ALP” registration that’s original to the car. Vanity plates can be expensive in England—”RH 3,” a plate on offer at this same auction,[could sell for over $130,000](https://www.iconicauctioneers.com/uk-registration-number-rh-3-rec17278-1-silverston-0726?pn=2&el=37281&pp=100) —so “B10 ALP” is a nice bonus.


# [1990 Mercedes-Benz 300E 3.4 AMG](https://www.iconicauctioneers.com/1990-mercedes-300e-amg-rec17058-1-silverston-0726?pn=1&el=36925&pp=100)

**Lot 229**


The W124-based Hammer stands out as one of the best-known and most sought-after pre-merger AMG models, but it’s not the only hot-rodded version of the original E-Class that came out of Affalterbach. The company also offered a cheaper and less powerful model called 300E 3.4. It received a modified, 3.4-liter evolution of the stock, 3.0-liter straight-six that was rated at 268 horsepower, up from 177 in the 300E.


One of 133 units built, and one of 30 ordered with a five-speed manual, this 300E 3.4 was delivered new in Germany and later sent to AMG for modifications by a private owner. It features the aforementioned straight-six engine that exhales through an AMG exhaust system, an AMG suspension system, and a Hammer-like body kit complemented by monoblock wheels. It’s been enjoyed; its odometer shows about 112,000 miles, but Iconic Auctioneers notes that it recently received an extensive cosmetic restoration.


Iconic Auctioneers expects to sell this 300E 3.4 for between £85,000 and £95,000 (around $114,000 and $127,000). That’s a lot for[a W124](https://www.hagerty.com/media/driving/mercedes-benz-w124-the-engineers-e-class-takes-on-the-alps/) , yet it makes this sedan considerably more affordable than a Hammer. As a bonus, the sale includes an AMG Conversion Confirmation Certificate that confirms its authenticity.

**Lot 207**


Few cars are as closely associated with the World Rally Championship as[the Lancia Delta Integrale](https://www.hagerty.com/media/buying-and-selling/your-definitive-lancia-delta-hf-integrale-buyers-guide/) . Over the past decade or so, Lancia’s best-known hot hatch has evolved from a relatively budget-friendly sports car to a seriously coveted classic that’s not entirely out of place parked next to exotics at cars and coffee.


The example in the Iconic Auctioneers sale catalog is a 1991 16-valve model finished in Grigio Metallic over a black leather interior. It was sold new in Japan, exported to the United Kingdom in 2014, and its odometer now displays 27,000 miles. Japanese-market cars often ended up modified to some extent, but this Delta Integrale looks largely stock with the exception of a newer Kenwood CD player.


Iconic Auctioneers expects to sell this Delta for £30,000 to £36,000 (about $40,000 to $48,000).

Built from 1993 to 1995,[the Clio Williams](https://www.hagerty.com/media/car-profiles/renault-clio-at-30-highs-and-lows-of-a-hot-hatch-hero/) was developed to homologate the model that Renault raced in the Group A and Group N categories of rallying. It was based on the regular-production Clio, which was marketed as a city-friendly economy car, and it gained a 16-valve, 2.0-liter four-cylinder engine, a specific suspension system, and sportier-looking styling characterized by a full body kit and gold-colored wheels.


Homologation rules required Renault to build 2500 units, but demand was so high that approximately 12,000 units were sold. Many were modified, raced, crashed, rebuilt, and raced again, so finding a good one has become easier said than done. The example in the Iconic Auctioneers sale catalog looks like it has been largely spared. It’s a later Phase 3 model with 15,182 miles on the odometer, no major modifications, and a recent service that included replacing the water pump, timing belt, and tires.


The pre-sale estimate is set at £30,000 to £35,000 (about $40,000 to $47,000).
