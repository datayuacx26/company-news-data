# Two Friends, a Big-Block Mopar, and the 100-Year-Old Mother Road

Markku Jaakkola has been a fan of American cars since he was a child. Growing up in Finland, he didn’t often come by American cars. Still, Jaakkola found some like-minded enthusiasts who shared a passion for vintage Detroit iron. One of Jaakkola’s closest friends was Hans Granberg, who drove a 1962 Imperial and was what Jaakkola described as “an encyclopedia of Mopar.” This summer, Jaakkola and another friend, Jacob Gilbert, will celebrate Hans’ life and their collective love of big Mopars with a journey on America’s most famous road.


Steven Pham


Jaakkola’s love of American cars blossomed further when he moved to Detroit in 2012 to pursue his Master’s in Engineering Management; he was at the heart of America’s auto industry and surrounded by the cars he’s always admired. Eventually, Granberg also made a trip to the United States and used the opportunity to buy a 1973 Chrysler New Yorker in Kansas. Granberg drove the car at the Woodward Dream Cruise and racked up plenty of miles in the big C-body before putting it in storage and returning to Finland, with plans to return and go on even more adventures. Unfortunately, Granberg passed away in 2024 at the age of 59.


Hans Granberg was a huge Mopar fan and was meticulous about finding the correct parts for his vintage fleet.


Courtesy of Markku Jaakkola


Jaakkola purchased Granberg’s New Yorker in 2024 and decided not just to get back on the road, but to fulfill his friend’s wish to go on another serious road trip. He fixed some of the car’s immediate issues, like a sticking rear wheel bearing and a dodgy fuel cell (which has since been replaced with a standard tank), but he needed help getting the car back on the road after it had languished in storage. The[Route 66 centennial](https://www.hagerty.com/media/entertainment/11-cultural-hits-of-route-66-at-100/) seemed like a great reason to get the car in tip-top shape, and a road trip across America’s most iconic highway would be a fitting tribute to Granberg. He called on his friend Jacob Gilbert, from Port Huron, Michigan, whom he’d met shortly after moving to the United States. Gilbert has been taking care of car collections for decades. He was a curator at the Sloan Museum and head of its restoration department when Jaakkola was a volunteer. Now he runs his own shop that specializes in restorations.


Markku Jaakkola (left) and Jacob Gilbert have been preparing the car for months.


Steven Pham


Over the past several months, Gilbert has gone over the car mechanically from end to end. A new fuel system, from the tank to the lines to the carburetor, will keep the 440 well fed on its journey. Likewise, the suspension and steering have been serviced, with new shocks and stabilizer links. An important recent addition was air conditioning. The New Yorker had originally been equipped with A/C, but it had been removed at some point in its life. The two have put a couple of hundred miles on the car each week this summer, taking it to various shows, and it seems ready for the trip. Still, Gilbert is packing a tool kit and a few spare parts, including a Mopar ignition module and ballast resistor. “Before, if it broke down it would be part of the adventure,” Gilbert said, “It didn’t have to be bulletproof.” This big Route 66 trip, however, has given them a goal and a deadline that has changed the game entirely.

The New Yorker is now ready for its 2500-mile road trip, and we’ll be joining them midway to see the car at some of the Mother Road’s most scenic locations. But Route 66 isn’t the only path on their agenda—the duo will also cruise the car up California’s Pacific Coast Highway to Monterey. If you’re going to Monterey Car Week, you’ll have a couple of opportunities to see the car in person.


Starting on Saturday, August 1, in Detroit at a Cars and Coffee at Pasteiner’s Collectibles and Hobbies, the two will head for Chicago and take Route 66 to Los Angeles. From there, it’s north on California Highway 1, the Pacific Coast Highway, to Monterey. The original deadline was Concours d’Lemons on August 15, although the timeline has moved up now that the car has been accepted at the Quail on August 14.

Jaakkola has picked up a few sponsors for the trip to pay for the food, lodging, and fuel along the way—the 440 is thirsty—including Visit Springfield Illinois, Route 66 Arizona, Jim Shaffer and Associates Realtors, Route 66 Navigation, the Walter P Chrysler Club, and, appropriately for a Finn, Sauna America. You can read more about the car on[the site Jaakkola built for the trip](https://detroitsunforgottenwheels.com/2026/01/28/1973-chrysler-new-yorker-route-66-centennial-project/) and perhaps see them on the journey or at their ultimate destination.
