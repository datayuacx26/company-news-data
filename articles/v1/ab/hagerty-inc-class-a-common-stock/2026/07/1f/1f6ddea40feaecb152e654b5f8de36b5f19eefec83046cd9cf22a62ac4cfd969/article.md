# Volkswagen Meets Veneer in This Wood-Bodied Beetle

Volkswagen’s Beetle was popular for several reasons. It was affordable, it sipped fuel, and it was easy to service and repair. It also lent itself to customization if the factory proportions, power, or stance weren’t just right for the owner. The fact that it was built in staggering numbers, and that its stamped-steel chassis made body swaps a snap, meant that VW Beetles were the go-to donor for dune buggy and kit car manufacturers for decades. We thought we’d seen just about every kind of custom VW Beetle out there, but this[“Plywagen,” currently up for bid on Hagerty Marketplace](https://www.hagerty.com/marketplace/auction/1952-Volkswagen-Custom/7447c09c-60cb-4e8f-a7f3-bf86dd10fb20) , is no fiberglass supercar clone or wannabe Manx.


Rosario “Ro” Capotosto started his creation with a 1952 VW Beetle, which makes the body that came off this chassis a rather desirable piece. Capotosto shed the steel sedan body and built an entirely wood roadster body using mostly ¼-inch ribbon-stripe mahogany plywood with maple trim. Despite promising “Easy-To-Follow Plans” when the car was on the cover of the November 1964 issue of *Mechanix Illustrated* , we haven’t seen any other rebodied Bug quite like it.

The car’s striking design is very much in line with traditional ’50s British roadsters like the MG T-Type and Morgan Plus 4. Both of those Brits feature prominent fenders and low-cut doors. This rear-engine roadster doesn’t have any doors, but with such a low step-in height it doesn’t seem to matter. Like a Morgan, Capotosto’s creation uses wooden support for the bodywork, in this case a perfectly fitted pine frame that uses glue rather than screws to keep its shape. The plans for the body are detailed in the aforementioned magazine, as well as several pages in Capotosto’s 441-page “[Complete Book of Woodworking](https://www.abebooks.com/9780060106133/Complete-Book-Woodworking-Capotosto-Rosario-0060106131/plp) .”


The rear hatch provides easy access to the engine and ample storage where the rear seats used to be.

A 1500-cc single-port engine is an upgrade over the 25hp original.

The same owner has cared for this unique creation for more than 50 years, and it looks just as it did when it was featured in *Mechanix Illustrated* more than 60 years ago. Mechanically, the car has a newer, 1500-cc single-port VW engine swapped in place of the original 1100-cc unit, and it has been converted to 12 volts for easier starting. Other than a fussy flywheel and the worn interior, it appears to have survived incredibly well. The unique body makes it a standout wherever it goes, but will also limit the buyer pool compared to a split-window Beetle. We’re curious to see where this custom creation ends up, and there’s about a week to go in the auction as of this writing, so we’ll have to be patient.
