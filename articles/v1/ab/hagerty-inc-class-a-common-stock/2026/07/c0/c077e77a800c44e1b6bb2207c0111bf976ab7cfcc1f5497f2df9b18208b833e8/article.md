# 5 Cars Gaining Value in an Ever-Widening Market

It’s summer, and we’re halfway through the year. That means we’ve also just updated all the prices on[Hagerty Valuation Tools](https://www.hagerty.com/valuation-tools) , and things are as wild as the last time we checked in. The supercar boom continues to expand even as the rest of the collector car market cools and as our[Hagerty Market Rating](https://www.hagerty.com/media/market-trends/the-collector-car-market-continues-to-cool-as-summer-heats-up/) continues to edge its way down. Halo cars like the[Ferrari F40](https://www.hagerty.com/valuation-tools/search?q=ferrari+f40) and[288 GTO](https://www.hagerty.com/valuation-tools/ferrari/288_gto/1985/1985-ferrari-288_gto-base?id=aCn1I000000DGCySAO) continue to post up big increases, but that’s much of the same news we covered[last quarter](https://www.hagerty.com/media/market-trends/hagerty-insider/5-cars-making-big-moves-in-a-mixed-market/) .


Some of our notable gainers of the collector car market this quarter made broad jumps, while others indicate a maturing and more nuanced segment of collector vehicle. Here are five of the most noteworthy winners of the summer.

## [1980–86 Ford F-Series Pickups](https://www.hagerty.com/valuation-tools/search?q=1985+ford+f-150)


Hagerty Marketplace | LauraAtkinson_iueg


**Average increase:** +32%


Arriving in 1980, Ford’s seventh generation F-Series pickups marked the first time since 1965 that Ford’s signature truck got a ground-up redesign. Styling evolved with more angular sides as well as a squared-off grille for the iconic “bullnose” look that the F-Series kept through the mid-1990s. The interior also greatly improved for look, comfort, and function with color-keyed trim in more places, better dash pad, and full gauge suite on higher trim levels. Power options were all over the board, from the immensely durable 300 cid inline-six, to the 302 and 351 Windsor V-8s up to a 6.9-liter diesel and 460 big-block for tasks requiring more grunt. Buyers flocked to the seventh generation F-Series, quickly making it America’s best-selling truck, a title that Ford has held to this day.


In general, vintage truck prices have been slow for a while, but there has been a notable uptick in activity being led by the F-Series. Drilling down a bit deeper, the jump in value is largely driven by heavy-duty models catching up to the lighter duty models. Historically, 3/4-ton and 1-ton models have commanded less than half tons, as their rides are significantly harsher while their gearing and larger engines make them especially thirsty beasts to operate. But it seems that this is beginning to matter less to buyers on the hunt for clean classic pickups. Other areas of the truck market seem to be maturing as well (see our fifth pick) so we’re watching for more headlines in the coming months.


## [2005–09 Ferrari F430](https://www.hagerty.com/valuation-tools/search?q=ferrari+f430)


Mecum


**Average increase:** +25%


Succeeding the[360](https://www.hagerty.com/valuation-tools/search?q=ferrari+360) , the Ferrari F430 took what was a solid starting point and turned up the wick. Propelling it was a 490 hp V-8 capable of screaming up to 8500 rpm, mated to either a six-speed gated manual transmission or the more common paddle-shifted F1 transmission. By 2008, Ferrari offered the lightened and sharpened Scuderia, which bumped output over the 500 horsepower mark as well as offering a limited run of 499 Scuderias in the 16M Spider trim. Praise has been heaped on the F430 for its overall improvements to the 360 in all aspects, including its ability to adapt to different driving styles from cruising to all-out performance, as well as the F430’s switch to a timing chain over the 360’s timing belt, leading to more reliable operation and less reasons to visit your local Ferrari service department.


Last quarter, we discussed more Ferraris than we could shake a stick at, and here we are again with Ferrari claiming one of the top spots on our list. While there were plenty of other Ferraris that returned to post big gains, the F430’s spot here comes from the fact that the rise in value didn’t come from the ultra exclusive Scuderia or 16M (oh by the way, that one just crested $1M), but from the standard F430. In the modern Ferrari hierarchy the F430 is still on the lower end at around $150,000 for a driver, so it is looking like a viable model for those priced out of newer models. As for future trends, time will tell, as long as the supercar market stays hot, I wouldn’t expect to see prices any lower at this point.


## [2005–06 Ford GT](https://www.hagerty.com/valuation-tools/ford/gt/2005/2005-ford-gt-base?id=aCn1I000000DUKgSAO)


James Lipman


**Average increase:** +25%


America’s first mainstream modern supercar, the Ford GT was first teased at the North American International Auto Show in 2002 to an ecstatic public. Ford immediately realized they had to build it, with Bill Ford, Jr. quickly announcing the car would go into production. Borrowing styling from the GT40 race cars of the ’60s, this mid-engine exotic rides on a steel space frame with aluminum and composite body panels. Power comes from a supercharged 5.4-liter DOHC V-8 producing 550 horsepower and 500 lb-ft of torque, paired to a Ricardo six-speed manual transaxle. This combination provided blistering performance, even by today’s standards, with a 0–60 time of 3.3 seconds and a top speed of 205 mph. Ford built the GT from 2005 to 2006, and production totaled 4038 examples, with 343 of those being the Gulf Oil liveried “Heritage” editions, as a tribute to the 1968 and ’69 Le Mans-winning car.


The 2005–06 Ford GTs is one of the original “instant collector” cars of the modern era, but since the introduction of the latest iteration of the GT, these cars have been rather sleepy in the market only posting moderate gains over the past eight years. The recent energy in the supercar market seems to have energized the GT, though, with low-mile or no-mile examples posting strong results and multiple, in-wrapper Heritage Editions breaking the $1M mark.


This recent shift means you’ll be hard-pressed to find a decent GT under $500,000 and a no-mile car with all four options (stripes, BBS wheels, painted calipers, McIntosh stereo) will put you over the $700,000 mark, which is approaching new (2017–22) GT money. Taking a quick look at the Heritage Editions, the increase this quarter was even greater for those at a 54% average increase, putting the top dollar past the $1M mark. It’s clear that the current supercar craze is affecting everything and the GT is just one clear example of how it doesn’t need to come from Italy to see the benefits.


## [2011–12 Lexus LFA](https://www.hagerty.com/valuation-tools/lexus/lfa/2012/2012-lexus-lfa-base?id=aCn1I0000008eWMSAY)


Broad Arrow


**Average increase:** +24%


There’s clever engineering, and then there’s obsessing over the details. The team responsible for the Lexus LFA fall solidly in the latter camp. Toyota could’ve released the car with an aluminum body, but apparently that wasn’t good enough, so part way through development, the design was scrapped so that the shell could be carbon fiber. The Yamaha-designed V-10 engine is so well balanced and responsive, it revs from idle to its 9000 RPM redline in 0.6 seconds and creates a unforgettable shriek of an exhaust note in the process. The body, meanwhile, is shaped so that every surface plays a part in directing air to achieve maximum downforce.


The entire process of creating the LFA took a decade to accomplish, but when it was was released, people weren’t exactly lining up to buy them. LFAs sat unsold in dealer showrooms unsold for ages. It has only been in the past five years that serious attention and interest has been directed at this marvel of automotive engineering. A steady rise in values has resulted.


It might not be as in-your-face as a Lamborghini, or have the cachet of a Ferrari, but for those who know, it’s an amazing machine. LFAs are rare to market with just 500 produced (including 50 Nürburgring Editions), so a handful of showings can really move the needle. Earlier in the year, a standard LFA hammered sold at[$1.8M](https://bringatrailer.com/listing/2012-lexus-lfa-36/) and a handful of others have reportedly changed hands privately above the $1M mark, so it’s safe to say the LFA has gotten caught up in the supercar whirlwind. If you’re interested in any LFA, it will be difficult to find one under $800,000 now compared to 12 months ago, when that would buy you a super low mile example. Of course, we will be keeping a very close eye on these cars as things continue to develop.


## [1984–89 Toyota Pickup](https://www.hagerty.com/valuation-tools/search?q=toyota+pickup)


Toyota


**Average increase:** +23%


Simply marketed to U.S. drivers as the Toyota Pickup (Hilux to everyone else), the 1984–89 Toyota Pickup represents the fourth generation of Toyota’s compact truck lineup and the model that cemented the nameplate’s reputation for reliability and durability in North America. The key to this durability came from Toyota’s immensely reliable 22R and 22RE engines, which are known to handle hundreds of thousands of miles without breaking a sweat. Their robust nature came at the expense of max performance, but who really buys a little pickup for the speed, anyway?


These trucks have a loyal fanbase and the Toyotas that managed to survive decades of hard use are highly sought after, with pristine examples bringing well into the $40,000 range. While the 23% increase is noteworthy, the move does bring an element of nuance. While 4×4 models did experience a nominal bump, the real action lies with the 2WD trucks, which tightened the value gap noticeably after numerous strong transactions this spring. The 4×4-equipped Toyota Pickups are still king, but it does show that buyers are becoming less picky about configuration and more interested in condition. Don’t worry, though, you haven’t missed the boat, these are still very affordable trucks averaging $10,000 to $12,000 for a solid driver (condition #3) example.
