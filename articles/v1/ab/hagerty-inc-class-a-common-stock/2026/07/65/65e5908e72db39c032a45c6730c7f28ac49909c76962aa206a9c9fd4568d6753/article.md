# Hagerty Indexes Trend Upwards Despite a Picky Market

*The Hagerty Price Guide Indexes—first published in 2009—are a series of stock market-style indexes that average the condition #2 (“excellent”) values of representative vehicles, or “component” cars, from a particular segment. These indexes are updated quarterly and provide an overview of how these segments of the collector car market are performing overall, as well as relative to each other.*


The year is more than halfway done, and it’s been a wild ride so far. Some areas of the market have fared well while others have cooled gently, all while being overshadowed by the explosive growth of modern exotics. While things tend to quiet down in the couple months leading up to the Monterey auctions in August, seven of our 11 market indexes showed growth, with none of our other indexes declining.


Yet again,[Supercars](https://www.hagerty.com/#h-supercar-index) are the bright spot in the market with broad and sustained growth beyond what anyone could’ve expected a year ago. While many of our other indexes ([British Cars](https://www.hagerty.com/#h-british-car-index) ,[Ferrari](https://www.hagerty.com/#h-ferrari-index) ,[American Muscle](https://www.hagerty.com/#h-american-muscle-car-index) ,[German Cars](https://www.hagerty.com/#h-german-car-index) ,[1950s American](https://www.hagerty.com/#h-1950s-american-car-index) ,[RADindex](https://www.hagerty.com/#h-radindex) , and[Japanese](https://www.hagerty.com/#h-japanese-vehicle-index) ) posted gains, many can be explained away by big increases in a handful of cars. While the[Hagerty Market Rating](https://www.hagerty.com/media/market-trends/the-collector-car-market-continues-to-cool-as-summer-heats-up/) still sits in “flat” territory, the market indexes and their component cars show the more nuanced nature of the collector car world. Anyone looking for clarity in this wild market may be looking for some time more.


## Blue Chip Index


Aston Martin


*The Hagerty “Blue Chip” Index of the automotive A-List is a stock market-style index that averages the values of 25 of the most sought-after collectible automobiles of the post-war era.*


Following last quarter’s 2% increase, Hagerty’s Blue Chip Index has remained rock steady heading into the summer. Of the 25 component cars, nearly 70% of them tracked no movement with the rest showing mixed results.


Late spring through early summer is typically a slow time for Blue Chip collectors, and this year is no exception. Upward movement was confined to just three cars. The Aston Martin DB5 jumped 17%, the 1965 Shelby GT350 gained 12% after last quarter’s bump, and the Shelby Cobra 427 notched a 4% increase. Balancing these increases out were five cars losing value: the Lancia Aurelia Spider America (-12%), Bentley S1 Continental (-1%), Tucker 48 (-3%), Rolls-Royce Silver Cloud (-3%), and BMW 507 (-1%).


Heading into August, this segment will be the one to watch with the auctions in Monterey, California setting the tone for expensive, top tier vintage collector cars for the remainder of the year.


## British Car Index


Hagerty Marketplace


*The Hagerty Index of British Cars is a stock market-style index that averages the values of 10 of the most iconic British sports cars from the 1950s-70s.*


Following last quarter’s 6% dip, Hagerty’s British Car Index pulled off a 2% recovery, ending six consecutive quarters of downward movement.


Of the 10 component cars, nearly half posted gains, while just three continued to retreat in value. Leading the pack was the MGA 1600 which posted a remarkable gain of 10%, closely followed by the Jaguar E-Type and Triumph TR3A, which gained 7%. Meanwhile, the Triumph TR6 posted a 12% loss, followed by modest drops by the Austin-Healey 100M Le Mans (-2%) and Jaguar XK120 Roadster (-1%).


For the past four years, the British Car Index has been trending downward, facing headwinds from a shifting market. While the recent gain does stir some optimism, past trends suggest further monitoring of this market for signs of true recovery.


## Ferrari Index


Broad Arrow


*The Hagerty Ferrari Index is a stock market-style index that averages the values of 13 of the most sought-after street Ferraris of the 1950s-70s.*


Hagerty’s Ferrari Index saw a sizable increase this past quarter, with a 9% jump. But this major shift came mostly thanks to one component car, the 250 LM. The rare (32 built) racer’s staggering 29% jump in value comes at the news of a private treaty sale well above our posted numbers in the price guide, putting this model into low-end 250 GTO territory in terms of value.


Outside of the 250 LM, only three other component cars saw changes. The 365 GTB/4 Daytona Coupe gained 6%, while the 330 GTC and 410 Superamerica adjusted downward by 2% and 1%, respectively. Otherwise, Enzo-era Ferraris have remained steady for the most part. The upcoming Monterey auctions are historically a good test for this segment, so it will be important to keep a close eye on the auctions for a true gauge of the classic Ferrari market.


## American Muscle Car Index


Mecum


*The Hagerty Index of American Muscle Cars is a stock market-style index that averages the values of the rarest and most sought-after muscle cars.*


Following a quiet beginning to the year, Hagerty’s Muscle Car Index enjoyed a 2% increase as summer heated up. This follows another flurry of activity in the late spring and although fewer component cars saw movement from the[previous quarter](https://www.hagerty.com/media/market-trends/hagerty-insider/in-a-drowsy-market-hagerty-indexes-highlight-models-still-wide-awake/) (eight instead of 11), the ones that increased did so by strong amounts.


The clear driver of this quarter’s movement was the staggering 75% jump by the American Motors AMX Super Stock. These are very rarely seen, and a strong sale of one this past quarter prompted a large adjustment. In addition, big increases were posted by the 1970 Chevelle SS LS6 (15%), 1965 Shelby GT350 (12%), and 1970 Oldsmobile 4-4-2 W-30 (10%). These jumps were only slightly tempered by the 1970 Buick GS455’s 18% loss, followed by moderate drops for the 1965 GTO, 1968 Shelby GT500 KR and 1969 Mustang Boss 429.


Despite the index’s overall gain, muscle cars remain a mixed bag as the segment looks to find clear direction amongst changing market conditions.


## German Car Index


Broad Arrow


*The Hagerty Index of German Cars is a stock market-style index that averages the values of 21 of the most sought-after cars from BMW, Mercedes-Benz, and Porsche from the 1950s-70s.*


Hagerty’s German Car Index clawed back last quarter’s 1% loss with an equally modest 1% gain. Of the 13 component cars, the three gainers managed to overpower the five losses we tracked. The main contributor to the jump was the earliest Porsche 356s produced at the Gmünd, Austria factory, which increased by 13% following years of no movement, as well as the 18% bump to the 1965 Porsche 356C 1600 SC Coupe. The 8% decreases to the 1967 Porsche 911S Targa and Mercedes-Benz 190SL, as well as more modest decreases across the board, helped temper the gain.


This is another case where one occasional move for a rare car changes the entire game. The underlying trend is that older German collectors are slowly cooling while new models are exploding in value.


## 1950s American Car Index


Mecum


*The Hagerty Index of 1950s American Classics is a stock market-style index that averages the values of 19 of the most sought-after collectible American automobiles of the 1950s.*


Hagerty’s 1950s American Car Index came in with a 1% gain, erasing last quarter’s 1% loss, but this segment still has ground to claw back after a year of consistent decreases. Of the 19 component cars, five gained in value while another five lost value over the previous quarter.


The main drivers of this increase were the 20% increase by the 1958 Studebaker Golden Hawk and the 10% bump to the 1953 Hudson Hornet. Neither car is a common sight in the market, so further growth of this magnitude is unlikely. Meanwhile, the 12% decrease by the 1950 Oldsmobile Series 88 Wagon and the 8% loss to the 1956 Ford Fairlane Sunliner and 1957 Fairlane Skyliner show that more common entries are showing signs of weakness.


While it has long been expected that 1950s American cars will struggle as younger and younger enthusiasts enter the hobby, the real picture is much more nuanced with a clear direction yet to materialize.


## Affordable Classics Index


Mecum


*The Hagerty Index of Affordable Classics is a stock market-style index that averages the values of 13 undervalued cars, priced around $40,000, from the 1950s-70s.*


Hagerty’s Affordable Classics Index entered the second half of the year tracking no movement, following the previous quarter’s 1% loss. Lack of index movement doesn’t indicate lack of movement in individual vehicles, though. Of the 13 component cars, four gained in value this past quarter while two decreased.


The notable gainers included the Volkswagen Beetle and Karmann Ghia, which increased by 5% and 7%, respectively, as well as the Datsun 240Z (5%), MGB (4%), and Studebaker Lark (2%). Downward movement was spread across the Triumph TR6 (-12%), 1949 Buick Roadmaster Sedanet (-10%), and Studebaker Avanti (-3%).


Some traditionally “affordable” classics have grown out of reach over the past few years, but some have also started to come back down to earth. Expect this trend to continue as some of the more mainstream parts of the market continue to soften.


## RADindex


[Brandan Gillogly](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/brandan-gillogly/)


*The Hagerty RADindex is a stock market-style index that averages the values of 21 collectible vehicles from the 1980s and 1990s.*


Hagerty’s RADindex keeps chugging along, and posted up another small increase of 1.5% this past quarter. The new all-time high comes at the hands of a number of small increases across seven of the 21 component cars, with no vehicles losing value. The biggest gainers were the 1987 Buick GNX and Lancia Delta Integrale, which both increased by 6%. Smaller increases came from the Delorean DMC-12 (3%), third gen Camaro Z28 (2%), C4 Corvette ZR-1 (2%), E30 M3 (1%), and Acura NSX (1%).


Slow and steady wins, and the RADindex keeps delivering. This collection of next generation collectors is as steady and sure as they come, and should enjoy a bright future as market interests continue to shift towards later collectors.


## Truck and SUV Index


Mecum


*The Hagerty Truck and SUV Index is a stock market-style index that averages the values of 19 collectible trucks and SUVs from the 1940s to the 1990s.*


Of our newly added indexes, Hagerty’s Truck Index is the only one that has tracked no meaningful movement. A lack of overall movement, however, does not mean there was no activity. Of the 19 component trucks, 10 saw movement with a half moving down while the other five increased.


The clear winners were the 1984–89 Toyota Pickup and 1948–52 Ford F-Series increasing by 14% and 12%, respectively, while the 1966–77 Ford Bronco’s surprising 14% loss offset the gains. The mixed bag of movement clearly shows that buyers are getting increasingly picky about what they’re buying and how much they’re willing to shell out. Discernment and restraint is a positive in the long term, something seemingly absent in some other areas of the market at the moment.


## Japanese Vehicle Index


Subaru


*The Hagerty Japanese Vehicle Index is a stock market-style index that averages the values of 19 collectible Japanese vehicles from the 1960s to the 2010s.*


After nearly a year of no movement, Hagerty’s Japanese Index roared to life increasing by 11%. This increase was largely driven by a spike in value of the Lexus LFA, which jumped by 18%, riding the wave of the modern exotic market. Coming back to earth, five additional component cars increased in value like the Toyota Pickup and Subaru SVX, gaining by 14% and 6%, respectively. No losses were logged while 12 others saw no change.


In broad terms, aside from the ultra-exclusive LFA, the Japanese market is tracking upward at a steady, sustainable pace. It’s a good sign that what was recently an emerging hobby segment has matured.


## Supercar Index


Broad Arrow


*The Hagerty Supercar Index is a stock market-style index that averages the values of 15 collectible modern supercars and hypercars.*


Following last quarter’s 19% increase, Hagerty’s Supercar Index continues to rise at a fast clip, rising by an additional 7%. Like with the previous quarter, the increase was largely driven by, well, everything. Of the 15 component cars, 13 posted movement, with only one of those decreasing in value.


Many of the increases were in the double digit territory as well, with cars like the 2005–06 Ford GT increasing by 35%, Porsche 959 by 24%, and Ferrari F40 by 20%. Other big movers from last quarter like the Ferrari F50 and 288 GTO also continued their rise posting gains of 12% and 13%, respectively. The only decrease was the more niche Bugatti EB110, which slid by 3%.


Supercars remain one of the most vibrant areas of the collector car market in 2026, and the latest update proves that last quarter was not an anomaly. If anything, things are heating up more with enthusiasm being spread to cars beyond the ones carrying Ferrari badges.
