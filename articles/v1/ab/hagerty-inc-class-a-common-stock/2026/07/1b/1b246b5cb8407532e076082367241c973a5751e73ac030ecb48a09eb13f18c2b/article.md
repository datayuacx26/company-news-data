# Aston Martin Dreadnought Unveiled as Joystick-Driven Military SUV

SUVs sell spectacularly well, so few were surprised to see Aston Martin plant its stake in the segment with[the DBX](https://www.hagerty.com/media/new-car-reviews/first-drive-2026-aston-martin-dbx-s-adds-aggression-and-loses-weight/) . Its second SUV just made its debut, and this time we didn’t see it coming. The new Aston Martin Dreadnought is a digital concept with army-like looks that was created for *Call of Duty: Modern Warfare 4.*


Aston Martin worked jointly with the game’s developers and publishers to design an SUV that’s pegged well outside the realm of reality. After all, a car headed straight to a virtual world doesn’t need to pass a crash test. There’s not much of a link between the Dreadnought and the DBX, but front-end styling cues such as the slanted grille with driving lights and a hood bulge remind us of older Aston models. From the side, the off-roader blends[Jeep Wrangler](https://www.hagerty.com/media/news/2027-jeep-wrangler-laredo-pairs-retro-looks-with-modern-off-road-capability/) -like ground clearance with the silhouette of a shooting brake.


Set in modern times, *Call of Duty: Modern Warfare 4* is a first-person shooter in which players can use a multitude of weapons ranging from pistols to military-grade material. That’s no match for a run-of-the-mill SUV, so the Dreadnought is decked out with exterior armor plating as well as adaptive combat-zone intelligence systems. The display on the dashboard provides car-specific information, such as the average speed, an inclinometer, and traction control settings, as well as menus labeled “weapons” and “kill streak.”


Aston Martin nonetheless added a large serving of luxury; its goal wasn’t to build a HUMVEE follow-up. The cabin is decked out with carbon fiber trim, Oxford Tan upholstery, and a metallic gold gear selector.

This is a digital concept that’s not headed for production, so the specs are a moot point. Aston Martin can claim that the Dreadnought is powered by a Boeing engine, a hamster spinning a little wheel, or anything in between. And yet, the specifications are surprisingly realistic: We’re told the Dreadnought uses a V-12 engine whose exhaust note can be clearly heard in the game and that it’s built on a carbon fiber chassis.


After launching the game, players will be able to find the Aston Martin Dreadnought in what the company describes as key points of interest within both DMZ and Call of Duty: Warzone modes. It’s too early to tell precisely what you’ll be able to do with the SUV, or where you’ll be able to go with it, but you won’t have to wait long to find out. *Call of Duty: Modern Warfare 4* will be available on several consoles, including PlayStation 5, Nintendo Switch 2, and XBOX Series X|S, and Steam, starting on Friday, October 23, 2026.


Nothing suggests that the Aston Martin Dreadnought, or anything like it, will see the light that awaits at the end of a production line. What’s the point, then? Good question. We’ll let Aston Martin answer it.


“Our brand diversification strategy is built on exploring new dimensions of luxury, and expanding Aston Martin’s footprint into the gaming world allows us to engage with a new generation on their own terms,” explained Stefano Saporetti, the company’s director of brand diversification, in a statement. “We are ensuring that the spirit of Aston Martin resonates powerfully with the luxury consumers of tomorrow.”


Aston Martin isn’t the only car company that has leveraged video games as a powerful marketing tool. Racing titles like *Gran Turismo* and *Forza* helped fuel the popularity of several Japanese-market-only cars, including[the Nissan R34 GT-R](https://www.hagerty.com/media/market-trends/hagerty-insider/market-spotlight/your-handy-1999-2002-r34-nissan-skyline-gt-r-buyers-guide/) . Some of these cars are worth serious money now that they’re old enough to legally import into the United States, and now that the kids who grew up racing them after homework have become adult collectors. Outside of the car world, Ford added a classic Mustang GT500[to Fortnite in 2025](https://www.hagerty.com/media/news/ford-and-fortnite-team-up-to-bring-gamers-a-shelby-gt500/) , and it made the mighty Mustang GTD[available in both Fortnite and Rocket League](https://www.hagerty.com/media/news/game-on-you-can-now-drive-a-ford-mustang-gtd-in-fortnite-and-rocket-league/) shortly after.
