# My 1963 Chevrolet Corvair Monza Spyder Belongs in the Family

In 1975, I told my mother that I was withdrawing from college. Heartbroken, she lamented that I would never get a college degree and thus not make it very far in life. But I started a van conversion business with my friend that went on to become modestly successful. That all came crashing down in 1979, during the second oil crisis. I suddenly found myself broke and dreaming of going back to college. Mom helped with the finances and Dad came through with my transportation: a[1963 Corvair 500 coupe](https://www.hagerty.com/valuation-tools/chevrolet/corvair/1963/1963-chevrolet-corvair-500) he bought for $200.


I found the Corvair easy to work on and economical. The 80-hp flat-six engine was underpowered, but the rear-engine layout had some unexpected bonuses—it was the only car on campus that could make it up the notorious Orchard Hill on the University of Massachusetts Amherst campus while covered in snow. The secret? Going in reverse.


Lane Raymond


I soon learned of the high-performance version of my base Corvair, called the Spyder. My brother was into Porsches at the time, and we lived about 1.5 hours from[Lime Rock Park](https://www.hagerty.com/media/motorsports/no-one-has-a-bad-time-at-the-lime-rock-historic-festival/) , in Connecticut. One time we were there with the Sports Car Club of America, and his 911 got dusted by a Spyder. I followed the guy into the pits and asked, “What the heck is that?” He said it was the stock engine, which was turbocharged from the factory to the tune of 150 horsepower. I looked at my brother and said, “We got to get one of these.”


Lane Raymond


I didn’t start looking in earnest until after I graduated. One day in 1983, I got a call from one of my college friends who was at a[swap meet](https://www.hagerty.com/media/lists/7-tips-for-a-great-day-at-a-swap-meet/) at Charlotte Motor Speedway. He said, “Bob, I found it.” It was a red 1963 Spyder convertible, for which the seller wanted only $1300. It had a power top, factory bumperettes, and a four-speed manual. I flew down at the end of April—and almost died driving it home because the turbo was seized and the exhaust was leaking.


Despite the drivability issues, the Spyder had good bones; it was solid, straight, and hadn’t been in any collisions. I fixed the turbo and exhaust leak and did a mediocre restoration on it in 1984. Then I drove it. All the attention I got certainly fed my ego. The Spyder demands that kind of respect. It really is a performance car, a vestige of the era of driving that raised me.


Lane Raymond


Life went on, and my Corvair was my solace whenever I needed to take a drive somewhere to collect my thoughts. I had been lucky enough to keep the car after a divorce from my first wife. As fate would have it, the Corvair was the conduit for meeting my second wife. She lived near[Clark’s Corvair](https://ssl.corvair.com/user-cgi/pages.cgi) , one of the only shops near me that sold the parts I needed for the Spyder.


Lane Raymond


The Corvair got a professional restoration in 2001. A good friend of mine was a skilled body guy, and he spent two years meticulously restoring the car. When I decoded the VIN, I learned that the Spyder had originally been black, so when the time came for paint, I had it resprayed the factory color. He did a phenomenal job—the paint on the doorjambs is nicer than that on my new Tesla. I swapped the original engine for a later 164-cubic-inch unit. It’s totally stock and runs and drives beyond brand-new. Ralph Nader[claimed](https://www.hagerty.com/media/automotive-history/will-the-corvair-kill-you/) the Corvair Spyder was “unsafe at any speed,” but I like to say it is gratifying at every shift.


My wife requested that I put seatbelts in the back of the car so that I could bring our twin son and daughter along in their child seats. When I used to pick them up from school, their classmates would go crazy. Those children are now in college. The Corvair has been with them all their lives.


Though it was originally red when he got it, Raymond had the Corvair resprayed to its original color when it was restored in 2001.


Lane Raymond


Indeed, after 43 years, the celebration of the Corvair has become a family affair. My oldest son from my first marriage took the photos, and my daughter helped me write this story. Her twin brother has helped me keep the car in tip-top mechanical shape, replacing the clutch, pressure plate, shift linkage, and brakes.


> The celebration of the Corvair has become a family affair.


I have already said in my will that my youngest son will get the Spyder—he’s put in more than his fair share of work into his car—though it’s debatable whether it is an asset or a liability. I can only hope the next generation will take care of it and honor the memories it holds.


Lane Raymond
