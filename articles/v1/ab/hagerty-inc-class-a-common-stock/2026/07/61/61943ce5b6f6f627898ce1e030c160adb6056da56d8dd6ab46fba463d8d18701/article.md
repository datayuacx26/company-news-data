# Nissan’s Manual-Equipped Z NISMO Starts Under $70K

When we drove the six-speed manual-equipped 2027 Nissan Z NISMO earlier this year, we lauded Nissan for adding the row-your-own option to what was already a very engaging car. “Iteration complete,” was the[succinct verdict passed down](https://www.hagerty.com/media/new-car-reviews/2027-nissan-z-nismo-manual-first-drive-iteration-complete/) by managing editor Eddy Eckart. We did not, however, know what this stylish sports car would cost you. We now have the answers.


Nissan


Nissan


Nissan released pricing details for the 2027 Z earlier this week. The headliner here is that whether you want the nine-speed automatic or the six-speed manual, you’ll pay the same base price. That’s awesome, because as our choices for three-pedal sports cars continue to dwindle, it’s not uncommon for the more involved option to command a price premium. In fact, the standard gearbox across the lineup is the six-speed; you’ll have to opt for the nine-speed—again, at no added cost.


Nissan


You can have the 2027 Nissan Z in three flavors: Sport, which is the entry-level car, Performance, a mid-tier trim that unlocks access to a few key components like a clutch-type, mechanical limited-slip differential and improved brakes, and the[top-dog NISMO](https://www.hagerty.com/media/new-car-reviews/2025-nissan-z-nismo-review-wants-needs-and-nine-speeds/) , which gets a host of go-fast flourishes including further improved brakes, special tuning, firmer suspension, and additional chassis bracing.


The Nissan Z Sport will start at $45,725, including a $1245 destination and handling fee. The entry-level Z still offers decent content, including a six-speaker sound system, an 8-inch touchscreen, automatic climate control, cloth seats with manual adjustability, and more. You’ll get smaller two-piston front brake calipers and smaller rotors, but you still get the 400-hp, twin-turbo, 3.0-liter VR30DTT engine.

Stepping up to the[Z Performance](https://www.hagerty.com/media/new-car-reviews/first-look-review-2023-nissan-z/) will run you $55,725, again with the destination and handling fee. This trim, in our eyes, unlocks a lot of the desirable goodies. Forged 19-inch wheels from RAYS replace the 18-inch units on the Z Sport while also offering a slight weight savings. Four-piston calipers up front will give you added stopping potential. Higher-spec Bridgestone Potenza S007 rubber livens up the handling and performance. New this year, the Z Performance also gets larger diameter shock absorbers to enhance stability and damping.

Inside, you unlock heated seats fitted with suede and leather, and new for this year, there’s a tan option instead of just black. A Bose eight-speaker sound system and a larger 9-inch touchscreen help improve the experience within the cabin.


The range-topping[Z NISMO](https://www.hagerty.com/media/new-car-reviews/2024-nissan-z-nismo-review-no-conquest-no-contest/) starts at $68,505. For the added scratch, you’ll unlock all the nicest, most performance-focused options in the lineup. Power jumps to 420 hp and 384 lb-ft of torque. For the 2027 model year, Nissan engineers added upgraded front brake rotors derived from the GT-R that save 19 pounds of unsprung weight and enhance thermal capacity to shrug off track abuse.

Forged RAYS wheels grow a half-inch in width at all corners, and Dunlop SP Sport MAXX GT600 tires replace the Bridgestones of the Z Performance model. Exterior flourishes include an exclusive three-piece spoiler, different front and rear fascias, and exclusive side sills. Inside, you get trim-exclusive Recaro seats shod in leather and alcantara, special interior badging, exclusive graphics for the digital instrument cluster, and all the sound and tech goodies from the Z Performance.

Both the Sport and Performance trims get some revised styling tweaks, including a body-colored bar in the grille opening up front, and a “Z” logo up front instead of the Nissan logo. We love that Nissan is leaning into the Z heritage with little tweaks. Speaking of heritage, a new color, Shinkai Green Pearl Metallic, joins the paint choices. Nissan says it’s “inspired by the green of the original S30-generation Nissan Z.” It looks wonderful. With that new green and the new tan interior color for the Z Performance, we warmly welcome the Z to the lauded “green over tan” club.

Though not as exciting as paint colors, Nissan engineers also revised the fuel tank design for all Z cars to help maintain fuel delivery in sustained, high-lateral-force cornering. It’s a little thing, but we admire Nissan for pushing to incrementally improve a low-volume product like the Z. There’s hope for enthusiast vehicles yet.
