# Hear Ford’s Le Mans Hypercar Crank to Life

Ford’s plan to compete at the 24 Hours of Le Mans in 2027 just got a little closer. Ahead of the new race car’s on-track debut in “just a matter of weeks,” Ford has started up the 5.4-liter, naturally aspirated, Coyote-based V-8 in the chassis, with the accompanying spec hybrid system, for the first time. It’s a big moment for the program, which Ford announced less than[18 months ago](https://www.hagerty.com/media/motorsports/ford-targets-overall-win-at-le-mans-in-2027-will-use-imsa-to-prepare/) .


The 24 Hours of Le Mans is the crown jewel of the World Endurance Championship (WEC, often pronounced “weck”), whose top class is known as Hypercar. The equivalent class in the U.S. road-racing series, the IMSA WeatherTech SportSCar Championship, is called GTP. We[confirmed in January of 2025](https://www.hagerty.com/media/motorsports/ford-targets-overall-win-at-le-mans-in-2027-will-use-imsa-to-prepare/) that Ford will compete in IMSA as a way to prepare for WEC.


Ford already competes in a production-based class of the IMSA WeatherTech SportsCar Championship with a pair of Mustangs.


[Cameron Neveu](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/cameron-neveu/)


Don’t let the “Hypercar” designation fool you: Ford’s as-yet-unnamed entry is a built-from-scratch race car. It’s also a cross-continental project. While the engine is designed, developed, and built[in-house](https://www.hagerty.com/media/motorsports/ford-plans-to-build-its-new-hypercar-racer-mostly-in-house/) in Michigan, the chassis is built in France by legendary race shop Oreca. One of four approved Hypercar constructors, Oreca also builds the chassis for the Acura ARX-6 (IMSA), the Alpine A424 (WEC), and the Genesis GMR-001 (WEC). To give you an idea of Oreca’s experience, it’s responsible for 10 consecutive wins at the 24 Hours of Le Mans.


Ford Racing / Pierre Simenel


“Hearing the Coyote V-8 come alive within its intended home for the first time confirmed that months of integration work between the powertrain and chassis teams had paid off,” writes Dan Sayers, program manager for Ford’s WEC Hypercar program. He[moved over to Ford from Red Bull Ford Powertrains](https://www.hagerty.com/media/news/ford-ceo-jim-farley-dishes-ambitions-for-2027-hypercar-class/) , where he helped develop the power unit for the 2026 Formula 1 regulations.


In that same release, Sayers confirms that track testing will begin next month, in August. Since the WEC race calendar is largely comprised of European tracks, it’s only natural that the tests will take place there.


Ford


Though Ford’s Hypercar program is still months away from its first race, many pieces have already fallen into place—most recently, the remaining two spots in the six-driver lineup. America’s Logan Sargeant, Germany’s Mike Rockenfeller, and Britain’s Sebastian Priaulx are joined by Australian Matt Campbell, the British-born Swede Tom Blomqvist, and fellow Brit Nick Yelloly. (Rockenfeller and Priaulx currently share the #64 Ford Racing Mustang in IMSA.) The latter three drivers are currently competing in the top class of WEC’s American counterpart, the IMSA WeatherTech SportsCar Championship. Right now, Campbell and Blomqvist drive for Acura, whose ARX-06 program is in its final year, and Yelloly for Porsche Penske Motorsport. Porsche, as racing fans will be well aware, sat out the World Endurance Championship this year, including the 24 Hours of Le Mans, and has not announced a plan to return.


Ford will join newcomers McLaren and Genesis on the WEC grid in 2027. They will face off against recent overall winners Toyota and Ferrari, plus Aston Martin, Peugeot, Cadillac, and BMW.


When will Ford officially unveil its Hypercar? They’re mum on the date, but its competition debut is a ways out—our money is on the 24 Hours of Daytona in January of 2027—but Sayers says its “track debut” is only “a matter of weeks.” If forced to speculate, we’d keep a close eye on the next race on the WEC calendar, its only stateside round, which takes place at the Circuit of the Americas in Austin, Texas, on September 6. However, if Ford wants to lean into the heritage angle, there’s no better place than the Goodwood Revival a few weeks later, September 18–20. All three of the Le Mans GT40s from Ford’s 1966 1-2-3 victory[will be at the Revival](https://www.goodwood.com/grr/event-coverage/festival-of-speed/fords-1966-le-mans-gt40s-enjoy-a-rare-reunion-at-the-2026-festival-of-speed/) , and that’s a photo op if we’ve ever heard of one.
