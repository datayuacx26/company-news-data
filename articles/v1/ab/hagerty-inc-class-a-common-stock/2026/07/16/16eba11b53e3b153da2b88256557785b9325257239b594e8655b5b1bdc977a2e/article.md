# The 6 Phases of a Project Car

The draw of a project car is something special. However, the emotional process of taking a machine that is broken, damaged, busted, faded, or generally just rough around the edges is a rollercoaster that can make even the most experienced a little sick to their stomach.


From first sight to last drive,[a project car](https://www.hagerty.com/media/tags/project-car/) is nearly always asking for something. Some project cars, when we finally get them home, ask for so much that we feel the same pit in our stomach as a free-fall drop. Others require us to work at such an agonizingly slow pace that there is nothing to do but wait and try to stay calm. Much like a rollercoaster, a project car has phases, and some of us have found that acknowledging them can help ease the process—or, at least, confirm that we’re not going crazy.


## Phase 1: Excitement


Josh Sweeney


When you’re at the amusement park, standing in line for a ride, seeing the excitement of those filing off the ride only increases the feeling of giddiness inside you. When it comes to project cars, that’s the shopping phase. Looking around for a project that catches your interest is something that, for some, could almost be classified as an addiction. ( *Kyle has absolutely zero experience with this “addiction.” — Ed.* ) Finding a car, negotiating and agreeing on a price, and hauling your treasure home is a high that some chase for a long time. In fact, it’s difficult to feel a similar amount of excitement toward the end of the project, when the reality of what the car is (and isn’t) is crystal-clear.


Excitement is a powerful motivator. Not all exciting moments in a project can be planned or timed conveniently, so whenever possible, harness your excitement to make progress. Why? Because shortly after excitement comes…


## Phase 2: Frustration


I hope I never look at my projects the way I look at dirty dishes.


[Kyle Smith](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/kyle-smith/)


Not every task or undertaking on a project car is going to be fun, interesting, or even novel. There will be times when everything a car needs boils down to *time* . Oil changes, brake flushes, and tire checks can get tiring if you repeat them over and over. True, the same mundane tasks can also be cathartic, but sometimes the task is no longer enjoyable, and it feels like one more thing that needs to be done in a world full of things that just need to be done. Welcome to the frustration phase.


There are, of course, also projects and tasks that feel like distractions from the fun stuff. Diagnosing and repairing a wiring issue that keeps the car from being reliable is rarely fun, for instance. On the other hand, not driving at all is even less fun.


## Phase 3: Joy


[Brandan Gillogly](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/brandan-gillogly/)


Project cars are not all bad, though. The frustration of waiting for the roller coaster to climb to the top of that first hill washes away when you drop down, and the adrenaline starts flowing. When it comes to project cars, that moment might be the[first drive](https://www.hagerty.com/media/opinion/mechanical-sympathy/my-ls-swap-corvettes-first-drive-went-exactly-as-expected/) , or perhaps the first time you drive your project to a show or a[Cars and Coffee](https://cars.coffee/) –type event. The joy of experiencing a machine you restored to working order can refill a drained supply of motivation and make you fall in love with a car again, whether it’s your first, second, or 150th drive. Of course, nothing good lasts forever…


## Phase 4: Despair


[Kyle Smith](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/kyle-smith/)


While frustration can be wiped away with a small amount of effort, despair is not so easily shaken. We all hope that clunk we noticed on the last drive is an easy repair…. and we all know what it feels like to discover the problem was far worse than expected.


Sadly, cars care little for what we hope or expect to find. In fact, it’s hardly uncommon to find five more problems without locating the one that prompted you to roll under the car. The overwhelming feeling of hoping for a quick fix and being confronted with a slew of new issues can dash any hopes we had for a good time with our old car. The phase of despair calls for taking a break, or at least finding some way to remind yourself why you entered into this.


## Phase 5: Fulfillment


Josh Sweeney


We take on project cars for a variety of reasons: self-improvement, learning, a challenge. Regardless of what made you think a vintage car might be worth stewarding, the process and experience can be something that grows your confidence and builds perceived personal value. Don’t get high and mighty, but be sure to celebrate or at least reflect on your journey with a project car in a way that allows you to acknowledge how you have changed and grown alongside the car over time. On my cars, there are a few repairs I did years ago that aren’t to my current standard, but they remind me how far I’ve come and that the work I do now is often better than my brain will let me believe.


## Phase 6: Bankruptcy


[Kyle Smith](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/kyle-smith/)


It’s often not one project—though it very much can be—that[empties one’s bank account](https://www.hagerty.com/media/opinion/mechanical-sympathy/car-people-are-lying-to-you-about-their-budgets-and-i-have-the-receipts/comment-page-3/) to the point of low funds or overdraft alerts. Instead, it is the chase of the project-car high. Anyone who has rebuilt an engine entirely themselves can attest to the feeling of hearing it run for the first time. That thrill is unique. At times, I wish I could replace it with drugs; drugs might be cheaper. Bringing something back to life, or making something that was boring into something not only interesting but unique and fun, often costs money. Whether it’s tools, storage space, parts, or a gift to your significant other to warm them to a new purchase, the only thing that will cause more damage than a misplaced blow with the big hammer while rebuilding suspension parts is letting your heart shop with your credit card.


The thing about phases is that they come and go. Being able to stick it out through the tough ones makes the fun ones that extra bit sweeter. Everyone’s experience of the project-car rollercoaster will be a little different depending on where you start, what you expect, and how long you stick it out.
