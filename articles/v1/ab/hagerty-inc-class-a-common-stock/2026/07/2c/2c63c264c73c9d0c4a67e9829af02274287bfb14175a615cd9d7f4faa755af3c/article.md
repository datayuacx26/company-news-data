# 2027 Ram 1500 Rumble Bee Priced in Muscle Car Territory

Ram turned its attention to the long-dormant street-oriented sport truck segment when it unveiled[the 1500 Rumble Bee](https://www.hagerty.com/media/news/ram-goes-full-send-with-rumble-bee-sport-trucks/) in May 2026. This revived hive includes four V-8-powered pickups developed to deliver on-road performance rather than off-road prowess. Pricing for the 2027 Ram 1500 Rumble Bee is out, and the numbers push this muscle truck right into muscle-car territory.


The four trims are called Rumble Bee, Rumble Bee 392, Rumble Bee 392 Track Pack, and Rumble Bee SRT, respectively. It’s not just a badge and a few extra features that set them apart. The base truck gets[a 5.7-liter Hemi V-8](https://www.hagerty.com/media/news/oh-snap-the-hemi-v-8-returns-to-ram-pickup-lineup/) rated at 395 horsepower and 410 pound-feet of torque. Next up is the 392 duo, which benefits from the familiar 6.4-liter Hemi V-8 that posts 470 hp and 455 lb-ft. Finally, the SRT uses the TRX’s supercharged, 6.2-liter V-8 that makes 777 horsepower and 680 pound-feet of torque.


Pricing for the full range is as follows:


- **2027 Ram 1500 Rumble Bee** : $62,790
- **2027 Ram 1500 Rumble Bee** 392: $72,790
- 2 **027 Ram 1500 Rumble Bee 392 Track Pack** : $82,790
- **2027 Ram 1500 Rumble Bee SRT** : $97,790


Note that these figures include a $2795 destination charge. For context,[the 1500 RHO](https://www.hagerty.com/media/new-car-reviews/2025-ram-1500-rho-first-drive-new-horse-allows-the-cart-to-shine/) starts at $77,790, and the TRX reigns over the 1500 lineup with a base price of $102,790 including destination. But if you’re in the market for a Rumble Bee, odds are you’re not cross-shopping it with a lifted off-roader. Over at Dodge,[the Charger Scat Pack Plus](https://www.hagerty.com/media/news/six-cylinder-2026-charger-is-wider-more-powerful-more-luxurious/) costs $62,680 with a 550-horsepower straight-six. Head to Ford, and you can drive home in a 500-horsepower, 5.0-liter V-8-powered[Mustang Dark Horse](https://www.hagerty.com/media/new-car-reviews/2024-ford-mustang-dark-horse-review-proven-ingredients-new-recipe/) for $66,770 if you don’t tick any option boxes.


The fast street truck segment, which seems to pop up and then disappear again every 15 or so years, is enjoying a mini-renaissance. GMC and Chevy have yet to enter the fray, and the Rams are in a class by themselves as far as factory-built hotrod trucks. That said, tuner-built trucks like those offered by Shelby and Roush are already on the market, and you can[go to your Ford parts counter](https://www.hagerty.com/media/new-car-reviews/factory-tuned-we-drive-fords-800-hp-mustang-700-hp-f-150-and-300-hp-maverick/) and have a factory-warrantied 700 horsepower by adding a Whipple supercharger and other goodies to your new F-150.


Seen in another light, the new Charger hasn’t sold as well as Dodge hoped, and we haven’t seen the long-rumored V-8-powered model yet. Cast this way, is the 1500 Rumble Bee the true follow-up to[the last-gen Dodge Challenger](https://www.hagerty.com/media/news/the-dodge-challenger-nameplate-is-dead-for-now/) ? On paper, it makes a strong case for itself: it’s V-8-powered, it’s rowdy, and it’s available[in numerous head-turning configurations](https://www.hagerty.com/media/news/dodge-challenger-gets-two-new-widebody-variants-for-2021/) .


The 2027 Ram 1500 Rumble Bee range is scheduled to reach showrooms in the coming months. The entry point into the range is due out in late 2026, while buyers who want the 392, the 392 Track Pack, or the SRT will need to wait until the first half of 2027.
