# According to You: The Coolest Engine Names

Perhaps it goes without saying, but the readers here at Hagerty Media really know their stuff. When I asked you to tell me[which engine has the coolest name](https://www.hagerty.com/media/hagerty-community/which-engine-has-the-coolest-name/#comments) , I was given more than enough material to whip up this follow-up article. But there’s more to this story, as I learned a few engine names in the process.


I didn’t know about Saab’s “Jungle Drums” nickname or AMC’s “Torque Command.” Even better, I haven’t heard phrases like “Blue Flame” and[“Knucklehead”](https://www.hagerty.com/media/market-trends/2023-bull-market-pick-1936-47-harley-davidson-knucklehead/) in such a long time that it was delightful to read them again. So have a look at some of the coolest engine names here, because I bet you will also be pleased to take this trip down memory lane.


## Chrysler


Paul Stenquist


**Dirty Dingus McGee:** Chrysler TNT.


**Dean C:** 440 Six Pack with Ram Air induction.


**DavidD:** Chrysler FirePower.


**Jeff B:** I might be a little jaded, but my first car was a ’71 Roadrunner with a 383 Magnum. How could you not love a motor called that? It was so fast. I swear it was underrated.


**teutonicscot:** The original 426 Hemi was known as “the Elephant,” based on its sheer size, which was pretty cool.


**Steve B:** So the Hemi was the Elephant. What are elephants supposed to be afraid of? A mouse! So small-block Chevies were the Mouse, and big-blocks were the Rat.


**MrBlack627:** Some favorites: Jeep’s “Go Devil,” and Mopar’s[“Max Wedge.”](https://www.hagerty.com/media/automotive-history/4-13-all-hail-the-413-max-wedge/)


## General Motors


Marketplace/David DeHaan/DMD Photographic


**hyperv6:** It’s not a Official GM name, but the big-block was called a Rat motor.


**Mark P** : I’ve always liked the sounds of the Tech IV, even though it was just a fuel-injected Iron Duke (hence why it was sometimes dubbed the “Low Tech IV”), which was stodgy as anything… but I am a model railroader, after all, and MRC is calling!


**DUB6:** When I was first getting into the hot rod scene, I learned that the Buick “Nailhead” was a tremendously popular engine. Lots of guys were pulling their old “Flatties” out to drop a Nailhead in. Always liked that name.


**Dirty Dingus McGee:** Pontiac Super Duty.


**Tuffy67:**[L88](https://www.hagerty.com/media/automotive-history/l88-corvettes-an-awesome-display-of-power-from-corvettes-early-history/) . Nothing more need be said!


**Pete:** Don’t need a name—409 says it all.


**Old Brad:** I have to go with the Pontiac Strato Streak. I had one in a ’55 Pontiac that I drove all through college in the ’70s. That engine was virtually bulletproof. I have one currently in a ’56 Pontiac, and it runs great.


**winguy:** Wildcat. My first car was a Buick Wildcat, and it had a Wildcat 445. The engine was the old reliable 401 Nailhead, but they used the torque ratings in the name.


**Tom:** Buick’s Wildcat 465 was a responsive beast in my Dad’s Wildcat convertible. The fact that it was a heavy boat of a car didn’t matter when the pedal was to the metal!


**1st gen Firebird:** For me, it’s the Turbo Fire 327. As a kid, I remember seeing the gold emblem on the valve cover, looking over the fender of my dad’s 65 Impala.


**Arlie:** The original muscle-car V-8, “Rocket 88”


**George H:** The Olds Rocket. The name shaped the marketing of the company until its demise decades later.


**Stephen G:** Even had a song about it.


**winguy:** Yup. Kathy Mattea recorded “455 Rocket.” Great song.


**DUB6:** Even before that, “Rocket 88” by Jackie Brenston and his Delta Cats in 1951.


**Brian L:** Chevy 348 big-block engine, called the “Turbo-Thrust.” I have the Tri-Power version in my ’58 Impala. The distinctive scalloped valve cover shape makes this a unique V-8.


**Jeff S:** How about the “FireBall 8”? That’ll show you how long I’ve been involved with cars.


**Dave W:** What about Corvette’s “Blue Flame”?


## Ford


Mecum


**Johnny Atomic:** Easily, by a mile, the coolest engine name ever is Cobra Jet.


**Daniel K:** I’ve always been partial to Ford products and think that the name “Cobra” or “Cobra Jet” had, like the snake, the ability to strike fast in whatever hood they were under. Those 429 and 428 engines made such a marketing impression that a family of Mustangs had the name.


**Randolph K:** I like the Ford Voodoo engine, a flat-plane crank 5.2-liter V-8.


**Greg:** Who’s the boss? Boss 302 / Boss 429.


**Andy:** Without a doubt, any Ford “Boss” anything!


**Ted in Canada:** Well, in the Ford line of engines, K-Code is one of my favorites.


**Jimbo5clan:** Best engine name—Ford 5.2-liter “Voodoo” GT350.


**Appliances are for Kitchens:** Can’t believe nobody had mentioned “Powerstroke.” Pick your displacement, but it perfectly captures the stump-pulling goal of a diesel truck while also leaning on the most important of the four-strokes.


**teutonicscot:** There’s also “Godzilla,” as it refers to both the Nissan GT-R and the Ford 7.3-liter V-8 engine for heavy-duty trucks.


**Timothy W:** I love that Ford had a Thunderbird V-8 that was available in other Fords, not just the sporty personal luxury coupe. Zetec is a pretty cool name, too.
