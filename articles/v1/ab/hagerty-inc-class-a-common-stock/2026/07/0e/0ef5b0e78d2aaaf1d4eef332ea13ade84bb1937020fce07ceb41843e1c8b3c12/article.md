# Twin-Turbo 1990 Nissan 300ZX Makes the Case for an Overlooked Japanese Delight

Japanese performance engineering was absolutely humming in the late ’80s and the early ’90s. Cars like the Mazda Miata, the Toyota Supra, the[Acura NSX](https://www.hagerty.com/media/car-profiles/buyers-guide-199196-acura-nsx/) , and many more were making waves across the globe. One car that sometimes gets overlooked in this era is the Nissan 300ZX. Its V-6 didn’t develop a reputation for withstanding a jet engine’s worth of boost, as did the[Toyota Supra’s 2JZ inline-six](https://www.hagerty.com/media/opinion/smithology/smithology-secrets-of-the-supra-that-broke-actually-wore-out-the-mold/) . The Nissan wasn’t driven in development by an all-time driving deity, as the Acura NSX was by Ayrton Senna. But you shouldn’t overlook this machine.


There’s a[1990 Nissan 300ZX twin-turbo five-speed](https://www.hagerty.com/marketplace/auction/1990-Nissan-300ZX/57c4f171-ee94-490a-8eca-a5e77bebf023) available on Hagerty Marketplace right now, and it very much has our attention.

The[300ZX nameplate](https://www.hagerty.com/media/market-trends/hagerty-insider/your-handy-1990-096-nissan-300zx-buyers-guide/) actually arrived in 1984 with the Z31-generation car, a more angular affair that retained stylistic ties to the 280ZX and the even older Datsun 240Z. But in 1990, Nissan gave the Z car a clean-sheet redesign that resulted in what you see here. The coupe’s slick styling has aged marvelously through the years, and the combination of a twin-turbocharged 3.0-liter V-6 with a five-speed manual and rear-wheel drive promises to be loads of fun.

This Super White over burgundy cloth example looks splendid, especially for a car that’s 36 years old with nearly 67,000 miles on the clock. According to the listing, the seller has owned this car since 1992, and they purchased it from The[Goodyear](https://www.goodyear.com/) Tire & Rubber Company, where the car was initially[bought for tire testing](https://www.hagerty.com/media/car-profiles/catching-up-with-michelins-m3-lightweight-and-the-man-who-revived-it/) . Aside from the tender care we’re sure it received during those first two years, we’d bet there are some interesting stories involving this car.

The listing states that recent service includes a new ignition switch, coolant hoses, a new thermostat, new front suspension rods, new fuel injectors, and a new clutch assembly. With an engine rated at 300 hp and 283 lb-ft of torque when new, these cars could really get down, and we’d bet that even today, this car would prove tractable and sufficient for a plenty spirited run down your favorite backroad.
