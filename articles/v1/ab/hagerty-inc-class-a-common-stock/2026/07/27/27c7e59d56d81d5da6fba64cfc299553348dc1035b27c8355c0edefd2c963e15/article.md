# Toyota May Already Be Prepping a Hotter Version of the GR GT

When it was revealed late last year, the[Toyota GR GT](https://www.hagerty.com/media/news/toyota-gr-gt-supercar-specs-pictures-details/) marked a new milestone for the Japanese automaker, finally signaling to enthusiasts what would come after the[Lexus LFA](https://www.hagerty.com/media/buying-and-selling/your-handy-2010-12-lexus-lfa-buyers-guide/) . The long, slinking shadow that the GR GT casts is stunning, and with a twin-turbo hybrid V-8, we certainly expect the supercar to walk the talk. At the same time, given that Toyota is taking the GR GT racing in the GT3 class, we couldn’t help but wonder if a spicier street version is in the works. Recent spy photos now have us believing that there is indeed a gnarlier, road-going version of the GR GT on the way.


Stefan Baldauf / SB-Medien


Stefan Baldauf / SB-Medien


Spy photographers recently captured several shots of this winged wonder testing at the Nürburgring, and there are lots of indicators that this is no ordinary GR GT (if there were such a thing). The first and most obvious change here is the large rear wing, a swan-necked, ironing board of a thing that should provide plenty of downforce to keep the rear end planted at higher speeds. It’s a smaller wing than the one fitted to the GT3 car—or at least, the GT3 car as it currently stands; the car hasn’t debuted yet, and Toyota could certainly decide to change the design between now and the first time it takes the green flag.


Stefan Baldauf / SB-Medien


The carbon fiber wheel arch vents over the front wheels are also new, though such units are found on the GT3 car. Engineers usually place vents like this to help reduce pressure inside the wheelwells, thus improving front downforce. There’s also a new, more extreme front splitter design that appears to have additional flaps at either corner.


Stefan Baldauf / SB-Medien


Stefan Baldauf / SB-Medien


Stefan Baldauf / SB-Medien


While it might be easy to write this off as the next iteration of the GT3 variant, note that this one still has five-lug wheels. All GT3 cars use a single, center-lock nut to make changing the wheels and tires easier during a race. Those five-lug wheels make us think that this is definitely a road-going variant of the car rather than another race car.


Stefan Baldauf / SB-Medien


If that is indeed the case, the question then becomes whether this is a package to tack onto the existing GR GT, or if it’s treated as a separate model. Consider the Porsche 911; while the track-focused[911 GT3 RS](https://www.hagerty.com/media/new-car-reviews/2023-porsche-911-gt3-rs-review-as-close-as-it-gets-to-a-le-mans-ready-racer/) is technically just loads of different components slapped on the normal 911, Porsche treats the GT3 RS as its own model because it’s fundamentally so different from the base car. (By the way, photographers noted that this prototype was testing alongside a 911 GT3 RS, likely for benchmarking purposes. Quite the measuring stick, we must say.)


We’re reaching a bit now, but let’s go back to the 911 lineup as a theoretical roadmap for Toyota’s supercar. With some models of the[911 now offering hybridization](https://www.hagerty.com/media/news/the-hybrid-911-has-arrived/) , the GT3 RS represents the purest distillation of the 911’s performance envelope because it does without any hybrid components. Perhaps this variant of the GR GT could ditch the electric motor and battery for weight savings and performance, just like the car it was testing against that day? We’ll have to wait and see.
