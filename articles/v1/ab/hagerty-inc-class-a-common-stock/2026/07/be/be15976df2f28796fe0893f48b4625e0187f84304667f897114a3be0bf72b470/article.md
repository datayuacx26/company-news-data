# 4 Levels of DIY Maintenance Preparation

Own an old car for any amount of time and something will need to be fixed. It’s just the nature of the beast; things wear out and require replacement or repair. Having a plan for getting that work done is critical to enjoying your time with a vintage car, rather than dreading the next clunk or scrape that will put your fun-to-drive ride up on the jackstands of shame once again.


As someone with a vehicle currently sitting pretty atop a par of yellow stands, with a score of projects in waiting or progress, I can say that while it’s impossible to be prepared for everything, it only takes a little effort to ensure your first feeling when things need work is not despair, but maybe even a little excitement. Here are four levels of preparation that will likely keep your car driving more and you stressing less.


## Dedicated Space


[Kyle Smith](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/kyle-smith/)


It doesn’t have to be flashy, covered, or even yours, but having a spot large enough for your project that is ready if and when that project comes to need attention is key to making your life easier as a do-it-yourselfer. If this is a parking spot in the driveway, that’s better than nothing, since a space ready for a project to sit for however long it takes to get the repair done is the true key to not only being successful but enjoying the process.


In the past I’ve had to do[differential center section](https://www.hagerty.com/media/maintenance-and-tech/welcome-to-diff-world-6-types-of-differentials-and-how-they-work/) replacements in gravel parking lots, but even that was a luxury considering the pickup was not in the way of other operations on the property, and I had the space to move in, around, and under without running afoul of other things. This meant I could actually work in the space, not dance around it trying not to disturb unrelated items, and anything taken off the project wouldn’t get jumbled in with something else nearby.


## Prepared Space


It’s pretty obvious what I’m working on, because everything else is buried.


[Kyle Smith](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/kyle-smith/)


Having space is one thing, but to follow the book of my childhood, “if you give a mouse a cookie…” Having a corner carved out will inevitably lead to keeping a few items at the ready for when your project needs attention. Having diagnostic and common tools relatively at the ready will help overcome the common hurdle that causes projects to stall before they ever begin: finding the time to get started. When starting requires a bunch of moving things around prior to actually putting hands on the project, it’s no surprise motivation can be scarce.


It’s not that your project space needs to be a staffed operating room, but having all or at least most of the items you will need for a project in the space you intend to do said work helps transition annoyance of a breakdown into motivation to curate a repair.


## Spares and Reference at the Ready


An open shop manual is always a good sign.


[Kyle Smith](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/kyle-smith/)


You’ve got space, you’ve got tools, and now it’s time to build up two key pillars of expertise: spares and[reference materials](https://www.hagerty.com/media/people/first-lady-beverly-rae-kimes-was-a-pioneering-historian-of-the-early-auto-industry/) . What are the common failure points on your car? What do you need to be prepared for those failures when they occur? Sometimes, this is having everything for an annual oil change on hand so that the job can be so simple as rolling under, draining, filter swap, and refilling. Having everything on hand often makes the job more fun, as you are only starting the project because you are ready. Reading through a service manual or watching a video to prepare for a job can take a surprising amount of stress out of even a simple project.


## Your Fixer on Speed Dial


Jordan Lewis


Sometimes things just don’t go your way. Getting in over your head or just plain lost comes with the territory of DIY work. Eventually, you are going to have to take on a project or repair that you haven’t done or you have no experience with, and despite hours with the service manual or days watching YouTube, the knowledge to get things back together will simply not be in your head. That’s when the most important tool might be your phone—or at least the person on the other end of the line.


Calling for help from more experienced hands and minds is a smart thing. Most of us are savvy enough to figure out just about anything given enough time, but is getting frustrated and chasing your tail really how you want to spend your time working on your project car? Didn’t think so.


Working on project cars is supposed to be fun, and with a small amount of effort you can stack the deck in your favor to actually have that good time. How much prep is required depends on the individual, but these four tips are a great start to enjoying wrenching time more than ever.
