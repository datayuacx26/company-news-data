# After Reviving Scout, Volkswagen Is Open to Building a Land Cruiser Fighter

Volkswagen turned the International-Harvester Scout[into a standalone brand](https://www.hagerty.com/media/news/scout-motors-unveils-2027-terra-pickup-and-traveler-suv/) to plant its stake in the lucrative off-roader segment. The electrified Scout models are well on their way to production, but the company has revealed it’s open to building a more traditional SUV aimed at[the Toyota Land Cruiser](https://www.hagerty.com/media/new-car-reviews/first-drive-review-2024-toyota-land-cruiser-is-one-for-the-die-hards/) .


Speaking with Australian publication *[GoAuto](https://www.goauto.com.au/news/volkswagen/amarok/volkswagen-revives-secret-amarok-suv-ambition/2026-07-21/99877.html)* , a Volkswagen spokesperson brought to light a secret, one-off SUV prototype that was based on the first-generation Amarok truck. The body-on-frame model would have been aimed at the Land Cruiser Prado and the Ford Everest, among others, but it was not approved for production. Scale was one of the biggest hurdles it faced: Its main market would have been Australia.


Albert Kirzinger, the head of design for Volkswagen Commercial Vehicles, shared[on his LinkedIn page](https://www.linkedin.com/in/albert-kirzinger-b604051b7/recent-activity/images/) in 2024 a sketch (pictured) that showed what the SUV could have looked like. It depicts a rugged family-hauler that’s decked out with off-road accessories such as a snorkel, a roof rack, and auxiliary lights.


The original Amarok retired in 2020. The current-generation model introduced in 2022 shares its basic underpinnings with[the Ford Ranger](https://www.hagerty.com/media/news/2024-ranger-fords-midsize-pickup-matures-adds-v-6/) , and it’s likely too far into its life cycle to spawn an SUV. That said, Volkswagen Commercial Vehicles, which oversees vans and trucks, isn’t giving up on the idea of taking on Toyota. The third-generation pickup tentatively due out around 2030 could finally take Volkswagen into this segment.


It sounds like the third-generation Amarok will again be developed jointly with another brand, though it’s too early to tell whether Volkswagen will continue to work with Ford or if it will find a new partner. The talks are ongoing, so Volkswagen still has time to add an SUV offshoot to the agreement if executives give the project the proverbial green light for production. The model has yet to be confirmed, however.


“Extending the model range is a long-desired wish, I would say. In particular, this rugged or closed pickup, for instance, as we would call it, where you have the same platform as the pickup. An SUV, but based on a pickup platform, a ladder-frame car,” Volkswagen Commercial Vehicles CEO Stefan Mecha told *GoAuto* .


Nothing suggests that the Amarok-based SUV would overlap with the Scout Traveler. While both would be marketed as go-anywhere SUVs, the Amarok derivative would likely land as a global-market car, and the odds of seeing it on our shores are low. In contrast, the Traveler was largely developed for U.S. tastes.
