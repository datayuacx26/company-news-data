# So, How’s That Honda XR600R Doin’?

For decades, I’ve sat in bewilderment as fellow enthusiasts regaled me with sob stories of projects that got sidelined and ended up sitting for years. Heck, there is[a whole show](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AaAc-TkhcJ0&list=PLHKCmmH-x9mJ_1KvX4AhFy0W9-3Neby1D) on the Hagerty YouTube channel dedicated to finding and resurrecting stuff that has been sitting for too long. Neglecting a project is not uncommon, but I swore it would never happen to me. I am better, I told myself. I wouldn’t let something I care about sit for months or years. That’s not me.


Well, that *wasn’t* me.


My 1988 Honda XR600R has[featured frequently](https://www.hagerty.com/media/opinion/mechanical-sympathy/the-end-of-my-xr600r-project-is-so-so-close/) in this column. Over nearly a year and a half, I poured work and effort into building the custom bike of my dreams.[It came out great.](https://www.hagerty.com/media/opinion/mechanical-sympathy/the-finishing-touch-on-my-honda-xr600r-project-was-simple/)[Then it stopped running.](https://www.hagerty.com/media/opinion/mechanical-sympathy/its-not-fun-if-its-easy-reconsidered/) The whole reason I built the bike was to run a two-day event in the Mojave Desert with a friend, and when time got tight, and I couldn’t diagnose the issue, I abandoned the XR600R on my motorcycle lift and packed my “backup” bike. The big thumper was left sitting in my garage, staring at a milk crate full of detailing products, collecting dust. This is the exact situation I told myself I wouldn’t enable.


This poor motorcycle has been stranded in this exact spot for at least six months.


[Kyle Smith](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/kyle-smith/)


I didn’t store the bike correctly. Didn’t drain the fuel, didn’t organize the parts I had taken off it. I just walked away. To be honest, I’m still not 100% sure why the bike doesn’t run. Most of me is convinced the problem is a lack of compression, but I haven’t been able to prove it. Worse, I haven’t even been thinking about trying. So the bike sits, patiently awaiting the attention it both requires and deserves, while doing its best to look pretty under the harsh LEDs that make my garage resemble an operating room (as a few of you have noticed).


It’s not that I *want* to be in this situation. I always knew that no one wanted to let a car sit. Yet there is something in me that refuses to accept the explanation that time just got away from me. I am looking for the reason I’m avoiding this project, not the excuse. (The excuse is: I have other fun toys to play with, and it’s summer.) The reason is… tougher to nail down. I think it’s some combination of these four:


1. There are “easier” or more exciting projects sitting right next to the 600R.
2. The excitement of taking the bike to an event was pushing me to keep things moving. Now that the event has come and gone, the project is adrift.
3. Diving back in first requires me to acknowledge that all the work I did up to this point was wrong at worst, misguided at best.
4. I’ve already spent too much money on this bike, and I know it’s gonna need more before it’s running again.


These aren’t real hurdles. After all, I am the same person who looked at this scruffy, incomplete, mangled XR600R a year and a half ago and said, “Yeah, let’s completely rebuild that.” It doesn’t feel appropriate to apply logic to a project[that was so illogical](https://www.hagerty.com/media/opinion/mechanical-sympathy/reasons-excuses-and-the-big-dumb-bike-of-my-dreams/) from the start. And yet, here I am.


Drive chain, carburetor, and wiring harness are just gathering dust under the bike.

I even tried retail therapy. A month ago, I bought the headlights for which I had been searching for years. My interest in Honda XR motorcycles traces back decades at this point, back to a retired dirt track XR650R that I rebuilt into a supermoto with a little Baja flair. The XR600R currently in my garage always felt like it needed dual headlights, but the right set proved elusive. Finally, an identical pair to the ones on my favorite XR650R popped up, and I got lucky enough to be first in line. I had found the true finishing touch, but even that didn’t put spurs into my belly to get working on the bike.


So, the Honda XR600R isn’t doing great, but I genuinely do appreciate the comments lightly prodding me for an update on the project. It’s riding season in Northern Michigan, and though my fleet of rideable motorcycles is down to just two, I am prioritizing fun in the sun. Motivation always ebbs and flows, and likely the smartest thing I can do is force myself to spend 20 minutes preparing the bike and the space for the next big step. Then, should motivation strike, I am ready and without excuse.


It hurts to backtrack so far that I’m preparing to pull the 100-pound single-cylinder out of the steel frame, but the sooner I can accept that unfortunate reality and push past the unpleasantness, the sooner this machine will be sliding down the dirt roads and trails of Northern Michigan again.


I’d say I can’t wait, but … well, we both know that’s not true.
