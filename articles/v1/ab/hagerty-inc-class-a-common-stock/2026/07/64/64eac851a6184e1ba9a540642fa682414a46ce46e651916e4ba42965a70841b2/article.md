# Fiat Topolino EV Launched as a Modern-Day Bubble Car

Decades ago, “bubble” cars, like[the BMW Isetta](https://www.hagerty.com/media/market-trends/valuation/plucky-1955-62-bmw-isetta-isnt-going-anywhere/) and[the Goggomobil T250](https://www.hagerty.com/media/automotive-history/cute-car-cute-name-it-was-impossible-to-not-love-the-goggomobil/) , filled the gap between motorcycles and regular cars. The category gradually disappeared as small, cheap econoboxes such as the original Mini arrived, but[the Fiat Topolino](https://www.hagerty.com/media/news/fiat-topolino-american-launch/) launched in the United States for 2026 just resurrected it.


Released in Europe in 2023 and closely related to the Citroën Ami, the Topolino is not a car—not exactly. It’s “a low-speed vehicle (LSV) designed for neighborhood and lifestyle use,” according to Fiat. It’s not a golf cart either, because it’s theoretically legal to drive on public roads with a speed limit of 35 mph or less. We say “theoretically,” because the Topolino is currently only legal to drive on private roads.


Visually, the Topolino is identical to the model that went on sale in Europe in 2023. It’s named after a city car that Fiat built from 1936 to 1955, and its styling is vaguely reminiscent of[the 500 released in 1957](https://www.hagerty.com/media/market-trends/hagerty-insider/your-handy-buyers-guide-1957-75-fiat-500-buyers-guide/) . It’s quirkier than either, with asymmetrical doors,[Citroën 2CV](https://www.hagerty.com/media/driving/citroen-2cv-seized-engine-roadtrip-story/) -like hinged windows, and a two-seater interior.


Buyers will have two variants of the Fiat Topolino to choose from: The base car and a beach-friendly trim called Dolce Vita. The latter offers a roll-back soft top that’s not unlike the 500’s and real ropes instead of doors. As of writing, the only exterior color available is an eye-catching shade of green called Verde Vita.


Fiat is almost synonymous with small cars, but the Topolino makes even[the 500](https://www.hagerty.com/media/news/fiat-500-goes-back-to-gas-with-new-torino/) look a little big-boned. It stretches about 95 inches long, 55 inches wide, and 60 inches tall, and it tips the scales at 1073 pounds. Power comes from an electric motor linked to a 5.4-kilowatt-hour lithium-ion battery pack. Driving range checks in at up to 46 miles, a full charge takes about five hours, and the top speed is limited to 19 mph.

Owners who want to make their Topolino street-legal on roads limited to 35 mph or less will be able to install a complimentary Low Speed Vehicle (LSV) kit that unlocks a 25-mph top speed. The kit should be available by the end of summer 2026, according to Fiat.


The 2026 Fiat Topolino is available now through select dealers, but the brand warns that it’s only sending “limited quantities” to our shores for now. Pricing starts at $13,995 excluding destination.
