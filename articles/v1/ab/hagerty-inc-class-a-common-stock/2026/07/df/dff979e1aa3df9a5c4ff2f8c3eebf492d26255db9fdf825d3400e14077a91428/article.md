# 6 RUFs Under One Tent at Broad Arrow’s 2026 Quail Auction

Since the mid-1970s—long before “restomodding” and “reimagining” old 911s became a thing—Ruf Automobile GmbH, aka RUF, has been churning out Porsche-based builds with incredible attention to detail and an equally incredible ability to go fast. And by “churning out,” we mean assembling them by hand at a shop in Bavaria, in very small numbers and for very large sums.


Given RUF’s low production volume along with relative obscurity and small presence in America, the cars haven’t always been market darlings. But with the strength of the Porsche brand in general, along with shifting tastes that are more accepting of specialized vehicles, RUFs are commanding more respect in collector circles, and they’ve been showing up at high-profile auctions more regularly. In a few weeks, one group of very special RUFs will be crossing the block at the most high-profile auction event of them all—Monterey Car Week. The six-strong squad “is certainly the biggest and most significant representation we’ve ever offered for the marque at a single sale,” says Alexander Weaver, VP and Senior Car Specialist at Broad Arrow, who is offering the RUFs at their Quail auction in nearby Carmel, CA.


Weaver also notes that “all of the models on offer come from an exceptional home with one of the foremost RUF collectors and enthusiasts in the hobby. We anticipate significant interest from today’s most active collectors and we feel our offering is illustrative of the generational shift in the market that is 100% here.”


Only twice has a group of RUFs even close to this size shown up at a single auction. Three years ago in Amelia Island,[Gooding & Co.](https://www.goodingco.com/auction/realized/amelia-island-auction-2023/) (now Gooding Christie’s) brought out four cars and all sold, with prices ranging from $335K to $1.55M. Gooding brought four more to Amelia[this year](https://www.goodingco.com/auction/realized/amelia-island-auctions-2026/) and three of them sold, with prices ranging from $274K to $850K. High estimates on the half-dozen-strong batch of RUFs in Monterey, meanwhile, range from $600,000 to $2.8M.


## [1985 RUF BTR III](https://www.broadarrowauctions.com/vehicles/ql26_r0083/1985-porsche-911-ruf-btr-iii)


Broad Arrow


Porsche’s original 911 Turbo, aka the 930, was already one of the fastest things on four wheels in the middle of the ’80s, but the RUF BTR treatment gave the whale-tailed German coupe enough oomph to keep up with, and sometimes beat, the most super of supercars. RUF also kept revising its BTR package, resulting in the “BTR II” and its ultimate iteration, the “BTR III.”


This car started life as a standard 1985 930 Coupe, painted in special-order non-metallic Dark Blue paint over a matching full leather interior. A previous owner snagged it in 1988 and immediately started chatting with both RUF in Pfaffenhausen and RUF’s U.S. distributor in New Jersey. A $37,000 package of BTR upgrades was ordered in 1989, and included was the 3.4-liter, 400-hp (up 100 cc and 100 hp from stock) BTR I engine.


Broad Arrow


Apparently not satisfied, the owner sent the car back for another round of upgrades for another, even more expensive BTR II conversion with more engine upgrades, lightweight bumpers, 17-inch Speedline wheels, Quaife differential, six-speed RUF gearbox, CTR brake kit, chassis bracing, and suspension upgrades. Even that wasn’t enough, though, because a few months later the engine got further upgrades to even beyond BTR III specs for a claimed 625-plus hp and 595 lb-ft. It last sold publicly in 2022 for $445,000, and Broad Arrow’s estimate for it in 2026 is $550,000–$600,000


## [2013 RUF Rt35](https://www.broadarrowauctions.com/vehicles/ql26_r0151/2013-ruf-rt35)


Broad Arrow


The Rt35 was the first RUF model to utilize the 991 platform, aka the seventh generation 911 that Porsche introduced for 2012. Its heavily massaged 3.8-liter twin-turbo sends 630 hp and 609 lb-ft to the rear wheels through a seven-speed PDK gearbox. Suspension tuning, fat carbon-ceramic brakes, forged 20-inch wheels, and body modifications rounded out the package.


RUF built just 35 examples, and this one is among “a smaller handful” with open bodywork. Wearing Carrara White over Luxor Beige and black, it shows 9900 miles. Broad Arrow’s estimate for it is $600,000–$700,000.


## [1990 RUF BTR III Cabriolet](https://www.broadarrowauctions.com/vehicles/ql26_r0147/1990-ruf-btr-iii-cabriolet)


Broad Arrow


The BTR (short for *Gruppe B Turbo RUF* ) marked the firm’s transition from Porsche tuner to serious manufacturer in its own right, though 911s still provided the essential framework. That isn’t just opinion, as the German government officially recognized RUF as an independent carmaker in 1981 and the company started using its own VINs, starting with the original BTR. Factory-built RUFs have VINs starting with “W09,” whereas standard Porsches (or standard Porsches later modified by RUF) use “WP0.”


Broad Arrow


Broad Arrow


This Iris Blue Metallic over Dark Blue leather cabriolet started life as a RUF, and it features body-color five-spoke Speedline Denloc wheels, CTR spoilers and brakes, RUF Bilstein Sport shocks, RUF Sport sway bars, front strut brakes, and a 27.7-gallon fuel tank. Out back is the 408-hp, 3.4-liter turbo and RUF six-speed. When new, it went straight from Pfaffenhausen to Japan, and then came to the U.S. in 2022. Shortly after getting here, it went up on Bring a Trailer, where it was a $345K no-sale, and then a few months later went to Mecum’s Monterey auction, where it sold for[$517,000](https://www.mecum.com/lots/520727/1990-ruf-btr-iii-cabriolet/) . Broad Arrow’s estimate for it this year is $800,000–$950,000.


## [1994 Porsche 911 RUF RCT EVO Speedster](https://www.broadarrowauctions.com/vehicles/ql26_r0148/1994-porsche-911-ruf-rct-evo-speedster)


Broad Arrow


Every so often, Porsche creates a “Speedster” version of one of its cars. They’re typically low-production, open-topped nods to the original lightweight 356 Speedster of the 1950s. Porsche built just under 1000 Speedsters on the 964 (1990–94) platform, and this one left Stuttgart ordered in Guards Red with a Tiptronic transmission.


It had a full life in Japan, racking up over 60,000 km over the course of about 10 years before going back to Germany in 2014. In 2015, its owner sent it to RUF for both a full restoration and a host of upgrades and custom options. RUF overhauled and upgraded the engine, then chucked the Tiptronic and replaced it with a six-speed. They also repainted it in Rosso Mugello, a Ferrari color, bolted on 18-inch RUF wheels, upgraded the brakes, and fitted a sports suspension system. Interior upgrades rounded out the work.


Broad Arrow


Broad Arrow


The red Speedster sold to an American owner in 2024, but went back to RUF for its RCT (RUF Carrera Turbo) EVO conversion. This entailed fitting a turbocharger for 425 hp, an additional oil cooler, carbon fiber bumpers, 19-inch RUF wheels, and bigger brakes. It has reportedly done about 300 miles since its latest trip to RUF, and has an $800,000–$950,000 estimate for Monterey. That’s about four times the price of a normal[964 Speedster](https://www.hagerty.com/valuation-tools/porsche/911/1994/1994-porsche-911-carrera?id=aCn1I000000CrX1SAK) in similar condition.


## [1995 RUF RCT EVO](https://www.broadarrowauctions.com/vehicles/ql26_r0149/1995-ruf-rct-evo)


Broad Arrow


Following on the success of its CTR Yellowbird, RUF started building its RCT (RUF Carrera Turbo) in 1992, using the then-new 964 platform. It followed the usual RUF formula of an upgraded and turbocharged engine, proprietary six-speed manual, and upgrades to the brakes, suspension, aero, and interior. The EVO specifications were introduced later, in 2020, with 425 hp and 420 lb-ft, RUF’s own limited-slip differential, further handling upgrades, and an integrated roll cage. Alois Ruf has described this version as “the most usable everyday model in the RUF range.”


Broad Arrow


This one started out as a RUF from new, finished in Guards Red over black. In 2024, and showing 45,606 miles, it went back to Pfaffenhausen for the EVO upgrades, which reportedly totaled €88,594.18. For a normal, non-car person, it might be hard to understand why this red coupe, which doesn’t look like much more than a regular 964 with some fancy wheels and a whale tail, might sell for over a million dollars. But Broad Arrow thinks it will fetch between $950K and $1.1M, and that’s a reasonable estimate, since another RCT EVO with wide carbon fiber bodywork sold for over[$1.2M](https://rmsothebys.com/auctions/az26/lots/r0084-1994-ruf-rct-evo-widebody/) earlier this year.


## [2024 RUF SCR “Mary Stuart”](https://www.broadarrowauctions.com/vehicles/ql26_r0150/2024-ruf-scr-mary-stuart)


Broad Arrow


Yes, it is very much shaped like a 911, but the RUF SCR was engineered from scratch, not from a base supplied by Porsche. There’s a carbon fiber monocoque with an integrated steel roll cage. There’s carbon fiber bodywork, shaped by computer analysis. There’s a 4.0-liter aluminum-block flat-six out back that makes 510 hp and 347 lb-ft, redlines at 8750 rpm, driving the rear wheels through a six-speed manual and limited-slip, and shoving the car to 199 mph.


Broad Arrow


Broad Arrow


Broad Arrow


This one is finished in Ultra Violet, a shade that reportedly shifts between purple and indigo depending on how the light hits it, and the cabin is trimmed in black and gray Alcantara with body-color leather cushion frames and seatbelts. Weaver describes it as “one of the most exclusive, made-to-order cars in RUF’s history as an independent manufacturer,” and adds that “this is the one to chase.”


Why is it called “Mary Stuart?” According to Broad Arrow, just six of the SCRs feature this custom rear decklid with a full-width spoiler that wraps around the tail. Porsche fitted a similar spoiler to its Targa Florio- and Le Mans class-winning Carrera RSRs in 1973, and it reminded someone enough of Mary, Queen of Scots’ fancy collar that the name stuck. This car is also reportedly just the second SCR imported to the U.S., and it was RUF North America’s official demonstrator for the model. It now shows just 176 miles and has an estimate of $2,500,000–$2,800,000.
