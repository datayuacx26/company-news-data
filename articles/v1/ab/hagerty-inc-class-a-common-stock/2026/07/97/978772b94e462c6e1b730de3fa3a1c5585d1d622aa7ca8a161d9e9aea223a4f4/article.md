# Final Parking Space: 1988 Chrysler Town & Country Turbo Mark Cross Wagon

The Final Parking Space series is well into its third year, and that means we’re long overdue for a discarded example of[the car that saved Chrysler](https://www.hagerty.com/media/car-profiles/how-the-k-car-saved-chrysler/) : the[K](https://www.allpar.com/threads/k-cars-plymouth-reliant-dodge-aries-and-chrysler-lebaron.229749/?utm_source=google&utm_medium=organic) . I found this loaded[1988 Town & Country wagon](https://oldcarbrochures.org/United%20States/Chrysler_and_Imperial/1988-Chrysler/1988-Chrysler-Full-Line-brochure/index.html) at[a yard in the San Francisco Bay Area](https://www.picknpull.com/locations/55/richmond-ca) a few months back.


[Murilee Martin](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/mmartin/)


If we’re splitting hairs here, I *have* documented a member of the extended K Family in a car graveyard: a[1987 Shelby CSX in Colorado](https://www.hagerty.com/media/opinion/final-parking-space/final-parking-space-1987-shelby-csx/) last year. However, that car was based on the Dodge Shadow, which lived on a platform that was derived from the K but isn’t considered a true K-Car.


Chrysler Corporation


The extended K Family Tree ended up with close to a dozen major branches during the 1981-1995 model years. The most influential was[the original Chrysler minivan](https://www.hagerty.com/media/magazine-features/hagerty-magazine/the-van-that-saved-chrysler-and-changed-the-way-america-hauled/) , but there were also upscale luxury sedans, sleek coupes, and even[a Maserati](https://www.murileemartin.com/Junkyard/JunkyardGallery-Maserati.html) along the way.


Chrysler Corporation


The[genuine Chrysler Ks for the United States market](https://www.murileemartin.com/Junkyard/JunkyardGallery-ChryslerKCars.html) are the[Plymouth Reliant](https://www.autoblog.com/news/junkyard-gem-1981-plymouth-reliant-station-wagon/) ,[Dodge Aries](http://www.thetruthaboutcars.com/2016/05/junkyard-find-1982-dodge-aries-station-wagon/) ,[Dodge 400](https://www.thetruthaboutcars.com/2018/11/junkyard-find-1982-dodge-400-landau-coupe/) ,[Dodge 600 coupe](https://www.thetruthaboutcars.com/2019/08/junkyard-find-1984-dodge-600-landau-coupe-with-5-speed-manual-transmission/) and[convertible](https://www.autoblog.com/news/junkyard-gem-1985-dodge-600-es-turbo-convertible/) , Chrysler LeBaron for 1982 through 1986 ([coupes](https://oldcarbrochures.org/United%20States/Chrysler_and_Imperial/1983_Chrysler/1983%20Chrysler%20LeBaron/slides/1983%20Chrysler%20LeBaron%20Brochure%2001.html) and[convertibles](https://www.autoblog.com/news/junkyard-gem-1982-chrysler-lebaron-convertible/) ) and 1988 ([sedans](https://www.autoblog.com/news/junkyard-gem-1988-chrysler-lebaron-turbo-sedan/) ), and the 1982-1988 Chrysler Town & Country Wagon. There were overseas K models as well, including[the Japanese-market Dodge Michigan](https://www.reddit.com/r/WeirdWheels/comments/1n3txsx/the_dodge_michigan_was_a_chrysler_kcar_exported/) , but we won’t be going down that rabbit hole right now.


1947 Chrysler Town & Country Sedan


[Brandan Gillogly](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/brandan-gillogly/)


The Town & Country name goes way back in Chrysler history, with the first use on[1941 “woodie” station wagons](https://oldcarbrochures.org/United%20States/Chrysler_and_Imperial/1942_Chrysler/1942-Chrysler-Town-and-Country-Folder/index.html) . Starting with the 1990 model year, the Town & Country name went onto the Chrysler-badged siblings of the Plymouth Voyager and Dodge Caravan. Sadly, Chrysler retired the Town & Country name with the debut of[the Pacifica](https://www.chrysler.com/pacifica.html) a decade ago.


[Murilee Martin](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/mmartin/)


Confusingly, there were two K-platform Town & Country-badged vehicles sold side-by-side at Chrysler dealerships for a few years: the Town & Country Wagon and[the LeBaron Town & Country convertible](https://oldcarbrochures.org/United%20States/Chrysler_and_Imperial/1985-Chrysler/1985-Chrysler-LeBaron-Brochure/slides/1985%20Chrysler%20LeBaron-12.html) . Both had faux-wood flanks, and the convertible came standard with a[Mark Cross leather interior](https://www.autoblog.com/news/junkyard-gem-1983-chrysler-town-and-country-convertible/) .


[Murilee Martin](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/mmartin/)


The 1982-1988 Town & Country Wagon was the station wagon version of the LeBaron sedan, although it didn’t get LeBaron badging. 1988 was the final model year for the K-Wagons, with production of the Reliant and Aries two- and four-door sedans continuing through 1989.


[Murilee Martin](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/mmartin/)


The “wood” paneling with “[wood-like styling accents](https://oldcarbrochures.org/United%20States/Chrysler_and_Imperial/1988-Chrysler/1988-Chrysler-Full-Line-brochure/slides/1988%20Chrysler-20.html) ” on these cars looked pretty convincing, even after the plastic began to fade in the sun.


[Murilee Martin](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/mmartin/)


This car has the optional Mark Cross interior package, which cost $1019 and could only be ordered if you also took the Luxury Equipment Discount Package for $2073 (that adds up to $8925[in 2026 dollars](https://murileemartin.substack.com/p/your-handy-inflationary-car-price) ).


[Murilee Martin](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/mmartin/)


The MSRP for this car was $12,899 ($37,202 after inflation), so its out-the-door price would have been well north of 50 grand in present-day money.


[Murilee Martin](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/mmartin/)


It has the top engine option for ’88, which added $678 to the bottom line ($1957 now).


[Murilee Martin](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/mmartin/)


This is the Chrysler 2.5-liter SOHC straight-four with turbocharging and electronic fuel injection, rated at 146 horsepower and 170 pound-feet. That was good power for a 2702-pound car in the late 1980s. The base engine in the 1988 Town & Country Wagon was a 100-horsepower naturally aspirated version of this engine.


[Murilee Martin](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/mmartin/)


The only transmission available was a three-speed automatic with column shift.


[Murilee Martin](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/mmartin/)


The air conditioning was included with the Luxury Equipment Discount Package, but this six-speaker AM/FM/cassette audio system with the distinctive “joystick” balance/fader control cost $289 ($834 today). You needed this rig if you wanted to hear[the era’s most distinctive tunes](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dQw4w9WgXcQ) in proper fashion.


[Murilee Martin](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/mmartin/)


Naturally, I took a couple of shots of this car with[a 35mm camera that was made in Michigan in the 1930s](https://murileemartin.substack.com/p/1936-ann-arbor-35mm-camera-visits) .


[Murilee Martin](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/mmartin/)


Since this car was built at[Newark Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newark_Assembly) in Delaware, I should have used a camera made in The First State.


[Murilee Martin](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/mmartin/)


This car still had a steering-wheel-to-brake-pedal lock attached when it took that fateful final tow-truck ride.


It appears that Lee Iacocca had Chrysler put most of its K-wagon TV budget into the Reliant and Aries.


However, when Town & Country wagon commercials *did* hit screens, Ricardo Montalban was there.
