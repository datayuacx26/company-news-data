# Final Parking Space: 1982 Renault Le Car DeLuxe 3-door

Four of our last five Final Parking Space subjects have been red-white-and-blue-flag-waving American cars and trucks, each equipped with a manual transmission. This week, we’re going to stick with the run of three-pedal machines from nations that[wave red-white-and-blue flags](https://www.elysee.fr/la-presidence/le-drapeau-francais) , which in this case means a French car sold in the U.S. by[a Wisconsin-based car company](https://www.hagerty.com/media/automotive-history/the-life-and-death-of-american-motors-nicely-told-in-a-six-part-pbs-series/) . I found this Le Car last week, at[a casse de voiture in Aurora, Colorado](https://www.upullandpay.com/locations/colorado/aurora/) .


Renault


Le Car was the U.S.-market name for[the Renault 5](https://www.hagerty.com/media/car-profiles/the-original-renault-5-is-an-overlooked-peoples-hero/) (also known as the R5), which was one of the most successful and influential European cars ever built. You get into some fascinating international automotive history when you look into Le Car’s story.


Renault


The 5 was[a revolutionary design](https://www.aronline.co.uk/facts-and-figures/raise-a-glass-to/projet-122-development-story/) , masterminded by Head of Planning Bernard Hanon and stylist[Michel Boué](https://driventowrite.com/tag/michel-boue-designer/) beginning in 1967. It was a contemporary of the other early “supermini” European cars that followed in the tire tracks blazed by[the BMC Mini](https://www.aronline.co.uk/cars/mini/ado15-development-story-1/) , including[the Autobianchi A112](https://www.hagerty.com/media/archived/engine-swapped-autobianchi-a112-track-ninja/) ,[Peugeot 104](https://storm.oldcarmanualproject.com/peugeot1041973.htm) , and[Fiat 127](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mBNYv4reHsY) , and it outsold all of them.


Renault


Under the skin, the 5 was based on[the mechanicals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_4#/media/File:Chassisroulant.JPG) of[the Renault 4](https://www.hagerty.com/media/automotive-history/looking-back-at-60-years-of-the-renault-4-the-do-it-all-blue-jeans-car/) , which debuted as a 1961 model.


[Murilee Martin](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/mmartin/)


The 4 was a good car for the money but looked quite old-fashioned by the late 1960s, so the 5 was made to appeal to buyers who wanted to look more stylish. That said, the 4 stayed in production all the way through 1994, so not all European Renault buyers preferred the 5.


One big advantage the 5 had over the 4, especially on[the car-abusing streets of Paris](https://murileemartin.substack.com/p/the-hooptie-vans-of-paris-2025) , was its plastic bumpers and, after a few years of production, its protective side cladding. It was the best-selling car in France for a dozen years after its 1972 launch, and it stayed in production in essentially its original form all the way[through 1985](https://autocatalogarchive.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/02/Renault-5-1984-FR.pdf) in Europe ([its Super 5 successor](https://www.autoweek.com/car-life/a46912067/renault-super-5-hatchback-is-down-on-the-parisian-street/) looked similar to the original 5 but was in fact a completely new design). Worldwide, it was assembled on five continents. In Iran, it was license-built as the Pars Khodro Sepand through 2000, after which Renault 5 bodies were mated to[Kia Pride](https://www.kia.com/in/discover-kia/our-story/heritage.html) aka[Ford Festiva](https://www.autoblog.com/news/junkyard-gem-1990-ford-festiva-l-plus/) chassis and[built through 2008](https://www.theautopian.com/how-the-original-renault-5-got-frankensteind-back-from-the-dead-with-kia-guts-in-iran/) .


The 5, then, was a sales hit all over the world… with the notable exception of the world’s biggest automotive market during the 1970s: the United States. Renault began selling the 5 in the United States and Canada under its original name in 1976, finding some showroom success among the Francophiles of the Great White North but getting the cold shoulder from the U.S. car shoppers who were already (mostly) shunning the[12](https://www.hagerty.com/marketplace/classified/1982-renault-r12/8fda502e-2aab-4fcb-9928-a49854388523) ,[15, and 17](https://storm.oldcarmanualproject.com/renault1517.htm) .


Renault


For 1977, Renault changed the name of the U.S.-market 5 to Le Car, and sales began to pick up. Le Car was priced competitively with its rivals; for 1977, its MSRP started at $3295, or about $18,810[in 2026 dollars](https://murileemartin.substack.com/p/your-handy-inflationary-car-price) . Soon, American amateur racers discovered that the 5 was competent on a racetrack, and it ended up dominating the SCCA’s Showroom Stock Class C.


Courtesy Ogunquit Police Department


A couple of American towns[bought Les Cars for their police departments](https://www.hagerty.com/media/automotive-history/in-the-1970s-police-departments-relied-on-the-renault-le-car/) .


American Motors Corporation


Renault hadn’t had a true sales hit in the USA since[the Dauphine](https://www.autoweek.com/car-life/a45875159/1961-renault-dauphine-gordini-found-in-california-junkyard/) , which was the second-best-selling imported car in the United States for the 1957–1960 period (after the Volkswagen Beetle). The Le Car didn’t break any showroom records at first, in part because the Renault dealership network in the United States was so small. Then, beginning in 1978, the American Motors Corporation and Renault began making deals and becoming ever-closer strategic partners.


American Motors Corporation


By 1983, Renault, then owned by the French government, owned nearly half of AMC, and Kenosha was building or preparing to build Americanized versions of the Renault 9 (the Alliance), the Renault 11 (the Encore), the Renault 21 (the Medallion), and a mashup of Medallion and Renault 25 components ([the Premier](https://www.autoblog.com/news/junkyard-gem-1991-dodge-monaco/) ). At the same time, Renault engineers and designers in Wisconsin created one of the most influential vehicles of the late 20th century:[the Jeep XJ Cherokee](https://www.hagerty.com/media/opinion/final-parking-space/final-parking-space-1984-jeep-cherokee-chief/) . The Medallion and Premier became Eagle models once Lee Iacocca had Chrysler buy AMC in 1987, with Chrysler scoring not just Jeep but the Premier’s advanced chassis design, which ended up as the basis of the[Chrysler LH series of cars](https://www.autoblog.com/news/junkyard-gem-1995-eagle-vision-tsi/) .


Renault


In the short term, the American Motors-Renault deal gave AMC-Jeep dealers a gas-sipping, stylish subcompact to sell plus an infusion of francs to advertise that car. Starting in 1979, Le Car could be bought and serviced all over the United States, and sales improved with each successive year. Le Car made American Honda salesmen lose much sleep over lost Civic sales, but it became a reasonably common sight on American roads for a while.


American Motors Corporation


Five-door Les Cars appeared here for the 1981 model year. The future was looking good for AMC-Renault.


Alex Kwanten


U.S. Electricar sold EV-ized Les Cars,[badged as Lectric Leopards](https://www.hagerty.com/media/car-profiles/the-nine-lives-of-the-lectric-leopard-the-original-electric-renault-5/) , through AMC dealerships for a few years.


[Murilee Martin](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/mmartin/)


I’ve long been fascinated by the history of the Renault 5/Le Car, but for decades, examples have been nearly impossible to find in[Ewe Pullet](https://www.ewepullet.com/) –type junkyards. In fact, today’s FPS subject is only the second boneyard Le Car I’ve documented in nearly 20 years, after this 1982 5-door[I found in Northern California in 2010](https://murileemartin.substack.com/p/reconstituted-junkyard-jewel-1982-1ce) . Before now, I’d found more defunct Dauphines ([a ’59](https://www.thetruthaboutcars.com/cars/down-on-the-junkyard/junkyard-find-1959-renault-dauphine-44503192) ,[a ’60](https://www.autoblog.com/features/junkyard-gem-1960-renault-dauphine/) , and[a ’61 Gordini](https://www.autoweek.com/car-life/a45875159/1961-renault-dauphine-gordini-found-in-california-junkyard/) ), scrapped 16s ([a ’65](https://www.autoblog.com/news/junkyard-gem-french-edition-renault-r16/) and[a ’69](https://web.archive.org/web/20150714011111/https://jalopnik.com/357733/possibly-the-only-1969-renault-16-in-america) ), abandoned Alliances ([one ’84](http://www.thetruthaboutcars.com/2012/03/junkyard-find-1985-renault-alliance/) and[two](http://www.thetruthaboutcars.com/2010/12/renault-alliance-still-on-the-scrapheap-of-history/)[’86s](https://www.autoweek.com/car-life/classic-cars/a34707853/1986-renault-alliance-is-junkyard-treasure/) ), and moribund Méganes ([an ’02](http://www.thetruthaboutcars.com/2013/11/reykjavik-junkyard-mystery-car-quick-what-is-it/) and[an ’07](https://www.autoblog.com/features/junkyard-gem-2007-renault-megane-cc/) ) than I had examples of the best-known U.S.-market Renault of modern times.


[Murilee Martin](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/mmartin/)


The final model year for Le Car in the United States was 1983, after which it was replaced with the Wisconsin-built Alliance/Encore.


[Murilee Martin](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/mmartin/)


Now we’ll take a closer look at today’s FPS car. The emissions sticker tells us that it was sold as a California-only model and moved to Colorado later in life.


[Murilee Martin](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/mmartin/)


It’s a three-door hatchback with the DeLuxe trim level (which included such features as power brakes and a cigarette lighter), so its MSRP would have been $4995, or about $17,689 in 2026 dollars. The cheapest possible 1982 Honda Civic listed at $5154, with the Datsun 210 starting at $4799 that year. The base ’82 Le Car cost a mere $4795, however, making it *the* most affordably priced new car available in the United States that year.


[Murilee Martin](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/mmartin/)


The color-coordinated stripes were also DeLuxe items.


[Murilee Martin](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/mmartin/)


The big sliding-soft-top sunroof cost $195, or $691 in today’s money.


[Murilee Martin](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/mmartin/)


The engine is a longitudinally-mounted pushrod 1.4-liter, with the transmission bolted to the front. Engine output was rated at 60 horsepower and 70 pound-feet.


[Murilee Martin](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/mmartin/)


The transmission is a[four-on-the-floor manual](https://www.autoweek.com/car-life/columns/a34301141/what-was-the-final-us-market-car-to-get-a-4-speed-manual-transmission/) , which was mandatory equipment in Le Car (an automatic was available for the later 5 in other markets).


[Murilee Martin](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/mmartin/)


The body and interior are in good condition for a cheap, 44-year-old econobox. If there’s any rust on this car, I didn’t see it.


[Murilee Martin](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/mmartin/)


Can it really have just 44,974 miles, or did the speedometer cable break in 1986?


[Murilee Martin](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/mmartin/)


I showed some of these photos to my car-nerd friends in Europe, and they couldn’t believe a rust-free classic like this could meet such a fate (they felt the same way about[the much rougher ’74 Ford Capri](https://www.hagerty.com/media/opinion/final-parking-space/final-parking-space-1974-ford-capri/) I spotted in the same facility two years ago). The original 5 is still beloved on the other side of the Atlantic.


Renault


Renault[now sells](https://www.renault.fr/vehicules-electriques/r5-e-tech-electrique.html) a[retro-styled electric 5](https://www.hagerty.com/media/news/the-new-renault-5-is-a-concept-car-made-real/) , but it’s not available in the United States.


Even if Renault proved willing to give this car Nissan or Mitsubishi badging for the U.S. market, American car shoppers don’t care much for small hatchbacks,[particularly ones with battery power](https://www.hagerty.com/media/news/fiats-new-500e-is-a-neat-urban-runabout-but-thats-about-it/) .


The original 5 remained fashionable into the 1980s.


Just the car you want for *Bullitt* -style jumps in San Francisco.
