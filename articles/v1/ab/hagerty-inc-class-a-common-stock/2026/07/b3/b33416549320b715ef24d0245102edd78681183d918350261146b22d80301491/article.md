# Toyota Adds Trailhunter Packages to 2027 Tundra and Sequoia

Toyota has announced a mid-cycle refresh for the 2027 Tundra pickup and the 2027 Sequoia full-size SUV. Alongside an updated exterior and more standard tech on the inside, both body-on-frame ‘Yotas get a significant new option: The Trailhunter package.


Toyota | Benjamin Hutton


Toyota | Benjamin Hutton


Toyota enthusiasts will recognize the Trailhunter name as a sort of second halo model for the smaller truck and SUV, the[Tacoma](https://www.hagerty.com/media/new-car-reviews/first-drive-2024-toyota-tacoma-hybrid-is-a-radical-leap-forward-and-a-spendy-one/) and the[4Runner](https://www.hagerty.com/media/news/the-trailhunter-is-the-ultimate-overlanding-2025-toyota-4runner/) . On those vehicles, opting for Trailhunter trim (Toyota calls them grades) takes you all the way to the tippy end of the price ladder, landing alongside the other halo model, the TRD Pro.


Toyota | Benjamin Hutton


But on the[Tundra](https://www.hagerty.com/media/new-car-reviews/2025-toyota-tundra-sr5-trd-rally-package-more-than-stickers-and-stitches/) and Sequoia, there are already two paths to get maximum content: The[TRD Pro](https://www.hagerty.com/media/new-car-reviews/2022-toyota-tundra-trd-pro-review-respectable-un-raptor/) , a more off-road-centric grade, and the[Capstone](https://www.hagerty.com/media/new-car-reviews/2023-toyota-sequoia-capstone-4x4-review-loses-cylinders-gains-performance/) , which nets you the most luxurious amenities on offer. Rather than further muddying the waters of the range-toppers, Toyota is offering the Trailhunter package as an option for the SR5-grade vehicles.


Toyota | Benjamin Hutton


Toyota | Benjamin Hutton


Though the pricing on the package has not yet been revealed, the Trailhunter package does offer meaningful upgrades for dirt-bound customers. On the hardware side, you’ll get Michelin LTX Trail tires wrapping package-specific, 18-inch bronze wheels, upgraded Old Man Emu suspension, front recovery hooks, additional underbody skid plates, and a locking rear differential. On the software side, Trailhunters enjoy Toyota’s Multi-Terrain Select, which adjusts mapping for the throttle, traction control, and braking to maximize traction, as well as Crawl Control, which is essentially an off-road cruise control used to maintain low speeds.


Toyota | Benjamin Hutton


Curiously, the Trailhunter package contains a lot of the same equipment as the TRD Off-Road package—things like a locking diff, skid plates, and Multi-Terrain Select, to name a few. However, the TRD Off-Road package uses Bilstein dampers, not Old Man Emu. When we talked with Toyota, they did not elaborate on product differentiation or target audience beyond stating that “The TRD Off-Road and Trailhunter can live side by side.” Without any additional technical specs, we’d speculate that the OMEs on the Trailhunter package may help deliver a slightly higher payload capacity, which can be useful when outfitting the bed of the Tundra (or the roof of the Sequoia) with all of the equipment you’d associate with modern-day overlanding. (Roof racks, tents, tool storage, portable fridges, and the like.)


Toyota | Benjamin Hutton


On the exterior, both the Tundra and the Sequoia—which essentially share the same front clip—boast simplified designs. The lower bumper cover isn’t as busy now, instead opting for a more rectangular layout that also nicely integrates new fog lights. The tweaks are subtle, but the newer one carries a little bit more quiet confidence, which we dig.


Toyota | Benjamin Hutton


Toyota | Benjamin Hutton


On the tech side, all 2027 Tundras and Sequoias will come standard with the larger 14-inch central infotainment screen that was once reserved for higher-dollar trims. The new screen gets a more smartphone-like layout with customizable widgets for frequently accessed items. Along with the new screen, a new built-in dash camera will capture 20-second clips of both manually- and automatically-triggered events. This could be anything from turning the camera on to record a buffalo passing across the road in front of you at Yellowstone National Park, or a close-call in which you applied heavy braking and the camera records what went down.


Expect more product details and pricing later this fall.
