# Final Parking Space: 1974 Jeep CJ-5

While I have no problem finding[discarded examples of the International Harvester Scout](https://www.murileemartin.com/Junkyard/JunkyardGallery-IHC.html) in the car graveyards along the I-25 corridor between Cheyenne, Wyoming, and Albuquerque, New Mexico,[the Jeep CJ](https://www.hagerty.com/media/archived/jeep-cj-the-greatest-of-the-1940s/) is a much different story. Here’s one that now sits in its final parking space in[a yard just south of Denver](https://coloradoautoandparts.com/#navigation) .


[Murilee Martin](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/mmartin/)


Before today’s FPS subject, I had documented exactly *one* Jeep CJ in a self-service wrecking yard:[this ’74 that I found near Denver back in 2024](https://www.autoweek.com/car-life/a60085332/1974-jeep-cj-5-junkyard-treasure/) .


[Murilee Martin](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/mmartin/)


In fact, before today’s Jeep, I had written about more junked Austin Champs than I had about junked CJs. These right-hand-drive British soldiers and their Rolls-Royce engines (seen above) ended their careers in the Centennial State.


[Murilee Martin](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/mmartin/)


It *is* possible to find the CJ-5’s rear-wheel-drive[DJ-5 Dispatcher](https://www.hagerty.com/media/automotive-history/the-first-modern-factory-built-ev-from-a-major-automaker-was-a-postal-jeep/) cousin in the Ewe Pullets of the land, because the US Postal Service bought so many of them during the 1969–1984 period, and it’s a major job to convert one to four-wheel-drive and then move the steering wheel to the left side.


[Murilee Martin](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/mmartin/)


If you’re looking for the simplest possible motor vehicle, essentially a steel box on wheels with an automatic transmission bolted to whatever engine Kaiser or AMC was able to source for the least money when built, the USPS DJ-5 is your car… but most examples don’t hold their value enough to avoid a junkyardy fate when they get worn out. I’ve[documented many DJ-5s](https://www.murileemartin.com/Junkyard/JunkyardGallery-Jeep.html) during my boneyard travels.


Aaron McKenzie


The CJ-5[has held its value](https://www.hagerty.com/media/market-trends/hagerty-insider/your-handy-1955-83-jeep-cj-5-buyers-guide/) in recent decades, and that means that rough ones nearly always get rescued before they[end up in the crusher’s cold steel jaws](http://www.thetruthaboutcars.com/2012/12/auction-to-crusher-12-weeks-in-the-lives-of-two-cars-at-a-self-service-wrecking-yard/) . If nothing else, they get stashed away as parts donors for restoration projects.


Stellantis


[The CJ-5](https://www.hagerty.com/media/buying-and-selling/the-jeep-cj-5-was-built-forever-to-go-wherever/) was built from 1954 through 1983 and was at heart a civilianized version of the Korean War–era[Willys M38A1](https://www.hagerty.com/media/buying-and-selling/korean-war-era-willys-jeep-sale/) . It wasn’t comfortable, but it was better suited for everyday street driving than[its CJ-3 predecessor](https://www.hagerty.com/valuation-tools/willys~jeep/cj~3a/1951/1951-willys~jeep-cj~3a-1!4_ton) , which stayed in production into the late 1960s.


[Murilee Martin](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/mmartin/)


If you were doing your vehicle shopping in Japan, you could buy a new Mitsubishi-built CJ-3 ([called the J3](https://www.mitsubishi-motors.com/en/company/history/car/) ) all the way through the late 1990s. The global Jeep CJ production story has too many plot twists to cover here, but it’s worth noting that Mahindra[built CJ-3s in India](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=erhORDnwJeQ) until 2010.


[Murilee Martin](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/mmartin/)


The direct ancestor of all the CJs was the original Jeep, the[Willys MB](https://www.hagerty.com/media/archived/steve-mcqueens-1945-willys-mb/) of World War II fame. Here’s a street-driven ’44 GPW (the Ford-built version) in the parking lot of[the Bastogne War Museum in Belgium](https://www.bastognewarmuseum.be/en/home-en/) .


American Motors Corporation


The CJ-5 was a narrow vehicle, which made it tippy but also capable of squeezing past obstacles on difficult trails. Unlike its military cousins, it had some comfort and convenience features and was designed to tolerate sustained pavement driving.


[Murilee Martin](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/mmartin/)


The CJ-5 was designed by Willys and went into production soon after that company was purchased by Kaiser Motors. The American Motors Corporation bought Kaiser-Jeep in 1970 and continued CJ-5 production through the end.


[Murilee Martin](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/mmartin/)


The CJ-5 became a much more street-capable machine during the AMC era. The wheelbase got a 2½-inch stretch for 1972, and beefy Kenosha engines replaced the ancient[Willys Hurricane F-head inline-four](https://www.curbsideclassic.com/automotive-histories/automotive-history-the-curious-f-head-engine/) and[Buick 225-cubic-inch V-6](https://www.hagerty.com/media/opinion/final-parking-space/final-parking-space-1968-jeepster-commando/) .


[Murilee Martin](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/mmartin/)


The base engine for the early AMC-era CJ-5 was the 232-cubic-inch pushrod straight-six, with the 258-cubic-inch six and 304-cubic-inch V-8 as optional equipment. This truck has the 258, which was rated at 110 horsepower and 185 pound-feet for 1974. This was good power for a vehicle that scaled in at just 2540 pounds (its stretched CJ-6 big brother weighed 2600 pounds and had the same engine choices).


[Murilee Martin](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/mmartin/)


The build tag on the dash tells us that this CJ-5 was built at[the plant in Toledo, Ohio](https://toledocitypaper.com/feature/jeep-fest-2021-a-brief-history-of-jeep-in-toledo/) , during July of 1974.


[Murilee Martin](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/mmartin/)


The transmission is the base three-speed manual. Four-speed manuals and three-speed automatics were available as options in 1974.


[Murilee Martin](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/mmartin/)


Air conditioning wasn’t available in the CJ-5 until 1977, but this luxurious heater/defroster/vent system was standard equipment for 1974.


[Murilee Martin](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/mmartin/)


Various soft- and hard-cab and[roof options](https://oldcarbrochures.org/United%20States/Jeep/1973-Jeep/1973-Jeep-Full-Line-Brochure/slides/1973_Jeep_Full_Line-13.html) were available from the factory, but this truck has an obvious aftermarket steel body added.


[Murilee Martin](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/mmartin/)


This is a[Koenig Iron Works Model 550 hardtop rig](https://www.ewillys.com/2019/11/09/koenig-hardtop-brochures/) , built in Houston. The pre-ZIP code address on this tag suggests that it is[a unit made before 1963](https://cj3b.info/Hardtops/Koenig.html) .


[Murilee Martin](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/mmartin/)


That explains the fact that the edge of the door hinge covers up the Jeep badge, since the AMC-era CJ-5 was longer than the Kaiser-era version. That rust hole below the door indicates scary corrosion problems that likely spelled doom for this Jeep.


[Murilee Martin](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/mmartin/)


This Koenig Iron Works window regulator design is a marvel of simplicity.


[Murilee Martin](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/mmartin/)


Do you really need more gauges than this?


[Murilee Martin](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/mmartin/)


This bolt-in roll bar looks like it’s[the “racing style” factory option](https://oldcarbrochures.org/United%20States/Jeep/1973-Jeep/1973-Jeep-Full-Line-Brochure/slides/1973_Jeep_Full_Line-13.html) , but it could also be aftermarket.


[Murilee Martin](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/mmartin/)


This was a very competent off-road vehicle, but the comfort level was punitive by the SUV standards of even a decade later.


[Murilee Martin](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/mmartin/)


Judging by the large amount of rodent poop inside, it sat outdoors for a lengthy period before coming here. I’m even more careful about[hantavirus](https://www.uchealth.org/today/take-precautions-with-hantavirus/) than I used to be, since a Colorado friend nearly died of it recently (he believes he caught it while tearing out the interior of an old RV).


[Murilee Martin](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/mmartin/)


This truck would have had an MSRP of $3574 when new, plus the 54 extra bucks for the 258 engine. That comes to about $26,091[after inflation](https://murileemartin.substack.com/p/your-handy-inflationary-car-price) . That’s quite a bit more than[a new Wrangler](https://www.jeep.com/wrangler.html) , which starts at $36,035, but that truck is like an intergalactic luxury spacecraft next to a mid-1970s CJ.


“Whatcha doin’ on yer day off?” It’s noteworthy that there’s exactly zero pavement driving depicted in this commercial.


“It’s got guts. Rugged Jeep guts.”


Even a decade later, the ride of the CJ was pitched as comparable to a high-speed jaunt on the back of a rampaging buffalo.
