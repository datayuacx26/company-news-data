# Which DIY Repair Saved You the Most Money?

Be it an automobile or any other product, a repair job usually takes three elements: the correct diagnosis, the appropriate replacement part(s), and the requisite labor to perform the repair. Repairs are often a team effort; just look at how many folks are required to run a service center. But there are plenty of enterprising individuals out there who believe they can do it by themselves. These do-it-yourself (DIY) repairs are a big part of automobile ownership, and they are easier than ever these days.


Why? Not only are many repair tools accessible and affordable, but the information is readily available online. We have dedicated repositories like[ALLDATA](https://www.alldata.com/us/en/oem-repair-information-provider?utm_source={source}&utm_medium={medium}&utm_term=alldata&utm_content=781567538064&utm_campaign=&gad_source=1&gad_campaignid=22923240669&gbraid=0AAAAADpwKh4op_8QzMePeEMRZWSgaSzbD&gclid=Cj0KCQjwjvfSBhDpARIsAEiOpStF5M7ABHB_J_197p9bvulrQ7XJ9J3tmmUechLLy9h7kIThVD637_waAotFEALw_wcB) ,[traditional automotive forums](https://www.hagerty.com/media/great-reads/did-facebook-kill-the-car-forum/) , social media groups (Facebook, Reddit, etc.), content creators on YouTube, and—should you prefer physical media—even automotive literature resellers on eBay. I generally like forums and YouTube videos for detailed explainers on a given matter, as they have saved me countless hours.


My job writing about cars on the Internet has intensified the issue, as DIY-ers inspire many pieces in my portfolio. I’ve likely done enough work to save myself six figures, so I really appreciate folks who spend the time to share their knowledge with others.


We all have to start somewhere, and I wish I had a video like this one from YouTube creator/personal friend[Brad Hansen](https://www.hagerty.com/media/buying-and-selling/what-does-it-really-cost-to-own-a-c4-corvette/#:~:text=This%20video%20from,1.09%20per%20mile.) when I began my DIY journey many decades ago. Hansen does a great job setting you up for success, not necessarily by teaching you how to do the repair, but how to get your mind ready for a DIY task.


While I enjoyed this particular video, it was more of a refresher course for yours truly. My most recent DIY effort included YouTube to ensure I correctly installed a rear window for my Ranger, after demoting that glass from a[prominent display location inside my house](https://www.hagerty.com/media/hagerty-community/what-car-parts-are-worthy-of-your-home/#:~:text=Just%20last%20week%2C%20I%20purchased%20a%20new%20rear%20window%20for%20my%20Ford%20Ranger%20(RIP%2C%20sliding%20rear%20glass%20and%20its%20leaky%20weatherstripping)%20and%20found%20it%20eye%2Dcatching%20enough%20to%20leave%20in%20my%20house%20for%20a%20while) . Aside from the color-matched rain gutter popping itself loose on the driver’s side, the conversion from a (leaky) sliding rear window to a single-piece affair went quite nicely.

It also feels great to share these thoughts with others, so here’s the question I propose to you fine folks:


**What DIY repair saved you the most money and how did you learn to do it?**


For me, it’s likely DIY heater-core replacements *(shivers)* .
