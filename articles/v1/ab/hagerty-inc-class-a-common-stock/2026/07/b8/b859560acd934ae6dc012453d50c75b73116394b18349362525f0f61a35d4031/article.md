# 1963 Cadillac Sedan de Ville: It Played in Peoria

Sometimes I see a car and know nothing of its history. So I, just like Joe Friday, stick to the facts, ma’am. Other times the car is owned by a friend, so I get the whole story, with details, pictures, etc. And in today’s example, the owner is proud to detail the car’s origins and early days.


[Thomas Klockau](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/tklockau/)


This Bahama Sand 1963 Sedan de Ville four-window hardtop sedan, one of 30,579 made, was originally sold by Travis Cadillac-Pontiac in Peoria, Illinois, on March 30, 1963.


[Thomas Klockau](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/tklockau/)


The buyer was a Mr. Daniel Moran, of Pontiac, Illinois, and the grand total was $5041.75. As an additional placard on the car’s dash continued, Mr. Moran “…stopped driving the car in 2007. It was placed in storage until 2018, when Mr. Moran passed away.


[Thomas Klockau](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/tklockau/)


“…as part of the estate sale, the car was sold to the Daniels family in August of 2019. The car is all original, it had 24,788 on the odometer when it was bought.” It further added that a Cadillac eight-track tape player was added to the car sometime in the 1970s.


Thomas Klockau


This time-capsule Cadillac was another attendee at the Patrick Cadillac show in Schaumburg, Illinois, back in August 2024. Even before I read the paperwork, I knew this car was something special.


[Thomas Klockau](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/tklockau/)


First of all, the car had not a mark on it. And the leather and fabric upholstery, even on the driver’s seat. Cadillac interiors of the 1960s were first rate in material quality, but after 61 years, the fabric should have shown at least some wear.


[Thomas Klockau](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/tklockau/)


However, in this car, it was perfect. Even before I knew anything about the car’s past, I knew it had to be a low-mile car.


[Thomas Klockau](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/tklockau/)


And I’d bet dollars to donuts this car was garaged from day one. Oh sure, you can get a first-rate interior restoration done if you know the right sources, but there’s just a certain something about original, factory interiors.


[Thomas Klockau](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/tklockau/)


And every other part of the car had the look of a well-preserved, well-loved car. Despite the rather quiet color combination of Bahama Sand with a matching interior, it just was a gorgeous car!


[Thomas Klockau](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/tklockau/)


Bolder 1963 color choices included Turino Turquoise, Matador Red, Somerset Blue, Royal Maroon, Brewster Green, Aspen White (with white, your author strongly recommends a bright red interior), and Basque Blue. Power was a V-8, of course—for 1963 it was a 390-CID unit with 325 hp.


[Thomas Klockau](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/tklockau/)


There were four available models in the de Ville series for 1963. There was the four-window hardtop sedan, which is what our featured car is, the six-window hardtop sedan,[the Park Avenue short-deck hardtop sedan](https://oldcarbrochures.org/United%20States/Cadillac/1963_Cadillac/1963%20Cadillac%20Prestige/slides/63cad14-15.html) (only 1575 were built), and the classic Coupe de Ville.


Thomas Klockau


Interestingly, the four-window, six-window, and Park Avenue de Ville sedans were all the same price: $5633. But the four-window was the belle of the ball, with 30,579 made, each weighing in at 4065 pounds. In comparison, the six-window sold 15,146 units, and the Park Avenue, as previously mentioned, a mere 1575.


[Thomas Klockau](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/tklockau/)


But the most popular 1963 de Ville of them all was the[Coupe de Ville](https://www.hagerty.com/media/opinion/klockau-classics/1966-cadillac-coupe-deville-gorgeous-in-gold/) , which sold 31,749 examples, each with a base price of $5386. Unsurprisingly, the six-window de Ville and the Park Avenue both disappeared after a final curtain call for 1964.


[Thomas Klockau](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/tklockau/)


1963 saw all-new, yet still clearly Cadillac, styling. The big crosshatch grille, fins, fender skirts and long, smooth lines all remained, but the skeg fins, which briefly appeared for 1961-62, had been removed. Fins were just slightly lower, and would finally disappear when the 1965 models started rolling into showrooms.


Thomas Klockau


As the 1963 sales brochure cajoled, “Can a Cadillac surpass its own great reputation? The 1963 Cadillac does just that. In beauty…in luxury…in performance…and in craftsmanship, the new Cadillac creations portrayed in this brochure surpass the famous cars they supersede.


Thomas Klockau


“We feel certain that after appraising the 1963 selections, and then after your first few minutes at the wheel, you will agree that Cadillac for 1963 has surpassed its own great reputation.”


[Thomas Klockau](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/tklockau/)


I look at all the silver, white, and gray crossover/SUV blobs on the roads today, and I truly wish I could get something even half as imposing as this 1963 Sedan de Ville! Barring that, I shall continue to work on my time machine, time permitting.


[Thomas Klockau](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/tklockau/)
