# 1988 Mercedes-Benz 560SL: Best of the Breed

The 1972–89 Mercedes SL roadster,[internally designated the R107](https://www.hagerty.com/media/market-trends/hagerty-insider/your-handy-1971-89-mercedes-benz-sl-buyers-guide/) , was a long-lived chassis. As with a lot of classics, the debate between car people usually centers on which ones are the best: the newest, or the earliest ones? While I do love the looks of the 1972–73 versions, with their slim, delicate chrome bumpers, the final ones (the 560s) remain my favorite, probably because they were still new cars, on the road and on TV, when I was a kid.


Thomas Klockau


Though Mercedes are known for their solidity, quality, and good driving dynamics, at least back then, it seems those last ones are just so much more of a treat to drive. As a friend of mine recently said of the 560SL, “ *Yes* ! They are just lovely cars and are perfectly happy in modern traffic.”


[Thomas Klockau](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/tklockau/)


While the R107 didn’t visually change much over the years, other than bumpers, wheel covers, and alloy wheel styles, there were several differently named versions. The changes in nomenclature are all attributable to the various engines residing under the hoods of different models.


[Thomas Klockau](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/tklockau/)


While there were 280SL and 350SL models early on, these were European-market only. The early U.S.-bound cars were badged as 350SLs, but had the 4.5-liter V-8 under the hood.


Thomas Klockau


Later on, there would be a 500SL, but no American-bound roadster, other than gray-market imports, would sport that badge.


Thomas Klockau


Honestly, the nomenclature is all very confusing, and not terribly interesting to your author. But regardless of the badge or the engine under the hood, all of these cars were desirable, then and now.


Thomas Klockau


Sporty and luxurious, they are perfectly appropriate parked in front of a country club, gymkhana, or the[Beverly Hills Hotel](https://www.dorchestercollection.com/los-angeles/the-beverly-hills-hotel) . And who could forget the one Eddie Murphy piloted around in *Beverly Hills Cop* ?


[Thomas Klockau](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/tklockau/)


The 560SL first appeared in U.S. showrooms for the 1986 model year. In addition to the new engine, it sported the new, flatter-looking, light alloy wheels seen on other new Mercedes-Benzes, from the[190E](https://www.hagerty.com/media/market-trends/hagerty-insider/mercedes-benz-evo-ii-market-spot/) to the[560SEL](https://www.hagerty.com/media/car-profiles/definitive-1979-92-mercedes-benz-w126-s-class-buyers-guide/) . A driver’s side airbag was standard.


Thomas Klockau


Of course, the big news was the 5.6-liter V-8, which by 1988 produced 227 hp and 279 lb-ft of torque. Said engine had a 9.0:1 compression ratio and a single overhead cam, and was coupled to a four-speed automatic transmission with a torque converter.


[Thomas Klockau](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/tklockau/)


The ’88 brochure extolled its virtues: “From a storied tradition of mighty Mercedes-Benz SL automobiles comes the mightiest SL of them all.


[Thomas Klockau](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/tklockau/)


“A most civilized two-seater that is equipped with both a removable steel hardtop and convertible folding top, the 560SL melds the verve of a fully open roadster with the year-round comfort of an enclosed hardtop coupe.


[Thomas Klockau](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/tklockau/)


“Powered by an indomitable 5.6-liter light-alloy V8, the 560SL Coupe/Roadster sprints to 55 mph in just a bit more than six seconds…with a track almost five feet wide, the surefooted 560SL plants its large-footprint, low profile tires firmly on the pavement. It is remarkably nimble, moving with balletic grace and precision.”


Thomas Klockau


They were just pretty—at least to your author. I even drove one myself years back. A friend at work, Joyce, had a tobacco brown metallic 1983 380SL, with saddle tan leather and chromed “Bundt” alloy wheels.


[Thomas Klockau](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/tklockau/)


They had bought the car when they lived in California, and brought it with them when they moved back to the Midwest. One time I washed and detailed the car for her, and enjoyed it quite a bit.


[Thomas Klockau](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/tklockau/)


Back in the mid-’90s, a friend of my parents, Teresa Perkins, had a black ’76 450SL, with tan leather and a gold pinstripe along both flanks. It was pretty sharp!


Thomas Klockau


Another friend of mine, Jonny Valadez, had a ’76 Signal Red and tan one too, a 450SL. I got to see it once when I joined him at a Cadillac-Buick club show in the Chicago suburbs, back in 2017.


Jonny’s 450SL at Rizza Buick, 2017.


Thomas Klockau


It was very distinctive among the modern jellybean crossovers and Escalades and Yukons in the parking lot!


[Thomas Klockau](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/tklockau/)


He also had a 300TD wagon affectionately nicknamed Klaus, a ’76 Monte Carlo Landau, and a ’78[LTD Country Squire station wagon](https://www.hagerty.com/media/opinion/klockau-classics/1977-ford-ltd-country-squire-suburban-luxury-from-the-wagonmaster/) . Sadly, he passed away in 2018. I’ve no idea what became of his Mercedes, or any of his other cars.


[Thomas Klockau](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/tklockau/)


Regardless, I loved all the 1972–89 SLs. While the all-new 1990 500SL was sharp and even more powerful than the final R107s, I must confess I like these cars more—perhaps they have more personality, or perhaps it’s just your author’s preference talking. Your guess is as good as mine!


Thomas Klockau


I immediately stopped the car and jumped out last September when I spied this seemingly showroom-new Signal Red example parked at the Freight House (a refurbished, well, freight house, with restaurants and a brewery) on the riverfront in Davenport, Iowa. It just sparkled, and I loved the Signal Red/saddle tan color combo—just classic!


[Thomas Klockau](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/tklockau/)


The green SL, equally nice, was spied at the Mt. Carroll, Illinois cruise night, back in 2016. Either one would be perfect transportation for a ride and dinner on a sunny late summer day!


Thomas Klockau


Oh, and I must confess I am not sure if either car is specifically a 1988, but I had the plush 1988 Mercedes-Benz S-Class brochure in my collection, so that provided the data I gathered for this column. But any 560SL, regardless of year, would be a fun summer cruiser.


Thomas Klockau
