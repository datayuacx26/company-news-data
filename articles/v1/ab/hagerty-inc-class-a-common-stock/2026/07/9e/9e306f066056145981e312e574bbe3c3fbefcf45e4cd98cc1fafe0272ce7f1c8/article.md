# Petersen Museum Displays Mullin Family Foundation’s Gift of French Art Deco Cars

The Petersen Automotive Museum has unveiled a new exhibit from the Mullin Collection. The cars, including coachbuilt rarities from Bugatti, Talbot-Lago, and Delahaye, were part of the Mullin Family Foundation’s gift to the Petersen[following Peter Mullin’s 2023 passing](https://www.hagerty.com/media/people/renowned-car-collector-peter-mullin-passes-at-82/) and the[closure of the Oxnard, California, museum](https://www.hagerty.com/media/news/mullin-museum-home-to-dozens-of-french-cars-will-close/) founded by Peter and his wife, Merle, where these cars had been displayed.


The 1934 Avions Voisin Type C27 Aerosport coupe is just one of the fantastic French automobiles previously on display at the Mullin Museum (shown here) that is now in the Petersen Automotive Museum.


[Brandan Gillogly](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/brandan-gillogly/)


“Peter believed these automobiles belonged to history, not to us,” said Merle Mullin, trustee of the Mullin Family Foundation, Petersen Automotive Museum board member, and internationally respected automotive collector. “Together, we built this collection with the dream of preserving the extraordinary artistry, innovation, and craftsmanship of French art deco automobiles. It was equally important to us that these masterpieces would one day be shared with the broadest possible audience. Peter always believed the Petersen Automotive Museum was the right steward for that responsibility, and it is deeply meaningful to see that vision fulfilled. We are proud to know this collection will continue inspiring generations of enthusiasts, historians, and future collectors.”

- 1931 Bugatti Type 50S by Million-Guiet
- 1934 Avions Voisin Type C27 Grand Sport Roadster

- 1951 Delahaye Type 235 Cabriolet by Saoutchik
- 1951 Bugatti Type 101C by Gangloff
- 1951 Talbot-Lago T26 Grand Sport by Stabilimenti Farina


While this display already represents one of the greatest collections of French art deco automotive artistry in one location, it’s just a preview of an even more expansive display the Petersen has planned for 2028. The Mullin Family Grand Salon, the Petersen’s main ground-floor display area, is currently filled with “[World Class: 100 Years of Mercedes-Benz](https://www.hagerty.com/media/news/world-class-100-years-of-mercedes-benz-at-the-petersen-automotive-museum/) .” Mullin was the chairman of the Petersen in 2015, and a champion of its renovation that year. He was the one who cut the ribbon at the ceremonial reopening in December 2015. The museum showed his vision then by filling the Mullin Family Grand Salon with coachbuilt cars. It’s fitting that the Mullin Family Foundation’s tremendous gift should have these beautiful works of automotive art in prominent display at a venue that Peter Mullin was so deeply involved in.
