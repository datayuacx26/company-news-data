# Never Stop Driving #191: Summer Reading

Reading builds your brain, or so the research shows. And since it’s summertime, when many of us take some R&R, this week, I’m sharing some of the greatest recent Hagerty reads. Rather than spending your porch, pool, or beach hours mindlessly scrolling through your phone, may I suggest you exercise your brain and perhaps learn a few things from the talented scribes at Hagerty? And don’t forget to sign up for[our free newsletters](https://www.hagerty.com/media/newsletter/) so you’ll always hear about our latest articles and videos.


[A Father-and-Son Road Trip to Death Valley in America’s Sports Car](https://www.hagerty.com/media/great-reads/father-and-son-road-trip-americas-sports-car-to-death-valley/)
Road-trip diaries are one of the toughest things to write—too often there’s no challenge to overcome, no drama, and therefore no story—which is why we’re selective with travel story assignments. Longtime Hagerty contributor John L. Stein, however, assembled the right ingredients when he and his Gen Z son set out for California’s Death Valley in a newly acquired 1963 split-window Corvette. Stein fluently weaves together road trip anecdotes, Corvette history, geography lessons, and touching pass-the-torch moments. Total home run.


Andrew Trahan


[Nashville’s Fairgrounds Speedway Faces an Uncertain Future](https://www.hagerty.com/media/great-reads/nashvilles-fairgrounds-speedway-faces-an-uncertain-future/)
The Fairgrounds Speedway in Nashville has hosted auto racing since 1904—five years before the Indianapolis Motor Speedway opened. Now a local coalition wants to replace it with affordable housing, and the fight has landed in court. While the track’s future is in doubt, none other than NASCAR’s unofficial mayor, Dale Earnhardt, Jr., has thrown in his support. Writer Cameron Neveu, who saw his first NASCAR race here at age six and returned for a race this spring, deftly presents both sides of the zoning battle, but we know where his heart lies.


[Cameron Neveu](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/cameron-neveu/)


[The SP40 Replica Edsel Roadster Parties Like It’s 1935](https://www.hagerty.com/media/car-profiles/the-sp40-replica-edsel-roadster-parties-like-its-1935/)
Edsel Ford and his styling chief Eugene Gregorie, who elevated Ford design with the Model A and the Lincoln Zephyr, also collaborated on a one-off speedster called the Model 40. Now an Argentine company has resurrected Edsel’s legendary personal project—this time with a carbon-fiber body, a modern Coyote V-8, and a half-million-dollar price. Our premier feature writer, Aaron Robinson, also has a fine hand with car reviews and deftly describes the SP40 while explaining that a nod to history is sometimes better than a straight re-creation.


[Brandan Gillogly](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/brandan-gillogly/)


[How the 1955 Chrysler C-300 Kicked Off the Muscle Car Era](https://www.hagerty.com/media/great-reads/when-america-got-moving-the-1955-chrysler-c-300-kicked-off-the-muscle-car-era/)
Long before Pontiac introduced the GTO, Chrysler launched the C-300, named for its 300-hp Hemi V-8. Does Chrysler deserve credit for kicking off the muscle-car era? Wrong question, maintains staff writer David Zenlea: “Whether this combination of style and power merits the moniker ‘The first muscle car’ is up for debate—frankly, a pedantic one.” Instead, Zenlea explains, the C-300 represents a major crossroads in automotive history, when companies struggled to automate, consumer tastes changed, and Chrysler leaned into horsepower to stand out.


James Lipman


[70 Years of the Interstate: How the U.S. Highway System Built the America We Know](https://www.hagerty.com/media/automotive-history/70-years-of-the-interstate-how-the-u-s-highway-system-built-the-america-we-know/)
While we just celebrated America’s 250th, this year also marks another major anniversary: 70 years ago, President Dwight D. Eisenhower, from a hospital bed, quietly signed the Federal Highway Act of 1956. His signature kicked off the greatest public works project in American history. In this deeply researched piece, staff writer Andrew Newton dives into the need for the bill, the history of our roads, and the huge impact freeways have had on our cities and landscapes. You’ll discover things here you didn’t know you wanted to know—always our goal.


Unsplash/Pete Alexopoulos


[“Mr. Best of Show” Paul Russell Reflects on Five Decades as a Master Restorer](https://www.hagerty.com/media/great-reads/mr-best-of-show-paul-russell-reflects-on-five-decades-as-a-master-restorer/)
When Ralph Lauren wants a classic restored, he calls Paul Russell, and for good reason. Over the past five decades, Paul Russell and Company has won more than 50 Best of Show awards at the world’s most prestigious concours events. How does Russell do it? How is he training the next generation of craftspeople? And what does it take to tackle a multimillion-dollar restoration? Staff writer Kirk Seaman sat down with Russell, Grand Marshal of the 2026 Greenwich Concours, for an in-depth discussion on those topics and much more.


Courtesy Paul Russell & Company


[What Makes a Car an American Car?](https://www.hagerty.com/media/automotive-history/what-makes-a-car-an-american-car/)
This question was front of mind earlier this year as we prepared our coverage for America’s 250th birthday. After all, no other nation has been shaped by the automobile as much as the United States. What makes a car uniquely American? It’s not just fins and V-8s, Aaron Robinson explains in his thought-provoking essay. Among his many insights is this assertion: “More than the cars of any other nation, our cars are mirrors that reflect back on us in perfect die-cast, cold-forged, and hot-stamped detail who we are and who we want to be.” Brilliant.


David McNew/Getty Images


[2027 Ram 1500 TRX SRT First Drive: Ameritruck](https://www.hagerty.com/media/new-car-reviews/2027-ram-1500-trx-srt-first-drive-ameritruck/)
“Seriously, don’t overthink this,” staff writer Nathan Petroelje advises early on in his rollicking review of this 777-hp bonkers pickup. “This is a half-ton pickup with more than a foot of suspension travel, launch control, 35-inch tires, and the Hellcat V-8. If you’re not able to have fun in the TRX, you may want to double-check for a pulse. If you can’t find one, hook some defibrillator paddles up to the TRX’s five-inch exhaust tips and blip the throttle two or three times. You’ll be right as rain.” Well said, Nate!


Ram Trucks


[Offy: Four Decades as the Racing Engine to Beat](https://www.hagerty.com/media/great-reads/offy-four-letters-four-cylinders-four-decades-as-the-engine-to-beat/)
Grab your favorite beverage, settle into your Adirondack chair, and get ready to savor every word of this comprehensive history of one of the most successful racing engines of all time. Contributing writer Preston Lerner’s deep dive into the Offy traces the engine’s roots not only to the early 1930s and machinist Fred Offenhauser’s shop in Los Angeles but even farther back, to the earliest days of engine development at French automaker Peugeot and, subsequently, to Harry Arminius Miller’s straight-eight engines here in the States. It’s a fascinating tale of engineering brilliance and American ingenuity, expertly told.


Cameron Neveu
