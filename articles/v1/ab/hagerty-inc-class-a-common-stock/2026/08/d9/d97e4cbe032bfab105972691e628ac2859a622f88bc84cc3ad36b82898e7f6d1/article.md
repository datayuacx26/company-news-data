# Big Vehicles Need Bigger Brakes: Brembo Boldika Answers the Call

How heavy are big pickup trucks and high-performance electric SUVs these days? Heavy enough that domestic manufacturers asked Brembo for bigger, more powerful brakes. The Italian outfit’s solution: the Boldika. It’s a new, bigger six-piston caliper with a unique design that allows for nearly 6300 lb-ft of braking torque.


That’s the most powerful caliper Brembo has offered for a passenger vehicle, roughly double the braking torque that Brembo’s existing six-piston calipers can muster in, say, a Chevrolet Corvette. OEMs are demanding this kind of performance, Brembo says, because trucks the size of a Ford F-250 and above, some weighing in at as much as 8000 pounds, may also be equipped to tow 15,000 pounds or more. Similarly, big electric SUVs and their heavier batteries can use more stopping power.


Brembo


Enter the Boldika, which uses a unique three-piece construction. The inner piece, where the caliper attaches to the vehicle’s hardware, is a cast-iron central bridge. The outer two halves, connected by bolts, are constructed from aluminum. Brembo says that this design “increases stiffness, delivering greater braking power, consistency and stability while reducing weight compared with a fully cast-iron alternative.” ****


Still, the calipers are big and beefy—they weigh about 31 pounds, compared with standard Brembo six-piston calipers that weigh roughly half as much. Thus, they’re designed to work with large rotors: On the sample unit we saw at a presentation in metro Detroit, the rotor measured 15.7 inches. As one might expect, bigger rotors mean bigger pads. Maximizing pad surface area increases braking performance and pad life, Brembo says.


The Boldika is compatible with wheels sized 20 inches and larger, and the unit is compact enough to remain suitable for packaging in trucks and SUVs commonly used for off-roading and heavy towing.


Brembo


Service is straightforward and similar to what the existing six-piston caliper requires. In this case, however, the Boldika is held on by four bolts instead of the usual two. Once it is off the rotors, pad swaps are as simple as removing the pins, swapping pads, and reinstalling the pins. Given the dissimilar metals that are in close contact, corrosion protection comes in the form of a zinc-nickel coating for the bracket, while the caliper is itself anodized and painted.


Hydraulic pressures are “not dramatically higher” than on a traditional caliper, says Benjamin Pohl, Brembo North America director of program management. At present, Brembo has no plans to combine the new Boldika caliper with[Sensify](https://www.hagerty.com/media/news/dry-brake-by-wire-systems-coming-soon-to-light-trucks-near-you/) —the company’s fluid-free electromechanical braking technology.


Brembo will manufacture the Boldika in North America, and it’s expected to remain exclusive to that market, where heavy-duty trucks and big EVs roam. The first application will be the 2026 Chevrolet Silverado EV RST Stars and Steel Edition. Right now, Brembo says, there is no aftermarket application in the works, so the Boldika will be sold exclusively to OEMs.
