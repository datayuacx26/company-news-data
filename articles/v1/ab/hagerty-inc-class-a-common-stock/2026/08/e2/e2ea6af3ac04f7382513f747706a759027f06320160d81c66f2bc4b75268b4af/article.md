# “We Had to Reboot the Entire System:” Inside Maserati’s Reset

Maserati is[turning 100 years old](https://www.hagerty.com/media/automotive-history/maserati-100-years-of-highs-and-lows/) in 2026. It’s also commemorating a victory from that inaugural year in the storied Targa Florio race, with Alfieri Maserati behind the wheel. As the brand celebrates its centennial, though, its future is shrouded in uncertainty. Rumors of[a looming sale](https://www.hagerty.com/media/news/update-stellantis-responds-to-our-hung-a-for-sale-sign-on-maserati-story/) have hovered above Maserati for years due to a number of factors, including a sharp decline in global sales, a lack of new products, and confusion about its on-again, off-again EV-only plans. And yet, its future looks brighter than it has in recent years.


*Hagerty* sat down with Cristiano Fiorio, the global chief marketing officer for Maserati, for a candid interview about what went wrong for the company, the measures that have been taken to put the Trident back on track, and what the next few years may look like.


“The overall automotive industry is undergoing the most profound transformation ever,” Fiorio told us. “We can argue whether it’s good or bad, but this transformation has particularly harmed traditional manufacturers. There are a lot of newcomers, and there’s a lot of new technology. The luxury world has been hit hard, too, and it’s not just cars: There are a lot of volatility factors around that can force a person to think two, three, four, or five times before buying a luxury good—whether it’s a house, a car, or a watch.”


Maserati has fared worse[than others](https://www.hagerty.com/media/news/its-still-a-bull-market-for-lamborghini/) , however. It sold fewer than 8000 cars globally in 2025, down from about 25,000 units in 2022 and about 50,000 in 2017. Fiorio openly acknowledged this drop and explained that the century-old company is facing an identity crisis. Should it go electric? Which segments should it steer clear of, and which segments should it embrace?


Many of these questions have been answered over the past couple of months, but finding the answers took time.


“We can say that there has been an era, up until 2024, when the overall direction for Stellantis was that the entire group, including Maserati, had to go entirely electric,” Fiorio explained. Reading between the lines strongly suggests that the “era” he’s referring to ended when former CEO Carlos Tavares[stepped down in December 2024](https://www.hagerty.com/media/news/carlos-tavares-stellantis-ceo-resigns/) . “Then, the era changed. Did it change too late or too soon? We can argue about that forever, but when it changes you have to reboot the entire system.”


John Elkann, the primary heir to the Fiat empire, stepped in as interim CEO between December 2024 and June 2025. Former Jeep CEO Antonio Filosa was appointed CEO of Stellantis in June 2025 and implemented[a series of far-reaching changes](https://www.hagerty.com/media/news/stellantis-fastlane-2030-investment-plan-new-cars/) , including investing approximately $70 billion[to bring new models to the market](https://www.hagerty.com/media/news/stellantis-7b-plan-means-11-new-vehicles-for-the-u-s-by-2030/) in key regions. Maserati will benefit from this investment.


[Ronan Glon](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/rglon/)


More importantly, it sounds like Maserati now has a clearer view of the road ahead. The brand released[three updated cars](https://www.hagerty.com/media/new-car-reviews/2027-maserati-grancabrio-trofeo-review-pictures-specs/) earlier in 2026, it reappointed Santo Ficili as CEO in July 2026, and it will launch a pair of E-segment models by the end of the decade.


“When \[Ficili\] first joined the company, the situation was pretty complex. It was recently announced that Maserati was going to go full electric, the launch of the Grecale had not gotten the right traction for a number of reasons, the market was under strong pressure, and there hadn’t been new products approved for Maserati’s future,” Fiorio said. “Our approach back then was, ‘OK, let’s fix all of the basics, and while fixing the basics let’s decide what our future looks like.’ We needed time to figure everything out.”


The volatility Fiorio alluded to makes planning the carmaker’s future exceptionally difficult. The debate surrounding electrification rages on globally, with different markets announcing wildly different regulations and changing them with little notice. Certainly, tech is important globally, but some markets want more of it than others. Geopolitical factors, including wars and tariffs, are affecting the automotive industry in a way that we haven’t seen in decades. In this context, building a product lineup is almost like guesswork.


“If you ask me today what the market is going to look like in five years, I would tell you that it’s very difficult to say. That’s why 90 percent of OEMs today say, ‘let’s offer everything’—gasoline, EVs, hybrids, and range extenders. This has a cost,” Fiorio noted.


Fiorio stopped short of revealing precisely what Maserati has in store, but he confirmed new products are finally in the pipeline. The brand will remain in the luxury segment and positioned as the flagship of the Stellantis group. Don’t expect to see a volume-oriented baby Maserati aimed at the Audi A4, for example. This strategy steers Maserati toward something closer to Porsche’s positioning in the market, with performance-focused cars, a strong identity pegged on heritage, and a diverse mix of drivetrains.


“The future of Maserati has to be in the space that Maserati owns: the luxury segment. But it has to be in segments where we were out, which was, let’s call it, a missed opportunity or something that happened. I don’t like to say ‘a mistake,’ because when you look back, it’s always easy to call something a mistake. But let’s say it was a missed opportunity. It is what it is. We didn’t have an E-segment car or a Quattroporte—we didn’t have a car in the two E-segments. Would this have been the solution to our problems? I don’t know. If you look back, probably the strategy was so different that we wouldn’t have had any way of developing these cars.”


Whether you call walking out of the E-segment a mistake, a missed opportunity, or something else entirely, Fiorio clarified that Maserati learned its lesson as global sales dropped. “Now, we have the possibility of saying ‘we want those cars.’ They belong to Maserati’s past, present, and future. So, there were a number of things that went wrong and that had to be fixed. These are fixed.”


[Ronan Glon](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/rglon/)


Production of the Quattroporte (shown above) and the smaller Ghibli[ended in 2023](https://www.hagerty.com/media/new-car-reviews/first-drives-final-v-8s-2024-maserati-ghibli-334-ultima-and-levante-ultima/) , while the Levante retired in 2024. Nothing suggests that the Quattroporte will return, because Maserati has previously clarified that it will launch a pair of E-segment cars in the coming years. That’s European market-speak for the space occupied by BMW’s 5 Series and[X5](https://www.hagerty.com/media/news/new-2027-bmw-x5-specs-pictures-pricing/) . In contrast, the Quattroporte competed in what Europe refers to as the F-segment, which is where you’ll find the Mercedes-Benz S-Class and BMW’s 7 Series.


Maserati will share additional details about its future plans during an investor day scheduled to take place in Modena, Italy, in December of this year. It’s the only brand within Stellantis to have its own investor day, which underlines that it remains a significant member of the group. The rumors claiming Stellantis had put a “for sale” sign on Maserati’s lawn? They’re nonsense, according to the executive. “Maserati has never been for sale, and it never will be,” Fiorio affirmed. “It is a strategic asset of the Stellantis group.”


Of course, it takes more than a strong business case, an optimistic message, and great cars to reboot a carmaker. But after several long years of uncertainty, Maserati now appears to finally have something it has lacked for much of the 2020s: a coherent strategy.


“I am extremely confident in the present and the future of Maserati. I know there will still be some tough times ahead, times when we will have to fight because the market is not fluid and there is good competition, but we have amazing cars,” Fiorio concluded.
