# Cadillac’s V-One Concept Is an IMSA GTP Car You Can Buy—Sort Of

Although it might look like it, the car pictured here is not a race car. Well, not in the “body-in-white, homologated for a series” way that we typically imagine. Meet the Cadillac V-One, a striking concept vehicle that sure seems to hint at customer-facing, privately owned versions of the company’s most cutting-edge race car.


Cadillac


Back in June 2024, there were rumblings that the[company was toying with the idea of a hypercar](https://www.hagerty.com/media/news/report-cadillac-wants-to-build-a-hypercar-to-rival-mercedes-amg-one/) to compete with the likes of the[Mercedes-AMG One](https://www.hagerty.com/media/news/mercedes-amg-one-nurburgring-video/) . It sure seems to us like this concept, unveiled today at The Quail, a Motorsports Gathering, is a signal that Cadillac management is now doing a lot more than just toying with that idea.


Cadillac


Cadillac


Cadillac


To IMSA fans, this thing looks immensely recognizable, which is intentional. Cadillac says the V-One is “rooted in” the company’s LMDh racing program and draws heavily from the[V-Series.R race car](https://www.hagerty.com/media/news/cadillac-corvette-earn-first-wins-of-26-on-the-streets-of-detroit/) (the actual one). And that phrase is accurate, based on what Cadillac is revealing about the shared components between the two. Chief among their overlap is the hybrid V-8 drivetrain that is famous in racing circles for whirring out of the pit stall before cracking the air with 5.5 liters of naturally aspirated fury. In the concept, the drivetrain will make 830 horsepower, which is actually *more* than what the race car makes, because the former isn’t built for a rulebook. Despite the greater output, Cadillac says the V-One concept “is intended to be easier to operate while maintaining a similar level of performance.”


Cadillac


While the body and proportions carry over directly from the race car, this concept gets a new wheel design, along with more V-Series badging and a hand-sprayed paint job that goes from silver to deep navy as you work your way back.


Cadillac


Cadillac


Inside, the typically spartan cabin of a race car is, well, still quite spartan—but tasteful. We’re particularly impressed with the separate seatbelt for your helmet. Don’t expect to be able to give ridealongs; this thing is a single-seater through and through.


Cadillac


Though the V-One is just a concept right now, we can *absolutely* see a world where Cadillac offers a handful of allocations to special (and obviously deep-pocketed) clients. Imagine shelling out $4 million for one of these and the necessary engineering and spare parts support to run four track weekends at locations of your choosing. (That, by the way, is roughly what Ferrari’s Corse Clienti program offers, just with a broader inventory of cars ranging from GT3 cars to full-blown F1 machinery.)

Of course, Cadillac isn’t the first brand to take a modern prototype and spin it into something more commercially oriented.[Porsche](https://www.hagerty.com/media/motorsports/team-penske-and-porsche-revive-two-great-liveries-for-road-america/) did it last year with the[963 RSP](https://www.hagerty.com/media/news/meet-roger-penskes-porsche-963-rsp-one-off/) , a one-off version of its cutting-edge hybrid racer built specifically for Roger Penske. Cadillac could absolutely choose to do something similar, but we’d expect—and hope—the company is interested in building more than one.
