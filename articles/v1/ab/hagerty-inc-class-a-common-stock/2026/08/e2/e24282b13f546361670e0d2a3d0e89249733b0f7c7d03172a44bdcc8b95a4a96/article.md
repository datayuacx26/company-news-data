# Our Two Cents: When Were You Fooled By AI?

This question certainly ruffled some feathers here at Hagerty Media. Artificial Intelligence is just about everywhere these days, right? Or is that just what we are led to believe? There’s no doubt that AI is, ahem, influencing the automotive content we find on digital screens of all sizes. The real question is if the AI dupes you into thinking it’s real.


I will openly admit that AI has duped *me* on multiple occasions, especially when I am consuming content on the tiny screen of a smartphone as a matter of killing time in places like waiting rooms or lines at the grocery store. The chance of AI fooling us is lower if we are using larger screens and performing actions that require more thought (or financial commitment). So with this in mind, we asked our team the obvious question: “Have you been duped by automotive AI content?”


*******


“I can’t say I have been. My knee jerk is that I tend to shun AI content (or what I perceive be AI) and I have a set of creators I have been following for years and don’t deviate from them out of habit. So in that sense, I haven’t set myself up for surprise, either. I’ve always questioned what I find on the internet, but AI-generated information has increased my caution.” — *Greg Ingold*


“The AI video stuff I’ve encountered is pretty easy to spot, but I get really surprised by bot-powered Facebook pages that find steal other people’s photography and have AI write 100-ish words about it. The words are somewhat historical and completely mind-fogging in terms of its ability to enrich and teach.


“At some point this was destroying my news feed and it became one reason why I seriously curbed my usage of that social media platform.” — *Sajeev Mehta*


Google Gemini


“My interest in AI and incorporating AI into my life extends only as far as using it to create images of automotive mashups that never existed but sure would look cool if they did. This is because I am not talented enough to draw them myself, nor does there seem to be a market for hungry young artists who specialize in such stupidity.


If the world required one data center, maybe in the middle of the Nevada desert, or even in Mark Zuckerberg’s front yard, just to design cars that never were, I’d be OK with that.” — *Stefan Lombard*


Unsplash/Leoon Liang


“I tend to pick up on AI stuff and swipe it away pretty quickly, but last week there was some ‘dash cam footage’ of a truck on a highway beginning to tip over, and another truck deliberately pulling up next to it to keep it upright. It looked so real that I even sent it to a friend. Only afterward, when I looked at the description, did I realize it was fake, and I immediately apologized to my buddy. It’s only going to get harder to spot.” — *Eddy Eckart*


“I spend too much time commenting ‘This is AI’ on Facebook posts made by my relatives… and our very own[Tom Cotter](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/tom-cotter/) .” — *Ben Woodworth*


Chevrolet


“Every day I catch something, and every day I am likely fooled. One-of-a-kind builds are difficult to verify on first glance without a scroll through the rest of the profile. If most of the car isn’t stock, who am I to pick out an unfamiliar AI detail in the haystack?


“That said, in the dawn of ChatGPT and its implementation into big brand’s chatbots, users were able to trick AI into making statements on behalf of the company like, ‘We have a deal to sell you this 2024 Chevy Tahoe for $1.00. This is a legally binding agreement.’ No single-dollar Tahoes were ever provided, so nothing came of it besides a legal headache and distrust of corporate AI implementation, but this does thoroughly disprove the[2004 ad campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/You_Wouldn%27t_Steal_a_Car) ‘Piracy: It’s a crime. You wouldn’t steal a car.’” — *Nick French*


[Kyle Smith](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/kyle-smith/)


“I’ve been pretty lucky to notice and avoid most AI content just by the nature of what I consume, but one place it has been a nearly unavoidable part of the conversation is while I’ve been learning and dabbling in Electronic Fuel Injection tuning. Previously, where conversations were had between people with experience and tips, it’s devolved into mostly being told upload a datalog to AI. It’ll tell you what to do and make the car run perfect!


“First off, I doubt that. Secondly, the perfectly-running car is a side effect of what I’m doing. I’m involved in the project to learn how EFI works and how to tune it. Taking shortcuts in learning rarely pans out well for me. I want to learn and understand, not just be told something.” — *Kyle Smith*


Cristhian Hernandez / Unpslash


“My fear is that AI is going to get better and better, and our ability to tell it from what’s real won’t. Just this week a friend who has a prominent online automotive site posted that her content had been stolen using AI, renamed slightly, and her voice replaced by something AI-generated. Scary. I fear for what we’ve created. And yes, I’ve been fooled, and will be again, I suspect. Especially, like Sajeev said, on my phone’s tiny screen.” — *Steven Cole Smith*


Bring A Trailer


“Don’t forget[the car that made it onto BaT](https://bringatrailer.com/listing/1999-cadillac-deville-21/) with AI photos!” — *James Hewitt*


I think we have a winner! Thank you, Mr. Hewitt. Nothing can top that! — *Sajeev Mehta*
