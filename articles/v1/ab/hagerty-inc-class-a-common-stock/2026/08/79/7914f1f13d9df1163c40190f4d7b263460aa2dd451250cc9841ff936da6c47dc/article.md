# 14 Important Cars from the 2026 RM Sotheby’s Monterey Auction

RM Sotheby’s held a three-day auction in Monterey this year, with the first day of sales mostly consisting of prewar greats from large collections. Otherwise, much of the buzz around this sale was consistent with the rest of Monterey Car Week (read our full analysis[here](https://www.hagerty.com/media/market-trends/2026-monterey-car-week-auctions-shatter-record-with-758m-in-sales/) ), and that buzz was about modern exotics. Many of the more than a dozen world auction records set by modern supercars were set at this auction, and total sales topped $376M, eclipsing the previous pandemic-era record from 2022. This is a staggering figure for a single car auction. While Mecum has topped $400M in Kissimmee before, it required nearly two weeks and thousands of vehicles to do so.


One hefty asterisk for this auction was the first production Ferrari Luce, which brought a[$40M](https://rmsothebys.com/auctions/mo26/lots/r0150-2026-ferrari-luce-tailor-made/) result. This was a charity lot and has essentially no relevance to the collector car market, but even without the Luce sale, this still would have been a home run of an event for RM Sotheby’s.


We were on the ground in Monterey and looked over the most important cars crossing the block over the course of the week. Here, we examine some of the favorites from RM Sotheby’s below.


## Lot 333: 1965 Iso Grifo A3/C


Jorge Guasso/RM Sotheby's


**Sold for $940,000**


*Chassis no. B0216. Mela Verde over black leather. Older restoration, #3 condition*


**Equipment:** 327-cid Corvette V-8, quadruple Webers, four-speed, centerlock alloy wheels, Dunlop Racing tires, woodrim steering wheel, alloy bodywork.


**Condition:** Believed to be one of 22 cars with the early riveted aluminum body. Had an accident early in its life and was rebodied at the factory. Sold new in Germany, imported here in the 1970s, and restored in Italy in 2012. Some scratches in the headlight covers and on the window frames. Presentable but older paint. Scratches in the rear window. Tidy underneath. Good interior other than slightly cloudy gauges. Lightly aged and very cool. The color. The rivets. The shape. It’s all awesome.


Jorge Guasso/RM Sotheby's


Jorge Guasso/RM Sotheby's


Jorge Guasso/RM Sotheby's


**Bottom line:** After engineer Giotto Bizzarrini departed Ferrari in late 1961 (along with several other higher-ups at the company), he eventually found steady work with the firm Iso, designing the four-seat, Corvette-powered, Bertone-bodied[Rivolta GT](https://www.hagerty.com/valuation-tools/iso/rivolta/1970/1970-iso-rivolta-ir_340?id=aCn1I000000DNoeSAG) for the company. Bizzarrini then shortened the Rivolta chassis, moved the engine further back, and a Giugiaro-penned, Drogo-built body was laid on top. *Sports Car Graphic* called it a “cross between a Sting Ray Corvette and a Ferrari GTO,” while its maker called it the “Iso Grifo A3/C” at first, then the “Bizzarrini GT Strada 5300” after the engineer set off on his own.


All of these cars are exceedingly rare, but the Iso-badged ones are much scarcer than the Bizzarrini-badged ones. The current high-water mark price-wise is the car bought new by singer Johnny Hallyday (aka “the Elvis Presley of France”), which brought[€1,805,000](https://rmsothebys.com/auctions/jg21/lots/r0043-1965-iso-grifo-a3c/) ($2,042,899) in 2021. The first one to sell for seven figures was a €1,244,400 ($1,409,905) result for a 5300 Strada in Paris in 2015. Given this one’s rebody and older restoration, it brought a more modest but still realistic price that was at the top end of its $750K–$1M estimate.


## Lot 266: 1958 Ferrari 250 GT Cabriolet Series I by Pinin Farina


Robin Adams/RM Sotheby's


**Sold for $4,927,500**


*Chassis no. 0809GT; Engine no. 0809GT. Rosso Metallizzatto over Naturale leather.* *Visually maintained, largely original, #3 condition.*


**Equipment:** 2953-cc/240-hp V-12, triple Webers, four-speed, Borrani wire wheels, Pirelli tires, woodrim steering wheel, Veglia gauges, dash clock, driving lights.


**Condition:** Shown at the 1958 New York Auto Show. Four owners since new, and the present owners have had it since 1985, only displaying it publicly twice. A few scuffs in the headlight covers. Several touch-ups on the front and mild cracks behind the passenger’s door. Tidy, unrestored but maintained engine, wheels, and chassis. Heavily aged and cracked, but not ripped, leather. Clean dash and clear, bright gauges. Good history, desirable configuration (covered headlights, bumperettes), and almost completely original other than a decades-old repaint in its original color.


**Bottom line:** The preservation, car show history, and the fact that it hasn’t been to market in forty years all did this Ferrari serious favors. So did its desirable closed-headlight configuration. How desirable? Well over a million dollars more desirable than the open-headlight cars, according to our price guide. This was a healthy but unsurprising price given all the above.


## Lot 342: 1952 Porsche 356 America Roadster by Gläser


Andrew Miterko Photography


**Sold for $2,205,000**


*Chassis no. 12353; Engine no. 40055. Ivory over green leather. Older restoration, #2 condition.*


**Equipment:** 1488-cc/70-hp flat-four, four-speed, hub caps, Dunlop tires, headlight stone guards.


**Condition:** One of 16 hand-formed aluminum-bodied America Roadsters and 10 known to survive. The first chassis fitted with a synchronized gearbox and reportedly the only one fitted from new with a hood-mounted fuel filler. Matching numbers engine. Fully restored by DK Engineering in the 2000s. Beautiful paint, brightwork, and interior. Even gaps. Extremely rare, and still presents well enough to show.


Andrew Miterko/RM Sotheby's


**Bottom line:** Before the lightened and focused[356 Speedster](https://www.hagerty.com/valuation-tools/search?q=porsche+356+speedster) debuted in 1954, there was the America Roadster, known internally as the Type 540 and aimed at racers in the United States. Porsche didn’t have a huge presence in the U.S. yet, and the America Roadster had a $4600 price tag (comfortably more than a new Cadillac), so production was minuscule. It is a significant model, though, and another one of the America Roadsters is even in the[National Historic Vehicle Register](https://www.hagerty.com/media/news/1952-porsche-america-roadster-national-historic-vehicle-register/) . This was a very rare opportunity for Porsche collectors, and the car sold right in the middle of its estimate range.


## Lot 276: 1953 Ferrari 212 Inter Coupe by Vignale


Robin Adams/RM Sotheby's


**Sold for $1,270,000**


*Chassis no. 0289EU; Engine no. 0289EU. Rosso over tan leather. Recent restoration, #3+ condition* .


**Equipment:** 2563-cc/150-hp V-12, triple Webers, outside filler cap, four-speed, painted Borrani wire wheels, woodrim steering wheel, Veglia gauges (including factory-installed altimeter), Marchal driving lights.


**Condition:** Represented as the last of 26 coupes bodied by Vignale and the last of six to this particular design. Originally painted in a gold metallic color. Represented as matching numbers. First restored in the 1970s (during which the chrome side trim and bumpers were removed), then received some restoration work in the 2000s and a reported full mechanical restoration from 2018 to 2024.


Robin Adams/RM Sotheby's


Robin Adams/RM Sotheby's


A driving event veteran with appearances at the Colorado Grand, California Mille, Monterey Historics, and Mille Miglia Storica. Grille trim doesn’t fit flush. Older paint. Some blemishes in the wheels. Scratched windshield frame. Mild wear to the steering wheel and driver’s seat. An early Ferrari with unusual but eye-catching Vignale coachwork, and best suited to more driving events.


**Bottom line:** Though Ferrari’s early road cars are often said to look better than they drive, the sheer variety of them is fascinating, and no two are exactly alike. This year’s Pebble Beach Concours featured a class for Vignale *and* one for Ferraris bodied by Vignale, so this was an astute time and place to sell such a car. It came to this auction in 2001, where it was a $220,000 no-sale, then sold here in 2008 for $742,500 and again in 2010 for[$654,500](https://rmsothebys.com/auctions/mo10/lots/r343-1953-ferrari-212-inter-coupe-by-vignale/) . The Ferrari market has done much over the past 16 years, of course, and this result for it in 2026 was just past the high end of the estimate range.


## Lot 259: 2005 Saleen S7 Twin Turbo


Josh Mackey/RM Sotheby's


**Sold for $1,105,000**


*Chassis no. 1S9SB18185S000062. Lizstick Red over black and gray leather.* *Original, #2 condition.*


**Equipment:** 427-cid/750-hp twin-turbo V-8, six-speed, slotted rotors, Pirelli P Zero tires, three-piece luggage set.


**Condition:** One of 30 Twin Turbo models built. Represented with 1000 miles. There’s some odd discoloration on the little plaque ahead of the shifter, but otherwise this presents like a new example of Steve Saleen’s home-grown hypercar.


**Bottom line:** The[S7](https://www.hagerty.com/media/market-trends/hagerty-insider/raw-and-race-bred-the-saleen-s7-offers-enzo-speed-for-far-less/) is a tube-frame, carbon-bodied coupe that could hang with exotics of the era like the Porsche Carrera GT and Ferrari Enzo. On track it raced against—and sometimes beat—Europe’s best. The S7 has always been cheaper than its European rivals, but with Carrera GT and especially Enzo prices up into the millions, that’s a pretty high bar. And, given the overall clamor for modern exotics in Monterey (and everywhere else, really) in 2026, it perhaps shouldn’t be a surprise that this car topped its $1M estimate and blew past its condition #1 value of $799K in our price guide.


## Lot 340: 1985 Ferrari 288 GTO


Mikey Noga/RM Sotheby's


**Sold for $11,555,000**


*Chassis no. ZFFPA16B000054789; Engine no. 00108. Rosso Corsa over Nero leather. Unrestored original, #1- condition.*


**Equipment:** 2855-cc/400-hp twin-turbo V-8, five-speed, Speedline centerlock wheels, Michelin Pilot Sport tires, Daytona-style seats, leather-wrapped Momo steering wheel, power windows, air conditioning, cassette stereo.


**Condition:** Showing 1541 km (~958 miles) on its odometer. Represented as being among the lowest-original-mileage examples remaining, with potentially only six or fewer true sub-1000-mile 288 GTOs believed to exist. The 99th one built out of 272 total. Ferrari Classiche Red Book certified. Full mechanical service in 2024. The seats show very mild signs of use, but this is a very well-preserved car, even by Ferrari standards.


**Bottom line:** This is yet another record for a halo model Ferrari, beating the previous record set earlier this year at Gooding’s Rétromobile sale ($10.9M). Prior to 2026, no 288 GTO had ever crossed the $5M mark at auction, but five have done so over the past eight months.


## Lot 317: 1991 Ferrari F40


Robin Adams/RM Sotheby's


**Sold for $8,365,000**


*Chassis no. ZFFMN34A7M0087345. Rosso Corsa over red cloth. Unrestored original, #1- condition*


**Equipment:** 2036-cc/478-hp twin-turbo V-8, five-speed, centerlock wheels, Scuderia shields, Pirelli P Zero tires, catalytic converters, non-adjustable suspension.


**Condition:** Incredibly low-mileage example displaying just 320 miles at the time of cataloging. The 94th of 213 examples specified for the U.S. market. Purchased new by auto industry executive Lee Iacocca, who reportedly took delivery in Italy and enjoyed the car in Europe briefly before taking it home. Visible looseness in the seat upholstery and mild detail scratching in the paint, but the mileage and ownership history count for a lot here.


**Bottom line:** RM sold this car 14 years ago in Phoenix for $781,000. That seems like another century at this point. As of 2026, Iacocca’s F40 is now the most expensive base-model F40 sold at auction, beating the previous record (from Mecum Kissimmee in January) by nearly $2M and our #1 condition value by 66%.


## Lot 349: 1995 Ferrari F50


RM Sotheby's


**Sold for $12,105,000**


*Chassis no. ZFFTG46A0S0104220. Rosso Corsa over black leather with red cloth inserts.* *Original, #2- condition.*


**Equipment:** 4699-cc/513-hp V-12, six-speed, Continental tires, black calipers, Scuderia shields, books, tools, three-piece luggage.


**Condition:** Minimally driven example currently displaying 6196 miles. Sold new to Mike Tyson. A few mild paint blemishes on the front bumper and nose. Light detail scratching in the paint generally, and on the engine cover. Lovely interior, though the carbon fiber trim is yellowing ever so slightly.


**Bottom line:** Showing only 502 more miles on it today than in 2017, when it sold for “just” $2.64M. Gooding then sold it here in 2022 with 6193 miles showing for $4.625M. The massive growth in prices here is less about Iron Mike’s ownership than it is about the stratospheric rise of Ferrari halo models (288 GTO, F40, F50, Enzo). Remarkably, this wasn’t even the most expensive F50 of the week. Mecum sold a yellow one for a record $14.575M.


## Lot 269: 2003 Ferrari Enzo


Erik Olsen/RM Sotheby's


**Sold for $9,410,000**


*Chassis no. ZFFCW56A930133919. Rosso Corsa over red leather. Original,* *#2- condition.*


**Equipment:** 5999-cc/660-hp V-12, Scuderia shields, black calipers, Bridgestone tires.


**Condition:** One of 498 built and 111 sold in the U.S. Represented with 9834 miles and two Texas owners. Ferrari Classiche Red Book. Major service last month. It looks great, of course, but there are two small scrapes on the very top of the nose. Once you see them, you can’t unsee them.


**Bottom line:** Nine months ago, if an Enzo sold for over $5M it would have been big news, and a $9.41M sale would have broken the previous record by 56%. This year, however, such numbers are the norm, and this was in fact the least expensive of the three Enzos sold in Monterey. Mecum sold one for $12.1M and Broad Arrow sold one for $10.675M. A correction may be due at some point after the head-scratchingly huge growth in this huge part of the market, but it certainly didn’t come in Monterey.


## Lot 347: 2008 Ferrari 430 Scuderia


[Andrew Newton](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/andrew-newton/)


**Sold for $1,352,500**


*Chassis no. ZFFKW64A480161297. Nero with gold stripes over tan leather, black cloth. Original, #2 condition.*


**Equipment:** 4308-cc/503-hp V-8, Novitec exhaust, paddle-shift six-speed, black wheels and calipers, Continental Extreme Contact tires, Scuderia shields, tinted windows.


**Condition:** Represented with 6200 miles. Serviced earlier this year. There are numerous paint cracks ahead of the right front grille. Otherwise, this car doesn’t show any sign of heavy use or hard track driving. These are unusual colors on a Ferrari, but they work.


**Bottom line:** The open version of this track-focused model—the[Scuderia 16M](https://www.hagerty.com/valuation-tools/ferrari/f430/2009/2009-ferrari-f430-scuderia_16m?id=aCn1I000000DA64SAG) —has garnered a lot of attention from market watchers this year by effectively doubling in value from where it was this time in 2025, and with some sales topping $1M. Well, the coupes are right up there as well. A year ago, this car might have sold for 300 or maybe 400 grand. Something serious is going on with modern Ferraris.


## Lot 325: 1989 Porsche 959 Sport


[Adam Wilcox](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/adam-wilcox/)


**Sold for $7,045,000**


*Chassis no. WP0ZZZ95ZJS905013; Engine no. 65H00319. Grand Prix White over gray cloth. Unrestored original,* *#2 condition.*


**Equipment:** 2849-cc/575-hp twin-turbo flat-six, six-speed, body-color wheels, Bridgestone tires, harnesses.


**Condition:** Three owners and driven only 4788 km (~2976 miles) from new. Presented in well-preserved and highly original condition. Delivered new to South Korea and subsequently owned by German Formula 1 driver Nick Heidfeld. Large chip in the right rocker panel. Beautiful interior that still smells new.


**Bottom line:** Porsche’s 1980s technical tour de force, the 959 is a rare car with 337 in total built. Most of those, however, are “Komfort” models with such niceties as air conditioning and a radio. Just 29 were “Sport” models like this one, with lightweight features, more power, a roll cage, and harnesses. Given the disparity in equipment and production, Sport models are worth roughly double the Komforts in our price guide, but they rarely come up for sale. Broad Arrow sold another white one with double the mileage this past March for[$5.5M](https://www.broadarrowauctions.com/vehicles/am26_r0188/1988-porsche-959-sport) , but this result in 2026 is a new record for a 959 of any kind, beating out the previous record—one of the[Paris-Dakar Rally 959s](https://rmsothebys.com/auctions/po18/lots/r0016-1985-porsche-959-parisdakar/) —by more than $1M (or 18%).


## Lot 350: 1968 Chevrolet Corvette L88


RM Sotheby's


**Sold for $747,500**


*Red with blue and white stripe over black. Older restoration, #2- condition.*


**Equipment:** 427-cid L88 V-8, M22 four-speed, side exhaust, air dam.


**Condition:** Built by Toye English’s RED team (who also built the famous Confederate-liveried “Rebel Corvette), raced for NART by Dave Heinz and Bob Johnson at the 1972 24 Hours of Le Mans, finishing 15th overall. Third overall at the 1973 24 Hours of Daytona. Bloomington Gold Hall of Fame inductee 2001, three-time entrant at Amelia Island Concours. Great older restoration to NART livery. Paint is very good, showing some minor light dispersion in the finish. The underbody isn’t perfect, showing some slight oxidation to exposed metal bits, and the interior presents some fading from age, but it is minor. A great restoration on a historic Corvette that’s held up over the years.


**Bottom line:** Displayed next to the Grand Sport at the preview, this historic L88 was almost easy to miss, which is a shame since it has great history and looks badass. The bidder didn’t go nuts for it, though, as this is well under the $900,000–$1,100,000 estimate. Since the 1960 Cunningham-raced Le Mans Corvette also offered here failed to sell, it seems that most sights were set on the Grand Sport and little else.


## Lot 341: 1963 Chevrolet Corvette Grand Sport


Genau AutoWerks Ingo Schmoldt/RM Sotheby's


**Sold for $18,705,000**


*Chassis no. 30837X100003. Blue and white over black. Older restoration, #3 condition.*


**Equipment:** 377-cid V-8, quadruple Webers, four-speed, Halibrand wheels, Goodyear tires, side exhaust, roll cage, Simpson harnesses.


**Condition:** One of five built and the first of three coupes. History at Sebring, Bahamas Speed Week, and in the SCCA. Driven in period by AJ Foyt, Jim Hall, and Dick Thompson. Brought back to its 1964 Sebring appearance in the 2000s. Restoration costs reportedly approached $1M, but today the car shows chopped, cracked, detail-scratched paint. Aged wheels, and the tires look ancient. Lightly scratched rear window. Clean interior. If this were a normal Sting Ray race car, it wouldn’t be anything special. But being a Grand Sport, this is one of the most important Corvettes ever made and now the most expensive one.


Genau AutoWerks Ingo Schmoldt/RM Sotheby's


**Bottom line:** Built to race against and beat Shelby’s Cobra in top-level sports car racing, the Grand Sport featured a new lightweight chassis, extensive use of alloy components throughout, disc brakes (production Corvettes used drums until ’65), different suspension, thinner fiberglass body panels, and Plexiglas windows for a weight savings of well over 1000 pounds compared to a standard Corvette street car.


Although the program showed great promise, a factory-built and run race car flew in the face of GM corporate policy, and the Grand Sport was axed before it could reach its potential and truly take the fight to Shelby. It’s nevertheless one of the most mythical Corvettes of all, and the last time a Grand Sport showed up to an auction was way back in 2009, when one of the roadsters hit a $4.9M high bid but went home unsold. This one blew past its $13M high estimate and indeed is now comfortably the most expensive Corvette ever sold at auction.


## Lot 328: 1996 McLaren F1 GTR


[Andrew Newton](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/andrew-newton/)


**Sold for $34,655,000**


*Chassis no. 10R. Blood Red with yellow-over-black graphics. Visually maintained, largely original, #2 condition.*


**Equipment:** 6064-cc/627-hp BMW V-12, six-speed, yellow OZ Racing wheels, Michelin Pilot Sport 3 tires, wing.


**Condition:** One of only two McLaren F1 GTR Short-Tail prototypes along with the 1995 24 Hour Le Mans winner chassis 01R. Used for race testing but not in competition. Retained by the factory and converted for road use. Sold to Pink Floyd drummer and noted collector Nick Mason.[Crashed](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=w7fynkNGZiM) at Goodwood and sustained significant front-end damage but refurbished at the factory to like-new condition. Unique and recognizable paint, desirable specs, and excellent condition. Along with the Shelby Cobra Daytona Coupe at Gooding Christie’s, this is the most anticipated car of the week.


Alex Penfold/RM Sotheby's


**Bottom line:** It was also anticipated to be the most expensive, with a presale estimate of “in excess of $35,000,000.” Indeed, with all the fanfare around modern exotics this year, the estimate made sense. The F1 is the king of modern exotics in terms of collectability. This one also has rare, racy specs. And no F1 has come up for auction yet this year. In the end, though, the crash history may have held the car back a bit. Other F1s are rumored to have sold for more privately, but the so-called “Pop Art F1” now holds the auction record for the model and for British cars overall, even if it brought a bit less than expected.
