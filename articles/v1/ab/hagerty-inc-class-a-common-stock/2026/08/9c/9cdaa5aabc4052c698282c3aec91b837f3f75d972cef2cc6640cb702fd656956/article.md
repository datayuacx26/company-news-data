# 7 Fun Cars for Hauling Around the Kids

Nothing alters your life quite like introducing children into it—whether they’re your own or somebody else’s. And nothing quite alters a child’s life quite like introducing them to fun cars—whether they’re your own or somebody else’s. Some cars are just more kid friendly than others, however, especially today, when simply shoving a child behind the seats of your Austin-Healey 100 isn’t really the charming solution it was 60 years ago. This week, we asked Hagerty Media staffers to name the cars they thought offered the right blend of fun and practicality (however you define it) to not only haul kids around, but to plant the seeds of automotive fandom at the same time. What would you add to the list?


## Cram Yourself in There, Kid!


Ford


I’d like to say any basic two-door sedan (not coupe) from the 1940s to ’60s so more people can understand what we have gained/lost as a society, but classic cars that old don’t have the right balance of “fun” and “safe” in modern times.


I am constantly surrounded by 5000-plus-pound vehicles driven aggressively in my neighborhood, so I’d need something with a modicum of safety for kid duty. To that end, I’m going to go with a late-model Mustang GT. It forces kids into small spaces, just like we used to do with two-door sedans, but it’s also pretty darn safe and a lot of fun. I reckon if more kids were forced to flex into the back of a coupe, we’d have less need for three-row CUVs.— *Sajeev Mehta*


## Top Down


[Eddy Eckart](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/eeckarthagerty-com/)


Gimme a big ol’ convertible. Nothing says special, especially among today’s high-riding traffic, like a long, sleek, burbly droptop. Sure, it’s not for everyday use, but you can easily cram a kid, a significant other, and a ton of stuff into[a full-size convertible](https://www.hagerty.com/media/car-profiles/beloved-buick-the-centurion-rides-again/) and make an occasion out of whatever you’re doing. I’d go GM B-body with a big-block, but I’m biased. Anything from about 1960 to 1975 will do just fine.— *Eddy Eckart*


## The Way Way Back


Marketplace/Craig Zimmerman


You can’t go wrong with an old wagon with a rear-facing jump seat in the cargo area. I was surprised to learn that mid-90s Camry wagons had them, as did the Taurus/Sable, and maybe even Chevy Celebritys (Celebrities?). I always loved that feature in the Volvo 245s I owned, though I was a kidless adult at the time and never actually rode back there. But I did spend some time in the way back of a couple 1970s monstrosities and always thought that was special. Like some entirely separate area carved out just for us kids.


For the right mix of throwback size and Dad-pleasing oomph, my pick would be a 1994–96 Buick Roadmaster with the LT1 engine. That’s a fine line, however, because rear-facing seats + kids stuffed with junk food + oomph = messy gross cleanup.— *Stefan Lombard*


## Any 4×4


Ford


Anything that can go off-road. My son absolutely loved our black 4×4 1986 Ford Bronco II—I’m not talking about barreling through the mud, more like just picking our way through trails in the Ozark Mountains, where my parents lived, enjoying sights you can’t see from the pavement. I also took my dad for his first ride in a four-wheel-drive vehicle, and after he got over his initial concern that a vehicle that travels on the road should not be able to climb a pretty substantial hill, he had a blast. Yeah, something with all four wheels powered, that’ll entertain any kid. And most adults.— *Steven Cole Smith*


## This 4×4


Toyota


I’ll speak specifically to a Northern Michigan setting here. I’d love to get an older 4×4 SUV for this scenario. But not too old. I think an[80-Series](https://www.hagerty.com/media/market-trends/why-the-80-series-land-cruiser-is-the-most-notable-4x4-of-the-90s/) Toyota Land Cruiser would strike a nice balance between simple enough to teach them maintenance on, fun enough to go have a few adventures and make some memories, spacious enough to not worry about fitting car seats and the associated infrastructure you haul around these days, and slow enough that you have to be mindful of your driving decisions so as not to put the gang at risk unnecessarily. I’d love one in green or silver.— *Nathan Petroelje*


## And This One, Too


Toyota


I’ll follow the theme that Nate and SCS set and go with a first-gen Toyota 4Runner. Not only are they durable and affordable, they’ve got two rows of seats, a removable fiberglass hardtop, and the option of rad ’80s graphics. When considering a kid-friendly classic, I’d want something multi-purpose, and this SUV-pickup-convertible would be the perfect combo of on- and off-roadable fun.— *Grace Jarvis*


## The Sport Compact of Our Time


Chris Stark


After some seat time in Eddy’s, my vote here is for a new Acura Integra[Type S](https://www.hagerty.com/media/car-profiles/for-me-acuras-integra-type-s-makes-the-case-for-embracing-evolution/) . What a fun car to drive, yet it is still refined enough to be tolerable during a school pickup line or medium road trip to go visit the grandparents. It’s quick enough to put a smile on my face and introduce a young mind to the delight that is being pushed back in the seat—or into the side bolsters through a corner.— *Kyle Smith*
