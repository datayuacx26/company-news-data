# 911 Carrera S MT Package Finally Brings Back the Manual

When it launched the updated 992.2-generation model for the 2025 model year, Porsche made the 911 Carrera and[911 Carrera S](https://www.hagerty.com/media/news/porsche-portfolio-expands-with-2025-911-carrera-s/) available only with[automatic transmissions](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_BH-N8t_1Fc) . While the base car remains only available with two pedals, the new MT Package brings the stick back to the S trim.


Offering a three-pedal option might sound like a step back in an era when the number of new manual cars is low and getting lower. And yet, Porsche tells us an overwhelming demand from customers convinced it to bring back the manual after a short hiatus. The gearbox is not a manual with seven speeds, however; the MT Package replaces the eight-speed automatic with[the Carrera T](https://www.hagerty.com/media/news/2025-porsche-911-carrera-t-specs-features-pricing/) ‘s six-speed manual.


There are no major mechanical changes to report, so power for the MT Package–equipped Carrera S comes from a twin-turbo, 3.0-liter flat-six rated at 473 horsepower and 390 pound-feet of torque. Porsche tuned the exhaust note specifically for the MT. Rear-wheel-drive is the only configuration available, so you’ll need to settle for the automatic if you’re after the all-wheel-drive 4S.

Offered on both the coupe and the convertible, the Carrera S MT Package doesn’t include any visual differentiation from the Carrera S when viewed from the outside, with the exception of a red, rather than black, “S” emblem on the back end. Cars with the MT Package benefit from a longer list of standard equipment, however, including rear-wheel steering, weight-saving center-locking wheels (available on a Carrera S for the first time), and the SportDesign front end. Inside, MT Package cars feature 14-way power-adjustable sport seats, a heated GT sport steering wheel and a shift knob made with real walnut wood.


So, is this just a Carrera T with a different emblem on the back? Not quite. It’s more thoroughly equipped (though, at 3327 pounds, only 11 pounds heavier) and it’s considerably more powerful (the T’s six is rated at 388 horsepower). It’s also more expensive.


Pricing for the 2027 Porsche 911 Carrera S MT Package starts at $169,450 for the coupe and $183,350 for the Cabriolet, including a $2350 destination charge. Deliveries will start by the end of 2026. For context, the base Carrera S costs $158,550 as a coupe and $172,450 as a Cabriolet, meaning the MT package represents about a 5% discount from a comparably equipped PDK model. (It’s also worth noting that some of these options aren’t available on the PDK cars.) If you’re in the market for a T, plan on spending $150,350 on a coupe and $164,250 on a Cabriolet.


It sounds like the vast majority of the Porsche customers who want to row their own gears live in North America. The MT Package is only available on the North American market, so even buyers in Porsche’s home country of Germany can’t get it. That’s hardly a surprise: Broadly speaking, Europeans tend to see the stick-shift as an anachronism, and not a very exotic one.
