# Piston Slap: Justy-fied Service for the Spark’s CVT?

Hagerty Media reader *RobH* writes:


Hi Sajeev,


I have a 2022 Chevrolet Spark 1LT with the CVT transmission. I purchased this car as a second, emergency-type vehicle when I retired from a full career of company-supplied vehicles. After deciding on a sub-$15,000 (CAD) budget and then seeing the sad state of 2006–10 Hondas in this price category, I happened upon this Spark at a GM store where I knew the Cadillac sales manager. I decided a well-kept Spark with remaining factory warranty might be a better spend.


Full disclosure: I was a manager at a Saturn store when Saturn used a CVT in the four-cylinder Vue. I wasn’t impressed, and neither were customers. Soon after, Saturn switched to a gear-driven four-speed automatic. Then, along with grenading CVTs in Nissans (and others), I have been leery of this design. Well, until now.


So here’s my Piston Slap question:


*What special needs do CVTs require, at what intervals, and should this service be completed more often?*


I’d like to keep this car for a long time and want to be certain I do what is required for the CVT. By the way, this awesome little Spark has moved from emergency-type usage to my daily to the golf club. It is a hoot and has all the features I need: Apple CarPlay, a great stereo, cold A/C, and it fits in ALL the parking spaces. It looks hilarious next to the Cayennes, ML500s, and Audi R8 and McLaren supercars, too!


*Sajeev* answers:


The *Piston Slap* series has irreverently discussed CVT gearboxes[since 2009](https://www.thetruthaboutcars.com/2009/07/piston-slap-justy-fied-freestylin%e2%80%99-over-cvts/) , hence the title that references the obscure Subaru Justy. CVTs in society have really changed since then: They are more prevalent, being force-fed turbocharged or hybridized power, to the point that fluid services need to happen almost as frequently as synthetic engine oil changes.


I’d like to think I’m smarter and savvier in the year 2026, and one thing is becoming clearer: Automakers care less and less about your long-term happiness with their product. We see it on a regular basis through the lens of[Platform Decay](https://www.hagerty.com/media/advice/piston-slap/piston-slap-platform-decay-brain-rot-and-diy-auto-repair/) , a recurring revenue model that is brilliantly personified in the scam of the lifetime fluid fallacy. But we’ve gone a little too high-level in this discussion, so let’s get back down to earth.


Chevrolet


Berkley Standard


If I Googled correctly, your Spark has the Jatco JF015E transmission. This CVT is used in several low-displacement, low-power applications that are commonplace in Southeast Asia. Doing more Googling for those cars suggests yours should get a service every 30,000–60,000 miles. Drilling down further, a seemingly reputable CVT rebuilder recommends inspecting the fluid every 30,000 miles. Their product’s warranty is void if there isn’t[proof of servicing every 60,000 miles](https://berkeleystandard.com/news/jf015e/#:~:text=NOTE%3A%20The%20fluid%20change%20interval%20for%20the%20JF015E%20CVT%20is%20every%2060%2C000%20miles.%20If%20the%20vehicle%20has%20been%20driven%20over%2060%2C000%20miles%20on%20a%20Berkeley%20Standard%20CVT%2C%20maintenance%20records%20showing%20proof%20of%20fluid%20change(s)%20at%2060%2C000%20mile%20or%20earlier%20intervals%20must%20be%20provided%20in%20order%20to%20ensure%20warranty%20coverage.) : That’s all I need to make up my mind.


Rob—do a CVT service within the parameters of 50,000–100,000 kilometers, but don’t be afraid to be on the more proactive side of those guidelines. Transmission Digest[wrote a comprehensive story](https://www.transmissiondigest.com/the-new-jatco-jf015e-when-is-a-cvt-not-a-cvt/) on the Jatco’s design and the two filters that need replacement. Rock Auto sells both filters for about $9 each, and fluid is between $9 and $20 per quart. Lucky you, these Jatcos do not have[a filter plasti-welded](https://www.hagerty.com/media/advice/piston-slap/piston-slap-maintaining-the-zf-8-speed/#:~:text=an%20aftermarket%20oil%20pan%20for%202015%2B%20Mopars%20with%20a%20replaceable%20filter%20as%20part%20of%20its%20improved%20design.) to a needlessly expensive plastic oil pan.


There’s just something so refreshingly honest about these Sparks (and other cars sold in Southeast Asia), as they seem immune to the Platform Decay we experience with so many vehicles in North America. So do this car a solid, and you will keep its gearbox alive for many years to come.


Heck, even[Nissan dealerships have the gall](https://www.glendalenissanla.com/blog/how-often-should-you-replace-your-nissans-cvt-fluid/#:~:text=If%20you%20drive%20in%20places%20like%20Sierra%20Madre%20or%20Pasadena%2C%20you%27re%20likely%20facing%20stop%2Dand%2Dgo%20traffic%2C%20hills%2C%20and%20heat.%20These%20things%20all%20put%20more%20stress%20on%20your%20transmission.%20That%20means%20you%20may%20need%20to%20replace%20your%20CVT%20fluid%20closer%20to%20the%2030%2C000%2Dmile%20mark.) to openly recommend a CVT service these days, so maybe things are looking up!
