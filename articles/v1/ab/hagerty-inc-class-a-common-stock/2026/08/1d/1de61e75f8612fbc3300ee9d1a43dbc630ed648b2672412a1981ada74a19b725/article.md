# The Next Mini Cooper Could Be Rear-Wheel Drive

The city-friendly Mini has changed a lot since the original model made its debut in 1959: It has gradually gotten bigger, quicker, and headed more upmarket. It has never strayed from its pioneering front-wheel-drive roots, however, but that could change in the next couple of years. The next Mini Cooper might share a rear-wheel-drive platform with BMW models, according to a recent report.


Currently found under[the i3](https://www.hagerty.com/media/news/2027-bmw-i3-3-series-specs-pictures-performance/) and[the iX3](https://www.hagerty.com/media/news/bmws-ambitious-ix3-packs-an-unusual-all-electric-driveline/) , the Gen6 architecture was developed for rear-wheel drive and rear-biased all-wheel-drive applications. The all-wheel-drive layout puts an electric motor over each axle, but making it front-wheel drive isn’t as simple as sending the rear motor back to the parts bin. BMW told *[Autocar](https://www.autocar.co.uk/car-news/electric-cars/next-mini-cooper-be-inspired-2001-r50-%E2%80%93-and-could-go-rwd)* that it’s not planning on developing a front-wheel-drive layout.


And yet, the British publication reports that the next Mini Cooper will ride on the highly modular Gen6 platform in a bid to keep development costs in check. Going rear-wheel drive would give the next Mini drastically different handling than the current car.


Interestingly, the gasoline-powered version of the next-gen Mini could remain front-wheel-drive. BMW’s Gen6 architecture is EV-only, so the ICE version (which is expected to stick around through the 2030s) will need to ride on a different platform. While this is pure speculation, it could follow the path blazed by[the next-generation 1 Series](https://www.autocar.co.uk/car-news/electric-cars/bmw-1-series-return-rwd-new-322bhp-ev) : Front-wheel drive for the ICE variant, which will ride on an evolution of the current car’s UKL2 platform, and rear-wheel drive for the EV, which will use the Gen6 platform.

Regardless of drivetrain configuration, the next Mini Cooper will still look like a Mini. “You can ask a Brit or a German or an Italian, you can ask a five-year-old, and they will recognize a Mini based on the proportions of the car,” Mini design boss Holger Hampf said in an interview with *Autocar* . “Every five-year-old will know this is a Mini. It’s my job to treat it with respect.”


What does a Mini look like, though? It largely depends on who you ask. Someone born in, say, 1950 might point to the original model from 1959. Someone born in 2000 will likely reference the retro-styled BMW reboot released for 2002. Hampf suggested that[the first new Mini of the 21st century](https://www.hagerty.com/media/automotive-history/the-bmw-era-mini-cooper-turned-25-this-week/) (which was called R50 internally; pictured) will influence the next-gen Cooper.


“Many people out there don’t actually have a recollection of the classic Mini, but they do have a recollection of one of the Minis we have done in BMW’s ownership in the past 25 years. I will make sure to connect to this past,” he revealed. As for the size, it sounds like nothing is set in stone. “The size discussion about Mini growing smaller, bigger and so on, we have that every day.”


The next Mini Cooper isn’t due out until the end of the decade at the earliest. Executives still have time to decide whether it will remain front-wheel drive or if it will send its power to the rear wheels, what it will look like, and whether it will be a mini Mini.
