# Report: Four-Door Ford Mustang Confirmed

Ford stopped offering sedans when it discontinued the Fusion in 2020. While the company remains heavily focused on SUVs and trucks, the four-door Ford Mustang that’s being discussed behind closed doors would be a splashy way for the Blue Oval to return to the sedan segment.


We’ve been[hearing rumors](https://www.hagerty.com/media/news/ford-mustang-sedan-trademark/) about a four-door Mustang[for nearly a decade](https://www.hagerty.com/media/news/four-door-mustang-turbo-v-8-rumors/) , but they’ve never materialized. They’re more credible than they’ve ever been, however, and anonymous sources who saw the sedan during a closed-door dealer meeting in Las Vegas in August 2026 unexpectedly drew several parallels to[the Porsche Panamera](https://www.hagerty.com/media/new-car-reviews/2024-porsche-panamera-review-cayenne-who/) . Attendees told industry trade journal *[Automotive News](https://www.autonews.com/ford/an-ford-hybrid-mustang-4-door-0807/)* that the sedan, which could wear the Mach-4 name when it debuts, is similar in size and design characteristics to the four-door Porsche.


This is pure speculation since we haven’t seen the car, but the Panamera references suggest that the four-door Mustang will wear a sporty, fastback-like silhouette. It may even attract the contentious “four-door coupe” label. It certainly won’t pick up where[the Taurus](https://www.hagerty.com/media/news/american-ford-taurus-production-ends/) left off. This might be the key to its success: Providing[the Mustang’s styling and performance](https://www.hagerty.com/media/new-car-reviews/2024-ford-mustang-dark-horse-review-proven-ingredients-new-recipe/) in a more spacious package.


Dealer sources revealed that executives promised to deliver a car capable of hitting 60 mph from a stop in less than four seconds, with more rear-seat space than the Panamera, and with a base price of under $40,000. We won’t have to wait long to find out whether the rumors are accurate this time around, as dealer sources suggest Ford will introduce the sedan before the end of 2027.


Looking beyond the four-door Ford Mustang, the product pipeline also includes an electric, mid-size truck[called Fathom](https://www.hagerty.com/media/news/getting-to-the-bottom-of-fords-28350-fathom/) that will start at $28,350 and a new, entry-level SUV that will be positioned at the bottom of the company’s portfolio. Ford has already confirmed that the Fathom is on its way, but news of the SUV comes from the same dealer meeting that was held in Las Vegas.


Anonymous sources who saw the prototype during the convention told *Automotive News* that the model will carry a base price of about $25,000. For context, the $29,000 Maverick currently stands out as the most affordable Ford model. The SUV’s name hasn’t been revealed yet, but dealers reported that it’s “boxy and wide” and that it’s reminiscent of the original Escape. The range will include hybrid and non-electrified variants, and sales are scheduled to start in 2029, so it may land as a 2030 model.


Ford is also planning more electric vehicles built on the Fathom’s platform and additional new models with a sub-$40,000 base price, according to separate reports. When asked for comment, a company spokesperson told *Hagerty* that it cannot comment or speculate on future products.
