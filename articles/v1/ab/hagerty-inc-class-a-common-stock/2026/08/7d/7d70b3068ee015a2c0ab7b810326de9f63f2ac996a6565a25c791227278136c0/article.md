# What’s the Best First Classic? A 1963 Plymouth Valiant Makes Its Case

We’ve been talking a lot about expensive cars over the[last few weeks](https://www.hagerty.com/media/tags/monterey-car-week/) , and about how these expensive cars are getting even more expensive. But one of the best things about this hobby is that you can have a fun, interesting car on almost any budget, including the nearly non-existent kind. Case in point: the 1963 Plymouth Valiant V200 that sold on Hagerty Marketplace last week for[$6500](https://www.hagerty.com/marketplace/auction/1963-Plymouth-Valiant/d3597b7c-6243-44b3-80ad-1a1eea3059af) , including buyer’s premium. An entry-level classic if ever there was one.


Hagerty Marketplace/PlymouthValiant


Plymouth first introduced the Valiant in 1960, and it was a polarizing design with baroque swirls, curves, and fins, but underneath was a solid compact-class car to compete with the likes of Rambler and the Ford Falcon. The 1963 model year got a major refresh with conservative but clean, squared-up looks.


The V100 was the base car, while the[Valiant V200](https://www.hagerty.com/valuation-tools/search?q=1963+plymouth+valiant+v200) came with more options, and the V200 Signet was the top of the line. All came with Chrysler’s enduring slant-six engines (a 273-cid V-8 arrived for 1964), and body styles included two- and four-door sedans, station wagons, and convertibles. The[1964 Barracuda](https://www.hagerty.com/valuation-tools/plymouth/barracuda/1964/1964-plymouth-barracuda-base?id=aCn1I000000DOIHSA4) was largely based on the Valiant, and though the Plymouth compact isn’t exactly a household name today, it soldiered on until 1976, when it was replaced by the F-body Volaré.


Hagerty Marketplace/PlymouthValiant


Hagerty Marketplace/PlymouthValiant


In classic-car terms, the Valiant we’re looking at here is just about everything that collectors *don’t* want. It’s a boring color with an even more boring name—Medium Beige. It’s not a sleek coupe or a sunny-day convertible but an everyman sedan. It has a six-cylinder engine, an automatic transmission, bench seats, and steel wheels. Some might yawn at that.


But here’s the thing: What collectors want isn’t the be-all and end-all of what’s cool or what’s fun. For $6500, this thing is chock-full of cool mid-century details and styling cues. Chrysler’s push-button TorqueFlite automatic is a fascinating piece of car history as well as fun to use. And, hey, some people like beige.


Within the last decade or so, the Valiant has also had a carburetor rebuild as well as a new radiator, water pump, heater core, thermostat, brake master cylinder, spark plugs, ignition wires, and starter. Which means it should be usable enough for the time being, and when it does break, it’s a simple enough car that’s easy to work on for those of even modest mechanical ability. So, not only is it downright cheap, but it would also make a great first classic for somebody. We love it for that.
