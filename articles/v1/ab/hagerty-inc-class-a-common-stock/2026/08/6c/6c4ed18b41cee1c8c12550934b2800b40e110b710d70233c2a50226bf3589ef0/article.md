# The Quail Honors Route 66’s Centennial

[The Quail by The Peninsula, A Motorsports Gathering](https://www.hagerty.com/media/tags/the-quail/) , is one of the most exclusive shows of Monterey Car Week. The gathering celebrates the engineering and design of the finest automobiles, which often means those built in limited numbers and sold to a select few. Exotic cars often receive top billing. The Quail also embraces designs and features that have a lasting impact on the automotive landscape. In 2026, the show honored the 100th Anniversary of Route 66 by creating a winding path that simulates America’s most famous highway, stretching from Chicago to Santa Monica.

The models selected to highlight Route 66’s centennial are a great cross-section of the kinds of cars and trucks you’d find on a road trip, with most cars hailing from the ’40s, ’50, ’60s, or ’70s. The selections were heavily stacked in favor of the Big Three, with Corvettes making the most appearances of any model. There were signposts at the Quail highlighting the states along the route, a model of the Gateway Arch, and artwork that seemed to evoke both Cadillac Ranch and Twin Arrows at once.

Fine examples of Tri-Five Chevys, Mopar muscle, and a ’32 Ford hot rod were curated to give the Route 66 display a major dose of Route 66 Americana. They also curated a Tucker and a Czinger to highlight some of the ingenuity that has sprung from America’s smaller automakers.


Jacob Gilbert (left) and Markku Jaakkola planned to cruise the New Yorker for the remainder of Monterey Car Week.


[Brandan Gillogly](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/brandan-gillogly/)


Parked prominently near the midway point of the Route 66 display was Markku Jaakkola’s 1973 Chrysler New Yorker. The car, along with Markku and his co-driver Jacob Gilbert, had recently driven from near their homes in Detroit to Chicago and on to Santa Monica on as much of what remains of Route 66 as possible.[We followed along for part of the drive](https://www.hagerty.com/media/automobilia/route-66-in-a-new-yorker-nine-days-eight-states-and-two-transmissions/) , and even kept up with the 440-powered Mopar as they turned north and drove the Pacific Coast Highway to Monterey Car Week. We asked the duo their thoughts on the epic drive. “We are here!” Markku said proudly. “Of course, we had some setbacks there on the way,” he continued, referring to the transmission swap performed in a driveway that Jacob performed midway through their trip. “But that was nothing,” Jacob added, as his experience with a wrench meant the two delayed their westward progress by hours and not days. The minor mechanical setback didn’t dampen the overall experience. “It was beyond any expectations,” Jacob added, “beyond belief.”


[Brandan Gillogly](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/brandan-gillogly/)


Jacob had driven most of Route 66 before, but never all in one go. “To see it all in one shot. . . you see all the beauty change from the desert to the high pines just in Arizona alone,” said Jacob, “just so many contrasts along the route.” Considering all the famous locations along the vast route, we asked the two of them if they had any advice on how to plan such a drive. “There’s a lot of stops along the way that personally, I never would have taken, but it was nice to slow down and to do them,” Jacob said, specifically calling out Petrified Forest National Park. “It depends on what you want to see,” added Markku. “I guess you would spend about two months if you wanted to stop everywhere.” Even after all the fun the two had on Route 66, they appreciate that it’s a huge undertaking that’s not for everyone. “Just get out and drive and enjoy the road and enjoy the public,” Jacob said. “It doesn’t have to be Route 66. . . just take the time to get out and do what you’re passionate about.” Especially, Marrku added, “in your classic car.”
