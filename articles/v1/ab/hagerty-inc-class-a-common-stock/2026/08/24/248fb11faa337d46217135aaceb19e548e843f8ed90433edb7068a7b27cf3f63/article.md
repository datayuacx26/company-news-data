# For Summertime Cruising, Few Cars Top My ’73 Riviera

This 1973 Buick Riviera GS is the beautiful queen of my driveway.


My dad owned it before me, but before that, all I know of its history comes from a faded pink bill of sale I found in the glovebox. It is from Ken Logan Buick of Iowa and dated 9/11/73, and it appears to be a sales invoice for two vehicles totaling $8,148.65. My dad doesn’t remember a lot of purchase-day details, but his story of acquiring the Riv in the late ’80s or early ’90s has one fun anecdote: He said he was driving it back home to Rockford, Illinois, with my younger brothers following him across Iowa, and that my brothers had trouble keeping up in their ’88 Mustang GT. I guess he had to “see what she can do” or “blow out the carbon” on the highway across those endless cornfields.


Ken Maupin


Shortly after he got it, Dad had the factory vinyl top removed when he had the Riv repainted in its current white, which is just a bit whiter than the ivory coat it wore originally. Considering she has no clearcoat, the car still shines up nicely with hand-applied Meguiar’s carnuba.


Dad owned a series of 10 Rivieras throughout my childhood—at least a few at a time, and a few of every year from ’66 through ’70 (plus[this ’73](https://www.hagerty.com/valuation-tools/buick/riviera/1973/1973-buick-riviera-gs?id=aCn1I000000DGfhSAG) ). He would acquire them rather impulsively and store them about town in rented parking spaces under apartment buildings and such, often without Mom knowing about them. He’d have the insurance and registration paperwork mailed to his dry-cleaning business, where he could catch them. One day, though, he made the mistake of using the home address on one of them and Mom saw it at home. She warned him that he’d better come clean with all of them right then and there, since we’d never know where they were if he died (there may have been another thinly veiled threat in there as well).


Ken Maupin


Overall, Mom was a pretty good sport about his car problem, and we all believe she liked to be seen in nice cars, though she’d refuse to admit it at the risk of being his “enabler.” As I write this, it occurs to me that he probably used this very situation to get her to go along with making their home’s 2.5-car garage into a “7.5-car” garage by knocking the back down and building it way, way out.


My job, prior to getting my driver’s license, was to start the Rivs periodically and keep them washed and waxed, and I only fell more in love with them by touching their curves and polishing their chrome.


This ’73 Riviera GS then ended up being the last Rivera he owned. In the 2010s, as he neared retirement and had sold off various cars to help his kids with college or their life-needs, he ended up with only this one. It was the remaining token of his booming Riviera years, and so it was extra special to me, too.


I had told him once that if he decided to sell this last one, I wanted dibs. Of course, when that day came, it was a bad time for me, right in the middle of helping my own kids through college, but we cut a payment deal, and he signed over the title trusting I wouldn’t skip town with it before payoff…

A Google search says they only made 3933 GS models in 1973, out of 34,080 Rivieras that year. I’m not sure how many of those 3933 got the center console and the power factory sunroof (installed by American Sunroof Co., subsidiary of GM), but I think those surely add to this car’s intrinsic worth and sheer desirability for cruising. To me, the big, striking body outside says luxury coupe while the console shift and 455 V-8 say youthful power, so it’s like I’m driving luxury muscle.


Dad had driven the car very little in the years before I got it, so it had needs before it could be a safe[sunny-weekend cruiser](https://www.hagerty.com/media/car-profiles/beloved-buick-the-centurion-rides-again/) . I started by getting the odo and fuel gauge fixed, so that I could at least tell when the 455 had drunk all of the 93 non-ethanol. I was thrilled to learn that even back in ’73, luxury features included a low fuel light. It goes on and off *a lot* as fuel sloshes in the tank when you near empty.


Dad and I love cars, but we aren’t mechanics, so we have to locate those rare trustworthy local shops or guys somebody knows who are reasonably priced and do good work. Fortunately, I have been blessed with a dear, gifted friend who is happy for the hangout time while he does the actual fixing, and I just buy the replacement thing and then hand down wrenches or hold the flashlight.


We are, however, the kind of enthusiasts who like to drive our cars. We keep them running, keep them washed and waxed, and grace the townspeople with them on errand day, when we catch breakfast with some pals, or when driving the missus to an early dinner.


Ken Maupin


So, she’s a survivor. Other than Dad nixing the vinyl roof and repainting, I added Pertronix ignition and Flowmaster Delta 50 mufflers on stainless steel dual pipes with chrome outlets, since the originals were rotted out. This makes the Riv more cruise-able, and it just sounds great, of course.


But each season there is something to repair or replace. The huge doors had chewed half-way through those big pins in the back of the door jamb, so they needed shimming and new door handles. But, hey, the vacuum-operated cruise control works (even the dash indicator), as does the vacuum-operated trunk release in the glove box. The AM/FM radio works—not that I listen to it instead of the Flowmasters—and so does the fan under the huge rear window tray to keep the glass clear.


Dad doesn’t know when the A/C stopped working or when the power sunroof came off the chain—a very expensive fix—but it manually slides open and closed, so since I only drive in the sun, I just slide it back with my hands and go. Midsummer cruises are pretty warm when you aren’t moving—that 455 makes a few BTUs—but most of the time, with the long door windows down, no mid-pillars, and the roof open, the “5-window A/C” is fine for cruising. It’s also an optimal configuration to acknowledge the honks, thumbs-up, and hollers of appreciation for the rolling artwork she is in this world of transportation blobs.


Ken Maupin


A few years ago, we went through a minor identity crisis when I installed BFG Radial T/As in place of the old white walls. She looked more sporty, but my own gut told me that the factory rally wheels, GS letters, console, and the 455 say just enough, and that the T/As were… yelling. Now back on whitewalls, we are all quite content.


If my ship comes in some day, I’ll attend to the sunroof, A/C, and some other minor issues, get a $10K paintjob, and have the rally wheels re-chromed. But any work would have to take place in the winter, because I can’t give up any of those precious three-season driving days in my beautiful Riv.
