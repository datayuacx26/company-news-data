# Red, White, and Blue-Chip: 7 Significant American Cars Selling in Monterey

Every year, the Monterey auctions assemble the most expensive consignment lists of any public sale anywhere in the world. Many of these princely automobiles date from the 1930s, 1950s, and 1960s, and many of them are from the other side of the Atlantic. Of the 10 most expensive cars ever sold at the Monterey auctions, almost all of them are either Italian or English. Just one hails from the U.S. of A.


In 2026, though, we can’t help but notice that there are some serious heavy hitters from our shores that are crossing the block. Historically significant, interesting, rare stuff: Duesenbergs, Cobras, L88s, V-12 Packards, etc. Below are our favorites.


## [1968 Chevrolet Corvette L88 RED/NART Le Mans](https://rmsothebys.com/auctions/mo26/lots/r0018-1968-chevrolet-corvette-l88-rednart-le-mans/)


RM Sotheby's


**RM Sotheby’s, Lot 350**


Corvettes have a long history of racing in Europe, and even though success was mixed in the early years, this car was one of the highlights. Its story starts with a Florida team called Race Engineering & Development (RED), which had great success in U.S. sports car racing with an L88-powered 1969 model, painted in Confederate flag livery and dubbed the “Rebel Corvette.” The RED team soon set their sights on Le Mans, but the French officials rejected their application.


Undeterred, they were able to get a hold of Luigi Chinetti, owner of the North American Racing Team (NART). A Le Mans veteran and winner, Chinetti happened to have four entries for the 1972 Le Mans race, and he agreed to give the Corvette boys his reserve spot, on the condition that their Chevrolet wear the same livery as Chinetti’s Ferraris.


The RED team built up a new car for the race, sourcing a wrecked ’68 Corvette and installing L88 components. Le Mans would be the car’s very first race, and in practice it sustained front-end damage. The team repaired it with a combination of aluminum, pieces of a wooden shipping crate, and duct tape. It started the race in 51st place, fifth from last, and there were fuel issues as well as significant rain, but drivers Dave Heinz and Bob Johnson made it up to 15th place overall and first in class by the checkered flag, becoming only the fourth Corvette up to that point to finish the 24-hour test of endurance.


After returning to the U.S., the car got its own Confederate flag livery and was campaigned with the original Rebel Corvette in the 1972 season. In 1973, it finished third at the 24 Hours of Daytona behind a 911 Carrera RSR and Ferrari 365 GTB/4. It ran as high as second overall at Sebring but retired, and continued to race in 1973 and ’74. It was restored in the ’90s back to its original Le Mans appearance, and has a $900,000–$1,100,000 estimate for Monterey.


## [1913 Mercer Model 35-J Raceabout](https://rmsothebys.com/auctions/mo26/lots/s0003-1913-mercer-model-35j-raceabout/)


Darin Schnabel/RM Sotheby's


Darin Schnabel/RM Sotheby's


**RM Sotheby’s, Lot 127**


In 1913, the Corvette wouldn’t come out for another 40 years. America already had a sports car, though, and that was the Mercer Raceabout. Founded in 1909 in Trenton, New Jersey (which is in Mercer County), the Mercer Automobile Company was led by the Roebling family (of Brooklyn Bridge fame) and completed its first cars in 1910.


The Raceabout is Mercer’s most famous creation. At 4.9 liters, its T-head four-cylinder was actually somewhat small compared to other performance cars of the day, but it made a respectable 55 horsepower and the roadster it powered didn’t weigh much at all, thanks to that rollerskate of a body and low-slung chassis. Each Raceabout came with a guarantee of 70 mph, and modified cars could hit 100. Most cars on the road back then struggled to go 45.


With a price tag of about 2500 bucks back when median household income was roughly 700, the Raceabout’s target audience was wealthy sportsmen, and many of them went racing. The one on offer in Monterey is a 1913 model, and thus comes with the sought-after four-speed (up from three). According to RM Sotheby’s, its known history goes back to 1939, when a collector found it in a storage garage and paid just $62.50 for it. In 2026, however, you’ll need $2.75M to $3.5M, according to the presale estimate.


## [1966 Shelby 427 Cobra Roadster](https://www.mecum.com/lots/1175604/1966-shelby-427-cobra-roadster/?aa_id=794437-0)


Mecum


**Mecum, Lot S96**


A fat-fendered 427 car, in blue with white Le Mans stripes, is the quintessential Cobra. It’s the look that so many of the kit cars and diecast models ape, but this one is the real deal, and it has led quite a life. It reportedly went to New York dealer Manhasset Ford when new for use as a demonstrator before going to its first private owner. He owned it for a couple of years, then advertised it for sale. The owner of a wrecking yard answered the ad and, during the test drive, pulled a gun and stole the car. Apparently, he had already bought another Cobra as an insurance write-off, and restamped the chassis number from that write-off onto his newly/illegally acquired Shelby sports car.


He then sold the car to the president of the Shelby Owners Association, and in 1978 French F1 driver/Le Mans winner Didier Pironi bought it. It wasn’t until the early 1990s that the chicanery was sorted out and the car was retitled with its proper chassis number. It was restored in the 1990s and converted to[427 S/C](https://www.hagerty.com/valuation-tools/shelby/cobra/1965/1965-shelby-cobra-427_s!c?id=aCn1I000000DCdXSAW) specifications, and later scanned as the model for the 427 Cobra in the 2011 video game *Forza Motorsport 4* . Mecum doesn’t have a presale estimate for it, but Bonhams sold this storied Cobra five years ago for[$1,050,000](https://cars.bonhams.com/auction/26887/lot/46/1966-shelby-427-cobra-chassis-no-csx-3242/) .


## [1935 Duesenberg Model JN Convertible Coupe by Rollston and Bohman & Schwartz](https://rmsothebys.com/auctions/mo26/lots/s0011-1935-duesenberg-model-jn-convertible-coupe-by-rollston-and-bohman-schwartz/)


Darin Schnabel/RM Sotheby's


**RM Sotheby’s, Lot 122**


There are multiple Duesenbergs on offer in Monterey this year, but this one is a particularly big deal. Actor Clark Gable ordered it new, but the Hollywood man didn’t just pick from a catalog and write a check. The car started out with coachwork from Rollston in New York City, but Gable had it sent directly to Bohman & Schwartz in California, sketched out what he wanted his new Duesenberg to look like, and worked with Bohman & Schwartz’s staff designer to make it a reality.


Darin Schnabel/RM Sotheby's


Darin Schnabel/RM Sotheby's


Gable reportedly enjoyed the car very much, but by the late 1940s it was in New Mexico, and in 1951 it sold to a professional wrestler. It was restored in the 1970s, and in the 1980s was part of the famous Blackhawk Collection. It joined the Sam and Emily Mann collection in 2005 and immediately received another restoration followed by a slew of concours appearances. For Monterey, the bespoke Clark Gable Duesenberg has a $5.75M–$8M estimate.


## [1960 Chevrolet Corvette LM](https://rmsothebys.com/auctions/mo26/lots/r0019-1960-chevrolet-corvette-lm/#/smallgallery_1434128)


Kegun Morkin/RM Sotheby's


**RM Sotheby’s, Lot 353**


In the 1950s, America’s biggest representative at Le Mans was wealthy businessman and sportsman Briggs Cunningham. He fielded a pair of modified Cadillacs there in 1950, and from 1951–55 entered cars built by his own B.S. Cunningham Company, piloted by American drivers. There were some successes, but an overall win at Le Mans eluded Cunningham, and he stopped building his own cars after 1955. He didn’t give up on racing, however, and his team instead pivoted to racing sports cars from other manufacturers like Jaguar and Maserati.


For Le Mans in 1960, Cunningham returned with a team of three Corvettes, aided by unofficial support from the factory and equipped with special-order features like a temperature-controlled radiator fan, quick-ratio steering, close-ratio gearbox, Positraction differential, heavy-duty suspension, heavy-duty brake linings, heavy-duty brakes, and race-spec fuel-injected engines.


Kegun Morkin/RM Sotheby's


Darin Schnabel/RM Sotheby's


This car is one of the three, given #1 for the race. Driving duties were supposed to fall to Briggs Cunningham and Zora Arkus-Duntov, but Arkus-Duntov, a GM employee, was ordered not to compete and Cunningham brought in American Bill Kimberly (of the Kimberly-Clark fortune) as a backup.


Cunningham started the race in the car and ran as high as 21st before handing the wheel over to Kimberly, who soon met with a sudden burst of rain and rolled the car, forcing its retirement. Of the Cunningham team’s other two Corvettes, one DNF’d but another finished eighth overall and first in class. All three were sold on, and this one fell off the radar for years, only to resurface in the early 2010s in modified and neglected condition. In 2021, it was reportedly reacquired by the Cunningham family (RM Sotheby’s sold it for[$758,500](https://rmsothebys.com/auctions/am21/lots/r0014-1960-chevrolet-corvette-lm/) that year) and restored back to its 1960 Le Mans appearance and specs, which cost over half a million dollars. Its estimate in 2026 is $2M–$2.5M.


## [1963 Chevrolet Corvette Grand Sport](https://rmsothebys.com/auctions/mo26/lots/r0081-1963-chevrolet-corvette-grand-sport/)


Genau AutoWerks Ingo Schmoldt/RM Sotheby's


**RM Sotheby’s, Lot 341**


Among the most mythical Corvettes of all are the five original Grand Sports, constructed to race against and beat Shelby’s Cobra in top-level sports car racing. The last time a real Grand Sport hit the public was way back in 2009, when one of the roadsters hit a[$4.9M](https://rmsothebys.com/auctions/az09/lots/r114-1963-chevrolet-corvette-grand-sport-roadster/) high bid but went home unsold.


When the all-new, second-generation Corvette arrived for[1963](https://www.hagerty.com/valuation-tools/search?q=1963+chevrolet+corvette) , it brought all kinds of improvements over its predecessor. But on track, and despite the potent Z06 competition package, it often couldn’t quite keep up with the significantly lighter Cobra. Zora Arkus-Duntov, the engineering force behind Corvette performance, created the Grand Sport to alleviate the car’s weight problem.


The changes to the Grand Sport were extensive, including a new lightweight chassis, extensive use of alloy components throughout, disc brakes (production Corvettes used drums until ’65), different suspension, thinner fiberglass for the body, and Plexiglas side and rear windows. The result was weight savings of well over 1000 pounds from a normal Corvette street car.


The Grand Sports showed up for testing at Sebring in late 1962 and showed great promise, but word soon reached the corporate bigwigs at GM, who were still honoring the company’s ban on factory racing efforts. That a few Chevrolet engineers had quietly built a factory race car was in direct violation of that policy, and the program was canceled. Instead of throwing his hands up, though, Arkus-Duntov schemed to loan the Grand Sports to private teams and support them unofficially.


Genau AutoWerks Ingo Schmoldt/RM Sotheby's


Genau AutoWerks Ingo Schmoldt/RM Sotheby's


Genau AutoWerks Ingo Schmoldt/RM Sotheby's


This car, Chassis 003, was entrusted to Illinois Chevrolet dealer Dick Doane, who campaigned it in the Sports Car Club of America’s (SCCA) C-Modified class, as the Grand Sports were so heavily altered from stock they weren’t eligible for production-based race classes. Despite competing against purpose-built machinery, Doane earned multiple class victories in the 1963 race season. Later that year, 003 went back to Chevrolet Engineering in preparation for that year’s races at Bahamas Speed Week, and Arkus-Duntov entrusted Texan racer John Mecom to take it and two other Grand Sports to Nassau for the event.


The Grand Sports now sported aluminum 377-cubic-inch V-8s fed through four Weber carburetors, and 003 qualified in third overall for the first race with Jim Hall behind the wheel, but all three Grand Sports retired with overheating issues. In the next day’s race, Augie Pabst wheeled 003 to fourth overall. In the finale, the Nassau Trophy race, 003 finished second in class and eighth overall, while another Grand Sport took class victory. More importantly, the Grand Sports proved faster than the Cobras, but after the event GM reiterated its ban on factory racing and the Grand Sport’s days were numbered.


Mecom entered 003 in the 1964 Sebring 12-hour race, with John Cannon and a young A.J. Foyt driving. Foyt started the race from 62nd position but was in the top 10 within the early hours before ending up in 23rd overall and second in class at the finish. By early 1965, the car was officially in private hands, now powered by a new 427-cubic-inch V-8, and entered by Texan Alan Sevadjian for Sebring, where it finished third in class.


In the 2000s, it was restored to its original 1964 Sebring specifications at a reported cost of nearly $1M, and its current output is estimated at roughly 600 hp and 600 lb-ft. For Monterey, its estimate is $11M–$13M. If it sells, it will be the most expensive Corvette ever sold at auction, and indeed the first one to break eight figures in a public sale.


## [1964 Shelby Cobra Daytona Coupe](https://www.goodingco.com/lot/1964-shelby-cobra-daytona-coupe?sortBy=ENDING_SOONEST&pageNumber=3)


Gooding Christie's


Gooding Christie's


**Gooding Christie’s, Lot 39**


When Carroll Shelby famously combined the lightweight British[AC Ace](https://www.hagerty.com/valuation-tools/ac/ace/1957/1957-ac-ace-bristol?id=aCn1I000000DKPBSA4) chassis with Ford V-8 power, his Cobras found success very quickly in American road racing. The Ace’s body wasn’t particularly slippery, though, and on some of the high-speed European circuits where Ferrari and its[250 GTO](https://www.hagerty.com/valuation-tools/ferrari/250_gto/1962/1962-ferrari-250_gto-si?id=aCn1I000000CwcWSAS) reigned supreme, the aerodynamics put Shelby at a disadvantage. So, he tasked engineer Pete Brock to come up with a coupe version of the Cobra.


The so-called “Daytona Coupe,” complete with sloping fastback roof and Kamm tail, was what he came up with. And it worked, as Shelby secured a World Sportscar Championship title in 1965 thanks to the Daytona Coupe and won their class at Sebring, Daytona, Le Mans, Monza, Reims, and the Nürburgring.


Gooding Christie's


Gooding Christie's


Gooding Christie's


Chassis CSX2300, the car on offer at Pebble Beach, is the third of just six original Daytona Coupes. Its first race was the 1964 Tour de France Automobile, a grueling multi-event competition on circuits, hill climbs, and public roads. Bob Bondurant and Jochen Neerpasch drove it, winning the first two stages before various mechanical maladies kept it from overall success. The next season, CSX2300 went to Daytona, where it qualified fifth and finished sixth overall and third in class. The next month at Sebring, it stalled a few feet after starting and was rear-ended by a Volvo, but went on to finish 13th overall and third in class, setting a class lap record in the process.


After Sebring, it went to Europe and got fresh white paint with blue and red stripes to represent Ford of France. Driven by Jo Schlesser and André Simon, it finished 12th overall and third in class at the Nürburgring. At the Reims 12-hour race, it was back in Shelby’s signature Guardsman Blue with Englishmen Jack Sears and John Whitmore driving, and they brought it home in ninth overall and second in class.


With Shelby shifting focus to the GT40 program, CSX2300 sold into private ownership, and in 1966 it was already in Japan, where it snatched overall victories at Suzuka and Fuji. By the late ’60s it was no longer a top-level competitor on track, and it sold to a young Cobra enthusiast in Tokyo who registered it for road use, installed a milder Ford V-8, and used it regularly on the street. By the mid-1970s it was back in the U.S., and sold to Carroll Shelby himself, who had it restored and kept it until the late 1990s. It was restored again in the 2000s.


With its Ferrari-beating, World Championship-winning history combined with extreme rarity, the[Shelby Daytona Coupe](https://www.hagerty.com/valuation-tools/shelby/cobra_daytona/1964/1964-shelby-cobra_daytona-base?id=aCn1I000000DJtFSAW) is one of the most sought-after sports cars made in America or anywhere. The last public sale of one was in 2009, when CSX2601 sold for $7.25M, then the highest price paid for an American car at a public auction. CSX2300 could take that record back for the Daytona Coupe in 2026, as Gooding Christie’s presale estimate is “in excess of $25,000,000.”
