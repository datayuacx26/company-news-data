# Final Parking Spaces: 1988 Ford Escort Pony and GT

The tale of the first-generation Ford Escort for North America is an interesting one, and I’ve managed to find one example apiece of the cheapest and the most expensive versions for the 1988 model year. Each is painted Oxford White, each was built at[Wayne Stamping and Assembly in Michigan](https://www.thewaynedispatch.com/history-of-the-ford-plant/) , and each was sold new out of the San Jose sales office.


Ford of Britain


Ford of Britain first deployed the Escort name in the middle 1950s, when it designated the lowest trim level of[the wagon version](https://storm.oldcarmanualproject.com/fordescort1955.htm) of[the Anglia 100E](https://storm.oldcarmanualproject.com/fordanglia1958.htm) .


Ford of Europe


The Escort lineage relevant to today’s article was born in 1967, when Ford of Europe introduced[the Mark 1 Escort](https://silodrome.com/history-ford-escort-mk-1/) . This was a small, rear-wheel-drive car, designed as the replacement for the Anglia.


The Mk1 Escort sold very well in Europe, and it became a fearsome competitor in road and rally racing. Today, it’s an all-time rally legend.


The Mark 2 Escort, introduced in 1975, stayed on more or less the same platform but got a bigger, more squared-off body.


Landewyck Tobacco S.A.


The Mk2 Escort also found great racing success.


Meanwhile, the European operations of General Motors and Chrysler were working to save money by building a genuine “[world car](https://www.hagerty.com/media/opinion/vision-thing/vision-thing-the-world-car-fallacy/) ,” which in this usage meant “a (mostly) European-designed small car that Americans will buy in quantity.” The first of these 1970s designs to reach that goal (sort of) was the GM T Platform.


The rear-wheel-drive T-body started out as a joint project between Opel and Isuzu, and the first version to hit showrooms was the Brazilian Chevrolet Chevette of 1973. The European-market[Opel Kadett C](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fd_nKnh66G8) followed later that year, followed by the Isuzu Gemini for Japan. Daewoo, Vauxhall, Holden, and all the rest of the far-flung GM Empire joined the T-Car party.


General Motors


In the United States, the T was sold as the Chevrolet Chevette (1976 through 1987 model years) and as the[Pontiac T1000/1000](https://www.autoweek.com/car-life/classic-cars/a1717991/junkyard-treasure-1982-pontiac-t1000/) (1981 through 1987 model years). This wouldn’t have happened if the Chevy Vega hadn’t[been such a flop](https://www.hagerty.com/media/automotive-history/the-infamous-vega-could-have-been-great-if-chevy-hadnt-rushed/) , and the North American Chevette ended up sharing no body panels with its overseas relatives once Detroit worked over the design. Perhaps it was more of a first cousin than a sibling to the T-Cars sold elsewhere on the planet, but nearly three million left American Chevrolet dealers and it can be considered a world- *ish* car success story.


During the middle 1970s, Chrysler Europe began developing a new front-wheel-drive small car[with the help of some American engineers and designers](https://www.allpar.com/threads/back-story-how-chrysler-chose-to-build-the-horizon-and-omni.229864/) . This became[the Simca/Talbot/Chrysler Horizon](https://www.aronline.co.uk/cars/chrysler-talbot/horizon/c2-development-story/) , which hit European showrooms as a 1978 model.


Chrysler Corporation


As happened with GM’s T-body once it reached our shores, the idea of making the North American version of the Horizon the same as its Euro-market counterparts went out the window once the crew in Michigan got to work. The resulting vehicles,[the Plymouth Horizon and Dodge Omni](https://www.hagerty.com/media/opinion/final-parking-space/final-parking-space-1988-plymouth-horizon-america/) , bore a strong resemblance to their relatives in the old country but couldn’t be considered true world cars as we’d understand the term today.


Chrysler Corporation


The Omnirizon allowed Chrysler to sell small cars that weren’t just rebadged Mitsubishis, and Omnirizon-based cars[and trucks](https://www.hagerty.com/media/opinion/final-parking-space/final-parking-space-1984-dodge-rampage-2-2/) were sold here from the 1978 through 1990 model years. Once again, a vehicle design from a Detroit carmaker’s European operations proved successful on this side of the ocean.


When the time came for Ford of Europe to update the Mk2 Escort with a modern front-wheel-drive platform in order to compete with the Honda Civic and Volkswagen Golf (aka Rabbit), the general idea was that there would be a North American version to replace[the aging Ford Pinto](https://www.hagerty.com/media/opinion/final-parking-space/final-parking-space-1978-ford-pinto-3-door-runabout/) .


Ford Motor Company


As was the case with the Chevette and Omnirizon, the Americanized Escort looked quite similar to its family members across the water but was a different car in many important ways. Both were launched as 1981 models and shared a chassis layout and many powertrain components, and both were sales hits, but their Focus successor was a much more *worldly* world car.


Still, Ford marketing in the United States emphasized the Escort’s worldwide ancestry.


Lincoln-Mercury Division, Ford Motor Company


The first-generation Escort and its Mercurized twin, the Lynx, were sold here for the 1981 through 1990 model years. Sales figures were excellent, topping four million total (and more, if you include the Escort-based[EXP](https://www.autoweek.com/car-life/classic-cars/a30320352/junkyard-treasure-1987-ford-exp-sport-coupe/) /[LN7](https://murileemartin.substack.com/p/reconstituted-junkyard-jewel-1982) two-seater and the Escort-derived[Tempo](https://www.autoblog.com/news/junkyard-gem-1985-ford-tempo-gl-sedan/) /[Topaz](https://autoweek.com/article/junkyard-treasures/junkyard-treasure-1987-mercury-topaz-gs-4wd) ).


[Murilee Martin](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/mmartin/)


The EXP got Escort badging after the mid-1985 facelift, being sold as either the Escort EXP Luxury Coupe or the Escort EXP Sport Coupe. 1988 was the final year for the EXP, while the Mercury LN7 was built for just the 1982 and 1983 model years.


Ford Motor Company


The first-generation North American Escort was available as a three-door hatchback, a five-door hatchback, and a five-door station wagon.


Lincoln-Mercury Division, Ford Motor Company


Ford and Mercury dealers thus had station wagons in three distinct sizes to sell for most of the decade.


[Murilee Martin](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/mmartin/)

[Murilee Martin](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/mmartin/)


Ford introduced the Pony name for the 1976 model year, when it was applied to[an extra-cheap version](https://oldcarbrochures.org/United%20States/Ford/1976_Ford/1976%20Ford%20Economy%20Leaders/slides/1976%20Ford%20Economy%20Leaders_Page_2.html) of the[Pinto](https://www.hagerty.com/media/opinion/final-parking-space/final-parking-space-1978-ford-pinto-3-door-runabout/) . It was[revived for 1986](https://oldcarbrochures.org/United%20States/Ford/1986_Ford/1986%20Ford%20Cars%2001-86%20Revised/slides/1986%20Ford%20Cars%20%28Rev%29.pdf-2024-5-29%209.23.41_Page_05.html) as the designation for the most affordable Escort, squeezing below the Escort L. From that time until the introduction of[the tiny Festiva](https://oldcarbrochures.org/United%20States/Ford/1988-Ford/1988-Ford-Full-Line-Brochure/slides/1988_Ford_Full-05.html) for 1988, the Escort Pony was the cheapest new car offered by Ford in the United States.


[Murilee Martin](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/mmartin/)


How cheap? The MSRP for this car was $6586, or $19,008 in 2026 dollars. That’s more than a grand cheaper than[the lowest-priced 2026 car](https://www.hyundaiusa.com/us/en/vehicles/venue) sold in the United States right now.

[Murilee Martin](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/mmartin/)


This car has many pricey options that would have made the out-the-door price significantly higher, including air conditioning and cruise control.

All Escorts for 1988 were equipped with 1.9-liter versions of[the CVH four-cylinder engine](https://www.gomog.com/allmorgan/cvhhistory.html) . The CVH, which was developed for the third-generation Escort on both sides of the Atlantic, used a “[cam-in-head](https://www.spannerhead.com/2013/05/03/technical-curiosities-opels-cam-in-head-engine/) ” design, which is something of an OHC/OHV mashup that has a head-mounted camshaft operating OHV-style lifters and rockers (essentially, a pushrod engine that doesn’t need pushrods).


[Murilee Martin](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/mmartin/)


The warmed-up CVH in this GT was rated at 115 horsepower and 120 pound-feet. Since the car weighed just a bit over 2500 pounds, this was enough power to make it fun to drive.


[Murilee Martin](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/mmartin/)


This is the engine in our FPS Pony. It’s got throttle-body electronic fuel injection instead of the multi-port EFI system on the GT’s CVH, and it was rated at 90 horsepower and 106 pound-feet of torque.


[Murilee Martin](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/mmartin/)


The GT has the base five-speed manual transmission.


[Murilee Martin](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/mmartin/)

Beginning with the 1991 model year, the North American Escort moved to a Mazda platform, which made it the same car as a[Mazda Protegé](https://www.autoblog.com/features/junkyard-gem-1992-mazda-protege-sedan/) under the skin. The Ford-Mazda partnership led to quite a bit of mixing-and-matching of technology and badging during the 1990s, including such machines as a[Mercurized Australian Ford Laser](https://www.autoblog.com/features/junkyard-gem-1991-mercury-capri-turbo/) , Mazda-badged[versions of the Ford Explorer](https://www.thetruthaboutcars.com/2019/07/junkyard-find-1994-mazda-navajo-lx/) and[Ranger](https://www.autoblog.com/features/junkyard-gem-1995-mazda-b4000-le-cab-plus/) , and a[could-have-been-a-Mustang](https://www.hagerty.com/media/buying-and-selling/hagerty-marketplace/the-1989-ford-probe-was-more-than-an-almost-mustang/) based on[MX-6](https://www.hagerty.com/media/opinion/final-parking-space/final-parking-space-1995-mazda-mx-6-ls/) mechanicals.


[Murilee Martin](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/mmartin/)
