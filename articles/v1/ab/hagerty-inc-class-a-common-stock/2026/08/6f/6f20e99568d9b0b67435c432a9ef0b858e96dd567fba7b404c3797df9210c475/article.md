# Aston Martin Valen Powers Into Pebble Beach

Aston Martin’s Q Branch has let loose the world’s most powerful front-engined car: the Valen.


The Valen makes its case for views and clicks at Car Week by boasting an 838-hp iteration of Aston’s own 5.3-liter twin-turbocharged V-12. There’s also 737 lb-ft of torque sent to the rear wheels via an eight-speed ZF automatic transmission which uses shift strategies derived from the Vantage GT4 race car.


Zero-to-60 mph is accomplished in three seconds flat, and the Valen’s Vmax is 214 mph. Aston Martin’s engineers have cut 220 lbs in weight compared to the previous lightest V-12 by using carbon fiber for the whole body as well as fitting a titanium exhaust system.


The Valen, which gets its name from the Latin *valens* , meaning strong or powerful, is derived from the[Vanquish](https://www.hagerty.com/media/news/astonishing-823-hp-for-new-vanquish/) , but with obvious additions for aerodynamics and cooling. What it loses in sophistication, it gains in aggression. Massive side vents duct air away from the engine bay and front brakes, while complex three-part side skirts, a ducktail spoiler, multi-part splitter, and jumbo rear diffuser provide downforce.

Aston Martin Chief Creative Officer Marek Reichman calls it “a significant departure from what has gone before,” and describes “an attacking stance that emphasises the front-engine rear-drive layout.” He adds: “Valen is a provocative front-engined supercar exuding increased potency and sporting intent, yet still celebrating the elevated luxury, meticulous detailing and unmistakable design signatures expected of a flagship Aston Martin.”


The show car is finished in Satin Andromeda Red, but buyers can opt for fully-exposed carbon fiber to show off the car’s construction. The interior is simpler than that of the Vanquish, with a slimmer center console à la[Valhalla](https://www.hagerty.com/media/videos/first-drive-aston-martin-valhalla/) and making use of 3D printing for various components. Coming under a new Special Vehicle Operations department within Q by Aston Martin, customers will have near-limitless choices when it comes to customizing the cabin, using semi-aniline leather or Alcantara. A Driver Focused option singles out the hot seat in a different colorway.

Those truly committing to the bit will also want to choose the lightweight pack, which shaves 37 lbs through the fitment of machined-from-solid aluminum suspension arms and knuckles, plus titanium bolts. Magnesium wheels reduce unsprung mass and are fitted with Pirelli P Zero Cyber tires, which feed data to the car’s systems in real time. The Valen also gets a revised ESP system, along with its own calibration for the Bilstein DTX semi-active dampers, steering, and carbon ceramic brakes.

Aston Martin Director of Vehicle Performance, Simon Newton, explains, “Everything we’ve done to the powertrain and chassis has been driven by the same objective; to intensify the character and capability to deliver a truly thrilling experience that’s accessible to all skill levels, but keen drivers will absolutely relish.”


Just 150 Valens are to be built, with the first cars set to be delivered in early 2027.
