# According To You: Crazy Auto Parts Recycling Stories

Creativity is all around us. We see it when Mother Nature ensures that a plant grows in the cracks of broken concrete, and when a child draws something that came from their fertile mind. It should come as no surprise that car enthusiasts can do the same. We asked how you[made something creative](https://www.hagerty.com/media/hagerty-community/whats-your-craziest-auto-parts-recycling-story/) out of automobile parts, and you did not disappoint.


The selection of reader feedback we took from last week’s question is a mix of the unexpectedly creative and creative reuse of parts from one car to another. I believe both recycling categories are sufficiently crazy in the best of ways. But the final say belongs to you, so give us your thoughts in the comments below!


## Scooped Up


Fierosound.com


**hyperv6:** I do this stuff all the time. The Indy Scoop on my Fiero, like the[1984 Fiero Pace Car](https://www.hagerty.com/media/automotive-history/pontiacs-1984-indy-pace-car-fiero-was-the-mid-engine-firecracker-we-never-got-to-buy/) , needed a screen. I used an old bit from a Malibu grill cut to size. The Center caps on the Prime wheels are from a Bonneville, reshaped and repainted to look stock.


The best is the Metro Airforce blaster I dry cars with. I built a stand for it, and to give it weight, I put my Dale Earnhardt Tire and wheel on it to hold it down. Makes a nice display.


## Emblematic Enthusiasm


eBay | Family Finds Collectibles


**Desmond G:** Not me and not even remotely crazy, but a friend of mine replaced a missing logo on the amplifier head of his half-stack with an ‘80s F-250 badge.


It was Texas; it seemed to make sense at the time!


## A Spark of Creativity


Amazon


**TG:** I once used a ’70s-era HEI ignition module and a ’90s-era dual-plug coil pack to get my buddy’s golf cart running. It didn’t play well with the factory pickup, but it did function.


## Merry Christmas!


Darin Schnabel ©2010 Courtesy o


**Hot Rod Lincoln:** My *(father? -SM)* had recently bought a ’69 Firebird convertible. The convertible frame was crushed by a tree, and he was bummed out about finding a replacement. At a swim meet, I saw a dad wearing a Firebird T-shirt. I asked him if he knew about a junkyard that might have vintage Firebird parts, and he did. On a snowy day, I went out to the yard and found two top frames. The cars had rotted into the ground. With a whole lotta rust buster spray, I was able to break the bolts loose, and I claimed one of the top frames. I carried it to the guy running the yard, and he only wanted $20.


I found a box that it would fit into, and wrapped it up and slid it under the Christmas tree. The surprise on his face was priceless. To this day, he has no idea where I found the top frame. It was worth every minute standing in a snowstorm removing the top frame.


## Creativity Runs Amok


Amazon | GAFONAXDRI Store


**Oversquare Bore:** My dad bought running boards. At install, he found they shorted him some hardware. Instead of sending just the missing nuts and bolts, the company sent a complete new kit – all brackets and braces along with a whole extra set of hardware, just not the actual running boards. Later, when it came time for me to fabricate a transmission crossmember for my LS Miata, I realized Hyundai Santa Fe running board brackets worked perfectly. (With a little help from the MIG!)


## Under Pressure


[Kyle Smith](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/kyle-smith/)


**Jim66:** I’m just a rookie at this stuff, but years ago I drove a ’66 Mustang from Toledo, Ohio to Cape Coral, Florida with a clutch plate that ended up missing 3 springs and a couple rivets. Just a little tough getting going, but once out of first, couldn’t tell there was a problem. The guy that replaced it showed it to me and was in awe that it didn’t disintegrate. Couldn’t stand to pitch it, so turned it into a clock and still is hanging on the wall.

## It Opens Doors…


Mecum


**Tinkerah:** My two favorites are the Hurst shifter ball as the doorknob on our main entryway (I always credit Mrs. Tinkerah and her hot rod heart for the idea), and my retractable air hose reel mounted just inside the garage door on a pair of ’60s-era car door hinges. It works inside with the door closed, or swung out in either of the two detents.


## Less Crazy, More Recycled?


Chevrolet


**Ron F:** Wanted to put leather inside my 2009 Chevy dually extra cab but that’s pricey!!! Was in the boneyard and here was an ex-cab Chevy with a light gray leather back seat (impossible to find in an ex-cab) in perfect condition. It had all the bolts out of it except the integrated seat belt bolt, which took a large star socket.


Paid a guy 10 bucks to remove and it was mine. Justified the cost of OEM front leather seat covers and installed myself and…I got leather!


**Waddy:** Not sure if this qualifies, but we had a Subaru Impreza wagon with two rusted front doors. I went up the wreckers near me, found the same car, different color, with two perfect front doors. I was by myself at the time. Wrenches out, I got one door loosened, then realized how heavy the damn thing was. Called my son-in-law; maybe he was just my daughter’s bf at the time, no matter.


He was off that day so we met at the lot. Two doors off, with me underneath while he loosened the last bolt and off we went. I think the total was $40, maybe less. After that, the Subaru had “a Nova Scotia paint job” which translates to: different color parts. The Subby lasted for a few more years like that.
