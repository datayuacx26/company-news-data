# Report: Audi Sport Is Fighting to Keep the Five-Cylinder Alive

Audi’s sonorous five-cylinder engine is[turning 50 this year](https://www.hagerty.com/media/news/audi-says-happy-birthday-to-the-five-cylinder-with-limited-edition-rs-3/) , and up until recently it looked like the end was near for it. That might not be the case after all: Audi Sport is searching for ways to keep the five alive for another generation, according to a recent report.


Unveiled earlier in 2026 and limited to 750 units globally,[the RS3 Competition Limited](https://www.hagerty.com/media/news/audi-says-happy-birthday-to-the-five-cylinder-with-limited-edition-rs-3/) marked the end of the current model’s production run. Credible rumors claimed that it also marked the end of Audi’s five-cylinder engine; CEO Gernot Döllner admitted that he wasn’t sure whether Audi would be able to justify making the engine compliant with the upcoming Euro 7 regulations.


Speaking to British magazine *[Autocar](https://www.autocar.co.uk/car-news/new-cars/its-so-characteristic-audi-mulls-options-five-cylinder-future)* , Audi Sport boss Rolf Michl opined that heritage alone makes investing in the five-cylinder worth it. “We’re well aware of the historical meaning of the five-cylinder. Evaluations are still running, and people who know me know that I also have a personal relationship to the five-cylinder. It’s so characteristic and also deeply rooted in history,” he said.


Carmakers are businesses that need to make money, so heritage flies out the window unless it’s an asset. Luckily, the five-cylinder brings more to the Ingolstadt-based company than five decades of history. It powers the RS3, which is positioned as the entry point into the Audi Sport portfolio, and it’s a unique selling point that none of its competitors offer. “The entrance into the RS world is important, because having a brand statement like that is amazing,” Michl explained. “Emotion starts in the compact segment.”

Put another way, the five-cylinder engine’s uniqueness could help put the RS3 at the top of a buyer’s shopping list. That person could in turn become a lifelong Audi Sport customer and step up to more expensive cars, instead of buying[a BMW M2](https://www.hagerty.com/media/new-car-reviews/2026-bmw-m2-cs-test-drive-such-a-talented-track-rat/) (one of the RS3’s most direct rivals, and which is also powered by a highly distinctive engine) and ending up a lifelong BMW M buyer.


To live on, the five-cylinder needs to clear at least two obstacles: The upcoming Euro 7 emissions regulations, which are scheduled to come into effect in November 2026, and volume-related concerns. Placing the turbo-five at the heart of a hybrid system would help keep emissions in check, but it’s a solution that would add cost, weight, and complexity to the models it ends up in. Audi has done it before, however:[The new RS5](https://www.hagerty.com/media/news/new-audi-rs5-specs-pictures-performance/) unveiled earlier in 2026 features a 630-horsepower, V-6-electric plug-in hybrid drivetrain.


One of the easiest ways for Audi to solve the volume problem would be to share the five-cylinder engine with its sister companies. The current turbo-five powers[the RS3](https://www.hagerty.com/media/news/2025-audi-rs3-specs-pictures-pricing/) and the Cupra Formentor VZ5, which is a European-market SUV that shares its platform with the Q3. Earlier rumors[claim](https://www.hagerty.com/media/news/2027-volkswagen-golf-r-five-cylinder-rumor/) Volkswagen will put the five-cylinder in the Golf R in 2027 to celebrate 25 years of[the R32](https://www.hagerty.com/media/market-trends/the-cheap-mk-iv-r32-is-dead-long-live-the-r32/) that the R traces its roots to. The hot hatch isn’t a high-volume model, either, but it could help Audi make a business case for the five.


Michl stressed that a decision about the five-cylinder’s future hasn’t been made yet.
