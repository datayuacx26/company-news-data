# Formula for Healing: Frank Kotnik’s 1976 Pontiac Firebird Formula

We enthusiasts view cars as more than just transportation or machines to be used and abused. Rather, they are present throughout our lives, witnesses to triumph and tragedy. This is a car story about a 1976 Pontiac Firebird Formula. But it is also a life story: there is achievement, satisfaction, heartbreaking loss, healing, and survival.


## Formula Won


Andrew Nussbaum


Frank Kotnik has loved cars since childhood, especially Pontiacs, which were his family’s choice. Back in 1979, Frank was a cocky 18-year-old kid from Queens. He had his whole life ahead of him. When it came time for his first car, he yearned for a model from the excitement division. As fate would have it, what he wanted was right next door.


Frank’s neighbor owned a 1976 Firebird Formula that he purchased new, and after three years and 44,000 miles, he was ready to part with it for $4000. Frank was already following in his father’s and grandfather’s footsteps, working as a blacksmith, so he was able to fork over the four grand. The ‘bird was in great condition, completely stock and covered in a gorgeous first-year color: Firethorn Red. The inside had red guts and white vinyl bucket seats. The Formula was not as famous as the Trans Am, but it was quite the first car for Frank.

The car had some interesting provenance, too. Not only was it built in the U.S. bicentennial year (1976) and Pontiac’s 50th anniversary, but it was ordered and delivered to a Queens, New York, Pontiac dealership: Myrtle Motors. Myrtle was renowned for its performance-minded management and mechanics, and they’d commonly tweak Pontiac’s heavy hitters for drag racing. Frank’s cousin Al just happened to be one of those mechanics, and upon inspection, he gave the neighbor’s Formula his thumbs-up.


Frank ran with a ‘70s hot rod theme, along with a few factory-favorite touches. First came the Trans Am wheel flares to join the optional rear spoiler that came on the car, followed by a Pontiac hood tach that looks right at home on the long, scooped hood. Then he went a little hippie-chic and had orange, gold, and silver graphics painted on the lower panels and rear quarters. Frank says he gave the painter free rein, but the design turned out a little loud.


Speaking of loud… when the exhaust needed replacing, Frank installed a pair of raucous Hooker side pipes. Completing the package, he swapped the factory Rally IIs for a set of chrome Westin wheels, wrapped in high-profile BFG tires. The funky Firebird looked and sounded the part, Frank says, and many passengers had lasting memories of the side pipes: namely, first-degree burns on their legs.


Andrew Nussbaum


Frank admits not doing much street or drag racing back in the day, describing his Formula as a “No Go Showboat,” because of its low-compression 350-cubic-inch V-8. Style over substance, but it got attention. The car was his beloved daily driver, pleasure cruiser, and clubbing chariot through New York’s five boroughs and all over Long Island. Pulling up in his flashy Firebird to the Copacabana, Studio 54, even the Playboy Club, always drew attention and usually granted him quick entry.


After a few years and another 30,000 miles, Frank recounts the day he heard a horrible noise coming from the engine. He brought it to Myrtle Motors, where cousin Al was hesitant to admit the obvious: the engine block was cracked. Frank ordered a new crate block, and in Al’s garage, they cherry-picked the old 350, assembled the new long block, and dropped it in.


Not long after, Frank got word that his cousin Peter in Pennsylvania had totaled his ’73 Grand Am. Peter’s dad told Frank that for $500, he could come and take the Grand Am’s 455 V-8 for his Formula. Frank agreed. In an interview with Hagerty, he describes ripping the crushed fascia off and pulling out the married 455 and TH400 transmission. Frank had every intention of using the big-cube motor to give his “no go showboat” some more go, but he was “getting busy with life and just never got around to it.” That 455 sat in his garage for three decades.

By 1985, Frank had settled down with his beautiful wife Tina. The Formula was still racking up miles and fender benders. Frank says that the second round of bodywork was lousy and left checking in the paint.


For the next few years, the Formula was relegated to weekend cruising, until in 1989, when Frank hurt his back and found it increasingly difficult to get in and out of his low-slung pony car. He also felt the aging side pipes had lost their appeal. He purchased a brand-new Pontiac Grand Prix and confesses that he “lost interest in the Formula and contemplated selling.” Ultimately, on the advice of friends and his own intuition, he kept the Formula and tucked it in his garage for the next twenty-seven years.


## Markus


In 2001, Frank’s son Markus Adam was born. Frank wasted no time introducing him to the world of cars, taking him to car shows as a toddler and giving him Hot Wheels and Matchbox toys. The *Cars* movie was also an early influence. But more than anything, what really drove Markus’ fascination was growing up around that half-covered Pontiac in the garage.


By his early teens, Markus was a real chip off the old engine block and knew his stuff. He would draw pictures of the Formula, wrote a school paper about it, and would frequently ask, “Dad, when are you going to get the Firebird running and put the 455 in?” It was obvious to Frank that Markus had become a member of the Pontiac tribe.


Courtesy Frank Kotnick


By 2016, Frank decided to begin working on the Formula as a father-son project. Step one was pushing it out of the garage so it could be flat-bedded to Harry Wesch Motors. The late Harry Sr. was a Pontiac mechanic who made a name for himself in the ’60s and ’70s wrenching at Myrtle Motors. Later, Harry Jr, took the reins. He got the Formula’s engine running, did the brakes, and checked everything for safety.


Courtesy Frank Kotnick


With the Formula awakened from its three-decade hibernation, Frank and Markus began restoring it back to stock. Markus was hands-on, disconnecting and removing the hood tach and Quadrajet carb, so both could be rebuilt. Next, he unboxed a new Pypes exhaust system to be installed at Harry’s after the rusted-out side pipes were cut off and tossed in the dumpster.


Courtesy Frank Kotnick


At every chance, Markus sat in the driver’s seat, grasping the steering wheel and pining for the day when he could pilot the iconic pony car. Markus became a fan of Smokey and the Bandit and yearned for a black/gold ‘77 Trans Am. After only a few months, in early spring of 2017, the Formula won second place in the work-in-progress class at a local car show.


On the evening of May 11, 2017, Markus died. While Frank and Tina were at a local event, he had accidentally strangled himself while engaging in a social media challenge known as the “choking game.” (Information on this dangerous, long-running behavior among teenagers and young adults can be found[here](https://www.allianceforhope.org/training-institute-on-strangulation-prevention/resources/the-choking-game) .)


Courtesy Frank Kotnick


As the father of a 15-year-old son, I find it hard to write this, but as Frank states, “I want to inform parents that this could happen. I don’t wish this, what we have gone through, on anyone.”


## A Voice from Beyond


Andrew Nussbaum


How does a person endure the pain of such a loss? And go on? Frank and Tina grappled with these questions as they tried to console each other in the aftermath. Frank explains that the answers came to him in a dream.


Soon after Markus passed, Frank decided to complete the Formula’s restoration, both to honor Markus and for his own healing process. One day at Wesch Motors, Harry Jr. offered Frank a Pontiac 455 that he had in the shop. It was completely rebuilt by famed Pontiac engine master and drag racer Butch Di Lorenzo. Frank was enticed, especially since the 455 in his own possession needed everything. Still, he kindly said no.


That night, Frank says, he had a dream: “I saw Markus and heard his voice telling me to finish the Formula and put the 455 in. When I woke up and looked at the clock, it was 4:55 a.m.” The next day, Frank went back to Harry Jr. and said, “Let’s do it.”

## Back on the Road


The Formula project finally came to an end after nine years. Frank extends his gratitude to Tommy Penito from the Moonlight Garage, Fred Brown from FB Performance, Mark & Geraldo from Northpoint Restoration, Richie and Ron from R&R Upholstery, and Joe, Joe Jr and Chris from Star Auto Glass.


Just as significant is the way Frank honors Markus’s memory: the[Magic Markus Foundation](https://www.hagerty.com/mailto:frankakotnick@aol.com) is a nonprofit dedicated to educating parents about deadly online challenges. As for Frank and Tina, the healing continues, but every time they fire up the Formula, they know Markus is smiling down on them.
