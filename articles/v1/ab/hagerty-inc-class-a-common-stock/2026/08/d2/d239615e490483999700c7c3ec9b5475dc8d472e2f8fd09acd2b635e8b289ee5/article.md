# Jaguar Shows Off Its Past and Previews Its Future in Pebble Beach

On a lovely little patch of the 17th Fairway at this year’s Pebble Beach Concours d’Elegance, there are five famous Jaguars from the past and one Jaguar from the future. The group of historic cars celebrates the British brand’s “evolutionary milestones in aerodynamics, lightweight design, and high‑performance engineering.” At the same time, Jaguar’s upcoming four-door electric GT model—the Type 01—shows off its production-ready interior, though the exterior remains wrapped in camo.

No proper Jaguar display would be without a D-Type, the voluptuous three-time Le Mans-winning model, and XKD507 is the first in the display lineup. A long-nose example, it raced to multiple victories with Briggs Cunningham’s race team, and wears the signature Cunningham colors of white with blue stripes. Next to it is the road-going version of the D-Type—the XKSS—which is essentially the race car with an added windshield and bumpers, and that’s about it.

Jaguar originally planned a run of 25 cars, but a fire at the factory destroyed much of the stock and only 16 were completed. The one on the grass at Pebble is perhaps the most famous XKSS, as its first owner was Steve McQueen. It’s now part of the permanent collection at the Petersen Museum.

Although an E-Type isn’t all that noteworthy at first glance among all the rarefied metal at Monterey Car Week, the Primrose Yellow 4.2-liter one here is in essentially new condition, just as it left Coventry in 1967. Its odometer shows just 381 miles, and this marks its first physical appearance on American soil.

A pair of XJ-Ss rounded out the historic display, both of them famous but for different reasons. The British Racing Green cabriolet was delivered new to Princess Diana, and features a special 2+2 seating configuration, custom leather and Harris Tweed upholstery, and export-style headlights. Diana, Princess of Wales, reportedly drove the car regularly between 1987 and 1991, and this is the car’s first appearance in North America. The other XJ-S, meanwhile, has seen plenty of time on American soil, as it won seven consecutive races and took home the 1978 Trans-Am championship with Bob Tullius’ legendary Group 44 team.

All these nods to Jaguar’s past lead up to where the brand—which has no *new* vehicles on sale in 2026—is pinning its hopes for the future. At center stage is a pre-production version of the all-electric Type 01. While the body is still covered in a camouflaging wrap, this event is the debut of the production-ready interior—a mix of perforated leather, carpet, cloth, and screens.

Aside from the screens and the clean, minimalist layout, the most eye-grabbing feature inside is the semi-floating central spine/console that runs all the way from underneath the dash and in between both pairs of seats into the rear of the car, essentially creating four individual spaces inside the car. Though there is a large panoramic screen that will house much of the car’s instrumentation and functions, there are physical buttons on the door panels and steering wheel.


[Brandan Gillogly](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/brandan-gillogly/)

When it hits showrooms, the Type 01 promises to deliver 1000 horsepower thanks to one electric motor up front and two in the rear, as well as four-wheel steering, height-adjustable air suspension, and active twin-valve dampers. It’s part of Jaguar’s new “creative philosophy” that “embraces bold designs” as well as “unexpected and original thinking.” The Type 01’s full, official debut will be at the New York Auto Show in October.
