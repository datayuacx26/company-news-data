# Ready for Anything: Thousands of Fix-Its in a 10-Pound Bag

I’ve broken down on the side of the road enough times that I now obsess over what tools to bring. That includes creating my own kit. I also keep an eye out for what others pack. Someone must have the Goldilocks kit—everything you need, nothing you don’t.


Turns out, though, everyone has a different definition of “need.” I thought the professionals, those amazing mechanics who can fix everything and frequently follow vintage-car rallies like the California Mille, would have a good sense. I’ve found, however, that when they’re charged with caring for a fleet, they don’t simply bring the carryall toolbox, but rather the one that has everything and requires a forklift to move.


We polled the Hagerty readership for tool-kit ideas. Turns out most of you are minimalists, bringing along only a way to jump-start a car, either via cables or a battery jump box, like one from Battery Tender ([Hagerty Drivers Club members](https://www.hagerty.com/drivers-club/marketing/partner-offers) enjoy a 20 percent discount). Perhaps that confidence comes from having roadside assistance. Indeed, one reader noted that his best “tools” are Hagerty Drivers Club roadside towing and a cellphone. That said, I still throw the kit pictured above into whatever I’m driving. Let me know what I’m missing.


**Electrical**


I once limped a Porsche 924 Turbo to Florida with supplies to bypass a fussy fuel-pump relay. At a minimum, I bring spare fuses, a small light, and a cheap multimeter.


**Miscellaneous**


This catch-all pouch includes tie wraps, a box cutter, and my favorite new find: a multibit screwdriver with handle storage that I bought for $20 from Amazon.


**Wrenches**


Most cars rely on a handful of bolt sizes, which means you can pare down to just those size wrenches. Additionally, I include a range of sockets and consider the adjustable wrench a must-have.


**Pliers**


Locking pliers are the universal choice, and if you have the room, I recommend bringing two sizes. After those, I always include large, adjustable-jaw pliers and small wire cutters.


**Hex Keys**


These are also called Allen wrenches because the tool was patented by William Allen in 1910. Bring both metric and standard sizes.
