# Final Parking Space: 1969 Chevrolet Chevy-Van

During the 1960s, each of the Detroit Big Three offered a line of forward-control compact vans. These sturdy, simple machines became icons of their era and became very popular recipients of[the custom-van treatment](https://www.hagerty.com/media/entertainment/5-custom-van-films-thatll-make-you-wanna-boogie/) during the following decade. Of the three, the GM-built version is the hardest to find today. I spotted this half-ton Chevy cargo van at[a boneyard just south of Denver](https://coloradoautoandparts.com/#navigation) .


Volkswagen


The first-generation[Volkswagen Transporter](https://www.hagerty.com/media/news/volkswagen-bus-turns-75/) began production in 1950 and sold increasingly well in the United States after Volkswagen of America was established in 1955. Detroit took note, especially with the decline of[the sedan delivery](https://www.hagerty.com/marketplace/classified/1940-chevrolet-sedan-delivery/a77f3e4e-0512-474f-8ce8-3abd2b9cb3ff) during that decade.


[Murilee Martin](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/mmartin/)


GM created a line of[Corvair-based forward-control trucks](https://www.thetruthaboutcars.com/cars/down-on-the-junkyard/junkyard-find-chevrolet-corvair-trucks-44512483) with rear-mounted air-cooled engines, which it sold for the 1961 through 1965 model years. These were interesting machines, but sales were poor and the design proved to be something of an evolutionary dead end.


Ford Motor Company Ltd


Ford was busy cooking up a compact forward-control van of its own at the same time, and Dearborn had a major advantage thanks to its operations in the United Kingdom. Under the Thames brand, Ford Motor Company Limited began selling compact forward-control trucks[beginning in 1957](https://cvcmag.com/commercials/the-versatile-thames-400e-range) . The Thames 400E family proved to be a strong seller, competing for sales against[GM’s Bedford CA](https://www.autominded.net/bedfordcalvh1959.htm) .


Ford Motor Company


Ford took the chassis of its versatile new compact car,[the Falcon](https://www.hagerty.com/media/opinion/final-parking-space/final-parking-space-1960-ford-falcon-fordor-wagon/) , replaced the independent front suspension with a solid kingpin front axle, and moved the engine back a few feet. The body was a simple box, with shape and styling seemingly inspired by the Thames 400E. The cargo version of this new van was called the Econoline, while passenger versions were badged as[Falcon Club Wagons](https://www.thetruthaboutcars.com/2022/05/junkyard-find-1966-ford-econoline-falcon-club-wagon/) and Falcon Station Buses. It first hit American Ford showrooms as a 1961 model, same as the Corvair vans.


Chrysler Corporation


The Ford vans sold very well even as GM struggled to move Corvans, and Chrysler wanted to grab a slice of that revenue pie. The[Dodge A100](https://www.hagerty.com/media/opinion/final-parking-space/final-parking-space-pair-of-dodge-a100-vans/) was the result, and it followed the Econoline formula with even beefier construction. The first model year for this van was 1964.


[Murilee Martin](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/mmartin/)


Meanwhile, the General was working on its own rival for the E-Series Ford. This was the first of a long line of G-Series vans, and it debuted as a 1964 model. The Corvair trucks continued to be produced through 1965, so Chevrolet buyers could choose between vans with air-cooled engines in the back and water-cooled engines in the front for a couple of years.


General Motors


The 1964-1970 Chevrolet G-Series cargo vans[were called Chevy-Vans](https://oldcarbrochures.org/United%20States/GM%20Trucks%20and%20Vans/1969_Trucks_and_Vans/1969-Chevy-Van-and-Sportvan-Brochure/index.html) (the hyphen was dropped later on), while the passenger versions were known as Sportvans. GMC dealers got their own G-vans, called the Handi-Van (cargo) and Handi-Bus (passenger).


General Motors


The vans of all three companies used the same layout: solid front axle, leaf springs at both ends, engine mounted in a doghouse between the front seats. At first, all three had straight-six engines, but Chrysler added an optional V-8 for 1965, and GM followed suit two years later.


General Motors


These vans could carry an outlandish amount of weight relative to their small footprints, and the forward-control design made them easy to pull up to cramped loading docks and into short parking spaces (the driver could look out the side window and see the left front wheel directly below).


General Motors


On the minus side, they were crude, noisy, ill-handling brutes that roasted front-seaters’ inside legs against their sizzling hot engine doghouses. With undersized drum brakes all the way around and no front crush space to speak of, safety was a concern even by 1960s standards.


General Motors


Each of the Big Three upgraded their vans as the 1960s became the 1970s. Ford was first, introducing[a new design](https://www.hagerty.com/media/opinion/final-parking-space/final-parking-space-1974-ford-econoline-e-200-supervan/) for the 1969 model year with a short snout to allow the engine to be moved forward to a position above the front axle instead of behind it.[GM](https://www.thetruthaboutcars.com/cars/down-on-the-junkyard/junykard-find-1972-gmc-vandura-pacific-telephone-edition-45135040) and Chrysler followed with[similar vans](http://autoweek.com/article/junkyard-treasures/junkyard-treasure-mid-night-cruzer-1976-dodge-custom-van) for the 1971 model year. Once Ford[lengthened its vans’ noses](http://www.thetruthaboutcars.com/2018/01/junkyard-find-1977-ford-econoline-150-campaign-van/) again for 1975, all three companies stuck[with](https://www.autoblog.com/features/junkyard-gem-1990-ford-econoline-vibe-palage) essentially[the](https://www.autoblog.com/news/junkyard-gem-1997-dodge-ram-2500-vanlife-edition/) same[vans](https://www.autoblog.com/features/junkyard-gem-1995-chevrolet-chevy-van-g20/) well into the 1990s.


General Motors


The GM G-Series vans got a facelift with a new curved windshield for the 1967 model year, along with a bigger engine bay to fit the small-block V-8. Otherwise, they stayed the same as their predecessors.


[Murilee Martin](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/mmartin/)


Before we wrap up our brief history of the forward-control Detroit vans of the 1960s, we need to address the topic of the Mystery Machine van from the original “Scooby-Doo, Where Are You!” series that aired during 1969 and 1970. We’ve all seen plenty of vans with Mystery Machine livery, and zealous aficionados of every van model sold worldwide will shout that *their* van was the original model for the Mystery Machine.


The truth is,[the original Mystery Machine](https://www.hagerty.com/media/entertainment/mysteries-of-the-scooby-doo-mystery-machine/) looks more like the Chevy-Van and the A100 Tradesman than it does the Econoline, but you could make a similar case for[the Renault Estafette](https://www.carmagazine.co.uk/car-reviews/renault/estafette-1959-1980/) or even[the UAZ-452](https://www.hagerty.com/media/news/russian-uaz-came-up-with-toughest-overlander-van/) .


[Murilee Martin](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/mmartin/)


Now let’s take a look at today’s FPS subject. The build tag says that it’s a half-ton model born in Pontiac, Michigan, and that its original engine was a six-cylinder.


[Murilee Martin](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/mmartin/)


It’s a short-wheelbase (90-inch) version with[the optional right-side cargo doors](https://oldcarbrochures.org/United%20States/GM%20Trucks%20and%20Vans/1969_Trucks_and_Vans/1969-Chevy-Van-and-Sportvan-Brochure/slides/1969_Chevy_Van_and_Sportvan-02-03.html) .


[Murilee Martin](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/mmartin/)


The original paint was light blue, but it received what was once a lurid purple paint job later in life.


[Murilee Martin](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/mmartin/)


The engine, if original, is a 230- or 250-cubic-inch OHV straight-six, rated at 140 or 155 horsepower. A 307-cubic-inch small-block V-8 with 200 horses[was available as an option](https://oldcarbrochures.org/United%20States/GM%20Trucks%20and%20Vans/1969_Trucks_and_Vans/1969-Chevy-Van-and-Sportvan-Brochure/slides/1969_Chevy_Van_and_Sportvan-10-11.html) .


[Murilee Martin](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/mmartin/)


One big benefit of a six-cylinder engine in these vans is that the exhaust manifold and cylinder head are further away from the driver’s right hip and leg than they are with the V-8. I’ve put in enough miles driving a V-8-powered A100 in summer to know the misery of the Doghouse of Fiery Pain (the engine sits a bit higher in the A100, adding to the discomfort).


[Murilee Martin](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/mmartin/)


The transmission is a column-shift manual. Most of these vans had three-speed manuals, but you could get a *four* -on-the-tree manual as an option during the 1968-1970 model years (if anyone can think of another American four-on-the-tree-equipped vehicle, let us know in the comments). Two-speed Powerglide and three-speed Turbo Hydra-Matic automatics were also available when this van was sold.


[Murilee Martin](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/mmartin/)


To reach the heater/vent/defroster controls, the driver had to reach under the steering wheel.


[Murilee Martin](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/mmartin/)


It had wood paneling at one point.


[Murilee Martin](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/mmartin/)


The 1997 Colorado emissions-test sticker shows that it was a runner in 1997.


[Murilee Martin](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/mmartin/)


The instruments are extremely basic.


[Murilee Martin](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/mmartin/)


There’s rust in the usual spots.


[Murilee Martin](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/mmartin/)


It appears that a tree fell on it and mashed the left front portion of the roof.


[Murilee Martin](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/mmartin/)


These vans have been just about extinct in junkyards for the last 15 or so years. Prior to today’s FPS van, I’ve documented just[a nicely customized ’65](https://www.murileemartin.com/DOTJ/65ChevyVan/DOTJ-65ChevyVan.html) , a[’69 long-wheelbase Sportvan](https://www.thetruthaboutcars.com/2016/05/junkyard-find-1969-chevrolet-chevyvan-108/) , and[a ’69 RV conversion](https://www.autoblog.com/news/junkyard-gem-1969-chevrolet-chevyvan-camper/) . All of them were as rough as this Chevy-Van.


[Murilee Martin](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/mmartin/)
