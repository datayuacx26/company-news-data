# Take a Look Inside the GR GT’s Aluminum Chassis—a Toyota First

With no Toyota emblems anywhere to be found, the GR GT is the first car by the automaker to be marketed solely under the Gazoo Racing brand. That’s not the only groundbreaking step for the car; perhaps most notable is its extensive use of aluminum in the chassis. Toyota gave spectators at the Rolex Monterey Motorsports Reunion an up-close look at the aluminum castings that make up the bulk of the chassis of the[GR GT](https://www.hagerty.com/media/news/toyota-gr-gt-supercar-specs-pictures-details/) and its racing sibling, the GR GT3. They also gave us a peek at the drivetrain.


The chassis, which uses nine large aluminum castings that are welded to aluminum tubing and extrusions, is a first for any Toyota product. The castings are an extension of Toyota’s engine casting expertise. Takashi Doi, the Project General Manager of the GR GT and GR GT3, told us that the low-pressure casting requires incredible precision, as the aluminum must flow through narrow passages in the molds around the cured sand cores.


The GR GT uses nine major chassis castings. The three that make up the engine crossmemeber and front suspension mounts have already been joined here.


[Brandan Gillogly](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/brandan-gillogly/)


The castings are machined where aluminum beams and extrusions will be welded to complete the structure.


[Brandan Gillogly](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/brandan-gillogly/)


The GR GT represents a major shift in thinking compared to the company’s last halo car, the Lexus LFA. Where the LFA was beautiful and artistic, crafted to evoke emotion with its Yamaha-tuned V-10 soundtrack, the GR GT is meant to be first and foremost a functional track car. The overall shape is less sculpture and more sledgehammer. We have to say, however, that its form-follows-function look has its own elegance.


[Brandan Gillogly](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/brandan-gillogly/)


Toyota is aware that the LFA is loved by enthusiasts for its looks, performance, sound, technology, and engineering. However, its beautifully woven carbon-fiber structure is also a liability. How many LFA owners want to take a risk on track, knowing that skipping off the wall could easily result in a complete loss because there is no way to repair that carbon-fiber structure? In contrast, Toyota is currently developing procedures to ensure that the GR GT’s chassis is repairable. It expects these cars to be driven hard.


There are still a lot of questions about the GR GT, and Toyota is keeping plenty of secrets. For example, the total output of the twin-turbo 4.0-liter V-8 has not yet been announced, although it is promised to be at least 641 hp and 627 lb-ft of torque. There’s also no word on exactly how many of the limited production run will be allocated to North America. So far, the number being discussed is 200-250 units. Andrew Gilleland, Senior Vice President of Toyota Motor North America’s Automotive Operations Group, said he will speak with each of those potential 200-250 buyers personally to get the GR GT into the hands of enthusiasts who will use it as intended. If you might be one of them, it’s probably time to brush up on your track driving skills. In the meantime, have a look at some of the technical details that Toyota was willing to divulge in the photos of the chassis and components below.


We also had the opportunity to take a look at a cutaway of the GR GT’s rear-mounted eight-speed automatic transaxle. The transmission sits behind the centerline of the rear wheels, and bevel-cut gears transmit torque forward to the limited-slip differential, allowing the GR GT to keep a compact wheelbase.


[Brandan Gillogly](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/brandan-gillogly/)


Rather than a traditional dual clutch, the GR GT uses a clutched hybrid motor. The small chain-driven component is a pump for transmission fluid.


[Brandan Gillogly](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/brandan-gillogly/)


The differential has a separate pump driven off a gear attached to the ring gear side of the differential. It’s the only straight-cut gear in the car, and its position, just aft of the driver, provides some racy mechanical noise.


[Brandan Gillogly](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/brandan-gillogly/)

One of the main aims of the whole GR GT project was to get the center of gravity as low as possible. The 4.0-liter V-8’s dry-sump oiling system allows it to nestle low into the chassis. Its tank is mounted on the right-hand side, outboard and slightly rearward of the engine.[Brandan Gillogly](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/brandan-gillogly/)
