# 8 Cars We Dream of Taking to the Track

There is nothing quite like experiencing a car unbridled on a race track. Getting the chance to really open it up and push a machine, limited only by physics and fear, is the sort of activity that starts seeping into your brain stem and altering your worldview about two seconds after your first pit-out.


With the Rolex Monterey Motorsports Reunion taking place this weekend during Monterey Car Week, we can’t help but think about all of the incredible machines stretching their legs around Laguna Seca. Naturally, it got us thinking: Freed of any restraint—money, parts, location, and more—what would we pick as our dream track car?


Naturally, the replies below are about as varied as you can get, because we’re a tasteful and diverse group, if we do say so ourselves. Check out our answers, let us know who you’d want to ride shotgun with, and then tell us what you’d wheel at the track.


## Blue Oval Belter


[Sajeev Mehta](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/sajeev-mehta/)


I want to repurpose/modernize a widebody 1983–86 Ford Thunderbird stock car, tweak the suspension for road racing, and reserve some money in the future to adapt a late-model Mustang independent rear suspension, an aluminum small-block Ford V-8, and whatever modern transmission suits the needs of my local race courses. That might include a six-speed manual, because the back straight of COTA is nearby and it’s still open to the public (for now).


At first I was thinking about a period-correct Wrangler Jeans livery, but someone as dyed-in-the-wool Ford blue as myself must go with Ricky Rudd’s Motorcraft livery. Who am I kidding? I have wanted this dream to become a reality since I was but a young lad, and here’s the proof. Now ask me about my dream dragster! — *Sajeev Mehta*


## Who Said Practical Can’t Be Fast?


Volvo


I don’t want to be the guy who rocks up to a track with all the gear and no idea, so I’m going to rule out anything with “Speciale” or “GT3” in the name. Instead, I’d love to pay tribute to my favorite era of the British Touring Car Championship and build a road/track[Volvo 850 wagon](https://www.hagerty.com/media/opinion/in-the-moment-of-wagons-and-losses/) . I’m thinking bigger turbo, manual transmission, BBS wheels, and some sticky Dunlop tires for authenticity. Then I’d surprise all the rich guys with their Nomex racing booties as I launch it over the curbs. Well, I can dream, can’t I? — *Nik Berg*


## Maximum Motoring, Down to the Atom


Matt Richardson


If I’m headed to the track, my goal is to spend time driving. Wrenching and tuning are things for the home garage, not the paddock. At least at my skill and finance levels. So, easy speed would be ideal.


That’s why I’d like an Ariel Atom. Reliable and durable Honda drivetrain, and light enough that it won’t consume tires and brakes at shocking rates, at least until I get a lot more pace in my eyes and hands. It’s also small enough that I wouldn’t need a big trailer, and my big red van could easily pull the thing around. That would be a fun summer, chasing lap times across the country, rolling around in a van with an Atom in tow. Maybe one day. — *Kyle Smith*


## No Timid Dreamers Allowed


Klemantaski Collection/Getty Images


I have about 10 minutes of actual track time in my repertoire, so a dream track car for me is a very different beast from a realistic track car. Realistically, I’d be quite content with a Miata or an S2000. In dreamland, however, where I am fully licensed, monied, and well-known as “Sideways Stefan,” I would absolutely snag the ultimate drive-it-to-the-track-and-race-it-and-then-drive-it-home Ferrari with a 250 GT SWB. Yellow, please, so they see me coming. And probably[an alloy-bodied one](https://www.hagerty.com/valuation-tools/ferrari/250_gt/1960/1960-ferrari-250_gt-swb?id=aCn1I000000DCyGSAW) , since I’m already here in the configurator… — *Stefan Lombard*


## Fast and Funky? Must Be French


I’ll shoot for the stars like Stefan, though this car will need a trailer … plus a team of French mechanics and a few cartons of cigarettes, probably. The Matra MS670 was good enough to win Le Mans three times, so it should be good enough for me. It’s also fast, but not so fast that it would be terrifying and dangerous. I also find the history of the whole Matra program fascinating, which is a nice bonus. Another nice bonus is what the car does for the senses. It looks spectacular, and the 3.0-liter V-12 is definitely in the running for best-sounding engine ever made. — *Andrew Newton*


## Seven Liters of Bowtie Basher


Chevrolet


In this dream scenario, I don’t have to think about the frightening cost of tires, brakes, or fuel. But still, my preference is always for a car that can drive itself to and from any track event. With that said, I want to indulge my inner hedonist with 7.0 liters of American fury—a[2014 Chevy Camaro Z/28](https://www.hagerty.com/media/lists/highs-and-lows-of-the-camaro-by-generation/comment-page-3/) . This was a factory road-course weapon sporting the aforementioned[LS7 V-8](https://www.hagerty.com/media/maintenance-and-tech/ls-lt-encyclopedia-modern-gm-small-block-v-8s-part-2/) , good for 505 hp. That’s more than enough to get the blood pumping but not so much as to be truly dangerous.


This being the Al Oppenheiser era in the halls of GM performance, the chassis got a load of attention, too: Multimatic dynamic spool valve dampers, carbon-ceramic brakes, and massive 305-series Pirelli Trofeo R tires. (Those tires are sketchy as hell in the wet, so you better believe I’d drive to the track with different shoes on the car and the big meats on a small trailer.) On top of all that was a serious diet in the form of weight reduction, which meant no standard stereo or A/C.


When new, this car sat untouched in showrooms, because it was $20,000 more than a base Corvette while being far less handsome and far less civilized. For me, though, it was a fearsome tool that harnessed GM’s arsenal for one thing: track dominance. To wit, the Z/28 could compete corner to corner with six-figure exotics. If I’m after a track car that feels special and I trust to hold up in the most demanding conditions, I’m picking the fifth-gen Camaro Z/28 every time. — *Eric Weiner*


## Dashing in a Dark Horse


Ford | Michael Simari


A Ford Mustang Dark Horse with a manual. I have seldom felt so comfortable, so quickly, in a performance car. An alternative would be—and I’m sorry to be so predictable—an NC Mazda Miata. I enjoy thrashing without trashing, and Miatas just seem to take a lickin’ and keep on lappin.’ — *Steven Cole Smith*


## Transfixed by Trans-Am


[Nathan Petroelje](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/nathan-petroelje/)


My knee-jerk reaction here was to go rational with something like a Miata or a Toyota GR86, but after Stefan proffered that Ferrari, well … I cannot help but think back to watching the[Historic Trans-Am cars rip around Laguna Seca](https://www.hagerty.com/media/motorsports/vintage-racing-at-laguna-seca-was-so-good-it-made-me-sick/) back in 2023 at the Rolex Monterey Motorsports Reunion.


The way those things shuffled and humped over curbs, the way they sounded (Oh, the way they sounded!), and even just the liveries on the cars themselves, I was transfixed. You drive them off the back outside wheel, dancing on a razor’s edge of slip and speed that makes it plainly obvious to spectators that you are absolutely hoofin’ the thing. I cannot imagine a better way to have fun on a racetrack than to go thundering around in one of these. Give me a bright blue 1967 Camaro with white racing stripes, plenty of race gas, and about three more sets of tires than I initially say would be required. We’re goin’ *driving driving* . — *Nathan Petroelje*
