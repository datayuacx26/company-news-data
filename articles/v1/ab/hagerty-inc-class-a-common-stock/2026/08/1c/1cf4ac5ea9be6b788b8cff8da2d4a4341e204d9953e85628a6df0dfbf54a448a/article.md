# Gallery: Bonneville Speed Week 2026

Each year, racers and spectators flock to the western edge of Utah to participate in one of America’s oldest and proudest racing traditions: Bonneville Speed Week. This year’s event kicked off on the Bonneville Salt Flats with the drivers’ meeting, where the congregation paused for a moment of silence for the members of the land speed community who had been lost since the last meeting, including racer[Chris Rasche.](https://www.hagerty.com/media/news/speed-demon-driver-chris-raschke-killed-in-bonneville-crash/) Representatives from the Southern California Timing Association (SCTA) shared the microphone with representatives from the Bureau of Land Management and the Save the Salt foundation, updating everyone on the conditions of the racing surface.


[Brandan Gillogly](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/brandan-gillogly/)


The highlight of the meeting was the presentation of the Hot Rod Magazine Trophy, given each year to the single fastest flying mile of the previous Speed Week. John McGann, editor of Hot Rod magazine, had the honor of awarding the trophy to Danny Thompson, who joined his father, Mickey, in claiming a spot on the historic award. Thompson, jokingly but endearingly, said it was “such an honor to get the George Poteet trophy,” referring to the late land-speed racer who had achieved the fastest Speed Week time more than any other racer. Poteet had battled with the younger Thompson for the honor at Speed Week for several years.


Danny Thompson took the microphone to accept the Hot Rod Magazine Trophy. To his right is car owner Don Ferguson, and on his left is Hot Rod editor John McGann.


[Brandan Gillogly](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/brandan-gillogly/)


Judging by the rows of race pits and the lineup at the two long courses, the car count was lower than last year. Also, high heat early in the week may have kept some racers from going all-out, waiting instead for cooler temps later in the week to have the best shot at a record. Still, we captured plenty of starting line action. Here are just a few of our favorite cars hitting the salt as well as some of the sights in the staging lanes and pits.

Many cars need a push to get going due to the high gearing required for top-speed runs.


The most conspicuous arrival on the salt was the JCB team and its hydrogen-powered, twin-engine streamliner. One engine powers each set of wheels. The team is competing in the gasoline class against Amir Rosenbaum’s 2010 AA Blown Gas Streamliner record of 348.342mph.


[Brandan Gillogly](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/brandan-gillogly/)

Racing is still underway on the salt, and it’s often not until the final day that the meet’s top speed is recorded. The early leader was the Strasburg/Wolf Blown Fuel Lakester, recording a speed of 382.528mph on Saturday. After some technical trouble, including a completely shredded tire, Danny Thompson and the Ferguson streamliner team captured the lead with a 396.949mph run on Tuesday. We’ll keep our eyes on the salt to see which team comes out on top in 2026.
