# The Cars of Friday

It’s Friday. I don’t have a job (until Monday, anyway). And I don’t have much to do (except write this thing).


That introduction, and the *extremely* poor paraphrasing employed, is all to say that I just rewatched the 1995 buddy stoner flick *Friday* , and the movie’s very first line, as delivered by Chris Tucker’s character Smokey, seemed like a fine place to begin. And what’s the point of writing about a 1995 buddy stoner flick, exactly?


Welp, I hadn’t seen *Friday* since it came out in theaters, and watching it again this week I was not only happy to reacquaint myself with that world, I was also struck by the variety and eccentricity of the film’s cars, which was a detail I had not stored in my brain lo these many years.


The premise of the movie, directed by F. Gary Gray, is simple: For starters, it’s Friday. Beyond that, 22-year-old Craig, portrayed by rapper Ice Cube (who co-wrote and helped produce the film), has just lost his job. His best friend Smokey takes up the challenge to get Craig high. They essentially spend the day on Craig’s front stoop discussing life, liberty, the pursuit of happiness, and Smokey’s problematic life choices. Along the way, a rotating ensemble of characters—and their cars—enter and exit the frame.


These are the standouts…


## A Herd of Impalas


New Line Cinema


Both a ’61 and a ’63 Impala convertible appear in *Friday* . The former is owned and driven by the character Big Worm (with a vanity plate that says as much). Big Worm, with his big perm, is Smokey’s supplier, and his intro to the movie comes about 10 minutes in, when he rolls up in his lowrider and drops the hydraulics to have a chat with Smokey, who is late on his payment. The car is finished in a fetching orange with a white stripe. Friday isn’t its only on-screen appearance, however. It also shows up in the music video for Ice Cube’s 2008 single “Do Ya Thang.”

The ’63 is owned by Smokey’s nemesis (Smokey has several nemeses, it seems) Hector. He rolls into frame in his green and gold lowrider around the half-hour mark, cornering on three wheels before nearly running down Pastor Clever, played by Bernie Mac (who has just arrived at Craig’s house in a slick ’81 Cadillac Seville). The car reappears in a flashback as Smokey rides in the back seat while cruising town with Hector, before he ends up running down the street in his underwear and hiding out in a pigeon loft…


## Not Your Uncle’s C3


New Line Cinema


Craig’s neighbor Stanley is the most manicured character of the whole movie. The landscaping of his lawn is perfect. The landscaping of his hair is perfect. And the landscaping on the front end of his C3 Corvette is perfect. Why? Because this one sports a Greenwood “Daytona” conversion.


New Line Cinema


John Greenwood turned his racing success with Corvettes on track in the 1960s and ’70s into a successful aftermarket business for street cars, with wild bodies and a limited run of turbocharged cars. Most of the fiberglass Daytona kits—probably including this one—were produced by Florida Corvette specialist Eckler’s. Stanley’s car is complete with a deep front valance, open headlights (quad squares in place of the quad round pop-ups), twin NACA ducts, and a big ol’ finned hood bulge to house some menacing built motor. Sadly, Stanley’s Vette is all show, no go, because at one point he gets in and starts it, and it sounds like… nothing at all.


## A Klockau Classic!


New Line Cinema


Craig and Smokey’s place on the front stoop gives them a great view of the neighborhood, including the across-the-street neighbors, Mr. and Mrs. Parker. The drama unfolds when Mr. Parker, played by Tony Cox, arrives home to find Pastor Clever and Mrs. Parker (Kathleen Bradley), uh, otherwise engaged. Hilarity ensues, but in the moments beforehand, we are treated to the excellent sight of Mr. Parker’s lovely[1972 Cadillac DeVille](https://www.hagerty.com/media/opinion/klockau-classics/1972-cadillac-fleetwood-brougham-sable-black-stunner/) . Channeling[my inner Klockau](https://www.hagerty.com/media/category/opinion/klockau-classics/) here, I’ll go so far as to claim this one wears a lovely shade of blue called St. Moritz Firemist Poly, which is darker and deeper than Zodiac Blue Iridescent. Underhood was a 472-cubic-inch V-8 making 220 SAE net horsepower.


## One Hero Car to Rule Them All


New Line Cinema


Smokey is by far the weirdest, funniest character in *Friday* , and in retrospect, no one but Chris Tucker could have infused him with so much humor, irreverence, and zing. The car he drives is the perfect embodiment of the character: a[Ford Pinto](https://www.hagerty.com/media/motorsports/how-the-pinto-became-nascars-regional-weapon-of-choice/) convertible. I hear you, reader: “Ford never made a Pinto convertible!” Nope, it sure didn’t. For good reason, too. In trying to track down any information on this contraption, which looks to be a ’76 with a ’75 grille, I turned to Hagerty’s resident movie-car finder,[Jamie Kitman](https://www.hagerty.com/media/category/opinion/picture-car-confidential/) , who, after confessing he hadn’t seen the movie and wasn’t familiar with the car, said what we’re all thinking: “OMG it must’ve been a death trap.”


New Line Cinema


Google is little help in discovering the origins of this or any other cut Pinto, which is probably for the best. No one needs more information about this. Curiously, the car’s paint job somewhat resembles that of the A-Team van—black with a red stripe angling up the side to finish in a big red spoiler across the back. We never see the car with the top up, but the framework is clearly there to raise and lower it, so we’ll just have to imagine a rainy day in LA and Smokey scrambling to stay dry in it.


The hood wobbles, the car backfires constantly, smokes profusely, and has great difficulty either coming to a stop or staying in gear when Smokey tries to park it. But clearly he loves it; when Smokey and Craig arrive back home after a quick trip to the corner store, he tells Craig, valet-style: “Watch my car, make sure nobody mess with it,” before he makes a show of setting the alarm with its trademark double-beep. To my knowledge, Smokey’s Pinto has never appeared in one of Ice Cube’s rap videos.


## Honorable Mention

New Line Cinema


Note the Toronado and GMC truck beyond Smokey’s Pinto and Pastor Clever’s Seville, respectively.


Plenty of other sweet rides make cameos in *Friday* . The car that slow-rolls past Craig’s house just before a drive-by shooting is a Fox-body Mustang GT convertible. Craig’s dog-catcher dad, played by a finger-lickin’ John Witherspoon, keeps his 1974 Dodge Kary Van parked in the driveway, right behind his mom’s black Volvo 740 Turbo. Craig’s sister gets dropped off in a 1987 Nissan 300ZX 2+2. And two background carport stars include an early ’70s GMC pickup and a ’67 Oldsmobile Toronado.

*Friday* isn’t a car movie. It is absolutely a buddy stoner movie. But, as the idiom goes, “choices were made,” and for the enthusiasts among us, those choices represent a fun opportunity to do some car spotting.
