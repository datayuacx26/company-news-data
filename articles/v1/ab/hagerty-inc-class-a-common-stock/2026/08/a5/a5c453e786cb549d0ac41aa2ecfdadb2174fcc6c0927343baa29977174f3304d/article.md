# 1975 Pontiac Grand Ville Brougham Convertible: Last Call for the Big Drop-Top!

I love Grand Villes. Knowing nothing about them, never seeing one, a person could still infer that it is something special. And big and luxurious. And they would be right! For a few brief years in the ’70s, the vaunted Bonneville became second banana, with the Grand Ville filling the top slot at Pontiac dealers everywhere.


[Thomas Klockau](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/tklockau/)


And the Grand Villes were, well, grand in every sense of the word. Whether the four-door hardtop, two-door coupe, or flossy, glossy convertible, all Grand Villes were 226″ long, with a 123.4″ wheelbase and Wide-Track 79.6″ width.


[Thomas Klockau](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/tklockau/)


And as the top of the line, they got Pontiac’s best and largest engine: A 455-CID unit with a four-barrel carburetor, producing 200 hp at 3500 rpm. GM’s terrific Turbo Hydramatic automatic transmission was also standard, of course.


Thomas Klockau


Starting in 1971, the Grand Ville replaced the Bonneville and Bonneville Brougham as Pontiac’s finest. But while it was certainly attractive and definitely luxurious, the name apparently didn’t have the same cachet as “Bonneville.”


Thomas Klockau


As a result, 1975 was the last year for the Grand Ville. And while the car had been known as just “Grand Ville” from 1971-74, in 1975 only it became the “Grand Ville Brougham.” But of course. It was certainly Broughamtastic.


[Thomas Klockau](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/tklockau/)


And while what was the ’75 Grand Ville Brougham would become, in 1976, the Bonneville Brougham, complete with the C-body style roof on coupes and sedans and the same excellent button-tufted interior in your choice of velour or Morrokide vinyl, the convertible would not return. Yep, 1975 was your last chance for top-down Broughaming in a big Pontiac.


[Thomas Klockau](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/tklockau/)


But what a way to go! They were certainly luxurious, smooth, quiet, and stylish. Like all 1975 Grand Ville Broughams and Bonnevilles, a new, very Cadillac-like front end was prominent, with cool new quad rectangular headlamps.


Thomas Klockau


Out back, revised full-width taillights now wrapped around the rear quarter panels, visible from the side. And the interior was just as plush as ever.


Thomas Klockau


And since we’re talking U.S. cars in the ’70s, there were exterior colors galore. Our featured car, seen at the 2016 Pontiac Nationals, just minutes away from Klockau Manor in Bettendorf, Iowa, was especially pretty in pastel Arctic Blue, with a blue interior with white seats. Very yacht clubbish.


Thomas Klockau


While it may look a little unusual, in 1975 one could order a GM car, depending on the model, with an all-blue, red, or black interior, with only the seats white. It is not seen frequently, but it is correct. On the other hand, you could still get a white interior with just the dash and carpeting a contrasting color. Either option looks pretty good on a convertible, in my opinion!


[Thomas Klockau](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/tklockau/)


Just 4519 1975 Grand Ville Brougham convertibles were built, but many have survived. And with this column, I have now written up the entire 1975 Grand Ville Brougham model lineup,[with the sedan](https://www.hagerty.com/media/opinion/klockau-classics/1975-pontiac-grand-ville-brougham-local-luxury/) and[two-door coupe](https://www.hagerty.com/media/opinion/klockau-classics/1975-pontiac-grand-ville-brougham-coupe-pontiacs-cadillac/) previously getting their own writeups. But of course, there’s always something extra special about a convertible. As the old commercials said, Ride, Pontiac Ride!
