# Toyota TRD 3000GT Is a Rare, Race-Inspired Supra, and It Could Be Yours

The Mk IV Toyota Supra has become one of the most valuable and sought-after Japanese sports cars of its era, but some variants of it are rarer than others. Broad Arrow[will auction off](https://www.broadarrowauctions.com/vehicles/ql26_r0201/1994-toyota-trd-3000gt) what many consider one of the most desirable Mk IVs during its The Quail Auction 2026: It’s one of 35 Toyota TRD 3000GT examples built, and it’s the only one based on an American-market car.


Production of the Mk IV (A80-gen) Supra started in 1993, and aftermarket tuners weren’t the only ones eager to modify it. Toyota used the platform to create a race car for the inaugural Japanese Grand Touring Championship to help cement the model’s performance credentials. Meanwhile, Toyota Racing Development (TRD) drew inspiration from the track-bound Supra to develop a more powerful street-legal evolution of the A80. Known as the TRD 3000GT, it made its global debut at the 1994 edition of the Tokyo Auto Salon.


The modifications made included wider wheels tucked under punched-out wheel arches, a larger intercooler hidden behind a redesigned front bumper, a model-specific hood with four cooling vents, and a body kit. Broad Arrow explains that these parts were available individually through TRD, so anyone could buy a hood or a rear spoiler and put it on their Supra. Only 35 units were fully converted in-house by Toyota and fitted with a TRD 3000GT identification plate, however. This is one of those cars.

Assigned chassis number JT2JA82J5R0011586, the example in Broad Arrow’s catalog started life as a 1994 Supra RZ fitted with the six-speed manual transmission. It was delivered new in California, where it was purchased by a Japanese tourist visiting the United States. The first owner shipped it back to Japan in early 1994 and had it converted to full TRD 3000GT spec in May 2001.


The car stayed in the Tokyo area until it was sold to a Danish collector in 2007. The current owner purchased it in 2015, and it has covered about 1500 miles over the last 11 years. Its odometer consequently shows about 68,000 miles, which is fairly low for a 32-year-old car. It hasn’t been kept fully stock, however. Broad Arrow reports several period-correct modifications such as an HKS Vein Pressure Converter and Graphic Control Computer ECU, Recaro seats, carbon fiber interior trim, and Defi gauges.


With the modifications, the twin-turbocharged, 3.0-liter straight-six makes nearly 500 horsepower according to a dyno test done in Denmark in 2009. That’s a big jump over the stock Supra’s 320-horse rating, but it’s not entirely surprising considering[the massive amount of power](https://www.hagerty.com/media/news/dr-frankensteins-e-type-is-a-750-hp-toyota-2jz-powered-monster/) that the famed 2JZ-GTE engine is capable of handling. This TRD 3000GT retains its six-speed manual gearbox.


Broad Arrow notes that this Toyota TRD 3000GT is offered on international documents, and that getting a U.S. title will require additional processing time. For peace of mind, the sale includes a Carfax report that lists a single entry in 1993 and a clean CarVX report (think of it as the Japanese equivalent to Carfax) that lists no accidents, mileage inconsistencies, or other potential problems.


This rare piece of Toyota history is estimated to sell for between $500,000 and $800,000 when it crosses the block[on August 13](https://www.broadarrowauctions.com/events/event/The%20Quail%20Auction%202026) .
