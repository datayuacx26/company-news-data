# The 2027 Dark Horse SC Convertible Is the Most Powerful Open-Air Mustang Ever

The last time Ford offered a truly range-topping, bonkers-power Mustang convertible, the year was 2013. Daft Punk’s “Get Lucky” was on every radio station, and each new episode of *Breaking Bad* or *The Walking Dead* was considered appointment television. The drop-top Shelby GT500 was busy cooking rear tires thanks to its 5.8-liter, 662-hp supercharged V-8, paired with a Tremec six-speed manual. Thirteen years later, Ford is introducing the Mustang Dark Horse SC convertible—a similar concept albeit with fresh ingredients and a very different mission.


In case you need a reminder, “SC” stands for “Supercharged” and not “Super Coupe” or “Super Convertible” despite the oddly Thunderbird-esque script. Under the hood is the same 5.2-liter supercharged V-8 as in the standard Dark Horse, making 795 hp and 660 lb-ft of torque. That makes the SC convertible the most powerful roadgoing convertible in the Mustang’s history.


As with the coupe version, the only transmission offered is a seven-speed dual-clutch automatic. Compared with the coupe, the SC convertible has softer tuning for the MagneRide dampers, shocks, and anti-roll bars. There are minor aero changes to the hood extractor and spoiler to account for the differently shaped rear decklid. The wheels are 20-inchers, and the Pirelli P Zero R tires are the same as those used on the non-track-pack SC Coupe. ABS and ESC tuning are more reined-in, as well.

“What we’re really doing with the SC,” said Ford Mustang marketing manager Ryan Shaughnessy, “is we’re deepening Mustang’s presence in the premium sports car segment to go head-to-head with the world’s elite sports car competition, like the Porsche 911, and win.”


Those who recall the 2013 GT500 would not call it a premium 911 competitor. It was raw, angry, and in some respects unpolished;[Autoweek](https://www.autoweek.com/drives/a1913536/2013-ford-mustang-shelby-gt500-convertible-review-notes-662-hp-convertible/) ** considered the chassis “tight” and “unsettled at times,” and noted that it was “basically undriveable” in the rain due to lack of traction. Having spent time in a[GT500 from this era](https://www.hagerty.com/media/market-trends/hagerty-insider/market-spotlight-2012-shelby-gt500/) , the interior isn’t so different from ordinary Mustangs of the period. It’s purposeful, functional, and fitting for a classic American muscle machine.

This time around, the Mustang Dark Horse SC convertible is making a real effort to lure in customers who want to make a visual statement on the street, rather than a numbers statement on the track. “If the coupe is the race-bred side of our DNA, this convertible is the pure street cred part of the formula,” said Mark Rushbrook, global director, Ford Racing, in a statement. “It’s just pure fun, fast, freedom.”

The attractive Precision Purple exterior paint on the vehicle we saw under studio lights shimmers and glints as you move around the car. The unique brake caliper color is a very Porsche-like customization detail, and upscale interior details available in Black Onyx and Space Gray—along with several seat belt colors and the option of Mustang GTD-like carbon trim—blow away anything one could imagine in an early 2010s Shelby product.


Ford is reentering this space because it believes, simply put, that there is a market for it. “Our base Mustang, like EcoBoost and GT, on a blended basis, about one in five vehicles is a convertible.” Shaughnessy shared with Hagerty. He would not comment on a target volume or take rate for the SC convertible. “We’ll build to customer demand; customers have asked us to do something like this on a Dark Horse.”

When asked how much softer the convertible is than the SC coupe, Mustang Dark Horse SC chief engineer Arie Groeneveld would not provide exact figures but promises superb driving dynamics. “Despite the softening of some of the elements to make that ultimate grand touring vehicle, the handling is outstanding,” he told us.


And, based on our recent drive of[Ford’s ultra-high-performance supercharged 5.0-liter V-8 Mustang](https://www.hagerty.com/media/new-car-reviews/factory-tuned-we-drive-fords-800-hp-mustang-700-hp-f-150-and-300-hp-maverick/) —to say nothing of the most recent supercharged GT500’s excellent road manners—we don’t expect the 2013 GT500 convertible’s rowdiness to persist here. Despite the huge numbers, this promises to be a pleasant, driveable package for cruising around Miami, Los Angeles, and the like.

Competition in this space includes the aforementioned Euro brands: Porsche with its various topless 911s (all the way up to the GT3 S/C and 911 Turbo cabriolet), Aston Martin with its Vantage convertible, and Mercedes-AMG with its SL 63 and SL 63 E-Performance. On this side of the pond—absent any comparable Camaro at present—it’s the drop-top versions of the Corvette Z06 and ZR1 that most line up with the Dark Horse SC convertible.


Pricing isn’t yet available, and orders will begin this fall with deliveries in spring 2027. Given the Dark Horse SC’s $108,485 starting price, we expect something several thousand dollars on top of that.
