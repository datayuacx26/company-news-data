# Reputation Management: A Very Specific Set of Skills

*Welcome back to[Reputation Management](https://www.hagerty.com/media/category/advice/reputation-management/) ! We are again revealing the underbelly of local automotive retailers—places like car dealers and service shops—with fictional service tales inspired by real customer reviews. How do we make these stories seem like they could really happen? Because of my years of experience with a Fortune 500 automotive retailer as a—you guessed it—reputation manager.* *—Sajeev*


Ron, my buddy from when I was an engineering student at UT Austin, is certainly a clever fella. With no solicitation on my part, he backdoor registered me into math and science classes with the best professors and the nicest lab facilities, a perk normally reserved for honor students like himself. We’d entertain ourselves with jokes and insults when the material got too dry, with daily recaps in his 1985 Mustang GT convertible that’d put a YouTube reaction video to shame. Sure, we were young and dumb like all college kids, but Ron was clearly the smartest guy in the room.


Mensa smart, or so I once thought. But, as I later learned, he’s in the[Triple Nine Society](https://www.triplenine.org/WhatisTNS.aspx) . That explains how Ron lived it up in college while never leaving the Dean’s List. His genius all but ensured a fruitful career in petroleum engineering, but he still needed my help after graduation to buy a new Mustang GT convertible back in 2001.


My premature departure from the bubble that is UT Austin’s famous “forty acres” forever altered our friendship. As we sat in the small Ford dealership where he was purchasing his soon-to-be prized possession, Ron noticed my mind was wandering. Perhaps he felt a twinge of nostalgia at my departure from engineering (and complications from my current level of unemployment), because what he said next never left me:


> *“You are destined for greatness, Sajeev. We just don’t know how it will manifest yet.”*


## ***


The year is now 2023, and Ron has been following the Reputation Manager’s exploits from his Facebook newsfeed. His once-pristine Mustang GT is now in dire need of restoration, as Ron’s focus has been on his three children and a series of C-suite successes at oil & gas businesses around Houston. The money didn’t change him, as he generally preferred a modest, almost humble lifestyle.


Mutual career appreciation aside, I still enjoyed hanging out with the dude. And the Reputation Manager has some pull in the car business, so it’s possible that I could take his Mustang under my wing, and have one of “my” Ford dealerships do most of the mechanical restoration work on our behalf.


Ford


I made the cross-town trek to visit Ron with this notion in mind, highlighting the discount on parts and labor I could provide to the project. But there was one fly in the ointment: Ron needed his 2006 F-150 SuperCrew back from his local Ford dealer, so the Mustang could take a well-deserved vacation. I had assumed the brand-new F-150 sitting in his driveway was a nice little treat for two decades of success, but it was merely a loaner truck.


Ford

His loyalty to his two older Fords was admirable, raising his stock in my book. And I fondly recall fixing his truck with parts I procured from “my” dealer, because that was an afternoon of poorly un-smashing bumper brackets and earning a little cred as a Reputation Manager who actually buys parts from the stores he manages. It’s one thing to be a clouted-up corporate employee, but it’s a different ball game when you’re also a customer.


What was supposed to be a quick afternoon of exchanging one truck for another, running errands, and reminiscing along the way went sideways in a hurry. Perhaps it was nobody’s fault, but the dealership triggered an unexpected avalanche. “We are sorry, sir, but we have no record of you bringing your truck in,” said the service advisor.


“I gave it to you and you gave me this new F-150! What on earth is going on here?” Ron exclaimed.


“Sir, please calm down. ***Are you sure you are at the right Ford dealership?*** “


Something changed in Ron’s eyes, and his facial expressions went flat. He didn’t lash out; rather, he looked within and didn’t like something he saw. The details are too personal for this story, but the disappearance of his beloved truck was literally his last straw.


I did what any good college buddy should do and subsequently intervened. I told Ron to check out those new Mustangs he liked, which instantly lightened up his face as he left the building. I asked the service advisor to summon the store’s General Manager. She balked initially, but I reminded her that the[booster plate](https://www.hagerty.com/media/advice/piston-slap/piston-slap-a-historical-boost-from-a-booster-plate/) on the front bumper of our loaner F-150 ensured we were in the right place.


After a few minutes, the General Sales Manager (GSM) came over and introduced himself. Like all pros in the car business, he was measured with his words. I wasn’t expecting such a short explanation of the dealership’s situation, because he coldly remarked, “We have no record of your friend’s truck, so I am going to ask for the keys back to our truck.”


I doubt either of us were ready for what came out of my mouth.


> “Sir, with all due respect, even if my friend didn’t drop off his truck here, we still have to pick up his special needs child from school in—checks clock—20 minutes using the truck you previously gave him. Your concerns must wait for another day. The situation at hand is like that movie where the main character bragged about his ‘Very Specific Set of Skills,’ because this isn’t my first rodeo when it comes to misplaced customer vehicles.”


> “I work in public relations for CarCountry, but forget about the calls I will make to the local media if we must Uber to that child’s school. Instead focus on how a high ranking CarCountry employee with friends at Ford will spread the word that the GSM of this dealership lost a father’s beat-up Ford truck used to shuttle three children. Even worse, he kicked us off the property with no transportation on a sweltering summer afternoon.”


I did my best to lay out the case for avoiding an expertly curated PR nightmare without it being a threat. Inside, I was absolutely furious, because the man who had my back in college was now in dire need of help. On that day I did my best to pay back the debt I owed for being snuck into the engineering honors college way back in the 1990s. We aren’t even, but this helped get a bit closer to equilibrium.


The GSM backed down and promised to personally sort this out. Ron was in no shape for driving, so he accompanied me as we ran errands and picked up kids. It was a brutal afternoon, and I only relaxed when I got home and ate dinner. The following morning, Ron rang me for a quick chat. He was in better spirits, and I soon figured out why when he told me what he wrote on their Google page.


Google


I read the detailed review about someone’s issues with a 2022 Chevrolet Silverado Custom and didn’t initially draw the connection. But then I remembered our antics in college, and quickly realized my buddy also doubles as a world-class internet troll. Odds are the dealership will never know who was behind the review, and it did nothing to ding their 4.4-star rating. Fair enough, I say.


Ron was right about me, as I was destined for some form of greatness. And my little stunt at the dealership did yield his truck and a profuse apology. He gave me a copy of *Vidya* , the official journal of the Triple Nine Society. It’s mostly member-generated content, and I found it surprisingly approachable and entertaining. Ron’s review of the Ford dealership was almost *Vidya-worthy* , proving that the extreme geniuses of the world are just like us: They have bad days at car dealerships too.


*The Reputation Manager will return…*
