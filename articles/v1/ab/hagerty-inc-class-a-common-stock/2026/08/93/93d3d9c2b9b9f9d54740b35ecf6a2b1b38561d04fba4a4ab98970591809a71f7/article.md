# 993-Based GXR-Evo Is a $1.35M Race Car from Gunther Werks

Founded in 2017, Gunther Werks “remasters” the final generation of Porsche’s air-cooled 911 to create its own evolutions of the sports car using modern engineering and materials. The California company has built an entire menu of boutique offerings, ranging from coupe, speedster, and Turbo models to the even-racier GWR coupe, the “experimental” and hugely customizable GXR, and[the 1000-hp, twin-turbo F-26](https://www.hagerty.com/media/news/911-resto-builder-gunther-werks-debuts-air-cooled-1000-horse-f-26/) that debuted during Monterey Car Week a year ago, nearly to the day.


What’s the California shop brought this year to woo the deep-pocketed audience that Car Week attracts? The fastest naturally aspirated car Gunther Werks has ever made, a street-legal race car called the GXR-Evo. It is limited to just 15 units, and pricing begins at $1.35M. (The car in these pictures is priced at $1.55M with add-ons, though Gunther Werks isn’t telling us what they are.)

The GXR-Evo uses a 4.0-liter, dual-overhead-cam, twin-valve flat-six developed by Rothsport, a regular partner of Gunther Werks. On paper, the engine sounds similar to the engine in the GWR, with its slide-valve throttle bodies and horsepower curve that peaks at 9000 rpm. The GXR-Evo makes 455 hp and 312 lb-ft of torque. The flat-six is backed by a “re-engineered” Getrag G50 six-speed manual that sends power to the magnesium center-lock wheels via a limited-slip diff with a carbon clutch.


Those power figures don’t raise eyebrows the way that the F-26’s force-fed four-figure output did, but what sets the GXR-Evo apart from Gunther Werks’ previous builds is its aerodynamic package and its aggressively stripped-down interior.

While Gunther Werks’ Coupe weighed 2677 pounds, and the GWR cut that figure to 2400, the GXR-Evo trims an additional 14 pounds for a dry curb weight of 2386 pounds. Like the GWR, the GXR-Evo takes advantage of a full carbon-fiber body. It has less glass, however: The rear and three-quarter windows have been replaced with carbon-fiber panels. On the rear, the window delete allowed Gunther Werks to add a shark fin, and on the three-quarter windows, to integrate additional cooling ducts for the engine. The giant rear wing is new and works in concert with a larger front splitter and canards to increase downforce.

The interior is all business, starting with the eight-point roll cage, fire-suppression system, quick-release steering wheel, and Motec digital display. A Racetech carbon-Kevlar seat and a Schroth six-point harness keep the driver secure and connected to the movements of the car. The GXR-Evo also boasts a built-in pneumatic air-jack system, and the JRZ three-way, external reservoir coilovers are easily adjustable.


Gunther Werks


To prep for track days, buyers of the GXR-Evo can develop a feel for their new Gunther Werks car using the MYSIM racing simulator that comes with the car. The sim is built to look like the car, made of aluminum and carbon fiber and fitted with the same steering wheel. Gunther Werks says that the sim is “precisely configured to mirror the dynamics of their individual vehicle.”


The GX-R EVO makes its debut today in Carmel Valley, California, at The Quail by The Peninsula, a Motorsport Gathering, whose focus on high-end, high-performance, and highly customizable vehicles makes it a perfect venue for a car like this. You tell us: If you had a cool $1.35 million and were strolling around The Quail with a glass of champagne, would you place a deposit?
