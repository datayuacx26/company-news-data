# A Dozen Can’t-Miss Honda Racers Are Headed to Monterey

It’s appropriate: The theme for this year’s Rolex Monterey Motorsports Reunion is officially a “Salute to Japanese Motorsports: A Tradition of Precision & Heritage.” So it makes perfect sense that Honda tapped its own world-class museum in Motegi, Japan, as well as the American Honda Collection Hall, its race team partners, and private owners to bring at least 27 cars and motorcycles to display, demonstrate, and race.


Iconic Honda and Acura race machines will be featured at premier Monterey Car Week events held from August 12th through the 16th at the Hagerty-backed Motorlux, The Quail, the Pebble Beach Concours d’Elegance, and the Rolex Monterey Motorsports Reunion at WeatherTech Raceway Laguna Seca, referred to hereafter as RMMR, because we’ll mention it a lot.


Honda


Those historic racing machines will represent Honda’s participation, along with hundreds of victories and multiple championships, encompassing the past eight decades in Formula 1, IndyCar, IMSA, SCCA, Baja racing, MotoGP, Dakar, AMA Supercross, the Isle of Man Tourist Trophy, Formula Xtreme and more.
“Our founder, Soichiro Honda, believed in competition as a means to improve our company, our engineers and our products and racing became a key part of the corporate culture of Honda,” and later at sister brand Acura. “Honda has won at the highest levels of motorsports here and around the world,” said David Salters, president of Honda Racing Corporation USA.


Honda


Beginning when Honda dipped a toe in the motorsports world at the Isle of Man TT motorcycle race in 1959, the company has been a force on track. It has won the Indianapolis 500 17 times and has claimed 25 Grand Prix and MotoGP Constructors’ Championships, six Formula 1 Drivers’ Championships, and six F1 Constructors’ titles. Largely that has come under Honda Racing Corporation, which was founded in 1982.


The following are our don’t-miss picks of a dozen Honda and Acura racing vehicles in bold, plus some additional suggestions, along with where they’ll be seen. These are just our favorites from a long list—there will be plenty of other interesting Honda motorsports examples to see this coming week.


Honda


***Formula 1:*** You’ll want to check out the **1986 Williams Honda FW11,** which will be turning some demonstration laps during RMMR. With the car, which won nine races with drivers Nigel Mansell and Nelson Piquet, Honda commemorates the 40th anniversary of its first F1 Constructors’ title. It’s powered by the 1.5-liter V-6 twin-turbo RA166E engine. Additionally, on display at RMMR will be the 2026 Aston Martin Racing Formula 1 car, which is powered by a Honda 1.6‑liter turbocharged V-6 hybrid unit.


Honda


***IndyCar:*** We love the fact that Honda is displaying the late **Alex Zanardi’s 1996 IndyCar,** which will be shown at RMMR at Laguna Seca, appropriate since that’s where the two-time CART champion, 30 years ago, made “[The Pass](https://youtube.com/shorts/zCJQx3shVw8?si=-ctGgeYtyJXYoNPc) ”—an incredible last-lap move overtaking Bryan Herta’s Reynard through Laguna’s legendary Corkscrew that gave Zanardi the race win. Honda will also turn some demonstration laps at RMMR in Alex Palou’s 2025 IndyCar, which took the Chip Ganassi Racing driver to eight wins, an Indianapolis 500 victory, and his fourth series championship. Also turning some demonstration laps at RMMR is the late Gil de Ferran’s 2001 IndyCar, which won the CART championship that year for Team Penske. The car is owned by Zak Brown, CEO of McLaren Racing, who bought the car this year from Roger Penske.


Honda


***IMSA:*** Here’s where Acura gets the glory, with the **Meyer Shank Racing Acura ARX-06 GTP,** which will be displayed at Motorlux and RMMR. Ushering in IMSA’s new hybrid GTP era, the car won the 2023 Rolex 24 at Daytona with drivers Tom Blomqvist, Colin Braun, Hélio Castroneves and Simon Pagenaud. The ORECA chassis is powered by the AR24e 2.4-liter twin-turbocharged V-6, which was designed, developed, and assembled by Honda Racing Corporation U.S. You’ll also see the Acura Team Penske ARX-05 Daytona Prototype displayed at RMMR; the ARX-05 raced in the IMSA WeatherTech SportsCar Championship from 2018 through 2022, after which the GTP car took over. Also displayed at The Quail and RMMR is a 2007 Acura ARX-01a, winner of the LMP2 class at the 2007 Twelve Hours at Sebring with drivers Dario Franchitti, Bryan Herta and Tony Kanaan. On display at RMMR is the Acura NSX GT3, the model that recorded 38 class wins in both IMSA and SRO. Actually racing at RMMR is the Comptech Spice Acura GTP Lights Prototype, the first Acura endurance prototype, which was driven by Parker Johnstone and powered by a modified 3.0-liter VTEC V-6, taking class wins at the 1991 and 1992 24 Hours of Daytona. And the[Acura Integra 40 Racer](https://www.hagerty.com/media/news/acura-integra-40-racer/) tribute car honors the IMSA International Sedan Series Manufacturers’ and Drivers’ Championships from 1987 to 1990. It’ll make demonstration laps at RMMR.


Honda


***24 Hours of Le Mans:*** On display at RMMR and the Concours d’Elegance is the **1996 Team Kunimitsu NSX GT2.** Following Honda’s first class win at Le Mans in 1995, driver Kunimitsu Takahashi entered the 1996 24 Hours of Le Mans as a privateer with the No. 75 NSX GT2, finishing third in the GT2 class, an impressive debut for a rookie team. It’s the only right-hand-drive NSX GT2 ever built, a configuration chosen to provide an advantage at the clockwise Circuit de la Sarthe. Also worthy of your attention is the Strakka Racing Acura ARX-01c. It will be displayed at the Concours d’Elegance, but it will also compete in the Scott Pruett Legends event at RMMR, driven by current Acura GTP IMSA driver and two-time Le Mans class winner Nick Yelloly.


Honda


***Japanese Super GT and Endurance:*** The **Raybrig NSX GT** won the 2018 and 2020 GT500 class championships in the Japanese Super GT series, and was driven by F1’s Jenson Button and Naoki Yamamoto to the 2018 title at the season finale at Twin Ring Motegi. Button himself will turn some demonstration laps at RMMR. We also recommend checking out the Honda S800 RSC, on display at RMMR. “RSC” stands for Honda Racing Service Center, the company’s racing enterprise in the late 1960s and early 1970s. A legendary machine to early Honda enthusiasts, this S800 RSC won the GT-1 class at the 1968 12 Hours of Suzuka and finished third overall with a tiny 873-cc engine that revs to 10,500 rpm.

***Touring Car:*** How can we not suggest you visit this **Honda Civic 1200** , displayed at RMMR? In 1974, Bob Boileau purchased a new Honda Civic 1200 and built the first American Honda road racing car, which would go on to win six SCCA GT5 championships. There are also a pair of Realtime Racing Acuras: One is an Acura Integra Type R, which will turn some demo laps at RMMR. From 1997 to 2002, the RealTime Acura Integra Type R dominated the SPEED Touring Car Championship, with 23 wins, five drivers’ championships and four manufacturers’ championships. The Realtime Racing Acura NSX, on display at RMMR, won eight races in the World Challenge GT series.


Honda


***Pikes Peak International Hill Climb:*** The **Acura Integra Type S DE5,** which will log some demonstration laps at RMMR, has been to the summit of Pikes Peak four times in the Time Attack 1 division; drivers include Loni Unser, Katherine Legge and Dai Yoshihara.


***Baja 1000*** : On display at RMMR is the **Honda N600 Baja,** the very first race car from American Honda. Powered by a 35-horsepower, 600-cc air-cooled two-cylinder engine, the microcar competed in the 1970 Baja 1000 driven by desert racing pioneers Dave Ekins and Bill Robertson Jr.


Moving to Honda’s two-wheel activations at Monterey Car Week:


***Grand Prix/MotoGP:*** Some good stuff here, arguably highlighted by **Nicky Hayden’s 2006 Honda RC211V,** on display at RMMR. This is Hayden’s 2006 Laguna Seca and championship-winning bike, powered by a 990-cc V-5 four-stroke engine (you read that right—it has three cylinders in the forward-facing side of the V and two cylinders in the back); it dominated the premier class with 48 wins, three rider championships and four constructor titles during its five-year run. Also on display at RMMR is the 2026 Honda RC213V, the MotoGP entry ridden by Luca Marini and Joan Mir. The RC213V has won seven constructor, six rider, and six team championships in an incredible 15-year run since it debuted in 2012. It’s powered by a Honda 1000-cc V-4 four-stroke engine and has a top speed of over 223 mph. And don’t overlook Mick Doohan’s 1997 Honda NSR500, displayed at RMMR: Doohan won the 1997 FIM Grand Prix championship on this bike on his way to five world championships. Its 500-cc two-stroke Honda NSR500 helped pioneer advanced GP technology, including the notorious “Big Bang” firing order and early electronic fuel injection. Doohan preferred the “Screamer” firing order engine, which is what this bike has. From 1984 to 2002, the NSR500 was ridden to over 100 race wins, carrying legends like Valentino Rossi, Eddie Lawson and Wayne Gardner to 10 riders’ world championships.

***Daytona 200:*** There’s a **1970 Honda CR750 replica** of Dick Mann’s bike, on display at RMMR. It’s the racing version of the world’s first modern superbike—the revolutionary four-cylinder Honda CB750—introduced in 1969. Honda entered four CR750s in the 1970 Daytona 200, and Mann went on to win the race, giving Honda its first Daytona 200 victory.


Honda


***Dakar Rally:*** Check out **Ricky Brabec’s 2020 Honda CRF450 Rally** , which will turn some demonstration laps at RMMR. Ridden to Dakar Rally victories in 2020 and 2024 by Ricky Brabec and in 2021 by Kevin Benavides, the Honda CRF450 Rally features a liquid-cooled, single-cylinder 450-cc engine and a massive multi-fuel-tank system designed for long, 250-plus-mile desert stages. Honda has won the Dakar Rally eight times.


Honda


***AMA Supercross/Motocross/Supersport:*** Check out **Jett Lawrence’s 2025 Honda CRF450R** , on display at RMMR, which was campaigned during the 2024 AMA Supercross and Pro Motocross seasons. It’s powered by a 450-cc single-cylinder SOHC, four-valve engine. Honda motorcycle racing fans will also appreciate the 2004 CBR600RR from AMA Formula Xtreme, also on display at RMMR. Miguel Duhamel was dominant in AMA Supersport and Formula Xtreme 600-cc racing from the late 1980s through the early 2000s, with the rider capturing eight national championships. Honda won a total of 11 with Duhamel, Doug Polen, Tom Kipp and Nicky Hayden. The production-based CBR600RR race bike features a 599-cc transverse inline four-cylinder, DOHC engine with powertrain, brake system, steering and suspension upgrades.
