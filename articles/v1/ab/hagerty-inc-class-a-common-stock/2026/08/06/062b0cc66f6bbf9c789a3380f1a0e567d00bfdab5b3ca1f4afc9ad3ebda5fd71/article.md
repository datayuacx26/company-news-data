# This 1963 Studebaker Avanti R2 Deserves a Revival

It’s hard to imagine the fervor with which Raymond Loewy, Bob Andrews, and Tom Kellogg must have worked to drum up the[Studebaker Avanti](https://www.hagerty.com/media/market-trends/hagerty-insider/the-long-remarkable-life-of-the-studebaker-avanti/) in just 40 days. The car, which was wild for the time and has an even wilder effect all these years since its mid-’60s appearance, looked like almost nothing else on the road when new. It was polarizing but undeniably fascinating.


Hagerty Marketplace | Gary Kessler


The production run of the original Avanti was short—from June of 1962 to December of 1963—and fewer than 5000 were produced in total at the[Studebaker plant](https://www.hagerty.com/media/automotive-history/studebaker-did-things-in-a-big-way-two-giant-cars-and-a-living-sign-you-can-still-see-from-space/) in South Bend, Indiana. Because the car is quite rare, there’s lots of incentive among Avanti fans to go to great lengths to keep the original cars running and driving despite many headwinds.


Hagerty Marketplace | Gary Kessler


Hagerty Marketplace | Gary Kessler


Which brings us to this[1963 Studebaker Avanti R2, which just sold on Hagerty Marketplace](https://www.hagerty.com/marketplace/auction/1963-Studebaker-Avanti/682895a7-7870-4851-acd6-8b06778e8e8c) for $16,585. The car is labeled as a project, but not in the way you might imagine. The car’s fiberglass bodywork wears Avanti Gold paint, and the Fawn interior looks to be in decent shape. Some exterior cosmetic imperfections, such as paint issues, chrome pitting, and rear deck staining, are noted in the listing.


Hagerty Marketplace | Gary Kessler


The intriguing bit here, though, is the drivetrain. This is an R2 model, meaning it comes equipped with a supercharger fitted to the 289-cubic-inch V-8. According to the listing details, the car starts, runs, and is drivable, but it will need a fair bit of mechanical work to be made roadworthy. Zakira’s Garage in Blue Ash, Ohio, went through the engine and discovered coolant in cylinder 8, per the listing notes. A[head gasket](https://www.hagerty.com/media/opinion/the-hack-mechanic/identifying-cracked-heads-and-blown-head-gaskets/) is required here, at minimum, says the shop.


Hagerty Marketplace | Gary Kessler


Hagerty Marketplace | Gary Kessler


Still, a mechanically inclined owner (or the right shop) should be able to get this thing back on the road again. And we hope that happens, because the rest of what’s here is a wonderful totem to the era. Features like the overhead control panel for the lights or the lever-style switches for the defroster and blower, a design trick inspired by aircraft, are a nod to the blue-sky thinking of the early 1960s. The pop-up vanity mirror inside the glovebox really helps drive home the “personal luxury coupe” aspirations that Studebaker hoped to pitch with the car.


Hagerty Marketplace | Gary Kessler


Hagerty Marketplace | Gary Kessler


A quick Google search pulls up plenty of sources for new head gaskets, which is great considering that we’d bet other, more Avanti-specific replacement parts are much harder to come by. As far as revivals go—we’ll stop short of calling this a restoration project—this one seems like a fantastic candidate for some TLC and years of driving enjoyment thereafter.
