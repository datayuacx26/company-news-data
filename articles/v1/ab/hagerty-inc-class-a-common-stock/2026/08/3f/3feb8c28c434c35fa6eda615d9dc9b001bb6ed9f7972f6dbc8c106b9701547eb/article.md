# Piston Slap: Safely Purchasing an S63 Flagship?

*Anthony* writes:


“Hi! I am about to purchase a 2017 Mercedes S63 cabriolet with 42k miles. Is it a wise practice to drop the oils? Which oils specifically? I hear mention of adding a sacrificial pigtail as well. *([We discussed these because of oil wicking](https://www.hagerty.com/media/advice/piston-slap/piston-slap-oil-wicking-electronics-and-sacrificial-pigtails/) being a problem in wiring harnesses. — SM)*


What should I look out for, and what’s the best way to make this a smooth transaction? ** Thank you!”


*Sajeev* answers:


This is a great question, because its answer is almost as complicated as the vehicle we’re discussing. The cheapskate in me fears there is no way to have a smooth transaction with a flagship Mercedes product after 9-plus years on the road, but we have several tools to minimize the risk.


The first and most important aspect is to get a late-model German specialty repair shop to perform a pre-purchase inspection on your behalf. The shorthand for this service is[PPI](https://www.hagerty.com/media/market-trends/hagerty-insider/do-you-really-need-to-see-a-car-before-you-buy-it/) , and it can save you a lot of money when negotiating the sales price.


At that age and mileage, this S63 likely has a fair bit of deferred maintenance, or possibly a deferred repair because the previous owner lost interest and would prefer not to replace a part that suffered a pothole hit, or anything else that fails due to unexpected incidents on the road. PPIs can spot problems you won’t, and that’s why they are worth their weight in gold.


Mercedes-Benz


But you have a possible alternative to the PPI, or at least a good supplement to one, if you happen to be buying the car from a reputable dealer (who doesn’t flip cars purchased sight-unseen from dealer auctions). A dealer should be able to provide a clean CARFAX (or a similar service) as well as recent service invoices to justify the asking price. The paperwork should come from a Mercedes-Benz dealership, proving the car was serviced correctly and regularly. (Dealership servicing is generally the gold standard, but when in doubt, get the PPI.)


With documentation in hand, you can do most of the remaining PPI yourself. Inspect for interior/exterior flaws, look for collision repair (overspray, missing hardware, etc.), test every power assist, cycle the power top several times, use everything in every menu on the dashboard’s screen, listen for problems on a test drive, make sure the sound system works and is playing through all the speakers, and so on. If something isn’t working, you can try lowering the price or just walk away. (Or you can just pay a professional for their time, hence the value of a PPI.)


Let’s say this S63 passes muster, and the deal is to your liking. Next up is your concern about “dropping the oils” at this age. And this mileage, because at 42,000 miles. I suspect it needs a[“B” service](https://www.mercedesbenzofdenver.com/what-is-an-a-service-or-b-service-.htm#:~:text=in%20the%20USA.-,Mercedes%2DBenz%20B%2DService,-The%20B%2DService) : After a little hunting in menus, I bet you can find that deadline on the dashboard. If you have the[MCT 7-speed gearbox](https://forums.mbclub.co.uk/threads/mct-gearbox-service-intervals.258428/) , it’s getting close to needing a fluid change, too. The 4-matic-equipped models might also need a transfer case service. If you don’t know a good European-savvy repair shop, consider this paragraph as your clarion call to address this situation.


Amazon


The aforementioned sacrificial pigtails (shown above) can be a good idea, but I don’t know if the issue applies to 2017 models. Instead of trying to verify the problem/solution, I would double down on finding a good local mechanic and asking them for their advice.


You are gonna need experts in the trade to keep an S63 running in top form. Which brings up a final point: If indeed you are buying from a dealership, ask the aforementioned experts what aftermarket warranty company they like to work with. Consider getting their recommended warranty from the selling dealer, as I suspect[a couple of ABC repairs](https://www.reddit.com/r/mercedes_benz/comments/bh86zq/is_abc_as_bad_as_they_say/) will pay for it.


Good luck with the purchase. These V-8 AMG flagships are beyond fantastic, but it will take a lot of legwork to protect your wallet from long-term stress.
