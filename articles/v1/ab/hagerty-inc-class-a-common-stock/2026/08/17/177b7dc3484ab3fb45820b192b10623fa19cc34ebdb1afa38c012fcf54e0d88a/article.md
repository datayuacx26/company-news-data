# 5 Wildly Different Trucks Reveal the Scope of Mopar’s Direct Connection Catalog

It’s only appropriate that Brian Szalk, the man in charge of Ram’s factory-backed parts catalog, is restoring—and modifying—one of the earliest muscle trucks. His 1978 Dodge Li’l Red Express, which he bought in pieces, is getting a driveline transplant courtesy of a rear-ended 2017 Ram 1500, which is donating its 5.7-liter engine and eight-speed transmission.


Dodge and Ram have a history of turning workaday pickups into enthusiast models, but stringent governmental regulations and leadership that pushed hard for EVs recently encouraged the companies to center their newest performance vehicles around an engine with six cylinders instead of eight. Thanks to yet another changing of the tides, the[Hemi V-8 is back in a big, big way](https://www.hagerty.com/media/news/oh-snap-the-hemi-v-8-returns-to-ram-pickup-lineup/) , starting with the trucks.


A few weeks ago, we brought you news of the[Rumble Bee muscle truck lineup](https://hagerty.com/media/search?s=rumble+bee) , a family of three short-bed, V-8–powered trucks ranging from mild to wild. Last week, Ram announced those Rumble Bees could be taken to even wilder heights through its Direct Connection program, a catalog of aftermarket parts that includes both warrantied components installed by your dealer and other, off-the-shelf components. With a Direct Connection supercharger kit ($10,995 each), the entry-level truck can make a targeted 600-plus horsepower, up from 395; the mid-level 392 model 700-plus, up from 470, thanks to a supercharger; and the range-topping SRT 900-plus, up from 777. You can also order suspension systems from Ridetech and cat-back exhausts from Magnaflow. And why wouldn’t you want to hear more of that V-8 roar?

As we’ll see shortly,[Direct Connection’s catalog](https://www.dcperformance.com/) covers the gamut of performance pickups, from street-focused muscle trucks to sand-dune-bounding off-roaders. Keep an eye out for more news this November, Szalk says, because Direct Connection is launching more products at the Specialty Equipment Market Association (SEMA) show. One example Szalk let us in on: carbon fiber wheels for both the Charger and the Rumble Bee families.


While these three trucks look factory and are backed by factory warranties, Szalk was careful to point out that you can’t order them like this: You order a Rumble Bee of your choosing, then go over to the parts counter at your local dealer to order the Direct Connection parts that one of your dealer’s Level III techs installs for you.


At last weekend’s MotorTrend Presents Roadkill Nights Powered by Dodge, the annual street-legal drag-racing festival held in metro Detroit since 2015, Direct Connection was also showing off a variety of builds in association with major suppliers, such as Fox Factory, Roush, and Hennessey. Their sheer variety reveals Stellantis’ ambitions for the program—and the breadth of its imagination for pickup-truck performance.

Since we began by discussing street-focused, single-cab muscle trucks, let’s start with the other extreme. This massive off-road build is based on a crew-cab Ram 2500 HD Rebel. The Rebel comes with the 6.4-liter Hemi, an electronic locking rear axle, and Bilstein off-road shocks. Fox took things to a new level with a set of 37-inch BFGoodrich KO3 tires wrapped around beadlock wheels from Method. Behind that impressive wheel/tire package are radius and control arms made of billet aluminum from Baja Kits Suspensions, whose action is controlled by Fox’s live valve, semi-active shocks, adjustable from inside the cabin. The Hemi exhales through a Magnaflow exhaust with Direct Connection–branded tips. The interior adopts the same color scheme as the outside—black and lighter gray leather accented with orange piping and suede.

No self-respecting off-road tuner would ignore the[TRX](https://www.hagerty.com/media/new-car-reviews/2027-ram-1500-trx-srt-first-drive-ameritruck/) , and neither did Fox. This truck’s government name is the Ram 1500 TRX SRT DC Concept. The driveline carries over from the stock TRX, with 777 hp and 680 lb-ft of torque shuffled through a full-time active transfer case, but the tires have been upgraded from the stock 35s to 37s supplied by Nitto. They’re wrapped around Method 709 beadlock wheels with flashy red rings that play off the red tow hooks in the front bumper and the wrap on the truck’s hood and rear haunches (yes, little dinos are hiding in the design). The truck sports a BDS leveling kit plus fold-away power steps from AMP, because this beast is *tall* . A Magnaflow exhaust with Direct Connection tips ensures everyone will hear you coming, and a set of lights from Baja Designs ensures you can see them cowering at your approach, too.

Now, for the other extreme! Fox also makes a lowered, supercharged version of the Ram 1500 called the DC650. Power from the 5.7-liter Hemi is up to 650 hp thanks to a twin-screw blower from Whipple with an oversized heat exchanger. That menacing stance comes courtesy of a 2-inch drop in front and a 4-inch drop in the rear and is enhanced by a front splitter, a rear spoiler, and dual black chrome exhaust tips. This model has been around for a while, but this particular truck, wearing the nostalgic color combo of B5 Blue with white (rather than the usual gold) accents, was built especially for this year’s edition of Roadkill Nights. Fox has dreams of updating it with a white leather interior, and we are in full support. More B5 Blue, please!


## Hennessey Maximus


[Grace Jarvis](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/grace-jarvis/)


Just as Fox wouldn’t leave the TRX alone, Hennessey jumped on the opportunity to modify the top-dog Rumble Bee SRT. Meet Hennessey’s “Maximus,” whose stated goal is to go 200 mph. Aerodynamics are a big part of that, so its factory air suspension has been lowered and the bed covered with a retractable hard tonneau cover. Hennessey added a front splitter, side skirts, and a rear wing (removable for top speed runs where max downforce isn’t the goal). Increased power is the other part of hitting 200 mph, so the stock 6.2-liter supercharged Hemi has been upgraded to an eye-popping 1032 hp and 947 lb-ft of torque. To put all that power to the ground, the truck wears a set of Hennessey 22×12-inch wheels with 325/40R22 performance tires.


## Ram Direct Connection by Roush


[Grace Jarvis](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/grace-jarvis/)


[Grace Jarvis](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/grace-jarvis/)


We’ve reported on this[Roush-Ram collab](https://www.hagerty.com/media/news/2027-ram-1500-direct-connection-by-roush-specs-pictures-pricing/) several times, but this is the first time we’ve seen the fruits of the project. Among this extroverted bunch of builds, the Ram Direct Connection by Roush is definitely the least shouty. It boasts a 2-inch lift, an active cat-back exhaust from Roush, and a set of 18-inch Roush off-road wheels with 33-inch all-terrains from General Tire. Roush focused its efforts on the chassis, which features custom-tuned twin-tube hydraulic dampers, an electronic locking rear axle, and underbody skid plates.
