# Getting to the Bottom of Ford’s $28,350 Fathom

Vaguebooking is defined as a cryptic message posted on social media, with the intention of eliciting a response from followers. There’s a new Ford on the way, and it might be among the most anticipated vehicles in recent times, in part because Ford’s done a good job keeping us guessing about the details. But it’s a big deal because of its modest $28,350 asking price, and in how it aids the American automobile industry as it faces external threats. Unfortunately for those champing at the bit to see this affordable new offering, the latest info on this segment-busting product from Ford comes with no photos, only a smattering of high-level details and an intriguing new name: *Fathom* .


Ford’s been teasing an EV truck (smaller than the departed F-150 Lightning) for[about a year](https://www.hagerty.com/media/news/fords-5b-ev-investment-includes-mid-size-pickup-new-platform-new-way-to-build-vehicles/) , heralding its technology as the foundation for many future vehicles. We learned more about the production methods involved during their[Unboxing Day](https://www.hagerty.com/media/maintenance-and-tech/unboxing-day-the-auto-industry-is-rethinking-the-way-it-assembles-evs/) in February, but this “Universal EV Platform” and its initial run of mid-size trucks have so far been vaguebooking at its finest.


Ford


Things have changed, as we learned that the Universal EV Platform will make a mid-size EV truck named Fathom. The news suggests it will be the size of a Ford Ranger, a gasoline-powered truck that’s only available in a crew cab body. While the Ranger starts at $33,550 with a $1895 destination charge, the new Ford Fathom starts at $28,350 with its smallest battery and a destination charge of $1595.


That stacks up well against the Ranger, which is the global embodiment of everything traditionalists love about the Ford Motor Company. (Especially in[Australia](https://www.automotiveglobespecialist.com/post/australia-s-best-selling-cars-of-2024-why-the-ford-ranger-took-the-crown?srsltid=AfmBOopuDG7oyEkLSTaCow8kemDQO0A7X7xoJuq4Ks9POJIOjiPa6kQt) .) But Fathom is the first vehicle aimed at a global demographic that’s likely never considered a Ford product. It thrusts the famed automaker squarely into some hotly contested real estate.


It starts at the Louisville Assembly Plant that we discussed during their Unboxing Day, as there’s a lot of Ford money in its production line ($2 billion). But the investment makes even more sense these days, as there’s a growing threat from Chinese EVs: 49,000 of them will reportedly be[sold in Canada](https://www.reuters.com/business/autos-transportation/chinas-carmakers-rush-canada-practice-run-us-sales-2026-06-25/) by the end of this year.


The prevalence of Chinese cars[in Mexico](https://www.reuters.com/business/autos-transportation/chinese-car-sales-mexico-surge-despite-new-tariffs-2026-07-20/) and their newfound acceptance in Canada is likely why Ford CEO Jim Farley[said](https://www.reuters.com/business/autos-transportation/ford-ceo-tells-employees-chinese-automakers-could-enter-us-market-next-decade-2026-07-30/) that Chinese cars might make it to American showrooms “in the next five to 10 years.”


Ford isn’t the only American automaker publicly sounding alarms, as GM’s Mary Barra has suggested that Chinese EVs in North America are a slippery slope. But Ford’s increasingly steady drips of information on the Fathom truck are a not-so-subtle reminder that they are still in the game.


Behind the highly polished promotional video lies real concern from Bill Ford himself, as[he suggested](https://www.freep.com/story/money/cars/ford/2026/07/15/bill-ford-automakers-china-competition/90927110007/) his company must “go toe-to-toe with China” and added, “We can’t expect to keep them out forever, and we have to ‌be able to beat them at their own game.”


Whatever the Fathom truck may look like on its Universal EV platform, it will likely set up the Ford brand far better against Chinese crossovers that are populating both north and south of the border. There’s a good chance that 2027 brings an onslaught of news stories and social media chatter from Canada about Chinese EVs: If Ford plays their cards right, the Fathom will be right there in the conversation.


Ford did give us a little more info on the Fathom. Every unit will be ready for Ford’s BlueCruise Level 2 “self-driving” technology, a touch screen with Apple Maps, and a digital key. The cabin will be larger than a Toyota RAV4, suggesting a mandatory two-row, crew-cab configuration. There will be both a truck bed and a frunk, and one of those spots will give owners the ability to perform bi-directional charging to help power their home in the event of a power failure.


The Fathom appears to be Ford’s response to everyone’s clamoring for cheaper vehicles, and ones that will match Chinese offerings. In theory, it will give more bang for the buck over the regular-cab[Slate EV](https://www.hagerty.com/media/opinion/picture-car-confidential/picture-car-confidential-45-slate-first-drive/) , doing so with a painted metal body. While photographic proof certainly would be nice, Ford is offering email updates[here](https://www.ford.com/electric/fathom/) , pre-orders in early 2027, and deliveries later in 2027.
