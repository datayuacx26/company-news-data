# 5 Tiers of Project Car “Upgrades”

The vast majority of vintage cars are project cars. Whether intentional by the owner or not, keeping up with old iron requires periodic projects and upkeep. While there are always places or ways to spend our hard-earned money on a project, some expenditures may prove more worthwhile than others.


Not everything is black and white in this space, though. Some upgrades or modifications have great value to one person, but none to another, and that’s okay. Cars are highly personal, as is how one values their money. Regardless, if we get honest with ourselves, not everything you might view as an “upgrade” is a great spend of hard-earned cash. Need proof? Here are five tiers of upgrades with a few examples. Maybe you’ll recognize a few.


## Only makes sense


[Kyle Smith](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/kyle-smith/)


The old hubs and new hubs don’t look much different except for age.


[Kyle Smith](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/kyle-smith/)


A prime example of this is updating old parts when they wear out, as I did on my LS-swapped 1985 Corvette when it was time for new[rear wheel bearings](https://www.hagerty.com/media/opinion/mechanical-sympathy/updating-my-old-corvettes-hubs-with-slightly-less-old-tech/) . Technology has progressed a good bit since 1985, and since my car is not a restoration, it only makes sense to embrace new technology and do some light adapting to install a modern wheel bearing. This makes the car easier and more enjoyable to drive, which should be viewed as a good spend of money. Some other examples of this would be upgrades like installing cupholders, power ports, a dual master cylinder conversion for the brakes, or even a tilting steering column.


## Worth it if you’ve got the cash


[Kyle Smith](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/kyle-smith/)


Wouldn’t it be great if you could have your cake and eat it too? For some, that’s possible with upgrades that are a little less than necessary but delightful if you can partake—kind of like most cakes. Think about vintage cars that are saddled with three- and four-speed transmissions despite having the power and space to fit an[overdrive](https://www.hagerty.com/media/maintenance-and-tech/ldn-overdrive-the-british-invention-that-let-the-worlds-drivers-enjoy-highways/) unit that would enable much more comfortable highway cruising. These are often fairly pricey improvements, whether in terms of cost or time, but, like the examples above, most upgrades in this category still improve the car’s function for its intended purpose.


## Cars and coffee showoff


[Kyle Smith](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/kyle-smith/)


Some modifications are purely cosmetic. That’s not a judgement; it’s a true statement. The idea of “cars as art” traces back significantly further than any of us would probably like to admit. It should come as no surprise that sometimes, owners choose to throw the whole aftermarket catalog at a car only to spend the vast majority of their time sitting behind said vehicle in a lawn chair. Super stiff coil-overs, heavy oversized wheels, or a massive-overlap,[barely drivable camshaft](https://www.hagerty.com/media/maintenance-and-tech/4-camshaft-selection-tips-for-your-project/) are all examples of this tier. The owner may have made these “upgrades” out of curiosity, pursuit of some perceived status, just for fun, or any number of other reasons, but it’s often easy to spot when the main reason was not performance.


## It’s all about speed


[Kyle Smith](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/kyle-smith/)


Of course we have to mention over-the-top performance or racing-oriented spending. Done right, this tier of upgrades is often as hard to argue with as the “it just makes sense” group above, but only after accepting that there will likely be great compromises in ride quality, overall vehicle usability, and comfort. Racing has always required sacrificing at least some of a vehicle’s practicality to make it faster. Think paying to delete features or intentionally making a car less civil.


## Well, that’s one way to spend your money


Mansory


We’re not here to judge, but sometimes we have to be reminded that for many of us, playing with cars is a hobby, and hobbies are not an accountant’s friend. Procuring, repairing, restoring, and driving cars can cost as much money as a person wants to spend. Just about anyone outside of our relatively small enthusiast sphere would chalk everything on this list up as frivolous, and that’s fine. We were going to spend that time or money on something no matter what; we might as well get a cool car out of the deal. Of course, what I consider cool is probably different than what you consider cool.
