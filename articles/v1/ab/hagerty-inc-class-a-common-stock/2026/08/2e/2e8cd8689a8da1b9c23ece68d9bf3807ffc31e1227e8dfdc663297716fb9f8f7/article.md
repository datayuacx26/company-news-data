# ’65 Mustang Pace Car Edition Seeks Another 44 Years of Loving Ownership

While it’s standard practice to refer to early builds of Ford’s iconic 1965 Mustang as 1964.5 models, that’s technically not true. There’s a “5” in the VIN to exclaim to all observers that these Mustangs are, in fact, of the 1965 model year. But there’s just something about the 1965 Mustang Pace Car, as it paced the Indianapolis 500 on one fine Saturday in May of 1964. There were 35 new Mustang convertibles at this race, but Ford made 190 hardtop replicas available for dealership retail sales. The Mustang pacing the Indy 500 was a huge moment, both for the model and for Ford Motor Company, and that moment, unfortunately, occurred in 1964.


So the allure of a 1964 “and a half” Mustang continues apace. Each of the 190 replicas was finished in Pace Car white with blue interior trim and the F-code small-block V-8 engine. There were unique body side decals, and specific identification codes under the hood. The latter were made by a grease pencil on the radiator’s core support, and the seller of this particular example on[Hagerty Marketplace](https://www.hagerty.com/marketplace/auction/1965-Ford-Mustang/a20f4431-62fa-4389-be36-f9643e976a07) ensured that what remains of those markings wasn’t lost in this Mustang’s sympathetic restoration.


Body side decals matching those of the official Pace Car were applied, and the hood/roof/trunk had three blue racing stripes along their centerlines. The commemorative Pace Car coupes left the factory with grease pencil codes on that radiator core support, including the words “PACE CAR” for posterity.

This particular Pace Car Edition is unit #41, and was also used as a promotional vehicle at the Indy 500. It has remained with its current owner for 44 years, who purchased it back in 1982 as “just a basic white Mustang.” But it became much, much more after that owner researched the paint code and commissioned a forensic etching of the radiator core support. Turns out the original owner was in Missouri, and this Pace Car Edition was his high school graduation present. Delivery was delayed because of the Mustang’s use at Indianapolis, where it was dented and repaired without its trademark body side decals. Each subsequent owner was ignorant of this Mustang’s true provenance until it was time for a proper restoration.


This Mustang Pace Car Edition has its original floors, trunk, doors, and engine compartment sheetmetal. Only the passenger-side quarter panel has evidence of repair from its tenure at the Indianapolis 500. An extensive restoration started in the 1980s, when the motor was rebuilt and the correct paint and graphics applied. By 2008, the Pace Car Edition had a rebuilt transmission, and a refreshed carburetor arrived in 2020.

While this is an older restoration that shows modest signs of its age, a treasure trove of documentation is included with the sale, pointing to a delightful tale of redemption for what was once a courtesy vehicle at “The Greatest Spectacle in Racing.”


This Pace Car Edition lost its identifying decals shortly after the checkered flag flew at Indy in 1964, but today we are immensely grateful for what became of this now-temporary setback. As of this writing, there were 12 days left on the auction,[with a high bid of $12,000](https://www.hagerty.com/marketplace/auction/1965-Ford-Mustang/a20f4431-62fa-4389-be36-f9643e976a07) . Back in 2020, a 1965 Pace Car Edition sold for a little over $20,000 on[Bring a Trailer](https://bringatrailer.com/listing/1965-ford-mustang-122/) , but the overall market for 1965 Mustang Coupes has been stronger as of late. Perhaps we will see if provenance and a fantastic story of redemption will have an effect on the final outcome.
