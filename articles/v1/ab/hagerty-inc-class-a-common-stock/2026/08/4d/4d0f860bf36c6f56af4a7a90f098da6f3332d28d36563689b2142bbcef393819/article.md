# Mercedes-AMG GT 53 4-Door Gets More Range, Less Power, and Straight-Six-Like Sound

Mercedes-AMG unveiled the 1153- and 805-horsepower variants of[the second-generation GT 4-Door](https://www.hagerty.com/media/news/2027-mercedes-amg-gt-4-door-specs-performance-pictures/) earlier in 2026. Less powerful variants are on the way, including the new Mercedes-AMG GT 53 4-Door positioned as the entry point into the range.


Like the GT 63 and the GT 55, the GT 53 is exclusively available with electric power. While the 63 and the 55 use three motors, the 53 features a dual-motor all-wheel-drive system with a peak output of 536 horsepower and 590 pound-feet of torque. We say “peak output,” because the motor assigned to the front wheels is configured to deliver boost. It’s disengaged in normal driving conditions, and it automatically re-engages when the system detects that the car needs additional power or more traction.


AMG quotes a zero-to-60 mph time of 3.8 seconds, compared to 2.3 seconds for the GT 63 and 2.7 seconds for the GT 55. Top speed is electronically limited to 143 mph. While most modern EVs use a single-speed transmission, the GT 55 is fitted with a two-speed transmission that features a short first gear for quicker acceleration and a long second gear for improved efficiency. This solution is uncommon in the EV world, but it’s not AMG-specific:[Porsche’s Taycan](https://www.hagerty.com/media/news/how-the-1093-hp-porsche-taycan-turbo-gt-became-lap-king-of-the-ring-and-laguna-seca/) , one of the GT’s main rivals, uses it as well.


The three GT variants share the same 106-kilowatt-hour lithium-ion battery pack, which boasts an 800-volt rating for quicker charging. The system is capable of charging at up to 600 kilowatts, and it delivers a maximum driving range of nearly 500 miles. Keep in mind AMG calculated that figure using Europe’s optimistic WLTP testing cycle; EPA figures haven’t been released yet.

While the GT is all electric, all the time, AMG went to surprising lengths to make it sound and feel like it has a combustion engine. The driver can configure the 53 to deliver simulated gear changes using the steering wheel-mounted paddles and what the brand refers to as “an authentically reproduced combustion engine sound.” The 63 and 55 models have this, too: They sound like the V-8-powered GT R. Befitting of its entry-level status, the 53 gets its exhaust note from the C238-generation[E53](https://www.hagerty.com/media/new-car-reviews/2025-mercedes-amg-e-53-hybrid-master-of-none/) Coupe’s straight-six.


Visually, the 53 looks similar to the 55 and 63 models with the exception of small details, such as the bumpers and the wheels. It’s a similar story inside, where the 53 offers a low seating position (which is unusual for an EV), sport seats, and[a lot of screens](https://www.hagerty.com/media/news/new-mercedes-amg-gt-4-door-interior-specs/) .


The 2027 Mercedes-AMG GT 53 4-Door will begin arriving on dealer lots in early 2027. Pricing hasn’t been announced yet, but it’s reasonable to assume that it will land with a six-digit price tag. It’s too early to tell which additional versions will join the range later in the production run, but what’s seemingly certain is that a gasoline-powered version of the GT 4-Door isn’t in the pipeline.
