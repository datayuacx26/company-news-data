# 1935 Duesenberg SSJ Special Speedster Wins 2026 Pebble Beach Concours d’Elegance Best of Show

The sun has set on the 75th Pebble Beach Concours d’Elegance. The Sunday event is the grand finale of[Monterey Car Week](https://www.hagerty.com/media/tags/monterey-car-week/) , and the Best of Show award is the grand finale to that grand finale. Fittingly, the 2026 award went to the 1935 Duesenberg SSJ Special Speedster of collector Harry Yeaggy, of Cincinnati, Ohio.


This year featured 200 cars from two-dozen classes—from Vignale-bodied Ferraris and Japanese motorsports icons to the cars of Royal India—all gleaming and shimmering under a light gray sky on the 18th green of Pebble Beach golf links, overlooking Carmel Bay. It also marked the end of a distinguished 40-year run for chairman Sandra Button. “First of all, I’m here for the cars,” said Button after presenting the Best of Show award for the final time. “They can’t speak for themselves, and it’s up to us to take care of them for the future. I’m happy to hand this over to \[Pebble president\] Vince Finaldi. He’s going to take the event forward, and I’m going to go have a lot more fun with cars.”

Only one automobile can take home the top honors (plus an engraved Rolex Perpetual 1908, among other prizes), and Yeaggy’s Duesenberg was a worthy winner. Duesenberg only ever built two SSJ Special Speedsters, and both were on the grass at Pebble Beach, competing in the Duesenberg class.


The Model J was Duesenberg’s exclusive offering from its introduction in 1928 until the company’s demise in 1937, in the midst of the Great Depression. Multiple wheelbases and body styles graced the Model J, which is widely regarded as one of the finest American automobiles of all time. The SJ was a higher-performance model, with a centrifugal supercharger fitted to the 420-cubic-inch Duesenberg straight-eight. The SJ’s output of 320 horsepower was a stratospheric figure in an age when a Ford Model A made about 40.


dpa/picture alliance via Getty Images


But there was an even more potent Duesie, called the SSJ, which rode on a short (for the time) 125-inch wheelbase and came fitted with a special dual-carbureted supercharged engine good for roughly 400 hp. Just two SSJs left Duesenberg’s Indianapolis factory: One sold to actor Gary Cooper, while the company lent the other to Clark Gable. Legend has it the two would race each other in the Hollywood Hills. Cooper’s car famously sold for $22M at auction in 2018 and was the most expensive American car ever sold at auction, until its record was broken at this year’s[Monterey auctions](https://www.hagerty.com/media/market-trends/were-live-from-monterey-car-week-2026/) , when a Shelby Cobra Daytona Coupe sold for $42.9M. Another ex-Clark Gable Duesenberg, a 1935 Model JN Convertible Coupe, also sold in Monterey this year, for[$9.08M](https://rmsothebys.com/auctions/mo26/lots/s0011-1935-duesenberg-model-jn-convertible-coupe-by-rollston-and-bohman-schwartz/) .

The Clark Gable SSJ, meanwhile, had been in the ownership of one family for decades before Yeaggy purchased it three years ago. Recently restored, it wears its original colors of black lacquer with light saddle leather.


Beginning in the early morning, the lawn in front of the main stage at the concours fills with a patchwork of picnic blankets, multicolored outfits, clinking champagne glasses, and periodic whiffs of cigar smoke and delectable food. Best of Show wasn’t announced until after 5 p.m., but this crescendo of Car Week commences when the winner drives onto the stage accompanied by an explosion of confetti that fills the air and then drifts down onto everything and everyone in its path.


This was the second Pebble Beach Best of Show victory for collector Harry Yeaggy (left).


[Brandan Gillogly](https://www.hagerty.com/media/author/brandan-gillogly/)


“I was here 19 years ago and I’ve been coming every year to do it and we did it again,” said Yeaggy, who won Best of Show honors in 2007 with another Duesenberg, the SJ “Mormon Meteor” Special Speedster. “People don’t realize how hard it is and how special it is to really win this award, and it’s amazing,” he added, also noting that Pebble Beach is the biggest concours win of them all, and “there’s not even a second. Everybody else is too far down the list.”


Duesenberg is the winningest American marque at Pebble Beach, with eight Best of Show trophies as of 2026. Mercedes-Benz and Bugatti have the most of all, with 10 trophies each.
