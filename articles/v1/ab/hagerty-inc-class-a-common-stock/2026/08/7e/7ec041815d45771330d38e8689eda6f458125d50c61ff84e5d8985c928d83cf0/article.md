# Nexera Vision Concept Hints at Acura’s Future

Few places are better than Monterey Car Week for manufacturers to introduce exciting new models and upcoming concepts. Acura is seizing the opportunity to do just that, and today at The Quail, a Motorsports Gathering, the Japanese carmaker unveiled the Nexera Vision concept. The car introduces the world to the new exterior and interior design direction that will guide future Acura vehicles.


Crafted at the Acura Design California studio in Los Angeles, under creative director and vice president of auto design Yasutake Tsuchida, the Nexera Vision is a[long, low, sleek coupe](https://www.hagerty.com/media/news/honda-nsx-tribute-by-italdesign-specs-pictures/) in a Matte Titanium finish that features dual-hinge butterfly doors, active aerodynamics, and a distinctive new lighting signature.

This “phantom lighting” is cleverly integrated into the car’s surfaces and disappears completely when not illuminated. The thin dual spears that create the effect front and rear reflect the Acura “A” mark. The new lighting signature will be incorporated into future Acura models, beginning with the next-generation RDX Hybrid, which is set to hit showrooms[in 2028](https://www.autonews.com/honda/an-honda-vs-acura-ice-strategy-revealed-0518/) .


The Nexera Vision rides on 22-inch centerlock wheels with forged carbon inserts, and the use of that material carries over to the coupe’s aero-channel fascia, rear diffuser, rocker panels, and inner door structures.

Acura says the cabin reflects a “precision-focused intimacy,” with all distractions eliminated. The seats, for example, are integrated into the floor for a low position. They are fixed, too, leaving drivers to adjust the movable pedals and steering wheel to accommodate themselves. That steering wheel, it should be said, has been reduced to its most minimalist shape. Materials used throughout the cockpit reflect a commitment to sustainability, including recycled aluminum, Alcantara that incorporates certified post-consumer recycled polyester, and “CircuLeather,” a genuine leather material “designed to support a more circular material lifecycle.”


“We are using the[40th anniversary of Acura](https://www.hagerty.com/media/news/acura-integra-40-racer/) as a springboard to our future,” said Yasutake Tsuchida. “Our goal with this new design direction is to define Acura performance through disciplined proportions, sculpted form, and the seamless integration of advanced technology, creating an expression that is both progressive and authentic to the brand.”

There has been no mention of what sort of powertrain—if any—resides in the Nexera Vision, though in keeping with the car’s “next era” look and feel, a safe guess would lean toward electric propulsion. But that’s not really the point here. The features Acura is highlighting on the Nexera Vision are a big departure from the look and feel of the existing lineup, so it will be interesting to see what actually makes its way from the unveiling at The Quail to the production line.
