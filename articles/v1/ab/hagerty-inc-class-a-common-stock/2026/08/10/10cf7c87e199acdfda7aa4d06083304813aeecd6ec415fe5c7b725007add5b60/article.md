# Defender Dakar Packs Rally Tech in a Street-Legal Package

The Defender, which morphed into a standalone brand under the Land Rover umbrella, won its class in the 2026 Dakar Rally with[an off-roader dubbed D7X-R](https://www.hagerty.com/media/news/land-rover-just-built-a-defender-to-dominate-the-dakar-rally/) . The brand is now injecting some of its desert-proven technology into a limited-edition super-SUV. Due out in 2027, the Defender Dakar will feature a 626-horsepower V-8, a rally-ready suspension system, and a model-specific interior.


Tall and wide, the Defender Dakar looks pretty much like the D7X-R, except for the race-specific decals. It gets roof-mounted lights, wheel-arch flares with exposed rivets, and a snorkel air intake system made with carbon fiber. Buyers will even have the option of ordering the model in two-tone Dakar Sand and Yanbu Turquoise, though other color combinations will be available.


We haven’t seen the interior yet, but Defender tells us that the Dakar will offer a 2+2 interior with trim-specific racing seats for the front passengers, a place to store helmets, and a trunk-mounted air compressor. Four-point racing harnesses will be optional.

Defender raced in the Dakar Rally’s Stock category, which is reserved for vehicles based on a production car; the D7X-R is related to[the Defender OCTA](https://www.hagerty.com/media/news/new-defender-octa-offers-everything-everywhere-all-at-once/) . This puts the Dakar in an interesting spot: It’s a street-legal car that’s inspired by a race car… which is based on a street-legal car. Like the OCTA and the D7X-R, the latest Defender is powered by a twin-turbocharged, 4.4-liter V-8 sourced from BMW and tuned to develop 626 horsepower and 553 pound-feet of torque. The power reaches the four wheels via an eight-speed automatic transmission, a two-speed transfer case, and there are locking center and rear differentials.


Flight Mode, a driving profile developed to ensure that the D7X-R lands smoothly after a jump, will be standard on the Dakar, along with two new modes called Gravel and Dunes, respectively. The flares cover 35-inch tires wrapped around 20-inch wheels, while a coil suspension system with Bilstein Advanced Dampers should make the Dakar at home on the most grueling terrain.


More details about the Defender Dakar will emerge during the 2027 Dakar Rally, which is scheduled to take place in Saudi Arabia from January 1 to January 15. Defender will start taking reservations in early 2027, though there’s no word on how many examples will be available. Pricing hasn’t been announced, but keep in mind the regular-production OCTA starts at about $159,000.
