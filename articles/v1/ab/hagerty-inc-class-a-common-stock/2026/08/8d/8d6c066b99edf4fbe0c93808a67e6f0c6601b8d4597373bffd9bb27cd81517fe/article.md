# 4 Vintage Off-Roaders That Embody Slow Baja

Michael Emery realized his “a job driving around Baja” dream. After he launched a podcast about the Slow Baja lifestyle—off-roading by taking it easy, using paper maps, stopping to smell the desert flowers and eat the local tacos—the guys at the NORRA Mexican 1000 asked him to guide their noncompetitive trip for street-legal vehicles. A few years ago, Emery broke out on his own with the goal of creating a more laid-back Mexican 1000 experience. He wanted the latitude to change the route as desired and imbue the adventure with more of the peninsula’s unique culture and cuisine.


Although the Slow Baja trip we took (which you can read about[here](https://www.hagerty.com/media/great-reads/go-slow-and-say-hello-celebrating-50-years-of-the-baja-500-with-vintage-iron/) ) was limited to old iron, other trips allow for all makes and models. In 2026, Slow Baja is offering three options: one for air-cooled steeds, another vintage-only run, and the “all-vehicle” Winter Expedition. If a south-of-the-border adventure is on your bucket list, the price of admission is about $4500 (for two). In addition to Emery’s wealth of local knowledge, trips include three squares a day, campsites, hotel lodging on the final night, and support staff, including a photographer, medic, and mechanic. The winter trip even provides a day of whale watching on Scammon’s Lagoon. Is Baja right for you? Learn more at[slowbaja.com](https://www.slowbaja.com/) . In the meantime, give a gander at some of the rigs that went with us on our journey.


## 1968 International Scout 800


Chris Collard


**Owner:** Josie Barnum


**What’s Special:** Scored on Craigslist. It wasn’t really a score, as Barnum’s mechanic, Dan Hayes (Binder Boneyard), said, “Please don’t try to drive that thing home before I look at it. You might die.” After going through the entire vehicle, including the drivetrain, suspension, DIY upholstery, and adding dual aluminum fuel cells, Barnum said, “This ended up being a very expensive decision. Seven years later, it’s fixed up and my daily driver.”


**What Barnum Loves:** “Nobody can look at it without smiling—including me.”


## 1972 Bill Stroppe Baja Bronco


Chris Collard


**Owner:** Andrew Norton


**What’s Special:** One-of-a-kind unicorn in the Stroppe world. When the original owner said he wasn’t keen to the[Baja Bronco’s white, blue, and orange color scheme](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BV1UZabkdwI) , Stroppe said, “Go to Pacific Ford, buy one, and bring it here. I’ll have it ready for you in a week.” It’s not stock, as Norton swapped in a 1998 5.0. But he trimmed it with a vintage C90x intake, Holley 600, and JBA “Shorty” headers. Down below are Bilstein shocks, a Wild Horses suspension, and BFGoodrich tires wrapped around an original set of Stroppe Super Steel wheels.


**What Norton Loves:** “Stroppe Baja Bronco package… love the story and history of our Tampico.”


## 1975 Dodge W-250 Rod Hall Tribute Truck


Chris Collard


**Owner:** Mack Johnson (Aka Sol Saltzman)


**What’s Special:** The late Rod Hall was the winningest off-road racer in the world during the early ’80s, pulling off 35 consecutive first-place finishes. Saltzman’s collection of old iron includes vehicles owned by Malcolm Smith and Ivan Stewart, and he added Hall’s championship ’77 Dodge to the collection. Keeping with the Rod Hall theme, he sourced this ’75 W-250 barn find, and with the help of friends created a nearly identical tribute truck—save the 12-valve Cummins diesel mill.


**What Saltzman Loves:** “It looks natural towing Rod’s race truck.”


## 1969 Land Rover Series II


Chris Collard


**Owner:** Ian Tydeman


**What’s Special:** While he was still in college, Tydeman said he purchased this “sad-looking Rover” not knowing the engine had been tossed for an Iron Duke 151 “with a number of issues.” His little brother talked him into getting some bigger tires (which required stretching the frame) and giving it new life with a reliable Toyota 3RZ 2.7-liter, G56 five-speed, and ultra-low-geared transfer case. Front and rear axles are Scout Dana 44s, both sporting ARB Air Lockers. When it rolled out of the shop, it was fitted with G-Fab bumpers and armor, Hella 4000 auxiliary lights, Warn M8000 winch, and countless custom mods.


**What Tydeman Loves:** “It has *Out of Africa* character, but with upgrades that make it quite reliable.”
