# 20 Eye-Catching Cars from the 2026 Mecum Harrisburg Auction

Mecum has done in Harrisburg what no other auction company has been able to do: Assemble a consistent series of consignment auctions in the Northeast.


Barrett-Jackson tried with consignment sales at the Mohegan Sun casino in Connecticut, one of the auction world’s more friendly and accommodating auction venues. In the dim and distant past Guernsey’s, Christie’s (long before Gooding), Kensington and Dennis Nicotra’s NY Auto Salon tried. RM and Bonhams have come back with catalog sales and Broad Arrow will put its own stake in the ground when it replaces Bonhams at Audrain Car Week in October of this year.


But *no* auction has been able to do what Mecum has with its eleven auctions in the cavernous Pennsylvania Farm Show Complex in Harrisburg, drawing nearly a thousand cars year after year. In 2026 the pattern was repeated with 678 of 923 lots sold, $27,674,130 changing hands and a creditable (but the lowest since 2019) 67.4% sale rate.


There were many recent cars in the lineup, a few of them late model high performance-cars but many others just late model used cars that attracted some curiosity but little collector interest.


Recent cars, built 2007 and later, accounted for 14.6% of the lots in the sale. Last year that population was an even higher 19.1%. The top three lots comprised two 2026 Corvette ZR1s that brought a total of $1,034,000 and a ’69 Mustang Boss 429 at $550,000. \[All amounts are “all-in” including buyer’s commission.\] Other than these eye-popping results prices were ordinary, reasonable, and even parsimonious. Except, as so often happens, when the red mist of acquisitiveness drops over bidders’ eyes, as it did with one of the 2026 ZR1s.


The other thing discerned in Harrisburg is that Mecum Auctions were, along with its compatriots Broad Arrow, Bonhams, RM Sotheby’s and Gooding Christie’s, keeping its most serious inventory in reserve for Monterey just a month later. There were plenty of good cars in Harrisburg and even a few remarkable ones, but the eye-candy, wallet-magnets, dream-boats were headed for California.


## Lot F102: 1970 AMC AMX


Rick Carey


**Sold for $68,200**


*Chassis no. A0M397X221313. Matador Red with black C-stripes over black vinyl. Visually maintained, largely original, # 3 condition.*


**Equipment:** 390cid/325hp V-8. 4-speed, rally wheels with trim rings. Wide Oval tires. Hurst shifter, Go Pack, 3.54 Twin Grip axle, power steering and brakes, tach.


**Condition:** Good exterior repaint and upholstery, but a sloppy engine compartment that is dusty from sitting and with some fluid seepage. An honest but superficially cosmetically redone driver.


**Bottom line:** The drivetrain combination of X-code 390/325hp V-8 and four-speed contribute substantial value to this AMX but it was fully valued and then some in this transaction. The buyer chose to concentrate on the lusty powerplant and overlook the cosmetic restoration’s age and shortcuts.


## Lot F122: 2009 Pontiac G8 GXP SLP Firehawk


Mecum


**Sold for $80,300**


*Chassis no. 6G2EP57W49L300734. Velocity Yellow with black hood over Onyx leather. Original, #2 condition.*


**Equipment:** 6.2L/530hp LS3 V-8, SLP supercharger, six-speed manual, spoiler, 19-inch five-spoke black alloy wheels, Firestone Firehawk tires, red calipers, sunroof, air conditioning, power steering and brakes, CD stereo.


**Condition:** Like new with 875 miles, one of nine built, the only one in Velocity Yellow, and the last produced. It was a $24,000 option package on top of the $40,000 2009 G8 GXP MSRP, but was a big boost in performance over the 415-hp[GXP](https://www.hagerty.com/valuation-tools/search?q=pontiac+g8+gxp) .


**Bottom line:** ** Let’s hope it was the Buick/Pontiac dealer who put this lemon yellow rocketship away with only the factory invoice at risk. For a retail buyer with $64K in opportunity cost bound up for 17 years the ROI is pretty miserable, and it would be better painted stealth black as a weekend driver. It’s also an AMG sedan competitor for, in that comparison, similar money for someone who can tolerate the screamin’ yellow paint.

## Lot S123: 1968 Dodge Charger R/T Hemi


Mecum


**Sold for $181,500**


*Chassis no. XS29J8B251328. Matador Red with white vinyl roof and white bumblebee stripes over white vinyl. Older restoration, #2- condition.*


**Equipment:** 426cid/425hp Hemi, automatic, pushbutton radio, Magnum wheels, red line tires, power brakes, 3.23 Sure Grip.


**Condition:** An older but very well-maintained restoration. Excellent paint, chrome, and interior. Impressive engine compartment. Fully restored and nearly like new chassis. No engine originality represented.


**Bottom line:** Some cars are worth more than they’re worth. This is one of them and shouldn’t figure into a value guide’s calculations. It is, first of all, a beautifully restored and preserved[Charger Hemi](https://www.hagerty.com/valuation-tools/dodge/charger/1968/1968-dodge-charger-r!t?id=aCn1I000000DD6CSAW) , a thunderous muscle car that takes full advantage of the TorqueFlite automatic. It’s not freshly restored but assiduously maintained, it’s better than it needs to be. It’s a $130–$150,000 all-in car. So why is it $165K hammer, $181,500 all-in? Because the seller was donating all, that’s “all”, the proceeds to ALS research, Her late husband recently succumbed to ALS and it is hard to imagine a more impactful, meaningful, heartfelt transaction.


## Lot S102: 1966 Dodge D/Dart Super Stock


Mecum


**Sold for $36,300**


*Chassis no. L023D62653933. Valiant White over red vinyl. Visually maintained, largely original, #3 condition.*


**Equipment:** 273cid V-8, four-speed, Wilwood line lock, bucket seats, Cragar S/S wheels, column-mounted tach, cheater slick rear tires, Doug’s headers, Edelbrock Torker intake manifold, Holley four-barrel.


Rick Carey


**Condition:** Scuffed aluminum trim, mediocre old repaint, good interior and dashboard. Orderly but aged engine compartment. Car number 33 out of a limited run of 50 “D/Darts” for NHRA D/Stock class competition.


**Bottom line:** This is a rare Dart and the only Super Stock series built by Chrysler Corporation in 1966. It has made the rounds over the years, no-saling at Indy 2009 and Kissimmee in 2011 on reported bids of $40,000 before selling at Indy 2013 for $27,820. Three months later it was a no-sale at Monterey on a $22,000 bid. It sold at Barrett-Jackson in 2014 for $44,000, then fell flat at Mecum Dallas in 2016 where it sold for $20,900. This result 10 years later is a recovery of sorts. The reception has been so wildly variable that it’s likely no one knows (or cares) what a D/Dart is and it would take more history research and a story about the model’s performance and competition purpose to build a legend and support a stronger value.


## Lot S21: 1893 Duryea Motor Carriage Replica


Mecum


**Sold for $55,000**


*Chassis no. 1051762. Black with light green chassis and black leatherette top over light green leather. Rebodied or re-created, #2- condition.*


**Equipment:** Center tiller steering, Club Car golf cart single-cylinder drivetrain with twin chain drive, electric carriage lights, bulb horn, hydraulic rear axle brakes, solid rubber tires.


Rick Carey


**Condition:** A recreation of the 1893 Duryea Motor Wagon, built by brothers Charles and Frank Duryea in Massachusetts and credited as the first gasoline-powered American automobile. Good paint, upholstery and top. Older and dusty but little used. Appears to be a mixture of some original coachwork bits and a re-creation chassis with modern engine and transmission. Runs and drives.


**Bottom line:** The main “collector” value in this re-creation lies in the old phaeton-style two-seat body. Speed potential is limited even with the Club Car powertrain by the big gearing reduction. It is an intriguing vehicle and, if only for its novelty, the new owner can justify just about any price paid for it. There aren’t going to be any comparables, and there aren’t any real Duryeas from this period outside of museums.


## Lot S114: 1961 Chrysler 300G Convertible


Rick Carey

**Equipment:** 413cid/375hp V-8, cross-ram dual quads, pushbutton automatic, power steering and brakes, power top, wheel covers, wide whitewalls, power rear-mounted antenna, power windows, pushbutton radio, console.


**Condition:** Quality old restoration. Well-maintained but dusty from storage. Sound upholstery with surface creases. Handsome engine compartment and chassis.

It stumbled when offered during COVID in RM’s online Shift auction when it was bid to $110,000 (which would have been $121,000 with commission) but didn’t sell. It really stood out here in Harrisburg as the quality ‘Sixties ’60s luxury performance convertible it is (a notation it shared with a couple of Caddies from the same era) and put down a solid result and another endorsement of real rarity, distinction and superlative restoration.


## Lot S115: 1970 Plymouth ‘Cuda AAR


Mecum


**Sold for $84,700**


*Chassis no. BS23J0B297222. Vitamin C Orange with black stripes over black vinyl. Older restoration, #3+ condition.*


**Equipment:** Triple-carbureted 340cid/290hp V-8, automatic, Ram Air hood, power brakes and steering, Rallye wheels with trim rings, Eagle ST tires, side outlet exhaust, pushbutton radio, remote outside mirror.


**Condition:** Overspray inside the back window. Good paint and chrome. Older chassis and underbody. Good engine compartment. Exhaust leak on the right exhaust manifold despite recent exhaust gaskets. Some engine compartment fluid residue. A desirable ‘Cuda that shows some age and use and warrants remedial attention but claims only a “Correct” engine, not an original one.


**Bottom line:** Plymouth and Dodge were cute, and clever. They managed to make their production Trans Am-qualified cars with 340cid engines (5.6 litres) while the Trans Am Mustangs, Cougars, Firebirds, and Camaros delivered 5-liter 302s to customers. Still claiming only 290hp from their tri-power engines, the street AAR Cuda and Challenger T/A were solid, lusty, flexible, satisfying road cars that today command less money than their FoMoCo and GM competition. This is a representative result and should be a rewarding car to drive.

## Lot S130: 1968 Plymouth GTX Hemi Convertible


Rick Carey


**Sold for $137,500**


*Chassis no. RS27J8G188130. Sunfire Yellow with green accents and white vinyl top over white vinyl. Unrestored original, #4 condition.*


**Equipment:** 426cid/425hp dual-quad Hemi V-8, automatic, power steering and brakes, wheel covers, red line tires, bucket seats, 8-track stereo, woodgrain steering wheel.


**Condition:** Parked since the 1970s, and pulled out of a barn in upstate New York in 2017. Dull, faded, surface rusted paint. Some fender, sill, and door bottom rot. Pitted, corroded chrome. Rebuilt engine and transmission represented as the originals. New gas tank, exhaust system, brakes, distributor and alternator. Rebuilt original carburetors. New top, upholstery and carpet included. A seriously intriguing car that has received just enough work to make it run, drive and stop without destroying its patina.


**Bottom line:** One of a handful of similar barn find cars that consignors brought to Mecum Harrisburg this year, this GTX Hemi Convertible had gotten just enough attention to bring it to a particularly rare state, good enough to drive while looking like it just got dragged out from under a tree. Bidders gave it plenty of attention but without losing perspective. It is a specialist piece, with limited appeal except to a real fan of unrestored cars and there is little about[GTX Hemi](https://www.hagerty.com/valuation-tools/search?q=plymouth+gtx+hemi) prices that can be discerned from this transaction. To someone who appreciates its originality and scrofulous preservation, however, it is a singular value.

Mecum


**Sold for $42,900**


*Chassis no. A4C798P271943. Purple with black stripes over gray and purple cloth. Modified restoration, #3+ condition.*


**Equipment:** 360cid/220hp V-8 with Hedman tube headers, dual exhaust, automatic, power steering and brakes, Holley 4-barrel, Cragar S/S wheels, Radial T/A tires, aftermarket distributor, polished aluminum radiator, Edelbrock Torker intake, rear axle torque reaction arms.


**Condition:** Stretched old upholstery. Missing the console latch. Sound repaint. Clean, customized engine compartment with custom distributor, polished carburetor, and aluminum radiator. It is rare, however, but that also means that in 1974 it wasn’t popular. A modified high performance[Javelin AMX](https://www.hagerty.com/valuation-tools/american_motors/javelin/1974/1974-american_motors-javelin-amx?id=aCn1I000000D03fSAC) might have been the bees’ knees in the 1980s but it has lost relevance (except as a funky statement) in the 2020s.
