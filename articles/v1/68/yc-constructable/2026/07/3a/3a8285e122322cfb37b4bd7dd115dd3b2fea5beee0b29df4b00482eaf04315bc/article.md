## What is construction company software?


Construction company software covers the tools a general contractor uses to run a commercial project from bid through closeout: field operations, documentation, communication, and the financial side. For years these lived as separate products, one for drawings, another for RFIs, a third for daily logs.


The category has been moving toward[connected construction management systems](https://constructable.ai/blog/construction-management-software) in one place. The reason is the cost of fragmentation. Many GCs run several disconnected tools, and the gaps show up as duplicate data entry, missed RFIs, and onboarding that drags on. When information jumps from one login to the next, someone eventually rekeys it, and something falls through.

## Fieldwire


Hilti acquired Fieldwire in 2021, and it remains a strong field execution tool for drawing-heavy work. Version control tracks revisions, markups and punch items link to plans, and the mobile app holds up offline. Pricing is per user, free for teams up to five.


That fits specialty trades comfortable running project management and accounting separately. The gap is financial: no budgets, schedule of values, commitment tracking, or change orders. GCs sort out financial data by hand, and per-user costs climb with every sub, owner, and consultant added, which is why many teams look at[Procore alternatives for mid-market contractors](https://constructable.ai/blog/procore-alternatives-mid-market-contractors) .


## RedTeam


Since 2006, RedTeam pairs project management workflows (RFIs, submittals, change orders) with financial reporting mid-market teams value, particularly work-in-progress reports. It bundles estimating and proposal generation, and subcontractor access carries no per-user charge. Low entry pricing keeps it a middle-ground option between point tools and enterprise systems.


That fits cost-conscious GCs running mostly office-side work. The field is where it thins out. Users describe the mobile experience as hard to use on site, RFIs and punch items cannot pin to drawing locations, and the native scheduler has reliability issues with work not saving. Our[Constructable vs RedTeam](https://constructable.ai/blog/constructable-vs-redteam) comparison covers these gaps in detail.


## INGENIOUS.BUILD


INGENIOUS.BUILD is built around portfolio oversight, giving owners and developers financial visibility across many projects at once. Cost tracking and reporting run deep, which users tend to flag as a real strength, and the project hierarchy handles multi-project organization the way a development portfolio needs.


That fits developers and owner organizations watching a portfolio, but less so for a GC running a handful of concurrent projects. Creating an RFI means associating it to a project after the fact, not starting inside the project. GCs who think projects first find the portfolio-level structure a poor fit for day-to-day project delivery workflows. Read our[Constructable vs INGENIOUS.BUILD](https://constructable.ai/blog/constructable-vs-ingenious-build) comparison for a full side-by-side.


## Buildertrend


Buildertrend runs scheduling, change orders, daily logs, and document management in one system, with a client portal built for homeowner-facing communication and selection management. Estimating and takeoff tools handle residential scoping, and it syncs to QuickBooks and Xero.


That fits home builders, remodelers, and specialty contractors on residential or light commercial work. The commercial side thins. Buildertrend was built for residential workflows, not the RFI-intensive, multi-stakeholder projects mid-size commercial GCs run, so scheduling and field coordination stay lighter than dedicated commercial systems.


## Trimble ProjectSight


[Trimble ProjectSight](https://www.trimble.com/en/products/projectsight) handles RFIs, submittals, drawings, and cost tracking, and it ties into Trimble's wider portfolio of field, survey, and estimating products.


That fits mid-to-large GCs already standardized on Trimble hardware and software, where the shared ecosystem pays off. Outside that ecosystem, the pull weakens, and pricing and setup lean toward larger teams with staff to run configuration.


## CMiC


CMiC runs as a full construction ERP, with accounting, payroll, job costing, and project management sharing one database. That single-database design keeps financial and project data tied together without a separate sync layer, and the accounting depth goes well past what most project management tools attempt.


That fits large enterprise GCs with in-house IT and finance to own a long rollout. For mid-size teams the math changes. Implementation runs many months, licensing sits at the enterprise tier, and field users find the mobile experience thinner than the office side. A GC running $20M to $150M rarely needs ERP weight this heavy.

## FAQ


### How do I choose between Constructable, Procore, and Autodesk Forma for a mid-size commercial GC?


Start with your project volume and team size. Procore and Autodesk Forma are built for enterprise contractors with staff to own configuration and budgets to absorb per-user pricing and multi-month rollouts. **Constructable** is built for $20M to $150M GCs who need a connected system running field, office, and financials from day one without a dedicated software team behind it.


### Is Fieldwire a viable alternative to Constructable for GCs who also need financial tools?


Not without a separate system running alongside it. Fieldwire handles drawing-heavy field work well, but it has no budgets, schedule of values, commitment tracking, or change orders. GCs end up sorting financial data by hand and paying per-user costs that climb with every sub, owner, and consultant added to a project.


### When does RedTeam make more sense than Constructable for a construction company?


RedTeam fits cost-conscious GCs who run mostly office-side work and want bundled estimating and proposal generation at a lower entry price. If your team relies heavily on mobile, needs RFIs and punch items pinned to drawing locations, or wants offline field access, RedTeam's mobile experience and drawing workflow create friction that compounds on active jobsites.


### Which construction company software tools work best for firms managing a development portfolio versus a single project at a time?


INGENIOUS.BUILD is built around portfolio oversight for owners and developers tracking cost and reporting across many projects simultaneously. The tools listed here that fit day-to-day GC project delivery ( **Constructable** , Procore, Autodesk Forma) are organized around individual projects as the primary unit of work. The right choice depends on whether your primary job is watching a portfolio or running a project.


### What should a construction company check before committing to any software on this list?


Verify three things: whether the ERP or accounting system you use has a native two-way sync (not every tool connects to Sage 100, Viewpoint Vista, or QuickBooks without manual workarounds), whether the mobile app works offline in the field, and how long the vendor's actual onboarding takes compared to what they advertise. Most implementations run two to six months in practice regardless of what the sales sheet says.
