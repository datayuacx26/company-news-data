## The table stakes: what every serious platform must do well


Before we talk about differentiation, it’s worth naming the **baseline** capabilities you should expect from any modern construction management system.


If a platform fumbles any of these, it’s not “best in class”.


### Drawing set management


You should be able to:

Manage drawing revisions and keep everyone on the latest set.

Add markups, take measurements, and drop location‑aware pins.

If a platform requires you to open Bluebeam every time you want to do real markup or takeoff work, it’s not really drawing‑centric.


### RFI management


Good RFI tooling should:


-


Make it easy to draft, send, and track RFIs.


-


Capture a clear ball‑in‑court and due date.


-


Attach supporting files and drawing references.


-


Show a trail of responses and revisions.


An RFI typically originates in the field or during plan review, gets routed to the architect or engineer, receives a formal written response, and closes out once the team acts on that answer. Breakdowns at any stage, a missed due date, lost context between handoffs, or an unclear ball-in-court, translate directly into schedule delays and, in disputed cases, costly claims. The[Navigant Construction Forum RFI study](https://www.cmaanet.org/sites/default/files/resource/Impact%20%26%20Control%20of%20RFIs%20on%20Construction%20Projects.pdf) found the average review and response for a single RFI takes about eight hours and costs roughly $1,080, meaning a project averaging 800 RFIs carries close to $860,000 in processing overhead alone. Understanding[why RFI management breaks down](https://constructable.ai/blog/construction-rfi-management-breakdown) is the first step to preventing it. The system you use either keeps that chain tight or quietly lets it unravel.


A defensible RFI record also requires a timestamped log: who sent what, when responses were received, and which drawing or spec section the question referenced. That audit trail becomes critical during owner disputes, subcontractor claims, or project closeout, and it has to exist inside the system, not reconstructed from email threads after the fact.


In **Constructable** , RFIs are **deeply linked to the drawings and Topics** they come from:


-


You can create an RFI directly from a markup or Topic in the Drawing Viewer.


-


The RFI record brings the **exact sheet location, context, and markups** with it.


-


Once answered, RFIs become part of the project’s **searchable knowledge base** .

## FAQs


### What is the difference between an RFI and a submittal in construction?


An RFI (Request for Information) is a formal question sent to the architect or engineer to clarify design intent, what something should be or how it should work. A submittal is a contractor-provided document (shop drawings, product data, or samples) submitted for architect/engineer review and approval before work proceeds. Both create formal, timestamped records that matter during disputes and closeout.


### What features should I look for in RFI management software?


At minimum, look for clear ball-in-court tracking, due date visibility, and drawing linkage so each RFI references the exact sheet or spec section in question. A complete response history and timestamped audit trail are equally critical, they're what protects you during owner disputes, subcontractor claims, or project closeout. Bonus points if RFIs can be created directly from markups in the drawing viewer.


### How does AI help with submittal management?


Instead of manually building a submittal log from scratch, AI can read your spec sections and extract the required submittal items, then auto-populate the structured fields, trade, spec reference, description, responsible contractor. Your team reviews and refines instead of typing everything from scratch. That compression of busywork is where AI earns its keep in construction software.


### Can one platform replace Procore and Bluebeam for RFIs and submittals?


Unified systems can handle RFIs, submittals, drawing markup, and field workflows in a single environment, reducing the context switching and version confusion that comes with juggling multiple tools. The trade-off is that purpose-built tools like Bluebeam have deep feature sets some teams rely on. The real question to ask is whether your team actually uses that depth, or just defaults to what's familiar.


### What is a submittal log and why does it matter?


A submittal log is the master tracking document for every submittal required on a project, what's been submitted, what's under review, what's been approved, and which spec section each item ties back to. Without it, items fall through the cracks and material procurement stalls. It's also a key closeout document that shows the owner every required submission was properly reviewed and approved.
