## What Fieldwire Gets Right (And What It Doesn't Cover)


Fieldwire has built a solid reputation among contractors who need to move away from paper drawings and start tracking tasks digitally. The software focuses heavily on plan viewing, markup tools, and basic task management, which appeals to teams making their first jump into digital workflows.


In practice, Fieldwire works well for smaller projects where you're mostly managing drawings and punch lists. Users report that the mobile interface feels intuitive for field crews, and the ability to mark up plans directly on a tablet solves real problems. But as project complexity grows, many teams run into friction. Reporting is limited, RFI workflows feel clunky, and integration with accounting or estimating software requires workarounds.


Pricing starts at $39 per user per month for basic features, rising to $89 per user per month at the Premier tier. For a small, stable team, that's a reasonable entry point. Where it gets complicated is when your project grows, and you start adding subcontractors, consultants, or owners who need occasional access—every new login adds to the bill.


The real question isn't whether Fieldwire works. It does. The question is whether it fits how your team actually operates, and what you'll need six months from now when projects get bigger and reporting demands get heavier.


## What is Fieldwire and How Does It Work?


Fieldwire launched in 2013 as a mobile-first field management tool and was acquired by Hilti in 2021. It's used on job sites by general contractors, specialty contractors, and field crews who need to manage drawings and tasks without being chained to a desk.


The software focuses on a few key functions. You can upload drawing sets and mark them up directly from a tablet or phone. Tasks and punch list items get created and assigned to specific locations on the plans, so there's no confusion about what needs attention or where. RFIs let you ask questions tied to drawing sheets, and photos get organized by location and task. Everything syncs when you have connectivity, but the app also works offline, which matters when you're deep in a concrete structure with no signal.


Pricing runs from a free Basic plan up to $89 per user per month for the Premier tier. That per-user model means costs add up as your team grows. Fieldwire is built for field execution, not back-office work. You won't find budgeting tools, time tracking, or financial reporting here. It handles the jobsite, not the books.


## Why Consider Fieldwire Alternatives?


Fieldwire handles field execution well. The interface works for superintendents who need to mark up plans, assign tasks, and track punch lists without fighting the software. If that's all your projects require, you're probably fine sticking with it.


But most teams eventually need more. Fieldwire doesn't include time tracking, billing, invoicing, or cost management. There's no way to track budgets, manage Schedule of Values, or get financial visibility without adding another system. That creates double entry and disconnected data between what's happening in the field and what's running through your books. As[construction technology adoption becomes non-negotiable](https://www.deltek.com/en/blog/construction-tech-trends-2026) , contractors need systems that connect field and financial data without forcing multiple logins.


The per-user pricing model becomes expensive when you add subcontractors, consultants, and owners who need access. You're paying for every login, even if someone only checks in once a week. Fieldwire also lacks native accounting integrations, so connecting it to QuickBooks or your estimating software requires workarounds or third-party tools.


If you're running complex projects where RFIs, submittals, change orders, and budgets need to live in the same place as your field data, Fieldwire won't close the loop. You'll still be managing multiple systems, which is exactly what most teams are trying to get away from.

## FAQ


### When should you consider moving away from Fieldwire?


If you're managing budgets, change orders, or Schedule of Values in separate systems, or if per-user costs keep climbing as you add subcontractors and consultants, it's time to look at alternatives that connect field work directly to financial tracking without forcing you to pay for every login.


### What features matter most when comparing alternatives to Fieldwire?


Look for native financial tools (budgets, commitments, change orders) that work together with your field management, accounting integrations that don't require workarounds, and flat-rate pricing that won't punish you for adding users as projects grow.


### How does flat-rate pricing compare to Fieldwire's per-user model?


With per-user pricing, costs climb fast: add a handful of subcontractors, a consultant, and an owner rep, and your monthly bill can double before the project really gets going. Flat-rate pricing lets you add unlimited users (including subcontractors, owners, and consultants) without triggering new costs every time someone needs access.


### Can you manage change orders and budgets directly in field management software?


Most field-focused tools like Fieldwire don't include native financial management, forcing you to maintain separate systems for budgets and change orders. Some alternatives, like **Constructable** and Procore, build financial tools directly into the same product where your field data lives, eliminating double entry between jobsite activity and accounting.


### How long does switching from Fieldwire typically take?


Onboarding timelines vary. Self-serve tools leave you figuring it out alone, while hands-on migration support can get your team up and running in under three weeks with data migration included and no extra consulting fees.
