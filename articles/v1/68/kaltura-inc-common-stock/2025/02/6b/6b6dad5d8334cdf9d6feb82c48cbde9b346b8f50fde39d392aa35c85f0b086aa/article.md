“AI-powered” has become the tech industry’s favorite buzzword, slapped onto everything from toasters that supposedly know exactly how brown you like your bread, to enterprise software promising revolutionary changes. It’s everywhere. In all this noise, it’s easy to become skeptical of any technology claiming AI capabilities. I get it — I feel the same way.


But here’s what I’ve learned while working at Kaltura: beneath all the hype, there are real problems that AI is genuinely helping us solve. In the world of content management, one of our most persistent challenges is making video libraries truly accessible. Consider streaming services managing thousands of hours of content — they’re constantly working to ensure viewers can discover relevant content.


Picture trying to find a specific conversation in years’ worth of recorded meetings or locating that one crucial moment in hundreds of training videos. It’s like having a library where all the books are unmarked and unsorted — you know the information you need is there, but good luck finding it.


## The Real Pain Points Nobody Talks About


Content providers face a fundamental challenge in today’s streaming landscape. While securing premium content remains a significant investment, the greater strategic hurdle is ensuring this valuable content finds its way to the right viewers. Even the most compelling content, when it would resonate most with viewers, loses value if they can’t discover it.


Think about your favorite streaming service. They might have the perfect show for you buried somewhere in their library, but if you never discover it, that content might as well not exist. I’ve seen this scenario countless times: services invest millions in acquiring premium content, only to have it underperform simply because viewers couldn’t find it at the right moment.


This isn’t just about new content either. There’s incredible value locked away in existing libraries — classic shows that would find new audiences, documentaries that become relevant again with current events, or series that viewers would love but never discover through traditional browsing. When viewers can’t find content that speaks to them, they get frustrated and leave. When they do find it, they’re more likely to stick around and even upgrade their subscriptions.


The most painful part? Many services are actually sitting on potential goldmines. They have the content that could drive higher engagement, increase subscription renewals, and boost premium VOD purchases. But without effective ways to surface this content to the right viewers at the right time, they’re missing out on significant revenue opportunities.

## How AI Actually Changes the Game


This is where AI fundamentally transforms how we work with video content, and I want to explain precisely how this happens. When we talk about AI in video management, we’re not just adding a fancy feature — we’re introducing a powerful set of tools that work alongside our content teams to unlock the full potential of video libraries.


Consider how content teams traditionally work with videos. They spend countless hours watching footage, manually noting what happens in each scene, transcribing conversations, and trying to capture every meaningful moment. It’s painstaking work that often means important details get missed simply because humans can’t maintain that level of attention for hours on end.


Now, AI transforms this process in several interconnected ways. First, it can analyze every frame of video and every word spoken, creating detailed metadata about what’s happening on screen. Imagine being able to instantly know every time your brand’s product appears in your content library or every mention of a specific topic across thousands of videos. This isn’t just faster than manual processing — it opens up entirely new ways to understand and use your content.


But AI goes beyond simple identification. It can understand context and meaning in ways that traditional keyword systems never could. For example, it can recognize the emotional tone of scenes, understand complex interactions between characters, and identify key narrative moments. This deeper understanding means viewers can find content based on what it’s actually about, not just what keywords happen to be in the description.


The real power comes from how these capabilities work together. When AI can understand both what’s happening visually and what’s being said while also grasping the context and meaning, it creates a rich web of information about your content. This comprehensive understanding means you can surface relevant content to viewers in ways that weren’t possible before.


But here’s what really matters: This isn’t just about saving time (though that’s a massive benefit). It’s about unlocking entirely new possibilities for your content.

## Beyond Automation: The Human-AI Partnership


The most exciting aspect of AI in content management isn’t about replacing human creativity — it’s about giving content teams superpowers. Let me explain what I mean by that.


It’s crucial to understand that AI isn’t here to replace content curators — quite the opposite. The human expertise of content professionals in understanding narratives, cultural nuances, and audience preferences remains irreplaceable. What AI does is amplify this expertise by handling the time-consuming tasks that previously kept curators from focusing on their most valuable work: making creative decisions and crafting compelling content experiences.


Think about how streaming services work today. A successful service needs to do three crucial things: acquire compelling content, help viewers discover that content at the right moment, and keep them engaged enough to maintain their subscriptions. Each of these challenges requires a deep human understanding of content, audiences, and storytelling.


What AI does is amplify the capabilities of content teams to tackle these challenges more effectively. When an editorial team curates content collections, AI helps them quickly identify relevant content across their entire library. This means they can spend less time searching and more time crafting compelling viewer experiences. For example, when a popular show releases a new season, editors can rapidly find related documentaries, behind-the-scenes content, or thematically similar shows to create rich, engaging content collections that keep viewers immersed in the experience.


The impact on content monetization is particularly powerful. Consider how streaming services typically upsell premium content. Instead of showing the same premium recommendations to everyone, AI helps identify which viewers might be interested in specific content based on their viewing patterns. Content teams can create targeted promotions that feel personally relevant rather than generic upsells. This leads to higher conversion rates on premium video-on-demand offerings and special content packages.


However, perhaps the most valuable transformation is in content discovery. By deeply understanding both content and viewer preferences, AI helps surface older content to new audiences who would genuinely appreciate it. This isn’t just about maximizing content value — it’s about creating those magical moments when viewers discover exactly the right show at the right time. Those discoveries drive longer viewing sessions, higher engagement, and, ultimately, better retention rates.


The key here is that AI isn’t making these decisions independently — it’s providing insights and capabilities that enhance human judgment. Content experts still craft the strategy, curate the collections, and make the final calls on how to present content to viewers. AI simply gives them better tools to execute their vision more effectively and at a larger scale.

As content libraries continue to grow, the organizations that thrive will be those that can efficiently manage, discover, and leverage their video assets. That’s not a trendy prediction — it’s a practical reality we’re already helping our clients navigate today.
