# Stop just expanding, scale: The smart way to grow

Growth is easy, scaling is hard. That’s the brutal truth most companies learn too late.
Year-over-year growth is every company’s dream. But in a world of shrinking budgets and rising complexity, growth alone can bury you.


There’s a common myth that as a company matures, its business operations naturally evolve in sync. As a business operations professional, I believe it’s not true. Business maturity doesn’t guarantee operational maturity. And when growth outpaces operational infrastructure, the result is often chaos: rising costs, inefficiencies, and unhappy customers.


Growth is the obvious goal. But at some point, **the way** companies grow becomes just as important as how much they grow. Companies that expand without intention often break their systems and risk burning out their teams. Those that scale smart, however, create sustainable, resilient systems that support long-term success.


In this article, I’ll break down the core principles of scalable operations: what they mean, why they matter, and how we applied them at Kaltura to support major growth without hiring more people or increasing our budget. Whether you’re an ops leader, a founder, a revenue leader, or simply someone curious about how to build systems that scale, this post will give you a practical framework you can adapt to your challenges.
Let’s dive into how to scale, not just grow.


## What is scaling? Understanding the difference between growth and scale


While often used interchangeably, growth and scale are not the same. Companies often proudly declare they’re growing when in fact, they’re scaling.


**Growth** refers to increasing **outputs** , such as revenue, users, segments, or products, by increasing **inputs** , including hiring more people, spending more on tools, or expanding budgets. **Scale** , in contrast, is about increasing outputs ***without*** increasing inputs at the same rate. It’s the ability to do **more with less** , through better systems and multi-use systems, optimized processes, and automation.


For example, if your revenue grows 30% and you hire 30% more staff to manage it, that’s growth. But if you achieve the same revenue increase by improving workflows, reusing tools, and reducing manual tasks, that’s scale.


Companies that focus only on growth often find themselves overwhelmed: bloated teams, inefficient operations, data silos, and frustrated leadership. Scale ensures that growth doesn’t come at the cost of stability.


## Why scaling?


Scale is not just nice to have; it’s a strong indicator of your success. It saves you time, money, and excessive energy on non-relevant processes. It helps you explore multi-usage systems to avoid additional training on new tools and maintain a “one source of truth” approach with no data silos. Multi-use systems will increase your effectiveness, boost team adoption, and enhance productivity.
It will help you build a resilient business, enabling it to handle a spike in volume and prevent a crash. And, of course, this will lead to significant cost reduction: fewer tools, less vendor dependency, reduced manual work, and reduced training, resulting in meaningful cost savings.


Scaling operations is powerful, but it comes with trade-offs. One of the biggest challenges is the loss of personalization. If your business depends on highly customized workflows or white-glove service for each client, scaling might not be the right fit. That’s because personalization, by nature, requires flexibility and manual attention, while scaling is about creating repeatable, standardized processes. The more tailored a process is, the harder it becomes to replicate at scale without significantly increasing costs or complexity.


## The role of operations in scaling


Operations are at the heart of every company’s ability to scale. Our job goes far beyond building workflows or automating tasks. We are the architects of systems that enable sustainable growth, regardless of headcount, budget, or market conditions. And the headcount is counting on us to support the company’s growth.


But our real responsibility is to prepare the business for surges in customer demand, changes in GTM strategy, or shifts in the competitive landscape. Scaling can start from the first day in the business. It doesn’t matter if you’re a young startup, a growing company, or a mature enterprise; it’s all about changing your **mindset** and thinking on a larger scale. Scaling isn’t one small project; it’s a mindset that can be implemented from day one.


But before we get into tactics, I need to address a common belief that I, personally, don’t share. Let me explain why.


## Four stages of business operations maturity


Not every company is in the same place, and that’s okay. What matters is knowing where you stand and building the right strategy from there. There are four stages of business maturity — Startup, Growth, Maturity, and Enterprise.


Not related to those are the four stages of ***business operations*** maturity.


The first stage of business operations is **Ad-hoc** : Teams operate with emails, spreadsheets, and disconnected tools. Processes are manual, inconsistent, and vary from person to person. Data is often siloed or missing, making reporting and visibility nearly impossible.


The second is the **Structured** stage **:** Basic tools like CRMs and project management systems are in place. Processes are documented but not optimized, and teams still rely heavily on manual effort. Data exists but lacks full accuracy or integration.


The third stage is **Automated:** Automation reduces manual tasks, such as quotes, alerts, and workflows, which are triggered without human intervention. Tools are integrated, and processes run with consistency. Teams begin to scale without adding headcount.


Last but not least is **Predictive:** We all want to be in this stage. Data is used proactively to anticipate churn, forecast pipeline, and guide decision-making. AI and BI tools surface insights before issues arise. Operations shift from reactive to strategic and foresighted. The system is working for us: predicted churn, new logos, etc.


And here is the myth: Many assume that as a company grows, its business operations naturally mature alongside it. For example, the startup phase needs to work with ad-hoc business operations. In reality, that’s rarely the case. There’s no real correlation between company size or stage and business operations maturity.


Scaling and building scalable operations isn’t about how big your company is. A startup can build predictive operations, and an enterprise can work in the **Ad hoc** operations stage. Scaling is about your mindset and your way of working; you can build for scale from day one.


At Kaltura, we walk the walk, and we are scaling effectively by focusing on reuse, automation, and strategic simplification. Let me show you how we did it in Kaltura.


## A practical case study: CSM expanding its responsibilities to new logos


When our Customer Success team was assigned a new business segment, the immediate instinct could have been to hire additional staff to meet the new demand. The growth approach would be: ‘Let’s increase our headcount and hire more sales reps, CSMs, or SDRs’.


We put on our scalable sunglasses and asked, ‘What do we already have that can be repurposed?’


To support a new business segment without adding headcount, we looked inward and found an opportunity to repurpose a system originally used by another team. We modified it and built a new automated workflow focused on lead engagement. We built an automated SDR machine. By identifying our highest-converting marketing channels, we designed sequences that automatically nurtured leads, offering value propositions and direct booking links. This allowed prospects to qualify themselves, so by the time they reached our team, they were informed, ready to engage, and far more likely to convert.


The result was a significant increase in MQL(Marketing Qualified Leads)-to-Meeting set conversion rates, a lighter workload for our CSMs, and no need to hire additional staff or purchase a new tool.
It was a powerful example of how reusing tools and reconfiguring internal systems can expand team capacity without raising costs.


## Why scaling matters more than ever


In a business environment shaped by AI and automation, the ability to scale operations is becoming a competitive advantage. Period. What once required large teams can now be accomplished with smart systems, predictive tools, and leaner processes.


When scaling is prioritized, the entire organization begins to operate with greater clarity and focus. Teams understand their roles and execute with consistency, supported by unified, reliable data. Leaders gain the ability to forecast with confidence, backed by systems that offer real-time visibility. Most importantly, resources are used efficiently, avoiding unnecessary hires or wasted time and allowing the business to grow without being weighed down. And as a result, growth becomes sustainable and resilient.


How can we talk about scaling in 2025 without mentioning AI?


## Scaling in the AI era


We all know that AI isn’t just a tool; it’s an accelerator for our day-to-day tasks. In a world where doing more with less is the goal, AI is a critical part of the equation.


As AI becomes a more integral part of our lives, it’s also reshaping the way we design and execute business processes, making them smarter, faster, and more efficient. The more effectively we implement AI, especially in revenue-related operations, from lead generation to conversion to a paying customer, the more value we can extract. It allows us to streamline processes, enhance decision-making, and help teams do more with less, freeing up time for rest or higher-impact work.


In the world of operations, AI has transformed everything in real time. Startups leveraging AI are scaling before they even grow, and today, a “lean” company often means an AI-native one. That’s why companies that invest in operational efficiency today are positioning themselves as tomorrow’s market leaders.


## Conclusion: Scaling isn’t a nice-to-have; it’s a strategic necessity.


If I could sum up everything in one sentence, it would be: **Put on the scaling sunglasses** . **** Change your mindset, avoid chaos, and save resources.


Scaling means building a business that grows without breaking, where chaos is avoided, resources are maximized, and operations become a competitive advantage. Whether you’re a startup laying foundations or an enterprise managing complexity, scalable operations need to be woven into your company’s DNA to stay resilient, flexible, and future-ready.


As operators, it’s our role to ask **bold** questions, challenge outdated systems, and architect the workflows and infrastructure that support growth, not just for now, but for every version of tomorrow’s business.


Because in a world shaped by AI, uncertainty, and speed, it’s not the biggest companies that win. It’s the ones that scale smart.


One thing is for sure: scaling is no longer optional; it’s a necessity. It’s the edge that separates the overwhelmed from the unstoppable.
