# Building Sovereign, AI-Ready Private Cloud for Southeast Asia: Viettel IDC and Broadcom’s Next Chapter

-
-
-


Across Asia, data residency expectations are increasing and the requirement for secure, regional cloud infrastructure is accelerating. As enterprises move from AI experimentation into full production, running fine-tuned models, building language models on internal data, and deploying agentic AI - the question of where training and prompt data physically resides has become non-negotiable.


To meet this regional demand, Viettel IDC is uniquely positioned to deliver sovereign-by-design infrastructure that satisfies strict jurisdiction and compliance requirements, in partnership with Broadcom.


## Why Customers Turn to Viettel IDC


Viettel IDC is the data center and cloud arm of Viettel Group, one of Vietnam's largest telecommunications companies. With 18 years in the market and operations spanning 15 data centers across more than 57,000 square meters, it serves over 40,000 customers, including more than 3,000 on its cloud ecosystem. In 2025, Broadcom recognized Viettel IDC as the VMware Cloud Foundation as a Service Partner of the Year for the Asia Pacific region, reflecting its leadership in delivering VCF-based managed cloud services to enterprise and government customers across Vietnam.


The challenges customers bring to Viettel IDC are consistent: aging infrastructure that is costly to scale, risky migrations, mounting sovereignty obligations, cost pressure, and a lack of AI readiness — often spread across several vendors. They want a partner for secure modernization and growth in the AI era, not just another infrastructure provider. Viettel IDC's answer is a sovereign cloud delivered as a fully managed platform, built on VMware Cloud Foundation (VCF).


## The Viettel IDC and VMware Cloud Foundation Advantage


Most providers assemble a cloud from separate parts: one stack for virtualization, another for containers, others for storage, networking, security, and AI, often across different vendors. Viettel IDC's advantage is that VMware Cloud Foundation consolidates all of that into a single, consistent operating model. Its public cloud services — ‘Viettel Virtual Private Cloud’ and ‘Viettel Cloud Server’ — and its fully isolated Viettel Dedicated Private Cloud all run on the same VCF-based foundation. Traditional VMs, cloud-native applications, and AI workloads operate together in one architecture, so customers gain lower operational complexity, stronger resiliency, and simpler governance instead of managing fragmented environments.


That foundation also lets customers modernize at their own pace, without re-platforming as they grow:


- Consolidate virtualization on Viettel Cloud Server or Viettel Virtual Private Cloud to cut operational cost.
- Expand into managed services — Managed Kubernetes, Managed Database, Backup & DR, and GPU Cloud — as needs grow.
- Evolve the most sensitive workloads into Viettel Dedicated Private Cloud, with consistent governance across public and private environments.


Every step sits on the same platform, so traditional VMs, containers, and AI applications run side by side under one set of lifecycle and security controls.


## How Viettel IDC's Customers Put VCF to Work


Across its customer base, Viettel IDC delivers these capabilities as a fully managed service with sovereign-grade security and governance:


- Government and public sector: Citizen-facing and internal digital-government systems, with managed Kubernetes modernizing legacy apps under local data-residency rules.
- BFSI and large enterprises: Core applications modernized on private cloud and managed databases, with resilient virtualization and disaster recovery.
- Telecom and digital platforms: Scalable container and compute for high-traffic services needing rapid provisioning and high availability.
- Universities and higher education: Managed GPU cloud, Kubernetes, and databases for e-learning, research, and AI programs.
- Enterprise AI: Private AI and GPU cloud for inference, model training, and knowledge platforms — keeping sensitive data inside Vietnam.


Among these verticals, one example is banking and financial services (BFSI) vertical, where infrastructure choices directly touch security, compliance, and customer trust. For Viettel IDC's BFSI customers, consolidating onto VCF translates into measurable outcomes: according to Viettel's experience, costs are reduced by roughly 25–40% and provisioning time cut by more than 70%. Application deployment cycles shrink from weeks to hours or days, and unplanned downtime incidents fall by 60–80%, with service availability reaching 99.99%.


Vietnam's Personal Data Protection Law took effect on January 1, 2026, and as a Broadcom Pinnacle Partner, Viettel IDC delivers sovereign-by-design controls that customers can audit, not merely trust.


## The Next Chapter


Together with Broadcom, Viettel IDC is establishing a managed, VCF-based sovereign cloud as a standard, leveraging its Vietnam data center footprint and the VCSP program to reach customers who need both global technology and local accountability. Over the coming year, it will expand its joint enablement program with Broadcom across Malaysia, the Philippines, Indonesia, Thailand, and Sri Lanka, bringing Private AI-as-a-Service and private cloud foundation capabilities to enterprises and governments in regulated sectors where AI must run inside the jurisdiction: banking and financial services, government, healthcare, and telecommunications.


That ambition reflects a wider regional shift. Across Southeast Asia, sovereignty is no longer just a feature of private cloud, it is becoming the reason for it. As governments strengthen residency rules, exemplified by Vietnam's Personal Data Protection Law, Cybersecurity Law, and Data Law, which closely parallel data protection frameworks across rest of Asia, infrastructure security and control have become a priority.


Viettel IDC is an example of that shift, providing an enterprise-grade private cloud that is sovereign by default and AI-ready by design — showing how the region's most sensitive workloads can modernize without leaving home.
