## What Changes in Operations with the Duplicata Escritural


The duplicata is an important instrument for credit, advance payment, assignment, and collateral operations. For decades, however, its management has relied on documents, files, and controls distributed among different participants.


Law No. 13,775/2018 established the legal framework for the Duplicata Escritural. Subsequently, BCB Resolution No. 339 established rules for how the ecosystem operates, including the role of the entities responsible for bookkeeping and registering the instruments. The model has also undergone subsequent regulatory updates, such as BCB Resolution No. 540.


For institutions, adapting involves much more than establishing a technical connection. It is necessary to review how data is received, validated, submitted, accessed, and updated throughout the operation.


## Fragmented Operations Increase Operational Risk


In many institutions, information about invoices, duplicatas, contracts, and credit operations moves through systems that do not communicate directly with one another.


An ERP may centralize commercial data, while the credit system records the financial transaction. Funds, receivables platforms, back-office teams, and collection departments may operate in other environments, using different rules and formats.


This fragmentation may result in:


- Discrepancies between invoice and duplicata data;
- Rejections when information is submitted;
- The need for manual reprocessing;
- Parallel controls maintained in spreadsheets;
- Difficulty monitoring the status of the instruments;
- Rework across credit, risk, operations, and back-office teams;
- Limited traceability of changes and events.


Under the Duplicata Escritural model, an inconsistency at any stage may affect the issuance, registration, negotiation, or settlement of the duplicata. For this reason, integration quality becomes a central part of regulatory compliance.
