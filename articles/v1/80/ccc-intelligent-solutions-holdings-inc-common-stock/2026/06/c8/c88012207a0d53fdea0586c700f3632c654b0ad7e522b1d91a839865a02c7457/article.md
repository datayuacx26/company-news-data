# Calibrations Aren’t “Extra” Anymore — It’s the Job

Let’s call it what it is: calibrations aren’t occasional add-ons anymore.


If you repair a vehicle with even minimal damage, one or more calibrations is very likely a part of completing the repair.


## Today’s Cars Work on Precision


Modern vehicles rely on cameras, radar, and sensors to guide how key systems operate:


- Following distance
- Lane positioning
- Object detection
- Driver alerts and responses


All of that depends on exact positioning — down to tiny measurements that can’t be observed or validated with a visual inspection.


## What Changes During a Repair? More Than You Think


You don’t need a major wreck to throw things off.


Everyday work can shift sensor position:


- Bumper removal or replacement
- Windshield removal
- Suspension or alignment work
- Front-end or body repairs


Even small changes can affect how those systems interpret what’s in front of them.


## The Gap Between “Looks Fixed” and “Is Fixed”


A vehicle can leave the booth looking perfect. Paint matches. Panels line up.


But under the surface?


- A camera could be aimed slightly off
- A radar unit could be reading distance differently
- A system could be reacting slower or differently than designed


That’s because these systems don’t care how the car looks — they rely on measurements and angles being exact.


## Why Does Precision Matter For Calibrations?


We’re talking about tolerances as small as fractions of a degree.


At speed, that tiny difference turns into real-world impact:


- Misread distance
- Incorrect detection
- Inconsistent system behavior


Even a slight shift in alignment can change how the vehicle responds. A camera that’s off by just one degree can cause a 5-foot miscalculation at 100 yards. And when a driver is traveling at 60mph, it takes just **3.4 seconds** to go 100 yards. A 5-foot miscalculation is more than enough to cause an accident.


## What Does a Calibration Actually Do?


Put simply: Calibrations tell the vehicle where everything is again.


It brings cameras and sensors back to manufacturer specs so they are positioned and operating properly:


- Read inputs correctly
- Communicate properly with the system
- Perform the way they’re designed to


Without that step, you’re leaving the job unfinished.


## Not a Specialty — A Standard Step


Across the industry, calibration is quickly becoming routine work.


Not just for heavy hits, but for everyday jobs:


- Glass replacement
- Alignments
- Minor collisions
- Parts replacement


If the repair affects position, geometry, or mounting — calibration is on the table.


## What This Means for Shops


The expectations have shifted.


Doing the job right now means:


- Checking if one or more calibrations are needed
- Following OEM procedures
- Making it part of your standard workflow


Because this isn’t about upselling — it’s about completing the work the right way.


## The Bottom Line


Calibrations aren’t a “nice to have”.
They’re not optional.
**They’re a regular part of the repair process.**


And shops that treat it that way are the ones staying ahead. Having the right tools and processes in place are critical to performing safe and proper repairs. Every time.
