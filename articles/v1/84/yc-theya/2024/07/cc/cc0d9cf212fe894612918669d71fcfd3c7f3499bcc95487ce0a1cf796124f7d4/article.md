# Inflation and Fixed Income: Bitcoin In Retirement Planning

Inflation progressively erodes the[purchasing power of our money](https://blog.theya.us/inflation-hidden-tax/) , impacting everyone in the economy. In particular, current retirees and fixed-income earners may find that inflation significantly undermines their ability to sustain their standard of living in later years. This highlights the necessity for younger generations and retirees alike to incorporate inflation considerations into their savings and investment strategies. What follows explores the implications of inflation on fixed-income earners and retirees, highlighting why proactive planning, informed by current economic realities, is essential for future financial security. ## **How Inflation Affects Fixed Income Earners and Retirees**


As many readers know, inflation is primarily caused by monetary expansion (i.e. money printing) from governments and central banks. In turn, inflation erodes the purchasing power of money. This leaves individuals and businesses struggling to keep up with a perpetually rising cost of living. For example, ten dollars may buy you 5 oranges this year, but will only buy you 3 oranges next year. The same logic can be applied to apples, houses, appliances, cars, and any good or service you can imagine.


Notably, inflation does not impact everyone equally. The Cantillon Effect dictates that those closest to the source of new money creation benefit disproportionately at the expense of those on the periphery of the financial system, who fall even further behind. One group inflation impacts most is current retirees and those living on fixed incomes. This is because their earnings are fixed in value, rather than growing through promotions, employment changes, or salary/wage increases. As a result of inflation, those earning a fixed income (such as those in retirement) are able to purchase fewer goods and services as time goes on since the purchasing power of their income is constantly being eroded. In other words, they are earning less money each year when measured in real terms, while earning the same amount nominally.


For example, consider a retiree who receives a fixed annual income of $40,000. If the annual inflation rate is 7%, the purchasing power of that $40,000 decreases each year. In the first year, the retiree can purchase $40,000 worth of goods and services. However, in the second year, due to 7% inflation, the same goods and services would cost $42,800. Therefore, the retiree's $40,000 would only buy about 93% of what it did the previous year. Over five years, with a consistent 7% inflation rate, the purchasing power of that fixed $40,000 income would effectively decrease to about $28,475 in today's dollars. After ten years, the purchasing power would drop even further, to about $20,810 in today's dollars. This means after 10 years at 7% inflation, the retiree can buy roughly half of what their fixed annual income originally bought, despite still receiving $40,000 nominally. Evidently, this is not sustainable in the long run.


To mitigate the adverse effects of inflation on fixed incomes, it is crucial to consider investment strategies that can help preserve or grow purchasing power. This can be done by diversifying investment portfolios to include inflation-resistant assets, such as[Bitcoin or Gold](https://blog.theya.us/bitcoin-vs-gold/) . By understanding and planning for the impact of inflation, younger generations can better plan for retirement and current retirees can work to maintain their standard of living. ### **Bitcoin as a Store of Value**


First and foremost, Bitcoin has emerged as an incredible store of value, evidenced by it being the best-performing asset of the last decade. This is due to a confluence of factors, but perhaps most notably its monetary properties. Bitcoin has a maximum supply of 21 million coins, making it inherently scarce and immune from the inflationary pressures that fiat currencies face due to unlimited money printing from central banks and governments.


This scarcity, combined with its decentralized nature that ensures no one can unilaterally dictate or change the rules of the network are contributors of its significant value appreciation. As a result, Bitcoin is increasingly seen as “digital gold,” offering a hedge against inflation and currency devaluation. Investors increasingly acknowledge its ability to preserve wealth over the long term, making it a valuable addition to retirement portfolios and an attractive option for younger generations aiming to accumulate future wealth.


### **Bitcoin in Pension Funds**


The adoption of Bitcoin in pension funds is a significant shift in institutional investment strategies that reflects growing confidence in the asset's long-term viability. Pension funds, historically conservative in their investment approaches, are beginning to allocate a portion of their portfolios to Bitcoin to diversify and protect against inflationary risks. This trend is driven by the need to achieve higher returns in an environment where traditional fixed-income assets may no longer provide sufficient returns to keep pace with inflation.


For instance, pension funds such as the Fairfax County Police Officers Retirement System and the Fairfax County Employees’ Retirement System have made strategic allocations to Bitcoin. This decision was influenced by the strong historical price performance of Bitcoin, its non-correlation with traditional financial markets, and its ability to serve as an inflation hedge. By incorporating Bitcoin into their portfolios, pension funds look to enhance the resilience and growth potential of their portfolio.


Pension funds are increasingly attracted to Bitcoin's liquidity and its growing acceptance within the traditional financial industry. Bitcoin now trades on major exchanges and its integration into financial services offers greater flexibility and accessibility. Clear regulatory frameworks, including the approval of[Bitcoin exchange-traded funds (ETFs)](https://blog.theya.us/bitcoin-vs-bitcoin-etf/) in the United States, provide institutional investors with the transparency needed to invest in this novel asset. These factors, combined with Bitcoin's tremendous growth potential and its resilience in times of economic uncertainty, make it a stellar addition to pension fund portfolios worldwide.
