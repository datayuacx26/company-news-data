# Bending Spoons Acquires Airtable: What's next?

On August 4, 2026, **Bending Spoons agreed to buy Airtable for $1.285 billion** . If your business runs on Airtable, this week’s acquisition probably made you wonder: should I be worried, and… what happens next?


The short answer is, Airtable will continue as it is for the next while, probably a long while… However, **will Airtable still keep up with where your business is going two or three years from now?** That's the uncertainty every acquisition introduces.


It's worth taking ten minutes to separate **what changes today from what might change later,** and what you can do about it either way.


If your team relies on Airtable to run important parts of your business (whether that’s internal operations, customer portals, partner workflows, or client delivery) this article is for you.


### **TL;DR**


- Bending Spoons agreed to acquire Airtable for $1.285 billion on August 4, 2026 ([Inc, 2026](https://www.inc.com/lucia-auerbach/bending-spoons-buying-airtable-for-1-billion-valued-at-11-billion-in-2021/91384406) ).
- Nothing changes in your Airtable account today. **Your Airtable base, automations, and integrations aren't going to stop working overnight**
- Bending Spoons’ past acquisitions have often been followed by **leaner teams and pricing changes** : Evernote's Pro plan rose from $37 a year to $250 a year after its 2022 acquisition, and WeTransfer lost 75% of its staff within two months of being bought in 2024.
- Many businesses are **reducing their dependency on Airtable's** roadmap by treating it as the database beneath a system designed around how they actually work.
- This is a good moment to check how dependent your business is on Airtable today and understand your options.


## **What did Bending Spoons actually agree to buy?**


Bending Spoons signed a deal to acquire 100% of Airtable's shares for an enterprise value of $1.285 billion, with an equity value of roughly $2.25 billion once Airtable's cash reserves are counted in. The deal was announced on August 4, 2026, and is expected to close later in the year, subject to regulatory approval ([Bending Spoons investor newsroom, 2026](https://investors.bendingspoons.com/newsroom/bending-spoons-agrees-to-acquire-airtable) ).


Airtable was valued at $11 billion in a 2021 funding round, so this deal values the company at a fraction of its old price tag ([Inc, 2026](https://www.inc.com/lucia-auerbach/bending-spoons-buying-airtable-for-1-billion-valued-at-11-billion-in-2021/91384406) ). Airtable still serves more than 500,000 organizations, including 80% of the Fortune 100, and both companies say they'll operate separately until the deal closes ([Bending Spoons investor newsroom, 2026](https://investors.bendingspoons.com/newsroom/bending-spoons-agrees-to-acquire-airtable) ).


Bending Spoons CEO Luca Ferrari said the company is "committed to investing in Airtable for the long run." Airtable co-founder and CEO Howie Liu framed the deal as giving Airtable "the resources and the long-term commitment" to keep building ([Bending Spoons investor newsroom, 2026](https://investors.bendingspoons.com/newsroom/bending-spoons-agrees-to-acquire-airtable) ).


## **What does Bending Spoons usually do after it buys a company?**


Bending Spoons has consistently run leaner businesses after acquisitions. That doesn't mean Airtable will stop working or that investment will disappear overnight. It does, however, raise reasonable questions about **how quickly the product will evolve from here.**


Bending Spoons is an Italian software company that has built its business by buying established consumer and productivity apps and running them with far smaller teams. As of 2025, the company generated $1.31 billion in revenue with around 620 employees across a portfolio serving more than 500 million monthly users ([TechCrunch, 2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/05/what-is-bending-spoons-everything-to-know-about-aols-acquirer/) ).


Its revenue per employee jumped from $1.12 million in 2023 to $2.57 million in 2025, which gives a sense of how the company runs its products after buying them: fewer people, more automation, and pricing that gets revisited ([TechCrunch, 2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/05/what-is-bending-spoons-everything-to-know-about-aols-acquirer/) ). **Here's what that looked like for three products it has bought in the past four years** .

## **What should you actually do in the next 90 days?**


Don't start planning a migration. Start by understanding how dependent your business is on Airtable today. Ask yourself four questions:


- **If Airtable doubled in price next year, what would actually change for your business?** Would you absorb the cost, limit access for users, or start looking for alternatives?
- **Who relies on Airtable every day?** List every employee, customer, or partner who works directly inside Airtable today. Which workflows would be most affected if Airtable's roadmap slowed down?
- **How much of your business depends on Airtable filling product gaps?** If you're already relying on[Airtable extensions](https://noloco.io/blog/top-10-airtable-extensions) or several linked Interfaces to patch gaps, that’s often a sign you've outgrown using Airtable as the place where your business runs, not just where data lives.


None of this requires an immediate decision. They simply help you understand how much control you have before circumstances force your hand.
