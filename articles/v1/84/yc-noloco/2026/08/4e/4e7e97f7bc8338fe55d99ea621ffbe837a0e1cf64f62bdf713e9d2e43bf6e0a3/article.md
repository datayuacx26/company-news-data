## What is Zite good at, and where does it fall short for growing teams?


Zite is built for speed. You describe what you want, it generates a working app, and you can have something clickable in minutes. Every Zite plan, including the free one, allows unlimited users with no per-seat fee, which can make it attractive for teams that want to avoid paying for every additional user.


The trade-offs become more important once the app needs regular changes and becomes part of day-to-day work. Zite's free plan includes 50 credits and 5,000 database records a month; the $15 Pro plan raises that to 100 credits and 100,000 records; the $55 Business plan goes to 200 credits and 250,000 records ([Zite pricing](https://www.zite.com/pricing) , checked August 2026). Most changes to a Zite app happen through a new prompt, not direct editing, which adds up in credits and time as the app grows.


That's a fair trade for a first draft. It's a worse trade once three people on your team need to tweak a view every week, or a client asks for their own login.


**Key takeaways**


- Zite's free plan caps out at 5,000 records and 50 credits a month; paid plans raise both limits but don't remove them.
- Unlimited users with no per-seat fee is a real strength, on every plan.
- Zite's AI-first workflow can be a good fit for getting started quickly, but teams making frequent changes should consider how they'll manage those changes over time..


## What should you look for in a Zite alternative?


Before comparing tools, it helps to know what you're actually solving for. Most teams outgrowing Zite have already answered the "is this idea worth building" question. What they're solving for next looks less like a faster prompt box and more like this:


- Can someone other than the person who built it make a change, without waiting on a new prompt or breaking something?
- Can a client or contractor be given their own restricted view, safely, without exposing the rest of the system?
- Does the app still hold up when the process behind it changes, a new step gets added, a role gets renamed, a client gets added mid-project?
- The ability to work with the data sources you already use, such as Airtable, spreadsheets, or a database.
- Pricing you can budget for, with clear limits you understand before the app becomes part of day-to-day operations.
- The option to still prompt for speed on a first build or a big new section, without giving up the ability to edit by hand afterward.

## How do you choose between Zite, Noloco, and the rest?


- **Choose Zite when:** one person owns the app, nobody outside your team needs a login, and you're still validating the idea.
- **Choose Noloco when:** you're building a portal your business will rely on day to day, and you need your team to run delivery and operations from one place while clients, partners, or other external stakeholders get the right access to the same underlying information.
- **Choose Softr or Bubble when:** visual app building is your priority and their approach better fits the kind of app you're building. Softr is a strong option for portals and directories, while Bubble is better suited to highly custom products and workflows.
- **Choose Retool when:** a developer on your team will own the build and wants more control over logic and integrations.
- **Choose Glide when:** your team works mostly from a phone, in the field, rather than at a desk.


## How do you move from Zite to a new platform without losing your data?


Here's the order that avoids the most common mistakes:


1. Export your data out of Zite first, or confirm you can connect to the same source (Airtable, a spreadsheet, or a database) that Zite was reading from.
2. List everyone who needs access and what they should be able to see: your team, any contractors, and any clients.
3. Decide where your business data will live before rebuilding. For example, you might keep Airtable as your source of truth, move to a database, or start with a new setup.
4. Rebuild your most-used view first, not every screen at once, so you can test the new setup with a real workflow before rebuilding everything else.
5. Invite one real client or teammate to test their specific permission level before rolling it out to everyone.

## Frequently asked questions


**What is Zite used for?**
Zite is an AI app builder. You describe what you want in plain language, and it generates a working web app connected to its own database. It's aimed at getting a fast, lightweight app up and running quickly.


**Is Zite free to use?**
Yes, Zite has a free plan with 50 credits and 5,000 database records a month. Paid plans start at $15 a month for a higher credit and record ceiling ([Zite pricing](https://www.zite.com/pricing) , checked August 2026).


**What's the difference between an AI app builder and a visual no-code builder?**
An AI app builder like Zite or Base44 generates and changes your app mainly through prompts, so every change, big or small, uses part of your credit allowance. A visual no-code builder like Softr or Bubble lets you drag, drop, and edit directly. **Noloco sits across both** : its AI assistant, Nola, can build from a prompt, but you can also edit it by drag and drop. The practical difference shows up after launch: with a pure AI builder, a small change still costs you a new instruction; with Noloco, only the AI-assisted parts use credits.


**Can I move my app from Zite to another platform later?**
Usually yes, as long as your data isn't locked entirely inside Zite's own database. If you're planning to switch, it's easier to connect your alternative to a data source you control, like Airtable or a spreadsheet, from the start.


**Which Zite alternative is best for apps that clients will log into?**


Softr and Noloco both support client-facing access, but they suit different levels of operational complexity. Noloco is better suited when the app needs to support more complex operations. For example, when your team and clients need to work from the same data across multiple workflows, with different roles, views, and permissions. That makes Noloco a stronger fit when the client portal is part of a larger system your business relies on to run day-to-day work.


**When does a team outgrow Zite entirely?**
A team should start reassessing its setup when the app becomes part of day-to-day operations and the original workflow no longer fits. For example, when several people need to maintain it, different roles need different access, or clients and partners need to use it regularly. At that point, a tool built to be run on and handed off, like Noloco, tends to fit better than one built mainly for a fast, lightweight app.
