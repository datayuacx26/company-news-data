# The New Era of FedRAMP: Expediting SSP Submissions via OSCAL

The Federal Risk and Authorization Program (FedRAMP) is designed to provide a standardized approach to security authorizations for cloud service offerings. Yet, despite efforts like the[FedRAMP Authorization Act](https://www.congress.gov/bill/117th-congress/house-bill/8956/text) , recent[reporting](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-23-105482) from the Government Accountability Office (GAO) shows a serious need for improvement as many agencies aren’t actually implementing the requirements in vendor acquisitions.


Up against these implementation challenges, FedRAMP is trying something new: adopting the use of Open Security Controls Assessment Language ([OSCAL](https://pages.nist.gov/OSCAL/) ) for all packages. What this means is that now all FedRAMP packages should follow OSCAL formats to provide machine-readable representations of control catalogs, control baselines, system security plans and assessment plans and results. Although it may sound like another box to check – especially for the organizations struggling to meet existing FedRAMP standards – OSCAL is expected to bring a number of benefits. For instance,[according to FedRAMP](https://www.fedramp.gov/FedRAMP-moves-to-automate-the-authorization-process/) :


- Cloud Service Providers (CSPs) will be able to create their System Security Plans (SSPs) more rapidly and accurately, validating much of their content before submission to the government for review.
- Third Party Assessment Organizations (3PAOs) will be able to automate the planning, execution, and reporting of cloud assessment activities.
- Agencies will be able to expedite their reviews of the FedRAMP security authorization packages.


This also means that the FedRAMP[marketplace](https://marketplace.fedramp.gov/products) of 400+ offerings must ensure their security controls are represented in OSCAL, opening the door for long-due compliance overhauls.
