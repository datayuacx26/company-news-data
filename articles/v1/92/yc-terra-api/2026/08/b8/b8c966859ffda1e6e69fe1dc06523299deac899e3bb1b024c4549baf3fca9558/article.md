# How I came second in The World's Hardest Triathlon | Terra

## Swim: Alone, and Well Paced


I actually really enjoyed the swim. Three point eight kilometers in Eidfjord, about an hour, roughly 1:35/100 m. The distance looks accurate, but the swim itself still felt slow, which could have been due to the current. Water temperature felt like 15–16°C to me and was comfortable.


I did the whole thing on my own, at my own pace. I desperately wanted to hang onto the feet in front of me, but I’ve previously been reminded of the limits of my swim fitness. With limited training, I can still start hard for three to five minutes and then blow up. So I didn’t.


I’m discarding the swim heart-rate file entirely. I’m not sure what was going on, because I was wearing a strap, but the HR on file is wrong.


## Bike: A bit of an Optimistic Start


***Figure 1:** Time in 25 W zones. How long I spent in each 25 W bin. The band 275–349 W is most of the ride; quite a bit of time freewheeling down descents (0–24 W); almost nothing above 375 W.*


The Bike: about 179 km, average speed just under 34 km/h, over three thousand meters of ascent, average power 275 W, normalized 300 W, average HR 132, median cadence 85.


Out of T1 I thought I could get away with a fairly hard start. That first climb is an awesome 1200m ascent up a valley, through rock tunnels and past misty waterfalls.


The numbers are blunt about this error of judgement. The steepest early block, roughly kilometers 10 to 15, nine-ish per cent, sat around 354 W normalized. Later climbs of similar character were closer to 285–300 W. A fifteen per cent drop. I definitely paid for this exuberance, and I had to purposely go easy for a section in the middle of the bike.


***Figure 2:** Climb power (first vs later) Average watts on sustained bike climbs. Left bars are the opening fjord climb (1a/1b); right bars are later climbs. Dashed line marks my first-climb level (~343 W). Later similar grades sit ~286–307 W, about 10–15% lower.*


What surprised me is how okay the later climbs still were. I mostly pushed between 275 and 349 watts, with the biggest bin at 300–324 W for over an hour.


Compared with two-plus years ago, I’m maybe five per cent off on power for this type of effort (but much more “sensitive” to high-intensity efforts), amazing given zero structured sessions. But I’m also five-plus per cent heavier, without the old fatigue resistance.


## Run: Dying on Zombie Hill, or Not?


Full run: about 42 km in just under four hours, overall 5:44/km, average HR 130, median cadence ~80 (≈160 steps/min). Almost no climbing until the mountain; then ~1,800 m of ascent to a 1,831 m finish.


The marathon was two very different segments. For 25 km it’s mostly very flat on a road along the shores of a stunning lake.


Then you hit Zombie Hill, and it felt like I was slowly dying. I ran most of it, with some short segments of walking to eat and drink. If you look at horizontal pace, it looks shocking, but amazingly vertically almost nothing changed.


On the flat (first 25 km) I averaged 4:15/km. Zombie Hill (roughly km 25–34) slowed that to 6:43/km while gaining about 800 m. From 34–37 it’s still uphill but rolling, and the pace came back a bit to 5:21/km. Then the last off-road block to the summit (~km 37–42) went to 11:51/km.


Vertical ascent rate (or VAM, meters climbed per hour) averaged about 726 m/h on the road climb (kilometers 25–37) and about 716 m/h on the off-road summit block. A one per cent difference. Even the final grind to 1,831 m, when I was hiking more than running, still put my VAM at roughly 766 m/h. I would never have guessed I was ascending so consistently!

## What I Take Away


This is purposely a data-focused post, and I will write about the psychological elements and fueling strategy later.


Norseman rewards the version of fitness that survives the second half of a very long day, and a mental flexibility to cope with the conditions. It’s a triathlon and an adventure, both mentally and physically. I still have enough of the old engine to get through these things, and my residual exercise is doing enough to help, which is perfect.
