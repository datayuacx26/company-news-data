# What Winning the World Cup Does to Your Health | Terra

## Post-Match Celebrations


Spain moved less than usual during the match, around -100 steps/h compared to a normal Sunday, but erupted the moment it finished. The Spaniards took on the streets, and the average steps spiked around 1am.


***Figure 2:** Average steps throughout a normal Sunday evening (in grey) compared to the world cup final (in red).*


The party wasn't spread evenly. Ranking cities by the share of users who went out afterwards, Madrid led, followed by Málaga, Barcelona and San Sebastian.


***Figure 3:** Spanish cities ranked by percentage of users who celebrated the world cup win after the match.*


## World Cup Final Viewers Across the Globe


We use the high heart rate and step count over a usual Sunday as the signal to find users that were watching the final. Not surprisingly, Spain leads in percentage of viewers (followed by Argentina of course). Whether Argentinians had less viewers, or had a lower heart rate signal because they were less excited (sorry for the loss) we wont tell…


Interestingly, Austria and Italy came in third and fourth. Perhaps neighboring countries tend to support each other more strongly or Spaniards living abroad tend to chose European countries to move to. The USA, Mexico and Canada had a low percentage of viewers, but its also important to consider the vast population in the region and the signal of course being weaker as a result.


***Figure 4:** World map of countries, color coded by percentage of viewers.*


## Spaniards Called in… Sick…


The average bedtime shift was 42 minutes later than usual, but this distribution had a long right tail with the top 25% of users going to bed 2 hours later or more.


Partying all night had a next-day cost and Monday steps fell by ~45%. Spaniards opted for working from home due to ‘transport restrictions’ or simply calling in ‘sick’ \[1\]. But at the end of the day- WE WON!
