# ACH vs Credit Card for Rent Collection: What Property Managers Actually Prefer

As rent collection moves further into digital channels, property managers need payment experiences that are simple for residents and efficient for the business. Checks, money orders, and manual processes create friction that most modern platforms are trying to reduce.


That shift creates an important question for property managers and the software platforms that serve them: which payment methods should be built into the rent collection experience?


For monthly rent, ACH is often the preferred option because it is designed for bank-to-bank payments and works well for large, recurring transactions. Credit cards still have a role, but they are usually better positioned as a flexible alternative rather than the default way to pay.


##


## Why ACH Works Well for Monthly Rent


Rent is a large recurring payment, so cost and consistency matter. Card fees can become expensive quickly because they are often based on a percentage of the transaction. For example, a 3% fee on a $1,800 rent payment equals $54.


[ACH helps address that cost challenge](https://usio.com/why-cities-are-switching-to-ach-to-cut-payment-costs-by-40/) by moving money directly between bank accounts. For property managers, that can mean lower collection costs, less manual follow-up, and a more predictable payment workflow.


It can also make rent easier for residents to manage. Once autopay is set up, payments can run automatically each month, which helps reduce missed due dates and last-minute payment issues.


##


## Why Cards Still Belong in the Payment Mix


[Cards are useful](https://usio.com/usio-card-issuing-solutions/) because they give residents flexibility, especially when they want convenience, rewards, or short-term cash flow support.


That flexibility can matter. A resident who needs another way to pay may be able to avoid a late payment by using a card. Property managers also benefit when residents have enough choice to complete a payment without calling the office or delaying rent.


The challenge is cost. Since card fees are harder to absorb on large monthly payments, many property managers are willing to accept them but do not want them to drive the majority of rent transactions.


A stronger approach is to offer cards as an option while guiding residents toward the lower-cost method for scheduled payments.


##


## What Property Managers Actually Prefer


Property managers want rent payments that are cost-effective, easy to manage, and convenient enough for residents to use consistently.


The ideal setup makes the most efficient method easy to choose while keeping other options available when residents need flexibility. This gives property managers more control over costs without making the resident experience feel limited or outdated.


In practice, the payment experience should not force one method for every situation. It should guide residents toward the best option for recurring rent while still supporting choice when another payment method makes sense.
