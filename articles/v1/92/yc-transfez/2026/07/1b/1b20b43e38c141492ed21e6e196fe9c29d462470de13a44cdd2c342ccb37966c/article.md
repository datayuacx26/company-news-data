# How to Work Abroad from Indonesia: A Complete Guide

[Living Abroad](https://blog.transfez.com/en/living-abroad/)


Let’s be real, the idea of working abroad sounds exciting until you actually sit down and try to figure out *where to start* . Visa requirements, job hunting in a foreign language, sending money back home… it can feel overwhelming fast. But here’s the thing: thousands of Indonesians make this leap every single year, and most of them figured it out step by step, just like you’re about to do. If you’re curious about[why so many Indonesians choose to work overseas](https://blog.transfez.com/en/why-do-many-indonesians-work-abroad/) , you’ll find the reasons are pretty compelling, better salaries, global experience, and a chance to grow in ways that feel impossible at home.


This guide is your starting point. Whether you’re dreaming of the corporate towers of Kuala Lumpur, the bustling tech scene in Singapore, or the structured work culture of Japan, we’ll walk you through everything you need to know about how to work abroad from Indonesia, practically, honestly, and without the fluff.

##


**Step One: Choose the Right Destination Country**


Not all destinations are created equal. The “best” country to work in really depends on your skill set, language ability, budget, and long-term goals. Before you start applying anywhere, do your homework on a few key factors.


###


**1. Match Your Skills to Market Demand**


Different countries are hungry for different types of workers. Malaysia and Singapore have strong demand for engineers, IT professionals, and hospitality workers. Japan actively recruits for manufacturing, caregiving, and food service roles through its *Specified Skilled Worker* program. The Middle East remains a top destination for construction, healthcare, and domestic work. Research the job market before committing to a destination, don’t just go where your friend went.


###


**2. Consider Language Requirements**


English proficiency opens doors in Singapore, Australia, and parts of Europe. If you’re eyeing Japan or South Korea, investing in language study early is non-negotiable.[Working in Japan](https://blog.transfez.com/en/why-work-in-japan/) comes with unique cultural rewards, but getting there requires genuine preparation, including passing Japanese language proficiency tests for many visa pathways.


###


**3. Look at the Cost of Living vs. Earning Potential**


A higher salary doesn’t always mean more savings. Cities like Singapore pay well but cost a lot. Kuala Lumpur offers a strong balance of earnings and affordable living, which is one reason it attracts so many Indonesian workers. Run the actual numbers before you get starry-eyed about a big salary figure.


##


**Step Two: Get Your Documents and Qualifications in Order**


This is where a lot of people get stuck, and it’s also where being prepared early gives you a massive advantage. Working abroad legally always starts with the right paperwork.


###


**1. Understand the Visa and Work Permit Process**


Every country has its own rules. Some require your employer to sponsor your work visa before you even arrive. Others have skilled worker visa programs you can apply for independently. Research the specific *work permit* or *visa* category that matches your job type, don’t assume a tourist visa can be converted later (in most cases, it legally can’t).


###


**2. Get Your Indonesian Documents Verified**


You’ll typically need:


- A valid Indonesian passport (at least 12–18 months validity remaining)
- Your *ijazah* (diploma/degree certificate), ideally with an apostille or notarized translation
- A clean criminal record certificate ( *SKCK* ) from the Indonesian National Police
- Your work experience letters, translated into English or the destination country’s language


Start gathering these early. Government-issued documents can take weeks to process in Indonesia.


###


**3. Register with BP2MI if You’re Going Through Official Channels**


*BP2MI* (Badan Pelindungan Pekerja Migran Indonesia) is the Indonesian government body that protects migrant workers. If you’re going through a licensed recruitment agency ( *Perusahaan Penempatan Pekerja Migran Indonesia / P3MI* ), make sure they’re officially registered. This protects your rights if things go wrong abroad.


##


**Step Three: Find and Land the Job**


Okay, the exciting part. Landing a job overseas is a mix of smart positioning, persistence, and knowing where to look.


###


**1. Build an International-Standard CV**


Your Indonesian resume format may not cut it abroad. Research the CV or *resume* conventions for your target country. In most Western and Southeast Asian markets, a clean one-to-two-page CV with quantifiable achievements performs best. Drop the photo and personal details (age, religion, marital status) that are common in Indonesian CVs, many countries find these irrelevant or even inappropriate.


###


**2. Use the Right Job Platforms**


Go where employers in your target country actually post jobs:


- **LinkedIn** , essential for professional and corporate roles globally
- **JobStreet** , strong for Malaysia and Singapore
- **Indeed** , widely used in Australia, the UK, and the US
- **Hello Work** , Japan’s official public employment service
- Company career pages directly, often overlooked but highly effective


###


**3. Network Before You Need To**


The Indonesian diaspora in most major work destinations is active and supportive. Join Facebook groups, LinkedIn communities, and WhatsApp networks for Indonesians in your target country. Many job opportunities, especially in Malaysia and Singapore, travel through these informal networks before they’re ever publicly posted.


##


**Step Four: Prepare Financially for the Transition**


Moving abroad costs money upfront, even if you have a job lined up. Many first-timers underestimate this phase, and it creates real stress in the first few months.


###


**1. Build a Pre-Departure Emergency Fund**


Aim to have at least three months of living expenses saved before you leave. This covers you during the gap between starting work and receiving your first full paycheck, plus any unexpected costs during the settling-in period.


###


**2. Plan How You’ll Manage Two Currencies**


Once you’re earning abroad, you’ll be juggling income in a foreign currency while your family and obligations back home are in Rupiah. This is something worth thinking through carefully, understanding[how to manage your finances across two currencies](https://blog.transfez.com/en/managing-finances-ringgit-rupiah/) can save you a lot of headaches and lost money over time.


###


**3. Think About Saving and Investing Early**


Working abroad is a financial opportunity, but only if you’re intentional about it. Many Indonesian workers abroad focus purely on remittances home, without building any personal savings or investments. Exploring[saving and investment strategies while working overseas](https://blog.transfez.com/en/saving-investment-strategies-malaysia/) early in your stint abroad can set you up for long-term financial health, not just short-term income.


##


**Step Five: Set Up Smart Remittances Back Home**


Most Indonesians working abroad send a portion of their earnings home regularly, whether to support family, pay for a child’s education, or build savings in Indonesia. Doing this efficiently matters.


###


**1. Use a Reliable International Transfer Service**


Traditional bank transfers can be slow and costly. Many Indonesian workers abroad use digital remittance services for faster, more convenient transfers.[Sending money to family in Indonesia from abroad](https://blog.transfez.com/en/send-money-to-family-in-indonesia/) is much smoother when you have a trusted, app-based service that lets you do it from your phone without visiting a bank branch.


###


**2. Set a Regular Transfer Schedule**


Rather than sending money home impulsively, build a routine. A fixed monthly transfer amount keeps your family’s budget predictable and helps you resist the temptation to overspend before sending.

Working abroad from Indonesia isn’t just a dream, it’s a very achievable plan when you approach it with the right information and a bit of patience. Start with one step at a time: pick your destination, get your documents sorted, build your international CV, and make a financial plan before you board that plane. The Indonesians who thrive overseas aren’t necessarily the most talented, they’re the ones who prepared the smartest. You’ve got this.
