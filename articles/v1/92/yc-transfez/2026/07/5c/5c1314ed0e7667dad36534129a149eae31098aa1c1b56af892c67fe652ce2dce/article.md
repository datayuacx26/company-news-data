# How to Find a Job Abroad: A Practical Guide

[Living Abroad](https://blog.transfez.com/en/living-abroad/)


You’ve been dreaming about it for a while now, working in a different country, building a career abroad, and actually living that expat life. But every time you sit down to figure out **how to find a job abroad** , it feels overwhelming. Where do you even start? Which platforms do you use? How do you stand out as a foreign applicant?


Trust me, you’re not alone. Thousands of Indonesians make this leap every year, and the ones who succeed aren’t necessarily the most qualified, they’re the ones who follow a smart, consistent strategy. If you’ve ever wondered[why so many Indonesians choose to work abroad](https://blog.transfez.com/en/why-do-many-indonesians-work-abroad/) , the short answer is: the opportunities are real, and they’re very much within reach.


This guide breaks down everything you need to know, step by step, so you can stop overthinking and start applying.

##


**Why Finding a Job Abroad Is Different from a Local Job Search**


Job hunting internationally isn’t just a bigger version of your usual job search. There are different rules, different expectations, and different barriers to navigate. Understanding these differences upfront will save you a lot of frustration.


###


**1. You’re Competing with Local Candidates Too**


Most employers overseas will first consider candidates who are already in the country. As a foreign applicant, you need to give them a compelling reason to sponsor a visa or hire remotely. Your **unique skills and international perspective** are your leverage, use them.


###


**2. Work Permits and Visas Matter Early On**


Before you apply anywhere, spend a little time understanding the work visa requirements for your target country. Some countries make it relatively easy for skilled workers; others have strict quotas. Knowing this upfront stops you from wasting time applying to jobs in markets that won’t realistically hire someone from abroad.


###


**3. Cultural Fit Counts More Than You Think**


International employers often screen for cultural adaptability, your ability to communicate clearly, work across cultures, and fit into their environment. Highlight any cross-cultural experience in your CV and interviews.


##


**Step-by-Step: How to Find a Job Abroad**


Here’s the practical roadmap. Follow these steps in order and you’ll have a clear plan of action instead of a pile of open browser tabs.


###


**1. Get Clear on Your Target Country and Industry**


Don’t just say “I want to work overseas.” Get specific. Which country? Which industry? Which role? The more specific you are, the easier it becomes to tailor your job search. Research job markets in countries like Singapore, Japan, Australia, the Netherlands, or the UAE, all of which actively hire international talent.


If you’re eyeing Southeast Asia’s financial hub, check out this[full guide on how to work in Singapore as a foreigner](https://blog.transfez.com/en/how-to-work-in-singapore-as-a-foreigner/) to understand exactly what’s involved.


###


**2. Tailor Your CV and LinkedIn for International Standards**


Your Indonesian-style CV may need a serious refresh. International employers typically expect:


- A concise, **one-to-two page CV** (no photo in many Western countries)
- Quantified achievements (numbers, percentages, impact)
- A clean, professional LinkedIn profile with a clear headline and summary
- Keywords aligned to the job description


Research the CV norms specific to your target country, they vary more than you’d expect.


###


**3. Use the Right Job Platforms**


Not all job boards are equal when it comes to international roles. Here are the most useful platforms to start with:


- **LinkedIn** , The gold standard for international professional networking and job hunting
- **Indeed** , Wide global coverage across industries
- **Glassdoor** , Great for research and job listings
- **Welcome to the Jungle** , Strong in Europe, especially for tech and startups
- **JobsDB** and **MyCareersFuture** , Highly relevant for Southeast Asia
- **JREC-IN Portal** , Specifically for Japan-based research and academic roles


Set up job alerts with your target keywords so opportunities come to you, not the other way around.


###


**4. Network Like It’s Your Part-Time Job**


Here’s an honest truth: a huge percentage of international jobs are filled through connections, not job boards. Start building your network *before* you need it. Connect with professionals in your target country on LinkedIn. Join industry groups, attend virtual events, and engage genuinely with content in your field.


Don’t be afraid to send a short, polite message to someone working at a company you admire. Ask for a 15-minute virtual coffee chat to learn about their experience. Most people are happy to help, they were once in your shoes too.


###


**5. Work with International Recruitment Agencies**


Specialized recruitment agencies can be game-changers, especially in fields like engineering, healthcare, IT, and finance. They already have relationships with hiring companies, understand visa processes, and can match you to roles that aren’t publicly advertised. Look for agencies that specialize in *expat placements* or international talent sourcing in your target market.


###


**6. Consider Remote Work as a Gateway**


One underrated strategy: land a remote role with a foreign company first. This lets you build credibility with an international employer, learn the work culture, and sometimes transition to an in-person role later. Platforms like **Remote.co** , **We Work Remotely** , and **Toptal** are excellent starting points for remote international work.


###


**7. Nail Your International Job Interviews**


Overseas interviews, especially for Western companies, tend to be *behavioral and competency-based* . Practice answering using the **STAR method** (Situation, Task, Action, Result). Research the company culture deeply, and always prepare thoughtful questions to ask at the end. Confidence and clarity matter as much as your credentials.


##


**Things to Sort Out Before You Move**


Once you’ve landed that job offer, the real prep work begins. Getting these logistics right early will make your transition much smoother.


###


**1. Understand Your Employment Contract**


Read every line. Know your salary, benefits, probation period, and what happens if things don’t work out. If anything is unclear, ask, or get a local legal expert to review it before you sign.


###


**2. Sort Out Your Banking and Money Transfers**


One practical thing many people overlook: how will you send money home to your family in Indonesia? Using a reliable international remittance service like **Transfez** makes it easy to transfer money back home from wherever you’re working. It’s worth setting this up early so your family has steady support from day one.


###


**3. Research Life in Your New City**


Cost of living, housing, transport, healthcare, all of these should be on your checklist. You don’t want to accept a salary that sounds great but leaves you struggling once you account for everyday expenses in an expensive city.
