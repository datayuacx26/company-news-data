**Why Singapore Is a Top Destination for Foreign Workers**


Before diving into the how-to, it helps to understand why Singapore keeps topping the list for expat workers. The city-state consistently ranks among the world’s best places to live and work, and that reputation is well-earned.


###


**1. A Thriving, Diverse Economy**


Singapore is a regional hub for finance, technology, logistics, and manufacturing. Major multinational companies have their Asia-Pacific headquarters here, which means there’s a constant demand for skilled talent, both local and international.


###


**2. High Salaries and Strong Purchasing Power**


Salaries in Singapore are competitive, and while the cost of living is high, many foreign workers find that their savings potential is excellent, especially compared to staying home. Many Indonesians working here regularly send money back to support family, using services like[smart remittance options](https://blog.transfez.com/en/send-money-to-family-in-indonesia/) to make it seamless.


###


**3. English as the Working Language**


Unlike some other Asian work destinations, Singapore operates almost entirely in English in professional settings. That removes a huge barrier for many internationally-educated workers.


###


**4. Gateway to Southeast Asia**


Being based in Singapore often means exposure to regional projects and travel. For career growth and networking, it’s hard to beat.


##


**Understanding Work Visas for Singapore**


This is the part most people stress about, and understandably so. Singapore has a well-structured work visa system, but it’s important to understand which pass applies to your situation before you start applying for jobs.


###


**1. Employment Pass (EP)**


The *Employment Pass* is designed for professionals, managers, and executives. It’s typically for those with a university degree and a job offer from a Singapore-registered company. This is the most common route for skilled foreign workers entering mid-to-senior level roles.


###


**2. S Pass**


The *S Pass* targets mid-skilled workers, think technicians, junior managers, or experienced tradespeople. It has a salary threshold requirement and employers also face a quota on how many S Pass holders they can employ.


###


**3. Work Permit**


Work Permits are for semi-skilled foreign workers in sectors like construction, manufacturing, marine, and domestic work. These are employer-sponsored and sector-specific.


###


**4. EntrePass**


If you’re an entrepreneur looking to start a business in Singapore, the *EntrePass* might be your path in. It’s designed for innovative startups with strong commercial potential.


Always check the official Ministry of Manpower (MOM) website for the latest eligibility criteria, since requirements do get updated periodically.


##


**How to Find a Job in Singapore as a Foreigner**


Here’s where things get practical. Finding a job in Singapore from abroad takes strategy, persistence, and a little insider knowledge. Let’s break it down.


###


**1. Polish Your Resume for Singapore Standards**


Singapore employers generally prefer clean, professional resumes, usually no longer than two pages. Focus on measurable achievements rather than just listing responsibilities. Skip the photo unless specifically asked.


###


**2. Use the Right Job Platforms**


The go-to platforms include LinkedIn, JobStreet, MyCareersFuture (Singapore’s government job portal), and Glassdoor. LinkedIn is especially powerful in Singapore, many hiring managers actively scout profiles there.


###


**3. Tap Into Your Network**


Don’t underestimate the power of warm introductions. Reach out to alumni networks, industry groups, or even Indonesian communities in Singapore. A personal referral can get your resume to the top of the pile faster than any application portal.


###


**4. Work With Reputable Recruitment Agencies**


Singapore has a robust recruitment industry. Agencies like Michael Page, Robert Half, Hays, and local specialists can connect you with employers actively looking for foreign talent. Make sure any agency you work with is registered with MOM.


###


**5. Apply Directly to Companies**


Research companies in your industry that have a Singapore presence and apply directly through their careers pages. Showing specific knowledge of the company always helps you stand out.


##


**What to Expect When You Arrive**


Landing the job is a huge win, but the work doesn’t stop there. Settling into Singapore as a working foreigner involves a few practical moves you’ll want to handle quickly.


###


**1. Register Your Pass and Get Your IC**


Once you arrive, your employer will help you register your work pass. You’ll also need to get your *FIN* (Foreign Identification Number), which is essential for opening bank accounts, renting apartments, and pretty much everything else.


###


**2. Open a Local Bank Account**


DBS, OCBC, and UOB are the big three. Having a local account makes receiving your salary and managing expenses much easier.


###


**3. Sort Out Housing Early**


Housing is Singapore’s biggest cost. Most foreigners rent HDB flats or private condominiums. Use PropertyGuru or 99.co to explore options. Start looking before you arrive if possible, the market moves fast.


###


**4. Understand the CPF (or Lack Thereof)**


Singaporean workers contribute to the *Central Provident Fund* (CPF), but Employment Pass holders are generally not eligible. This means more take-home pay, but also means you need to be more intentional about saving and investing on your own. **Tips to Thrive in Singapore’s Work Culture**


Getting hired is step one. Thriving in the workplace is a whole other chapter.


###


**1. Punctuality Is Non-Negotiable**


Singapore’s work culture values efficiency and respect for time. Being late, even by five minutes, sends a message you don’t want to send.


###


**2. Be Direct but Respectful**


Singaporean professionals communicate fairly directly compared to some Southeast Asian cultures. It’s okay to voice opinions clearly, as long as you’re professional and respectful.


###


**3. Lifelong Learning Is Expected**


Singapore has a strong culture of upskilling. Don’t be surprised if your employer encourages, or even sponsors, training and certifications. Embrace it.


###


**4. Build Relationships Outside Your Bubble**


Singapore is incredibly multicultural. Make an effort to connect with colleagues from different backgrounds. It enriches your experience and your professional network.
