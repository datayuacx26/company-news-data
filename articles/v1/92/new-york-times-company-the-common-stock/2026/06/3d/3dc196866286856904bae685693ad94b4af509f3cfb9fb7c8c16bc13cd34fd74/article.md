# Introducing the 2026-27 Local Investigations Fellows

The Local Investigations Fellowship welcomes six fellows from six states, who will report from the communities where they live.


We are proud to announce the next class of the[Local Investigations Fellowship](https://www.nytco.com/careers/local-investigations-fellowship/) . The program, led by Dean Baquet, a former executive editor of The New York Times, gives journalists the opportunity to produce[signature investigative work](https://www.nytimes.com/spotlight/local-investigations-fellowship) focused on the state or region they’re reporting from. Their reporting will be published by The Times and made available free for co-publication by local newsrooms.


“This is the program’s fourth fellowship class, and we’re thrilled to work with them on producing important accountability reporting from their communities,” said Dean. “Their work will add to the fellowship’s strong roster of investigative journalism from regions across the country, and we’re excited to help foster this next generation of investigative reporters.”


The new fellows are from Illinois, Louisiana, Mississippi, North Carolina, South Carolina and Texas. They are:


## **Asher Lehrer-Small**


Asher Lehrer-Small is a freelance reporter based in Houston. He previously covered education for The Houston Landing and The 74, a nonprofit newsroom focused on America’s public schools. He is the recipient of an Education Writers Association’s National Award for education reporting and a Texas Managing Editors Award for community service reporting. He was also a 2025 Livingston Awards finalist. Asher will


[continue to examine police and security guards in Texas schools](https://www.nytimes.com/interactive/2026/05/27/us/texas-schools-police-force-students-uvalde.html) .


## **Bobbi-Jeanne Misick**


Bobbi-Jeanne Misick is an investigative reporter covering immigration and immigrant communities in Louisiana for Verite News. Since 2020, Bobbi-Jeanne has covered changes in immigration enforcement tactics and in immigration courts, detention and deportation in Louisiana and Mississippi, home to one of the largest clusters of federal immigration detention facilities in the country. She has reported for ProPublica’s Local Reporting Network, NPR and Type Investigations. Bobbi-Jeanne’s work has been recognized by the Edward R. Murrow Awards and nationally as an Investigative Reporters and Editors Awards finalist. Bobbi-Jeanne will continue her reporting on immigration in Louisiana.


## **Conor Hughes**


Conor Hughes is the Greenville County reporter for The Post and Courier based in Greenville, S.C., where he has been covering the Upstate region for a decade on a range of beats. His reporting has led to the arrest of a long-ignored serial sexual predator, the release of an innocent man from prison and increased accountability for a housing nonprofit forcing tenants to live in slum conditions. Conor will report on the short-term lending industry in South Carolina.


## **Farrah Anderson**


Farrah Anderson is an investigative reporter focused on criminal justice and public accountability. Her reporting with the Invisible Institute and Illinois Public Media won the 2025 Richard H. Driehaus Award for Investigative Journalism. Anderson has reported for public media stations across the Midwest and studied journalism at the University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign. She will write about Chicago with the Investigative Project on Race and Equity. Her fellowship is funded by the Field Foundation, a private and independent foundation that supports community power building in Chicago.


## **Malena Carollo**


Malena Carollo is an investigative reporter based in Raleigh, N.C. She previously reported for The Markup and The Tampa Bay Times. Her investigation for The Markup and The Washington Post found that a sweeping change to an algorithm deciding who gets a lifesaving liver transplant hurt patients in several poorer, more rural states in the South and Midwest. Malena holds a master’s degree from Columbia University and will examine for-profit medical services in North Carolina.


## **Sarah Whites-Koditschek**


Sarah Whites-Koditschek is a former investigative reporter for AL.com in Birmingham, Ala. She has won national awards from the Society for Professional Journalists and the Public Media Journalists Association as well as a Regional Edward R. Murrow award. She previously worked in public radio as a reporter and producer at WHYY in Philadelphia and KUAR in Little Rock. She is a graduate of the Columbia School of Journalism’s Stabile Center for Investigative Journalism, and she completed a fellowship in investigative reporting at Wisconsin Public Radio and the Wisconsin Center for Investigative Journalism. Sarah will report from Jackson, Miss. for Mississippi Today.


This group joins Mukta Joshi and Rosemary Westwood, who were introduced as the first local investigations fellows of the[Deep South Today Investigative Reporting Center](https://www.nytco.com/press/introducing-the-first-fellows-for-new-regional-investigative-reporting-center/) created in collaboration with The New York Times.


Investigative reporting that was produced through the fellowship has received national recognition. In 2025, The Times, in collaboration with The Baltimore Banner and with support from Big Local News, won the[2025 Pulitzer Prize for Local Reporting](https://www.pulitzer.org/winners/22642) for an investigation into the deadly opioid crisis. The reporters won the[2024 George Polk Award for Local Reporting](https://www.liu.edu/polk-awards/current-winners) and the[2025 Frank A. Blethen Award for Local Accountability Reporting](https://www.poynter.org/prizes/) and were finalists for the[2025 Livingston Award](https://wallacehouse.umich.edu/announcing-the-2025-livingston-awards-finalists/?utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=Announcing%20the%202025%20Livingston%20Award%20Finalists&utm_content=Announcing%20the%202025%20Livingston%20Award%20Finalists+Preview+CID_d72b6476472404fc3a5572a74391a06e&utm_source=Campaign%20Monitor&utm_term=2025%20Livingston%20Awards%20finalists&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=Announcing%20the%202025%20Livingston%20Award%20Finalists&utm_content=Announcing%20the%202025%20Livingston%20Award%20Finalists+CID_d72b6476472404fc3a5572a74391a06e&utm_source=Campaign%20Monitor&utm_term=2025%20Livingston%20Awards%20finalists) . A yearslong examination of abuses by sheriffs departments in Mississippi was a finalist for[the 2024 Pulitzer Prize for Local Reporting](https://www.pulitzer.org/prize-winners-by-year/2024) and the[Goldsmith Prize for Investigative Reporting](https://shorensteincenter.org/announcing-the-2024-goldsmith-prize-for-investigative-reporting-finalists/) in 2024 and[2026](https://goldsmithawards.org/honoree/abuse-of-power-beyond-the-goon-squad/) . The team was also awarded the[Peter F. Collier Award for Ethics in Journalism](https://ethicsandjournalism.org/2026/03/04/nyu-ethics-and-journalism-initiative-announces-awardees-marty-baron-as-keynote-speaker-for-the-2025-2026-peter-f-collier-awards-for-ethics-in-journalism/) , the[First Amendment Coalition’s Free Speech and Government Award](https://firstamendmentcoalition.org/news/post/the-alabama-solution-the-oaklandside-and-mississippi-today-new-york-times-reveal-honored-for-outstanding-contributions-to-free-speech-and-open-government/) and the[Investigative Reporters and Editors Tom Renner Medal for Outstanding Crime Reporting](https://www.ire.org/2026/05/05/announcing-the-2025-ire-awards/)


To support the data work of our fellows, The Times will continue collaborating with[Big Local News](https://biglocalnews.org/#/login) , a data-sharing journalism program based at Stanford University. Big Local News will be working again with our fellows on obtaining and analyzing data for their projects and providing ongoing training on investigative data techniques.


In addition to producing signature investigative work, fellows will receive frequent training opportunities to learn investigative reporting techniques, travel to our New York offices and attend conferences for additional training and mentorship. Times editors will also visit fellows in their reporting regions.


Applications for the 2027-28 group of fellows open on July 1. Journalists interested in a fellowship based in Mississippi or Louisiana can visit this application[form](https://job-boards.greenhouse.io/thenewyorktimes/jobs/4705165005) year-round.
