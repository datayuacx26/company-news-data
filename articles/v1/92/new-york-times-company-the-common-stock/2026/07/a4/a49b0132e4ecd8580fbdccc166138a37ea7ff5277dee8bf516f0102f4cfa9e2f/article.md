President Trump said his “Board of Peace” had[reached a disarmament deal in Gaza](https://www.nytimes.com/live/2026/07/30/us/trump-news) . “For the first time, Hamas officially has committed to an actionable plan for relinquishing all its weapons, which will be followed by Israeli withdrawal from Gaza,” the board said. We have more news below. I’m going to start with the United States Coast Guard.


Image


Dave Philipps, who covers the United States military and veterans for The Times, has spent the past few years focused on the brain injuries suffered by members of elite and specialized units. He’s written about[artillery crews](https://www.nytimes.com/2023/11/05/us/us-army-marines-artillery-isis-pentagon.html) who repeatedly fire high-powered cannons and about[SEALs](https://www.nytimes.com/2024/06/30/us/navy-seals-brain-damage-suicide.html) who do the same with shoulder-fired rockets. He’s written about what happens to the brains of[fighter pilots](https://www.nytimes.com/2024/12/08/us/navy-pilot-brain-injury-topgun.html) who catapult from aircraft carriers, and to those of[fast-boat crews](https://www.nytimes.com/2024/11/12/us/brain-trauma-cte-navy-speedboat.html) who pound across the ocean.


And now he’s turned his attention to[the crews of Coast Guard rescue boats](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/07/30/us/coast-guard-rescue-boat-surfmen-brain-injury.html) , who work and train in heavy, crashing seas in order to save lives. They’re called surfmen, and their work is among the most challenging and dangerous jobs in the service.


It is work that comes at a cost, Dave reports. They take a beating from the ocean. Many veteran surfmen experience headaches, memory lapses, anxiety attacks, nightmares, mood swings and cognitive problems that can be symptoms of brain injuries.


“A 20-foot wave breaking on you is like being in a car crash,” a surfman named Aaron Harris told him. “If you are out training in big surf, that’s like 20 car accidents a day.” Harris was on the boats for 22 years, before retiring in 2024.


The surfmen are alone, Dave discovered. The Coast Guard has no regular testing protocol for brain injuries. It delivers no formal briefings on the dangers of pounding waves. The website it maintains on brain injuries is more than 10 years old. Dave found that many of the links that appear under a heading reading “Where to get help” are inactive.


The surfmen suffer, and their families suffer, and the results can be fatal.
