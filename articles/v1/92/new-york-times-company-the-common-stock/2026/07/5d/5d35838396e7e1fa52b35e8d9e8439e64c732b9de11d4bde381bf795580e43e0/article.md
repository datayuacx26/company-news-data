# A New Brazil Bureau Chief and Correspondent

Adam Ellick and Ana Ionova, two top-notch journalists, will help International explore broader use of video. Read more in this note from editors on the International desk.


International correspondents appeared in more than 120 videos published by The Times last year, putting our reporting from around the world in front of vast new audiences. But there’s more work to do in this medium, and we’re excited to announce an important part of our strategy to embrace it: a hybrid text-and-video bureau in Brazil, anchored by two top-notch journalists who will help us explore how video can be a basic part of how we cover the world.


A 20-year Times journalist and filmmaker,


Adam Ellick will be our new Brazil bureau chief in São Paulo. He brings deep experience in both foreign correspondence and video journalism to this new assignment. At various times in his long career, he co-led The Times’s Video department, helped conceive the Visual Investigations team and co-produced a


[video on how an Afghan woman was burned to death](http://www.nytimes.com/video/world/asia/100000004108808/the-killing-of-farkhunda.html) that was featured in an entry recognized with a Pulitzer Prize in 2016. Adam is a former senior video correspondent with reporting experience from Iran to North Korea. In 2009, he met a then 10-year-old schoolgirl named Malala Yousafzai and brought her story to the world in a documentary called


[“Malala’s Story.”](http://www.nytimes.com/video/world/asia/100000001835296/class-dismissed-malala-yousafzais-story.html) Adam was also a co-author of the 2014 Innovation Report, a cornerstone of our digital transformation.


Adam most recently started the Opinion Video team, which has won 11 Emmys and the company’s first Academy Award. The team produced satire, collaborated with external video influencers, commissioned original reporting inside Covid I.C.U.s and Gaza, and exposed abuse at Nike that led to major changes in women’s sports.


Adam will join


Ana Ionova , our new Brazil correspondent based in Rio de Janeiro. She began working with The Times as a freelance journalist in 2021. Since then, Ana has delivered an impressive body of exclusive stories and distinctive features, including those that explore how Brazil’s history shapes its present, like memorable pieces on


[the controversy behind a beloved Barbie-like idol](https://www.nytimes.com/2023/08/15/world/americas/brazil-barbie-xuxa.html) and


[the disappearance of rooms for maids from Brazilian homes](https://www.nytimes.com/2024/08/15/world/americas/brazil-maid-rooms-racist-past.html) . In the past year, as interim bureau chief, she covered in


[text](https://www.nytimes.com/2025/09/02/world/americas/jair-bolsonaro-brazil-coup-trial.html) and


[video](https://www.nytimes.com/video/world/americas/100000010388194/what-we-know-about-bolsonaros-conviction.html) the landmark trial of former President Jair Bolsonaro for attempting to stage a coup; and shed light on how Latin American drug trafficking is


[changing to evade](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/04/17/world/americas/trump-drug-trafficking-south-america-brazil.html) President Trump’s military strikes.


Ana, who is fluent in Portuguese and Bulgarian,


began her career in Canada and the Balkans, before joining Reuters in London.


She moved to Latin America in 2019, reporting for The Los Angeles Times, the BBC World Service, The Guardian and others. She was also part of a team that produced the


[award-winning](https://www.thepressawards.com/Winners2026) investigative podcast series


[“Missing in the Amazon.](https://open.spotify.com/episode/16KOeNUl4IpTo8njzG9Zsp) ”


We expect both Adam and Ana to work in both text and video. Please join us in congratulating them on their new roles.


— Phil Pan, Greg Winter, Josh Keller and Diego Ribadeneira
