# New Role for Jason Bailey

Jason has been promoted to deputy news editor on the Culture Desk. Read more in the below note from Sia Michel.


We are thrilled to announce that Jason Bailey has been promoted to deputy news editor on Culture, joining the leadership team as he oversees the desk’s breaking news and investigations.


Jason has been a driving force behind some of our strongest news coverage, including the Trump administration’s takeover of the Kennedy Center, the trial of Sean Combs, the killing of Rob Reiner and Michele Singer Reiner and Matthew Perry’s death. He has ably led our news operations, along with Kevin Flynn and Mike Peed, finding the most urgent and high-value scoops and storylines that meet readers’ demands. Jason’s promotion comes nearly four years after he joined Culture as a news editor working with Michael Cooper, Culture’s previous news deputy, who left the desk six months ago to assume the role of Politics editor.


“Jason is an unflappable, insatiably curious editor,” Michael said. “He can go toe-to-toe with TMZ on a celebrity death one day, and follow up the next with sophisticated investigations of film tax credits or a series exploring how a copyright suit against Andy Warhol that made it to the Supreme Court could transform the art world. Reporters clamor to work with him, and his fellow editors feel relief when they have him in their foxhole when big news breaks.”


Some recent Great Reads that Jason shepherded include a look at how some classical musicians train as soldiers to


[perform in the military](https://www.nytimes.com/2024/05/27/arts/music/military-bands-west-point-army.html) , a piece on a struggling Hollywood writer who tried her hand at


[“The Price Is Right”](https://www.nytimes.com/2024/11/05/arts/television/price-is-right-emily-winter.html) and the story of a little-known


[Christopher Nolan film](https://www.nytimes.com/2024/03/07/movies/christopher-nolan-larceny.html) that the director doesn’t want you to watch.


“Jason is a reporter’s dream editor,” said the reporter Matt Stevens, who has worked closely with Jason as we grow our California news coverage. “He’s incredibly thoughtful, unflappable under pressure, and he elevates and sharpens every story he touches. He’s with us in the trenches no matter what time the news breaks. And he gets our best work out of us because he sees the best in us.”


Jason also directs our video game coverage, looking at how


[The Legend of Zelda](https://www.nytimes.com/interactive/2023/05/04/arts/zelda-nintendo-history.html) changed the world of gaming, the ways publishers use V.I.P. programs to


[keep players spending](https://www.nytimes.com/interactive/2023/05/04/arts/zelda-nintendo-history.html) and how a group of


[socialists created a hit game](https://www.nytimes.com/interactive/2025/09/17/arts/disco-elysium-zaum-estonia.html) that led to rivalry and bitter lawsuits.


“As an editor, Jason is not only incisive, fair-minded and creative — he is also fun,” said the reporter Julia Jacobs, who has covered high-profile news and enterprise with Jason. “He helps make it a joy to report and chase scoops, while encouraging his reporters to lean into the offbeat ideas that get us excited. When I got interested in examining


[the use of a four-letter profanity in the movie industry](https://www.nytimes.com/interactive/2025/12/19/arts/swearing-pg13-r-rating.html) , for example, he was delighted by the challenge of figuring out how to shape that rather obscene subject for the pages of The New York Times.”


Before joining Culture, Jason spent three years as the night editor on National. He joined The Times in 2016 as a copy editor in Sports before working as an editor on the Express and Flex desks. He is an independent thinker and a natural skeptic, as anyone who has heard his hot takes at the pop culture meeting will understand.


Please join me in congratulating Jason on his new appointment.


— Sia
