# Who Wins (and Who Loses) in the Democrats’ New 2028 Primary Calendar?

Democrats across the country agree that their[new primary map](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/07/24/us/politics/democrats-south-carolina-nevada-2028-presidential-race.html) will redraw the road to their party’s 2028 presidential nomination. But, as they look to what’s widely expected to be a crowded and competitive contest, no one is quite sure what turns it will take.


Instead of Iowa and New Hampshire, long the traditional openers of the primary process, Democratic candidates will navigate their way through a six-state gantlet that starts in South Carolina on Jan. 22, winds through Nevada, New Hampshire, New Mexico and Michigan and ends in Virginia on Feb. 29.


The cross-country sprint has significantly broadened the race — geographically, racially and ideologically, Democratic strategists say.


“It really could benefit anyone,” said Rob Flaherty, a deputy campaign manager for former Vice President Kamala Harris’s 2024 run and the co-host of “Nobody Knows Anything,” a podcast about national politics. **** “I follow things pretty closely, and I have no idea how it’s going to bounce.”


Here’s what the calendar does — and doesn’t — tell us about the 2028 Democratic primary race.


### **Black voters will gain new power.**


The new map is good news for Black voters, increasing their power in the earliest stage of the contest. While the electorate in Iowa and New Hampshire is largely white, a majority of South Carolina Democratic primary voters are Black.


“Black voters are the backbone of this party,” said Christale Spain, the chair of the state’s Democratic Party,[in an interview](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=R-Fb8aL5jHk&t=5112s) with the political commentator Roland Martin last week. “We should continue to elevate Black voters role in the process.”
