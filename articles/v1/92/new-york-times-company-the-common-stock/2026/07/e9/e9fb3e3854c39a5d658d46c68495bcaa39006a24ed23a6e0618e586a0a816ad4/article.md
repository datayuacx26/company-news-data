# Jason Stallman Is Our Next Obituaries Editor

Jason, who has spent two decades at The Times, will lead the Obits team. Read more in this note from Joe Kahn, Marc Lacey and Carolyn Ryan.


We’ve produced obituaries since our very first edition, on Sept. 18, 1851, and we’ve become a standard bearer of the form.


We’ve documented significant lives “from Daniel Webster to David Bowie,” as Bill McDonald, our recently retired Obits editor, put it in “The New York Times Book of the Dead,” which celebrated more than 10,000 obituaries through the ages.


Our next Obituaries editor has a big legacy to maintain but also a mandate to reinvent. Who better to pull this off than a Times veteran who is both steeped in our values and unafraid to try new things? Congratulations to Jason Stallman, who will take the helm of Obits later this summer.


Jason, who has spent more than two decades at The Times, needs no introduction. But we’ll offer one nonetheless. Memorable, creative journalistic projects have happened on his watch. Remember “


[Snow Fall](https://www.nytimes.com/projects/2012/snow-fall/index.html) ,” which was produced during Jason’s successful stint on Sports? And who can forget the powerful and critically acclaimed “


[Framing Britney Spears](https://www.nytimes.com/article/framing-britney-spears.html) ” documentary he oversaw?


Jason knows how to inspire reporters to do their best work, as he did as Sports editor and executive producer of The Times’s documentary unit. Most recently, after a stint at The Athletic, he’s continued to elevate our reporting as a senior story editor on National, where he edited the recent piece delving into the


[secret racial history of a former correspondent’s family](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/05/15/us/degrange-family-history-race.html) . Jason also knows deadlines, having spent time as a copy editor in Sports and a news editor on National, and we all know Obits always has deadlines looming.


The Obits desk that Jason inherits is a much-admired operation, having inspired a number of books and a documentary (


“One comes away from ‘Obit’ grateful that the paper has at its disposal a team of humane, gifted people who make commemorating the dead a lively, lasting art,”


[our reviewer said of the doc](https://www.nytimes.com/2017/04/25/movies/review-obit-documentary-new-york-times.html) ).


A consistent audience favorite, our obituaries are authoritative, lyrical and representative of the range of our global newsroom. Obituary writers produce some of the finest writing anywhere, including in the comments affixed to their pieces. And our view of who merits a Times obituary has expanded over the decades, a point reinforced by


[Overlooked](https://www.nytimes.com/spotlight/overlooked) , a special project led by Amy Padnani.


Special thanks to Adam Bernstein, the Obits deputy, for filling in as the interim editor. And please join us in congratulating Jason on his new role, which he’ll begin on Aug. 10.


— Joe, Marc and Carolyn
