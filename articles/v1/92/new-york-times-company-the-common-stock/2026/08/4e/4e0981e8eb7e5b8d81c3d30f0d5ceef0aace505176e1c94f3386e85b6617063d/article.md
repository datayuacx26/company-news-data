# Darline Graham Advances to the Republican Senate Runoff in South Carolina

Senator Darline Graham, the sister of the late Senator Lindsey Graham, advanced on Tuesday to the special Republican primary runoff for Senate in South Carolina, when she will face Representative Ralph Norman, a longtime hard-line conservative lawmaker, according to The Associated Press.


The matchup on Aug. 25 will offer a sharp contrast in experience, though few policy differences. Ms. Graham, who stepped in to complete her brother’s Senate term after he died last month, has offered little in the way of specifics about her positions, other than to back President Trump and the Republican Party line. Mr. Norman is a five-term congressman and vice-chairman of the ultraconservative House Freedom Caucus.


Ms. Graham did not win the special primary outright, despite having Mr. Trump’s endorsement — an outcome, as she said on Tuesday morning, that she expected in the crowded race. Mr. Norman and other more experienced Republicans ran knowing that Ms. Graham, 62, a longtime advocate for people with disabilities, had been largely unknown and untested as a political novice. As of Tuesday, she had been in office for 28 days.


“This is a very emotional night,” Ms. Graham told reporters gathered in Columbia, S.C., on Tuesday night, “because I’m usually standing out here celebrating with Lindsey. I know he’s looking down right now, smiling.”


She rejected criticism that she had effectively inherited the Senate seat, saying that her parents taught her and her brother “that you’re not given anything in life.”


“If you want something, you work for it,” she said, vowing to listen to Republicans on the ground over the next two weeks. “That’s how I’ve tried to live my life.”
