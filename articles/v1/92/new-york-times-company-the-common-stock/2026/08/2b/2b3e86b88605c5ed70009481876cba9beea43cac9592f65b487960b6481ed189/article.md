# As Death Toll From Colombia Earthquake Rises to at Least 181, Rescuers Hunt for Survivors

Desperate searches for survivors of a deadly Colombian earthquake continued throughout the night Monday and into Tuesday, with firefighters and other emergency workers using pickaxes and other tools to hack through the rubble in affected cities.


The death toll had risen to[at least 181 people](https://x.com/NoticiasCaracol/status/2087162383357661262) by Tuesday morning, according to Asocapitales, an association of Colombian cities. At least 1,310 people were injured and 153 buildings have collapsed.


The governor of Chocó, the region in western Colombia that was the epicenter of the quake, said Tuesday morning on national radio that the full extent of the devastation was not yet known, in part because communications were still down in eight municipalities in her department.


“What we don’t have is a complete tally of the physical damage,” said the governor, Nubia Carolina Córdoba Curi, “because it is truly massive.”


Late Monday the U.S. State Department said that it was[committing $15.5 million](https://x.com/statedept/status/2086937905541357951?s=61) in aid for emergency shelter, food, protection and earthquake assessments.


“We are united in prayer with the Colombian people and stand ready to support President @ABDELAESPRIELLA,” the department said in a statement on X, referring to Colombia’s new president, Abelardo De La Espriella.
