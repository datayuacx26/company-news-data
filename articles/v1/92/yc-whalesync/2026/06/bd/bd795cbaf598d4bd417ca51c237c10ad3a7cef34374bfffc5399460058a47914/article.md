# How to Sync Webflow CMS to HubSpot in 5 Minutes

Webflow CMS is where modern marketing teams build and manage the structured content that powers their websites: blog posts, landing pages, case studies, integration directories, and resource libraries, all stored as collections rather than hand-coded pages. HubSpot is where those same companies run their go-to-market engine, tracking the contacts, companies, deals, and conversations that make up every customer relationship.


The problem is that these two systems rarely talk to each other. The content on your website and the data in your CRM describe the same business, yet they live in separate silos. When a customer story changes, someone has to update it in both places. When sales closes a new logo that belongs in your customer directory, someone has to copy it over by hand. Sooner or later, the site and the CRM disagree.


Syncing Webflow CMS to HubSpot fixes this. Your CRM becomes the source of truth for customer and product data, your website reflects it automatically, and because the sync runs in both directions, edits made in Webflow flow back into HubSpot too.


In this guide, we'll walk through how to connect and sync Webflow CMS to HubSpot in about five minutes using Whalesync, with no code required.


## Why two-way sync beats CSV exports and Zapier


Most teams try to bridge the gap with one of two workarounds, and both fall short quickly.


**CSV exports** give you a snapshot. The file is accurate at the moment you download it and stale the moment anything changes. To keep your website current, you have to export, clean, and re-import over and over, matching columns by hand every time. Typos creep in, duplicates pile up, and records deleted in one tool linger forever in the other.


**Zapier-style automations** move data in one direction, one record at a time, in response to a trigger. That works for notifications, but it struggles with a living dataset. If a record is edited in both tools, nothing reconciles the two versions. If a zap fails silently, the record simply never arrives. And because triggers only fire on new events, the records you already have never get synced at all.


Whalesync takes a different approach. It creates a true two-way, real-time sync between Webflow CMS and HubSpot. Rather than firing one-off automations, it keeps entire tables aligned, so a change made in either tool shows up in the other automatically. There is nothing to script and nothing to babysit: the whole setup is no-code from end to end.

## What syncs between Webflow CMS and HubSpot


On the HubSpot side, Whalesync can sync contacts, companies, deals, tickets, tasks, notes, calls, meetings, quotes, line items, and products, plus custom objects. On the Webflow side, it works with your CMS collections, whatever they hold: blog posts, case studies, directory listings, team pages, and more.


Because the sync is two-way, you decide where each kind of edit happens. Update a company record in HubSpot and the matching Webflow item updates automatically; fix a typo in Webflow and the correction flows back into HubSpot. Both tools stay accurate without anyone copying and pasting.


## Three ways teams use a Webflow CMS to HubSpot sync


### Customer stories that publish themselves


Your sales and success teams already keep company records rich and current in HubSpot. Sync the relevant records into a Webflow collection and your customer page becomes a live reflection of the CRM: when a new logo closes or a customer's details change, the website updates without anyone filing a ticket to the web team.


### An integration or partner directory managed from the CRM


If your partnerships team tracks listings as HubSpot companies or custom objects, syncing them into Webflow lets the directory maintain itself. Partner details edited in HubSpot appear on the site immediately, and content tweaks made in Webflow flow back so the CRM never falls behind.


### Product content that matches your catalog


With products and line items living in HubSpot, syncing them into Webflow keeps product and pricing pages aligned with what your team actually sells. Rename a product or update a description once in HubSpot and every page that draws on that record follows along.
