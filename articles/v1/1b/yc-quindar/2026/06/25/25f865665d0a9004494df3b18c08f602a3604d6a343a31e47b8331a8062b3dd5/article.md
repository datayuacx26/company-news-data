# From Data to Decisions: Accelerating Constellation Operations with Mission Control, Fleet Planning, and Asset pages

Blog


June 5, 2026


Christina Geller


Operating a large satellite constellation is no longer a data problem — it’s a decision problem. Operators today aren’t lacking telemetry, contact schedules, or task execution data. The challenge is synthesizing that information fast enough to act. As one team put it, “we have the data, but it’s requiring too many clicks and collation to understand”.


With the release of the Mission Control, Fleet Planning, and Asset pages, Quindar is redefining how operators move from raw data to real-time action and leading the shift from monitoring individual spacecraft to managing systems.


**The Three Layers of Mission Management**


Quindar’s interface follows a three-layer model. Each page plays a distinct role, but the real power comes from how they work together.


Mission Control is there to direct an operator’s attention to what’s happening across the system right now while Fleet Planning is there to help them decide what’s going to happen next. When an event occurs, Quindar routes the operator to the individual Asset page and helps them understand exactly what is happening and what Quindar is doing about it.


The real acceleration comes from knowing how and when to chain these pages together.


**Mission Control: Instant Situational Awareness**


*Mission Control in Quindar enables operators to quickly look at the health and status across the constellation, ground network, and infrastructure.*


Mission Control is your status board for the entire constellation. It is designed to be on your MOC screens or your laptop’s homepage and answers, at a glance:


- Where are the problems?


- How severe are they?


- What systems are impacted?


Historically, operators have had to piece together data steams across the system, navigating across multiple pages, dashboards, and datasets. The result? Gaps in awareness and delayed response times.


Mission Control eliminates that fragmentation by aggregating asset health, infrastructure health, and network status into a single view. Anomalies and their severity are surfaced immediately, prioritizing attention on those critical moments and enabling operators to identify issues before diving into details .


This is how operators achieve awareness at scale **** through a view that is clear and prioritized, even as hundreds of contacts and tasks execute simultaneously.


**Fleet Planning: From Awareness to Foresight**


*Fleet Planning page in Quindar show the timeline of activity and tasks scheduled across the fleet.*


If Mission Control is about now, Fleet Planning is about next. Without Fleet Planning, operators must mentally stitch together timelines across assets or resort on spreadsheets and other inefficient planning tools. With it, operators have a coherent operational picture of the entire fleet as they can:


- Visualize hundreds of simultaneous contacts, maneuvers, and tasks


- Understand how actions across the constellation interact


- Identify conflicts or risks before they materialize


Fleet Planning is the moment where operators move from reacting to managing at scale **** —seeing contacts and tasks all executing seamlessly, not combing through chaotic spreadsheets or flipping back and forth between multiple screens.


**Asset Page: Precise Execution and Control**


*The individual Asset Page in Quindar enables manual management when necessary.*


When an asset status changes on Mission Control or a specific-asset needs closer inspection, operators drop into the Asset Page. This is where situational awareness becomes action.


As[previously mentioned](https://www.quindar.space/blog-article/smarter-ops-for-high-orbit-assets) , the Asset page is where automation and manual decision-making meet. When something goes wrong, the operator can dig into the details and either watch a runbook resolve the issue or manually manage the task queue until the anomaly is resolved.


**Operating Constellations, Not Satellites**


As constellations scale, operators cannot monitor each spacecraft individually nor can they jump between screens fast enough to maintain awareness. Quindar’s three-layer approach solves this by rolling up complexity into actionable views and encouraging system-level thinking.


Mission Control keeps an eye on the whole system, Fleet Planning contextualizes the broader operational timeline, and the Asset page enables rapid action.


This isn’t just a UI improvement — it’s an operational shift towards better mission management at scale. With these three pages, your operators can see the whole constellation, rapidly plan across the constellation, and know exactly what to do to keep the mission successful.
