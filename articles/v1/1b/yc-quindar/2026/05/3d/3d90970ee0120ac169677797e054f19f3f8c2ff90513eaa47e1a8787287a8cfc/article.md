# Planning for the Time You Need Across the Fleet

Blog


May 26, 2026


Christina Geller, Director of Product Management


In fleet operations, you’re rarely planning around a specific antenna or ground station. You’re planning around time.


You need 5 minutes here. A contact there. Coverage across the fleet without necessarily caring which ground stations provides it. But planning has been fragmented in spreadsheets and side conversations. Answering a simple question — *“Do we have coverage when we need it?”* — has required stitching together multiple sources of information.


Quindar changes that. The latest release introduces Fleet Planning and Access Windows, bringing planning and availability together into a single workflow centered around time.


**Bringing Planning into the Platform**


Until now, fleet planning has often happened outside of Quindar. Teams would map out plans manually, then translate them back into the system for execution. That gap created friction —between planning and operations, between intention and reality – and grew larger as additional satellites came online.


The new **Fleet Planning** page closes that gap. It gives teams a dedicated place to:


- See their fleet in one view
- Plan across assets
- Align on a shared operational picture


Planning moves out of spreadsheets and into the same system used to operate the fleet. Operators no longer must click back and forth between different pages or tools to understand what is happening when across the constellation.


Instead, operators can leverage the timeline view to see where gaps in contact might occur or when an opportunity exists for scheduling more tasks. The table view shows you where may need to manually intervene with the queue, address tickets, or response to open requests as well as all the upcoming contacts. Information that can be exported and shared with team members who aren’t quite willing to let go of their spreadsheets.


**Making Availability Visible**


Historically, visibility with contacts has been fragmented. Operators have had to piece together availability across providers, antennas, and systems just to answer a basic question: *“What coverage do I actually have?”* .


That’s where Access Windows come in. Access Windows unify availability across the ground network and surface it as a single, time-based view. Instead of thinking in terms of individual antennas or providers, operators can now focus on what they need:


- Coverage from 10:00 to 10:15
- A 5-minute contact window in the next three hours
- Multiple contacts across a given time range


Behind the scenes, Access Windows roll up availability across ground stations, antennas, and providers. What you see is not a single antenna, but the total opportunity for contact.


Access Window scheduling across multiple ground station providers enables scheduling for the exact time and duration required for contact.


This shifts the planning model entirely. Instead of asking, “ *Which ground station can support this?”* . Operators can now ask: “Do I have access when I need it?”. And, just as importantly, they can act on that answer immediately.


Within Fleet Planning, this means operators can identify a time window, understand if coverage exists, and plan against it without needing to search across availabilities and select the exact contact upfront. The system handles the complexity of the network, so teams can stay focused on mission needs.


**From Guesswork to Mission Clarity**


With Fleet Planning and Access Windows, planning and availability are no longer separate steps. They’re part of the same workflow.


There’s no need to reconcile spreadsheets, cross-check systems, or fill in gaps manually. The full picture — the plan and the availability to execute on that plan — is already there, grounded in actual system data. This creates clarity on:


- What the fleet can support
- How well the plan aligns with reality
- Where adjustments can occur as mission needs evolve


Instead of asking *“Will this work?”* after the fact, teams can move forward knowing that it will with Quindar.
