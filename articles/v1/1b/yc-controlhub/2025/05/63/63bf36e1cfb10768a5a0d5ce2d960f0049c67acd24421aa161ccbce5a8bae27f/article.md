# PO Number: Generation & Use Explained

Now more than ever B2B are purchasing online, and while over all the processes required purchase orders or PO might not seem the main character in the film. It’s extremely important to get all the details right.


After all, without a complete purchase order, no transaction could happen.


With that in mind, let’s dedicate ourselves to going over the basics of PO


## What’s a Purchase Order?


[Purchase orders](https://www.controlhub.com/blog/purchase-order) play a huge role in businesses. A purchase order document serves as a binding document and a legal agreement between the buyer and seller, detailing the specifics of the purchase, such as quantity, type, and agreed-upon price of the products or services. This formal document is a crucial part of the purchasing process, laying the groundwork for effective communication, clear expectations, and smooth transactions. It essentially acts as a roadmap for both the[buyer and seller.](https://www.controlhub.com/purchasing-terms/what-is-sales-receipt)


‍


### The main components of PO


- The date when the order is placed
- Item descriptions
- Terms of payment
- Deadlines like delivery dates
- Taxes if applicable
- Contact information
- Billing address
- The PO number


‍


## What's a PO Number?


Each[PO](https://www.controlhub.com/purchasing-terms/4-types-of-purchase-orders) is assigned a unique number, acting as a distinct identifier that simplifies tracking and management. This unique identifier essentially breathes life into the lifecycle of every business purchase, providing traceability from initiation to fulfillment.


‍


An efficient[PO numbering](https://www.controlhub.com/blog/po-number) system cannot be overstated. It ensures accuracy in records, enabling swift resolution of queries and disputes and aiding in forecasting and reporting. Integrating purchase approval software with the numbering system enhances the efficiency further. It's like a pulse check, allowing executives to monitor procurement activities and make informed decisions.


## How to create a Purchase Order number?


### Manual vs Automated


Before getting started you’ll have first to determine if you are going to create the number manually or if you are going to use software for it.


Manual PO numbers might be best suitable for small businesses that don’t have to deal with lots of[purchasing](https://www.controlhub.com/blog/what-are-three-types-of-purchasing) .


On the contrary, if the company is a rather large one, there will be a heavy amount of purchasing that’s required, making it logical to rely on the help of an automated program to generate it.


But how?


Let’s find it out


**Step 1: Define the Components of the**[PO Number](https://www.controlhub.com/blog/po-number)


Decide on the structure and components of your PO numbers. Typically, a PO number includes:


Prefix: Optional but often used to denote the type of PO (e.g., 'PO' for regular purchase orders).


Sequential Numbers: A unique numerical identifier that increases with each new PO.


Suffix or Year: Optionally, you can include a suffix indicating the fiscal year or other relevant information.


**Step 2: Determine the Format**


Choose a format that works for your organization:


Numeric: Sequential numbers only (e.g., 001, 002, 003...).


Alphanumeric: Combine letters and numbers (e.g., PO001, PO002...).


Date-based: Include the order date in the number (e.g., PO2024-001).


**Step 3: Establish a Prefix (This step is not mandatory)**


If you decide to use a prefix, determine what it will be, a common one is PO. This helps distinguish PO numbers from other transaction types within your organization.
