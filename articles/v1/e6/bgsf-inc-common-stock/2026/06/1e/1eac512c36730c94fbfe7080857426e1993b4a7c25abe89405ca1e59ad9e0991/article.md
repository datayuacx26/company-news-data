# What Netflix Can Teach Multifamily About Resident Experience

Resident experience in multifamily has shifted from being a “nice-to-have” to a core driver of retention, renewals, and reputation. And while property managers don’t have algorithms on par with Netflix, there’s a lot to learn from how the platform keeps millions of users engaged without them ever having to think about it.


At its core, Netflix is successful because it removes friction and makes every interaction feel personal, timely, and effortless. That same mindset can transform how communities engage with residents, from leasing to renewal and everything in between.


Here’s what multifamily leaders can learn from the Netflix approach to experience design.


##


**The Multifamily Experience**


##


###


**1. Personalization: From “One-Size-Fits-All” to “Feels Like It Knows Me”**


Netflix doesn’t just show content—it curates it. Every homepage is unique, shaped by viewing history, preferences, and behavior patterns. The result is a platform that feels built specifically for each user.


Multifamily communities often still rely on broad, generic communication: mass emails, one-size-fits-all newsletters, and standardized messaging that doesn’t reflect individual resident needs or preferences.


But residents expect more now. Personalization in multifamily can look like:


-


Tailored communication based on lease stage (new move-in vs. long-term resident)


-


Targeted amenity messaging (fitness users vs. pet owners vs. remote workers)


-


Preference-based updates (text vs. email vs. app notifications)


-


Customized renewal offers based on behavior and engagement


When communication feels relevant, residents are more likely to engage—and less likely to tune out.


###


**2. Recommendations: Helping Residents Discover Value They Didn’t Know They Needed**


Netflix is famous for its recommendation engine. It doesn’t wait for users to search. It actively suggests what they might enjoy next.


Multifamily communities can apply the same concept to resident experience by shifting from reactive communication to proactive value delivery.


Instead of waiting for residents to ask, communities can recommend:


-


Amenities they haven’t used yet (pool events, coworking spaces, pet amenities)


-


Community events based on interests or past attendance


-


Service upgrades like package lockers, valet trash, or tech-enabled conveniences


-


Local partnerships (gyms, coffee shops, dog walkers, etc.)


The goal isn’t to sell—it’s to reveal value that residents might overlook. When residents consistently discover new benefits, perceived value increases, even if rent doesn’t change.


###


**3. Timely Communication: The Right Message at the Right Moment**


One of Netflix’s quiet superpowers is timing. It sends notifications when they’re most relevant: “New season available,” “Because you watched…,” or “Trending now.”


In multifamily, timing is often where communication breaks down. Messages are either too early, too late, or too generic.


Improving timing means aligning communication with real resident moments:


-


Move-in: guided onboarding messages spaced across the first 30 days


-


Seasonal transitions: HVAC reminders before peak heat or cold


-


Maintenance: proactive updates before residents submit requests


-


Community events: reminders at optimal engagement windows, not just once


-


Renewal period: personalized outreach before the resident even starts shopping elsewhere


Timely communication reduces frustration and builds trust because it shows awareness rather than automation.


###


**4. Anticipating Needs: Solving Problems Before They Become Problems**


Netflix rarely asks users what they want—it predicts it. Whether it’s buffering quality adjustments or next-episode autoplay, it removes friction before the user even notices it.


Multifamily teams can adopt a similar mindset by shifting from reactive service to proactive operations. Examples include:


-


Identifying maintenance issues before they escalate through smart building tech


-


Anticipating seasonal service demand (AC, plumbing, snow removal, etc.)


-


Noticing resident behavior signals that indicate dissatisfaction (reduced engagement, late payments, fewer amenity visits)


-


Preparing staffing levels ahead of peak times rather than responding to them


Anticipation builds a sense of care. Residents may not see the behind-the-scenes effort, but they feel the difference in consistency and reliability.


###


**5. Seamless Digital Experiences: Making Every Interaction Effortless**


Netflix works because it removes complexity and delivers a user-friendly experience. No instructions needed. No confusion. Just click and go.


On the other hand, multifamily digital experiences often still feel fragmented with multiple portals, inconsistent apps, manual processes, and unclear communication channels.


A seamless resident experience might include:


-


One unified resident portal for payments, maintenance, and communication


-


Mobile-first maintenance requests with real-time updates


-


Digital leasing and renewal workflows that don’t require printing or scanning


-


Integrated communication across email, SMS, and app notifications


-


Easy access to community information without searching multiple platforms


The less effort required from residents, the higher their satisfaction, even if they never explicitly say it.


##


**The Takeaway: Experience Is the Product**


Netflix doesn’t win because of its content alone. It wins because it understands experience is the product.


Multifamily communities are no different. The apartment itself is only part of the value. The real differentiator is how residents feel day-to-day: how easy it is to communicate, how supported they feel, and how seamlessly their needs are met.


When multifamily operators start thinking like Netflix, by personalizing communication, recommending value, timing interactions, anticipating needs, and simplifying digital experiences, resident satisfaction stops being reactive and starts becoming intentional. And that’s where retention begins to shift from chance to strategy.
