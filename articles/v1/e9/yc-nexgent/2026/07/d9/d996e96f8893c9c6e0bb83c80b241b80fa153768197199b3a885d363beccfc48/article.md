# How to Get an IT Job Without a Degree: Certifications, Entry Roles, and Your Career Path

Are you considering a move into the IT field, but worried that not having a college degree will hold you back? You are not alone. Career changers, veterans, and tech enthusiasts from all backgrounds are entering IT and cybersecurity roles without a traditional degree. This guide explains how to get an IT job without a degree, which certifications open the door, what entry-level positions to target, and how to build a sustainable, upward career path.


If you want to know how to get an IT job without a degree, the most effective path is to focus on earning industry-recognized certifications, building hands-on experience through home labs or projects, and targeting entry-level roles that value skills over formal education. Employers in IT increasingly hire based on practical ability and verifiable skills, not just diplomas.


## Is a Degree Necessary for an IT Career in 2026?


The IT sector is rapidly evolving. While some job postings still mention degree requirements, the reality is that most employers prioritize real-world skills and certifications. Major tech firms such as Google, Apple, IBM, and Microsoft have publicly committed to skills-based hiring. According to recent data, 78% of companies claim to use this approach, yet many job descriptions still list degrees as preferred. For motivated candidates, this means you can absolutely break in without a four-year degree by proving your technical know-how and hands-on ability.


## How Do Certifications Replace a Degree in IT?


Certifications are the new currency for IT job seekers without a degree. They demonstrate that you meet industry standards and are job-ready. Entry-level certifications like CompTIA A+, CompTIA Network+, and Cisco CCNA validate your foundational knowledge and troubleshooting skills. Specialized certifications such as CompTIA Security+ or Linux+ help you transition into cybersecurity or systems roles. Not only do these credentials signal readiness, but many include practical labs that show employers you can actually do the work.


### Top Entry-Level IT Certifications


- **CompTIA A+** : For IT support/help desk roles; covers hardware, OS, troubleshooting.
- **CompTIA Network+** : For network support and junior network admin roles; covers networking fundamentals.
- **CompTIA Security+** : For entry-level cybersecurity jobs; covers security concepts and risk management.
- **Cisco CCNA** : Recognized by employers for network admin positions; focuses on routing, switching, and network operations.
- **Microsoft Fundamentals** : Good for foundational knowledge in Microsoft environments.
- **CompTIA ITF+** : An introductory certification for those brand new to IT concepts.


### Certification Timeline and Investment


Certification costs and timelines vary, but most can be completed in a few months with consistent study. Investing in a well-structured learning path helps you stay focused and makes the training more effective.


## What Are the Best Entry-Level IT Jobs Without a Degree?


In today’s market, several high-demand IT roles are open to candidates with the right certifications and skills. Here are some of the most accessible positions for career changers:


- **Help Desk/IT Support Specialist** : The classic starting point. You’ll troubleshoot user issues, manage tickets, and gain broad exposure to IT systems. Customer service skills are as important as technical expertise. Typical salaries: $45,000–$65,000.
- **Desktop Support Technician** : Focuses on hardware/software issues, installations, and user support. Strong Windows administration and troubleshooting skills are key. Salaries: $48,000–$68,000.
- **Junior Network Administrator** : Assists with network setup, monitoring, and support. CCNA or Network+ are often required. Salaries: $55,000–$72,000.
- **Junior Systems Administrator** : Works with operating systems, servers, and directory services. Knowledge of PowerShell, Windows Server, or Linux helps. Salaries: $55,000–$75,000.
- **Cybersecurity Analyst (entry/junior)** : Monitors systems for threats and supports security tools. CompTIA Security+ is a common baseline. Salaries: $65,000–$95,000.


### Advancement Paths from Entry-Level IT Jobs


- From **Help Desk** to **Network Admin** or **Systems Admin**
- From **Support Specialist** to **Cybersecurity Analyst** (with Security+ or further study)
- From **Junior Network Admin** to **Network Engineer** or **Security Engineer**


With each new certification and hands-on project, your opportunity for advancement and higher pay increases. Many of today’s IT managers started in support roles and worked their way up.


## Building Hands-On Experience Without a Degree


One of the best ways to stand out is by building a home lab. This is a personal setup where you practice configuring networks, servers, or security tools. Document your home lab projects and include them on your resume and LinkedIn profile. Employers value real-world troubleshooting and initiative over classroom theory. You can also build experience by volunteering IT support or contributing to open-source projects. Exploring[how to build a home lab for networking](https://www.blog.nexgent.com/blog/how-to-build-a-home-lab-for-networking) can significantly boost your skill development.


## How to Read IT Job Postings Without a Degree


Job ads often list a bachelor’s degree required—but this is frequently negotiable. What they usually want is certifications plus practical experience. If a degree is preferred rather than required, focus on showcasing your certifications, portfolio, and ability to solve problems.


## Essential Skills for IT Success Beyond Certifications


Technical skills are critical, but soft skills can make or break your IT job search. Employers seek candidates who can communicate clearly, document solutions, and work patiently with users.


## Step-by-Step Action Plan: How to Get an IT Job Without a Degree


1. **Choose a Path:** Identify the IT area that interests you most—support, networking, systems, or cybersecurity.
2. **Earn an Entry-Level Certification:** Start with CompTIA A+, Network+, or Security+ depending on your goal.
3. **Build and Document a Home Lab:** Practice skills, take screenshots, and write up your projects.
4. **Update Your Resume:** Put certifications and hands-on projects at the top.
5. **Apply Widely:** Target jobs that list degrees as preferred or do not mention degrees at all.
6. **Prepare for Interviews:** Be ready to demonstrate technical skills and explain your learning journey.
7. **Pursue Ongoing Learning:** Add specialized certifications as you gain experience.
