# What’s next for SunOpta?

EDEN PRAIRIE, MINN. — The recent acquisition of SunOpta — a plant-based beverage, broth and fruit snacks manufacturer — by global beverage solutions provider Refresco may have surprised some in the industry, given that SunOpta appeared to have recently[turned its business around](https://www.foodbusinessnews.net/articles/28307-better-for-you-focus-paying-off-for-sunopta) , becoming profitable after a period of[negative net income](https://www.foodbusinessnews.net/articles/27140-sunopta-sees-surge-in-revenues) .


“SunOpta was doing well,” said Brian Kocher, chief executive officer, SunOpta, speaking to *Food Business News* . “We were growing revenue. We were expanding margin. We continued to bring on new customers. But also, we were probably constrained a little bit in that we were only so big and we only had so much financial capacity to invest in growth and further expansion. While we were healthy and there were no financial concerns, this wasn’t a liquidity or a credibility issue. It was about there being more business opportunity out there, and if we had more financial scale we might be able to capture more business opportunities than we could do on our own.”


In February,[Refresco entered into an agreement to acquire SunOpta](https://www.foodbusinessnews.net/articles/29792-refresco-to-acquire-sunopta) , with the deal finalized in May. Refresco paid $6.50 per share of SunOpta in cash, for a total valuation of approximately $1.1 billion. SunOpta is now a fully owned subsidiary of Refresco, and shares of SunOpta will no longer be publicly traded.


Kocher said SunOpta’s full range of capabilities — private label plant-based beverages, creamers, broths, ready-to-drink protein shakes and fruit snacks, along with its line of[Sown brand plant-based creamers](https://www.foodbusinessnews.net/articles/24621-sunopta-expands-sown-coffee-creamer-lineup) — is complementary to Refresco’s output of private label ready-to-drink coffee, tea, plant-based drinks, energy drinks, sports drinks, juices, carbonated soft drinks, mineral waters and alcoholic beverages for the European, Australian and North American markets.


“It was a very similar approach to the commercial market,” Kocher said. “There are very complementary product offerings, so those fit in well together. Ultimately, it ended up being a very attractive financial value for our shareholders as well.”


Kocher added, “I don’t think you’ll see big wholesale changes like SunOpta doesn’t exist anymore. Of course we do. We’re delivering now as part of the Refresco organization. ... I’m really excited that we get aligned with a company that is so similar and yet completely complementary. Sometimes, in acquisitions in particular, that might not be the case. I think if another plant-based beverage company acquired us, for instance, I think there’d be a lot more redundancies.”


**Common-sense collaboration**


Some of the other complementary aspects to the deal, Kocher said, include potentially reducing costs for materials through a stronger bargaining position in the market.


“This deal wasn’t as much about what I’ll call cost synergies, as it was about having common-sense collaboration,” he said. “We both buy cardboard, for example. Are there ways that we can collaborate there? We’re both approaching the commercial market. We have some strength in customers that maybe they don’t necessarily have some strength in, and vice versa. Are there ways that we can help each other and collaborate in that environment?”

Another collaborative possibility is addressing the rising growth of energy in food and beverages across the multiple product formats that Refresco and SunOpta now combine to manufacture.


“Energy continues to gain speed,” he said. “It could be energy in plant based, it could be energy in fruit snacks. We’ve got 21 food scientists at SunOpta that work on delivering solutions for our customers. They may be able to help some of Refresco’s food scientists as well, so it’s really the power of combining our resources and capabilities to deliver the best answers for our customers.”


**Expansion opportunities**


Refresco — based in Rotterdam, The Netherlands — is the largest independent bottler of beverages for retailers and major brands in the world, the company states on its website, with over 75 manufacturing sites in Europe, Australia and North America. With a capacity far surpassing SunOpta’s seven manufacturing facilities (six in the United States, one in Canada), the opportunities now exist for SunOpta to expand its product reach beyond North America.


“Certainly, we’ll be in tune and aware of some opportunities outside North America for either plant-based beverages or fruit snacks,” Kocher said. “There may be an opportunity for us to share practices, leverage technology and potentially leverage sales distribution. But right now, I would think collaboration is high on the list. The actual sales of product outside the US might be a little lower on the priority list.”


“I don’t think you’ll see big wholesale changes like SunOpta doesn’t exist anymore. Of course we do. We’re delivering now as part of the Refresco organization,” said Brian Kocher, chief executive officer of SunOpta.

SunOpta’s fastest-growing segment in recent years has been its[fruit snacks manufacturing](https://www.foodbusinessnews.net/articles/24789-sunopta-expands-washington-facility) , which has seen double-digit growth for 21 consecutive quarters, and accounts for approximately 20% of SunOpta’s total revenue. The company recently[invested $25 million](https://www.foodbusinessnews.net/articles/28805-sunopta-investing-25-million-in-fruit-snack-expansion) in an additional fruit snack production line at its Omak, Wash., facility to increase capacity and handle ongoing demand for the product. That line just opened this week.


“There are some categories in food that are growing, but there’s not a lot,” Kocher said. “But this one (fruit snacks) is growing and continues to grow at double digits. So, we’re excited about the opportunities in the fruit snacks category.”


As for the current state of plant milk sales — SunOpta manufactures oat, almond, soy, coconut, hemp, and others for clients — Kocher said, “Almond continues to drive some growth in the category. The good news with a company like SunOpta is there will be ebbs and flows in the various commodities. We’ve actually seen a little bit of increase in soy, for example ... but right now I’d definitely say oat is the top seller because of its applicability and broad usage range in coffee and coffee houses. We continue to see oat growing.”


Overall, Kocher sees Refresco’s acquisition of SunOpta as a win/win for the future growth of both companies.


“There’ll be a lot of work to do in terms of how we collaborate and how we bring that collaboration to life for the benefit of our customers, but I’m really excited about what lies ahead for SunOpta, for our customers and for our employees,” he said.
