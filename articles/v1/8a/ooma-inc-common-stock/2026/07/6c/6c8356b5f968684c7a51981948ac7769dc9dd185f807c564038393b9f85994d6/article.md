# Common tech terms people pretend to understand


[Husain Sumra](https://www.ooma.com/author/hsumra/)


July 27, 2026 | 8 min read


Technology has become so woven into everyday work that it’s easy to forget how many systems operate behind the scenes. We use terms like “cloud,” “firewall,” “bandwidth” and “encryption” regularly, but many people aren’t entirely sure what those terms mean or how they fit together.


To help cut through the jargon, the team at[Ooma](https://www.ooma.com/) created an infographic that explains common technology terms in plain English and shows how the different pieces of today’s digital workplace connect.


That understanding is becoming increasingly important. Today, 92 percent of U.S. jobs require digital literacy skills. Whether you’re collaborating remotely, managing customer communications or using cloud-based business tools, a stronger grasp of technology fundamentals can help you work more confidently and effectively.


## Why digital literacy matters


Modern technology is designed to be user-friendly, but understanding what’s happening behind the screen can make a significant difference when problems arise.


A video call that suddenly freezes, a file that won’t sync or an application that loads slowly can have many possible causes. Knowing the difference between a network issue, a software problem and a cloud-service outage helps narrow down the source of the problem and speeds up troubleshooting.


A stronger technology vocabulary also improves communication. Employees can describe issues more accurately, collaborate more effectively with technical teams and feel more confident when adopting new tools and platforms.


As businesses continue to rely on cloud applications, remote collaboration and connected devices, digital literacy has become a practical workplace skill rather than a specialized technical one.


## Networking: How devices stay connected


Networking makes it possible for computers, phones, tablets and other devices to communicate with one another and access online services.


Terms related to networking often describe how information travels from one location to another. Some refer to the speed and capacity of an internet connection, while others identify devices on a network or manage the flow of traffic between them.


Routers, IP addresses, bandwidth and Wi-Fi all play a role in helping information reach the right destination. Even a fast internet connection depends on reliable networking equipment to deliver consistent performance across multiple devices.


## Cybersecurity: Keeping information safe


Cybersecurity focuses on protecting systems, data and digital identities from unauthorized access and online threats.


Many cybersecurity terms relate to protection and verification. Passwords, multi-factor authentication, encryption and firewalls help confirm identities, secure information and control who can access sensitive resources.


Other terms describe the threats organizations and individuals face. Malware, phishing attacks, ransomware and other cyber risks are designed to steal information, disrupt operations or gain unauthorized access to systems.


Understanding the basics of cybersecurity helps employees recognize risks and make safer decisions when working online.


## Software: The tools people use every day


Software includes the programs and applications that tell computers and devices what to do.


Operating systems manage hardware resources and provide the foundation for everything else running on a device. Applications handle specific tasks such as word processing, messaging, accounting, customer service and video conferencing.


Many software platforms also communicate with one another through APIs and other background technologies that allow data to move between systems. Those connections help businesses automate processes and keep information synchronized across multiple tools.


## Core technology: Hardware and the cloud


Every digital experience relies on a combination of physical equipment and remote computing resources.


Hardware includes the devices people use every day, such as laptops, smartphones, servers and networking equipment. These physical components provide the processing power needed to run applications and perform tasks.


Cloud computing moves much of that work beyond a single device. Applications, files, databases and services can be hosted on remote servers and accessed through the internet, allowing people to work from virtually anywhere while keeping information centralized and accessible.


The flexibility and scalability of cloud technology have made it a foundation of modern business operations.


## How everything works together


Technology functions as a connected ecosystem rather than a collection of separate tools.


When someone opens an application, the device’s hardware provides processing power while the operating system manages resources needed to run the software. If information is needed from the internet, networking equipment routes the request to the appropriate destination while cybersecurity systems help verify and protect the connection.


Many requests eventually reach cloud-based servers, where data is stored, processed and exchanged with other applications. The results travel back through the network and appear on the user’s screen, often in a matter of seconds.


What feels like a simple action often involves hardware, software, networking, cloud infrastructure, and security technologies working together simultaneously. Understanding those relationships helps make everyday technology feel less mysterious and can give you greater confidence when using the digital tools that power modern work.


## Technology glossary


Use this glossary as a quick reference for understanding the concepts that power modern devices, applications and networks.


### Networking terms


#### Bandwidth


Bandwidth refers to the amount of data an internet connection can transfer over a given period of time. Higher bandwidth allows more information to move at once, helping support activities such as video conferencing, file sharing, streaming and cloud-based applications.


#### IP address


An IP address is a unique identifier assigned to a device connected to a network. Similar to a mailing address, it helps data find the correct destination when information is sent between devices across the internet.


#### Network


A network is a group of connected devices that can share information and resources. Networks can be as small as a home setup with a few devices or as large as the global internet.


#### Router


A router directs data between devices and the internet. It manages network traffic and helps ensure information reaches the right destination, whether that’s a computer, smartphone, printer or another connected device.


#### Wi-Fi


Wi-Fi is a wireless networking technology that allows devices to connect to the internet and communicate with one another without physical cables. Most homes and businesses rely on Wi-Fi to connect laptops, phones, tablets and other devices.


### Cybersecurity terms


#### Authentication


Authentication is the process of verifying a user’s identity before granting access to a system, application or account. Common authentication methods include passwords, security codes, biometric scans and multi-factor authentication.


#### Encryption


Encryption protects sensitive information by converting readable data into coded information. Only authorized users with the proper credentials or encryption keys can access the original content.


#### Firewall


A firewall monitors and filters incoming and outgoing network traffic based on security rules. It helps prevent unauthorized access while allowing legitimate communications to pass through.


#### Malware


Malware is software designed to damage systems, disrupt operations, steal information or gain unauthorized access to devices. Common types of malware include viruses, ransomware, spyware and trojans.


#### Multi-factor authentication (MFA)


Multi-factor authentication requires users to provide two or more forms of verification before accessing an account. This additional layer of security helps protect against unauthorized access, even if a password is compromised.


#### Phishing


Phishing is a cyberattack that uses deceptive emails, text messages, websites or other communications to trick people into revealing passwords, financial information or other sensitive data.


#### Ransomware


Ransomware is a type of malware that locks or encrypts files and demands payment to restore access. It is one of the most disruptive forms of cybercrime affecting businesses and individuals.


### Software terms


#### API


An API, or Application Programming Interface, allows different software applications to exchange data and work together. APIs help connect systems and automate processes that would otherwise require manual effort.


#### Application


An application, often called an app, is a software program designed to perform specific tasks. Examples include messaging apps, productivity tools, accounting software and customer communication platforms.


#### Integration


An integration connects two or more software systems so they can share information and work together. Integrations help reduce manual work and improve efficiency across business processes.


#### Operating system


An operating system is the core software that manages a device’s hardware and resources. It provides the foundation that allows applications to run and users to interact with computers, smartphones and other devices.


#### Software


Software refers to the programs and applications that run on a device. Everything from email platforms and web browsers to accounting systems and video conferencing tools falls into this category.


### Core technology terms


#### Cloud computing


Cloud computing delivers applications, storage and computing resources over the internet. Instead of relying entirely on local devices, users can access software, files and services hosted on remote servers.


#### Cloud storage


Cloud storage allows files and data to be stored on remote servers that can be accessed through the internet. It enables users to retrieve information from multiple devices and locations.


#### Device


A device is any piece of hardware that connects to a network or performs computing tasks. Common examples include laptops, desktop computers, smartphones, tablets, printers and smart devices.


#### Hardware


Hardware includes the physical components of a technology system, such as computers, smartphones, servers, routers and networking equipment. While software provides instructions, hardware performs the actual computing work.


#### Server


A server is a computer or system that stores data, hosts applications or provides services to other devices. Many websites, cloud applications and business systems rely on servers to function.


## Building confidence with technology


Technology doesn’t have to feel complicated. Understanding a few foundational concepts can make it easier to troubleshoot problems, communicate with colleagues, evaluate new tools and adapt as technology continues to evolve.


The more familiar you become with the language behind networking, cybersecurity, software and cloud computing, the easier it becomes to make informed decisions about the technology you use every day.
