Here's a pattern we see with every YC batch: a small, brilliant team spends 3 months heads-down building product. They nail demo day. They raise a round. Then they look up and realize they have zero organic traffic, zero content, and zero SEO presence.


Meanwhile, the company two desks over started publishing on week one. By demo day, they have a real library of indexed pages, they are ranking for long-tail keywords, and they have a steady trickle of inbound leads. Six months later, that trickle is a river.


**Content compounds. But only if you start early.**

No judgment. They are PhD-level engineers building cutting-edge AI. Content infrastructure just is not their world. I have been doing this for 20 years and I still learn new things every week. But here is what we told them, and what I would tell every YC company:

### Month 3-6: Compound Growth


- Your early posts start ranking in Google
- Organic traffic begins flowing

- You have a library of content to share with investors, customers, and recruits


## Why Starting Early Actually Matters


I am not going to hand you a projection table. Anyone who promises you a specific visitor count by month six is guessing, and the real number depends entirely on your niche, your competition, and how consistently you actually publish.


Here is the part that is not a guess. Organic search compounds for two structural reasons:


- **Indexing has a lag.** A page published today does not rank today. Google has to crawl it, index it, and accumulate enough signal to place it. That lag is measured in weeks or months, and the clock does not start until the page exists.
- **Every ranking page is a permanent asset.** A post that ranks keeps earning visits with no additional spend. A paid click earns you one visit and then it is gone.


The cost of waiting is the lag itself. A team that publishes in week one has pages accumulating signal while the team that waits until after demo day is still sitting at zero. Both teams might reach the same destination. One of them gets there months earlier, and months of runway is the one thing an early-stage company cannot buy back.


Our own oldest posts still drive meaningful traffic today. That is the entire argument.


## What We Have Learned Dog-Fooding Our Own System


At Cosmic, we practice what we preach. We are a YC company (W19) and we use our own AI agents to run our content operation. After decades of building content systems for other people, there is something deeply satisfying about finally having a platform that works the way I always wished the old tools would. Here is what we have learned:


- **Consistency beats perfectionism.** A good post published every week beats a perfect post published once a quarter
- **Technical content performs best.** In our own analytics, our developer guides, tutorials, and head-to-head comparisons consistently out-earn opinion pieces by a wide margin
- **AI drafts, humans edit.** The best workflow is letting AI handle the first draft, then adding your unique perspective and experience
- **Distribution matters as much as creation.** A great post nobody sees loses to a good post shared everywhere
- **Compound effects are real.** Our oldest posts still drive significant traffic. Every new post builds on the foundation
