# Best Contentful Alternatives in 2026

If you've been shopping for a Contentful alternative, you already know the pattern: great product, pricing that scales faster than your traffic, and a feature roadmap that prioritizes enterprise accounts over indie teams.


This list cuts through the noise. We compared seven platforms on pricing, AI features, developer experience, and content modeling flexibility. Cosmic is on this list too, and we'll be upfront about where it fits and where it doesn't.


---


## What to look for in a Contentful alternative


Before picking a platform, decide which of these matters most to your team:


- *Free plan generosity:* Does it let you build something real before paying?
- *Pricing transparency:* Can you predict your bill as you scale?
- *Developer experience:* REST API, SDK quality, TypeScript support, local preview?
- *AI-native features:* Content generation, AI agents, semantic search built in, or bolt-on?
- *Content modeling:* How flexible are the field types and relationships?
- *Team collaboration:* Roles, permissions, editorial workflows, draft/publish controls?


Keep those in mind as you read.

## 2. Sanity


*Best for: teams that want maximum content modeling flexibility and are comfortable with a steeper setup curve.*


Sanity uses a code-first content studio (Sanity Studio) that you configure in TypeScript. This gives developers full control over the editing experience, but it means non-technical editors often need developer help to get set up.


- *Strengths:* extremely flexible schema, GROQ query language, real-time collaboration, strong community.
- *Watch out for:* the free plan allows up to 20 users but limits you to 2 datasets (projects). Pricing scales by API usage on higher tiers, so costs can climb on large teams with heavy traffic.
- *AI features:* some AI Assist features are available, but they are add-ons, not core.


---


## 3. Storyblok


*Best for: marketing teams and agencies that prioritize visual editing and a component-based approach.*


Storyblok's Visual Editor is genuinely good. Non-technical editors can see exactly what content will look like before publishing, which reduces back-and-forth with developers.


- *Strengths:* best-in-class visual editor, strong agency ecosystem, good localization support.
- *Watch out for:* the free plan is limited to 1 user. Pricing steps up significantly for team features. Webhooks and advanced workflows require higher tiers.
- *AI features:* AI translation and some generation features are available on paid plans.


---


## 4. Hygraph (formerly GraphCMS)


*Best for: teams with complex content federation needs or existing GraphQL infrastructure.*


Hygraph is built around a federated content approach, pulling content from multiple sources into a single API layer. If your stack already uses GraphQL, Hygraph fits naturally.


- *Strengths:* content federation, strong GraphQL API, good localization, 3 seats on the free Hobby plan.
- *Watch out for:* pricing can be opaque for teams that are not already GraphQL-native. The 10-component limit on free can become a bottleneck on complex content models.
- *AI features:* limited compared to AI-native platforms.


---


## 5. Strapi


*Best for: teams that want full control and are comfortable self-hosting.*


Strapi is open-source and self-hostable, which makes it popular with teams that have strong DevOps capabilities and data sovereignty requirements.


- *Strengths:* open source, full self-hosting option, large plugin ecosystem, flexible content types.
- *Watch out for:* self-hosting means you own the infrastructure, security patches, and uptime. The managed cloud option closes that gap but adds cost. AI features are limited.
- *AI features:* minimal out of the box.


---


## 6. Prismic


*Best for: marketing-focused teams building slice-based page layouts with Next.js, Nuxt, or SvelteKit.*


Prismic's Slice Machine is a unique approach: developers define reusable page sections (slices) in code, and editors compose pages from them. Works well for landing pages and marketing sites.


- *Strengths:* Slice Machine is a genuinely clever editorial model, strong framework integrations (Next.js, Nuxt, SvelteKit). Free plan includes 4M API calls/month.
- *Watch out for:* the slice model can feel rigid for content-heavy applications (blogs, catalogs, documentation). Free plan is limited to 1 user. AI features are limited.
- *AI features:* some AI writing assistance, but not a core differentiator.


---


## 7. Builder.io


*Best for: teams that prioritize visual page building and A/B testing over structured content management.*


Builder.io is more of a visual CMS and page builder than a traditional headless CMS. If your primary use case is landing pages and marketing experiments, it fits well.


- *Strengths:* powerful visual editor, A/B testing built in, good e-commerce integrations.
- *Watch out for:* structured content modeling (think: a catalog of 10,000 products with complex relationships) is not its primary strength. Pricing is usage-based and can scale unpredictably.
- *AI features:* AI-assisted design and generation features on paid plans.

## How to choose


Here's a simple decision framework:


- *You want AI agents and automation built in:* Cosmic
- *You need maximum schema flexibility and have developer resources:* Sanity
- *Your editors need a visual preview before publishing:* Storyblok
- *You have GraphQL infrastructure or content federation needs:* Hygraph
- *You need self-hosting and data sovereignty:* Strapi
- *You're building slice-based marketing pages:* Prismic
- *Landing pages and A/B testing are your primary use case:* Builder.io


All seven platforms have free tiers or trials. The fastest way to validate fit is to spin up a project and build something real before committing.
