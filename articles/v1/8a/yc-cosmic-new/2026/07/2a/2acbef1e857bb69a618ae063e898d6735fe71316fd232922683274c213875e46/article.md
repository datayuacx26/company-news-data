# Cosmic Rundown: Romania's Land Registry Wiped, OpenCode Security Concerns, and EU Data Sharing

## Romania's Land Registry Database Wiped


A[security incident wiped Romania's entire land registry database](https://news.risky.biz/risky-bulletin-hacker-wipes-romanias-entire-land-registry-database/) , affecting property records across the country. The attack highlights the fragility of centralized government databases and the importance of robust backup strategies.


The[Hacker News discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48978605) dives into questions about backup procedures, why a single point of failure existed, and what this means for citizens whose property records may now be in question. Several commenters point out this is a wake-up call for government IT infrastructure worldwide.


## OpenCode Raises Security Alarms


A detailed writeup on[alarming issues with OpenCode](https://wren.wtf/shower-thoughts/stop-using-opencode/) is making rounds. The author documents concerning patterns in how the tool handles sensitive data and makes network requests.


The[HN thread](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48978112) has developers sharing their own discoveries and debating whether the issues are deal-breakers or acceptable tradeoffs. For teams evaluating AI coding tools, this is worth reading before committing to any particular solution.


## EU-US Data Sharing for Visa-Free Travel


European Digital Rights is raising concerns that[the EU is negotiating to share sensitive citizen data with the US](https://edri.org/our-work/the-eu-is-about-to-sell-our-most-sensitive-data-to-the-us-for-visa-free-travel/) as part of visa-free travel arrangements.


The[discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48977711) explores the privacy implications, what data would be shared, and whether this represents a fundamental shift in how the EU approaches citizen privacy protections. Developers building applications that handle EU user data should pay attention to how these negotiations evolve.


## $500k WordPress Exploit Found with $25 and GPT-5.6


A security researcher documented how they[found a WordPress remote code execution vulnerability](https://slcyber.io/research-center/exploit-brokers-pay-500000-for-a-wordpress-rce-i-found-one-with-gpt5-6/) using GPT-5.6 and minimal compute costs. Exploit brokers pay $500,000 for this class of vulnerability.


The[Hacker News thread](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48975665) debates whether AI-assisted security research democratizes vulnerability hunting or creates new risks. Either way, the economics of security research are shifting.


## China's Open-Weights AI Strategy


An analysis argues that[China's open-weights AI strategy is winning](https://werd.io/american-ai-is-locked-down-and-proprietary-its-losing/) against American proprietary approaches. The piece examines how open model releases from Chinese labs are gaining developer adoption globally.


The[conversation](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48979269) touches on geopolitical implications, developer preferences for open models, and whether this trend will influence US AI policy.

A piece on[frontier lab economics](https://www.emergingtrajectories.com/lh/frontier-lab-economics/) examines Kimi K3, Qwen 3.8, and what the author frames as Anthropic's potential challenges. The analysis looks at how rapidly evolving competition affects sustainability for AI labs.
