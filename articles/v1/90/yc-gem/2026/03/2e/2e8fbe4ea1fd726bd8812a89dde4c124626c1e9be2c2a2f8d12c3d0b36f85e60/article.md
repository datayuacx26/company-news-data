# What is an applicant tracking system? Definition, benefits & how it works

SJ Niderost


Posted on Mar 23


Applicant tracking systems are used by 98% of Fortune 500 companies and roughly 75% of organizations overall. If you've applied for a job online in the past decade, your application almost certainly went through an ATS.


But the ATS category has evolved dramatically from its origins as a simple database for storing resumes. Modern applicant tracking systems are AI-powered recruiting platforms that actively source candidates, intelligently screen applications, automate outreach, coordinate interviews, and surface data-driven insights—not just track applicants passively.


This guide explains what an applicant tracking system is, how it works across the recruiting lifecycle, the tangible benefits it delivers, essential features to evaluate, and how to choose the right system for your team.


## **What is ATS: An applicant tracking system?**


An applicant tracking system (ATS) is software that helps organizations manage the entire recruiting and hiring process digitally. Many organizations of all sizes and industries use applicant tracking systems to manage their job application process and hiring funnel efficiently. It serves as the central hub where job requisitions are created, positions are posted to job boards, applications are collected and screened, candidates are tracked through interview stages, and hiring decisions are documented.


For employers, an ATS functions as a recruiting management platform that centralizes candidate data, automates repetitive tasks, and provides visibility into hiring progress. For candidates, it’s the system that receives and processes their applications—parsing resumes, screening qualifications, and routing them to the appropriate recruiters.


Modern ATS platforms have expanded far beyond basic applicant tracking to include AI-powered sourcing that finds candidates before they apply, automated screening that evaluates fit intelligently, candidate relationship management (CRM) for nurturing talent pools, intelligent scheduling that coordinates interviews automatically, and analytics that turn recruiting data into actionable insights.


The evolution reflects a fundamental shift in how recruiting works: from passive databases that store applications to active platforms that help you find, evaluate, engage, and hire the best candidates. The first applicant tracking systems were developed in the late 1990s and were housed on premises, but today, applicant tracking systems are predominantly deployed in the cloud as SaaS solutions. ATS technology has evolved to support modern recruitment requirements, including virtual recruiting and mass recruiting, and the future of ATS will be in the cloud, leveraging third-party infrastructure and offering various infrastructure ATS options.


Note for candidates: To ensure your resume is read properly by ATS, use standard headings and include relevant keywords.


## **How does an applicant tracking system work?**


An applicant tracking system manages the complete recruiting workflow from job requisition through hire. Applicant tracking software accelerates the hiring process by streamlining recruitment and hiring tasks, making it easier for organizations to manage candidates efficiently. Here’s how it works at each stage:


### **1. Job requisition and posting**


The hiring process begins when a hiring manager submits a job requisition through the ATS. The system captures essential details: role requirements, compensation range, department, hiring manager, and job description.


Once approved, the ATS creates the job listing and automatically distributes it across multiple channels: your company’s career site, major job boards like Indeed and LinkedIn, niche industry job boards, social media platforms, and employee referral portals. Posting jobs to various career sites and job boards is streamlined through the ATS, making it easy to manage each job opening efficiently. This multi-channel distribution happens with one click rather than manual posting to each platform individually.


Modern ATS platforms also optimize job postings for search engines and mobile devices, ensuring candidates can find and apply to your roles easily, regardless of how they’re searching.


### **2. Application collection and resume parsing**


As candidates apply from different sources — your career site, job boards, employee referrals, or direct applications — the ATS collects everything into a single pipeline. The system is specifically designed to collect candidate information from all sources and organize it for efficient review. This eliminates the chaos of managing applications across email threads, spreadsheets, and multiple platforms.


Resume parsing is one of the ATS’s core functions. The system automatically extracts key information from resumes and applications—work experience, skills, education, certifications, contact information—and structures it into standardized fields. This allows recruiters to compare candidates side-by-side without manually reading every resume.


The ATS also deduplicates candidates who apply to multiple roles, maintaining a single candidate record with complete application history rather than creating separate profiles for each application.


### **3. Screening and ranking**


This is where traditional and modern ATS platforms diverge most significantly.


Traditional ATS screening relies on keyword matching and knockout questions. If a job description mentions “Python” and a resume contains that keyword, the candidate scores higher. If someone answers “no” to “Do you have 5+ years of experience?” they’re automatically rejected. This approach is rigid and often misses qualified candidates whose resumes use different terminology.


AI-first applicant tracking system screening evaluates candidates contextually rather than just matching keywords. The AI understands job requirements, interprets career trajectories, evaluates how past experience relates to role needs, and ranks applicants by actual fit rather than keyword density. Modern systems also consider complete candidate context—including past applications, interview feedback, and relationship history—to make smarter screening decisions. Candidate matching algorithms in the ATS help identify qualified applicants and filter out unqualified candidates, ensuring that only the most suitable candidates are advanced in the hiring process.


For high-volume roles receiving hundreds or thousands of applications, AI-powered screening can process what would take days of manual review in minutes, surfacing the best matches automatically. An ATS allows hiring managers and recruiters to cut through the volume of résumés received so they can focus their time on the most qualified applicants.


### **4. Interview management and scheduling**


Once candidates pass initial screening, the ATS coordinates the interview process. It manages interview scheduling across multiple interviewers and candidates, distributes evaluation criteria and scorecards to interview panels, tracks interview feedback in one centralized location, and maintains communication with candidates throughout the process. During the interview stage, the ATS supports hiring managers and recruiters by providing tools and information to prepare for and conduct interviews efficiently, while also streamlining scheduling interviews within the system.


Advanced ATS platforms include intelligent scheduling that automatically finds optimal interview times considering time zones, interviewer availability and preferences, interviewer load balancing to prevent burnout, and room or video conference availability. Candidates can often self-schedule from available time slots, eliminating the back-and-forth coordination that traditionally consumed hours of recruiter time.


### **5. Offer management and hiring**


When a hiring decision is made, the ATS handles offer creation with templated offer letters, approval workflows routing offers to appropriate stakeholders, e-signature integration for quick acceptance, and documentation of all hiring decisions for compliance.


Once an offer is accepted, the ATS hands off to onboarding systems, ensuring candidate information transfers seamlessly rather than requiring manual re-entry of data that already exists in the system. The ATS streamlines the onboarding process by providing new hires with all the information they need to start making an impact from day one, improving integration, productivity, and retention.


### **6. Analytics and reporting**


Throughout the recruiting process, the ATS tracks key metrics: time-to-fill (days from requisition to hire), cost-per-hire (recruiting expenses per position filled), source effectiveness (which channels produce the best hires), pipeline conversion rates (percentage advancing through each stage), offer acceptance rates, and candidate demographics for compliance reporting.


Modern platforms go beyond basic reporting to provide predictive analytics forecasting hiring timelines, benchmarking against industry standards, identifying bottlenecks in your recruiting process, and demonstrating recruiting ROI to leadership. This data transforms recruiting from an intuition-driven function to an evidence-based operation.


## **Benefits of using an applicant tracking system**


The right ATS delivers measurable improvements across multiple dimensions of recruiting effectiveness:


**Centralized candidate data eliminates chaos.** Without an ATS, candidate information lives in scattered email threads, individual recruiter spreadsheets, hiring manager notes, and disparate job board accounts. An ATS provides a single source of truth where everyone sees the same candidate information, complete application history, and current status. This centralization prevents candidates from falling through the cracks and ensures nothing is lost when recruiters change roles or leave the organization.


**Dramatically faster time-to-hire.** According to SHRM research, the average time-to-fill in 2023 was 44 days. Organizations using modern ATS platforms with AI-powered automation report 30-40% reductions in time-to-hire by automating application screening, eliminating scheduling coordination, and accelerating candidate communication. In fact, an ATS can reduce the average hiring cycle by as much as 60%. By moving from offline and paper-based processes to an automated digital process, an ATS accelerates the hiring process and streamlines recruitment and hiring tasks. In competitive talent markets, hiring speed often determines whether you land top candidates.


**Reduced cost-per-hire through automation.** Manual recruiting processes are expensive. Recruiters spending hours screening applications, coordinating interviews, and updating spreadsheets can’t focus on high-value activities like candidate relationships and strategic hiring. LinkedIn research shows that automation can reduce recruiting costs by 30-50% by handling repetitive tasks automatically, reducing reliance on expensive external recruiters and sourcing tools, and preventing mis-hires through better candidate evaluation.


**Improved candidate experience at scale.** Candidates notice when applications disappear into black holes or when scheduling takes weeks of back-and-forth emails. An ATS enables automated acknowledgment emails immediately after application, regular status updates as candidates progress through stages, self-service scheduling that respects candidate time, and fast, professional communication throughout the process. Even candidates who aren’t hired leave with a positive impression of your organization. ATS platforms also help organizations attract both passive and active candidates, building a robust talent pool for future hiring needs.


**Better compliance and audit trails.** Recruiting involves significant legal compliance requirements: EEOC documentation in the US, GDPR compliance in Europe, OFCCP regulations for federal contractors, and various state and local hiring laws. An ATS automatically maintains complete audit trails of all hiring decisions, stores candidate data in compliance with privacy regulations, documents evaluation criteria and interview feedback, and generates reports for regulatory requirements. This documentation protects organizations from discrimination claims and regulatory penalties. ATS platforms also support fair decision making by standardizing evaluation criteria and reducing bias throughout the hiring process.


**Data-driven hiring decisions replace gut instinct.** An ATS surfaces patterns that aren’t visible in manual processes: which sourcing channels produce the best hires, where candidates drop out of your pipeline, how long each hiring stage actually takes, which interviewers are most/least predictive of success, and whether your process introduces bias. These insights enable continuous improvement in recruiting effectiveness.


**Enhanced team collaboration reduces bias.** When interview feedback lives in individual email accounts or verbal conversations, it’s easy for bias to creep in. An ATS provides structured scorecards with consistent evaluation criteria, shared visibility into all candidate feedback, standardized interview questions across candidates, and documentation of why candidates were advanced or rejected. This structure helps hiring teams make more objective, defensible decisions. ATS platforms enable recruiting teams to collaborate efficiently, providing shared access to candidate data and integrated task management features that streamline recruiting workflows and improve overall efficiency.

Companies with a diverse workforce benefit from the diversity of ideas and perspectives, which can drive innovation and organizational strength. An ATS can support diversity and inclusion initiatives by helping reduce hiring bias and promoting fair evaluation practices.


## **Key features to look for in an ATS**


Not all applicant tracking systems offer the same capabilities. When selecting an ATS, it’s important to evaluate your organization’s specific needs and the capabilities of the applicant tracking software to ensure it supports your recruitment process effectively. Here are the essential features to evaluate:


**AI-powered sourcing and screening.** The most significant differentiator among modern ATS platforms is the depth of AI capabilities. Look for systems that offer contextual candidate evaluation rather than just keyword matching, intelligent application screening that considers complete candidate history, automated talent rediscovery that surfaces past candidates for new roles, and AI-powered sourcing across large candidate databases. Basic keyword matching is no longer sufficient when you’re competing for top talent.


**Resume parsing and candidate profiles.** The applicant tracking system should automatically extract information from resumes regardless of format, create structured candidate profiles for easy comparison, deduplicate candidates who apply to multiple roles, and enrich profiles with additional data from public sources.


**Multi-channel job posting and distribution.** Evaluate whether the system posts to major job boards automatically, integrates with your career site, distributes to social media channels, supports employee referral programs, and optimizes postings for search engines and mobile devices.


**Pipeline and workflow customization.** Your ATS should adapt to how your team works, not force you into rigid processes. Look for customizable hiring stages and workflows, configurable approval chains for different role types, automated actions triggered by candidate progression, and role-based permissions for different team members.


**Interview scheduling and coordination.** Advanced scheduling capabilities save enormous recruiter time: automated calendar coordination across multiple interviewers, self-scheduling links for candidates, time zone handling for distributed teams, interviewer load balancing, and automated reminders and confirmations.


**Candidate relationship management (CRM).** The best ATS platforms include CRM capabilities for managing candidates before they apply: talent pool segmentation and nurturing, automated engagement campaigns, sourcing pipeline management, and relationship tracking over time. This proactive capability is what distinguishes modern platforms from legacy systems that only handle active applicants.


**Analytics, reporting, and benchmarking.** Recruiting should be data-driven. Your ATS should provide pipeline metrics and conversion rates, source effectiveness analysis, time-to-hire and cost-per-hire tracking, diversity and inclusion reporting, predictive analytics for forecasting, and benchmarking against industry standards.


**Integration ecosystem.** Even the best ATS needs to connect with other systems. Evaluate integrations with your HRIS for employee data synchronization, calendar systems for scheduling, background check providers for seamless background checks, assessment platforms, video interviewing tools, and communication platforms. Native integrations are more reliable than third-party connectors.


**Compliance and data security.** Your ATS handles sensitive candidate data and must meet regulatory requirements: SOC2 and other security certifications, GDPR, CCPA, and other privacy compliance, EEOC and OFCCP reporting capabilities, data retention and deletion policies, and audit trails for hiring decisions.


Common ATS systems used by organizations today include Workday, Greenhouse, Lever, iCIMS, and BambooHR.


## **Implementation and integration of an ATS**


Implementing an applicant tracking system (ATS) is a strategic process that can transform the way organizations approach recruiting and hiring. The first step is to thoroughly assess your organization’s hiring needs and identify specific pain points in your current hiring process. Involving hiring managers and the hiring team early in the selection process ensures that the chosen ATS aligns with your unique requirements, from managing job descriptions to streamlining candidate communication.


Once you’ve selected the right applicant tracking system, it’s crucial to configure it to reflect your organization’s workflows. This includes setting up dynamic job descriptions, integrating with preferred job boards, and establishing consistent candidate communication templates. Seamless integration with other HR technologies—such as career websites and benefits administration platforms—creates a unified recruiting and hiring process, allowing candidate data to flow effortlessly between systems.


A well-implemented ATS empowers hiring managers and the hiring team to collaborate efficiently, track candidates at every stage, and make data-driven decisions. By centralizing all aspects of the hiring process, organizations can accelerate their hiring efforts, reduce time to hire, and deliver a superior candidate experience from application to onboarding.


## **Best practices for using an applicant tracking system**


To get the most value from your applicant tracking system, it’s essential to follow best practices that optimize both efficiency and candidate engagement. Start by defining clear hiring workflows within the ATS, ensuring that hiring managers and the hiring team are aligned on each stage of the hiring process. This clarity helps everyone understand their roles and responsibilities, reducing confusion and delays.


Leverage the ATS to post job openings across multiple job boards and career websites, maximizing your reach to potential candidates and building a diverse talent pool. Consistent communication and candidate engagement are key—use the ATS to automate updates, schedule interviews, and keep candidates informed throughout the process, enhancing their overall experience.


Effective candidate management means using the ATS to organize potential candidates, track their progress through the hiring pipeline, and analyze recruitment metrics. Regularly reviewing these metrics helps hiring teams make informed hiring decisions and continuously improve the recruiting process. Additionally, provide ongoing training for hiring managers and the hiring team to ensure everyone is comfortable with the ATS system and aware of new features or updates.


By following these best practices, organizations can streamline their recruiting efforts, attract top talent, and make the most of their investment in applicant tracking technology.


## **Traditional ATS vs. AI-first ATS: what's changed**


The applicant tracking system category has undergone a fundamental transformation that most organizations don’t fully appreciate. Understanding this evolution is critical to choosing the right platform.


**Traditional ATS (2000s-2010s)** functioned primarily as a system of record. These platforms focused on storing applications in a database instead of file cabinets, screening resumes through basic keyword matching, tracking candidates through predefined pipeline stages, documenting hiring decisions for compliance, and generating basic reports on recruiting activity.


The traditional ATS was passive—it waited for candidates to apply, then helped you manage them. It required manual effort at every stage: recruiters manually sourced candidates outside the system, manually reviewed every application, manually coordinated all scheduling, manually updated candidate status, and manually compiled data for reporting. The ATS simply provided structure for work that humans performed.


**Modern AI-first ATS (2020s+)** represents a category shift from systems of record to systems of action. These platforms don’t just track recruiting activity—they actively do the recruiting work.


AI agents in modern platforms proactively source candidates from databases of hundreds of millions of profiles, automatically screen applications based on contextual fit rather than keywords, personalize candidate outreach based on career background and history, coordinate interview scheduling across complex calendar constraints, rediscover past candidates who are matches for new roles, detect fraudulent applications and credentials, and surface insights that improve recruiting strategy over time.


The architectural difference is fundamental. Traditional ATS platforms were built before AI existed and have added AI features on top through integrations or recent updates. AI-first platforms built AI into their foundation, giving the AI access to complete candidate context—every interaction, application, interview, and relationship across your entire recruiting database. This complete context enables dramatically smarter automation. The incorporation of AI into ats software and applicant tracking software has helped recruiters improve their time-to-hire and quality-of-hire, making the hiring process more efficient and effective.


The practical impact is substantial. According to recent industry data, teams now handle 40% more job requisitions than in 2021, while applications per role have increased 2.7x. Traditional ATS platforms require proportionally more recruiters to handle this volume. AI-first platforms allow teams to manage increasing volume without adding headcount—because the AI handles work that previously required manual human effort.


The choice between a traditional and an AI-first applicant tracking system isn’t about features. It’s about whether your recruiting system assists you or actively does the work. As recruiting volumes continue to increase and talent competition intensifies, this distinction becomes the most important factor in ATS selection.

## **FAQ**


### **What does ATS stand for?**


ATS stands for Applicant Tracking System. It's software that manages the recruiting and hiring process digitally, from job posting through candidate tracking to final hire. The acronym ATS is used universally across the recruiting and HR industry to refer to these platforms.


### **Can candidates see if a company uses an ATS?**


Yes, candidates can usually tell when a company uses an ATS. The most obvious indicators are: applying through a branded application portal rather than email, receiving automated confirmation emails after applying, being asked to create a candidate profile or account, seeing structured application forms with specific fields rather than just uploading a resume, and receiving automated status updates as their application progresses.


Some job seekers try to “optimize” their resumes for ATS systems by using specific keywords or formatting. However, modern AI-first ATS platforms evaluate candidates contextually rather than just matching keywords, making traditional “ATS optimization” tactics less relevant than simply presenting your qualifications clearly and accurately. Still, it is important to use relevant keywords in your resume that match the job description and industry terms, as this helps ensure your application is properly read and considered by the ATS.


### **Do small businesses need an ATS?**


Small businesses benefit from an ATS once they're hiring more than 2-3 people per quarter or receiving more than 25-50 applications per role. Without an applicant tracking system, small teams struggle to keep track of candidates across email threads and spreadsheets, provide timely communication to applicants, maintain organized hiring processes as they grow, and document hiring decisions for compliance.


The key for small businesses is choosing an ATS that matches their current needs without overwhelming complexity. Entry-level platforms like Workable or JazzHR provide core applicant tracking functionality with fast setup and transparent pricing. As hiring volume grows, teams can graduate to more sophisticated platforms with AI, sourcing, and CRM capabilities.


Avoid enterprise ATS platforms designed for 1,000+ person companies if you're a 20-person startup—you'll pay for complexity you don't need and face implementation timelines that don't match your urgency.


### **How much does an applicant tracking system cost?**


Applicant tracking system pricing varies dramatically based on company size, feature set, and platform type. Entry-level systems start around $75-250/month for basic applicant tracking. Mid-market platforms range from $250-1,000+/month depending on active jobs and features. Enterprise systems typically charge $6,000-12,000+ per user annually, with implementation costs often adding $10,000-50,000+ in the first year.


However, focus on total cost of ownership rather than just the ATS license fee. A seemingly cheaper applicant tracking system that requires five additional tools for sourcing ($500-800/user/month), CRM, scheduling, engagement, and analytics often costs more than a comprehensive all-in-one platform. Factor in implementation costs, integration expenses, training time, ongoing support, and the productivity cost of managing multiple vendors.


Many teams discover that consolidating onto a comprehensive platform actually reduces their total recruiting technology spend by 30-50% while improving functionality.


### **What is the difference between an ATS and a CRM?**


An Applicant Tracking System (ATS) manages candidates who have already applied to your jobs—tracking them through interview stages, storing feedback, managing offers, and maintaining compliance documentation. The ATS is reactive, handling candidates who come to you.


A Candidate Relationship Management (CRM) system manages candidates you're proactively sourcing and nurturing before they apply. It's designed for building talent pipelines, running engagement campaigns, tracking outreach, and maintaining relationships with passive candidates over time. The CRM is proactive, helping you find and engage candidates.


Many modern recruiting platforms combine both capabilities in one system. This integration is valuable because candidates can move seamlessly from CRM (prospect) to ATS (applicant) without data loss, you can see complete relationship history when reviewing applications, past applicants can be re-engaged through CRM campaigns for new roles, and unified data enables better AI recommendations based on full candidate context.


Traditional standalone applicant tracking system platforms lack robust CRM capabilities, requiring teams to add separate tools. Modern all-in-one platforms include native CRM alongside ATS functionality, providing unified workflows from first candidate touch through hire.
