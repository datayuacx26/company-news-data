## The Science and Benefits of Mushrooms in Coffee


Health and wellness science has focused on adaptogenic and functional mushrooms for years, investigating potential health benefits of specific mushrooms like Lion's Mane, Chaga, Cordyceps, and more. Altogether, these types of mushrooms have shown promising results in terms of supporting immune health, helping people resist physiological and mental stress, and supporting longer-lasting mental clarity and focus. But when you're leveling up your morning routine to create practices that are exactly right for your health and goals, it helps to have a deeper understanding of the potential power of each mushroom.


### Benefits of Lion's Mane


Lion's Mane mushrooms are full of bioactive compounds. Researchers are conducting ongoing animal and human studies into these compounds to identify their benefits. Currently, scientists believe that Lion's Mane can have positive effects on gut health, the cardiovascular system, and the brain. Some of the most likely health benefits these mushrooms (and mushroom extracts) can offer may include:


- Hericenone and erinacine compounds that can stimulate brain cell growth
- Protective effects against aging and dementia
- Relief from some depressive and anxious feelings (but not as a medical substitute for pharmaceuticals)
- Better gut health by promoting healthy bacteria and stymying unhealthy bacteria
- Reducing cellular and stomach inflammation
- Helping to lower triglycerides and regulate metabolism
- Boosting the immune system
- Helping manage inflammation from oxidative stress


### Benefits of Chaga


Chaga mushrooms may offer similar health benefits, according to ongoing studies. Many people take Chaga mushroom extracts due to their potential anti-inflammatory properties through the mushroom's polyphenol content. Chaga mushrooms may also help boost the immune system due to the presence of beta-glucan polysaccharides, making it a great autumn boost. Scientists also see encouraging results in anti-cancer studies, which indicate that Chaga mushrooms might help white blood cells fight against cancer.


### Benefits of Cordyceps


Cordyceps are rich in nutrients and micro compounds that may offer health benefits. The mushroom has been popularly used in traditional medicine in China, and modern scientific studies indicate its high concentration of polyphenols can prevent cellular damage, and its adaptogenic properties can further reduce cellular stress and aging, especially for skin cells. Additional research offers promising results for managing blood sugar levels.


### Benefits of Maitake


Maitake is a known source of Vitamin D, making it a nutritious source of flavor and richness. Scientists are also uncovering additional potential benefits, such as:


- High concentrations of beta-glucans that may aid in cancer prevention and strengthen immune system health, as well as support a healthier heart.
- SX-fraction polysaccharides that may help with diabetes management.
- Better arterial function.


All combined, extracts from these adaptogenic mushrooms may help boost your immune system, reduce chronic conditions, and help promote heart health. Scientists are still conducting studies into the specific health benefits, and this selection of mushrooms is particularly promising.


## Why Add Protein? The Latte That Does More


Most morning routines include a nod to macronutrients, not just micronutrients. Depending on your goals, you might be looking for routines and rituals that help you manage your calories, increase muscle growth, or maintain satiety and energy throughout the whole morning.


Protein is the key to all of these goals and more. There are many different formulas people use to calculate their ideal protein intake, with fitness-focused audiences preferring a higher intake. The Mayo Clinic stands by the calculation of 0.8 grams per kilogram of body weight per day, or around 60 grams for someone who weighs 165 pounds.


This gets tricky if you don't like heavy breakfasts or breakfasts at all. But adding a light plant-based protein powder to your morning coffee is a great way to get 10 grams of protein—nearly 20% of your daily recommended amount if you're 165 pounds—in a cup. This early morning protein is more than a great start to your daily goal. It can provide a slight metabolic boost to start your day off right, and well-timed doses of protein can help support muscle growth and maintenance (especially if you pair your protein coffee with a morning workout).


Many people also find that protein powder helps with satiety and appetite management. While many breakfast foods (like donuts, cereals, and fruit smoothies) can make you temporarily full and then ravenously hungry in just a couple of hours, protein can help you feel full for hours, so you can eat more intentionally and say goodbye to hunger pangs in your first hours at work.
