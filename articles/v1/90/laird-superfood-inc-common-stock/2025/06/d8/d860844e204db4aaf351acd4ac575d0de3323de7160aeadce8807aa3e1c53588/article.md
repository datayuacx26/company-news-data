## What Makes Coffee a Superfood?


There is no official FDA definition of what a superfood is or the numerous health benefits it might provide. Merriam-Webster defines it like this:


"A food (such as salmon, broccoli, or blueberries) that is rich in compounds (such as antioxidants, fiber, or fatty acids) considered beneficial to a person's health."


Working with this understanding of what a superfood is can help you categorize potentially healthy foods as authentic superfoods and foods that might just be caught up in the superfood marketing craze. For example, wine enthusiasts regularly contend that red wine might just be a superfood—but its potential health drawbacks stop it from making the cut. Similarly, a lot of green juices might be packed with vitamins but stripped of all their fiber. So it's important to take care whenever you encounter a 'superfood' label.


Coffee is often characterized as a superfood, and we think it fits that definition to a T. Coffee beans are chock-full of antioxidants, which neutralize free radicals and play a role in protecting cells and DNA.


Blending coffee with other highly nutritious foods can make it even more powerful. Functional mushrooms such as Chaga, Lion's Mane, Maitake, and Cordyceps include nutritious compounds that can support better cognition, improve energy without the jitters or crashes of too much coffee, and provide smoother mental clarity. It's becoming more and more popular for people who want to support their health to add functional mushrooms to their diet through a mushroom coffee blend.

## Addressing Common Concerns and Misconceptions


With all the buzz about caffeine and mushrooms, the Internet is full of a lot of misconceptions. As you shop around for your new favorite coffee, here are some misconceptions to avoid:


- Functional mushrooms like Chaga, Lion's Mane, and Cordyceps are not psychedelic mushrooms, and they are not known to have psychedelic effects.
- Know what level of caffeine you're comfortable with. Most functional mushroom coffee blends have approximately half the caffeine of traditional coffee, allowing the energizing and cognitive benefits of functional mushrooms to shine through. But some brands use very caffeinated coffee grounds to increase the caffeine. Take the time to research your products before you drink more than your usual cup, especially if you're sensitive to caffeine.
- Mushrooms aren't medicine. While the science behind functional mushrooms when it comes to chronic inflammation and mood is becoming more robust, that doesn't mean mushrooms can replace medications. Talk to your physician about your unique health and wellness concerns and the role superfoods can play in your health journey.
