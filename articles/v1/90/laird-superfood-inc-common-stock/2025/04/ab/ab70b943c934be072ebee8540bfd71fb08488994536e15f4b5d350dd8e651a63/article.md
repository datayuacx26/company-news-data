## The Importance of Digestive Enzymes To Support a Healthy Digestive System


As we age, our digestive system undergoes significant changes that impact how our body absorbs nutrients. As a result, maintaining a healthy gut and digestive system is essential to support your well-being.


Part of that process includes lifestyle changes. Smaller, more frequent meals can help with digestion, as can a regular exercise routine and weight management. But what you put into your body also matters, particularly as it relates to digestive enzymes.


Digestive enzymes are proteins made by your body to break down food and maximize the nutrients you can get from that food. They live in your mouth, stomach, small intestine, and pancreas. In fact, Johns Hopkins Medicine describes the pancreas as the "powerhouse" for the entire digestive system. This is where carbohydrates, proteins, and fats are broken down, and their impact is maximized.


The most common digestive enzymes include:


- Amylase, which breaks down carbohydrates and lives in your mouth and pancreas
- Lipase, also at home in the pancreas, which works on breaking down fats
- Protease, the third enzyme in the pancreas, typically breaks down proteins


Meanwhile, lactase and sucrase are digestive enzymes that live in the small intestine, breaking down lactose and sucrose sugar. All of them work in concert, focusing on different foods you consume to get the maximum nutrients possible from them.
