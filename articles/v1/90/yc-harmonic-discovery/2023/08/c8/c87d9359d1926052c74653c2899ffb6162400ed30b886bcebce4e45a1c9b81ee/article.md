In the world of drug discovery and molecular research, virtual screening of compound libraries using protein-ligand docking has emerged as a pivotal technique. This approach allows scientists to navigate the vast chemical space, identifying potential hits for further exploration. Notably, the quality of these hits is closely tied to the scale of libraries screened. With the advent of ultra-large chemical libraries like ZINC 20 and Enamine REAL, *the sheer volume of data generated by docking experiments has escalated rapidly* .


At Harmonic Discovery we use docking in tandem with our machine learning approaches to hypothesize how molecules may or may not bind to specific polypharmacological profiles. Docking performance is highly influenced by several parameters such as the software used, the structure of the receptor, how the chemical library was prepared, or the presence of water molecules in the binding pocket. We noticed that there was a significant gap in tools for secure storage and handling of docking parameters and results which impact the reproducibility of docking experiments.


We needed a tool to ensure the reproducibility of docking experiments and thus DockDB was born. DockDB serves as an all-inclusive Relational Database Management System (RDBMS), offering researchers a robust computational infrastructure to establish, access, and manage dedicated docking studies.


**DockDB: A Comprehensive Framework for Large Scale Docking Studies**

The typical files generated for a docking experiment


Upwards of thousands of files can be generated during the course of a single docking run. Even on established docking websites like DUD-E, it’s often complicated to navigate the deluge of file types, parameters and results that relate to any given experiment. When studying the polypharmacology of drugs (as we do here at Harmonic Discovery) this problem grows *exponentially* .


Press enter or click to view image in full size


The foundation of DockDB is defined by a comprehensive database schema that encompasses essential fields and tables pertinent to docking experiments. These tables store information and relations between the parameters, structures, and result files used throughout the docking process as well as timestamps them, bringing clarity to docking experiments.


A notable feature of DockDB is its compatibility with any docking software, such as SMINA, DOCK3, OpenEye FRED or Molecular Operating Environment (MOE). Additionally, DockDB is flexible enough to be hosted either locally or on cloud platforms such as Google Cloud, AWS and Azure, featuring a database version management system to facilitate controlled schema modifications. User management with DockDB is also streamlined, with predefined roles and groups ensuring supervised access. Additionally, we include a standalone software package that empowers users to seamlessly input, modify, extract and analyze data from DockDB.

**In just a few lines of code, you can upload, handle, and evaluate large-scale docking results**


Press enter or click to view image in full size


DockDB can be accessed by code in four key steps:


- Connecting to the database
- Define the search query
- Submit the query to DockDB
- Process and analyze


In a real-world example we wanted to determine the best crystal structure to use for screening a chemical scaffold series against a kinase called BTK. BTK has over a hundred publicly available crystal structures to download. We used DockDB to automate the storage, extraction, and analysis of the SMINA docking results. In *just 6 lines of code* , we were able to compare all BTK crystal structures in terms of enrichment for known actives versus decoys and determine the best BTK structure for our drug discovery project.


**Making DockDB Available to Everyone**


With its comprehensive features, seamless integration with diverse docking software, and robust data management capabilities, we think that DockDB addresses a critical need in the field. At Harmonic Discovery we’ve been using DockDB for quite some time now and we think that the broader community would find our tool useful!
