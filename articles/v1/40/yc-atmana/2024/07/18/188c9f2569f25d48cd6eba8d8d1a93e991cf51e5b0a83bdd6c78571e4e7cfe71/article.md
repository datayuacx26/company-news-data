# What is the Difference Between Circumcised and Uncircumcised?

Wondering what the difference is between Circumcised and Uncircumcised. Well, this is a common point of confusion amongst many.

## **Circumcised vs. Uncircumcised: What is the Difference?**


The main difference between a circumcised (cut) and an uncircumcised (uncut) penis is the absence (or presence) of a foreskin.


A circumcised penis basically has had its foreskin removed, revealing the head (glans). At the same time, an uncircumcised penis has its foreskin (as a sleeve of skin) covering the glans. This difference brings about certain changes affecting hygiene, sensitivity, and personal preference.


We will learn more as we move deeper into the article.


## **How Common is Circumcision?**


Well, to understand how common circumcision is, let’s look at some data:


- According to the[UNAIDS](https://www.unaids.org/en/resources/presscentre/featurestories/2007/february/20070226mcpt1) , about 30% of males worldwide—roughly 670 million people—are circumcised.
- A 2016[research](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4772313/) estimated this figure to be between 37% and 39%, with the United States having a higher rate of 71.2%.
- Moreover, the highest circumcision rates are found in the Middle East, South Korea, and the U.S., while it is less common in Europe, Latin America, and much of Asia.
- According to the[CDC reports](https://www.cdc.gov/nchs/data/hestat/circumcision_2013/Circumcision_2013.htm) , circumcision rates among newborn males in the U.S. declined by 10% between 1979 and 2010.


## **Understanding the Differences Between Circumcised vs. Uncircumcised**


If you want to get a better insight into Circumcised vs. Uncircumcised, here are some aspects to consider:


### **1. Size**


To begin with, one of the most common doubts when it comes to circumcised vs. uncircumcised penis is about the size. It’s pretty common to wonder if circumcision affects penis size?


Well, here is what you need to know:


**Circumcised:**


Circumcision does not affect the size of the penis.


The size of penis primarily depends on genetics and blood flow to the penile tissues. Removing the foreskin does not change the size of the erect penis.


While it may appear slightly less bulky when flaccid, the overall size during erection remains unaffected by circumcision.


**Uncircumcised:**


On the other hand, an uncircumcised penis might seem bulkier when flaccid due to the presence of the foreskin. However, once erect, the foreskin retracts, and the penis appears similar in size to a circumcised one.


Basically, removing a foreskin doesn’t affect the other penile tissues or the size of your penis when erect.


**The only difference between circumcised vs. uncircumcised penis in terms of size is that a circumcised penis may appear slightly less “bulky” when flaccid. That is, the difference in appearance is mainly noticeable when the penis is not erect.**


### **2. Overall Appearance**


**Circumcised:**


As you know, a circumcised penis has the foreskin removed, so this leaves the head (glans) exposed at all times, whether flaccid or erect.


This, in turn, can make the skin around the head appear tougher and thicker. Moreover, the glans itself is always visible and may have a slightly different texture compared to the rest of the penis.


**Uncircumcised:**


An uncircumcised penis has the foreskin covering the glans when soft. This foreskin can be retracted, especially during urination and erection, revealing the head of the penis. The appearance changes as the foreskin pulls back, often looking bunched up.


**Overall, when it comes to appearance, the primary difference is the constant exposure of the glans in circumcised penises versus the varying exposure in uncircumcised ones, where the foreskin can cover or reveal the head.**


### **3. Sensation**


**Circumcised:**


Well, men who are circumcised may experience different sensations compared to those who are not. However, this is still debatable.


Some[studies](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21672947/) have suggested that circumcision might lead to more orgasm difficulties. On the other hand, other[research](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3383191/) has found no direct link between being circumcised and overall sexual satisfaction.


**Uncircumcised:**


For uncircumcised men, the[foreskin plays a significant role](https://www.auajournals.org/article/S0022-5347(15)05535-4/abstract) in sensation. The foreskin is particularly sensitive to touch, and its movement can enhance sexual pleasure by stimulating the head of the penis. Additionally, the mucous membranes in the foreskin can produce mucus that increases sensitivity.


**Basically, sensation is a personal experience and varies widely among different people. While circumcision might alter specific aspects of penile sensitivity, it doesn’t necessarily reduce overall sexual pleasure.**


### **4. Sexual Performance**


**Circumcised:**


Well, circumcision does not negatively impact sexual pleasure or function. Many men do not notice any change in sensitivity after the procedure.


Moreover, some people have also reported enhanced pleasure and improved control over ejaculation, potentially reducing the risk of premature ejaculation. This improvement can lead to longer-lasting, more satisfying sexual experiences for both partners.


**Uncircumcised:**


Uncircumcised men typically do not experience changes in sexual performance due to the natural state of the foreskin. Some studies suggest they may have a lower risk of premature ejaculation, though the evidence is mixed.


**Overall, sexual function and pleasure remain largely unaffected by the presence or absence of the foreskin.**


### **5. Fertility**


**Circumcised:**


Circsuon shows no impact on Fertility. As sperm production primarily takes place in the testicles and not the penis, the fertility remains unaffected.


Moreover, factors like diet, lifestyle, and overall health play a more significant role in male fertility.


**Uncircumcised:**


Similarly, being uncircumcised does not directly impact fertility. As the foreskin plays no role in sperm production, so fertility is unaffected by the presence of the foreskin.


As mentioned above, the key factors for fertility are a person’s health, lifestyle choices, and nutritional habits. Maintaining good overall health is important for optimal reproductive function.


**Therefore, the presence or absence of the foreskin doesn’t show any effects on fertility.**


### **6. Hygiene**


**Circumcised:**


When it comes to hygiene, a circumcised penis generally requires less maintenance. Regular washing during your bath or shower is usually sufficient to keep it clean and healthy.


However, without the protective foreskin, the penile skin may be more prone to dryness or irritation. Therefore, it is advisable to wear loose-fitting underwear and avoid tight clothing to prevent discomfort.


**Uncircumcised:**


Well, an uncircumcised penis requires a bit more attention to hygiene. Cleaning beneath the foreskin is important to prevent the buildup of smegma—a mixture of dead skin cells, oils, and bacteria.


In case of not maintaining proper hygine it can lead to unpleasant odors and potential issues like balanitis or phimosis.


**Therefore, while circumcised, the penis generally has less to worry about regarding hygiene; regular and gentle cleaning under the foreskin is important for uncircumcised men to avoid potential issues like smegma buildup and related complications.**


### **7. Risk of infection and STIs**


**Circumcised:**


Circumcised men generally have a lower risk of contracting certain infections and STIs. According to[research](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15890696/) , circumcision reduces the likelihood of urinary tract infections (UTIs), particularly in infancy, as it prevents the buildup of smegma, which can lead to the growth of bacteria.


Additionally, circumcised men are significantly less likely to contract HIV from heterosexual intercourse, with a[reduction in risk by 50-60%](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5478224/) . The risk of other STIs, such as herpes and HPV, is also lower in circumcised men.


**Uncircumcised:**


Uncircumcised men may be at higher risk for infections and certain STIs. This is because the foreskin can trap pathogens and urine, which increases the chances of developing UTIs.


Infections like phimosis and balanitis are also more common if proper hygiene isn’t maintained.


Furthermore,[studies](https://reproductive-health-journal.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/1742-4755-11-25#:~:text=HIV%20prevalence%20was%20significantly%20lower,%25%20(p%20%3C%200.001).) suggest uncircumcised men have a higher risk of contracting HIV and other STIs, such as herpes, compared to their circumcised counterparts. Bascaiily proper hygiene is important for uncircumcised men to reduce these risks.


**Therefore, while both circumcised and uncircumcised men should practice safe sex and maintain proper hygiene, circumcision appears to offer some protection against certain infections and STIs.**


### **8. Lubrication**


**Circumcised:**


In the case of a circumcised penis, the absence of the foreskin means natural lubrication is reduced. While this usually doesn’t impact sexual satisfaction, it may require extra lubrication in specific situations, like during anal sex, to maintain comfort and pleasure.


**Uncircumcised:**


Uncircumcised men benefit from the natural lubrication provided by the foreskin, which can enhance sexual experiences without needing additional lube. This natural moisture can help maintain comfort during various sexual activities.


**Moreover, circumcised men might need extra lube for certain activities, whereas uncircumcised men typically rely on their natural lubrication.**


### **9. Penile Cancer and other problems**


**Circumcised:**


Circumcised men are generally[less prone](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/langlo/article/PIIS2214-109X(17)30386-8/fulltext) to penile cancer. Research indicates that circumcision during infancy significantly reduces this risk. Additionally, circumcised men are less likely to experience issues such as phimosis, paraphimosis, and balanitis, which can lead to severe complications like obstructed blood flow and tissue damage. Maintaining proper hygiene is also easier, which further lowers the risk of infections and related problems.


**Uncircumcised:**


Uncircumcised men have a[higher chance](https://www.cancer.org/cancer/types/penile-cancer/causes-risks-prevention/prevention.html) of developing penile cancer. Moreover, the presence of smegma, phimosis, and higher susceptibility to infections are primarily responsible here, contributing factors to an increased risk of penile cancer.


Good hygiene practices are crucial to minimize these risks.


**Therefore, circumcised men generally face fewer penile health issues and a lower risk of penile cancer compared to their uncircumcised men.**


## **The Bottom Line**


In the end, choosing between circumcision and remaining uncircumcised is a personal decision. Circumcision offers several health benefits, such as reducing the risk of certain infections and conditions. However, maintaining a healthy uncircumcised penis requires more diligent hygiene practices to prevent issues.


Moreover, if you are experiencing problems or want to minimize the risk of future complications, it is best to consult a specialist. They can provide personalized advice and, if needed, recommend circumcision as a preventive measure. Your health and comfort should guide this choice; professional guidance can help ensure the best outcome.


## **Difference Between Circumcised and Uncircumcised: FAQs**


### **Q1. What is the right age to get circumcised?**


The right age to get circumcised varies based on cultural, religious, and personal factors. It can be performed at any age, from infancy to adulthood. However, it’s best to consult with healthcare providers to understand the best timing for your situation.


### **Q2. Can you get circumcised as an adult?**


Yes, adult circumcision is possible and is performed for various reasons, including medical, cultural, or personal choice. It’s a simple procedure that can be discussed with a healthcare provider to address any concerns or questions you may have.


### **Q3. Are there medical reasons for circumcision?**


Yes, circumcision can be recommended for medical reasons such as to treat conditions like phimosis (tight foreskin), recurrent infections, or certain urinary problems.
