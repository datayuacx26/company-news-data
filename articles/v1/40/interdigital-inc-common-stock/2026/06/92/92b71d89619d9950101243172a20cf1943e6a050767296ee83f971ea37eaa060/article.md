# Haptics Hold the Key for Greater Accessibility, Immersivity in Live Sports

# Haptics Hold the Key for Greater Accessibility, Immersivity in Live Sports


June 2026


Philippe Guillotel


[Video](https://www.interdigital.com/blog?tags=Video)[Haptics](https://www.interdigital.com/blog?tags=Haptics)


This year, the World Cup will arrive in North America with unprecedented scale. Stadiums across the United States, Canada, and Mexico are expected to host millions of fans in person, while billions more will watch the action globally. Data shows that more than 5 billion people engaged with the 2022 tournament, including[2.7 billion fans watching via streaming platforms](https://inside.fifa.com/tournament-organisation/audience-reports/qatar-2022) – a number that is only expected to rise as digital viewing becomes the dominant mode for live sports consumption.


In addition to being[the most attended](https://www.beinsports.com/en-us/soccer/fifa-world-cup-2026/articles/fifa-world-cup-2026-set-to-break-the-all-time-attendance-record-2026-03-26) , this World Cup will be the most streamed, most personalized, and potentially the most immersive in history.


For decades, the evolution of sports broadcasting has been defined by improvements in sight and sound: higher resolution video, better color fidelity, the addition of spatial audio. But a new dimension that has the potential to fundamentally reshape how fans experience the game is emerging: touch.


## Haptics for Accessibility


[Haptics](https://www.interdigital.com/post/revealing-the-magic-of-haptics) , the science of transmitting information through tactile sensations, is rapidly moving from niche applications into the mainstream of media and entertainment. What began as a tool for accessibility is now poised to redefine immersion for all viewers.


The implications are particularly profound for the estimated 430 million people worldwide who are deaf or hard of hearing,[according to the World Health Organization](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/deafness-and-hearing-loss) . For this community of sports fans, traditional broadcasts have always been incomplete – missing the roar of the crowd, the subtle cues of momentum, and the emotional cadence of a match. Thankfully, we’re already seeing early, powerful examples of haptics transforming the viewers’ experiences in live sports environments.


In a stadium pilot deployed by English Premier League team Newcastle United FC, the team[rolled out a program](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MC2C44ktofw&list=PLb39HY4ZwBVkAsowfS0QPnDaNz9A5yZzm&index=4) designed to help deaf and hard of hearing fans experience a[more immersive and inclusive game environment](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/cer39xj4wrro) while at the stadium. The program piloted the use of[haptic-enabled jerseys](https://www.htworld.co.uk/news/wearables/world-first-haptic-shirts-help-deaf-fans-feel-statium-atmosphere/) embedded with vibrotactile sensors that equip them to translate crowd noise and on-field action into synchronized physical sensations. Microphones capture the intensity of the stadium atmosphere, which is then encoded into haptic signals that allow deaf and hard-of-hearing fans to feel the swell of the crowd, the rhythm of play, and the emotional peaks of the match in real time.


## Haptics for All


This accessibility breakthrough reveals an even more exciting potential: a fundamentally new layer of experience and immersivity that benefits everyone.


Imagine watching a decisive penalty kick and sensing the tension through subtle vibrations or experiencing the excitement of a scored goal as a wave of tactile feedback that mirrors the energy of the stadium itself. This potential is rapidly advancing, and at InterDigital, we have spent years advancing the foundational technologies and shaping the media standards that make this technology possible at scale.


InterDigital was among the pioneering companies selected by MPEG, the global standards body for video and media, to shape the industry’s first international standard for the creation, encodement, and delivery of haptic media. With years of foundational research and expertise, our engineers contributed to the architecture that enables tactile signals like vibration, force, and motion to be encoded and delivered alongside audio and video in a unified stream.


This milestone was solidified when MPEG published the first haptics standard in January 2025. This new standards framework began to classify haptics as a first-order media type and helped to create a common language to communicate touch interoperability across devices, platforms, and global markets.


Standards are what transform innovation into infrastructure. Infrastructure is what enables scale – delivering added accessibility to audiences in need and added layers of immersivity for all audiences.


## Realizing the Future


To realize this future, InterDigital collaborates across industry. In February, InterDigital partnered with gaming pioneer Razer to establish the[Haptics Excellence Center](https://ir.interdigital.com/news-events/press-releases/news-details/2026/InterDigital-and-Razer-Launch-Haptic-Excellence-Center/default.aspx) and accelerate experimentation, commercialization, and developer engagement around haptic-enabled experiences. By bringing together leaders in hardware, software, and content creation, our initiative aims to help translate technical capabilities into real-world applications, from gaming and immersive media to live sports streaming.


Collaboration is crucial, as devices – from wearables and gaming peripherals to connected home systems – rapidly evolve to support richer haptic experiences. By aligning innovation with standards and fostering cross-industry partnerships, we can ensure that these capabilities scale seamlessly across the global media ecosystem.


## The Time is Now


The timing of this development could not be more significant. As streaming becomes the primary gateway to live sports, the integration of haptic data into broadcast workflows opens the door to entirely new ways to experience the action.


Living rooms can become extensions of the stadium. Sofas, handheld devices, and wearables can synchronize with live feeds, delivering a layered sensory experience that bridges the physical distance between fan and field.


For broadcasters, this represents a new creative canvas, and for technology companies, haptics represent a new frontier of innovation. But fans win most, with a deeper, more human way to experience the moments that matter.


The World Cup and other global sporting events like it are special because they create a shared experience that transcends geography, language, and culture – and even the limits of our senses.


I’m truly excited for the days when our innovation and standards will ensure fans not only watch the game, but feel it.
