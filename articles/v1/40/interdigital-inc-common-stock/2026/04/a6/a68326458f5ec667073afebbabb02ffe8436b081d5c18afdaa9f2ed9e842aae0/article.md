# The Technology Behind The Game

# The Technology Behind The Game


Behind every streamed match is a foundation of technology and intellectual property that makes global, real-time sport possible—often unseen, but essential


April 2026


Liren Chen, President and CEO, InterDigital


[HDR](https://www.interdigital.com/blog?tags=HDR)[Video](https://www.interdigital.com/blog?tags=Video)[World IP Day](https://www.interdigital.com/blog?tags=World%20IP%20Day)


Sport is one of the few things that still brings people together wherever they are. Whether it's the Super Bowl, the World Cup, or Wimbledon, hundreds of millions of people share the same experience at the same moment. This year, the World Intellectual Property Organization has chosen sport as the theme for World IP Day, and it's an obvious choice. Sport and intellectual property are closely intertwined.


The Nike swoosh, the Olympic rings, the NFL logo; these are among the most recognized trademarks in the world, and the legal protections that sit behind them are part of what makes them valuable. Clubs, leagues, and governing bodies build businesses on the back of their IP rights. Athletes themselves can become brands. Broadcasting deals are built on exclusivity, which is itself a form of IP protection. Without it, there would be far less investment in sport at every level.


But there is another layer of IP in sport that is perhaps less understood, and it may be the most important of all: the patented and widely adopted technology that determines how we actually watch the game.


For most fans today, experiencing sport means a screen. It might be a 75-inch television in a living room, a phone on a commuter train, or a tablet propped up on a kitchen counter. Whatever the device, the experience depends on decades of foundational research in wireless technology to help you connect, and in video technology that brings the action to life. It’s research that must happen long before any broadcaster can deliver a picture, and long before any fan presses play.


At InterDigital, that research is what we do. Our Video Lab has been working for decades on the core technologies that underpin how video is compressed, processed, delivered, and presented in the highest quality.


Take video compression. When a camera captures a football match or a tennis final, it generates a vast amount of data. Without compression, it would be impossible to transmit that footage; certainly not at the speed and quality that a live broadcast demands. The High Efficiency Video Coding standard, better known as HEVC or H.265, roughly doubles the compression efficiency of its predecessor, which means broadcasters can deliver the same quality picture at half the data rate, or a far superior picture at the same rate.


As with many standardized technologies, each new codec builds on previous generations of video compression. InterDigital engineers are a key part of the HEVC story, as they have contributed to the development of every major codec over the last 30 years. Our engineers helped develop these advances in video compression. They helped guide the standards development process from key leadership positions. And the results speak for themselves – HEVC is now the prevalent standard for 4K and Ultra HD content worldwide.


Beyond compression, there is the question of how a picture looks. High dynamic range, or HDR, is the technology that makes a sport broadcast feel vivid and real — the difference between a flat image and one where you can see the texture of the grass, the bright floodlights against a dark sky, and the full range of color in a crowd of 80,000 fans. InterDigital has been a leading contributor to technologies underpinning HDR standards, developing the technology that allows that visual richness to be encoded, transmitted, and rendered faithfully on screens of every kind.


This work matters even more now because of how fundamentally the sports viewing landscape has changed. Live sport used to mean a broadcast signal, a cable subscription, and a television. Today, it increasingly means a streaming platform. Amazon Prime Video has the rights to NFL games and the UEFA Champions League. Apple TV+ has Major League Soccer. Netflix has also entered the live sports market. Last year, Disney launched a new ESPN streaming offering. This shift has happened fast, and it has happened because the underlying technology — the compression, the delivery infrastructure, the picture quality standards — has been developed to the point where streaming live sport at broadcast quality is now reliable and scalable.


None of that would be possible without the foundational research that companies like InterDigital have invested in over decades. Today’s streaming platforms did not have to invent their own video compression algorithms or develop their own HDR techniques. They benefit from an open, collaborative system of global technology development and standardization that distills the best ideas from the best engineers around the world and puts them to work for everybody.


World IP Day is a reminder that intellectual property is not an abstraction. IP, in whatever form, connects investment in ideas with the benefits that flow from those ideas. For sport, that system stretches from the trademark on a club badge all the way to the patents that ensure a penalty shootout streams flawlessly to a billion screens. Both matter. And both deserve to be protected.
