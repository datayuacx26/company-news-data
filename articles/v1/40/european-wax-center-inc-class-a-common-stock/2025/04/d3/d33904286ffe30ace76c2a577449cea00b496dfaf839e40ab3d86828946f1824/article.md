# Expert Tips to Help Prevent Razor Bumps

Razor bumps are an unwanted side effect that almost anyone who shaves has experienced. Small, inflamed, and often irritating, these bumps can throw off your skin’s smoothness and your confidence. They happen for two main reasons: 1) Hair struggles to grow back properly, often curling back into the skin instead of growing outward. 2) Skin becomes irritated after repeated contact with a blade and breaks out in a bumpy rash. Either way, the result is redness, irritation, and a frustrating cycle of bumps that just won’t quit.

Most people think razor bumps form

## **A Sharp Razor Makes All the Difference**


Dull blades don’t just give a poor shave—they tug at the skin, causing microtears that invite irritation, bacteria, and bumps. The sharper the blade, the cleaner the cut, reducing the risk of hair getting trapped beneath the surface. Multi-blade razors provide a smoother glide, but they should be replaced regularly—after five to seven uses—to help prevent buildup and bacteria.


## **Shaving Smarter, Not Harder**


Technique matters. While shaving against the grain might seem like the fastest way to smooth skin, it’s actually one of the biggest culprits behind razor bumps. Going against the direction of hair growth tugs at the hair, causing it to snap back beneath the skin instead of growing outward. This can lead to irritation and painful, inflamed bumps.


Instead, shaving


with


the grain minimizes friction and allows hair to grow back at a more natural angle. Using short, gentle strokes—not pressing down too hard—further prevents unnecessary irritation.


## **Hydration: The Secret to Smoother Skin**


Dry skin is more prone to razor bumps because it creates a rougher surface for shaving and increases friction. Hydrated skin, on the other hand, allows for a closer, smoother shave with less irritation. A good moisturizer helps soften hair, making it easier to cut without pulling. Keeping skin hydrated with daily moisturization strengthens its natural barrier, making it more likely to withstand potential irritation caused by shaving.
