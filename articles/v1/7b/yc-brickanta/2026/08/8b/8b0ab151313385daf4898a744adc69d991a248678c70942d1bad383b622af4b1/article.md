# AI-driven knowledge transfer in large companies and company groups

#### Insight


Most large contractors already have AI in the house. It sits in an estimating app at one subsidiary, a document tool at another, and in a handful of free accounts nobody in IT knows about. Each tool may work fine on its own task, but together they make nothing.


For a group with thousands of employees and dozens of companies, that is an expensive road to pursue. An app can solve one specific step for a department, but an AI capability makes the whole group better, and it rests on three things no single app can own: shared data, shared security and shared knowledge.


### Tools that don't collaborate


Fragmentation actually costs more than the licences. The estimator at one company uses one tool to read tender documents. The lawyer at another uses something else for contract review. Procurement has a third. None of them sees what the others have found, and none of them leaves behind anything the next project can use.


Underneath, so-called *shadow AI* is running. Sensitive bid documents get pasted into private chat accounts because it is faster than waiting for an internal solution. That risk doesn’t show up in any system map, because it was never procured.


### The group's knowledge is locked inside each company


A company that has submitted thousands of bids already holds the answer to most of what turns up in the next tender package. Somewhere in the group, someone has priced the exact boundary between two trades that nobody wants to take on. Someone else lost money on how that particular client handles change orders in its standard contract. An estimator in the north won a bid with a design solution that would work just as well in the south.


The problem is that they don't know about each other. The knowledge sits in employee's heads, in project folders only one department opens, and in experience that was never written down. When someone leaves, it walks out with them.


A shared knowledge base turns past projects into reference material for the next review. AI Documents reads the full package and surfaces contradictions between documents, deviations from standard contracts, unclear responsibility boundaries, penalties and schedules. The review then starts from what the group has already paid dearly to learn.


### The data question decides whether it happens at all


For a large contractor, security is part of the commercial decision itself. Competitively sensitive estimates, security-classified projects and the client's confidential documents cannot go into a tool that ships everything onward and hopes for the best.


Brickanta is ISO/IEC 27001:2022 certified, runs on European data residency and does not train models on customer data. Source files stay in the customer's SharePoint and are only fetched when needed instead of being copied into yet another system. For enterprise customers, vectorisation, databases and knowledge graphs can sit in the customer's own Azure environment, and on-prem deployment is possible where security-classified information requires it. IT gets its own view of active sessions, decision traces, permissions and attempted prompt injection.


### A partner has to master both crafts


Construction law, estimating and procurement take decades to understand properly. Agentic architecture, data governance and information security are a craft of their own. Most vendors have one of them. A general AI platform knows everything about infrastructure and misses why a demoted priority rule is expensive. A construction app understands that, and cannot meet the group's IT requirements.


Brickanta is built to sit in both. The construction law module answers legal questions with reference to actual court rulings from the Praxiom database. The procurement module assembles the right trade-specific document package for each subcontractor instead of sending all hundreds of files to everyone. Every finding traces back to document, page and passage, which is what allows the lawyer to take a position and the estimator to price with confidence.


Consolidation in the industry has built groups out of companies that are each genuinely good. The synergies are booked in procurement and financing, while the combined experience from thousands of projects stays inside each individual company. That is the most expensive unused asset in a construction group, and no app reaches it.
