# Where AI Pays Off First in Construction, According to McKinsey

#### Article


What does AI actually do for your firm if you start today? McKinsey's[new report](https://www.mckinsey.com/industries/engineering-construction-and-building-materials/our-insights/how-ai-is-reshaping-the-future-of-the-aec-industry?_sp=dc2e7e00-5360-43a9-b2d6-a41e68b82eae.1784819669758#/) on how AI will reshape the construction industry maps where the value shows up across three phases, and it says the firms that move early are best placed to build lasting advantage, while the ones that treat AI as a minor upgrade risk ceding ground to others. The math is simple. The sooner you start, the more it compounds, and the head start you build is the one thing money cannot buy back later.


### Phase One: Better Bids From Month One


McKinsey puts the fastest returns in the first 18 months, in the repeatable workflows where fragmented information causes delay and rework. Its near-term list runs from winning and pricing projects to planning, and procurement. For a contractor the first of those is the bid, and that is where Brickanta starts. From your first week, you are able to bid faster, and the surprises that used to eat up your margin are made visible before you sign the contract. ### Phase Two: An Advantage That Compounds


McKinsey's middle phase, 18 to 48 months in, is where a firm's own data becomes the advantage. The assumptions and thought process behind a decision, the alternatives and trade-offs, are what competitors find hardest to replicate. Here's what we'd add. That advantage builds from the first project you capture cleanly, so starting earlier means arriving at this phase with more to draw on. Every bid you run through Brickanta becomes structured, traceable knowledge, and the[Knowledge Base](https://brickanta.com/blog/the-most-valuable-database-in-construction-is-the-one-you-already-own) turns past projects into references for new ones.


### Phase Three: Ready When The Building Site Catches Up


McKinsey's long phase, past the four-year mark, is when AI moves onto the site itself, connecting design, planning, logistics, equipment, and site execution into a more automated operating system. Our reading of what it means for your firm is simple. The site systems of that era will run on structured project data and consistent workflows, which is exactly what phases one and two build, so a firm that started early arrives with a working foundation in place while the rest start from a blank page.
