# The Hidden Cost of Manual Evidence of Insurability

Evidence of Insurability (EOI) has long been a necessary but often overlooked component of the employee benefits ecosystem. For carriers, administrators and employers alike, EOI serves a critical purpose: ensuring applicants meet eligibility requirements when enrolling in coverage above guaranteed issue limits. Yet despite its importance, many organizations continue to rely on fragmented, manual processes that introduce delays, create administrative burdens and frustrate applicants.


As benefit programs become more digital and consumer expectations continue to rise, the traditional EOI process is increasingly out of step with modern enrollment experiences. The organizations that succeed in the next decade will be those that eliminate friction, automate decision-making and deliver seamless experiences from enrollment through underwriting.


**Why EOI Has Become a Bottleneck**


The challenge with EOI is not the requirement itself—it's the process surrounding it.


Many post-enrollment EOI workflows require carriers and administrators to reconcile employee data across multiple systems, validate eligibility, collect health information, communicate with applicants and manually adjudicate submissions. Each handoff increases complexity. Each delay creates additional follow-up work. And every additional step introduces the risk of applicant abandonment.


The consequences are significant:


- Prolonged coverage determination timelines.
- Increased operational costs.
- Lower applicant completion rates.
- Higher administrative workloads.
- Poorer overall customer experiences.


When EOI remains disconnected from enrollment and benefits administration workflows, organizations often spend more time managing exceptions than delivering value.


**The New Standard: Real-Time Decisioning**


The most significant advancement in EOI technology is the ability to automate adjudication and provide real-time decisions wherever possible. Rather than routing applications through lengthy manual review cycles, automated decisioning engines can evaluate eligibility, apply underwriting rules and determine outcomes immediately.


This shift delivers benefits across the value chain:


**For applicants:** Quicker decisions and faster access to coverage.


**For carriers:** Reduced processing costs and more efficient operations.


**For administrators:** Fewer manual touchpoints and streamlined workflows.


The result is a dramatically improved experience while maintaining compliance and underwriting rigor.


**Engagement Matters More Than Ever**


One of the most overlooked challenges in EOI is applicant engagement. Even when individuals intend to complete coverage requirements, cumbersome forms, multiple logins and poor communication can lead to incomplete submissions.


Modern EOI platforms address this challenge through digital-first experiences featuring:


- Single Sign-On (SSO) integration.
- Pre-filled applicant information.
- Dynamic questionnaires.
- Automated reminders and notifications.
- Real-time feedback during the submission process.


These capabilities reduce friction and make it easier for applicants to complete required actions without interruption.


Organizations implementing these approaches have demonstrated measurable improvements in completion rates and overall participation. Our["Streamline Evidence of Insurability Processing" fact sheet](https://www.ssctech.com/resources/form/streamline-evidence-of-insurability-processing) shows 65% of submissions are completed via SSO integration, and some implementations have achieved a 300% increase in completion rates.


**Automation Is About More Than Efficiency**


When people think about automation, they often focus on cost reduction. While reducing administrative overhead is certainly valuable, automation's greatest contribution may be consistency.


Automated EOI platforms can validate census data, confirm eligibility requirements, enforce plan rules and standardize workflows across systems. This reduces the need for manual reconciliation while helping organizations maintain accuracy across the enrollment journey.


By replacing fragmented, manual processes with integrated rules-based workflows, organizations can create a more predictable operating model that scales efficiently as enrollment volumes grow.


**Integration Is No Longer Optional**


Today's benefits ecosystem is increasingly interconnected. Enrollment platforms, HR systems, carriers and administrators all contribute critical data throughout the coverage lifecycle.


As a result, EOI solutions must be able to operate both as integrated components within larger benefits ecosystems and as standalone applications when necessary. Modern platforms accomplish this through API connectivity, file-based integrations and flexible deployment models that support diverse organizational requirements.


The ability to ingest employee and election data, validate eligibility, apply automated adjudication rules and generate completed state-approved forms within a unified workflow creates substantial operational advantages.


**What Success Looks Like**


The future of EOI is not merely digitized forms. It is intelligent orchestration.


Organizations leading this transformation are building processes that:


- Digitize the entire EOI lifecycle.
- Deliver automated decisioning whenever possible.
- Increase applicant engagement.
- Reduce manual intervention.
- Integrate seamlessly within broader enrollment ecosystems.


The impact can be substantial. SS&C Brix™ is able to support high-volume EOI processing with more than 3.5 million EOI forms processed, over $430 billion in requested coverage handled and approximately 80% automated decisioning.

For years, EOI has been viewed primarily as an administrative necessity. That perspective is changing.


As organizations modernize enrollment and benefits administration, EOI is emerging as an opportunity to improve operational efficiency, accelerate coverage decisions and elevate the customer experience. The organizations that embrace automation, real-time decisioning and integrated digital workflows will be better positioned to meet applicant expectations while reducing operational complexity.
