# Impinj Intern Program: A Season of Learning, Connection, and Fun

# Impinj Intern Program: A Season of Learning, Connection, and Fun


## The 12-week summer program offers hands-on learning, mentorship, and networking — plus fun outings and lasting career connections.


This summer, 18 interns joined Impinj — most in Impinj’s Fairview (Seattle) office — for a 12-week program blending meaningful work, professional growth, and plenty of fun. Each intern tackled a project from start to finish, working side-by-side with Impinj engineers and other department leads.


The Impinj Summer Internship Program is designed to provide undergraduate and graduate students with a comprehensive learning experience that goes beyond traditional work assignments. It offers a unique blend of professional development, mentorship, and networking opportunities to help interns thrive in their roles and prepare for successful careers.


Outside the office, the interns explored Seattle together. This year’s highlight was a trip to the Museum of Illusions in downtown Seattle, along with happy hours, peer networking, and plenty of team bonding.


The benefits are many. Students develop valuable skills through interactive learning sessions and receive personalized mentorship and guidance from early-career colleagues.


The program offers more than just a resume boost. It’s a chance to access hands-on experience, leadership insights, and a network of peers and mentors that lasts well beyond the summer. In return, Impinj engineers and other employees gain fresh perspectives and energy the interns bring to the table.


Curious about what it’s like from an intern’s perspective? Keep reading for a Q&A with this year’s cohort.


### What inspired you to apply for an internship at Impinj, and how has your experience met your expectations so far?


**Krish Sahni,** embedded protocol engineering intern:
I wanted to work somewhere that blends hardware and software in real products. Impinj seemed like a place where I could learn that in depth, and it’s been exactly that — lots of learning, helpful people, and interesting challenges.


**Purab Shah,** RF regression engineering intern:
I was drawn to Impinj because of its unique position at the intersection of hardware, software, and data, with a mission to “Connect Every Thing.” The opportunity to work on meaningful, real-world projects that impact how devices communicate was incredibly appealing. So far, the experience has exceeded my expectations, from the hands-on technical challenges to the welcoming, collaborative culture that encourages learning at every step.


**Nalyan Varela,** RF test engineering intern:
I was inspired to apply to this internship because of the interesting and unique project description of working on RF test development for an anechoic chamber. My experience has been great. I’ve learned so much while working on my project, which perfectly aligns with my studies and interests.


**Sara Duan,** system validation engineering intern:
I read a Forbes article about how the checkout bins at Uniqlo work. That short read was my introduction to RFID, sparking my interest in Impinj.

### **How would you describe the Impinj internship program and the company culture?**


**Natalie Olson,** information technology intern:
Impinj’s internship program is truly designed to set up new interns for success. It has given me the chance to learn from mentors and learn more about the company. The internship program really feels like a way to connect you to Impinj, which aligns with how I’d describe the company culture. Everyone is so willing to give advice and answer questions, which has really enhanced my experience here!


**Ashika Palacharla,** field programmable gate array (FPGA) engineering intern:
Being surrounded by engineers who are so passionate about their work has enhanced my learning experience during the internship. All of my questions continue to be met with patience, thoughtfulness, and expert knowledge. With a company culture that encourages curiosity and openness, it has been extremely valuable to easily connect with engineers through the program and gain insight into their educational and career journeys, areas of focus, and current responsibilities in their roles.


**Vibha Sathish Kumar,** communication systems engineering intern:
The company culture at Impinj is collaborative and supportive. Throughout my internship experience, I’ve consistently felt encouraged and valued by the team. I also love how approachable everyone is at Impinj. They are always willing to answer your questions and share about their work.


### What specific skills or practices have you developed or improved upon during your internship at Impinj, and how do you envision applying them in your future endeavors?


**Kedi Yan,** RF system engineering intern:
During my internship at Impinj, I’ve developed hands-on experience with CST Studio, an EM simulation tool I hadn’t used before. My prior experience was mainly with ANSYS HFSS, so this has broadened my technical skill set and allowed me to contribute to a project that could enhance distance measurement capabilities in RAIN RFID systems. I also strengthened my project management skills — not just through my daily work, but by learning from experienced engineers and insights shared during the Leadership and Connection sessions. These skills will be valuable in both academic and industry settings as I continue my career.


**Congyi (Steven) Zhu,** digital experience marketing intern:
The most valuable skill I developed was the ability to think systematically and work independently. I was responsible for designing, planning, and executing the scope my manager assigned to me. This was different from my school and previous internships, where I primarily completed predefined tasks. In the future, I plan to create my own product, with the help of AI, and the process I learned during this internship will make that much more achievable.


**Skylar Qian,** tag test engineering intern:
Being able to read and understand such a large codebase that I had never touched before was something I had never had to do. Throughout this internship, I got a lot of practice being able to look through hundreds of files for the right command and am much better at understanding and chasing functions.


**Sung Min Kang,** research intern:
One of the most valuable lessons I’ve learned is how to pivot quickly when something isn’t working. The pace of industry is much faster than academia, and this agile mindset has been eye-opening. It’s a practical approach that’s closely tied to solving real-world problems and improving lives. I plan to carry this mindset forward in both research and future collaborations.


### What project are you working on at Impinj?


**Nalyan Varela,** RF test engineering intern:
My project is to develop antenna testing code for Impinj’s new anechoic chamber at the Beacon Hill office. The scope of my project involves building Python code from the ground up to control the anechoic chamber, plot basic data, and control Impinj products for testing.


**Shifa Somji,** platform machine learning engineering intern:
As a machine learning platform intern on the platform architecture team, I’ve worked on conducting feature importance analysis for a system that predicts RFID tag transitions.


**Vibha Sathish Kumar,** communication systems engineering intern:
I’m working on the systems team at Impinj. My project compares the performance of two tag modulation schemes (in Gen2 and Gen2x) and evaluates their reliability in noisy environments.


**Congyi (Steven) Zhu,** digital experience marketing intern:
I’ve worked on the Website (Re)volution Project, which is an overhaul of Impinj’s website to better meet evolving business objectives, audience needs, and marketing strategies.


### What advice would you give to future interns joining Impinj so they can make the most of their experience?


**Purab Shah,** RF regression engineering intern:
Take initiative in both your project work and, just as importantly, your networking. Ask questions early and often; people here are incredibly generous with their time and knowledge. Don’t limit yourself to just your immediate team; attend the leadership sessions, reach out to engineers in other groups, and learn how the pieces fit together. Lastly, document your work and your learnings, as not only will it help you reflect, but it’ll also make your contributions more visible and impactful.


**Ashika Palacharla,** field programmable gate array (FPGA) engineering intern:
Be ready to embrace the unknowns. Typically, summer intern projects are a small part of a significantly larger, more complex system. It’s important to remember that your responsibilities are to learn and deliver on your part; it is okay to not understand all of the fine details of the larger system, but it is always beneficial to stay curious and put in the effort to stay informed on the broader team goals.


**Shifa Somji,** platform machine learning engineering intern:
My biggest advice is to be proactive about reaching out to people across different teams — don't just stick to your immediate project group. Take advantage of all the networking events and don't be shy about asking questions during presentations!
