# Data Accuracy: Helping Solve Grocery’s $473 Billion Food Waste Problem

# Data Accuracy: Helping Solve Grocery’s $473 Billion Food Waste Problem


## RAIN RFID tags in food labels can enable 360-degree insight into perishable items, helping grocers keep food on shelves and out of landfills.


**Key takeaways:**


- Food waste is a pressing challenge, bearing costs for food retailers, shoppers, and the environment.
- Grocers face pressure on multiple fronts: labor, margins, on-shelf availability, food traceability and recalls, and sustainability.
- New data report from Impinj explores how these challenges are exacerbated by data blind spots and shows how retailers can leverage RAIN RFID to enable food supply chain leaders to tackle spoilage, lost sales, and preventable environmental impact.


When food expires on store shelves, no one wins. Expired food ends up in landfills, driving emissions; customers experience empty shelves and frustration; and retailers lose billions in potential profits.


In fact, food waste costs U.S. retailers and consumers an[estimated $473 billion](https://refed.org/articles/slow-progress-big-opportunities-in-food-waste-reduction-insights-from-refed-s-food-loss-and-waste-estimates-for-2022/) each year.


**Data blind spots are costing grocers — and the planet**
To maximize freshness and keep customers happy, grocers need dependable data about every item they make, transport, and sell as those items move through supply chains. Unfortunately, few achieve that level of visibility. According to a recent[Impinj data report](https://www.impinj.com/library/Resources/Whitepapers/The-State-of-Supply-Chain-Integrity-for-Food-Groce) , 90% of food and grocery leaders say their organizations are equipped to drive accurate supply chain visibility, but only a third are doing so. Food supply chain leaders are experiencing data blind spots that can drive serious consequences.


**Why now: The five pressures driving food waste**


- **Labor is scarce.** Manual counts and freshness checks consume hours on a daily basis.
- **Waste erodes margins.** Spoilage and heavy markdowns cut into already-thin margins.
- **On-shelf availability slips.** Out-of-stocks and replenishment imbalances can mean missed sales opportunities.
- **Recalls demand precision.** Far-reaching recalls take safe food off shelves unnecessarily; more targeted pulls can benefit customers and margins.
- **Sustainability matters.** Decaying organic waste in landfills contributes to greenhouse gas emissions.


Grocers and food retailers that rely on barcodes for supply chain insight aren’t getting the complete picture. Printed barcodes can fail if they’re obscured or if there are ink smears. And barcodes require one-at-a-time, line-of-sight scans of each labeled item; a tough-to-execute workflow tax on retail operations. The results: Gaps in the data that grocers need to keep shelves fresh.


**RAIN RFID delivers item-level visibility and efficiency**
RAIN RFID is different, as it can enable item-level insights into the location, freshness, expiration status, and other key information about individual products. RAIN can read up to 1,000 items per second, at a distance of up to 10 meters, and without direct line of sight — delivering the speed and scale grocery operations demand.


What does this mean for food supply chain leaders and retailers? Soon-to-expire goods are quickly surfaced for rotation or quick sale. Store teams are notified as soon as inventory runs low. And automated freshness checks compress hours of manual work.


**Market momentum: Walmart expands RFID in fresh foods**
Leading retailers are recognizing the benefits of RFID. In late October,[Walmart announced](https://corporate.walmart.com/news/2025/10/22/walmart-and-avery-dennison-collaborate-to-enhance-freshness-and-increase-operational-efficiency-using-rfid) it is bringing RFID into its stores’ meat, bakery, and deli departments to improve freshness tracking, reduce unsold food, and streamline replenishment — using digital use-by dates and item-level identities so associates can rotate and mark down products faster. The fresh perishables rollout signals that item-level visibility is now moving from pilot to scale at one of the world’s largest grocers.
