# Questions Walmart CPG Suppliers Should Address


### **Q1: What is a RAIN RFID Tag, and what’s inside?**


To choose the right tags, suppliers first need to understand how they work. The layers inside a tag determine performance, especially on hard-to-read products like liquids, metals, or small items.


- **RAIN RFID Tag:** The label you see and apply to a product. It can look like a sticker, hangtag, or fabric label and often includes printed text, graphics, or a barcode.
- **RFID Inlay:** The core technology layer inside the tag, consisting of an antenna and endpoint IC (sometimes referred to as a tag chip). This is the engine that enables RAIN RFID functionality.
- **Endpoint IC:** The “brain” of the inlay. This tiny tag chip drives sensitivity, read range, and determines how well the tag performs across different surfaces and packaging.


**In short:** Tag is what you attach to a product, inlays are the technology inside, and the endpoint IC is what makes the whole system work.


Once you understand the layers inside a tag, it becomes clearer why product packaging — and especially “hard-to-read” surfaces like liquids, metals, or small form factors — can affect performance.


---


### **Q2: What makes a product “hard to read”?**


Products with hard-to-read surfaces are items whose packaging and/or contents interfere with RAIN RFID readability. Common examples include:


- **Liquids** such as personal care products, beverages, and cleaning products that absorb RF energy.
- **Metals** like canned goods, aerosol cans, and foil packaging that reflect RF signals.
- **Small items,** including cosmetics, travel-sized items, and bottle caps, that provide limited surface area for a tag.


For many CPG suppliers, these products sit at the heart of the product mix. Solving for reliable reads prevents downstream compliance and performance issues without disrupting packaging or branding.

### **Q3: How do I choose the best tags for my products?**


Think of tag selection as finding the right tool for the job. The process starts with[ARC-certified inlays](https://rfid.auburn.edu/walmartspec/) that meet Walmart’s required specs – this ensures compliance. From there, suppliers should evaluate:


- Product and packaging needs: What’s being tagged matters. Liquids, metals, and small items may need compact tags or special placement.
- Work with your label provider: To help validate on real packaging by testing tag placement on your actual packaging to ensure consistent results in real-world settings.
- Build on performance: High-performance endpoint ICs enable smaller tags to pass ARC specs, improve read range, and broaden versatility across packaging type.
