# Retail Inventory Accuracy: The Inditex 0.57% Benchmark

# The Data Advantage Behind Inditex’s 0.57% Unsold Inventory


##


As demand volatility accelerates, the gap between retailers who have real-time inventory visibility and those who don’t is becoming impossible to ignore.


### **Key Takeaways:**


- Inditex ended 2024 with just 0.57% unsold inventory, while the rest of the fashion industry sits between 10% to 20%1. They achieved this by producing according to real demand, not forecasts or assumptions.
- According to[Impinj’s 2026 Retail Trends Report](https://www.impinj.com/retail-trends-report-2026) , demand volatility is the top challenge facing retail leaders, with more than double the respondents reporting it is their biggest challenge year over year; yet fewer than half of retail leaders say their organization can actually withstand it — a gap that shows up directly in lost profit and avoidable waste.
- Bridging the gap requires physical intelligence: a foundational data layer that makes the physical world visible and provides AI systems with a continuous stream of real-world data to optimize inventory, fulfillment, and merchandising decisions.


Inditex, the parent company of Zara and seven other global fashion brands, operating close to 5,500 stores worldwide, ended 2024 with 0.57% of unsold inventory. The rest of the fashion industry? Somewhere between 10% to 20%.


The gap isn’t about better buying decisions or tighter operations; it’s about data. Specifically, knowing exactly what you have, where it is, and how it’s moving in real time, across every store.


[In Inditex's 2025 Annual Report](https://www.inditex.com/itxcomweb/api/media/59f58f71-301d-4a50-85a6-0441fb9f37c5/ENGL-CCAAeIGGrupo2025.pdf) , they describe a model “tailored to demand” - short production runs, proximity sourcing, and fully integrated store and online inventory. The result: replenishment responds to what’s actually selling, not last month’s forecast. Online orders are fulfilled from confirmed store inventory. And when a customer asks for an item, staff can locate it in seconds.


The result? Margins protected, waste minimized, and operational precision at a global scale.


### **The Gap is Widening – and the Cost is Rising**


****


Most retailers recognize demand volatility as their #1 challenge, and according to Impinj’s 2026 Retail Trends Report, it has nearly doubled as the biggest challenge year over year. Yet only 42% of retail leaders say their organization is actually resilient to it. The industry knows the problem is growing. But most aren’t equipped to handle it.


As demand becomes more volatile and harder to forecast, the consequences (and costs) of an inaccurate inventory picture compound quickly:


- Missed sales when items can’t be located or confirmed in-stock
- Excess inventory that didn’t match actual demand
- Strained margins from markdowns needed to move what didn’t sell
- Failed fulfillment promises when online orders are promised against inventory that doesn’t exist, hurting brand perception


External pressure is intensifying, too. Emerging E.U. regulations – including the[Ecodesign for Sustainable Products Regulation](https://enterprise.gov.ie/en/what-we-do/the-business-environment/ecodesign-for-sustainable-products/) – are moving toward holding brands financially accountable for overproduction. Buying or producing closer to demand isn’t just operationally smarter; for brands with European exposure, it’s becoming a compliance requirement.


The key to Inditex’s success is a strong data foundation: a near real-time, item-level picture of everything in stock, on the floor, in the backroom, in transit, and available to fulfill orders right now. With that foundation in place, employees locate items in seconds, replenishment is triggered by actual sales velocity, and online orders are fulfilled from store inventory with confidence – because the system reflects reality.


### **Physical Intelligence is what makes that foundation possible**


When the foundational data is right, everything above it performs better. The issue is that in the past, the physical items moving throughout supply chains and retail stores were mostly invisible to enterprise software. No longer.


Impinj makes physical items — every piece of retail merchandise, every package — visible and traceable in real time, at scale, across 1,000s of stores, warehouses, and fulfillment centers globally. The Impinj platform, utilizing RAIN RFID wireless technology, transforms every item into a continuous stream of relevant physical-world data – a digital twin.


This is Physical Intelligence. It is real, giving you real-time information about the item or shipment – its precise location and quantity. It is what gives the AI infrastructure you've already invested in a reliable data stream to analyze, so it can see what's missing before it matters, act on what's moving before it's lost, and connect what's physical to what's possible. It is why the world's leading retailers, logistics companies, and manufacturers — across thousands of sites globally — run on Impinj.


And it is how you close the gap between inconsistent, inaccurate data and what is real (physical inventory or information about shipments in the supply chain), right now, most enterprises assume is out of reach.
