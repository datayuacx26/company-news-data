## Performance Max Ad Examples by Ecommerce Category


### Fashion and Apparel


A mid-size women's clothing brand running Performance Max Google Ads structured their campaign around three asset groups: new arrivals, sale items, and a specific hero product (a bestselling linen dress). The hero product asset group outperformed the others by 40% on ROAS.


What made it work:


• **Headlines** : "Linen Dress Under $80," "Free Returns on Every Order," "Worn by 12,000+ Customers" — each one addresses a different objection or desire


• **Images** : Six lifestyle photos across different settings (beach, brunch, travel), two product-on-white shots, one close-up of the fabric texture


• **Descriptions** : Led with the specific use case ("Your go-to summer dress for every occasion") rather than generic brand copy


• **Audience signals** : Uploaded a customer list of past purchasers in the same price range, plus a custom segment built from competitor brand searches


The sale and new arrivals asset groups used broader copy and stock-adjacent imagery. They converted, but at roughly half the efficiency. The lesson: specificity wins.


### Home Goods and Furniture


A direct-to-consumer furniture brand used Performance Max assets to promote their modular sofa line. They made one structural decision that changed their results: they created separate asset groups for each sofa configuration (2-seat, 3-seat, sectional) rather than one asset group for the entire line.


Each asset group included:


• Headlines tied to the specific configuration: "Sectional That Fits Any Room," "2-Seat Sofa, Ships in 5 Days"


• Room-setting lifestyle images matched to the configuration size


• A video walkthrough of that specific product (under 30 seconds, no voiceover, just product + text overlays)


• Audience signals built from users who had visited that specific product page


ROAS on the segmented approach was 3.1x compared to 1.8x on their previous single-asset-group structure. Google Ads AI max performs better when it has a clear, narrow brief — not a broad one.


### Beauty and Skincare


A skincare brand promoting a vitamin C serum built their Performance Max campaign around the customer's problem, not the product. Their top-performing asset group was titled "Dull Skin" internally and every asset addressed that specific concern.


Headlines included:


• "Brighter Skin in 4 Weeks"


• "Vitamin C Serum Dermatologists Recommend"


• "See the Difference or Your Money Back"


Their images showed before/after results (compliant with platform policies), ingredient close-ups, and the product in a bathroom context. Descriptions opened with the problem: "If your skin looks tired no matter how much you sleep, this is why."


This asset group generated a 28% higher click-through rate than their product-first asset group running simultaneously. Problem-aware copy outperforms product-aware copy when the customer is still in discovery mode — which describes most Performance Max placements.


### Sporting Goods and Outdoor


An outdoor gear retailer used Performance Max placements to promote their hiking boot line ahead of spring. They built asset groups around activity type (day hiking, backpacking, trail running) rather than product features.


The backpacking asset group included:


• Headlines: "Boots Built for Multi-Day Trails," "Waterproof to 10,000 Steps," "Free Shipping on Orders Over $75"


• Images: Boots in use on rugged terrain, a flat-lay with gear, a close-up of the sole


• A 15-second video showing the boot in wet conditions


• Audience signals: Custom segments from searches like "best backpacking boots 2024" and "waterproof hiking boots review"


The activity-segmented structure let Google's AI match the right asset group to the right search intent. A user searching "backpacking gear" saw different creative than someone searching "trail running shoes" — even though both were in the same campaign.


## How to Structure Your Own PMax Campaigns


### Start With Asset Group Architecture


The most common PMax mistake is treating the campaign like a catch-all. One asset group for your entire catalog gives Google's AI no useful signal about which products to show to which customers.


A better structure:


Asset Group Type When to Use Example


Product-specific Hero SKUs or bestsellers "Blue Linen Dress - Size S-XL"


Category-based Broad product lines "Women's Dresses"


Offer-based Promotions or seasonal pushes "Summer Sale - 30% Off"


Problem-based Awareness-stage audiences "Fix Dry Skin"


Start with three to five asset groups maximum. Add more once you have conversion data to guide decisions.


### Build Audience Signals That Actually Signal Something


Google's documentation on audience signals is vague by design — they want you to let the algorithm figure it out. But advertisers who provide strong first-party signals consistently outperform those who rely on interest categories alone.


Prioritize these signal sources in order:


• **Customer match lists** — Upload your existing customer email list. This is your highest-quality signal.


• **Website visitor segments** — Create segments from product page visitors, cart abandoners, and checkout starters separately.


• **Custom intent segments** — Build segments from competitor brand searches and high-intent category keywords.


• **In-market audiences** — Use these as a supplement, not a foundation.


Audience signals don't restrict who sees your ads. They tell Google where to start learning. Better starting points mean faster optimization.


### Write Headlines That Cover the Full Funnel


Performance Max serves ads across placements where users are at very different stages of intent. Your 15 headlines need to cover that range.


A practical breakdown for 15 headlines:


• **5 headlines** : Specific product or offer (what it is, what it costs, what's included)


• **4 headlines** : Benefits and outcomes (what the customer gets or feels)


• **3 headlines** : Trust and social proof (reviews, guarantees, shipping)


• **3 headlines** : Urgency or differentiation (limited stock, unique feature, comparison)


Avoid repeating the same idea in different words. Google's AI will find the best combinations — but only if the combinations are meaningfully different.


### Use Video Even If It's Imperfect


Many ecommerce advertisers skip video because they don't have production resources. That's a mistake. Performance Max will auto-generate video from your images if you don't provide one — and auto-generated video almost always underperforms.


A 15-to-30-second video shot on a smartphone outperforms auto-generated video in most tests. Keep it simple: product in use, text overlay with your headline, end card with your offer. No voiceover required.

## Common Performance Max Asset Mistakes to Avoid


Even well-structured campaigns make these errors:


• **Uploading fewer than 10 headlines** : Google needs variety to test combinations. Fewer headlines mean fewer combinations and slower learning.


• **Using the same image in multiple formats** : Crop your images for each format (square, landscape, portrait) rather than letting Google auto-crop. Auto-crop often cuts off the subject.


• **Mixing unrelated products in one asset group** : A single asset group for "all accessories" gives Google no useful signal. Split by product type or price point.


• **Ignoring the asset performance labels** : Google labels assets as "Low," "Good," or "Best." Replace Low-rated assets every two to three weeks.


• **Setting audience signals and never updating them** : Your customer list and visitor segments should be refreshed monthly. Stale signals slow down optimization.


## Frequently Asked Questions


### How many asset groups should a Performance Max campaign have?


Start with three to five asset groups organized by product category, hero SKU, or offer type. Add more once you have at least 30 conversions per asset group to guide optimization decisions.


### What are the most important Performance Max assets to get right first?


Headlines and images have the highest impact on early performance. Prioritize writing 15 specific, varied headlines and uploading at least eight images in multiple formats before worrying about video.


### Can you see which Performance Max placements are spending the most budget?


Google's native reporting shows placement breakdowns at the campaign level, but not by asset group. Tools like Sprites.ai surface placement-level data by asset group so you can see exactly where your budget is going and which placements are driving conversions.


### How long does it take for a Performance Max campaign to exit the learning phase?


Most campaigns need two to four weeks and at least 50 conversions to exit the learning phase. Avoid making major changes to assets, budgets, or bidding during this period, as each change resets the learning clock.


### What's the difference between Performance Max and Smart Shopping?


Smart Shopping was Google's previous automated campaign type focused on Shopping and Display placements. Performance Max replaced it in 2022 and adds Search, YouTube, Gmail, and Discover placements. PMax also requires more asset types and gives Google's AI broader control over where and how ads appear.
