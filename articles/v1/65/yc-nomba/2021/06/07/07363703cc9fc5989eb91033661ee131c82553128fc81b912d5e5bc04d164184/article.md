The daily lifecycle of an Agent revolves around funding your Kudi wallet in order to process transactions, collecting payments, helping customers to withdraw cash and finally reconciling the numbers at the end of the day.


Like any other business, the primary reason for business failure is simply running out of cash. As an Agent, you need to be able to manage and monitor your finances to be able to grow the business — balancing your inflow and outflow and ensuring that you always have the cash to float the business is critical to surviving and even more thriving as an Agent.


In light of that, we’ve identified some financial terms you need to be familiar with.


## **Cash Flow**


Your cash flow statement basically explains how much you have made at a time (T) the amount consumers have withdrawn and the cash balance at the end of a day, week or month. It contains daily monies made which are difficult to track in a Profit and Loss statement.

With a cash flow statement, you can easily know where the money is coming from, when it does and how much cash you have at hand. You need a logbook to track every transaction Cash In, Cash Out or Bills payment.


## **Profit and Loss**

Image by[Gino Crescoli](https://pixabay.com/users/AbsolutVision-6158753/?utm_source=link-attribution&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=image&utm_content=2656028) from[Pixabay](https://pixabay.com/?utm_source=link-attribution&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=image&utm_content=2656028)


Profit and loss show your revenue and expenses over a period of time. This report is the most important report every business needs to have. Every Agent needs to prepare and review its profit and loss statement periodically.


The profit/loss is determined by comparing your income against expenses incurred in running your outlet(s). The profit and loss statement contains vital information that can guide a business owner in making business decisions.


***Summation of Income - Summation of Expenses = Profit/Loss***


## **Savings**


Press enter or click to view image in full size


To bring about longevity in your business, you need to cultivate the habit of savings from the much you make. In doing that, saving at least 30% of your profit daily is highly recommended. These savings can go into building new outlets and employing more staff to effectively run the business.


Your burn rate — how much you are using to run your business — should not take over your entire profit. If this happens you will not have much to save and growth will be impossible.
