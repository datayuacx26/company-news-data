## **What damage prevention can do before breakage shows**


Hair does not wait for a dramatic snap to start changing. Older lengths and ends live through repeated styling, brushing, washing, environmental exposure, color services, and heat \[1\]. Over time, that weathering can make the fiber more vulnerable to roughness and breakage. A strengthening shampoo earns its place when the goal is routine damage defense: cleanse without unnecessary harshness, help maintain a conditioned feel, and support smoother handling so hair is not fighting friction every time you detangle or style \[3\] \[1\] \[2\]. ## **When a repair-focused formula makes more sense**


Switch from “maintain and defend” to “target and support” when the signs are already there: brittle lengths, rough texture, dullness, tangles that feel new, extra breakage, or visibly frayed ends \[1\]. Those are cues that shampoo may still matter, but shampoo alone may not be the whole answer \[1\].


Damaged hair fibers do not biologically heal like living tissue \[1\]. Repair-focused cosmetic care can help hair feel softer and easier to manage and may help reduce the look and feel of breakage within the limits of the formula; it should not be framed as permanently reversing all forms of damage \[1\].

## **FAQ**


### **Can I use a strengthening shampoo every wash?**


A strengthening shampoo can fit regular wash days when the formula suits your hair and scalp feel \[1\]. The better question is whether it leaves your hair clean, conditioned, flexible, and manageable, not stiff, coated, or rough \[1\]. Formula type and hair condition matter \[1\].


### **Which conditioner should I pair with Nº.4?**


Pair Nº.4 Bond Maintenance Shampoo with Nº.5 Bond Maintenance Conditioner as the wash-day conditioner step. Apply Nº.5 to mid-lengths and ends after shampooing, leave on for 1–3 minutes, then rinse well. Together, they form the OLAPLEX wash-day duo for breakage-conscious routines. \[7\]


### **Can a repairing formula fix split ends?**


Repair-focused care can improve the look and feel of damaged lengths, but it should not be treated as a permanent fix for split ends \[1\]. Once an end is fully split, trimming is the cleanest way to remove that split portion.


### **Is bond-building the same as protein?**


Not necessarily. Protein-focused formulas and bond-focused formulas are different approaches within strengthening and repair care \[1\]. N°.3PLUS is positioned as a protein-free pre-shampoo treatment using patented OLAPLEX Bond Building Technology™ \[4\].


### **Should color-treated or heat-styled hair use prevention, repair, or both?**


Often, both roles make sense: a strengthening shampoo for the routine foundation, and a repair step when hair shows brittleness, roughness, dullness, tangles, or breakage \[1\]. Heat, color services, and repeated handling can contribute to cumulative weathering, so match the routine to the level of visible stress. \[1\]


### **When should I see a professional stylist or dermatologist?**


See a professional stylist when breakage, split ends, or chemical-service damage needs hands-on assessment before the next color or heat service \[1\]. Consider a dermatologist when the issue involves sudden shedding, scalp discomfort, irritation, or a change that does not look like ordinary cosmetic damage. \[1\]
