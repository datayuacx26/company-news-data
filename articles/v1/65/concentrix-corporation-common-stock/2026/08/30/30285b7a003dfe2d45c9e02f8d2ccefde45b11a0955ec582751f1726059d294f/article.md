# Sifting Through the Evidence: Unblocking Dispute Bottlenecks with Document Intelligence

When a dispute is raised, the focus tends to fall on the decisions made. Was the transaction legitimate? Should the customer be refunded? Who ultimately pays? But in practice, most disputes begin with something much more basic: gathering and interpreting evidence.


As we explored in our white paper,[Rewriting the Rules: How AI Is Transforming Fraud and Dispute Resolution](https://www.concentrix.com/insights/thought-leadership/how-ai-is-transforming-fraud-and-dispute-resolutions/) , the dispute stage is often where the process grinds (practically) to a halt. Before a claim can be assessed and liability determined, businesses need to work through the documents submitted by both customers and merchants, all of which is rarely simple.


Each dispute brings its own collection of documents, often in different formats: invoices, screenshots, email threads, delivery confirmations, photographs, transaction logs. Individually, they may be straightforward. At scale like this, they create a significant operational burden. The challenge is to make sense of it quickly enough to keep the process moving. That’s exactly where document intelligence shines.


## Dispute Operations Are Drowning in Documents


Every dispute generates a collection of inputs from multiple sources, each contributing to the overall picture. Customers may submit screenshots or correspondence to support their claim. Merchants respond with receipts, delivery confirmations, or proof of fulfilment. Payment networks have their own documentation requirements. All of it needs to be reviewed, validated, and aligned before a decision can be made.


This is where much of the operational complexity sits. In many businesses today, this work is still handled manually. Teams are required to open, read, interpret, and cross-reference documents one by one, often across different systems and formats.


The number of chargebacks globally is[expected to grow 37% from 2025 to 2029, reaching 359 million transactions annually](https://www.mastercard.com/us/en/news-and-trends/Insights/2025/what-s-the-true-cost-of-a-chargeback-in-2025.html) . This also means businesses may see longer lists of supporting documentation in need of processing. Documents arrive in different formats, with different structures, and varying levels of completeness, and plenty require context or judgement to understand properly.


Before any automation can take effect or decision can be made, the evidence must first be understood. And when that understanding depends on manual effort instead of document intelligence, the entire process begins to slow down.


## Why People Struggle to Handle Evidence at Scale


It’s all to do with volume. At a small scale, manual review works. A case handler can open a document, read through it, interpret the details, and apply the relevant rules. The process may be slow, but it is manageable.


Humans are well suited to handling complexity and making nuanced decisions. But they are not designed to process large volumes of unstructured information repeatedly and at speed. Documents must be opened, understood, and matched to the specific requirements of the case.


Information needs to be cross-referenced against transaction data, customer history, and scheme rules. Where something is missing or unclear, follow-up is required. In and of itself, none of this is especially complex. But repeated hundreds or thousands of times each day? This is where the process begins to break down.


There is also an issue of consistency. Different reviewers may interpret the same document in slightly different ways. Over time, this leads to variation in outcomes, even when the underlying rules are the same.


## Evidence Is the Gatekeeper of Liability


Once a dispute is raised, the process quickly moves beyond intake and into a more complex phase where the focus shifts to settling who’s going to pay. That settlement depends heavily on what can be proven. If the evidence is incomplete, the process stalls. If it is weak, the case is likely to be lost. And if it takes too long to gather and validate, the entire resolution timeline stretches out.


This is particularly visible in representment. Even when merchants challenge disputes, success rates are often limited, not necessarily because the claim is invalid, but because the supporting evidence is insufficient, inconsistent, or poorly structured. The outcome isn’t determined solely by the facts of the case, but by how effectively those facts are documented and presented.


In this sense, evidence doesn’t just inform the decision – it controls the whole process. It determines whether a case can move forward, how quickly it progresses, and how likely it is to reach a successful outcome.


## The Role of Document Intelligence


There are clear Natural Language Programming (NLP) and Optical Character Recognition (OCR) opportunities to automatically read the documents both customers and merchants provide. These technologies are already capable of extracting structured information from unstructured sources, identifying key details within invoices, interpreting text within emails, and even analysing images such as delivery confirmations.


Applied effectively, these changes the role of the system. This is where AI can begin to make a meaningful difference. Not by replacing decision-making, but by accelerating the process that sits in front of it.


Instead of relying on humans to read and interpret each document, the system can extract relevant data automatically, classify the type of evidence being provided and map it against the requirements of the case. An invoice can be scanned and matched to a transaction. A delivery photo can be linked to an address and timestamp. A screenshot can be interpreted within the context of a customer claim.


The result is a shift in how evidence is used. Rather than acting as a barrier that slows the case down, evidence gathered through document intelligence becomes structured, searchable, and immediately usable.


Cases can move forward without waiting for manual review at every step. Decision engines can operate with more complete information. And human intervention can be reserved for the situations that genuinely require judgement. AI can enable the system to reach a point where a decision can be made with unprecedented speed and consistency.


## A Safe, High-Impact AI Use Case


Not every application of AI in fraud and dispute management carries the same level of risk or complexity. Some involve ambiguous decisions, evolving patterns, or outcomes that are difficult to explain. Evidence handling is different.


The parameters are relatively clear. Payment schemes define what constitutes valid documentation. Regulatory frameworks set expectations around timelines and processes. Outcomes are bounded: a claim is either supported, challenged, or escalated. This structure makes it well suited to automation.


Much of the work involved in handling evidence is repeatable. Extracting key fields from a document, identifying whether required information is present, and mapping that information to predefined rules are all tasks that can be performed consistently by a system. Where edge cases arise, they can be flagged for human review without slowing the entire process.


This is where the impact becomes tangible. By removing the need for manual interpretation at every step, enterprises can reduce processing time, improve consistency, and lower operational cost. At the same time, they retain control over decision-making, with human oversight applied where it adds the most value.


The goal is to take repeatable work out of human queues so that people can focus on the parts of the process that require judgement. Evidence handling and document intelligence fits that model closely.


When AI initiatives struggle to move beyond experimentation, it stands out as a practical, low-risk starting point that addresses a clear bottleneck and delivers measurable improvements.


## Focus on Bottlenecks over Buzzwords


When organizations think about applying AI to fraud and dispute operations, the focus often lands on the most visible parts of the journey. Chatbots, customer interfaces, and front-end experiences tend to attract attention first. They’re easier to demonstrate, easier to explain, and closer to the customer.


But the biggest gains sit in the parts of the process that slow everything else down. The high-volume, repeatable tasks that create delay, cost, and inconsistency at scale. Evidence handling is one of those points. It sits at the centre of every dispute, determines whether cases can progress, and yet remains heavily manual in many companies.


Addressing that bottleneck with document intelligence allows cases to move faster, decisions to be made with better information, and teams to focus on higher-value work. Removing friction from within improves both operational efficiency and customer experience. The goal is to build a process that can move at the speed of the problem it is trying to solve.


Real transformation comes from identifying where the system is under strain and applying automation in a way that unlocks flow across the entire lifecycle.
