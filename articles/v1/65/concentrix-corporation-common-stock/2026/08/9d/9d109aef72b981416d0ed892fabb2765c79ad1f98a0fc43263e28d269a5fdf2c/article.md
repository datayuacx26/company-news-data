# What I Kept Hearing at the Databricks AI Summit, and Why It Matters for Contact Centers

Earlier this year, I attended the Databricks AI Summit in San Francisco. It’s one of those events where you walk in expecting to hear about data engineering and walk out thinking about the future of work.


What struck me most wasn’t any single keynote or product announcement. It was a theme that kept surfacing in hallway conversations, panel discussions, and demos alike: **AI is no longer a back-office tool** . It’s moving to the front lines — and contact centers are squarely in its path.


## The Shift Everyone Was Talking About


For years, AI in the enterprise meant better analytics, faster reporting, smarter recommendations buried inside dashboards that only data teams ever opened. That’s changing fast.


At the Summit, the conversation had moved decisively toward *agentic AI* — systems that don’t just surface insights but take action. Autonomous agents that can reason, plan, and execute multi-step tasks without a human in the loop for every decision.


For contact centers, this is a significant inflection point. We’ve spent a decade automating the easy stuff — FAQs, simple transactions, routing logic. Agentic AI opens the door to automating genuinely complex interactions: billing disputes, technical troubleshooting, multi-system lookups, even empathetic escalation decisions.


## What the Data Teams Are Building


One of the most interesting sessions I attended focused on how enterprises are using Databricks’ Unity Catalog and Mosaic AI to build governed, auditable AI pipelines. The emphasis on **data governance** resonated with me — because in CX, trust is everything.


Customers don’t just want fast answers. They want accurate ones. They want to know their data is being handled responsibly. The infrastructure being built at the data layer — lineage tracking, access controls, model monitoring — is what makes it possible to deploy AI in customer-facing contexts without taking on unacceptable risk.


## The Gap Between Hype and Readiness


I’ll be honest: not everything I heard was reassuring. There’s still a significant gap between what AI can do in a controlled demo and what it can reliably do in a live customer interaction at scale.


Hallucination remains a real problem. Context windows have limits. And the organisational change management required to deploy AI agents in a contact centre — training, quality assurance, escalation design — is often underestimated.


The companies that are getting this right aren’t the ones moving fastest. They’re the ones moving most deliberately: piloting in low-risk channels, measuring obsessively, and building the human-AI collaboration model before they scale.


## Why This Matters for CX Leaders


If you lead a contact centre or a CX function, you might be wondering what a data engineering conference has to do with your world. More than you’d think.


The platforms being built at events like the Databricks AI Summit are the same ones your technology partners are building on. The architectural decisions being made today — about how AI agents are trained, governed, and deployed — will shape what’s possible in your contact centre in 12 to 24 months.


Understanding that landscape, even at a high level, puts you in a much stronger position to ask the right questions of your vendors, your IT teams, and your own leadership.


## What I’m Taking Away


Three things stuck with me from the Summit:


1. **Agentic AI is real, and it’s coming to CX.** The question isn’t whether autonomous agents will handle customer interactions — it’s when, and how well.
2. **Governance is a competitive advantage.** The organisations that invest in data quality, auditability, and responsible AI practices now will be the ones customers trust later.
3. **The human layer still matters.** The best AI deployments I heard about weren’t replacing human agents — they were making them dramatically more effective. That’s the model worth building toward.


The Databricks AI Summit isn’t a CX conference. But it might be one of the most important events for CX leaders to have on their radar. The infrastructure being built there is the foundation everything else will run on.
