# The Dispute Tightrope: How to Balance Financial Risk and Customer Trust

By the time a customer raises a dispute, something, somewhere, has already gone wrong. Whether it’s a transaction they don’t recognise or a payment they didn’t expect, disputes can signal something deeper. Businesses must now make a decision on whether what happened counts as identity theft or a benign mistake.


As we explored in our white paper,[Rewriting the Rules: How AI Is Transforming Fraud and Dispute Resolution](https://www.concentrix.com/insights/thought-leadership/how-ai-is-transforming-fraud-and-dispute-resolutions/) , these moments are becoming more frequent and visible. Dispute volumes are rising, expectations are shifting, and the way organizations respond to scenarios that impact financial risk is increasingly shaping how they are perceived. Global fraud losses[are projected to hit $107 billion by 2029, a 141% increase from 2024](https://cropink.com/ecommerce-fraud-statistics?) .


Sitting at the intersection of risk, compliance, and customer trust, what was once treated as a back-office process is now a decidedly front-office experience.


## Disputes Sit at the Intersection of Risk and Experience


When a dispute occurs, multiple pressures are colliding at once. There is financial risk to manage, regulatory requirements to meet, and customers who expect both clarity and speed. Each of these forces pulls in a slightly different direction in defining the overall experience for the customer.


From a risk and compliance perspective, the process is tightly controlled. Institutions are required to investigate claims within strict timelines. Decisions carry financial consequences and determine whether it’s the bank, the merchant, or the customer that ultimately absorbs the cost.


At the same time, the customer experience expectation is shaped by a very different set of standards. Customers are used to real-time updates and intuitive digital journeys. When money is involved, those expectations only intensify.


Resolution timelines are often lengthy. Depending on the complexity of the case and the involvement of multiple parties, they can stretch from weeks into months. The bigger issue, however, is what the customer experiences during that time. Too often, they sense that the process is happening somewhere out of sight.


A lack of transparency is one of the most consistent sources of frustration for customers. They’re used to tracking everyday transactions in real time, seeing when a delivery is dispatched, exactly where it is en route, and when it will arrive. When it comes to disputes, that visibility disappears.


The result is uncertainty. Customers don’tknow what stage their case is in, whether any progress has been made, or what they are expected to do next. That uncertainty quickly turns into repeated contact. This happens because the customer is attempting to gain clarity about the dispute.


This is why disputes must be treated as opportunities to build customer trust. The way an organization handles a dispute is an opportunity to demonstrate reliability at a moment when the customer is actively questioning it.


## Reputational Risk and the Role Friction Plays


Customer frustration is now increasingly public. When disputes are slow or poorly handled, financial risk goes up and customers don’t absorb that experience quietly. Whether its on social platforms., reviews or forums, they turn individual cases into visible signals about the brand.


A single unresolved dispute can quickly become a wider narrative about poor service, lack of responsiveness, or a failure to protect customers. This is what changes the stakes. And it’s why dispute handling is increasingly about managing brand perception.


## Why Legacy Processes Worsen FInancial Risk


While the experience gap is visible at the front end, its root cause sits further back in the process. Most dispute operations are still built on fragmented, manual workflows that were never designed to support real-time communication or seamless customer journeys.


Information is spread across systems. Cases move between teams. Updates depend on handoffs rather than automation, and nothing progresses until every piece of information has been gathered, verified, and passed along.


The result is a disconnect between what the customer expects and what the organization can realistically deliver. From the outside, it appears as poor communication or lack of responsiveness. From the inside, it’s a system that was never built to support transparency at scale.


Now, the challenge of managing financial risk becomes an operational one. Until the underlying workflows are connected and streamlined, improvements at the surface level will always be limited.


## Transparency and Empathy as a Competitive Advantage


The organizations that stand out, ensure that they resolve their cases faster. But they’re also the ones that make the process feel understandable, visible, and controlled, reducing uncertainty even when resolution itself takes time.


Transparency is central in this. Clear updates, defined stages, and visible progress help customers understand where they are in the process and what to expect next. It removes the need to chase for information and reduces the sense that the issue is being handled somewhere out of sight.


Disputes are often emotionally charged interactions, in which customers aren’t just looking for an outcome: they’re seeking reassurance too. That’s where empathy becomes critical.


[Handled well, disputes can reinforce trust rather than erode it.](https://www.concentrix.com/insights/thought-leadership/how-ai-is-transforming-fraud-and-dispute-resolutions/) They provide an opportunity to demonstrate responsiveness, clarity, and care at a moment when it matters most. And in a market where products and pricing are often similar, that experience can act as a point of differentiation.


The organizations that recognise this shift are treating disputes as a customer experience to be designed (rather than a cost center to be managed).


## AI’s Starring Role Is Orchestration


AI improves dispute handling by reshaping where and how those interactions happen.


Much of the dispute process is repetitive and structured. Collecting information, validating inputs, applying rules, routing cases, and tracking progress are all tasks that can be handled more efficiently through automation. When that happens, the overall process becomes faster, more consistent, and easier to manage at scale.


That, in turn, creates space for something more valuable. Instead of spending time gathering basic details or navigating fragmented systems, human agents can focus on the moments that actually require judgement and reassurance. The goal should be to support the customer rather than check off an administrative task lists.


Orchestration becomes especially important, because AI works best when it’s part of a connected system, linking data, decisions, and workflows across the full lifecycle of a dispute. It makes sure that information flows between stages, that rules are applied consistently, and that cases progress without unnecessary delay. It also enables better visibility, allowing organizations to provide clearer, more timely updates to customers.


## Navigating Dispute Management and Customer Relationships


Disputes have always been part of financial services. What’s changed is what they now represent.


As well as resolving a claim or determining liability, disputes are opportunities for customers to actively assess whether they trust the organization handling their money. This is where speed, clarity, and communication matter as much as the resolution.


Handled poorly, they create friction, frustration, and, increasingly, public criticism. Handled well, they can reinforce confidence and demonstrate reliability under pressure.


Dispute management now touches risk, compliance, customer experience, and brand all at once. How a company handles it says a lot about how well the business actually runs.


The leaders are redesigning the process end to end—connecting systems, giving customers more visibility, and using automation to move faster without losing the human side of the experience.
