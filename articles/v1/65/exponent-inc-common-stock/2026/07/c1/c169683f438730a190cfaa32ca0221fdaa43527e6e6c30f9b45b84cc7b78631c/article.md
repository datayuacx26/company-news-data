# How Can ConOps Drive the Evolution of AV Safety Cases?

## What Clients are Talking About


-


Safety cases are updated when there are meaningful changes to the application (ConOps) or operating environment (ODD), such as new operating conditions, service models, or development milestones. New data, learnings, or system changes may also trigger updates to keep safety claims and evidence aligned with how the system is deployed and its safety goals.


-


As AV systems progress through development and deployment, organizations often maintain multiple safety cases specific to different stages of operations. In practice, safety-related work may also be distributed across different documents and processes around which a safety case is later built, rather than being driven by a robustly developed safety case framework. This type of approach makes it harder to assess how the work all fits together.


-


A safety case framework should define the full set of claims and types of evidence needed to support a system's intended operation over time. A safety case is a stage-specific instantiation of that framework, reflecting the current capabilities, deployment model, and available evidence. A safety case may be a partial implementation of the framework. This distinction helps organizations plan for future safety requirements while managing present-day deployments.


-


Exponent works with AV developers to structure and define a concept of operations, align it with ODD definitions, and integrate both into scalable safety case frameworks. Our teams assess how changes in system capabilities and operations affect safety claims and evidence, including evaluating the coverage and coherence of frameworks and safety cases as systems progress. We also connect operational assumptions to safety case claims and help ensure that system updates consistently trigger updates to supporting evidence where necessary.
