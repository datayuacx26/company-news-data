# Norra vs UpKeep for Skilled Nursing Equipment

For a skilled nursing facility, Norra and UpKeep solve different problems. UpKeep is a top-rated CMMS built to manage maintenance work orders and preventive-maintenance schedules. Norra is purpose-built for skilled nursing durable medical equipment, reporting room-level location automatically with no staff scanning. If your goal is finding equipment and killing rental waste, Norra is the pick.


The reason to be precise about that split is money. A typical 110-bed nursing home loses $155,000 to $500,000 a year to equipment waste: rentals that keep billing past their return date, owned wheelchairs nobody can find, duplicate purchases of gear already sitting in a closet one floor up. The[median skilled nursing facility runs on a 1.8 percent operating margin](https://www.cms.gov/) , so that waste can equal most of a building's annual profit. Any tool you evaluate has to move that number. A tidier maintenance log does not, unless it also tells you where the billable equipment is. ## What UpKeep is, and where it genuinely wins


UpKeep is a well-built, mobile-first CMMS, and one of the highest-rated in its category. Its core job is maintenance management: create a work order, assign it to a technician, schedule recurring preventive maintenance, and keep a full repair and cost history for each asset. It layers on condition monitoring through sensors that watch vibration, temperature, and runtime, plus total-cost-of-ownership and depreciation reporting across multiple sites. Thousands of companies across manufacturing, energy, food service, fleet, and facilities management run on it, and its core plan starts around $24 per user per month.


The honest credit: for maintenance work, UpKeep is excellent, and a skilled nursing facility has real maintenance work. If you want to schedule preventive maintenance on HVAC units, boilers, and generators, dispatch repair tickets to your plant crew, and hold a clean audit trail of every fix, a CMMS like UpKeep is purpose-built for exactly that. Norra is not a full maintenance CMMS, and this piece will not pretend otherwise. On work-order management and preventive-maintenance scheduling, UpKeep wins.


## Where a CMMS falls short for equipment location


The gap opens the moment your question changes from "what needs fixing" to "where is it." UpKeep is designed around asset condition and maintenance history, not continuous physical position. It does not offer real-time location for equipment moving around a building. In UpKeep, an asset's location is a field a technician assigns, or the last place someone scanned its QR sticker. That is fine for a fixed boiler that never moves. It breaks for a wheelchair, an oxygen concentrator, or a low-air-loss mattress that changes rooms several times a week.


On a nursing floor, the update never happens. Nurses and aides already lose 30 to 60 minutes per shift hunting for equipment, and they are not going to stop and scan a QR label every time a pump rolls to a new room. So the location field drifts from reality within weeks, and a location you cannot trust is one you stop opening. This is the structural reason general asset and maintenance tools struggle in skilled nursing, and we lay it out in full in[why skilled nursing should not use general asset software](https://www.norra.io/blog/why-skilled-nursing-should-not-use-general-asset-software) .


That drift is where the money hides. The single largest equipment leak in skilled nursing is rentals that should have gone back: a facility keeps paying a daily rate on a rented mattress or wound-therapy pump long after the resident stopped needing it, because nobody can confirm the unit is sitting unused in a storage room. A maintenance CMMS cannot catch that, because catching it depends on knowing the live location and status of every billable item without asking anyone to do anything. That is the workflow Norra automates, and we walk through it in[how software stops duplicate rentals](https://www.norra.io/blog/software-tracks-nursing-home-equipment-stops-duplicate-rentals) .

## Can you use both?


Yes, and some operators do. The two tools do not overlap much. A CMMS answers "what maintenance is due and who is doing it." Norra answers "where is this equipment right now, and are we paying rent on something we already own." Running Norra for durable medical equipment location and a CMMS for building-systems maintenance is a coherent setup. The mistake is buying a maintenance CMMS to solve an equipment-location and rental-waste problem, because a work-order tool with a manual location field cannot close that gap. ## Why location accuracy matters for surveys


There is a compliance angle too. Equipment that is missing, mislabeled, or unsafe shows up in survey findings, and[F689, the accident-and-hazards tag](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/chapter-IV/subchapter-G/part-483) , is the most-cited F-tag in standard surveys, appearing in roughly a quarter of them. Being able to produce a live, room-level equipment picture on demand is a survey asset. A maintenance log of past repairs is useful, but it is not the same as showing a surveyor exactly where every piece of equipment is and confirming nothing is unaccounted for.
