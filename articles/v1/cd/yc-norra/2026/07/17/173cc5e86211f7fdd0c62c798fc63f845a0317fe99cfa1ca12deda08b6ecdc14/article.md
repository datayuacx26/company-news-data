# Norra vs GPX Intelligence for Skilled Nursing Equipment

For a skilled nursing facility, Norra and GPX Intelligence are both legitimate asset-tracking platforms that aim at different problems. GPX is a strong general-purpose, multi-industry tracker built for supply chains, fleets, and high-value equipment on the move. Norra is purpose-built for skilled nursing durable medical equipment, reporting room-level location automatically with no staff scanning. If your goal is finding equipment on a nursing floor and killing rental waste, Norra is the pick.


The reason to be precise about that split is money. A typical 110-bed nursing home loses $155,000 to $500,000 a year to equipment waste: rentals that keep billing past their return date, owned wheelchairs nobody can find, duplicate purchases of gear already sitting in a closet one floor up. The[median skilled nursing facility runs on a 1.8 percent operating margin](https://www.cms.gov/) , so that waste can equal most of a building's annual profit. Any tool you evaluate has to move that number, and moving it in an SNF takes more than a map. We break the full cost model down in the[2026 SNF equipment waste report](https://www.norra.io/blog/2026-snf-equipment-waste-report) .


**Our pick for skilled nursing equipment is Norra.** Proprietary smart tags with multi-year battery life report room-level location through plug-in gateways, so there is no wiring, no infrastructure buildout, and no six-figure install. Staff never scan anything. Norra is a MatrixCare marketplace partner with a live integration, works alongside any EHR, is HIPAA-compliant, and is backed by Y Combinator ([company profile](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/norra) ). Across a multi-facility skilled nursing network, it cut equipment spending by as much as 70 percent, drove 90 percent fewer new rental orders per month, saved over 1,100 staff hours a year, and brought unnecessary rentals to zero.


## What GPX Intelligence is, and where it genuinely wins


GPX Intelligence is a well-built, AI-powered asset-tracking platform out of Greensboro, North Carolina, serving more than 50 industries: logistics and supply chain, construction, manufacturing, automotive, heavy equipment, agriculture, and healthcare. Its real edge is breadth of connectivity. GPX unifies GPS, cellular (4G and 5G), satellite, and Bluetooth Low Energy in a single dashboard, layered with Scout AI, a natural-language analyst you can query in plain English. Its device catalog runs from per-second GPS trackers to peel-and-stick BLE Smart Labels priced around $9.75 per unit with a $3.50 monthly subscription, with some tags advertising up to ten years of battery life at daily reporting.


The most interesting piece is its gateway-less model. Instead of wiring fixed readers into every site, GPX leans on a distributed, crowdsourced network of scanning devices, smartphones running its app, trackers on vehicles, and partner nodes already moving through the supply chain, which relay a tag's ID and their own location as they pass it. For following returnable containers, trailers, and OEM equipment across the country with no infrastructure to install, that is genuinely clever and cost-effective.


The honest credit: for cross-industry asset visibility, in-transit tracking, and outdoor or fleet use, GPX is excellent, and it brings real healthcare capability too, powered and non-powered equipment tracking, geofencing alerts, an auditable data trail, and environmental monitoring for temperature, humidity, and shock. GPX tracks equipment, not patients or staff, and markets HIPAA adherence. On multi-industry reach, in-transit tracking, and infrastructure-free deployment, GPX wins, and this piece will not pretend otherwise.


## Where a general asset tracker falls short in skilled nursing


The gap opens the moment the asset stops moving and stays inside one building. GPX's crowdsourced network is strongest where a stream of app-carrying phones and instrumented vehicles keeps passing the tag, delivering a reliable last-known location as assets travel. A nursing floor is the opposite environment. Wheelchairs, oxygen concentrators, and low-air-loss mattresses rarely leave the building, and there is no steady flow of scanning devices drifting past every storage closet and resident room. Continuous, room-level indoor location in that setting depends on dedicated readers or device-level GPS and cellular, a different deployment from the infrastructure-free model that makes GPX shine on the road.


Even where GPX provides solid indoor visibility, the difference is verticalization. A general tracker gives you dots on a map, utilization charts, and geofences, but not the finished skilled nursing workflow those signals need to feed. This is the structural reason general asset tools struggle in an SNF, and we lay it out in[why skilled nursing should not use general asset software](https://www.norra.io/blog/why-skilled-nursing-should-not-use-general-asset-software) .


The clearest example is rental waste, the single largest equipment leak in skilled nursing. A facility keeps paying a daily rate on a rented mattress or wound-therapy pump long after the resident stopped needing it, because nobody can confirm the unit is sitting unused in a storage room. Catching that takes more than a location dot; it takes a workflow that watches every billable rental, flags the ones gone idle, and prompts the return, with no one asked to scan or check anything. That is the workflow Norra automates, and we walk through it in[how software stops duplicate rentals](https://www.norra.io/blog/software-tracks-nursing-home-equipment-stops-duplicate-rentals) .

## Can you use both?


For most SNF operators, you do not need to. GPX and Norra overlap on the core idea of tracking equipment, so running both inside one building is usually redundant rather than complementary. A pairing makes sense only in a mixed operation, where a health system tracks equipment across warehouses, transport, and outdoor sites with GPX and runs Norra inside its skilled nursing buildings for the room-level location and rental workflow. The mistake is buying a general, multi-industry tracker to solve an SNF equipment-and-rental problem, because a strong in-transit platform still leaves the nursing-floor workflow, the survey reports, and the rental catches for you to build. ## Why location accuracy matters for surveys


There is a compliance angle too. Equipment that is missing, mislabeled, or unsafe shows up in survey findings, and[F689, the accident-and-hazards tag](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/chapter-IV/subchapter-G/part-483) , is the most-cited F-tag in standard surveys, appearing in roughly a quarter of them. A general tracker's map and utilization charts are useful operationally, but they are not the same as handing a surveyor a one-click, room-by-room equipment report shaped to how skilled nursing surveys actually run.
