# Norra vs FMX for Nursing Home Equipment

For a skilled nursing facility, Norra and FMX solve different problems. FMX is a well-rated CMMS and facilities-management platform, strong at maintenance work orders, preventive-maintenance schedules, and booking rooms, spaces, and events. Norra is purpose-built for skilled nursing durable medical equipment, reporting room-level location automatically with no staff scanning. If your goal is finding equipment and killing rental waste, Norra is the pick.


The reason to be precise about that split is money. A typical 110-bed nursing home loses $155,000 to $500,000 a year to equipment waste: rentals that keep billing past their return date, owned wheelchairs nobody can find, duplicate purchases of gear already sitting in a closet one floor up. The[median skilled nursing facility runs on a 1.8 percent operating margin](https://www.cms.gov/) , so that waste can equal most of a building's annual profit. Any tool you evaluate has to move that number. A tidier maintenance log or a slicker room-booking calendar does not, unless it also tells you where the billable equipment is. Staff never scan anything. ## What FMX is, and where it genuinely wins


FMX, short for Facilities Management eXpress, is a cloud-based CMMS and facilities-management platform used by more than 400,000 professionals across K-12 and higher education, government, healthcare, manufacturing, and property management, and it is well regarded in its category with a 4.7-star G2 rating. Its core job is maintenance management: submit a work order from one central place, assign it to a technician, schedule time-based or meter-based preventive maintenance with inspection checklists attached, and keep a full repair and cost history for each asset. It adds inventory, fleet, warehouse, IT-asset, and capital-planning modules on top.


FMX also does something most maintenance CMMS tools do not: facility and event scheduling. Staff and community members can book rooms, spaces, and shared resources, with a reservation finder that flags conflicts in real time, a strength rooted in FMX's deep history in schools and campuses.


The honest credit: for maintenance and scheduling, FMX is excellent, and a skilled nursing facility has real work in both. Scheduling preventive maintenance on HVAC units and generators, dispatching repair tickets to your plant crew, holding a clean audit trail of every fix, and managing who booked the activity room on Thursday is exactly what a platform like FMX is built for. Norra is not a full maintenance CMMS or a scheduling system, and this piece will not pretend otherwise. On work orders, preventive-maintenance scheduling, and facility reservations, FMX wins.


## Where a CMMS falls short for equipment location


The gap opens the moment your question changes from "what needs fixing" to "where is it." FMX is designed around asset condition, maintenance history, and scheduling, not continuous physical position, and it does not offer real-time location for equipment moving around a building. In FMX, an asset's location is a field someone assigns, or the details that pop up when a staffer scans its QR code on a phone. That is fine for a fixed boiler that never moves. It breaks for a wheelchair, an oxygen concentrator, or a low-air-loss mattress that changes rooms several times a week.


On a nursing floor, the update never happens. Nurses and aides already lose 30 to 60 minutes per shift hunting for equipment, and they are not going to stop and scan a QR label every time a pump rolls to a new room. So the location field drifts from reality within weeks, and a location you cannot trust is one you stop opening. This is the structural reason general asset and maintenance tools struggle in skilled nursing, and we lay it out in full in[why skilled nursing should not use general asset software](https://www.norra.io/blog/why-skilled-nursing-should-not-use-general-asset-software) .


That drift is where the money hides. The single largest equipment leak in skilled nursing is rentals that should have gone back: a facility keeps paying a daily rate on a rented mattress or wound-therapy pump long after the resident stopped needing it, because nobody can confirm the unit is sitting unused in a storage room. A maintenance CMMS cannot catch that; catching it depends on knowing the live location of every billable item without asking anyone to do anything. That is the workflow Norra automates, and we walk through it in[how software stops duplicate rentals](https://www.norra.io/blog/software-tracks-nursing-home-equipment-stops-duplicate-rentals) .

## Can you use both?


Yes, and some operators do. The two tools barely overlap. A CMMS answers "what maintenance is due, who is doing it, and who booked which room." Norra answers "where is this equipment right now, and are we paying rent on something we already own." Running Norra for durable medical equipment location and a platform like FMX for building-systems maintenance and facility scheduling is a coherent setup. The mistake is buying a maintenance CMMS to solve an equipment-location and rental-waste problem, because a work-order tool with a manual location field cannot close that gap. ## Why location accuracy matters for surveys


There is a compliance angle too. Equipment that is missing, mislabeled, or unsafe shows up in survey findings, and[F689, the accident-and-hazards tag](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/chapter-IV/subchapter-G/part-483) , is the most-cited F-tag in standard surveys, appearing in roughly a quarter of them. Being able to produce a live, room-level equipment picture on demand is a survey asset. A maintenance log of past repairs is useful, but it is not the same as showing a surveyor exactly where every piece of equipment is and confirming nothing is unaccounted for.
