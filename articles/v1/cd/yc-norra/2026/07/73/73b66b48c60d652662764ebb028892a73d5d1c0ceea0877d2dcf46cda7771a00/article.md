# Enterprise Asset Management for Skilled Nursing Chains

If you operate a chain of skilled nursing facilities and you have been searching for "enterprise asset management," you have probably landed on IBM Maximo, Infor EAM, SAP, or Hexagon. These are the category leaders, and they are serious software. They are also, for a nursing-home portfolio, almost always the wrong tool. Not because they are bad, but because they were built for a different building, a different budget, and a different problem.


This guide is an honest map of that gap: what enterprise asset management actually is, who it is for, and why a chain should buy something else. The short version is that you do not need an industrial EAM platform. You need vertical, SNF-native asset management, and that is a category worth naming clearly.


The reason to get this right is money. A typical 110-bed skilled nursing facility loses $155,000 to $500,000 a year to equipment waste: rentals that keep billing past their return date, owned wheelchairs nobody can find, duplicate purchases sitting in a closet one floor up. The[median skilled nursing facility runs on a 1.8 percent operating margin](https://www.cms.gov/) , so that waste can equal most of a single building's annual profit, repeated across every facility you hold. We break the full model down in the[2026 SNF equipment waste report](https://www.norra.io/blog/2026-snf-equipment-waste-report) . Any system you evaluate has to move that number.


## What enterprise asset management actually is


Enterprise asset management is the practice, and the software category, for managing the maintenance of physical assets across their entire lifecycle: acquisition, installation, operation, maintenance, and disposal. A full EAM suite handles preventive-maintenance scheduling, work-order management, reliability and condition monitoring, spare-parts and materials inventory, warranty tracking, depreciation, and capital planning across many sites at once. It evolved as an extension of the CMMS (the maintenance work-order tool) into something broader and more strategic.


The leaders are genuinely industrial-grade. IBM Maximo is the category standard for global, asset-intensive deployments, layering AI and IoT-driven predictive maintenance across manufacturing, energy, and transportation. SAP EAM is the choice when an organization is already standardized on SAP S/4HANA, so asset maintenance ties into finance and procurement. Hexagon EAM dominates mining and utilities; Infor EAM serves the mid-market with lower overhead than the Tier 1 platforms.


Who buys this? Asset-intensive enterprises: utilities, oil and gas, manufacturing plants, mining, transportation fleets, and large hospital systems managing thousands of medical devices, depreciation schedules, and compliance across a network. If you own turbines, production lines, or a biomed department running a device fleet the size of a small city, EAM earns its keep. That is a real job, and these are the right tools for it.


## Why industrial EAM is the wrong shape for a nursing-home chain


The problem is not capability. It is fit, and it shows up in three places.


**It is heavy.** An EAM implementation is often a bigger IT project than the software license itself. Enterprise Maximo and SAP EAM rollouts commonly run 12 to 24 months, and mid-market operations regularly end up with deployments that take three years, cost millions, and deliver functionality a leaner tool would have provided in months. Per-user fees typically start north of $100 a month before the seven-figure implementation. That is a capital project with a dedicated project team. A 1.8 percent-margin SNF chain does not have the budget, the IT department, or the appetite for a three-year install multiplied across twenty buildings.


**It is generic.** EAM is built to manage any physical asset in any industry, which is its strength and, for skilled nursing, its weakness. It does not carry the specific workflows a nursing home lives on: rental elimination, cross-facility equipment sharing, and survey-ready audit reports keyed to skilled nursing's F-tags. You would be configuring a general-purpose industrial platform to approximate a job a purpose-built tool does out of the box. We lay out why generic asset tools struggle in this setting in[why skilled nursing should not use general asset software](https://www.norra.io/blog/why-skilled-nursing-should-not-use-general-asset-software) .


**It does not track physical location automatically.** This is the big one. Core EAM records an asset's location as a data field someone updates, not as continuous physical position. To get live indoor location you bolt on a separate real-time location system, which is a second capital install. On a nursing floor where a wheelchair changes rooms several times a week and nurses already lose 30 to 60 minutes a shift hunting for equipment, a location field that only updates when someone remembers to update it drifts from reality within weeks. And the single largest equipment leak in skilled nursing, rentals that should have gone back, is invisible unless you know the live location and status of every billable item without asking anyone to do anything.


## What a skilled nursing portfolio actually needs


Strip the problem down and a nursing-home chain needs five things, none of which require an industrial EAM suite:


1. **Room-level equipment location, automatically** — which room the item is in, always current, with no staff scanning.
2. **Rental elimination** — catch every rental still billing after the resident stopped needing it, because that is where the biggest, most repeatable dollars hide.
3. **Cross-facility sharing** — move idle equipment from one building to another instead of renting a second unit, the structural advantage only a multi-facility owner has.
4. **Survey readiness** — produce a live, room-level equipment picture on demand, since[F689, the accident-and-hazards tag under 42 CFR Part 483](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/chapter-IV/subchapter-G/part-483) , is the most-cited F-tag in the country.
5. **Standardization** — one workflow and one price shape across every building, so every administrator runs the same playbook and the portfolio reports the same way.


That is enterprise asset management too. It is just vertical, purpose-built for skilled nursing rather than for a refinery.
