# Sortly Alternatives for Nursing Home Equipment Tracking

If you run a skilled nursing facility and Sortly is not keeping up with your equipment, the best alternative depends on what you track. For durable medical equipment that moves between rooms all day, Norra is the SNF-native pick: it reports room-level location automatically with no staff scanning. Asset Panda, AssetTiger, EZOfficeInventory, and UpKeep each fit a narrower job.


Here is the honest version, because Sortly is a good product. It is one of the cleanest, most affordable inventory apps on the market, and for the right job it is hard to beat. The problem is not quality. It is fit. A nursing floor breaks the one assumption every scan-and-edit inventory app is built on: that a person will keep the record current. Below is where Sortly still earns its place, why it strains against skilled nursing equipment, and the alternatives worth a look, each with the job it actually does best.


## What Sortly is, and where it fits


Sortly is a visual, folder-based inventory app used by more than 15,000 businesses across construction, warehousing, retail, education, medical offices, and a dozen other verticals. You create a record for any item, add custom fields and high-resolution photos, and organize everything into nested folders. The mobile app turns a phone into a barcode and QR scanner, so counts and check-in/check-out go quickly. It adds quantity alerts when stock runs low, date-based alerts for maintenance, a move workflow with full activity history, and cloud sync across phone, tablet, and desktop. Plans scale by item count, from a free 100-item tier up through enterprise.


The honest credit: for a small, mostly static catalog, Sortly is excellent and inexpensive. If you are tracking supplies, consumables, tools, or back-office assets that stay put, it is one of the best simple tools you can buy, and switching away from it would be a mistake.


## Why Sortly strains on a nursing floor


The catch is structural, and it is not a knock on the software. Sortly updates through exactly three paths: manual entry, a barcode or QR scan, or a photo. It uses no sensors, so there is no automatic location. Every location it shows was put there by a person, which means the map is exactly as accurate as staff discipline: every item, every move, forever.


On a nursing floor that discipline does not hold. Nurses and aides already lose 30 to 60 minutes per shift hunting for equipment. Asking them to stop and scan a wheelchair every time it rolls to a new room is added work during the exact moments they have none to spare. Within a few weeks the database drifts from reality, and a location system you cannot trust is one you stop opening. We lay out the full argument in[why skilled nursing should not use general asset software](https://www.norra.io/blog/why-skilled-nursing-should-not-use-general-asset-software) .


That drift is where the money hides. A typical 110-bed nursing home loses[155,000 to 500,000 dollars a year](https://www.norra.io/blog/2026-snf-equipment-waste-report) to equipment waste: rentals that keep billing past their return date, owned wheelchairs nobody can find, duplicate purchases of gear already sitting in a closet one floor up. The median skilled nursing facility runs on a 1.8 percent operating margin, so that waste can equal most of the building's annual profit. The single largest leak is rentals that should have gone back, and catching it depends on knowing the live location and status of every billable item without asking anyone to do anything. A scan-and-edit catalog cannot do that by design.

### 2. Asset Panda, the flexible general-purpose catalog


Asset Panda is a well-built horizontal asset tracker. You create a record for any kind of asset, add custom fields, attach warranty documents, and check items in and out by scanning a label. It is flexible and inexpensive to start, and it works across IT hardware, tools, vehicles, and general equipment. Like Sortly, it has no live location layer, so it depends on staff scanning every item on every move.


**Best for:** organizations that need a customizable catalog across many asset types on a small budget, and can enforce scanning discipline.


### 3. AssetTiger, the free-tier option


AssetTiger offers a genuinely free plan for up to 250 assets, with barcode scanning, check-in/check-out, maintenance schedules, and depreciation tracking. For a single facility that wants a no-cost asset list and has someone who will keep it current, it is the lowest-cost entry point in this list. It shares the same ceiling: location is only ever as fresh as the last manual scan.


**Best for:** budget-zero single facilities that need a basic asset register and have strong scanning habits.


### 4. EZOfficeInventory, the check-out-heavy tracker


EZOfficeInventory is stronger on reservations, check-out chains of custody, and service tickets than Sortly, which makes it a good fit where equipment is loaned out and returned on a schedule. It is more configurable and correspondingly more complex. Again, its location is manual, so on a floor where equipment moves without a check-out step, the same drift applies.


**Best for:** facilities with a formal check-out desk and staff who log every loan and return.


### 5. UpKeep, when the real need is maintenance


If the pain is not "where is it" but "is it serviced," UpKeep is a maintenance-first CMMS built around work orders, PM schedules, and technician workflows. It tracks assets in service of maintenance, not location. It is the right tool when your gap is preventive maintenance on plant and building equipment, and the wrong tool if you need to find a wandering wheelchair.


**Best for:** maintenance and facilities teams that need work orders and PM tracking more than live location.

## How to choose


The deciding question is whether the thing you track moves, and whether a person will reliably update it. If you are cataloging supplies, tools, or back-office assets that stay put, Sortly is a great, affordable answer and you should keep it. If you are tracking durable medical equipment that moves between rooms all day, the manual model is the wrong fit no matter how good the app is, because the accuracy depends on staff time you do not have.


For that case, look for four things: room-level location (you need the room, not sub-meter precision), no wiring buildout, an operating expense instead of a capital install, and a system that updates without staff scanning. That last one is the whole game on a nursing floor, and it is what we mean by[zero-workflow equipment tracking](https://www.norra.io/blog/zero-workflow-equipment-tracking-no-staff-scanning) .
