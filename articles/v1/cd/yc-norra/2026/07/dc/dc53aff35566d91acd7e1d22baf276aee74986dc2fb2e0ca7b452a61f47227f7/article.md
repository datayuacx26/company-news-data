# Norra Alternatives for Skilled Nursing Equipment Tracking

If you are evaluating Norra for skilled nursing equipment tracking, the honest set of alternatives falls into three groups: hospital-grade RTLS (CenTrak, Sonitor, Securitas Healthcare, Kontakt.io), general asset apps (Asset Panda, Sortly, AssetTiger, EZOfficeInventory, Snipe-IT), and outsourced equipment services like Agiliti. Each wins a specific case. For an SNF that wants room-level location with no staff scanning, Norra remains the purpose-built pick, and this guide says plainly where the others earn their place.


## The margin math every alternative has to beat


Before comparing tools, fix the number they all have to move. A typical 110-bed nursing home loses 155,000 to 500,000 dollars a year to equipment waste: rentals that keep billing past their return date, owned wheelchairs nobody can find, duplicate purchases of gear already sitting in a closet one floor up. The median skilled nursing facility runs on a 1.8 percent operating margin, roughly 200,000 dollars of profit on 100 beds. That means equipment waste alone can equal 77 to 150 percent of everything the building earns in a year. We break the full cost model down in the[2026 SNF equipment waste report](https://www.norra.io/blog/2026-snf-equipment-waste-report) .


The point is not the technology. It is that any alternative you pick has to recover that waste, not just produce a tidier list. That single test sorts the field faster than any feature grid.


## How to evaluate a Norra alternative


Four axes decide whether a tool fits a nursing floor rather than a hospital or a warehouse. Keep them in mind through the list below, because they explain the ranking.


1. **Location you can trust without effort.** You need to know which room an item is in right now, without asking anyone to scan it. A catalog that is only as current as staff discipline is not a location system.
2. **Room-level, not sub-meter.** Hospitals pay for sub-room certainty. A nursing home needs the room. Paying for clinical precision you will never use is wasted budget.
3. **An operating expense, not a capital project.** A 1.8 percent margin business needs a predictable monthly cost, not a six-figure wired install and a construction schedule.
4. **SNF workflows, not just a database.** Rental elimination, exit detection, and survey-ready reporting are what turn location data into recovered dollars. A general inventory table does not carry them.


## The best Norra alternatives for skilled nursing


### 1. Hospital-grade RTLS: CenTrak, Sonitor, Securitas Healthcare


These are the strongest real-time location systems on the market. CenTrak is the enterprise benchmark, with more than 2,000 healthcare deployments and sub-room precision built for operating-room workflow and infusion-pump tracking. Sonitor uses ultrasound positioning that does not pass through walls, so its room-and-bay certainty is excellent. Securitas Healthcare brings a deep safety and RFID portfolio used across large health systems. All three report location automatically with no scanning, which is the right model.


The reason SNF operators look past them is fit, not quality. They are built for hospitals, priced for hospitals, and installed like a hospital project: wired readers, a ceiling buildout, tens to hundreds of thousands of dollars of upfront capital, and months to go live. That is precision and infrastructure a nursing home pays for and does not need. We cover that gap in full in[room-level tracking without hospital RTLS costs](https://www.norra.io/blog/room-level-equipment-tracking-without-hospital-rtls-costs) .


**Best for:** hospitals and health systems that need sub-room clinical precision and have the capital budget and IT staff for a wired enterprise install.


### 2. Kontakt.io


Kontakt.io is a modern, cloud-era location platform focused on hospital care operations: asset tracking, staff duress, and patient flow. It is lighter than legacy wired RTLS and the technology is credible. Two things make it the wrong default for skilled nursing. Pricing is typically per-tag, which quietly discourages the full coverage you actually want, and the product is built around hospital operations rather than SNF economics or SNF workflows like rental elimination and survey reporting.


**Best for:** hospital care-operations teams that want a cloud-era location platform without legacy infrastructure.


### 3. General asset apps: Asset Panda, Sortly, AssetTiger, EZOfficeInventory


This is the group most SNF operators actually compare Norra against, because it is inexpensive and familiar. Asset Panda, Sortly, AssetTiger, and EZOfficeInventory are well-built horizontal inventory apps. You create a record for any asset, add custom fields and photos, and check items in and out from a phone by scanning a barcode or QR label. AssetTiger is free to start, Sortly is visual and simple, and EZOfficeInventory adds maintenance scheduling and reservations. Across IT hardware, tools, and general equipment, they do the job at the lowest upfront cost in this category.


The honest credit: on day-one price and raw flexibility, these apps win. The structural catch is not a knock on the software. A barcode app only knows where an item was the last time a human scanned it, so the map is exactly as accurate as staff discipline: every item, every move, forever. On a nursing floor that discipline does not hold. Nurses and aides already lose 30 to 60 minutes per shift hunting for equipment, and asking them to stop and scan a wheelchair on every move is added work during the moments they have none to spare. Within weeks the data drifts, and the single largest leak, a rental that should have gone back, is exactly what a stale catalog cannot catch. We lay out why in[why skilled nursing should not use general asset software](https://www.norra.io/blog/why-skilled-nursing-should-not-use-general-asset-software) .


**Best for:** single facilities with near-zero budget and unusually strong scanning discipline, or back-office assets that rarely move.


### 4. Snipe-IT


Snipe-IT is an open-source IT asset management system: hardware, software licenses, accessories, and consumables tracked through their lifecycle, with a mobile app for check-in and audits. It is genuinely good at what it was built for, and the open-source model appeals to teams that want to self-host and customize. It is an IT asset tool, not a location system and not a clinical-equipment product, so it shares the general-app limitation on a nursing floor: it knows where something was checked out, not where it is now.


**Best for:** IT teams that want an open-source, self-hosted asset and license tracker.


### 5. UpKeep


UpKeep is a mobile-first CMMS, a computerized maintenance management system. Its strength is work orders, preventive-maintenance scheduling, and parts inventory. If your primary problem is maintenance coordination rather than finding equipment, a CMMS is the right category. It is not a real-time location platform, and it does not carry rental elimination or survey reporting, so it solves a different problem than the one that drives most SNF equipment waste.


**Best for:** facilities whose main gap is maintenance work-order management, not equipment location.


### 6. Agiliti


Agiliti is not software you run. It is an outsourced medical-equipment service that manages rental, delivery, cleaning, maintenance, and biomed repair, largely for hospitals, across a nationwide fleet. For an organization that wants to hand the entire equipment-management function to a vendor, it is a serious option. The tradeoff is that you do not own a platform or the visibility. You get a managed service and, often, a rental relationship, rather than a live map of your own owned equipment and a workflow built to drive rental spend to zero.


**Best for:** operators that want to outsource equipment management as a full service rather than run their own tracking platform.
