# What are Push Notifications?

**Push notifications are clickable pop-up messages sent from apps or websites directly to a user's device, delivering real-time updates, alerts, and information even when the app isn't open.** They use push technology to instantly notify users about important events, enabling immediate engagement without requiring users to actively check for updates.


The number of worldwide smartphone users will surpass[7.9 billion by 2028](https://www.statista.com/statistics/330695/number-of-smartphone-users-worldwide/) . That’s no surprise, given that smartphones are becoming both cheaper and more necessary for shopping, dining, transportation, and more. More essential services are moving to smartphones, so consumers have little choice if they want to keep up with the times.


Times are changing, and that’s a good thing for businesses. Push notifications (also known as[push messages](https://www.magicbell.com/blog/what-are-push-notifications) or push alerts) are a powerful marketing tool for acquiring and retaining customers — all they need is a smartphone. Push services, such as Apple's APNs and Google's FCM, use backend infrastructure like google cloud to deliver notifications reliably across platforms and to any mobile device.


Push notifications weren’t always around, though. In June 2009, Apple launched the for its smartphones. Soon after, push notifications were developed and adopted on[android](https://www.magicbell.com/blog/what-is-an-android-push-notification-and-how-does-it-work) and ios devices, with mobile operating systems enabling this technology and making real-time communication possible across a wide range of mobile devices.


Push messages are an accepted part of downloading smartphone apps. Still, this relatively new frontier has a lot of benefits for brands that strategically leverage the power of push notifications. Push notifications are a key driver of customer engagement on any mobile device, helping brands enhance user experience, retention, and personalized interactions. Check out this guide to learn what push notifications are, why they're so helpful, and best practices to improve your push notifications.


## What Are Push Notifications?


*Photo by*[Jonas Leupe](https://unsplash.com/@jonasleupe) *from*[Unsplash](https://unsplash.com/photos/person-holding-black-android-smartphone-QQ2d5naZee0)


Push notifications are brief, clickable messages sent directly to users’ devices (mobile phones, tablets, or desktops) to deliver timely information, alerts, or updates. Push notifications appear even when users don’t have your app or website open, making them an effective way to reengage with your audience.


Push notifications display differently depending on the user’s smartphone and operating system (OS). For example, notification features and behaviors can vary between an iOS device and an Android device. Notification grouping on iOS devices and notification categories on Android help organize alerts, making it easier for users to manage notifications. Device messaging protocols, such as APNS for iOS and Firebase Cloud Messaging for Android, ensure notifications are delivered securely to the user's device. Generally, most smartphones support text-based notifications, while more advanced devices support rich media notifications with images and GIFs.


Instead of hoping users will check their crowded email inboxes (they probably won’t), you can send a push notification to engage in a less distracting environment. If you nail your messaging and timing, you’ll[improve user engagement](https://www.magicbell.com/blog/implementing-push-notifications-in-ios-apps) and foster brand loyalty at scale.


The neat thing about push notifications is that they’re incredibly versatile. Use cases vary by industry:


- **Retail:** Direct shoppers back to their abandoned carts with push notifications. These reminders are also great for alerting customers about flash sales or sending order updates.
- **Wellness:** Healthcare providers use push notifications to remind patients about their appointments, reducing no-shows. Fitness apps also use these notifications to remind users to log their workouts or get their daily steps in.
- **Banking:** Banks send push notifications for fraud alerts, stock updates, or bill reminders
- **News:** Some news websites send real-time push notifications that alert readers about breaking news or trending topics.
- **Travel:** Flight status updates, hotel booking reminders, and location-based offers are common use cases for push notifications in the hospitality industry.
- **Streaming services:** If you have Netflix, you’ve likely received a push notification about your favorite shows or recommended new releases. Many streamers send these notifications with live event reminders, show reminders, and personalized recommendations.


When reengaging users, keep in mind that Android users and iOS users may have different opt-in processes and notification experiences, which can affect how and when they receive your messages.

### Types of Push Notifications


Push notifications have so many applications, but not all push notifications are the same. Push notifications come in several forms, with the primary types being web push notifications, mobile push notifications, and desktop push notifications. Each type differs in terms of delivery platform, technology, and use case:


- **Web push notification:** These are the easiest notifications to send. They[work through the user’s browser](https://www.magicbell.com/web-push) , like Google Chrome or Firefox. Users have to opt in to receive these push notifications. However, there’s no need to create an expensive app to send[web notifications](https://www.magicbell.com/web-push) , making this a win for businesses on a budget. You can still reach subscribers even when they aren't actively browsing your site
- **Mobile push notification:** You need a native app to[send mobile push notifications](https://www.magicbell.com/mobile-push) , or you can use a[progressive web app](https://www.magicbell.com/blog/what-is-a-progressive-web-app) to deliver app-like push notifications through the browser. Native apps require not only developing your own app but also persuading users to download the app on their device. The upside is that these notifications access app data, which makes it easier to[offer a more personalized experience](https://www.magicbell.com/blog/implementing-push-notifications-in-ios-apps) . Mobile push notifications allow app publishers to send targeted messages to specific users based on their behavior or preferences. They can also use mobile features like geolocation or the phone’s camera. Additionally, mobile push notifications can include rich notifications, such as images, videos, or interactive elements, to enhance user engagement.
- **Desktop notification:** It might not sound as exciting as other types of push notifications, but a[desktop notification](https://www.magicbell.com/blog/building-a-react-notification-system) is perfect for targeting business-to-business (B2B) audiences and any user who prefers to use a desktop. Desktop notifications appear on users’ desktops, usually appearing as a desktop popup and display even when the app or browser isn’t open.


Web push notifications are great for re-engaging website visitors, mobile push notifications excel in delivering personalized and dynamic content, and desktop push notifications ensure users stay informed about essential updates on their computers. Choosing the right type (or combination) of push notifications will help you reach users at precisely the right time.

Desktop Notification Desktop apps Software updates or meeting reminders Works even when the browser or app isn’t open


### Push Notifications Versus Email and SMS


Push notifications are just one of many potential ways businesses can contact their customers. If you’re already investing in email campaigns and[text marketing (SMS)](https://www.magicbell.com/blog/what-are-sms-notifications-and-why-are-they-important) , you might wonder if push notifications are even worth your time.


The short answer is yes.


Email marketing is the standard in many industries. It[supports long-form content and even rich media](https://www.magicbell.com/blog/email-notification-design-the-essential-guide-to-getting-it-right) like images and attachments. It’s pretty easy to automate and forge regular touchpoints with subscribers.[Transactional emails](https://www.magicbell.com/blog/what-is-a-transactional-email-and-why-is-it-important) , for example, confirm a shopper’s purchase or let a customer know their order has been shipped.


However, there's a good chance no one will open — let alone read — the message you toiled so long on. Email open rates, which hover around[32%](https://mailchimp.com/resources/email-marketing-benchmarks/) , are much lower than push notifications and[SMS](https://www.magicbell.com/blog/what-are-sms-notifications-and-why-are-they-important) , making them less ideal for urgent messages.


SMS is a more personalized way to communicate with users, delivering short messages directly to their smartphones with fewer distractions. Open and response rates are *much* higher, with a[98% open rate](https://textellent.com/blog/sms-marketing-statistics/#:~:text=72%25%20of%20retailers%20prioritize%20SMS,for%20fast%20and%20personal%20updates.) . Still, you have to worry about texting rates and a lack of support for rich media.


Push notifications combine the best of email and SMS. They’re urgent, support rich media, and get high response rates. To receive notifications, users must opt in and grant permission, especially on mobile devices. This opt-in process ensures that only engaged users, particularly mobile users, are targeted, allowing businesses to reach people on their smartphones with immediate, relevant updates that are more likely to be seen than emails.


In many cases, response rates are as high as[90%](https://wisernotify.com/blog/push-notification-stats/#:~:text=Push%20notifications%20have%20high%20open,agree%20to%20receive%20push%20notifications.) , giving SMS a run for its money. The good news is that you don’t have to choose between them — email, SMS, and push notifications all have a place in your outreach efforts. It’s all about knowing when to leverage each option for maximum engagement.

## Mobile Push Notifications


Mobile push notifications are a powerful way for businesses to connect directly with users on their smartphones and tablets. These notifications appear on users’ devices even when the app isn’t open, making them an essential tool for boosting user engagement and retention. With mobile push notifications, app publishers can send personalized messages, special promotions, and timely updates tailored to specific user segments and behaviors.


For example, a retail app might use mobile push notifications to remind users about items left in their shopping cart, or a travel app could send real-time flight updates. Mobile push notifications rely on robust push notification services like Apple Push Notification Service (APNs) for iOS devices and Firebase Cloud Messaging (FCM) for Android devices. These notification services ensure that messages are delivered quickly and securely to users’ devices, helping businesses stay top-of-mind and drive action.


By leveraging mobile push notifications, brands can deliver relevant content at the right moment, encourage users to return to their app, and create a more personalized experience that keeps users engaged over time.

## How Do Push Notifications Work?


*Photo by*[Dan Counsell](https://unsplash.com/@dancounsell) *from*[Unsplash](https://unsplash.com/photos/a-cell-phone-sitting-on-top-of-a-table-next-to-a-cup-of-coffee-ZsimLXUS_cY)


It’s a bit technical, but push notifications need two things to work:


- **Client application:** A client application is where your user receives and interacts with notifications. In this case, it’s a smartphone app or web application.
- **Application server:** An application server is the web server where you host the application. You have to send all information in the push notification to the server, which then sends the notification to your subscribers.


But that’s just the start. Devices and operating systems differ, so developers must learn how to work with the operating system’s Push Notification Service (OSPNS).[iOS and Android operating systems](https://idapgroup.com/blog/push-notifications-mechanism-for-ios-android/) have their own OSPNS that[developers use to deliver push notifications](https://www.magicbell.com/docs/channels/mobile_push) . This is how the process works once you get the basics down:


- **Build an application:** Once you understand the basics of how the OS works, you need to build an application with OSPNSs enabled that connects with iOS and[Android push notifications](https://www.magicbell.com/blog/what-is-an-android-push-notification-and-how-does-it-work) smartphones. The app publisher is responsible for configuring push notification services and ensuring the app is set up to receive notifications.
- **Register with the push service:** To access the respective operating systems' OSPNS, you must register with each OS's[push notification software](https://www.magicbell.com/mobile-push) . For Android devices, this often involves using backend infrastructure like Google Cloud, which supports notification delivery through services such as Firebase Cloud Messaging.
- **Grab your API:** After registering, you then[receive access to the API](https://www.magicbell.com/mobile-push) to communicate with the OSPNS. From there, package the push notification capability in the code for the client application and the instance on the app server.
- **User installation:** Once the end-user installs the app, the app and device register with OSPNS and generate a[unique ID](https://proandroiddev.com/how-to-generate-android-unique-id-38362794e1a8) . Users can manage notification permissions and preferences through their device settings, allowing them to control how and when they receive notifications. You can use this to tailor services and push notifications to different users.
- **Send the notification:** To send a notification, you can create each message manually and push it to the client app or use the OSPNS API to send automated messages. You can also create an elaborate workflow to[create tailored messages to individual users](https://www.magicbell.com/docs/libraries/magicbell-react) according to different parameters. These parameters include user demographics, subscription status, time of the day, usage pattern, location, etc. Device messaging protocols ensure secure and reliable delivery of notifications to user devices. You’ve got a lot of flexibility here — that’s the beauty of push notifications!


## Implementing Push Notifications


Implementing push notifications effectively requires a thoughtful strategy and the right technical setup. App publishers should begin by outlining their push notification strategy—deciding what types of push notifications to send, how often to send them, and which users to target. This planning stage is crucial for ensuring that notifications are relevant and welcomed by users, rather than disruptive.


Next, it’s important to set up the necessary infrastructure. This includes integrating a reliable push notification service and configuring an app server to manage and send notifications. App publishers must also ensure they obtain user permission before sending any push notifications, as respecting user preferences is key to building trust and maintaining high engagement rates.


Personalization is another best practice when implementing push notifications. Tailoring messages based on user behavior, preferences, or location can significantly improve user engagement. Additionally, limiting the frequency of notifications helps prevent alert fatigue and keeps users interested in receiving updates.


By following these best practices and regularly reviewing the performance of their push notification campaigns, app publishers can create a notification service that not only reaches users but also drives meaningful engagement and long-term retention.

## How to Enable Push Notifications


Enabling push notifications is a straightforward process that starts with user consent. When users first launch an app, they are typically prompted to grant permission for the app to send notifications. This can also be managed later through the app’s settings or the device’s notification preferences. Once users opt in, the app is authorized to send notifications directly to their device.


App publishers use push notification services like Apple Push Notification Service (APNs) for iOS devices and Firebase Cloud Messaging (FCM) for Android devices to manage and deliver notifications. These services provide the necessary APIs and SDKs to send notifications efficiently and securely to users on both platforms. Cloud providers also offer higher-level services like[AWS Pinpoint](https://www.magicbell.com/blog/what-is-aws-pinpoint) that add segmentation and campaign management on top of these push delivery systems.


By enabling push notifications, users can receive timely and relevant updates—such as breaking news, order confirmations, or personalized offers—right on their mobile devices. For app publishers, enabling push notifications is a powerful way to send notifications that drive user engagement, encourage app usage, and keep users informed about important updates.

## Common Push Notification Challenges


While push notifications have many benefits, developers must[get notifications right](https://www.magicbell.com/blog/in-app-notification-design) to prevent customer churn. Users can manage their notification preferences through device settings, and different mobile operating systems offer various options for notification management and permissions. Let’s dig into the most common challenges of push notifications and how to address them.


### Alert Fatigue


Too many notifications can lead to alert fatigue; in fact,[61% to 78% of users](http://go.leanplum.com/rs/959-TQV-890/images/Inside_the_Best_Mobile_Communications_Brands_LP.pdf) delete applications that send them too many.


The likelihood that users will delete apps that send too many messages varies by generation. Millennials are most likely to delete apps that send too many notifications (78%), and Baby Boomers are least likely to delete apps that send too many (61%). Gen Z falls somewhere in the middle, with 67% of Gen Z-ers deleting applications that send too many notifications.


On the other hand, too few notifications can have the same effect, with 95% of new app users likely to churn if they don't receive any notifications within the first 90 days. Find the right balance of notifications — by segmenting users, sending timely notifications, and creating a personalized experience — to engage users while[avoiding alert fatigue](https://www.magicbell.com/blog/help-your-users-avoid-notification-fatigue)


### User opt-ins


You can’t send push notifications if people don’t opt in — that’s the law. Push notifications still have the same challenge as email and SMS lists because you must persuade users to opt in. No one will receive your push notifications if you don’t convince people to subscribe.


The best way to boost your list is to focus on the value of subscribing. Offer new subscribers discounts or coupons, access to subscriber-only deals or content, and other perks that give people a reason to say yes.


Also, don't send users a popup when they first open your app or visit your site asking for an opt-in. Give them time to engage with you before asking for their information. This approach warms the relationship and helps people see your notifications as helpful, not a nuisance.


### Bad timing or irrelevant messaging


Nobody wants to receive a notification at 2 AM. They also don't want to receive a flurry of messages about fly fishing when they've never gone fishing a day in their life.[Timing and relevance](https://www.magicbell.com/blog/help-your-users-avoid-notification-fatigue) have a huge impact on engagement.


Notifications sent at the wrong time or with irrelevant content can disrupt users, leading to annoyance or disengagement. Implement behavior-based triggers (e.g., abandoned cart alerts) and time-zone targeting to ensure notifications arrive at the right moment.


## 6 Push Notification Best Practices


Push notifications can be an effective addition to your business, but simply setting up notifications isn’t enough. Using push notifications strategically is crucial to maximize their effectiveness and avoid common pitfalls. You don’t want people to ignore you, but you also don’t want to annoy subscribers. Follow these best practices to[craft notifications that drive action](https://www.magicbell.com/blog/examples-of-transactional-email-templates) while improving the user experience.


### 1. Master your timing and frequency


When and how often you send notifications has a significant impact on engagement.


Sending notifications when people are most likely to engage, like during active browsing hours, improves open rates and conversions. For example, a fitness app might send workout reminders in the morning when users are more likely to exercise. You can also master timing by setting up behavior-based triggers, like pinging users when they're close to a retail location.


Other common triggered messages include:


- **Abandoned cart reminder:** "Still thinking it over? The items in your cart are waiting!"
- **Milestone achievement:** "Congrats! You've completed 50 workouts this month — keep it up!"
- **Re-engagement campaign:** "We've missed you! Here's a 15% discount to welcome you back."
- **Transaction alerts:** "Your order has shipped and is on its way."


However, keep in mind that too many notifications will backfire. Always implement frequency caps, especially if you use automation or trigger-based messaging. These caps limit the number of notifications a user receives in a day or week, preventing overwhelm.


Check your analytics if you aren't sure when or how many messages to send. Many messaging platforms will tell you how many people interacted with your push notifications and when. Gather more of this data over time to make informed decisions about future push notification flows.


### 2. Personalize Content


[Seventy-eight percent of people](https://instapage.com/blog/personalization-statistics/#:~:text=Content%20personalization%20statistics,-Individual%20customer%20stakeholders&text=78%25%20of%20U.S.%20Internet%20users,they%20feel%20about%20a%20brand.) say personalized content increases purchase intent. Translation: users are more likely to buy more stuff if you take the time to customize notification content to their experience.


Instead of sending generic messages to your entire audience, target them with tailored content. Segment users based on their behaviors, preferences, and demographics. For example, you can categorize users by location, purchase history, or app engagement levels to create notifications that align with their needs and interests.


Yes, this means you have to craft separate messages for each segment. That takes more effort, but the payoff is worth the time spent crafting personalized messages.


### 3. Tweak Your Copywriting


- **Create a sense of urgency:** Push notifications are excellent for real-time communication. Your copy should communicate a sense of urgency to drive immediate action. Time-sensitive phrases like “limited time offer” or “ends today” compel people to answer right away.
- **Keep it short:** The shorter the text, the better. Keep it under 140 characters so the user's phone doesn't cut off your message. Streamline the message with a single call to action and avoid cramming too much information into a single notification. If you need to share more information, the CTA should direct the user to a landing page with additional details.
- **Be clever or funny:** You need to know your audience for this one. But if they would appreciate it, throw in a few light-hearted puns or pop culture references to turn more heads.


Not sure whether your copy resonates with subscribers? Conduct an A/B test (also known as a split test). With this option, you send different versions of a message to two small groups of people in the same segment, measure their engagement rates, and send the winning message to the rest of the people in the segment. Over time, you'll learn which messaging your audience likes best.


### 4. Always Request Opt-Ins


Legally, you need explicit permission to send push notifications. Plus, gathering consent frames the notification experience as a valuable service instead of an annoyance.


Create a pre-permission strategy that gets subscribers in the right frame of mind. Display a pre-permission prompt as a soft ask first. This approach lets users understand the benefits of enabling notifications and prepares them for the actual request, increasing the chances of a positive response.


You can increase subscriptions by:


- Explaining the value of subscribing upfront, like accessing exclusive discounts or sales.
- Triggering pre-permission prompts at relevant moments — such as after a user adds an item to their cart — when they're more inclined to opt in.
- Offering a simple yes/no approach to allow users to choose whether they want to subscribe now or later.
- Incentivizing new opt-ins with a limited-time coupon offer or perk like free shipping.


### 5. Test Your Push Notifications


Always test your push notifications before sending them to your users. There are various testing tools you can use to evaluate your message content and design.


[Webpushtest.com](http://webpushtest.com/) is a free tool that provides a demo of standards-based web push notifications across all platforms, including iOS. With free testing tools at your disposal, there's no excuse for sending poorly designed messages or messages with errors.


### 6. Review Your Performance


You put a lot of time and resources into push notifications, and you deserve to see results from your hard work. Measuring the success of your push notification campaigns is essential to understand what works, optimize future efforts, and drive user engagement. Use key metrics to track how users interact with notifications, and leverage A/B testing and analytics tools to refine your strategy continually.


Every organization is different, but we recommend tracking metrics like:


- Open rate
- Click-through rate (CTR)
- Conversions
- Engagement rate
- Opt-ins
- Opt-outs
- Retention rate


You can easily measure this data with platforms like Google Analytics,[Firebase](https://www.magicbell.com/blog/how-to-use-firebase-to-send-push-notifications) , OneSignal, and Braze. Integrating these tools with your mobile app or website makes it a cinch to monitor real-time performance and spot trends.


Tracking data is important, but action matters more. Let the data guide your efforts for future campaigns. Learn from past performance to design more effective push notifications going forward.

### What strategies can I use to increase the click-through rate of my push notifications?


You can increase click-through rates and engagement by:


- Segmenting your subscriber list and personalizing notification copy
- Adding rich media, like photos and GIFs
- Keeping messages short and limited to a single call-to-action
- A/B testing to see which messages perform better
- Sending time-sensitive messages like “limited-time offer” or “only five left in stock”


### How can I make my push notifications more transparent and trustworthy?


Users had to trust your business to opt into notifications in the first place. Preserve that trust by:


- Always obtaining explicit consent to send notifications. Some companies require two-step opt-ins to ensure users know what they're signing up for
- Avoiding misleading or clickbait content
- Providing clear opt-out instructions
- Allowing users to customize their communication preferences
- Clarifying who is sending the message. Display your logo or brand name in every notification
