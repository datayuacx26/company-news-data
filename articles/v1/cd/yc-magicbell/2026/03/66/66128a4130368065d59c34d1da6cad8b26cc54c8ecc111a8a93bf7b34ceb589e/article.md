# What Are SMS Notifications, and Why Are They Important?

**SMS notifications are short text messages sent to mobile phones with timely alerts, updates, and reminders.** They are not marketing texts. They are event-based messages with 98% open rates that reach users with no internet or app needed.


Businesses use many digital channels to reach their customers. These tools help them engage in a reliable, low-cost way. Old-school marketing can be pricey. If you lack a large ad budget, you need a cheaper way to share info with your audience. That is where mobile messaging helps. SMS messages max out at 160 characters. So clear and short wording is key.


Tools like[push notifications](https://www.magicbell.com/blog/what-are-push-notifications) , iMessage, or SMS texts all reach your audience in real-time. If you want a low-cost way to connect and grow your business, SMS may be the answer.


## Defining SMS Text Messaging


SMS stands for Short Message Service. It is the most common way to send texts on mobile devices. People use SMS to send key updates and alerts. Phones and tablets on a cell network can send and get SMS texts. SMS needs cell data. It cannot be sent over Wi-Fi alone. You need a valid phone number to send and get messages.


Even iPhones use SMS to send texts to Android devices. The bubbles turn green when sent through SMS or MMS (Multimedia Message Service). A message counts as received once it shows in the messaging app.


SMS messages can only have 160 text characters. Photos, video, audio, or other rich media get sent as MMS. SMS is used for timely updates, alerts, and outreach across many industries.


### SMS vs MMS vs RCS


Knowing the differences between formats helps you choose the right one:


**SMS (Short Message Service):**


- Text-only messages
- 160 character limit (standard encoding)
- Works on all mobile devices
- Lowest cost per message


**MMS (Multimedia Message Service):**


- Supports images, videos, audio, GIFs
- Up to 1600 characters of text
- Needs data or MMS-capable plan
- Costs more (about 3x SMS)
- Not all phones support MMS


**RCS (Rich Communication Services):**


- Rich media messaging
- Read receipts, typing indicators
- Buttons and carousels
- Needs RCS-enabled device and carrier
- Mostly Android only
- Falls back to SMS if not supported


**Tip:** Use SMS for the widest reach. Add MMS only for high-value visual content when your audience supports it.


## What Are SMS Notifications?


Now that we know what SMS text messaging is, let us look more closely at SMS notifications.


SMS notifications are alerts that let businesses reach their audience. When an SMS is sent, it shows up on the user's phone. The user also sees a notification. It can pop up in several places:


- **Lock screen** - Visible even when the phone is locked
- **Messaging app** - Default SMS app (Messages, Samsung Messages, etc.)
- **Notification center** - Stays until dismissed
- **Badge** - Red dot on the messaging app icon


This is why SMS has such high open rates. Users see the message many times before they unlock their device.


The SMS goes right to the user's device. You don't need them to check your app or visit your site. SMS sends timely updates right to the user's inbox. This means key info reaches users fast. Make sure users have opted in before you send messages. Users can control when and where they get texts in their device Settings. You can track how many messages are sent for review and compliance.


### Key Traits of SMS Notifications


**Direct Delivery:**
SMS goes right to users' phones. They don't need to check an app, visit a site, or open email. This makes SMS great for time-sensitive messages.


**Works on Any Device:**
Push notifications need an app install. Email needs internet. SMS works on any phone with cell service. This ranges from the latest iPhone to basic feature phones. SMS is the most open channel out there.


**Character Limits:**
Standard SMS allows 160 characters with GSM-7 encoding. Messages with emojis or special characters use UCS-2 and allow only 70. Longer messages split into segments. Each segment is charged on its own.


**Opt-In Required:**
Users must opt in to get SMS from businesses. This creates a high-intent audience. People who get your texts chose to hear from you. This leads to higher engagement than channels with passive opt-ins.


**Two-Way Messaging:**
SMS is not one-way. Users can reply. This makes it great for customer service, appointment confirmations, and campaigns.

## SMS Notifications vs Push Notifications


SMS and push notifications serve different roles. Here are the key differences between these two channels.


### Delivery and Requirements


**SMS Notifications:**


- Sent via cell network (works with no internet)
- Needs a phone number (personal info)
- Works on any mobile device, even feature phones
- No app install needed
- Carriers charge per message


**Push Notifications:**


- Sent via internet (needs Wi-Fi or data)
- Works through OS push services (APNS, FCM)
- Needs app install (mobile push) or site permission (web push)
- Free to send (just hosting costs)


### Comparison Table


Feature SMS Push Notifications


**Character Limit** 160 (standard) / 70 (Unicode) ~40 best / 240 max


**Rich Media** No (needs MMS) Yes (images, videos, GIFs)


**Buttons** No (must use links) Yes (built-in)


**Deep Links** Yes (URL only) Yes (in-app linking)


**Cost** $0.0075-$0.01 each Free


**Open Rate** 98% 90%


**Works Offline** Yes (queued) No (needs internet)


### When to Use SMS vs Push


**SMS wins when:**


- Time-critical alerts (OTP codes, fraud alerts)
- Order updates (shipping, appointments)
- Users don't have your app
- Older users who prefer SMS
- Two-way chats are needed
- Guaranteed delivery matters


**Push wins when:**


- App engagement and retention
- Rich media (images, videos)
- High-frequency updates
- Cost-sensitive bulk messaging
- In-app actions needed
- Content tips and suggestions


**Use Both:**
Most strong strategies use both channels:


- Send push first
- If not opened in 2 hours, send SMS backup
- Or: Push for low-urgency, SMS for high-urgency
- Or: Let users pick their channel in settings

### Step 2: Get Phone Numbers


**Number Types:**


**Long Code (10-digit number):**


- $1-2/month per number
- 1 message/second speed
- Best for: Low volume, two-way chats


**Toll-Free Number (1-800, 1-888, etc):**


- $2-5/month per number
- ~3 messages/second speed
- Best for: Medium volume, pro look


**Short Code (5-6 digits):**


- $500-$1000/month + setup fees
- 100+ messages/second speed
- Best for: High volume, marketing


**10DLC Registration:**
In 2023, US carriers began requiring A2P registration for long code texting. This verifies your business, logs your use cases, and sets speed limits based on trust score.


### Step 3: Get Consent (TCPA Compliance)


**Valid Consent Requires:**


- Written consent (checkbox, web form, keyword opt-in)
- Clear notice of who sends and how often
- Msg&data rates notice
- Opt-out info (Reply STOP)
- Links to terms of service and privacy policy


**These Do Not Count as Consent:**


- Pre-checked boxes
- Buying alone (you need separate marketing consent)
- Giving a phone number for other reasons
- "Soft opt-in" with no clear agreement


**Keep Records:**
Store for each user: phone number, consent time, consent method, IP (if online), consent text shown. Keep records for 4+ years (TCPA rule).


For full compliance details, see our[SMS notification best practices](https://www.magicbell.com/blog/sms-notification-best-practices) .


### Step 4: Handle Opt-Outs


**Required Keywords to Honor:**


- STOP, STOPALL, UNSUBSCRIBE, CANCEL, END, QUIT


**What You Must Do:**


- Process within 10 seconds
- Add to block list right away
- Send a confirmation message
- Send no more messages (unless they text START to opt back in)


## SMS Notification Best Practices


To boost engagement while staying compliant and trusted:


- Get clear written consent. Honor opt-outs right away.
- Send messages Tue-Thu, 10am-8pm in the user's time zone.
- Stay under 160 characters. Include a clear call-to-action.
