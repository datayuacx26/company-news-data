# The 20 Most In-Demand Careers of the Next Decade and how to get into them

The[World Economic Forum estimates that by 2030](https://www.weforum.org/press/2025/01/future-of-jobs-report-2025-78-million-new-job-opportunities-by-2030-but-urgent-upskilling-needed-to-prepare-workforces/) , about 170 million new jobs will be created globally — while 92 million existing roles quietly disappear. So yes, some jobs are dying. But a whole wave of new ones is rising, and the people who position themselves early will have a serious edge.


The good news is that you don’t need to be a genius or have the “right” degree to break into in-demand careers. You just need direction, discipline, and the willingness to start before you feel ready.


Here are 20 careers (grouped by industry) that are already growing and will only keep growing through the rest of this decade.


## AI, Data & Analytics


*Probably the most talked-about space right now and for good reason.*


The entire world runs on data. Every time a bank detects a suspicious transaction, a streaming platform recommends a show, or a hospital predicts patient risk, there’s a data or AI professional behind it.[This industry is hiring fast](https://blog.getaura.ai/ai-hiring-trends) and still can’t hire fast enough.


### 1. AI & Machine Learning Specialist


Think of this as the person who builds the brain behind the machine. AI and Machine Learning Specialists design, train, and refine systems that learn from data — from fraud detection engines to the recommendation algorithm deciding what you watch next.


**What qualifications do you need?** A degree in computer science, mathematics, or statistics is a strong foundation, but many successful practitioners come from self-taught backgrounds. What matters more is your ability to build and deploy working models.


**Key skills:** Python or R, machine learning frameworks (TensorFlow, PyTorch), natural language processing, cloud platforms, model ethics and bias testing.


**How to get started:** Start with free or low-cost courses — Andrew Ng’s Deep Learning Specialisation on Coursera is one of the most recommended. Build projects. Enter Kaggle competitions. Even a GitHub profile with three good, documented projects can open doors faster than a certificate.


**Annual Median Salary:** $120,000 – $160,000 (global/remote market)


### 2. Data Analyst


If AI specialists build the brain, Data Analysts are the translators, they turn raw, messy data into clear, actionable insights that managers and executives can actually use to make decisions.


**What qualifications do you need?** No single degree is required. People come into this role from economics, accounting, psychology, and even humanities backgrounds. A diploma or certification in data analytics can work just as well as a degree if paired with strong portfolio projects.


**Key skills:** Excel, SQL, Python or R, Power BI or Tableau, statistical thinking, storytelling with data.


**How to get started:** Build case studies using public datasets — think national sales data, e-commerce datasets, or even your own personal finance records. Platforms like Google Data Analytics Certificate (Coursera) are beginner-friendly and employer-recognised. Nigerian companies in telecoms, banking, retail, and healthcare are actively hiring.


**Annual Median Salary:** $65,000 – $100,000 (global/remote market)


### 3. Big Data Specialist


This is data work at industrial scale. While a Data Analyst might work with a spreadsheet of thousands of rows, Big Data Specialists handle datasets so large that standard tools can’t even process them. Think: every transaction across a national bank in one quarter.


**What qualifications do you need?** Typically a degree in computer science, data engineering, or statistics. However, specialised certifications in tools like Apache Spark, Hadoop, or cloud platforms (AWS, Google Cloud) can substitute or complement formal education.


**Key skills:** SQL, Python, distributed computing systems, cloud data platforms, data pipeline design.


**How to get started:** Start with foundational data analytics, then layer in tools like DataCamp’s data engineering track. Look for internships or junior data engineering roles at tech companies or banks with large data operations.


**Annual Median Salary:** $100,000 – $140,000 (global/remote market)


### 4. Operations Research & Optimisation Analyst


These are the people who figure out the most efficient way to do anything such as how to route delivery trucks, how to schedule hospital staff, how to manage supply chain disruptions. It’s maths meeting real-world problem solving.


**What qualifications do you need?** A degree in mathematics, industrial engineering, economics, or statistics. Postgraduate study helps significantly for more advanced roles.


**Key skills:** Mathematical modelling, simulation, scenario planning, forecasting, advanced analytics software.


**How to get your foot in the door:** Internships in logistics companies, airlines, or supply chain firms are natural entry points. Look for roles labelled “business analyst,” “logistics analyst,” or “supply chain analyst” — these often do similar work.


**Annual Median Salary:** $80,000 – $100,000 (global/remote market)


## Software, Fintech & Digital Infrastructure


*Technology roles now account for more than half of the fastest-growing jobs globally.*


Every business — from a corner pharmacy to a multinational bank — now depends on software. That makes people who build and protect digital infrastructure some of the most valuable workers on earth.


### 5. Full-Stack Software Developer


Full-stack developers build both the visible front-end of digital products (what you see and click) and the back-end infrastructure (what makes everything actually work). They’re the architects of apps, platforms, and web systems used by millions of people daily.


**What qualifications do you need?** A computer science degree is common, but the field is increasingly portfolio-driven. Bootcamp graduates and self-taught developers regularly land strong roles when their work is visible and demonstrable.


**Key skills:** JavaScript, Python, React or Vue, Node.js, cloud computing, Git, API design, Agile development.


**How to get your foot in the door:** Free platforms like freeCodeCamp and The Odin Project are excellent starting points. Build real projects — a working web app beats any certificate on its own. Put everything on GitHub. Contribute to open-source projects to get noticed.


**Annual Median Salary:** $95,000 – $130,000 (global/remote market)


### 6. Cybersecurity Analyst / Digital Trust Officer


Every company that stores data — which is every company — needs someone protecting it. Cybersecurity Analysts monitor systems for threats, respond to attacks, and build the defences that keep organisations safe. As the digital world grows, so does the attack surface.


**What qualifications do you need?** Degrees in computer science, information security, or IT are common, but certifications can carry just as much weight in this field. CompTIA Security+, Certified Ethical Hacker (CEH), and CISSP are globally respected.


**Key skills:** Threat detection, network security, ethical hacking, incident response, cloud security, cryptography.


**How to get your foot in the door:** Start with free platforms like Cybrary or TryHackMe — both offer hands-on labs and beginner paths. CompTIA Security+ is a widely recognised entry-level cert. Look for IT support roles first, then you can transition into security.


**Annual Median Salary:** $95,000 – $120,000 (global/remote market)


### 7. Cloud Solutions Architect


Cloud computing is now the backbone of the digital economy. Cloud Solutions Architects design and manage the infrastructure that keeps businesses running in the cloud — everything from data storage to application deployment to disaster recovery.


**What qualifications do you need?** A background in IT, software engineering, or systems administration. Cloud certifications from AWS (Amazon Web Services), Google Cloud, or Microsoft Azure are practically essential and highly respected.


**Key skills:** Cloud platform architecture, networking, cybersecurity, coding (Python, Java), cost optimisation, infrastructure design.


**How to get your foot in the door:** AWS, Google Cloud, and Microsoft Azure all offer free training tiers and certifications. Start with foundational cloud certifications, build small cloud projects, and look for junior cloud engineer or DevOps roles as entry points.


**Annual Median Salary:** $120,000 – $160,000 (global/remote market)


### 8. FinTech Engineer / Blockchain Developer


Fintech is reshaping how money moves — payments, lending, investments, insurance. FinTech Engineers build the financial systems of the future: payment APIs, digital wallets, lending platforms, and blockchain-based infrastructure.


**What qualifications do you need?** Software engineering is the foundation. Specialisation in financial systems, payment processing, or blockchain architecture sets candidates apart. Relevant certifications in blockchain technology (like the Certified Blockchain Developer from Blockchain Council) add credibility.


**Key skills:** Software development (JavaScript, Python, C++), blockchain architecture, payment APIs, cryptography, financial system logic.


**How to get your foot in the door:** Start with general software development, then target internships or entry-level roles at fintech startups. In Nigeria, companies like Paystack, Flutterwave, and Moniepoint offer excellent early-career exposure. Build or contribute to a small blockchain project to demonstrate initiative.


**Annual Median Salary:** $110,000 – $150,000 (global/remote market)


## Green Economy & Sustainability


*The climate transition is now one of the biggest drivers of new jobs globally.*


Every company on earth is under pressure to reduce its environmental impact. That pressure is turning into jobs — lots of them. The green economy isn’t just for “tree huggers.” It’s a serious career path with serious money behind it.


### 9. Renewable Energy Engineer


Renewable Energy Engineers design and deploy the infrastructure powering the clean energy transition: solar farms, wind turbines, battery storage systems, and smart grids. In Nigeria, where power supply remains unreliable, this skill set is especially valuable.


**What qualifications do you need?** A degree in electrical, mechanical, or energy engineering. Postgraduate study in renewable energy systems is a strong differentiator. Short courses in solar PV systems, battery storage, and wind energy design are accessible entry points.


**Key skills:** Renewable energy system design, grid integration, energy storage, environmental standards compliance, data-driven energy modelling.


**How to get started:** In Nigeria, organisations like REAN (Renewable Energy Association of Nigeria) offer local training. Look for internships with solar energy companies, power engineering firms, or development organisations working on energy access projects.


**Annual Median Salary:** $80,000 – $110,000 (global/remote market)


### 10. Environmental Engineer


Environmental Engineers tackle waste management, water treatment, pollution control, and environmental compliance. As global regulations tighten around emissions and waste, companies need specialists who can keep them on the right side of the law — and the planet.


**What qualifications do you need?** A degree in civil, chemical, or environmental engineering. Certifications in Environmental Impact Assessment (EIA) and ISO 14001 (environmental management systems) significantly boost employability.


**Key skills:** Environmental impact assessment, water and waste systems, regulatory compliance, pollution modelling, project management.


**How to get started:** Look for internships at engineering consultancies, government environmental agencies, oil and gas companies (which have heavy environmental compliance obligations), and NGOs focused on environmental sustainability.


**Annual Median Salary:** $75,000 – $100,000 (global/remote market)


### 11. Sustainability Specialist / ESG Officer


Not everyone in the green economy is an engineer. Sustainability Specialists translate environmental goals into business strategy. They handle ESG (Environmental, Social, Governance) reporting, carbon accounting, and compliance with sustainability regulations — making sure a company’s “green” commitments are real, not just marketing.


**What qualifications do you need?** Almost any degree can be a starting point. What matters is pairing your existing education with sustainability certifications — such as those from the Global Reporting Initiative (GRI) or the Chartered Institute of Environmental Management.


**Key skills:** ESG reporting, carbon accounting, regulatory knowledge, stakeholder engagement, sustainability impact assessment, systems thinking.


**How to get your foot in the door:** Volunteer with environmental NGOs or climate-focused nonprofits to build experience. Look for corporate sustainability or CSR roles, which are often the gateway into dedicated ESG positions at larger organisations.


**Annual Median Salary:** $70,000 – $100,000 (global/remote market)

## Healthcare & the Care Economy


An ageing global population, expanding access to healthcare, and growing awareness around mental health are all fuelling serious demand in this sector. And unlike tech, care economy jobs are very difficult to automate away.


### 12. Nurse Practitioner / Advanced Practice Nurse


Nurse Practitioners are highly trained nurses who can diagnose conditions, prescribe treatments, and serve as primary care providers — especially in communities where doctors are scarce. Globally, demand is surging. Nigerian nurses are already benefiting from international recruitment, particularly in the UK, US, and Canada.


**What qualifications do you need?** An accredited nursing degree (B.Sc. Nursing or equivalent), followed by clinical experience and — for the Nurse Practitioner level — a Master’s in Nursing Practice. International licensure exams (like NCLEX for the US) open up global opportunities.


**Key skills:** Clinical diagnosis, patient communication, emotional intelligence, pharmacology, adaptability to digital health tools, multidisciplinary collaboration.


**How to get your foot in the door:** Start with accredited nursing education and seek clinical placement at teaching hospitals. For international practice, prepare for relevant licensure exams and explore programmes designed for internationally educated nurses.


**Annual Median Salary:** $115,000 – $130,000 (global/remote market)


### 13. Medical & Health Services Manager


Behind every well-run hospital is someone who isn’t a doctor or nurse — someone managing operations, budgets, compliance, and strategy. Medical and Health Services Managers ensure the business side of healthcare actually works, and with the rise of digital health, their role is becoming even more complex and valuable.


**What qualifications do you need?** A degree in health administration, public health, or business administration. Many practitioners have a clinical background combined with an MBA or a Master’s in Health Services Management.


**Key skills:** Health informatics, regulatory compliance, financial management, strategic planning, leadership, familiarity with electronic health records (EHR) systems.


**How to get your foot in the door:** Internships or administrative roles in hospitals or health clinics provide a strong foundation. Look for graduate programmes in health administration, or consider professional certifications from bodies like the American College of Healthcare Executives (ACHE).


**💰 Annual Median Salary:** $100,000 – $120,000 (global/remote market)


### 14. Social Worker & Mental Health Counsellor


Mental health is no longer whispered about — it’s a growing public health priority. Social workers and counsellors are increasingly embedded in schools, workplaces, hospitals, and NGOs. As awareness grows, so does demand.


**What qualifications do you need?** A degree in social work, psychology, or counselling. Postgraduate study and professional licensure (such as LCSW or equivalent) are typically required for clinical practice. Specialised certifications in trauma-informed care, addiction counselling, or cognitive behavioural therapy add significant value.


**Key skills:** Active listening, crisis intervention, therapeutic techniques, cultural sensitivity, case management, referral network navigation.


**How to get your foot in the door:** Volunteer work and internships with NGOs, community mental health centres, or schools are common entry points. Many professional associations offer supervision programmes for early-career practitioners.


**Annual Median Salary:** $55,000 – $75,000 (global/remote market)


## Design, Marketing & Human Experience


### 15. UX/UI Designer & Human-AI Interaction Designer


Every app you use, every website you browse — someone designed that experience. UX (User Experience) and UI (User Interface) Designers make sure digital products are intuitive, accessible, and worth coming back to. As AI gets woven into more products, a new niche is emerging: designers who specialise in how humans and AI systems interact.


**What qualifications do you need?** No single required degree. Many UX/UI designers come from graphic design, psychology, fine arts, or communication backgrounds. What matters most is a strong portfolio.


**Key skills:** Figma, Adobe XD, prototyping, usability testing, human-centred design thinking, collaboration with developers, accessibility principles.


**How to get your foot in the door:** Learn Figma (it’s free to start). Take Google’s UX Design Certificate on Coursera. Build a portfolio through personal projects, redesign exercises, or freelance work. Platforms like Behance and Dribbble are where hiring managers look.


**Annual Median Salary:** $85,000 – $120,000 (global/remote market)


### 16. Digital Marketing Specialist


The digital advertising market is massive and still growing — in some countries, online channels now make up over 80% of total advertising spend. Digital marketers connect products to the right people across search engines, social media, email, and increasingly, AI-powered platforms.


**What qualifications do you need?** No mandatory degree — many great digital marketers are self-taught. Degrees in marketing, communications, or business provide context. Certifications from Google (Google Analytics, Google Ads), HubSpot, and Meta Blueprint are widely valued.


**Key skills:** SEO/SEM, content creation, social media marketing, email marketing, marketing automation, paid advertising, data analysis.


**How to get your foot in the door:** Manage social media or run a small ad campaign for a local business, church, or personal project. Build a portfolio of real results. Look for marketing assistant or content creator roles as entry points, then develop into a specialist.


**Annual Median Salary:** $55,000 – $90,000 (global/remote market)


### 17. Digital Learning & Skills Development Specialist


This is a career built specifically for the era we’re in. As workforces need to upskill rapidly, organisations need people who can design and deliver effective learning experiences — combining instructional design with AI, VR, and adaptive learning technology. Think of it as curriculum design meets tech product development.


**What qualifications do you need?** Degrees in education, instructional design, organisational psychology, or human resources. Certifications in learning design tools and platforms (like Articulate 360, or an ATD certification) are practical differentiators.


**Key skills:** Instructional design, learning management systems (LMS), data-driven learning analytics, curriculum development, knowledge of adult learning principles.


**How to get your foot in the door:** Start in L&D (Learning & Development) coordinator roles at corporate HR departments. Contribute to online course platforms as a course creator or curriculum designer. Build a portfolio of training materials or a course you’ve designed and delivered.


**Annual Median Salary:** $70,000 – $95,000 (global/remote market)


## Education & Knowledge Work


### 18. University & Higher Education Instructor / Online Course Creator


Despite all the talk about AI replacing teachers, the WEF emphasises continued strong demand for educators — especially in countries with young and growing populations (Nigeria, hello). And beyond traditional academia, the e-learning market is booming, creating an entirely new category of “teachers” who build courses for global audiences.


**What qualifications do you need?** Traditional universities require a Master’s degree minimum, with a PhD preferred or required at senior levels. For online education and vocational instruction, deep subject-matter expertise often matters more than formal credentials.


**Key skills:** Subject expertise, pedagogy, curriculum development, research (for academia), public speaking, content creation (for online educators), assessment design.


**How to get your foot in the door:** For academia: pursue postgraduate study, publish research, and seek teaching assistant or graduate assistant roles. For online education: build a course on platforms like Teachable, Udemy, or Coursera. Starting a newsletter or YouTube channel in your area of expertise is increasingly a legitimate first step.


**Annual Median Salary:** $60,000 – $95,000 (global/remote market)


## Automation, Robotics & Mobility


### 19. Robotics & Automation Engineer


Robots are transforming manufacturing, logistics, agriculture, and healthcare. Robotics and Automation Engineers design, build, programme, and maintain these systems. Crucially, the WEF notes that automation changes tasks within jobs rather than wiping out entire professions — which means this field creates as many opportunities as it disrupts.


**What qualifications do you need?** A degree in mechanical engineering, electrical engineering, computer science, or mechatronics. Short courses in robotics, control systems, and automation (available on Coursera, edX, and MIT OpenCourseWare) are strong entry supplements.


**Key skills:** Robotics programming, control systems, embedded systems, computer vision, mechanical design, project management.


**How to get your foot in the door:** Build demonstrable robotics projects — even small ones. Competitions like FIRST Robotics are well-regarded. Target internships in manufacturing, automotive, or logistics companies that are investing in automation.


**Annual Median Salary:** $85,000 – $120,000 (global/remote market)


### 20. Internet of Things (IoT) Engineer


IoT is the network of physical devices — from smart home gadgets to industrial sensors to wearable health monitors — that collect and share data. IoT Engineers design, build, and manage these connected ecosystems, and the field is expanding rapidly across agriculture, healthcare, manufacturing, and smart cities.


**What qualifications do you need?** Degrees in electrical engineering, computer engineering, or computer science. Given how fast the field evolves, certifications in IoT platforms (like AWS IoT or Cisco’s IoT certifications) are practical and increasingly respected.


**Key skills:** Embedded systems programming, cloud integration, UI/UX design for hardware-software interfaces, networking, data security, hardware prototyping.


**How to get your foot in the door:** Get hands-on with a Raspberry Pi or Arduino — these are inexpensive hardware kits you can use to build real IoT projects at home. Document and share your builds on GitHub or LinkedIn. Look for embedded systems or hardware engineering roles at tech startups.


**Annual Median Salary:** $90,000 – $120,000 (global/remote market)


These 20 careers share a few common threads. They all require continuous learning. They all reward people who can show evidence of their skills, not just credentials. And most of them are accessible to people who are willing to start now — even without a traditional head start.
