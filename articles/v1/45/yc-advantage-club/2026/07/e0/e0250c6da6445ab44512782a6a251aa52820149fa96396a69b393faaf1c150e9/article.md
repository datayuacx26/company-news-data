# Leadership Insights: A conversation with Dr. Sriharsha A Achar

**Sriharsha**
It’s always good to talk about the past, although it has little bearing on the future, nevertheless. I was raised in Goa. I also started my initial career there. And that’s where my zest for life comes from. So, I did my bachelor’s degree in electronics and telecom from the College of Engineering, Goa, my Master’s in industrial engineering from the IIT School of Industrial Engineering at Goa University, and my MBA and HR, all from Goa. I completed my PhD much later in life, in 2006, which is about 17 years ago. So, my first career jump into the corporate sector took me to Lucknow, and that’s where I polished my skills of interacting with people.


And I’ve lived and worked in Bangalore for a very long time, almost 12 years. That’s where I honed my management skills, as I was in the IT, ITES, and pharma industry. That was the exposure that I acquired there.


And I’ve been living in the north for a very long time now, specifically in Gurgaon. The last 17-plus years in NCR have been interesting, to say the least. That’s where insurance exposure happened to me. And above all, organization-building exposure, because that’s the time I came into the CXO position.


And that is what I’m enriching myself with currently, not just people. So, over the last four years, I’ve been operating out of Chennai, and I try to be with the family in Gurgaon whenever I can, as and when I get a few days off. Having lived in the Philippines, the US, the Middle East, and having traveled to various other parts of the world, mainly for work, I have added additional perspectives, flavors, and exposure to professional life as well as personal life.


Now, to my mind, all HR professionals who are successful in their respective domains or fields or careers are the ones who have not only surmounted the organizational challenges and stakeholders’ expectations, but also have gone beyond that extra mile, thought out of the box consistently, to make a difference to the human side of the business. Even today, I wake up every morning as if it’s my first day at the office and look forward to new learning. And with all the new kinds of people joining the organization every day, there is an immense amount of learning opportunities.


So, this discipline is perhaps the reason for what I am today as a son, as a husband, as a father, a friend, a neighbor, a professional, and above all, a human being. So that kind of sums up my 38 years of career thus far.
