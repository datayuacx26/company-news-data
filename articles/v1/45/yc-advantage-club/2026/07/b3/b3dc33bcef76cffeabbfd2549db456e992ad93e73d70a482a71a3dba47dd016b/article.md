# Leadership Insights: A conversation with Seepika Singhal

**Seepika:**


The journey has been filled with twists and turns, and if I start with the beginning, I was born and brought up in a humble and grounded family setup. I never ever thought in my childhood that I would end up in HR as a profession. So I was good in studies, but I always wanted to prove myself in what I picked up, whether it was academics or co-curricular. So we were thirteen people cohabiting in one single household, and all the key decisions were taken by the head of the family, who used to be my grandfather, and those were my affirmative years. And I take a lot of pride in saying that I have imbibed all the values that have saved me today, the values of coexisting, adaptability, and respect that have come from the upbringing that I had. So there has been a continuous desire to prove myself and grow. And when I was in a small town, career services were very limited.


So, being a good student, I had only two options that everyone expected me to do: either engineering or medicine. But I was kind of a challenger to that status quo. I wanted to do something different. I didn’t know at that time what difference it could make. But my parents trusted in me. I said that I want to be there where a lot of innovative and creative minds will be. So I wanted to come to Delhi University and do my graduation in humanities from here. And I pursued that despite everyone around me saying that toppers like you are not doing engineering or medicine, who else? Why are you not writing the engineering entrance examination? I said, no, I’m not interested in doing whatever everyone is doing. That’s not my cup of tea. I came with the mindset of doing civil services but eventually I saw towards the final years of my graduation that a career in management was something that attracted me more.


I generally started to explore how admissions take place and all about it, and I appeared in all management exams and was admitted. And finally, I ended up taking admission in Sri Ram College of Commerce to do my master’s. So this was my educational journey, and this is a very interesting concept that I share with a few of my folks. I learned this concept of return on investment very early in my life. And I got admission in Narsee Monjee, Hyderabad, Amity Law School, and all these colleges. But I thought, let me see, what is the end outcome? So the line of sight was very clear to me. And I compared the statistics of placement. I saw that all these students who come out of these colleges end up in nearly similar companies and with similar packages.


So I thought, I am investing in a college where the teachers, the kind of environment, the university environment, SRCC being the prestigious one, and at a very nominal fee. It was minuscule in comparison to what other colleges were charging, and also the travel and accommodation costs. I thought that if I ended up getting a package of similar lines, why invest more, so I thought of the return on my investment. I didn’t want it to end up where, from my first salary, I start giving EMIs. So I thought, let me do this, and then I was fortunate to be selected from the campus. I was one of the very first students who got campus placement at the beginning of the journey. Then HCL happened to be a very fulfilling 5 1/2 years of differentiated experience. In HCL Technologies, I got a lot of leadership positions, worked in multiple geographies, multiple assignments, and different roles. Then from there I moved to Capgemini.


Capgemini was totally an international organization; from my IT pure play Indian company to Capgemini was completely different altogether. And the geography for me also was different. There I was taking care of North America as a compensation leader, and post-Capgemini again, I had fulfilling years of learning and delivering, and proved myself in that organization.


Then I acquired the leadership position in Luminous Electric, where I was leading the rewards function. That was again kind of a challenge for me because from my IT setup company to manufacturing, dealing with blue-collar employees, white-collar employees, and Indian payroll and coming up with benefits, you have to think about policy, think about the blue-collar worker who’s able to understand that policy. So that was totally a challenge to me, which inspired me to take up that position, and I enjoyed that also.


I seamlessly worked with the leaders and created differentiated rewards platforms and brought in experiential R&R practices at Luminous. So I was instrumental in bringing experience-based rewards in Luminous at that time. So from there, moving the employee EVP, working to an EVP in Luminous and then working in HT Media, which is totally a different company, very creative people, different talent mindset. From ST Media, I moved to R1 RCM, which was in healthcare and had a very huge presence in India as well as the US. So I had all these differentiated experiences. And then finally, I’m in video technologies, a very insurgent organization, with new ways of working on digital superpowers, CI, and dealing with very talented employees here.


So I’m feeling grateful that whatever opportunities I have had in my career journey have made me what I am today. And I have learned from the leaders across geographies and a lot of industries, different industry segments that I worked in. And one thing that keeps me honest is that because the context is different, so one thing that you have created as a success in one company may not be replicated in another. So it always keeps you challenged, keeps you continuously engaged. And so, I keep reinventing myself with these talents.
