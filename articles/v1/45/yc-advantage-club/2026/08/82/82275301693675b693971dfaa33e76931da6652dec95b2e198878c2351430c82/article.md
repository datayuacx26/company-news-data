# Leadership Insights: A conversation with Rajiv Naithani, Chief People Officer at Persistent Systems

**Rajiv**


First of all, I think wellness will not work if it is driven by the calendar. That’s the number one factor. Number two, it will always work when there is role modeling, and that’s very important. And third, of course, as an organization, you have a commitment to well-being.


Since you mentioned my personal commitment, my personal philosophy of life is that my life is based on four pillars of well-being: physical well-being, intellectual well-being, emotional well-being, and spiritual well-being.


Physical well-being is all about fitness. This body is my responsibility; I need to look after it like a vehicle. If I don’t maintain it, it will break down at some point. If I’m able to come to the studio to talk to you, it’s because I am mobile, and that’s my responsibility. The second aspect of physical well-being is what I eat, but that’s very personal, so I won’t delve into it.


The second pillar is intellectual well-being. I sometimes struggle with people who say, “I’ve done enough academically. It’s all about the job now.” I think as human beings we should never stop studying. My mantra for learning is “I don’t know.” That brings a lot of humility to learning even from kids. I learn a lot from my daughters and from youngsters. A few days back, I was having a review with one of the younger team members, and it was a first-time learning opportunity for me. I was asking like a kid to understand more about the model this person was presenting.


In my definition of learning, there’s a 70:20:10 model: 70% on the job, 20% is all about the environment, mentoring, and coaching, and 10% is the most important one, where you’re investing in your own development and growth, spending your time, money, and effort. If you’re doing all three, it gives you an amplified learning and growth proposition.


Emotional well-being is all about being aware of your own emotions and how you leverage that by understanding others and building relationships with them. My spiritual well-being has actually helped me understand the power of resilience. There are always pressure situations, but the pressure is outside. If my inner resilience is high, I won’t have stress. That’s a formula for stress too: pressure divided by resilience. Any object, if the pressure is higher than the resilience of the object, will break. Similarly, as a human being, if my inner resilience is higher than the pressure outside, I will not break; I will always find a reason, and I will be able to do it.


Now, in the context of corporate, role modeling becomes very important. As a leader, am I actually talking to my colleagues, team members, and employees about paying attention to their physical well-being? Am I practicing that first? If I’m not, how would I even role model that?


Similarly, for emotional well-being, am I creating a psychologically safe environment for them? Can they speak their mind? Can they challenge me on some of my perspectives? Of course we’re all professionals and respectful to each other, but challenging doesn’t mean being disrespectful. It’s about a different perspective. Am I giving voice to the most marginalized person in the team setting? That is psychological safety.


Now, since you mentioned MyLife@Persistent, it’s interesting that you could figure that out about Persistent. Credit goes to my marketing research team; they’ve done a great job. Persistent has a dedicated function we call MyLife@Persistent. This function is curated to ensure it is really dedicated to the holistic well-being of our employees. It’s not part of HR Business Partner roles, not part of HR operations, and not a part of L&D as well. It’s a dedicated team, and the leader of this team directly reports to me. So as an organization, we are committed to the holistic well-being of our employees, across every dimension, including physical, emotional, learning, financial, and social well-being too, because we live in a community; we work in a community.


I have not found any other organization in the tech services industry, or the tech industry as a whole, with a dedicated function on holistic well-being. That’s very unique at Persistent, and I take a lot of pride in it.
