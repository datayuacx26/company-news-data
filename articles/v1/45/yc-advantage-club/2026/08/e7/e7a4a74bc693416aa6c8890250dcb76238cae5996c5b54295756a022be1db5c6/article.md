# Leadership Insights: A conversation with Simin Askari, SVP – HR & Business Excellence at DS Group

**Simin**


You’re right, I’m in the FMCG sector, and I’d say that while there have been improvements in inclusion, organizations try to make sure there are inclusive workspaces. There are challenges, both structurally and culturally. Structurally, in a manufacturing and sales-oriented organization, there are very few women on the shop floor or out in the field. But there’s also a mindset and cultural bias that exists, stemming from questions around leadership capabilities to assumptions about whether there will be continuity at various life stages of a woman. So FMCG as a sector unfortunately has these challenges, but as I see it, my job as a leader is not only to get women into the workspace but also to try to make the workspace and ecosystem compatible so they can thrive.


For young girls, I’d say there will be challenges. Your career is never a straight path; there will always be ups and downs. You need to make sure you overcome those challenges and be prepared for them. Something I always tell young girls: don’t lose hope, don’t be apologetic about your ambitions, speak openly about them, and work on building your knowledge. If you go into a meeting well-prepared and knowledgeable, nobody’s really going to question whether you’re a man or a woman. Your competence speaks for itself. I’ve never liked the term “woman leader.” I’m just a leader who happens to be a woman. That’s what I try to tell people, including my own daughter.


Independent women go out there and speak their minds. You’ve got the knowledge in the back of your head. What’s also important is knowing how to make sure you’re visible. There are lots of times when we face challenges and try to step back, but that’s when you start becoming invisible, and people just start stepping over you and moving on to the next person. And the third thing, which is the most important, is to keep learning, be curious, and stay hungry for knowledge. The more you understand different things that aren’t even in your field, the more valuable you become to an organization, and then there’s no way anyone’s going to ask whether it’s a woman or a man.
