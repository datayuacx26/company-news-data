# Technology Stopped Being a Moat. Here's Where the Defensibility Went.

This is the first in a series we'll run once or twice a month on the Lua partner network: how we think about it, why it's built the way it is, and what it means for the people building on top of us.


Let's start with the uncomfortable bit. In AI, *technology is no longer a moat* .


The models are commoditising. Capability that felt like magic a year ago is now a line item with a dozen providers competing to sell it to you cheaper. Being first to a feature buys you a few weeks, maybe a quarter, and then someone ships the same thing. That's doubly true with enterprise buyers, who are only interested in their own troubles and the solutions you can provide. They want to see that you're the real deal before they'll trust you with anything that matters.


So if the technology itself can't protect you, where does a durable advantage actually come from?


## It’s all about the relationship


Strip competitive strategy back to its bones and it comes down to a simple question:


> *What is a client willing to pay you for, over a competitor?*


We've established that having a technological advantage is no longer a reliable way to win or retain a client. Even if you have that advantage, it is so unlikely that you'll maintain it that it doesn't play much of a role in the decision-making process. Equivalently, pricing significantly lower than your competitors is akin to slapping a Band-Aid on a gunshot wound. It signals that it's your only differentiator and that your product is likely not as good as others'.


So what are potential buyers of AI agents really looking for?


Far less than you'd think, they are not buying AI for its own sake. Most have internal pain points and inefficiencies that have simply been unsolvable until now, and they only care about fixing them. They don't care what your product can do until they can see what it does for them.


What they need, then, is someone who understands their business and industry well enough to ask the right questions, make the right recommendations, and build custom solutions that actually solve their problems. And that's the catch. Every industry is different and carries its own challenges, which means it is impossible for us to serve all the markets we'd like to on our own, there would be no reason for clients to trust that we know what we’re talking about.So, when the technology layer is interchangeable, the defensibility lives in distribution and trust. At Lua, we're building a network of partners who are experts in their fields to own industries and geographies, so that every client works with someone who understands and can deliver real value. None of that shows up in a feature comparison, and none of it can be cloned overnight. That is the moat.


## Why a network compounds where a team can't


A moat like this isn't built in a day, and it doesn't hold up on its own. What makes it hold is that it compounds, and if you're in it, you feel that compounding directly.


Most sales are one-off: you win a deal, bank it, and start again from zero on the next. A client relationship built on real understanding is the opposite. Once it's there, it keeps producing, and each client you win and keep becomes the base you build the next one on, in the same industry, off the same growing reputation. Your book doesn't reset every quarter, it gets denser and harder to take from you. The asset is yours, and it grows.


It's also why a network beats a central team at this, and why we build it this way instead of trying to do everything ourselves. A team in one place can only learn so many markets before the cost of each new one catches up with it. A network carries no such weight. Each expert goes deep on their own patch, reaching into places no central team could credibly serve, and the people doing the reaching keep the upside rather than handing it upward.


And you do it on your own terms. You run your own business, own your own clients, and answer to your own market, not to a quota someone else set. Because the platform underneath already handles the hard parts, the security and data protection your clients demand included. Your energy goes into the relationship rather than the plumbing. The part that's easy to underestimate is the community around it: people building on the same platform, hitting the same problems and sharing what works, so you're never solving from scratch, and the network gets smarter as it grows, not just bigger.


## So why now?


The market is moving faster than almost anyone is comfortable with. Businesses everywhere are scrambling to put AI to work, and the demand for people who can actually deliver it is running miles ahead of the supply. That gap is the whole opportunity, and gaps this wide don't stay open.


Move into it now, and you start compounding immediately. Every client you win and keep makes the next one easier and your position harder to attack. Leave it, and before long, you're competing for the same clients in a crowded field, against people who got there first and already earned the trust you'd only just be starting to build.


The experts who will own their industry or their region in five years are the ones moving today. That is why we're putting everything we have into this network, and why there has rarely been a better moment to build on top of it. If that sounds like you, come and talk to us.
