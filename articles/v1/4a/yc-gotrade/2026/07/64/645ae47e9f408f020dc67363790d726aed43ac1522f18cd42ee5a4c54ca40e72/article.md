# Alphabet and Tesla Slide as Investors Question the Payoff From Heavy Spending

## Tesla's Margin Collapses to 1.4% and Cash Flow Turns Negative


Tesla's report cut even deeper. According to[The Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/investing/2026/07/23/teslas-operating-margin-just-fell-to-14-and-free-c/) , Tesla reported revenue of $28.24 billion, up 26% year over year, and delivered 480,126 vehicles during the quarter. However, operating margin declined to 1.4% from 4.1% a year earlier. Capital expenditure rose to $5.79 billion as Tesla invested in AI infrastructure, Cybercab, robotics, and manufacturing capacity. The company recorded negative free cash flow of $1.09 billion, compared with positive free cash flow of $146 million a year earlier. For Tesla investors, the focus is not only on whether its projects can generate future growth, but also on whether the company can protect margins while continuing to fund them.

## Why Wall Street Punished Two Strong Reports


Step back and the pattern is what matters. Both companies are spending enormous sums to build for an AI-driven future, and in both cases investors decided the spending, not the growth, was the story. Per Investing.com, Matt Miskin of Manulife John Hancock Investments noted that strong earnings simply weren't translating into stock gains as investors questioned whether the capital-expenditure bills could be justified. It didn't help that oil chose the same week to spike. Brent crude settled above $100 a barrel for the first time since May, US futures cleared $92, and the VIX fear gauge jumped to 18.7 after touching 20.3 intraday, its highest in nearly a month. On the New York Stock Exchange, falling stocks outnumbered rising ones almost three to one.


## The Bottom Line


The Alphabet and Tesla selloff does not automatically mean their long-term strategies will fail. It shows that investors are becoming more selective about companies making large AI and growth investments. Revenue growth remains important, but the market now also wants clearer evidence that spending can translate into stronger margins, sustainable cash flow, and attractive long-term returns.
