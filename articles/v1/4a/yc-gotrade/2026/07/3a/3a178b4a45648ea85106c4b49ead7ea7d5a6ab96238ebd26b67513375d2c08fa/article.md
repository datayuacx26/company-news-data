## Alphabet Cloud Revenue Jumps 82% in Q2


Alphabet's latest quarter has set the benchmark for this week's earnings. According to[The Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/investing/2026/07/27/why-is-everyone-talking-about-alphabet-stock/) ,[Alphabet (GOOGL)](https://www.heygotrade.com/en/us-stock/googl/) reported Q2 revenue of $119.8 billion, up 24% from a year earlier. It was also the company's 12th straight quarter of double-digit revenue growth.


Google Search and Other revenue rose 17% to $63.3 billion, while YouTube advertising revenue increased 13% to $11.1 billion.


Google Cloud delivered the strongest growth. Revenue surged 82% year over year to $24.8 billion, representing roughly one-fifth of Alphabet's total revenue. Cloud operating income more than tripled to $8.8 billion, with an operating margin of 35.6%.


Alphabet's total operating income increased 30%, while its overall operating margin rose two percentage points to 34%. The company has a market capitalization of around $3.9 trillion to $4.0 trillion and trades at a forward price-to-earnings ratio of about 24 times.


At the same time, Alphabet raised its 2026 capital expenditure guidance to between $195 billion and $205 billion, mainly to fund AI infrastructure.


Investors still reacted positively. According to[The Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/investing/2026/07/27/why-is-everyone-talking-about-alphabet-stock/) , GOOGL shares rose 2.13% on July 25, suggesting that strong growth helped offset concerns about higher spending. The Motley Fool also noted that a $10,000 investment in Alphabet ten years ago would now be worth around $84,000, equal to an annual return of roughly 24%.


The market is now balancing two key factors. The Fed's rate outlook will influence the cost of funding the AI buildout, while upcoming earnings will show whether that spending is translating into stronger revenue and profit growth.


With Microsoft, Meta, Apple, and Amazon still set to report, this week could determine whether concerns over AI spending become a bigger warning for tech stocks or remain a temporary source of volatility.
