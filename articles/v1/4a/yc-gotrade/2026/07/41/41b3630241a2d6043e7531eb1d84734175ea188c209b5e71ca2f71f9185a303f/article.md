# OpenAI Plans $30 Billion AI Data Center in Georgia

Cooling runs on a closed loop that recirculates water. That is a deliberate choice. Water use has become a sore point at big data centers, and OpenAI clearly knows it.


Georgia Power likes the setup. "Companies like OpenAI are making real commitments when it comes to responsible operation," said senior vice president Aaron Mitchell, as quoted by[The Atlanta Journal-Constitution](https://www.ajc.com/news/2026/07/tech-giant-openai-plans-to-build-20-billion-data-center-near-georgia-coast/) .


## Jobs, Taxes, and an $80 Million Community Package


For a coastal county, the numbers are big. The site should employ about 400 people in six-figure jobs when it opens in 2028, then grow to 1,000 by 2032, according to[The Atlanta Journal-Constitution](https://www.ajc.com/news/2026/07/tech-giant-openai-plans-to-build-20-billion-data-center-near-georgia-coast/) .


There is a tax break attached. The Effingham County Industrial Development Authority signed off on a 50% property-tax abatement running 15 years. Even with that discount, OpenAI is on track to become the county's biggest taxpayer once the lights are on.


The company also put $80 million on the table for local schools, public safety, healthcare, job training, housing, veterans services, and small businesses. That package, plus the tax profile, is a big reason the authority said yes.


**Total project cost** $30B+ at full build ($20B headline local commitment)


**Power secured** 3.2 GW from Georgia Power


**Jobs** 400 (2028) growing to 1,000 (2032)


**Community fund** $80 million


**Timeline** Several-hundred-MW online 2028, full build 2032


## Stargate and the Capex Read-Through


Georgia is one node in Sam Altman's "Stargate" build-out, OpenAI's plan to pour capital into a few giant US compute hubs. To get there, it walked away from planned sites in the UK, Norway, and Texas, per[Investing.com](https://ca.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/openai-plans-30b-georgia-data-center-megaproject-4746644) . It also expects to go public at some point.


The bigger shift is what counts as scarce. Not money. Not chips. Power. Multi-gigawatt access is the new gatekeeper for AI infrastructure, and it is quietly redrawing the map of where these things get built.


OpenAI wants the next build to be cheaper. "There's a bunch of lessons around how these things should be designed that can cut both costs as well as make it faster to build," its VP of compute strategy said, according to OpenAI, which plans to open-source its data-center blueprints.


So where does a retail investor actually get exposure? Not OpenAI itself. The read-through runs to the names selling the picks and shovels.[Nvidia (NVDA)](https://www.heygotrade.com/en/us-stock/nvda/) supplies the accelerators that fill these halls.[Oracle (ORCL)](https://www.heygotrade.com/en/us-stock/orcl/) and[Microsoft (MSFT)](https://www.heygotrade.com/en/us-stock/msft/) sit in the cloud layer behind Stargate-scale projects, and Microsoft is still OpenAI's closest partner. When one project commits to several gigawatts at once, these are the tickers that tend to move.
