# Short Squeeze Explained: Meaning, Strategy, and Risks

A **short squeeze** occurs when a heavily shorted stock rises sharply, forcing short sellers to buy back shares to close their positions. That buying pressure pushes the price even higher, which forces more short sellers to cover, creating a self-reinforcing cycle.


Short squeezes can produce dramatic price spikes in a very short time. They are not driven by improvements in a company's fundamentals. They are driven by positioning, pressure, and forced liquidation. Understanding short squeeze meaning helps investors recognize these events and decide whether participation makes sense for their risk tolerance.


## What Is a Short Squeeze


A short squeeze happens when the price of a heavily shorted stock starts rising, putting short sellers under increasing pressure. Because short sellers borrow shares and sell them with the expectation of buying back at a lower price, a rising price means growing losses.
