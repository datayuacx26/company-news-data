# Your Face Closes the Deal Before You Say Anything

## The Tenth of a Second That Follows You Everywhere


Someone lands on your LinkedIn profile, your team page, or your speaker bio. Their brain has already decided whether you look competent and trustworthy, and it happened faster than they can consciously register.


Princeton psychologists Janine Willis and Alexander Todorov[found that people form those judgments after just a tenth of a second of looking at a face](https://www.princeton.edu/news/2006/08/22/snap-judgments-decide-faces-character-psychologist-finds) , and that more time to study the face only made them more confident, not more accurate. Trust was among the judgments viewers formed fastest. In other words, the photo is doing the talking, and it never stops.


That is the case for hiring a professional headshot photographer rather than cropping yourself out of a wedding photo. Here is what a good one delivers, what it is worth, and how to do it at scale.


## What you are actually paying for


*A genuine smile was the biggest single lift to how likable you read.*


A phone can capture a face. What it cannot do is control the variables that move perception, and those variables are measurable.


Photo-rating platform PhotoFeeler ran the numbers on this. In a study built on[more than 60,000 ratings of 800 business profile photos](https://blog.photofeeler.com/perfect-photo/) , the strongest factor for likability was a genuine smile with teeth visible, worth an average lift of 1.35 points on their scale. Formal dress drove the largest gains in perceived competence and influence, while sunglasses and shadowed eyes dragged scores down. A skilled photographer directs all of this live: the squint that reads as confidence instead of fear, the jawline, the lighting that keeps your eyes clear.

Skipping the professional route carries a price you pay later.

Employers are looking, too: a[CareerBuilder survey found 70% of employers research candidates on social media, and 57% of them rejected someone based on what they found](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/more-than-half-of-employers-have-found-content-on-social-media-that-caused-them-not-to-hire-a-candidate-according-to-recent-careerbuilder-survey-300694437.html) , with inappropriate photos the most common trigger. The photo is not decoration. It is evidence.


Even the C-suite is not exempt. Snappr's look at[the Fortune 500 CEOs with the best and worst headshots](https://www.snappr.com/blog/the-fortune-500-ceos-with-the-best-and-worst-headshots) shows how many leaders still get the basics wrong.

Snappr's own analysis found that[professional headshots increase perceived trust and credibility by 78%](https://www.snappr.com/blog/how-professional-headshots-build-trust-and-increase-brand-credibility-by-78) .
