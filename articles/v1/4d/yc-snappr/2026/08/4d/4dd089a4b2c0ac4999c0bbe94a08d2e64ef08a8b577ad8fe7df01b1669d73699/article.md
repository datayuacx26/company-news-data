# How We Built Snappr Pro for Our Customers

## The feedback we heard in every single interview


Between February and April 2026, our design and engineering leads sat down with nine of our highest-volume customers and asked one blunt question: what is annoying about working with us?


The answers were more specific than we expected. A dental group supporting 100 offices across eight states told us three people were sharing one login. An event agency where thirty planners each run twenty-plus events a year was rebuilding photo albums on a third-party hosting site because she was receiving eight to ten separate galleries per event. A venture firm hosting 160 events a year had five photographers she never wanted matched with again and no way to say so.


Different industries, different volumes, the same underlying problem. Everyone in the room was booking photoshoots at real scale, and none of them were doing it on tooling built for that. These were not businesses that needed better photos. They were businesses that had outgrown the way they were buying photos.


That gap is not unique to us. Content Marketing Institute's B2B research found that 45% of organizations lack a scalable model for content creation and 54% name lack of resources as a top challenge. The same research found that among companies with dedicated content teams, 54% run those teams with just two to five people. Small teams, growing visual demands, tooling built for one person.


So we built Snappr Pro. Here is every feature in it, and the customers who asked for it.


## Ask one: let my team in


This was the loudest request in the set, and the evidence was better than a survey answer. Four of the nine accounts were already sharing a single password.


A dental group had three people booking from one login. A venture firm had handed her credentials to an outside contractor to migrate photo files. A funeral-home marketing agency had given her bookkeeper the username so she could pull invoices. An agency's video editor was logging in as his boss to download footage. Nobody framed this as a security concern. They framed it as the only way to get their work done.


One customer went further and specified the tier for us: three seats for a growing team, unlimited for enterprise. That is exactly what shipped. Snappr Pro includes a team workspace with up to three accounts, each with its own login, and a shared history so every booking and gallery sits in one place regardless of who created it.


The detail that matters operationally is the audit trail. One customer described bookings landing under her name but carrying a colleague's phone number, so the photographer called the wrong person. Separate logins fix the attribution, not just the access.


## Ask two: stop making me retype the same brief


Six of the nine accounts described the same ritual. Write out what you want. Send it to the rep. The rep forwards it to the photographer. Repeat next month, from scratch.


One customer summarized his brief as an itemized list and described the process as copy, paste, copy, paste. An event manager was building a custom one-page manifest for every booking, covering room locations, floors, walking directions and shot timing. An agency owner had a fixed template with a four-hour timeline, a b-roll checklist and a per-video script list.


Pro now includes creative guidelines you save once and apply to every shoot. Your style preferences, your must-have shots, your logistics notes, stored on the account and visible to the photographer before they arrive. Consistency here is worth real money: a Lucidpress study of more than 200 organizations found consistent branding can increase revenue by 33%, and the same study found 81% of companies still deal with off-brand content. Guidelines are how you stop that at the source rather than fixing it in post.


## Ask three: let me keep the good ones and skip the bad ones


Three accounts asked to save preferred photographers. Two asked to block specific ones.


The reason was never about micromanagement. It was about not starting over. A multifamily developer explained that rebooking a photographer she had already worked with saved her a site visit: he knew the team, he had access, she did not need to re-explain anything. An event agency wanted the same photographer across a multi-day conference because clients recognize him by day two and relax on camera.


Blocking came from harder experiences. One customer had a photographer miss both named VIPs in a room of 150 people. She wanted a way to make sure that never happened again.


Pro adds favorite photographer management: mark the creators you want back, and block the ones you do not. Worth noting the dissent we also heard, because it shaped how we prioritized this. One customer with roughly 100 bookings said favoriting was not a priority for her, because quality had been consistent enough that she never needed it. Demand for this feature is partly a symptom, which is why we treated marketplace quality as the parallel workstream rather than calling the feature a fix.


## Ask four: I know the photo exists, I just can't find it


Two accounts asked for search across their whole image history rather than folder-by-folder hunting. One of them was already using our portal in preference to her own company's internal database, purely because it was easier to navigate.


Pro adds smart image search across your gallery. Search a term like team portrait and get matching images from across every booking you have ever made, not just the shoot you happen to have open.


This one is quietly commercial. Salsify's consumer research found 61% of shoppers say images and videos are the biggest product page factor in deciding whether to buy, and that one in three abandon a purchase over low-quality or missing images. Assets you cannot find are assets you cannot publish. Our guide to[why professional photography is essential for every business](https://www.snappr.com/blog/why-professional-photography-is-essential-for-every-business-11-powerful-reasons) covers the downstream revenue case in more detail.


## The pricing insight we did not go looking for


We asked everyone the obvious question: would you pay a subscription? Most said yes, vaguely. One answer reframed the whole model.


A customer who books events at the start of every semester explained that she only books a photographer when she is confident an event will succeed. Sometimes she skips one, the event goes well anyway, and she is left wishing she had covered it. A membership, she said, would remove that hesitation and let her take more risk on marginal events.


Per-booking pricing makes every shoot a judgment call about whether the event deserves it, and the marginal ones quietly never happen.


So[Snappr Pro cuts the service fee from 15% to 9%](https://www.snappr.com/pro) on every booking, for $49 a year at launch pricing rather than the standard $99. There is no spend commitment. At that price the membership typically pays for itself inside the first handful of bookings, which means the calculation stops being per-shoot and starts being annual.


## What Pro is, and what it isn't


Pro is for teams buying corporate business photography regularly enough that pay-as-you-go has started to chafe, but who are not ready for an enterprise contract. Multi-location brands, in-house and independent agencies, event and marketing teams, ecommerce, real estate, automotive marketplaces.


It is not the enterprise product, and we were careful not to pretend otherwise. If you need API access, custom workflows, unlimited seats or bespoke integrations, that conversation is still[Snappr for Enterprise](https://www.snappr.com/enterprise) . Pro is the tier that was missing underneath it.


Three features we deliberately did not include, because customers told us not to: API access, workflow automation and a full media library were all ranked nice-to-have by the same people who ranked seats and guidelines as essential. One called them leisure features. Shipping less turned out to be part of the brief.


Galleries still land within 48 hours, or 72 for events. Photographers are still vetted, still local, still bookable in about two minutes. If you want the underlying case for treating this as revenue infrastructure rather than a cost line, our piece on[replacing stock photos with professional images increasing leads by 45%](https://www.snappr.com/blog/how-replacing-stock-photos-with-professional-images-can-increase-leads-by-45) is the shortest version of the argument. And BrightLocal's controlled study is a useful reminder of the floor: sites with genuine owner photos earned trust from 46% of consumers, generic imagery 33%, and sites with no images just 21%.

## Frequently asked questions


### What is included in Snappr Pro?


[Snappr Pro](https://www.snappr.com/pro) is an annual membership that adds a three-seat team workspace, saved creative guidelines, favorite and blocked photographer management, and smart image search across your gallery. It also reduces the service fee on every booking from 15% to 9%.


### How much does Snappr Pro cost?


[Pro is $49 for the first year](https://www.snappr.com/pro) at launch pricing, down from the standard $99. It is billed as a single annual payment, auto-renews, and can be cancelled at any time. There is no spend commitment attached.


### How is Pro different from pay-as-you-go?


Pay-as-you-go gives you one login and the standard 15% service fee. Pro is built for teams: multiple seats with separate logins, shared booking history, reusable shoot guidelines, photographer preferences that persist between bookings, and a lower fee per shoot.


### When does a Snappr Pro membership pay for itself?


Because the membership cuts the service fee by six percentage points, it typically covers its own cost within the first handful of bookings at launch pricing. Teams booking monthly or more usually clear the $49 well inside the first quarter.


### Should I choose Pro or Snappr Enterprise?


Choose[Pro](https://www.snappr.com/pro) if you need a few seats, consistent shoot guidelines and a better rate without a spend commitment. Choose[Snappr Enterprise](https://www.snappr.com/enterprise) if you need API access, custom workflow automation, unlimited users or bespoke integrations, which come with an annual contract and a dedicated account manager.
