# How to Get Your Barbershop Found on Google

Someone nearby just typed "barbershop near me" into their phone.


Google returns three shops. Just three. Those are the ones in the map pack: the section with star ratings, hours, and a "Book Online" button. Below that block is a list most people don't scroll through.


Your shop is either in those three results, or it isn't there.


A fully optimized Google Business Profile(GBP) is how you get in. This guide covers the setup, the ranking factors that actually move the needle, and how to build a review system that keeps you at the top.


## Why the Map Pack Is Where New Clients Decide


Nearly half of all Google searches are local. "Barbershop near me." "Best fade in \[city\]." "Walk-in barber open now."


When those searches happen, Google surfaces three businesses above everything else. Those three get the clicks, the calls, and the bookings. The rest get almost nothing.


Here's what makes it harder than it looks: according to a 2026 SQUIRE analysis of 17,913 barbershops across the top 25 U.S. metros, 83% already rate 4.5 stars or above. Star ratings are no longer a differentiator. They're table stakes.


What separates the shops at the top is review volume and recency. Shops using an automated review system average 84% more reviews than those relying on manual outreach. In the top 10 U.S. markets, that gap widens to 117%.


Your Google Business Profile is the engine behind all of it.


## Step 1: Claim and Verify Your Profile


If you haven't claimed your profile, go to[business.google.com](https://business.google.com/) and create or claim your listing.


Google will ask you to verify ownership by phone, text, email, or video, depending on your situation. Verification can take a few days, and your updates won't go live until it's complete.


If your shop is already showing up on Google Maps but you haven't claimed it, claim it now. Unclaimed profiles can carry wrong hours, missing information, and no way for you to respond to reviews.


## Step 2: Choose the Right Primary Category


This single field carries more ranking weight than most shop owners expect.


Your primary category tells Google what your business does. Select "Barber Shop." Not "Hair Salon," not "Men's Grooming," not a general category that doesn't match your service. The primary category is one of the top signals Google uses for local ranking. Getting it wrong means your profile may show up for the wrong searches, or not at all for the right ones.


Add secondary categories (Men's Salon, for example) after your primary is set. But don't let them dilute your main one.


## Step 3: Build Your Service Menu


Google lets you list individual services on your profile. Most shop owners skip this. That's a real missed opportunity.


Each service entry gives Google more context about what you offer. A client searching for "kids haircut near me" is more likely to find your shop if "kids haircut" is in your service list. Same for beard trims, lineups, fades, and brow work.


Add a price or price range for each service. Clients want to know what to expect before they book. And a complete, specific service menu signals to Google that your profile is detailed and active.


## Step 4: Add Photos That Show Your Shop


Shops with more than 100 photos on their Google profile get significantly more views and direction requests than those with just a few.


Upload a mix: exterior (so clients can find you on the street), interior (so they know what to walk into), and work photos (haircuts, lineups, fades, beards). Use real photos from your shop, not stock imagery.


Frequency matters too. Adding new photos on a regular cadence signals to Google that your business is active. It also gives potential clients a current look at your shop rather than something from three years ago.


## Step 5: Connect Your Booking Link


Google lets you add a booking URL directly to your profile. When clients find your shop in the map pack, they see a "Book Online" button that takes them straight to your scheduler.


This closes the gap between the search and the appointment. A client who finds your shop, reads your reviews, and can book without leaving Google is far more likely to actually book than one who has to track down your website separately.

## Step 6: Build a Review System


Reviews are the hardest part of your GBP to control and the most important for your ranking.


Joey Mitnick, owner of Buffalo Joe's Barber Lounge in Melissa, TX, looks at his shop's reviews every single day. "Google reviews are a lot of people's first impression of you," he says. "To everyone who hasn't been here before, you are who your Google reviews say you are, whether you want to believe it or not."


The problem with asking clients one by one is that it doesn't scale. Most clients won't leave a review unless the ask is perfectly timed and easy. Manual outreach misses the window most of the time.


Brittany Ball, owner of Manifest Barbershop Company in Dillon, SC, frames the broader principle clearly: "Once you're consistent and convenient for your clients, that's a big key of keeping them."

## Keep the Profile Active


Setting up your profile is the foundation. Keeping it current is what maintains your ranking over time.


Update your hours whenever they change, including holidays. Answer questions in the Q&A section before clients have to ask them. Post updates when you add a barber, change your services, or run a promotion.


Consistency across the web matters too. Your business name, address, and phone number should match exactly across your website, social profiles, and any other directory listings. Inconsistencies create confusion for Google and for clients searching your name.
