# AI Writing Tool Comparison: Type.ai vs. Grammarly Premium

Before LLMs changed the way people approached their writing tasks, there was Grammarly. Launched in 2009, the service started as a way to help students improve their grammar and spelling. As word processing moved to the cloud and became browser-based, Grammarly launched browser extensions and plugins to integrate its features everywhere its users were typing.


You could say that Grammarly was the predominant force in computer-assisted writing, but Grammarly would tell you to use the active voice for the point to really land.


With the introduction of incredibly competent AI chatbots, the market for a generative AI tool like ChatGPT with helpful Grammarly-like features exploded. Developers all vying for the subscription dollars of people in need of writing assistance went to work. But now there’s a glut of options.


The label of "AI writing assistant" has become kind of a catch-all for apps that cover a lot of different use cases. We can't speak to all of them and, really, we don’t want to.


But we can tell you about Type.ai: it wasn't created merely as an[alternative to Grammarly](https://type.ai/) . We had other ideas for it, and understanding what they are can help you determine which is the better choice for you.


**As an AI writing assistant, I can save you the time of reading this whole post. Here's the TL;DR:**
• Type.ai is a full AI-powered document editor, not just a grammar plugin. It's designed to streamline writing while keeping you in control.
• Grammarly focuses on polishing existing writing, acting more like a teacher by emphasizing grammar, tone analysis, and inclusive language.
• Type.ai accelerates content creation with draft generation, AI rewrites, and in-line commands.
• Grammarly emphasizes proofreading and learning, offering real-time error-checking, plagiarism detection, and AI-powered citation tools.
• The main difference: Type.ai is geared for professionals seeking enhances greater efficiency, while Grammarly refines your writing with mechanical precision.

### **Generating Drafts**


The hardest draft to start is the first one. It’s also the least vital version of anything you write: there’s no question it’s going to change—a lot—from where it was when you first finished it. For content writers, who often operate inside a topic niche and find themselves writing the same things over and over again, the first draft can be downright painful.


*How do I say the same thing I’ve said a million times before in a new way?*


Creating that first draft in Type takes only a couple of minutes. You can give it the parameters of the document you’re creating (like how many words, whether you’re writing blog posts or essays, who the target reader is) and then also supply it with other collateral.


If you’re working off a brief that outlines what you’re supposed to write, you can hand that off to Type, along with supporting URLs and other documents that contain any facts and figures you want to include. You can also hand it examples of your own writing so that the draft will mimic your voice.


Type.ai is also suitable for academic writing, providing tailored language suggestions and editing features to enhance the quality of academic work.

The AI can handle these transformations while preserving your core message.


This is particularly useful when you need to repurpose content for different audiences or platforms—or for when you're supposed to be writing concise content, and need help to cut words without cutting meaning.


But it’s also a great tool for when you’re just trying to find the best way of saying something. Rewrites can be done “in-line” with Type, meaning you can just highlight the small chunk of text you want to tweak and tell the AI what changes you want. You can go back and forth with the AI, editing and revising until you’ve got it just right, and then replace the original with that finished text directly into the document body with a single button click.

Grammarly made a name for itself with spelling and grammar checking plugins for word processors and web browsers, but they also offer a standalone Premium product that includes AI text generation.


Still, you'll see from the feature highlights that the focus of Grammarly is more about polishing up existing writing and learning the fundamentals. It's mechanics over creativity, and that’s not a bad thing. The app caters more to the user who needs instruction rather than collaboration.

### **Error Checking**


Grammarly is known for its in-line grammar checker, highlighting everything from basic spelling mistakes and word choice suggestions to complex sentence structure issues and grammar errors to pointing out run-on sentences like this one—all while you type.


By now, we’re all familiar with the red and blue underlines in our documents and how to deal with them—Grammarly may have perfected this functionality.


### **Tone Analysis**


One of the ways in which AI is implemented well in Grammarly is its analysis of—and improvements on—writing tone. By "tone," we mean the overall quality of the voice: professional, conversational, casual, those kinds of things.


The best writers are aware of—and can control—their tone, but that kind of skill takes time to perfect. Grammarly can analyze your writing tone for you, and help you to adjust your text where it needs to be. This is extra helpful in cases where the wrong tone can undermine your message.


### **Inclusive Language**


Better late than never, the world has started to catch on that words often have a power beyond their simple definitions. This heightened awareness means that what is and is not acceptable speech is changing frequently.


Grammarly Premium has a built-in awareness of those touchy words and phrases—not just obvious slurs but also those subtle phrases that reinforce systemic inequities. We're talking about terms like "mankind" instead of "humankind," or casually referring to a business meeting as a "powwow."


### **Plagiarism Checker**


Grammarly's plagiarism detection system works like many others, scanning your content "against billions of web pages" to identify potential matches. Like other plagiarism detection systems, it is prone to false positives now and again.


We tested it ourselves, and it flagged an original phrase we wrote, "Artificial intelligence (AI) has the ability to significantly..." The system identified this as having come from an interview with Tim Cook that our team had never encountered—and which doesn’t seem to exist.


Still, false positives are tolerable when it's also flagging real plagiarism.


### **AI Citation Generator**


Understanding the growing importance of AI disclosure in academic and professional writing, Grammarly offers an automated citation tool. This feature generates properly formatted citations in APA, MLA, and Chicago styles, helping writers maintain academic integrity without having to remember all the arcane structural rules about citing in each format. This is especially useful for students who will never have to use this skill outside of school, saving valuable brain matter for other, possbily more tender, memories.


### **Performance Score**


To quantify writing quality, Grammarly implements a scoring system that evaluates multiple factors including word count, reading level, and vocabulary usage. This score isn't arbitrary—it's calculated within the context of your chosen document goals, providing a meaningful metric for improvement. The system helps writers track their progress and identify areas for enhancement.


### Grammarly Assistant and Browser Extension


The centerpiece of Grammarly AI resides in its Assistant feature, which appears as a pop-up window interface. This dynamic tool adapts its AI shortcuts based on your text selection, offering contextual suggestions for improvement.


Grammarly Assistant can also be used on various social media platforms, enhancing writing style and grammar while interacting on these platforms.


While it provides a comprehensive list of writing ideas and one-click revision options, it has one notable limitation: users can’t preview suggested changes within their document’s context before applying them.

### **Under the Hood: Large Language Models (LLMs)**


At the heart of both Type and Grammarly's generative AI features are the LLMs that power them.


- **Type offers you a choice between Anthropic's Claude and OpenAI's ChatGPT** for its advanced AI features. Each of these LLMs[has its own strengths](https://blog.type.ai/post/claude-vs-gpt) , and you can switch between them depending on what you're trying to accomplish.
- **Grammarly's LLM was developed in-house and incorporates GPT to an extent, but they've been fairly quiet about its details.** We can safely guess that it was trained in a way that supports Grammarly's mission of polishing up text and focusing on mechanics and writing goals. Anything beyond that educated guess is a less educated, more wild, guess.


One thing that's important to know about LLMs is that their use is limited by the developers. AI processing is intensive and costly, so users aren't going to get unfettered access to it.


With Type, you can avoid limits altogether by using Speed mode, which is the faster (and less processor-intensive) method of generating text. In Power mode we use rate-limiting to make sure our users stay within bounds, but we've set it up in such a way that you should never reach the limit. If you do, you can purchase additional credits to tide you over til the monthly reset.


If you're weighing the pros and cons of Grammarly Premium, know that they also have monthly prompt limits: 2,000 requests per user. On its own, this would be enough, but bear in mind that if you don't like the results, it still counts toward your limit. Refining those results is another request.


The good news is there's a limit counter in Grammarly so at least you know where you stand.


### Tone, Voice, and Writing Style


Both tools are able to adjust their writing style according to your preferences, but each handles it a different way.


With Type, you can include a description of the tone/voice that you want with each[writing prompt](https://blog.type.ai/post/how-to-write-ai-prompts-that-get-results) you give it. For example,


\[code block\] "Write a 2,000 word blog post showing the differences between Type and Grammarly Premium in a conversational, but still professional, tone."


Alternatively you can upload a reference document and just tell Type to mimic the style found in it. While you can't save a profile of your voice/style for later use, you can[save a prompt](https://blog.type.ai/post/ai-prompt-saving) —including the instructions you gave for the writing style.


Grammarly's approach is to move everything into a uniform style: namely, your unique voice. The more you use it, the more it learns and analyzes your writing style—it then starts making suggestions based on that.


You can also define different "voice" profiles to use for different bits of writing—documents might be more formal, messages more casual. Each profile has a number of descriptors you can choose from to adjust the voice (e.g. enthusiastic, serious, etc.), but there's no way to provide it with an example to mimic.


### **Research**


Neither Type nor Grammarly are meant to be used as a research assistant; in both apps, the AI chat assistant is working off of a static LLM and a knowledge cutoff date—somewhere in mid-2024 (as of this writing). You should be careful not to ask anything of your AI assistant that requires real-time knowledge: neither one knows anything beyond their cutoff date.


With that said, Type is able to at least retrieve and incorporate new information when you use it to generate a draft. If you need facts and figures incorporated into your writing, you can add what are called "knowledge sources" when you're creating the prompt that will write your first draft.


You still need to do the research yourself. But you can compile it all into a document which you'll attach like a copy brief, or you can just give Type the URLs that contain the information you want to include.


### **Proofreading**


This is, of course, in Grammarly's wheelhouse and what makes it a great product for someone who needs extra guidance with their writing. Not only does it highlight errors as you make them and offer fixes, it often explains why something is wrong or gives more context on the grammatical rule at play.


Type can proofread your work, but you need to explicitly ask it to. You can get a quick mechanics check through the Review feature (discussed earlier), where you can either accept or reject the suggestion. Or you can engage through the chat window by asking Type to look for and fix specific things.


Think of the difference between the two like this: with Grammarly, you're in a class and asking the teacher to educate you. With Type, you're in a writer's workshop and asking your peer for input.

## **Frequently Asked Questions**


### **Is there a better option than Grammarly?**


It depends on your specific needs. Grammarly is an excellent tool for polishing and refining existing content, focusing on grammar, punctuation, style, and tone. However, if you’re looking for a tool that does more than just editing—like generating content from scratch, brainstorming ideas, or drafting long-form pieces— **Type.ai** may be a better option.


Type.ai is designed for users who need advanced AI-powered content creation capabilities, making it ideal for marketers, content creators, and professionals who prioritize speed and creativity. Pairing Type.ai with Grammarly can also give you the best of both worlds: powerful content creation and impeccable editing.


### **What is the main difference between Type.ai and Grammarly Premium?**


Type focuses on advanced AI-powered content generation, offering tools for drafting, brainstorming, and creating original content quickly. It's best suited for professionals looking to boost their efficiency. Grammarly Premium, on the other hand, is more focused on refining and enhancing existing text through grammar checks, style suggestions, and tone adjustments. This makes is a better option for students and other people just learning to write.


### **Should copywriters use Grammarly?**


Grammarly is a great tool for those who are learning how to write clean, error-free prose in any context. It’s a tool for learning the rudiments of English spelling and grammar; professionals who know these rules and enjoy bending them for creative purposes may find Grammarly an intrusive tool. If a tool like Grammarly existed for music, the Blues could not exist because it breaks the rules.


Professional copywriters, authors, journalists, and anyone who’s already successfully wielding the English language for a career usually aren’t well served by Grammarly Premium. If you’re the Type of writer who wants to maintain control over your output, Type is a more suitable solution for AI writing assistance. It’s designed to support writers, not teach or replace them.
