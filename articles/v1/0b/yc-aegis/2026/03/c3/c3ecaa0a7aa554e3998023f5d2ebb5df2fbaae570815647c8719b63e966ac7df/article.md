## The Challenge of Rising Denial Rates in Healthcare


Denial volume and complexity are climbing. Industry analyses citing AMA data estimate roughly 15% of claims are denied on first submission, and the share of providers reporting increasing denials rose from 42% in 2022 to 77% in 2024, underscoring a broad-based trend of tightening payer scrutiny (revcycle industry analysis).


Beyond the revenue delay, every denial adds friction and cost: HFMA has pegged average rework at $118 per denial, and federal audit recoveries are intensifying, with recent years reflecting multibillion-dollar clawbacks - an unmistakable signal that compliance pressure is rising (HFMA and audit pressure overview).


Why is this accelerating? Several forces converge:


- Greater coding granularity and policy churn across payers
- Eligibility and benefits complexity that shifts final liability post-service
- More prior authorization requirements and medical necessity standards
- Data fragmentation between EHRs, clearinghouses, and payer portals


When denials are discovered weeks after submission, appeal windows shrink, cash flow gaps compound, and staff spend unproductive hours hunting data across systems.

### Early Detection to Prevent Revenue Loss


Real-time notification surfaces denials or at-risk claims before appeal windows close, cutting the chance of unrecoverable write-offs (denial prevention perspective). Early detection in real-time denial tracking means knowing new denials within hours, not weeks, for immediate action to prevent cash loss.


Typical flow from alert to resolution:


- Denial event hits the dashboard within hours, tagged with payer, reason, and appeal clock.
- AI prioritizes by financial impact and likelihood to overturn; queues are routed to the right owner.
- The assignee launches a guided checklist to correct data, attach documentation, and submit an appeal.
- Status updates and payer responses sync back in real-time until payment posts.

Granular analytics shift the focus from chasing individual denials to fixing systemic issues. Dashboards that break denials down by payer, reason code, service line, and rendering provider reveal the fastest path to impact—whether that’s a coding education refresh, tighter eligibility checks, or prior authorization workflow fixes (denial analytics explained). Near-real-time reporting also helps teams pinpoint whether patterns stem from coding, eligibility, or medical necessity documentation.
