# How to compare two Word documents

I used to compare two documents by positioning them side by side on one small screen and then manually scanning for errors, sentence by sentence. As you'd expect, that method morphed my face into an alarming scrunch, and more importantly, it was ripe for human error.


Then I learned that every word processing app I use has a built-in tool to do exactly this.


If you're straining your eyes to spot the difference between two documents, here's how to compare two Word documents in the desktop app. Note: There's no built-in tool to compare documents in the web version of Word.

## How to compare two Word documents


Microsoft makes it really easy to compare two Word documents in the desktop app, but it isn't immediately obvious.


1.


Open a new document in Word.


2.


In the upper ribbon, click **Review** .


3.


Click **Compare** , and then click **Compare Documents** .


4.


In the *Compare Documents* window that appears, update the following fields:


-


**Original document** : Choose the first document you want to use in your comparison.


-


**Revised document** : Choose the other version of the document that you want to use in your comparison.


-


**Label changes with:** To make it easier to spot differences, assign a label—for example, the writer's name—to mark changes in the revised document.


-


**In:** By default, Word will display changes between the two documents in the original document. I prefer to compare documents in a *new* document in case I ever want to revert back to the original of either version. To do this, click **New document** in the *Show Changes* section. (If this option isn't visible, click the down caret \[∨\].)


5.


Click **OK** .


Word then merges the two documents together and tracks differences as comments in the *Markup Area* in the side panel of the doc. If you're scanning through just the doc, Word also applies a red font color and underlines any differences in text.


## How to merge Word documents


Let's say you want to incorporate all the changes from the second version into the original document, without the markups. Here's how to quickly do this after you've compared both documents.


1.


Click the **down caret** (` ∨` ) **** next to *Accept* in the *Review* tab.


2.


Click **Accept All Changes** . Or, you can click **Accept and Move to Next** to manually review each change and merge select edits.


If you compare two documents in a *new* document, Word takes changes from the *Revised document* (the second file) and merges them into the *Original document.*


And if you want to merge Word documents without comparing them first, here's how.


1.


Click **Review** in the upper ribbon.


2.


Click **Compare** , and then click **Combine Documents** .


3.


In the *Combine Documents* window that appears, select the two documents you want to combine.


4.


Click **OK** .


When you're done, don't forget to hit the Ctrl+S[shortcut](https://zapier.com/blog/microsoft-office-shortcuts/) to save your changes.


### Compare vs. combine in Word: What's the difference?


Where **compare** lets you easily see the differences between two documents, **combine** lets you merge two documents with different changes made by multiple editors into a single document. Use combine when you already have edits (tracked changes) in the drafts.


Neither of these features is available on Word for Web because the online version tracks changes similarly to[Google Docs](https://zapier.com/blog/google-docs-formatting-tips/) .


## How to compare two Word documents side by side


If you prefer to do visual gymnastics, you can also compare two Word documents side by side.


1.


Open the two Word documents you want to compare.


2.


In either of the documents, click **View** .


3.


In the *Window* group, click **View Side by Side** . (You might only see the icon for it, which looks like two documents next to each other.)


4.


Optionally, click **Synchronous Scrolling** to scroll both documents at the same time.


Once you're done comparing documents, click **View Side by Side** again to return to your normal, single file view.


## How to compare documents in Google Docs


The steps to compare two documents in Google Docs varies slightly from Word, but it's just as straightforward to do.


1.


Open the first document you want to compare.


2.


In the menu, click **Tools** , and then click **Compare documents** .


3.


In the *Compare documents* window that appears, select the second document you want to use in your comparison, and enter a name in the *Attribute differences to* field. You can even include comments from the second document. Then click **Compare** .


4.


In the *Comparison is ready* window that appears, click **Open** . This will create a new document. (Unlike Word, there's no option to populate changes into the original document.)


Similar to Word, any differences between the two documents are tracked in suggestion mode as[Google Docs comments](https://zapier.com/blog/how-to-use-google-docs-comments/) . And to give you an at-a-glance view of differences directly in the doc, Google Docs treats changes with a different font color.


## How to merge documents in Google Docs


Google Docs doesn't have a dedicated tool to combine two documents. But there is a workaround.


1.


Follow the above steps to compare documents.


2.


In the new document's menu, click **Tools** , then click **Review suggested edits** .


3.


To accept all the suggested edits at once, click **Accept All** . If you want to manually review each suggestion first, click the up (∧) or down (∨) caret next to *Suggested edits* , and the *Accept All* button will automatically change to *Accept* . Now you can accept edits one at a time.


You can also preview what your document would look like if you accepted all the edits. Click the down caret (v) next to *Show suggested edits* , and then select **Preview "Accept all."** That way, you can look at the document before you end up having to manually correct weird[capitalization](https://zapier.com/blog/capitalize-text/) or other formatting issues.


## How to compare documents and files using free tools


Let's say you want to quickly compare two versions of text that weren't originally drafted in Word or Google Docs. There are online tools designed to do just this. Some even let you compare PDFs, PowerPoint presentations, and code.


Here are three document and file comparison tools you can use for free.


### Draftable


[Draftable](https://www.draftable.com/compare) is a ridiculously easy-to-use free tool that lets you compare Word documents, PowerPoint presentations (only` .ppt` , not` .pptx` ), and PDFs.


Upload your two files, click **Compare** , and in seconds, you'll have a side-by-side comparison with differences highlighted directly in the doc and listed to the side (similar to Word and Google Docs).


### QuickDiff


If you have only plain text to compare—as in, no formatting like bold and underline or elements like tables and hyperlinks—then[QuickDiff](https://quickdiff.net/) is a decent option.


Copy and paste the two bits of text you want to compare, and then click **Check for differences** . You can even tell QuickDiff to ignore changes like inserted or deleted blank lines and letter case.


There are a few downsides to QuickDiff worth mentioning.


-


**Spots errors at a sentence or paragraph level only** . QuickDiff highlights entire sections (sentences or paragraphs) that contain differences. This means that if there are minor changes within any given sentence or paragraph, you still have to manually review that section to find the difference.


-


**Limited accessibility** . QuickDiff uses only visual cues to highlight differences, which isn't helpful if you have limited vision.


### Diffchecker


[Diffchecker](https://www.diffchecker.com/) is a full file comparison tool that can highlight differences in code, documents, and images. It has a web app, but you can also buy the desktop version if you want to avoid uploading your documents to the platform's cloud servers.


The platform relies on optical character recognition (OCR) to scan PDFs, compare images, and scan multiple Excel documents. To scan full folders, you'll need to pay for the Pro version, which starts at $21/user/month.


Here are some of the downsides of choosing Diffchecker:


-


**There's no mobile support.** Diffchecker doesn't have a mobile application for "on-the-go" document checking.


-


**The desktop version requires payment.** The free version requires you to upload documents or paste text individually, while the desktop version lets you access files without uploading them to a cloud server.


## Play spot the difference for fun—not for work


I love a good game of *Spot the difference* when it's strictly for entertainment (or a cheesy participation ribbon). But the stakes are infinitely higher when you need to do it in a professional capacity.


Don't risk the chance of human error—or scaring off your coworkers if you make the same furiously scrunched face I do. Use your word processor's built-in features or an online comparison tool to spot the differences for you.
