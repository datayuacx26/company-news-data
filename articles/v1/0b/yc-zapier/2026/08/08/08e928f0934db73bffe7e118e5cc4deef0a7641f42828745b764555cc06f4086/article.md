# AI job search tips: 9 AI tools to help you land your next job

From writing your resume for you to automating networking emails, there are tons of ways[AI](https://zapier.com/blog/what-is-ai/) can help streamline your[job search](https://zapier.com/blog/how-to-find-a-job) . But more importantly, AI can also help you cut through the noise, secure interviews, and impress hiring managers—as long as you use it well.


According to a[survey from Software Finder](https://softwarefinder.com/resources/ai-and-the-job-search) , 75% of job seekers use AI tools as part of their job search, with 14% using AI throughout the entire application process. Those who did were more likely to secure a higher-paying job than their previous role (77% compared to 48% of those who *didn't* use AI).


But there are also a lot of bad ** ways to use AI for job searching, so I scoured the trove of AI job search tools, use cases, and how-tos out there to find the best options. These are the nine tools I'll ** use next time I'm looking for a job—I hope they help you too.

### 1. AI resume builders (to help draft and format your resume)


Condensing an entire job into three high-impact bullet points is no easy feat, and if you're like most humans, there's nothing you dread more than talking about yourself. I've easily lost hours staring at a blinking cursor, writing, deleting, rewriting, condensing—and on and on.


An AI resume writer—like[Kickresume](https://www.kickresume.com/en/) ,[Teal](https://www.tealhq.com/tools/resume-builder) ,[Huntr](https://huntr.co/) , and[Resume Worded](https://resumeworded.com/) , among others—will do most of that work for you. All you need to do is fill in your job title. Then the[AI resume builder](https://zapier.com/blog/best-resume-builder/) will use AI to come up with relevant bullet points. You can ask the tool to generate as many bullets as you need, then choose those most relevant to your actual work experience. The tools can also write your resume summary, education, skills, and other sections.


Once the writing part is handled, you choose from a bunch of design templates and customize colors, fonts, and more to make it feel true to your personal brand.

### 2. AI job matching tools (to help you find good-fit roles)


Sorting through job listings, hunting down requirements and salary ranges, and doing your due diligence on the company culture all eat up valuable energy you could be spending on impressing hiring managers and securing the right role.


That's where AI job matching platforms can help: they use AI to match you with job listings likely to be a good fit for both sides, based on your skills, experience, and what you're looking for in a company or position. Platforms like[Talentprise](https://www.talentprise.com/ai-job-discovery/) ,[ResumeNerd](https://www.resumenerd.com/) ,[Autojob](https://autojob.app/) , and[Sonara](https://www.sonara.ai/) are all good options here.


With Talentprise, for example, you'll complete a series of questions on your preferences for a job (like location, willingness to relocate, remote vs. in-office, and when you're available to start) and fill out your "biography," which covers resume stuff like work experience, skills, and education. Then, you'll complete the Talentprise assessment, which measures personality and work traits, along with your values and motivation, to create a holistic profile for you.


Then Talentprise's AI matches you with relevant job listings on the site and surfaces your profile to those recruiters. So once you complete your profile, "applying" to best-fit jobs is as close to a passive endeavor as you could hope for.


LinkedIn has also added some AI-powered tools for job seekers with a LinkedIn Premium subscription. From the job listing page, paid users will see "Determine your fit and how to stand out" along with a few sample prompts.


It's a chatbot-like experience, and you can ask the AI questions like "Am I a good fit?" and "How can I best position myself?" You can also get AI help to tailor your resume for the job and create a cover letter.

### 4. AI resume optimization tools (to tailor your resume for specific roles)


The reality of applying for jobs now is much different from what it was a decade ago. Today, many recruiters and hiring managers rely on algorithms and automated[applicant tracking systems (ATS)](https://zapier.com/blog/best-applicant-tracking-systems/) to scan applicant resumes for the right keywords. If those terms don't feature in your resume, you may get kicked to the "no thanks" pile well before a human ever sets eyes on your application—even if your skills and experience are actually a perfect fit.


AI optimization tools, like[Teal](https://www.tealhq.com/) ,[Enhancv](https://enhancv.com/resources/resume-checker/) ,[Kickresume](https://www.kickresume.com/en/resume-checker/) ,[Resume Worded](https://resumeworded.com/) ,[Huntr](https://huntr.co/) , and[SkillSyncer](https://skillsyncer.com/) , ensure your resume makes it past discerning ATS algorithms and into the "interview please" pile.


These tools compare your resume,[cover letter](https://zapier.com/blog/how-to-use-chatgpt-to-write-a-cover-letter) , and even LinkedIn profile against the keywords found in the job listing you select. They rate how well your resume matches with the role and offer suggestions for how to better optimize your resume for each job.


Here's what a match rate report looks like in SkillSyncer.


Many of the above tools can analyze and grade your LinkedIn profile, too.[Careerflow](http://careerflow.ai/) is another option for that. The tool provides actionable suggestions to optimize your LinkedIn and an interactive AI wizard to help you put those suggestions into action—all in a compact and easy-to-use Chrome extension.


### 5. AI cover letter generators (to write a cover letter draft)


Writing personalized cover letters for each role is possibly the biggest time suck when applying to jobs—but it's also necessary to make an impression. You can[use ChatGPT (or any AI chatbot) to write a cover letter](https://zapier.com/blog/how-to-use-chatgpt-to-write-a-cover-letter/) . Or you can go with a purpose-built AI-powered cover letter generator like[Kickresume](https://www.kickresume.com/en/ai-cover-letter-writer/) and[CoverDoc.ai](https://coverdoc.ai/) to make the process a whole lot faster and easier.


Provide some basic details about the job you're applying for, your preferred writing style, and your LinkedIn profile, and the tool will generate a personalized cover letter draft for you. ### 6. Gemini for Google Sheets AI function (to personalize networking at scale)


[Networking](https://zapier.com/blog/networking-through-human-connection/) —when done in a tasteful, genuine way—can be an effective way to connect with potential employers and industry contacts. But it can be challenging to personalize these messages at scale. That's why most cold outreach is, well, crap.


One approach is to use AI to generate personalized outreach messages en masse. Input a list of recipients and some basic information about each person (e.g., their name, job title, and company). Then you can[prompt Gemini to generate a unique message](https://support.google.com/docs/answer/15820999) for each person based on this data.


The new AI function works much like a typical formula: you type` =AI` and, within parentheses, your prompt, and specify which cells Gemini should reference.


You can keep it simple like this, or ask Gemini to further personalize messaging based on the recipient's LinkedIn profile, tweets, psychological AI, and post engagement.


To use the AI function, you'll need a paid Google Workspace account that includes Gemini for Google Sheets. Alternatively, you can use the[GPT for Sheets add-on](https://workspace.google.com/marketplace/app/gpt_for_sheets_and_docs/677318054654?hl=en&pann=sheets_addon_widget) for free. It takes a few more steps to get set up (you can find a[detailed how-to from Expandi](https://expandi.io/blog/linkedin-outreach-using-gpt) ), but it will ultimately land you the same functionality.


Once again, you'll need to review each message and make sure it's authentic to who you are as a professional, but this tool can streamline the outreach process by a massive amount.


### 7. AI-powered interview coaching tools (for interview practice and feedback)


[Interviews can be nerve-racking](https://zapier.com/blog/interview-paralysis/) , but AI-powered interview coaching tools like[Interviews by AI](https://interviewsby.ai/) ,[Interviewer.AI](http://interviewer.ai/) ,[Huru](https://huru.ai/) , and[AIApply](https://aiapply.co/ai-job-interview) can help you practice and get feedback in a low-stakes environment. You can also use[Gemini Live](https://gemini.google/overview/gemini-live/) in the same way.


These tools simulate a real interview and give you personalized feedback based on your responses. It can help you identify areas where you need to improve, like body language, tone of voice, or specific interview questions you struggled with. Some tools even tailor the questions you're asked based on common job titles or even custom job descriptions you provide.


By practicing with these tools, you'll feel more confident and prepared for those higher-stakes real interviews. You'll also have a better sense of what to expect, which can help calm your nerves and let you focus on nailing that first impression.


There are even real-time AI tools like Final Round's[Interview Copilot](https://www.finalroundai.com/interview-copilot) to help guide you through active interviews in real-time.


### 8. AI-powered negotiation tools (to get the best offer)


Negotiating your salary can be a fraught process—especially if you're not sure what a fair salary range looks like for your position and experience. AI-powered compensation tools like[Payscale](https://www.payscale.com/for-individuals) can help.


These tools use AI to analyze data from thousands of job listings and salary surveys to provide you with an estimate of what *you* should earn based on details like the job title and your experience, education, and location. They can also offer insight into the benefits and perks you should ask for—like flexible hours or[remote work options](https://zapier.com/blog/how-to-ask-to-work-from-home/) . Plus, you can fill out granular information on any existing job offers you have to see how they stack up.


You can even[use an AI chatbot to help judge a job offer](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/how-use-ai-negotiate-your-salary-bethany-ren/) , sussing out whether the offer is fair, identifying additional salary or benefits to ask for, and even running through mock negotiations.


By using these tools to research salaries before you sit down at the negotiating table, you'll have a better sense of what to expect and be better equipped to make a strong case for what you deserve.


### 9. Your brain (for being a human)


AI can add a ton of speed—and scale—to your job hunt. But quick reminder: both you and the hiring manager are human. When you're applying to and interviewing for a new job, your application materials need to speak to who you are and what you're looking for in a role and company. And they need to be honest: your new company will figure it out very quickly if you don't actually have the skills and strengths the robot made it sound like you have.


As more job seekers turn to AI—and hiring managers drown in heavily AI-generated applications—it's more important than ever to humanize and differentiate your job hunt.


While AI can help you move more quickly, it's always worth investing the time to ensure the results represent you, the human. Not to mention, other applicants will be using AI, too, so if you don't humanize your application materials, you won't be able to cut through the noise.
