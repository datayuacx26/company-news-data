## Buffer is better for creators and small businesses, but bigger orgs will prefer Hootsuite


All businesses, from solopreneurs to large organizations, are on social media—but not all of them have the same needs. If you're just starting and need something simple to schedule your posts and manage your social media engagement, Buffer is the better (and far more affordable) choice. But if you're running a highly visible social media account, Hootsuite's features are far more powerful.


I'll start with analytics. Buffer recently revamped its analytics tool, which is now dubbed *Insights* . You can see performance across all of your channels, view charts and reports, and—as you might guess from a tool called "Insights"—there are plenty of recommendations built in. Buffer does a good job of distilling your performance data into practical takeaways: in the example below, it calls out a specific post that received strong engagement and suggests reposting it.


Image source:[Buffer](https://buffer.com/insights)


Hootsuite's analytics can handle everything Buffer does and more, particularly when it comes to the sort of things large organizations care about. Each of your social media profiles gets a Social Score, which aggregates activity like post views, interactions, clicks, shares, and comments so you can get a high-level sense of how you're doing. And Hootsuite's industry and competitor benchmarking makes it easy to compare your performance against industry averages, or against specific social profiles belonging to competitors.


If you're creating custom reports, Hootsuite gives you more options than Buffer. You can view aggregated performance across social networks at a glance, and filter based on attributes like post type and tag. Hootsuite also offers platform-specific filtering options: for Instagram Business, for example, you can filter based on collaborators.


Apart from offering tons of customization options, Hootsuite also attracts larger organizations by positioning itself as an all-in-one "Social Operating System." While Buffer focuses on posting, engagement, and analytics, Hootsuite goes further with a suite of five (and shortly, six) products:


-


**Wisdom** , a platform-wide AI layer


-


**Perch** , for planning, creating, and scheduling your posts


-


**Nest** , a unified inbox for handling DMs, comments, and customer support


-


**Lumen** , a social listening tool for monitoring trends and sentiment


-


**Parliament** , for providing approved content for employees to amplify


-


**Vigil** , a social media governance and compliance tool (launching soon)


I'll share more about these tools shortly, but for now, if you're coming at this decision from the perspective of a large organization or enterprise, the important thing to know is that Hootsuite has made every effort to put the social features you need into one platform. Buffer doesn't have an equivalent for Lumen, Parliament, or Vigil, and while you can handle comment engagement with Buffer, it doesn't offer a real customer support solution like Hootsuite's Nest tool.


The good news, if you're a Buffer-curious creator or small business, is that none of those missing features are really necessary for you anyway. You need to create and schedule posts reliably, scale your audience, and adapt your strategy based on how posts perform—and Buffer helps with all of that.


Here's what Buffer's platform looks like:


-


**Create** , a kanban-style home for all your in-progress post ideas


-


**Publish** , a queue for scheduled posts and approvals


-


**Community** , for responding to comments from across your social channels


-


**Insights** , for analytics and recommendations


Buffer also has some less-prominent features that make life easier for creators and small businesses. Its Streaks feature, for example, helps to subtly nudge you to build a regular posting habit by tracking how many weeks in a row you publish content—and resetting to zero if you miss a week.


Another feature that's heavily geared toward creators and small business owners is Buffer's Start Page. It's like a mix of Linktree and building your own website for your[link-in-bio](https://zapier.com/blog/link-in-bio/) . You can easily use one of Buffer's templates, customize the colors, layout, and links, and add it to your social profiles.


## Buffer makes team collaboration simple; Hootsuite is better for org-wide management


Buffer's Team plan lets you add unlimited users to your account. You can invite as many teammates, freelancers, and clients as you want at no extra cost. This is perfect for large teams or social media managers managing multiple clients, freelancers, and employees simultaneously. And the permission settings are straightforward: someone can either have admin access, full access, or approval-required access.


The simplicity of Buffer's approach to collaboration is a plus for most teams. It's easy to share feedback and add notes on posts before they're approved, and managers can approve or reject posts with a single click. Your team gets access to a shared content calendar so everyone can see what's in the queue. And since Buffer's team plans include unlimited collaborators, you don't have to worry about endless cost increases as you grow.


Image source:[Buffer](https://buffer.com/collaborate)


That said, depending on the size of your organization, Buffer's team structures and permissions can feel limiting. Let's say you're hiring a freelancer and you want to give them access to Buffer to create custom reports for you. Since there's no way to set up custom permissions that allow them to *only* handle reporting, you'd need to give them admin access, which also gives them unnecessary access to the rest of the platform.


Hootsuite gets a lot more granular. Instead of all-or-nothing permissions, you can set up custom permissions that specify exactly what certain users can do. If you have a customer support rep and you only want them to be able to reply to comments and answer inbox messages, it's easy to set up the permissions you need. There are also nifty workflow advantages, like the ability to filter your content calendar to see who's holding up approval.


You can also manage access collectively by grouping users into teams. While Buffer has a flat permissions structure, Hootsuite lets you add employees to teams and apply permissions at three layers: organization, team, and social account.


The drawback is the cost. With Hootsuite, you need to pay for each team member, which adds up fast—and might deter you from adding people who'd otherwise be useful to your feedback or approval process.


## Buffer connects to more social media platforms, but Hootsuite is catching up


Buffer tends to be one of the quickest social media tools to support new networks and new post types. A good example of this is Buffer's connection with Mastodon, a decentralized social network with fewer than a million monthly active users. (Hootsuite doesn't support Mastodon.) Buffer was also early to connect with[Bluesky](https://zapier.com/blog/what-is-bluesky/) and[Google Business Profile](https://zapier.com/blog/google-business-profile-optimization/) , though Hootsuite now supports both of those.


WhatsApp is one area where Hootsuite has an edge: you can send and receive WhatsApp messages through your inbox, which is handy in regions, like Europe and Latin America, where WhatsApp is often the default channel for customer service.


## Hootsuite has an excellent social listening feature


Hootsuite has an excellent[social listening](https://zapier.com/blog/social-listening-for-audience-analysis/) dashboard called Lumen that's now available to users on any plan. Lumen monitors over 150 million websites and 30+ social channels, and it can even detect brand mentions in images and video. Just choose the area you market in, specify the brand, topic, or person you want to track, and get a full report on what people have been saying about it.


If you want to dig deeper, Lumen includes reports on sentiment, top themes, demographics, influencers, and a world map showing where your topic is most frequently discussed.


You can also compare one topic to another. I used Lumen to pull together some data supporting the eternal "Coke versus Pepsi" debate. (Pepsi may sell fewer cans of soda, but they're winning on the social sentiment front.)


## Hootsuite's AI layer is platform-wide; Buffer has a simpler AI assistant


Both Hootsuite and Buffer have[AI features to help social media managers](https://zapier.com/blog/best-ai-social-media-management/) save time. But Buffer's AI assistant is limited to generating ideas and drafting posts. Hootsuite's AI efforts are more ambitious: its Wisdom conversational AI layer operates across the platform, handling post generation but also research, strategy, and analysis.


Every social media manager dreams of clicking a button and watching— *poof* —as their social calendar automatically populates. Buffer's AI assistant is pretty good at this: when you come up with an idea for a post, you can quickly use AI to flesh it out, then leave it on Buffer's Kanban board for review and publishing. You can rephrase, shorten, and expand your post with a click.


While Buffer's AI drafting tool was excellent when it launched in 2023, it feels a bit dated now. Audiences are getting sensitive to signs of AI writing, and Buffer's default output sounds pretty robotic. You can tweak the voice on a per-prompt basis, but that's not a particularly efficient workflow, and Buffer doesn't offer centralized editorial settings or a brand voice tool to make things more consistent.


Hootsuite's AI is in a different category. Wisdom, an AI layer, works across all of Hootsuite's tools rather than just focusing on text generation. Whereas Buffer's AI assistant runs on an[OpenAI model](https://zapier.com/blog/openai-models/) , Wisdom is powered by in-house models that draw on Hootsuite's proprietary social data and more than 150 million data sources.


Here are a few things it can do:


-


Spot trends from social listening data and audience conversations


-


Draft social posts in bulk with prompts like *Create a week's worth of posts*


-


Create specialized agents, like a Social Media Analyst


-


Adapt your social strategy based on your performance data, industry trends, and what competitors are doing


-


Identify content that's worth amplifying through employees


You can also connect other parts of your marketing stack with Wisdom, expanding the data it can draw from to include tools like Ahrefs, Notion, and Profound.


Even with all of Wisdom's AI power, writing is still a weak link. The drafting experience is better than Buffer's, since there's a persistent AI chat window along with specific recommendations, but it still takes finagling to get something useful. Hootsuite does say that Wisdom takes your post history into account to keep the brand voice consistent as it drafts content, but I didn't see much evidence of that in my testing. Unfortunately, just like Buffer, there's no central hub for persistent brand voice settings.


## Buffer is way more affordable and has a free plan


Hootsuite has no free plan, while Buffer has a generous one—with up to three social media accounts and 10 scheduled posts per channel. The free plan doesn't have performance analytics, approval workflows, or comment scheduling, but it's still enough for creators and solopreneurs who just want to schedule their posts in advance. And you still have access to the AI assistant, storage for up to 100 ideas, and all other content creation features (like importing visuals directly from Canva).


Even when you move to the paid plans, Buffer's Essentials plan is only $5/month/channel (billed annually), while Hootsuite's starting price is $99/month (billed annually) for up to ten social media accounts. For one user publishing across 10 social channels, you'd pay twice as much for Hootsuite. Buffer also offers a discount if you need to connect more than 10 channels.


If you have a team, Buffer gets even more affordable. Rather than paying per user (like you would with Hootsuite), Buffer gives you unlimited users as long as you're on the $10/month/channel Team plan. For a team of 10 marketers managing 10 channels, you'd pay $100/month for Buffer. With Hootsuite, you'd pay $990/month ($99/month per user)—and that's assuming you're on the lowest-tier Standard plan.


If budget is at all a concern, go with Buffer.


## Hootsuite is the only real option for customer service


When Buffer and Hootsuite first launched, they were essentially post scheduling tools. But customer service is an unavoidable part of any social media effort these days, as customers rely on social platforms to raise their complaints, share their experience, and ask for help. Both apps let you reply to comments from within the platform, but Hootsuite is designed around customer service in a way that Buffer isn't.


For starters, Hootsuite offers granular permissions over who has access to your social media inbox—which includes DMs, comments, and mentions. You can choose who can respond to these messages, who can only view them, who can take over messaging from other agents, and more.


Inside Nest, Hootsuite's inbox tool, you can filter conversations by *Open* , *Resolved* , and *Assigned to me* . You can also trigger automated responses, set up rules to route conversations to the appropriate person, pull in teammates, and leave detailed notes so nothing slips through the cracks. If you're used to customer service tools like[Zendesk](https://zapier.com/blog/automate-zendesk-tickets/) , you'll feel right at home here.


Buffer offers Community, an engagement dashboard that helps you manage conversations across all your social networks. You can filter comments, save replies so you can quickly handle common questions, and track an internal metric ("comment score") to see how diligent you are about engagement.


Image source:[Buffer](https://buffer.com/community)


Buffer is intentional about *not* offering a dedicated customer service tool. ([Buffer used to have one](https://buffer.com/resources/sunsetting-reply/) , but shut it down after deciding it was overkill for most small businesses.) Unfortunately, this also means that Buffer doesn't manage your direct messages, so you'll need to log in to your apps for that—or find another tool if that's a make-or-break feature for you.

## Hootsuite vs. Buffer: Which should you choose?


Hootsuite is a Buffer alternative aimed at social media teams within larger organizations. If you run a small business, you probably don't need it yet, but as you scale, Hootsuite's features get increasingly relevant. Here's how to decide which option makes sense.


**Use Buffer if:**


-


You have a limited budget


-


You're a solopreneur, creator, or small team


-


You want straightforward scheduling and engagement without a learning curve


-


You don't need social listening, employee advocacy, customer service, or compliance features


**Use Hootsuite if:**


-


You're managing a large or regulated org with complex approval needs


-


Customer service through social channels is a core part of your job


-


You want one platform for content, inbox, social listening, and employee advocacy instead of stitching several tools together
