# What the world's game teaches commercial real estate leaders

For those of us who have spent a lifetime playing and following football, it has always been much more than a sport. It is a global language that transcends borders, cultures, and generations in a way few other institutions can.


An estimated four billion people follow football worldwide, making it the world's most popular sport by participation, fan base, and geographic reach. Every four years, the FIFA World Cup captures the attention of billions, while competitions such as the UEFA Champions League unite audiences across continents. With a ball and an open space often being all that's required, the game's accessibility has helped make it a defining part of communities around the world.


That global perspective is one reason I found[Willy Walker's recent conversation with Ukrainian football legend Andriy Shevchenko](https://www.walkerdunlop.com/webcast/andriy-shevchenko) so compelling. While the discussion covered everything from the World Cup to leadership and Ukraine's resilience, what resonated most with me were the lessons that extend well beyond the pitch. They are equally relevant to commercial real estate professionals navigating today's increasingly interconnected capital markets.


## Excellence is earned long before it's recognized


Shevchenko is one of the few players to have won the Ballon d'Or, football's highest individual honor. Awarded annually to the world's best player, the Ballon d'Or reflects far more than goals scored or trophies won. It recognizes sustained excellence, leadership, consistency, and the ability to perform at the highest stakes.


The same is true in commercial real estate.


Successful transactions rarely come down to a single negotiation or market opportunity. They are built on years of developing relationships, understanding markets, earning clients' trust, and consistently delivering results. Recognition, whether in sport or business, is usually the outcome of disciplined preparation rather than a singular achievement.


## Momentum favors the prepared


One of the webcast's recurring themes was momentum.


Reflecting on Spain's victory over France, Shevchenko described a team that succeeded not simply because of individual talent, but because of preparation, cohesion, and belief. He contrasted that with moments throughout his own career where momentum shifted unexpectedly, including the famous 2005 UEFA Champions League Final between AC Milan and Liverpool.


Commercial real estate professionals understand this dynamic well.


Markets can change quickly. Interest rates move. Capital becomes more or less available. Investor sentiment shifts. Momentum is rarely predictable, but organizations that have invested in strong relationships, thoughtful strategy, and disciplined execution are far better positioned to respond when market conditions evolve.


Success is seldom about reacting fastest. More often, it comes from having prepared long before the opportunity presents itself.


## Teams outperform individual stars


One of Shevchenko's observations struck me as particularly relevant for our industry.


Discussing England's national team, he noted that success comes not from assembling the most talented individuals, but from building the strongest team. The collective always outweighs the individual.


That principle applies directly to commercial real estate.


Complex transactions require expertise across debt, equity, investment sales, valuation, legal, and advisory disciplines. No single professional closes a transformative transaction alone. The best outcomes emerge when specialists work together toward a shared objective, bringing different perspectives while remaining aligned around the client's goals.


In today's environment, collaboration has become a competitive advantage.


## Great leaders continue learning after every performance


Another aspect of Shevchenko's career stood out to me.


Even after winning major titles, he described reviewing match footage to identify where he could improve, studying both his individual decisions and his contribution to the team's overall performance. Continuous improvement became part of his routine rather than something reserved for setbacks.


The same mindset distinguishes the most successful real estate professionals.


Every transaction offers lessons. Every financing, acquisition, or disposition provides an opportunity to refine assumptions, improve execution, and strengthen future decision-making. Organizations that create a culture of learning often build advantages that compound over time.


## Resilience creates lasting value


The conversation inevitably turned to Ukraine and the extraordinary resilience demonstrated by its people over the past several years.


Regardless of industry, resilience is ultimately about adapting to uncertainty while continuing to move forward. Shevchenko spoke about innovation, leadership, and maintaining optimism despite extraordinary challenges. Those qualities are equally valuable in commercial real estate, where market cycles continually test organizations' ability to remain disciplined and focused.


The firms that endure are rarely those that avoid adversity altogether. They are the ones who continue to invest in relationships, people, and long-term strategy even when conditions are difficult.


## A global perspective for a global industry


Commercial real estate has become increasingly international. Capital crosses borders, investors seek opportunities across continents, and local markets are influenced by global economic forces.


Football has operated this way for decades.


The world's best players compete internationally. Clubs attract supporters from every continent. Global tournaments bring together nations with different cultures but shared aspirations. There is a lesson in that interconnectedness.


Leadership today requires understanding not only local markets but also the broader forces shaping the global economy. It requires curiosity, adaptability, and an appreciation for the fact that lasting success is built through relationships that extend well beyond individual transactions.


That may be why conversations like[Willy Walker's with Andriy Shevchenko](https://www.walkerdunlop.com/webcast/andriy-shevchenko) resonate so strongly. On the surface, they are about football. Beneath the surface, they remind us that leadership, preparation, resilience, and teamwork are universal principles, whether your field is a football pitch or the global commercial real estate market.
