### How do red light therapy devices work?


Red light therapy devices work by delivering specific wavelengths of red and near-infrared light into your skin, where they interact with your cells to support energy production and healing. This process (known as photobiomodulation) may help reduce inflammation, improve circulation, and promote tissue repair.


At a basic level, these devices use **low-level light therapy (LLLT)** . The light penetrates your skin at different depths, depending on the wavelength, and reaches your cells' mitochondria, which are responsible for producing energy.


Here's a simple breakdown of what happens:


- **Light absorption:** Your skin absorbs red (around 630–660 nm) and near-infrared light (around 800–880 nm)
- **Cellular energy boost:** The mitochondria use this light to produce more ATP (cellular energy)
- **Improved function:** Cells may repair faster and function more efficiently
- **Supportive effects:** This can lead to reduced inflammation, improved skin health, and pain relief


Red light tends to target **surface-level concerns** like skin health, while near-infrared light penetrates deeper, making it more useful for muscles and joints.


It's important to remember that results depend on consistent use and the specific condition you're targeting. Not all devices are created equal, which is why choosing the right one matters—especially if you plan to use your HSA or FSA.

### How to evaluate the specs of a red light therapy device


When you're comparing red light therapy devices, the specs can quickly get confusing. Focusing on a few key factors will help you choose a device that actually delivers results—and aligns with HSA or FSA eligibility requirements.


1. **Wavelength:** Wavelengths determine how deeply the light penetrates your body, with red light (around 630–660 nm) targeting skin and near-infrared (around 800–880 nm) reaching deeper tissues. Look for devices that clearly list these ranges for your intended use.
2. **Irradiance and dose:** Irradiance measures the power of the light delivered to your skin, while dose refers to the total energy over time. Higher isn't always better, but you want a device that provides consistent, clinically relevant output.
3. **Treatment time:** Each device recommends a specific session length based on its power and design. Shorter treatment times often indicate higher efficiency, but consistency matters more than speed.
4. **Beam angle and coverage:** Beam angle affects how concentrated or spread out the light is, while coverage determines how much of your body you can treat at once. Wider coverage is better for convenience, while narrower beams can offer more intensity.
5. **Build safety:** Look for features like proper heat management, FDA registration (if applicable), and eye protection guidelines. Safety matters especially if you plan to use the device frequently or on sensitive areas.
6. **User experience (UX):** A good device should be easy to set up and use consistently, with intuitive controls and clear instructions. If it's complicated, you're less likely to stick with it.
7. **Budget tiers:** Prices can range from under $100 for small devices to several thousand dollars for full-body panels. Higher cost doesn't always guarantee better results, but extremely cheap devices may lack the power needed for effective treatment.
8. **Published third-party research:** Look for devices backed by independent studies or clinical data, not just marketing claims. This is especially important if you plan to justify your purchase with a Letter of Medical Necessity (LMN).


Taking the time to evaluate these specs helps you avoid wasting money on ineffective devices—and ensures you're making a smart, compliant purchase with your Health Savings Accounts (HSAs) or Flexible Spending Accounts (FSAs).

## How to use red light therapy devices correctly to see results


There is no one-size-fits-all approach to using red light therapy devices. The device type and recommended application will vary depending on the medical condition you have and the treatment plan you're on.


### How long should a single red light therapy session be?


Most red light therapy sessions last between **10 to 20 minutes per treatment area** , depending on the device's power and your specific use case. Higher-powered devices may require shorter sessions, while lower-powered ones often need more time.


Always follow the manufacturer's guidelines, since overuse doesn't necessarily improve results. Consistency is more important than longer sessions.


### How often do you need to use a red light therapy device for it to work?


Most red light therapy devices are used **3 to 5 times per week** , though some people use them daily depending on their goals and the device's intensity. For chronic conditions or recovery, more frequent use is common early on, followed by a maintenance routine.


Consistency is the most important factor, as results build over time rather than from a single session. Always follow the manufacturer's recommendations to avoid overuse and get the best long-term results.


### How far away from your skin should you hold a red light therapy device?


Most red light therapy devices should be used about **6 to 12 inches** away from your skin, but the ideal distance depends on the device's power and design. Higher-powered panels often work best at a slight distance, while lower-powered or wearable devices may be used directly on the skin.


For example, some panel sized devices recommend you stay 12 to 24 inches away while some LED masks or wraps require direct contact for treatment to be effective.


Distance affects how much light energy your body absorbs, so staying within the recommended range is key for effectiveness. Always follow the manufacturer's guidelines, since distance affects how much light energy your body absorbs.


### How should you prepare your skin before a red light therapy session?


Start with **clean, dry skin** by gently washing your face or the treatment area to remove makeup, oils, lotions, or sunscreen. This ensures the light can penetrate effectively without any barriers blocking absorption.


Remove jewelry that may be in the way of the light treatment itself, and consider removing other wearables that will make it difficult for you to complete your application. Avoid applying skincare products right before your session unless the device specifically recommends it. Some ingredients can reflect light or cause sensitivity, which may reduce effectiveness or increase irritation.


It's also a good idea to **check your skin for sensitivity or irritation** before starting, especially if you're treating a specific condition. Proper prep helps you get more consistent results and reduces the risk of discomfort during your session.


### How can you measure whether a red light therapy device is working for you?


The best way to measure results is by tracking consistent, real-world improvements in the condition you're treating over time. Red light therapy doesn't deliver instant results, so progress is usually gradual.


Here are a few simple ways to evaluate effectiveness:


- **Track symptom changes:** Pay attention to reductions in pain, inflammation, stiffness, or skin issues. Small improvements over a few weeks are a good sign it's working.
- **Take progress photos:** For skin-related concerns, photos taken weekly can help you objectively see changes in tone, texture, or acne.
- **Monitor recovery time:** If you're using it for muscle recovery, note whether soreness decreases faster after workouts.
- **Keep a usage log:** Track how often and how long you use the device, along with any changes you notice. This helps you stay consistent and spot patterns.
- **Evaluate after 4–8 weeks:** Most people start noticing measurable results within this timeframe if the device is effective and used correctly.


If you want to be more technical in your approach, you could also run tests to verify that it's actually working by using a camera or solar power meter to verify that it's emitting specific wavelengths of light.


In short, consistency and tracking are key. If you're not seeing results after several weeks, it may be worth adjusting your routine or reevaluating the device you're using.

## 6 safety essentials when using red light therapy devices in the home


Using a red light therapy device at home is generally safe when done correctly, but it's important to follow a few key precautions. Understanding these safety essentials helps you avoid irritation, protect your eyes, and get the most effective results from each session.


### 1. Protect your eyes


Since red light therapy uses light, one of the main considerations is direct exposure to your eyes. Since this light can be intense, it's always recommended that you use protective measures to keep your eyes safe. Use protective goggles when treating your face or staying close to a panel. Avoid staring directly at the LED lights and keep your eyes closed during treatment.


### 2. Follow proper usage guidelines


As with any medical treatment, it's important to stick to your treatment plan. Make sure you follow the manufacturer's instructions, adhering to the recommended session time and frequency, avoiding overuse. Maintain a safe distance when applying treatment, positioning the device 6 to 12 inches away for optimal penetration without overheating.


### 3. Prepare your skin for treatment


To be effective, RLT requires red and near-infrared light to penetrate the skin and stimulate mitochondria. Products like sunscreen, makeup, and lotions can block the light and reduce its effectiveness. Even dirt can impede the process and impact results. Make sure you clean and dry your skin before application so nothing is going to block the red light treatment.


### 4. Store and handle with care


When the device isn't in use, make sure you store it properly and safely. Before putting it away, ensure the device has fully cooled down, especially if you used it for a long treatment. Store it somewhere that children can't access it to avoid potential misuse.


### 5. Consider your health history


Factor in your pre-existing health conditions and history. While RLT is not inherently invasive or dangerous, there can still be complications or conditions that may cause you to reconsider using RLT. If you have a photosensitive condition or take photosensitizing medication, you may want to check with your medical practitioner before starting RLT treatment.


### 6. Be cautious with certain conditions


Red light therapy is a relatively new treatment method, so it's still in early stages of research and development.
