# Food Is Medicine Programs: How Centene Helps Members Improve Health

*Food Is Medicine (FIM) programs integrate nutrition support directly into care, helping people better manage chronic conditions and broader health needs, including pregnancy. At Centene, these programs provide practical options, including medically tailored meals and produce prescriptions, to help address conditions like diabetes, hypertension, and heart disease.*


## Why Nutrition Access Matters For Better Health


Access to nutritious food helps people follow care plans and manage chronic conditions more effectively. Chronic conditions can affect daily life, overall health and quality of life, and they are also a major driver of healthcare costs.[More than 75% of adults](https://www.cdc.gov/pcd/issues/2025/24_0539.htm) in the United States live with at least one chronic condition, many of them[diet-related](https://www.cdc.gov/pcd/issues/2025/24_0539.htm) . When people do not have consistent access to nutritious food, managing these conditions becomes more difficult, which can lead to worse outcomes. Nutrition access is an important part of managing many chronic diseases.


## How Centene Connects Nutrition Support To Whole-Person Care


Centene’s approach to supporting members with diet-related chronic conditions goes beyond providing access to nutritious food. Food Is Medicine programs are clinically integrated nutrition interventions, recognizing that food can be an important part of treatment. These programs help ensure members not only have access to healthy food, but also receive the guidance and support needed to make nutrition part of their overall care plan. That means helping people access the right foods, understanding how nutrition supports their health and building habits that can improve long-term outcomes.


Haleta Belai, Vice President, National Drivers of Health, Centene


“When food is part of clinical care, it changes how people manage their health,” said Haleta Belai, Vice President, National Drivers of Health, Centene. “We see members who aren’t just getting a meal — they are getting personalized support that reflects their everyday realities. Whether through nutrition education, condition-specific guidance, or access to healthy foods that fit their budget and lifestyle, these programs empower people to make lasting changes that improve their health and well-being.”


Research shows that when nutrition support is integrated into care, it can help people better manage chronic conditions and improve overall health. That includes setting clear, realistic goals, coordinating nutrition support alongside other health needs and connecting members to resources like nutrition counseling when it can help. It also means paying attention to what is working, so programs can continue to improve and expand.


Community partnerships are central to how this work comes to life. Across the country, Centene collaborates with local partners, including grocers, farmers’ markets, regional organizations, and other community-based food suppliers, to help ensure members can access nutritious food. By sourcing food through local vendors whenever possible, these partnerships not only make it easier for members to receive culturally relevant and accessible nutrition support, but also help strengthen local food systems and support local economies. Together, these partnerships connect members to care where they are, helping them better manage their health while creating positive impacts in the communities they call home.


## Food Is Medicine Programs Are Designed to Meet Different Member Needs


FIM programs connect nutrition support to healthcare. Centene's approach focuses on connecting members to the type of nutrition support that best fits their needs through three core programs:


- **Medically Tailored Meals (MTMs):** Structured meal support for people with chronic conditions, such as members recovering from a recent hospitalization.


- **Medically Tailored Groceries:** Condition-specific groceries for members managing a chronic condition at home, as well as postpartum mothers who need convenient access to nutritious foods that support recovery and overall health.


- **Produce Prescriptions:** Vouchers or incentives for fruits and vegetables, often supporting individuals at risk for or managing diet-related conditions such as prediabetes or obesity.


Each program serves a different purpose. Some members need more structured meal support at key moments, while others benefit from groceries and coaching that help them build healthy habits. These programs are tailored to individual needs, integrated into care and typically last four weeks to six months. They are designed to complement—not replace—existing nutrition assistance programs by providing targeted nutrition support tied to a member’s health condition and care plan.


## How Food Is Medicine Is Delivering Results


Research and early program results show how FIM programs make a difference.


[Studies show](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamainternalmedicine/fullarticle/2730768) that MTMs may reduce hospitalizations and lower overall healthcare costs. Produce prescriptions have been associated with better nutrition and reduced food insecurity, with[growing evidence](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamainternalmedicine/fullarticle/2730768) across other FIM programs showing similar potential.


These programs have also been linked to improvements in clinical measures such as blood sugar, blood pressure and weight, helping people better manage their conditions.


Together, research and experience from FIM programs point to a simple outcome: when people have better access to nutrition support, they are better able to manage their health, stay connected to care and support healthier outcomes.
