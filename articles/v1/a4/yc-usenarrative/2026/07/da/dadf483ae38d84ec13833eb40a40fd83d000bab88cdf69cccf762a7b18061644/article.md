# The 2026 World Cup Was Won on Short Form Video


The tournament is over, the numbers are published, and one of them is doing something the others are not.


FIFA's digital summary, released on July 8 with the last eight still alive, reported 30 billion impressions, 1.7 billion engagements and 14.5 billion video views across TikTok and YouTube alone, with roughly 20 billion video views across all platforms. Measured against the same number of tournament days in 2022, impressions rose 130 percent and engagement rose 160 percent. Video views rose 485 percent.


Those three growth rates are not supposed to be that far apart. When they are, it means something structural changed in how people consume the event.


## What the gap means


Impressions measure how many times something appeared. Video views measure how many times somebody chose to watch. Impressions more than doubled, which tracks with a bigger tournament, 48 teams and 104 matches instead of 32 and 64. Video views went up almost six times, which does not track with anything about the format.


What changed is behavior. In 2022 a large share of World Cup consumption was reading about it. In 2026 it was watching clips of it. Fans did not just see more World Cup content, they watched dramatically more of what they saw, and they watched it in the form of short vertical video.


The individual results back this up. The most viewed clip of the tournament on TikTok was the Viking Row celebration at 174 million views. Neymar congratulating the Norwegian players after their match reached 70 million. Neither of those is a goal. Both are moments that a broadcast producer might not have flagged and that a fan absolutely did.


## The rest of the picture


None of this came at the expense of the live product. Attendance through the round of 16 reached 6,259,584, filling 99.7 percent of available seats at an average of 65,204 a match, and FIFA added 54 million followers during the tournament, 23 million of them on TikTok. The clip is what brings the next person to the stadium.


For everyone who is not FIFA, the lesson is uncomfortable in a useful way. The World Cup had a global operation cutting content across dozens of channels and languages simultaneously.
