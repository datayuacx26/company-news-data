# Women's Sports Got the Distribution. Now It Needs the Volume.


For most of the last decade, the argument about women's sports was an argument about access. Put the games somewhere people can find them and the audience will follow. That argument has now been settled, and the settlement is producing a new problem.


Nielsen reported in March that US audiences consumed 46 billion minutes of women's sports in 2025. NWSL regular season viewership was up 61 percent. The 2025 NWSL Championship averaged 1.18 million viewers on CBS, the first game in league history to clear a million. The WNBA had its most-watched season ever, averaging 1.3 million across ESPN networks. At the Milan Winter Olympics in February, the women's hockey gold medal game between the United States and Canada averaged 5.3 million viewers across USA Network and Peacock and peaked at 7.7 million.


## What 2026 actually looks like


The distribution response has been aggressive. This season the WNBA will put a record 216 of its 330 regular season games on national broadcasts, spread across ABC and ESPN, NBC and Peacock, CBS, Prime Video, ION, USA Network and NBA TV. That is eight national partners for one league.


The LPGA went further. For the first time in the tour's history, every event and every round is available live across the country in 2026. Not a highlight window, not a weekend cut-in, all of it.


Both of those are real wins, and both of them quietly multiply the work. Eight partners means eight sets of promotional obligations, eight sets of rights windows and eight audiences arriving from different places with different expectations about what a clip should look like.


## The gap nobody budgeted for


Women's sports properties have historically run lean content operations, because for a long time there was not much to distribute and not much money to distribute it with. Those teams are now being handed a national broadcast calendar built for an organization three times their size.


This is where growth stalls if it stalls at all. A league can win the carriage fight and still lose the attention fight, because the audience that discovers a game on ION on a Tuesday finds the team the next morning through a clip, not through a schedule. If the clip is not there within the hour, the discovery does not convert into a follow.
