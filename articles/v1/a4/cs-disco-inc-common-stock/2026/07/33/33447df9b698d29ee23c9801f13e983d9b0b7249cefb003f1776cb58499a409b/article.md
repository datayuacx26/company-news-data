# The Shift to Fact-Centric Legal Timelines: Powered by Agentic AI

For decades, the legal industry has settled for some version of a status quo — teams sitting surrounded by binders of printed documents or endless open tabs, desperately copying dates from email headers, pasting text fragments into spreadsheets, and attempting to manually map Bates numbers to a narrative.


**Yet generative and agentic AI have completely rewritten this workflow.**


Historically, chronologies were built around files, tracking when a *document* was created rather than when the *underlying* *event* actually occurred. Today, the industry is rapidly moving away from rigid, document-centric chronologies toward fact-centric, AI-accelerated timelines that focus on the true sequence of human behavior.


To understand why and how this shift is transforming modern practice, we have to look at how teams actually use this data throughout the life of a matter.

### **Mapping data to the litigation lifecycle**


A timeline provides an overarching understanding of the matter itself, high-level case strategy, and key decisions throughout the case. It is a **dynamic analytical engine** , a single source of truth whose function shifts based on what the case requires at that moment.


For example:


- **Early case assessment and strategy:** Functioning as a baseline narrative repository, allowing teams to immediately evaluate case merit, estimate financial exposure, and make early, informed settlement decisions.
- **Pleadings and responses:** Serving as a verification engine, giving teams a sequential framework to cross-reference alleged events against verified data and draft precise complaints or answers.

- **Deposition preparation and execution:** Acting as a witness-specific filter, enabling teams to instantly isolate events tied to a single individual, map out targeted lines of questioning, and catch contradictions in real time.
- **Motions practice:** Structuring the case evidentiary framework, streamlining summary judgment preparation by automatically segregating undisputed facts from heavily contested allegations.
- **Trial preparation and presentation:** Transforming into a visual storytelling canvas, allowing trial teams to convert their underlying factual database into compelling courtroom exhibits that present a persuasive narrative to a judge or jury.

## **The structural divide: Document-centric vs. fact-centric timelines**


The traditional — and probably most common — approach to timelines is document-centric. To build a better narrative, however, teams must first understand the fundamental flaw in how traditional chronologies are constructed.


### **The document-centric legacy**


Document-centric timelines are built entirely around files — contracts, emails, Slack messages, or hard drives — and prioritize the moment a document was created or received. A standard legacy timeline is built in Word or Excel with rigid columns for dates, authors, recipients, a generic "details" box, and a Bates number.


The core vulnerability here is that a document-centric approach forces the team to look at the case through the lens of a document dump. It buries the actual narrative under a mountain of metadata.


### **The fact-centric reality**


Fact-centric timelines invert this model. They are organized around the actual occurrences, allegations, and interactions driving the case, regardless of when — or if — a document was generated.


From a foundational layer of facts, case teams can map multiple cross-indexed documents, witness testimonies, legal issues, and strategic notes to a single event. It treats the narrative as the anchor and the documents as the supporting evidence lines.
