# PLA vs. PLA+: Differences, Properties, and Comparison Guide

## PLA vs. PLA+: Applications Comparison


The PLA vs. PLA+ application comparison highlights that PLA is ideal for prototyping and simple prints, while PLA+ is better suited for more durable, functional parts that require improved strength and heat resistance. PLA and PLA+ are used in non-colored food packaging for products with short shelf lives, biomedical devices, and textiles. However, these applications rely on standard, unmodified PLA in regulated industries where material certification is required. PLA+ extends the usability of PLA by offering improved mechanical and thermal performance. It is suitable for additional applications, including automotive interior components (trim pieces, door panels, and floor mats) in low-stress, non-structural roles. Standard PLA is well-suited for low load-bearing applications and moderate temperature environments (below 50°C). PLA+ is used in similar applications where improved strength, impact resistance, or elevated heat tolerance (up to 60-75°C) is desired.


### PLA vs. PLA+: Part Accuracy Comparison


The PLA vs. PLA+ part accuracy comparison reveals that while they offer good print precision, PLA+ tends to provide better layer adhesion and less warping. It makes it more reliable for achieving high-accuracy prints in larger or more complex parts. PLA-printed parts tend to have lower dimensional accuracy compared to PLA+. They shrink as they cool when parts are printed via fused deposition modeling (FDM) printer. Dimensional accuracy in FDM printing is not a fixed material property; typical tolerances depend on printer calibration, nozzle size, layer height, and process control rather than whether PLA or PLA+ is used.


### PLA vs. PLA+: Speed Comparison


The PLA vs. PLA+ speed comparison shows that PLA prints faster due to its lower extrusion temperature. PLA+ requires slightly more time due to its higher temperature and improved layer adhesion. Standard PLA parts are printed faster than PLA+ parts. Average print speeds using standard PLA filaments are around 40-100 mm/s, while print speeds for PLA+ average 40-80 mm/s.


### PLA vs. PLA+: Surface Comparison


The PLA vs. PLA+ surface comparison demonstrates that PLA has a smoother finish, while PLA+ offers a more durable and slightly textured surface, making it ideal for parts requiring extra strength and impact resistance. PLA-printed parts have a relatively rough surface texture due to the extrusion process used for printing. Smooth, shiny surfaces are achieved on PLA parts with post-processing. PLA+ printed parts tend to have smoother, glossier surfaces as-printed.


### PLA vs. PLA+: Heat Resistance Comparison


The PLA vs. PLA+ heat resistance comparison highlights that PLA has relatively low heat resistance and begins to soften near its glass transition temperature of approximately 55–60 °C. PLA+ withstands higher temperatures, making it more suitable for applications exposed to heat. PLA+ formulations have better heat resistance than PLA. It is because PLA+ contains additives that allow it to maintain its mechanical properties at higher temperatures than standard PLA. However, not every PLA+ material has better heat resistance than PLA. Some PLA+ materials have equivalent heat resistance to standard PLA.


### PLA vs. PLA+: Biodegradability Comparison


The PLA vs. PLA+ biodegradability comparison shows that PLA and PLA+ are biodegradable, as they are derived from renewable resources (corn starch). PLA has a higher degree of biodegradability than PLA+, as PLA is derived from plant materials (cornstarch and sugarcane). PLA+ is biodegradable to a certain extent. However, it is not as environmentally friendly as standard PLA because of the additives used to make PLA+. Check the material data sheet of the PLA+ grade you’d like to purchase, particularly if biodegradability is a critical consideration.


### PLA vs. PLA+: Toxicity Comparison


The PLA vs. PLA+ toxicity comparison indicates that PLA and PLA+ are non-toxic and considered safe for general use, as they are derived from plant-based materials. PLA+ is not toxic, but PLA+ contains additives not used in standard PLA. It is because the additives used in PLA+ are non-toxic elastomers such as thermoplastic polyurethane (TPU). Toxicity levels are the same between PLA and PLA+.


### PLA vs. PLA+: Cost Comparison


The PLA vs. PLA+ cost comparison shows that PLA is more affordable due to its simpler composition. PLA+ tends to be more expensive due to the additives that enhance its strength and durability. Standard PLA costs \[$15 to 20\] per kg of filament. PLA+ starts at $25/kg and can reach $35/kg.
