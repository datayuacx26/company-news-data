# PLA vs. ABS: Differences between ABS and PLA Filaments

PLA and ABS are two of the commonly used 3D printing filaments, but they differ in key areas (strength, ease of use, and heat resistance). Acrylonitrile Butadiene Styrene (ABS) is stronger and more impact-resistant than Polylactic acid (PLA). ABS is ideal for parts exposed to mechanical stress (automotive or tool components) in terms of strength and durability. PLA is more suited for decorative or non-functional items where strength is not a priority in terms of heat resistance. ABS has a higher glass transition temperature, allowing it to withstand higher temperatures without warping. PLA has lower heat resistance and deforms under heat, making it unsuitable for high-temperature applications.


PLA is easier to print than ABS because it does not require a heated print bed and is less prone to warping. ABS needs a heated bed and shrink, leading to potential print failures if not appropriately managed. The cost differences stem from the materials' properties. PLA is cheaper to produce, making it more cost-effective for users focused on aesthetics or non-functional prints. ABS, with its higher strength and heat resistance, comes at a slightly higher cost due to its greater durability and applications in more demanding environments.


## What Are the Advantages of PLA Compared to ABS?


The advantages of PLA compared to ABS are listed below.


- **Biodegradability:** PLA is derived from renewable plant-based sources, making it biodegradable. ABS is petroleum-based and non-biodegradable.
- **Printability:** PLA is easier to print with and requires lower extrusion temperatures compared to ABS. PLA prints at temperatures ranging from 180°C to 220°C, while ABS needs 210°C to 250°C for optimal results.
- **Environmental Impact:** PLA has a lower environmental impact due to its plant-based origin. ABS production relies on non-renewable fossil fuels.
- **Odor:** ABS releases unpleasant fumes, requiring proper ventilation. PLA produces less odor during printing
- **Surface Finish:** PLA prints have a smoother surface finish and are ideal for detailed models. ABS exhibits a more matte finish and requires additional post-processing.
- **Strength and Durability:** ABS offers better strength and impact resistance, making it more suitable for parts that endure higher stress. It is a good alternative when smooth surface finishes are needed and falls under the "[What is PLA](https://www.xometry.com/resources/3d-printing/pla-3d-printing-filament/) ?" category.
- **Cost:** PLA is less expensive than ABS, making it a more economical choice for everyday printing needs.

ABS has a higher melting point and tends to shrink more as it cools, causing warping and less precision when printed without an enclosure. PLA’s ability to maintain stable dimensions at room temperature makes it ideal for precise prints without the need for controlled environments. ### Does PLA Require Less Bed Adhesion and Print Preparation Than ABS?


Yes, PLA requires less bed adhesion and print preparation than ABS. PLA adheres easily to most print beds without the need for a heated surface. It requires minimal preparation (a clean print bed or a light layer of adhesive). ABS, compared to PLA, requires higher temperatures and a heated bed to reduce warping. ABS benefits from an enclosure to maintain temperature consistency during printing. PLA’s ease of use makes it more accessible for quick prints and reduces the likelihood of print failures caused by bed adhesion issues.


### Is PLA Better Suited Than ABS for Decorative or Non-Functional Prints?


Yes, PLA is better suited than ABS for decorative or non-functional prints. PLA provides a smoother surface finish and more vibrant colors, making it ideal for detailed designs or visual projects. It is easier to print with, reducing the likelihood of warping or failure, which is essential for intricate, non-functional items. ### Does PLA Maintain Color and Appearance Better Than ABS Over Time?


Yes, PLA maintains color and appearance better than ABS over time. PLA is less prone to fading or yellowing when exposed to UV light, making it a more durable choice for items that are displayed or used for long periods. It holds its vibrant colors and glossy finish longer than ABS, which degrades and loses its luster with exposure to sunlight. PLA's superior surface finish makes it more resistant to the effects of aging, while ABS requires additional post-processing to maintain its appearance. ### Is ABS Biodegradable, Unlike PLA?


No, ABS is not biodegradable like PLA. PLA is derived from renewable resources such as corn starch and is biodegradable under certain conditions. It breaks down naturally in composting environments, making it more environmentally friendly than ABS. ABS, in comparison, is petroleum-based and does not decompose easily in natural environments. It takes hundreds of years to break down, contributing to long-term environmental impact. For eco-conscious applications, PLA is preferred over ABS due to its compostable nature.


### Is ABS Stronger Compared to PLA?


Yes, ABS is stronger compared to PLA. ABS has superior impact resistance and durability, making it suitable for parts that need to withstand physical stress. It performs better under higher temperatures and is less likely to crack or break under impact. PLA is more rigid but tends to be more brittle, making it less suitable for functional parts that endure wear and tear. ABS’s strength and flexibility make it the preferred choice for mechanical applications, while PLA is more commonly used for decorative or low-stress prints.


### Does ABS Resist Heat Better Compared to PLA?


Yes, ABS resists heat better compared to PLA. ABS is able to withstand higher temperatures, with a heat resistance up to ~85–95 °C, while PLA starts to deform around 60°C. It makes ABS more suitable for applications exposed to heat (automotive parts or appliances). PLA is more sensitive to high temperatures and warps or loses its structural integrity when exposed to heat for prolonged periods. ABS’s superior heat resistance makes it a better option for functional parts that need to endure higher thermal conditions.


### Is ABS More Durable Compared to PLA?


Yes, ABS is more durable compared to PLA. ABS has higher impact resistance and withstands more physical stress without cracking. It is more flexible and less brittle than PLA, making it ideal for parts that are subjected to regular handling or mechanical wear. ABS performs better in high-temperature environments, where PLA softens and loses its structural integrity. For example, ABS is commonly used in automotive parts or functional prototypes, while PLA is more suited for decorative items and low-stress applications. The added durability and heat resistance of ABS make it a better choice for long-lasting and functional parts.


### Does ABS Warp More Easily Compared to PLA?


No, ABS does not warp more easily compared to PLA. ABS is more prone to warping due to its higher printing temperature and tendency to shrink during cooling. PLA, with a lower melting point, experiences less shrinkage and is less likely to warp during printing. ABS requires a heated bed and controlled environment to minimize warping for larger prints. PLA is printed without a heated bed and tends to maintain its shape more easily. The difference in temperature sensitivity and shrinkage characteristics makes PLA a better option for prints that need to avoid warping.


### Is ABS More Flexible Than PLA?


Yes, ABS is more flexible than PLA. ABS has better impact resistance and bends without breaking, making it ideal for functional parts that require durability and flexibility. PLA is more rigid and tends to be brittle, which leads to cracking or breaking under stress. ABS’s flexibility allows it to withstand physical stress better than PLA in applications (automotive parts or tools). PLA’s rigidity, while suitable for decorative and low-stress prints, makes it less ideal for parts that need to endure mechanical forces.

## What Are the Main Material Differences Between ABS and PLA for 3D Printing?


The main material differences between ABS and PLA for 3D printing are listed below.


- **Printing Temperature** : PLA has a lower printing temperature, between 180°C and 220°C, while ABS requires higher temperatures, ranging from 220°C to 250°C. The difference affects the ease of printing, with PLA being easier to print at lower temperatures.
- **Strength and Durability** : ABS is stronger and more impact-resistant than PLA. ABS is more flexible and handles higher stress without breaking. PLA is more rigid and brittle, making it less suitable for parts that undergo mechanical stress.
- **Heat Resistance** : ABS has better heat resistance, maintaining its shape at higher temperatures (up to 100°C). PLA begins to deform at around 60°C, limiting its use in high-temperature environments.
- **Surface Finish** : PLA provides a smoother, shinier finish compared to ABS. ABS is smoothed with acetone to achieve a glossy finish, while PLA’s finish is more consistent.
- **Environmental Impact** : PLA is biodegradable and made from renewable resources, making it more environmentally friendly than ABS, which is petroleum-based and not biodegradable.


### How Do ABS Fumes Compare to PLA Fumes During Printing?


ABS fumes are compared to PLA fumes during printing by their chemical compound (styrene). ABS emits styrene, a volatile organic compound that irritates the respiratory system and causes long-term health risks if inhaled in large amounts. PLA produces minimal fumes compared to ABS, mainly consisting of harmless, biodegradable compounds (lactide). ABS requires proper ventilation or an enclosure to minimize exposure to harmful fumes, while PLA is considered safer to print with in non-ventilated spaces. PLA’s fumes pose a much lower health risk compared to the more toxic fumes released by ABS during printing. Using acrylonitrile butadiene styrene (ABS) and poly-lactic acid (PLA) presents a risk of exposure to respirable particles according to “Particle emissions from fused deposition modeling 3D printers: Evaluation and meta-analysis,” written by P Byrley and BJ George on November 12, 2018. PLA is considered safer to print with in indoor environments, as it poses lower health risks than ABS.


### Which One Is Better for Temperature (Heat) Resistance, PLA vs ABS?


ABS is better for temperature (heat) resistance compared to PLA. ABS withstands temperatures up to 100°C without deforming, making it ideal for parts exposed to heat (automotive or appliance components). PLA begins to soften at around 60°C, making it unsuitable for high-temperature applications. ABS’s higher glass transition temperature allows it to maintain its shape and strength under heat, while PLA’s lower heat resistance leads to warping or failure in hot environments. For heat-resistant applications, ABS is far superior in heat resistance, as it bears temperatures up to 100°C or more without losing structural integrity, according to “PLA vs. ABS – Which Is Better?” written by BigRep, published on June 03, 2025.

It is used for parts that require durability (protective covers and automotive components). PLA is more brittle and tends to break under stress, making it less suitable for applications requiring high impact resistance. ### Which Material Cools and Solidifies More Predictably, PLA or ABS?


PLA is the material that cools and solidifies more predictably. PLA has a lower melting point, ranging from 180 to 220°C, and solidifies fast once the print head moves away. Its cooling process is less prone to warping, and the material retains its shape better without the need for additional temperature control. ABS has a higher melting point and tends to shrink and warp as it cools. It makes ABS more challenging to work with for larger prints, as it requires a heated bed and an enclosure to prevent temperature fluctuations that affect the solidification process.


### Which Material Provides Better Overall Tensile Strength, PLA or ABS?


ABS is the material that provides better overall tensile strength. ABS has higher impact resistance and endures more stress before breaking or deforming. It is more flexible, allowing it to absorb forces without fracturing. PLA is more rigid and tends to break under stress, offering lower tensile strength. ABS is ideal for applications where durability and mechanical strength are required (automotive parts or functional components). PLA is more suited for decorative items that do not undergo significant mechanical stress.


### Which Material Shows Less Warping During Printing, PLA or ABS?


PLA is the material that shows less warping during printing. PLA has a lower melting point and solidifies fast, which reduces the likelihood of warping as it cools. ABS tends to shrink more as it cools, leading to higher stress and warping on larger prints. PLA's lower temperature sensitivity makes it easier to print without the need for a heated bed or enclosure, unlike ABS, which requires both to prevent warping. It makes PLA a more reliable material for precise prints and smaller projects.


### Which Material Delivers Higher Dimensional Accuracy, PLA or ABS?


PLA is the material that delivers higher dimensional accuracy. PLA has lower shrinkage during cooling, which results in fewer distortions and better precision in prints. ABS, on the other hand, tends to shrink more as it cools, leading to potential warping and dimensional inaccuracies. PLA's ease of printing without the need for a heated bed ensures that the printed object retains its intended shape and size. ABS requires more careful temperature control to maintain dimensional consistency, making PLA a more reliable material for accurate prints.

ABS is the material that performs better in outdoor environments. ABS has higher Ultraviolet (UV) resistance and withstands prolonged exposure to sunlight without degrading. PLA, being biodegradable, breaks down faster when exposed to outdoor elements (sunlight and moisture). PLA’s sensitivity to environmental factors makes it more appropriate for indoor use or short-term outdoor projects.

ABS is the material that has superior layer adhesion. ABS bonds better between layers due to its higher printing temperature and improved layer fusion. The higher extrusion temperature of ABS allows for better adhesion between layers, reducing the likelihood of delamination or layer separation. PLA, with its lower printing temperature, struggles to bond layers as effectively in prints that require strength and durability. ABS’s improved layer adhesion makes it ideal for functional parts that must endure stress and wear.

## What Are the Similarities Between PLA and ABS?


The similarities between PLA and ABS are listed below.


- **3D Printing Compatibility** : PLA and ABS are used for 3D printing. Each is compatible with consumer-grade 3D printers and is readily available in filament form.
- **Ease of Use:** Polylactic Acid and Acrylonitrile Butadiene Styrene are relatively simple to print with, though ABS requires a heated bed for optimal results. Its moderate printing temperatures make them accessible for general 3D printing applications.
- **Strength and Durability** : PLA and ABS offer adequate strength and durability for various printing needs. ABS is more flexible and durable, while PLA is more rigid but is suitable for a range of functional prints.
- **Surface Finishing** : PLA and ABS are post-processed to achieve smoother finishes. Sanding, painting, or chemical smoothing is possible with either material to improve the surface quality.


### What Are the Mutual Alternative 3D Filaments for PLA and ABS?


The mutual alternative 3D filaments for PLA and ABS are listed below.


- **Polyethylene Terephthalate Glycol (PETG):** PETG is a versatile filament known for its durability, chemical resistance, and ease of use. The alternative combines the strength of ABS with the comfort of printing of PLA, making it a popular alternative.
- **Thermoplastic Polyurethane (TPU):** TPU is a flexible filament with excellent elasticity and abrasion resistance. It is ideal for creating rubber-like prints and is used for phone cases, wearables, and flexible parts.
- **Nylon** : Nylon is a strong, durable, and flexible filament that offers great impact resistance and wear properties. It is suitable for functional parts and mechanical applications, but it requires a heated bed to print correctly.
- **Acrylonitrile Styrene Acrylate (ASA):** ASA offers similar properties to ABS but with better UV resistance and outdoor durability. It is ideal for outdoor applications and parts exposed to the elements.
- **High Impact Polystyrene (HIPS):** HIPS is used as a support material for ABS prints, but is printed on its own for low-stress applications.
