# 7075 Aluminum Alloy: Composition, Properties, Strength, and Common Uses

## What Are the Disadvantages of 7075 Aluminum?


The disadvantages of 7075 Aluminum are listed below.


- **Limited Weldability** : 7075 aluminum has poor weldability due to its high strength and the presence of certain alloying elements. Welding reduces the strength of the material around the weld area, making it unsuitable for applications that require extensive welding.
- **Corrosion Susceptibility** : 7075 aluminum has moderate to poor corrosion resistance, is more susceptible to stress corrosion cracking (SCC), and performs worse than 6061 aluminum in marine and highly corrosive environments unless protected by cladding or coatings.
- **Higher Cost** : 7075 aluminum is pricier than other aluminum alloys due to its complex composition and high strength. The higher cost is a limiting factor for projects with budget constraints or circumstances that do not require the alloy's specific performance characteristics.


### What Limitations Should Be Evaluated Before Using 7075 Aluminum?


Corrosion, heat sensitivity, and welding limitations should be evaluated before using 7075 aluminum. 7075 aluminum is more susceptible to stress corrosion cracking in environments with high moisture or chemicals, which compromise its durability. Heat sensitivity is another factor, as the alloy loses significant strength at elevated temperatures, making it unsuitable for applications exposed to heat. Welding is challenging with 7075 aluminum, as its poor weldability leads to weakened areas around the welds, reducing its strength. Each limitation indicates that 7075 aluminum is not suitable for all environments. Engineering judgment is required to assess the specific performance needs and environmental conditions to ensure that 7075 aluminum will deliver optimal results. Careful evaluation of each factor is essential before selection, as it provides an excellent strength-to-weight ratio.


#### Is 7075 Aluminum More Susceptible to Corrosion Than 6061 Aluminum?


Yes, 7075 aluminum is more susceptible to corrosion than 6061 aluminum. 7075 aluminum has lower natural resistance to corrosion in environments exposed to moisture or chemicals. The alloy is more prone to stress corrosion cracking compared to 6061, which has better inherent resistance to oxidation, while it does offer some corrosion resistance. 7075 aluminum requires surface treatments to improve its durability (anodizing or coating) to enhance its corrosion resistance. Each treatment helps protect the material from environmental factors and ensures long-term performance in marine or highly corrosive applications, where 6061 performs better without additional treatment.


###### Does 7075 Aluminum Require Protective Coatings in Corrosive Environments?


Yes, 7075 aluminum requires protective coatings in corrosive environments. 7075 aluminum is more susceptible to stress corrosion cracking and oxidation compared to other aluminum alloys like 6061, while it has some resistance to corrosion. Coatings are recommended to improve their durability in harsh environments, with anodizing being one of the most common methods. Anodizing improves corrosion resistance, surface hardness, and wear resistance, but it does not increase the tensile strength or yield strength of 7075 aluminum. The protective coating helps extend the service life of 7075 aluminum, making it more suitable for applications in marine, aerospace, and other environments where exposure to moisture, chemicals, or saltwater accelerates deterioration.


## What are the Common Trade Names of 7075 Aluminum?


The items listed are standard alloy designations used in different specification systems


- **Alloy 7075** : The general designation used in most regions to refer to this high-strength aluminum alloy.
- **UNS A97075** : The Unified Numbering System (UNS) designation for 7075 aluminum, used in North America for standardization.
- **AlZn5.5MgCu** : AlZn5.5MgCu is a chemical composition–based designation commonly used in European (EN/DIN) systems and corresponds to EN AW-7075.


### How Are Commercial and Industry Trade Names for 7075 Aluminum Used?


The identifiers Alloy 7075, UNS A97075, and AlZn5.5MgCu are standardized alloy designations, not trade names, and are used to identify certified grades of the alloy that meet specific standards and performance criteria. The trade names (Alloy 7075, UNS A97075, and AlZn5.5MgCu) help distinguish different grades of 7075 aluminum, ensuring that the material meets the necessary specifications for particular applications. The trade names are essential for sourcing the correct material and Mechanical properties are confirmed by temper designation and material certification, not by the alloy name alone. #### Are Trade Names Important When Sourcing Certified 7075 Aluminum Materials?


Yes, Certification and authenticity of 7075 aluminum are established through standardized alloy designations, temper, governing specifications, and material test reports (MTRs)—not trade names. Authenticity and compliance are confirmed by Alloy designation (e.g., AA 7075 / UNS A97075 / EN AW-7075), Temper (e.g., T6, T73, T76), Applicable standard (ASTM, AMS, EN, ISO), Mill Test Certificate (EN 10204 3.1 / 3.2 or ASTM equivalent). Suppliers and manufacturers can verify that the material is genuine and certified by referencing the trade names, minimizing the risk of using substandard or incorrect alloys in critical components.


###### Is 7075 Aluminum Good for Welding?


No, 7075 aluminum is not good for welding. The alloy has poor weldability due to its high strength and specific alloying elements, which make it prone to hot cracking during the welding process. 7075 aluminum experiences a significant loss of strength near the weld zones when welded, reducing the material’s structural integrity. The presence of zinc in the alloy further complicates welding, as it leads to the formation of brittle phases in the heat-affected zones. The issues make 7075 aluminum less suitable for applications that require extensive welding. 6061 aluminum is preferred in some cases, which has better weldability.


## What is the Operating Temperature Range for 7075 Aluminum?


The operating temperature range for 7075 aluminum spans from -50°C to 150°C. 7075 aluminum retains much of its strength at lower temperatures, making it suitable for use in cold environments. 7075 aluminum performance declines at elevated temperatures. The material begins to lose strength beyond 150 °C, with significant degradation occurring as the temperature increases. 7075 aluminum is inappropriate for high-heat applications. Most high-strength aluminum alloys, including 7075, 7050, and 2024, experience similar strength degradation above ~120–150 °C. For true high-temperature service, titanium alloys, steels, or nickel-based alloys are required. The alloy maintains good performance within its working temperature range but requires careful consideration when exposed to extreme heat, as its strength and hardness are notably affected by high temperatures.


### How does temperature affect 7075 aluminum performance?


Temperature affects 7075 aluminum performance by reducing its strength at elevated temperatures. 7075 aluminum begins to progressively lose strength above ~120 °C, with accelerated degradation as temperature and exposure time increase beyond ~150 °C. The heat weakens the temper of the material, leading to a loss of the strength achieved through heat treatment processes, such as T6 tempering. 7075 aluminum becomes more prone to deformation and stress, limiting its use in high-temperature environments. The reduction in strength makes 7075 aluminum unsuitable for applications requiring continuous exposure to elevated temperatures, such as in engine components or other high-heat environments. Its use is restricted in high-temperature settings, while 7075 aluminum performs well in applications that require high strength at room temperature.


#### Does Elevated Temperature Reduce the Strength of 7075 Aluminum?


Yes, elevated temperature reduces the strength of 7075 aluminum. 7075 aluminum experiences a notable decline in tensile strength and hardness. 7075 aluminum begins to progressively lose strength above ~120°C, with more rapid degradation occurring as temperature and exposure time increase beyond ~150°C. The reduction in strength occurs due to the weakening of the temper achieved through heat treatment processes like T6. 7075 aluminum is not heat-resistant, and prolonged exposure to high temperatures causes it to become more prone to deformation and stress. 7075 aluminum is unsuitable for applications that require sustained exposure to high heat, such as engine components or high-temperature environments, where alloys with better heat resistance are more appropriate.
