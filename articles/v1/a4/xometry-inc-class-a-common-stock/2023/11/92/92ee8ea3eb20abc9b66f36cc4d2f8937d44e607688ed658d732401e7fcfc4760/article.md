# 30 Types of Grinding Machines and Their Uses

Grinding machines come in many types, from light and portable to large and precise. Grinding machines have unique applications, costs, and power ratings. Manually operated grinding machines are used for low-precision cutting, cleaning, sharpening, and polishing work, while large CNC-operated precision grinders are used to create perfectly flat or cylindrical surfaces that meet tight tolerances. In all cases, grinding is a conventional process that uses mechanical force to slowly cut away material. Grinding machines can be used to create sharp cutting tools, flat cylinder heads, gear wheels, cutting tools, and much more. The article explores 30 types of grinding machines and examines how they work, how they differ, and how much they might cost you.


Types of grinding machines and their uses are listed below.


- **Angle Grinder** : An angle grinder performs cutting, grinding, and surface cleaning in a handheld format. Fabrication tasks rely on an angle grinder for fast material removal across metal and masonry. Compact design allows access to confined areas where large machines fail. Disc changes support multiple operations within one tool (cutting disc, flap disc).
- **Bench Grinder** : A bench grinder sharpens tools and removes burrs through dual rotating wheels. Workshop maintenance depends on a bench grinder for restoring edges and preparing small parts. Fixed mounting provides stability during grinding operations. Dual wheels support coarse grinding and fine finishing (rough wheel, fine wheel).
- **Die Grinder** : A die grinder handles precision grinding with small rotary bits in tight spaces. Detail work relies on a die grinder for fine shaping and polishing tasks. High-speed rotation supports accurate control over small surfaces. Compact body improves access to intricate areas (engraving, mold finishing).
- **Hand Grinder** : Hand grinder supports light grinding tasks with direct manual control. Repair work uses a hand grinder for small corrections and edge refinement. Simple design allows quick setup and operation. Portable structure supports use in different locations (surface touch-up, edge cleanup).
- **Belt Grinder** : A belt grinder uses a continuous abrasive belt for shaping and smoothing surfaces. Metalworking processes rely on a belt grinder for contouring flat and curved parts. Adjustable belt speed controls the material removal rate. Wide belt area supports even surface finishing (knife shaping, plate smoothing).
- **Pedestal Grinder** : Pedestal grinder handles heavy grinding on large components with a stand-mounted structure. Industrial setups use a pedestal grinder for rough shaping and tool maintenance. Elevated position improves operator control during grinding. Strong motor supports continuous operation under load (tool shaping, edge grinding).
- **Bore Grinder** : Bore grinder refines internal diameters with controlled abrasive contact. Engine work uses a bore grinder for accurate hole finishing. Internal grinding improves surface smoothness and dimensional accuracy. Precision control ensures consistent results inside cylindrical parts (engine bores, sleeves).
- **Precision Grinder** : Precision grinder achieves tight tolerances and fine surface finishes on critical parts. High-accuracy manufacturing relies on a precision grinder for exact measurements. Controlled movement ensures minimal material removal during finishing. Fine adjustments support detailed machining work (instrument components).
- **Universal Grinder** : The universal grinder performs multiple grinding operations through adjustable configurations. Machining setups use a universal grinder for internal and external grinding tasks. Flexible setup supports tool grinding and shaft finishing. Multi-purpose design reduces the need for separate machines (tool shaping, shaft work).
- **Jig Grinder** : The jig grinder delivers extremely accurate hole placement and finishing. Toolmaking relies on a jig grinder for complex geometries and fine tolerances. Precision movement supports detailed internal grinding. High accuracy ensures alignment in critical components (mold cavities, jig parts).
- **Gear Grinder** : Gear grinder finishes gear teeth for smooth engagement and accurate motion. Mechanical systems rely on a gear grinder for proper gear profiles. Controlled grinding improves contact between gear teeth. Precision shaping supports efficient power transmission (transmission gears).
- **Surface Grinder** : Surface grinder produces flat and smooth finishes with controlled passes. Manufacturing uses a surface grinder for finishing plates and tools. Magnetic tables hold workpieces in place during grinding. Consistent motion ensures uniform surface quality (machine bases, dies).
- **Cylindrical Grinder** : A cylindrical grinder shapes external round surfaces through rotation along a central axis. Shaft production depends on a cylindrical grinder for a uniform diameter. Rotational grinding ensures smooth and accurate finishes. Adjustable settings support different workpiece sizes (rollers, spindles).
- **Flexible Grinder** : A flexible grinder uses a flexible shaft to reach confined areas. Maintenance work relies on a flexible grinder for detailed grinding tasks. Flexible connection allows movement around complex shapes. Extended reach improves access to hidden surfaces (internal corners, narrow gaps).
- **Tool and Cutter Grinder** : The tool and cutter grinder sharpens and restores cutting tools with precise angles. Machining operations rely on a tool and cutter grinder for tool maintenance. Adjustable fixtures support different tool geometries. Sharp edges improve cutting efficiency in machining tasks (end mills, drills).
- **Center Grinder** : The center grinder restores the accuracy of 60° center holes in a workpiece to ensure precise rotation. Lathe operations rely on accurate centers for part alignment. Grinding the center hole improves stability and concentricity during subsequent machining. Precise geometry ensures the workpiece axis remains true during high-precision turning or grinding.
- **Centerless Grinder** : A centerless grinder removes material without clamping the workpiece. Production lines rely on a centerless grinder for consistent sizing. Regulating wheel controls workpiece rotation and feed. Continuous process supports high-volume output (pins, rods).
- **Form Grinder** : Form grinder shapes complex profiles with a formed abrasive wheel. Specialized manufacturing uses a form grinder for detailed contours. Custom wheel shapes define the final profile. Accurate shaping supports precision components (gear profiles, molds).
- **Plunge-Cut Grinder** : Plunge-cut grinder feeds the wheel directly into the workpiece. Machining tasks rely on a plunge-cut grinder for rapid material removal. Fixed feed direction supports groove and shoulder creation. Controlled depth ensures consistent cutting results (slot formation).
- **Wet Grinder** : A wet grinder uses a continuous flow of coolant to manage thermal expansion and prevent metallurgical damage. Precision manufacturing relies on wet grinding to maintain part dimensions and wheel life. Cooling washes away swarf and prevents the grinding wheel from loading. This setup supports high-precision finishing on hardened steels.
- **Disc Grinder** : A disc grinder uses a rotating abrasive disc for surface finishing. Fabrication work relies on a disc grinder for flat surface preparation. Large disc area supports even contact with the material. Fast rotation improves material removal speed (metal plates).

Steady power supports continuous operation. Versatile design supports cutting, grinding, and polishing (metalwork, surface cleaning).


## 1. Angle Grinder


An angle grinder, referred to as a side grinder, uses a grinding disc to cut, deburr, polish, and clean. Angle grinders are unique in that they’re portable, handheld devices. The electric motor is housed within the tool’s body or head and spins whichever attachment you affix to it. The most common attachments are grinding discs and wire wheels. The high-speed rotation of the grinding disc allows the toothless disc to cut through metal with ease. The grinding disc cuts via abrasion rather than shearing forces. Angle grinders can cut, polish, sand, and grind. Sanding and grinding functions are usually used to remove paint and rust or to deburr metal. Common commercial hand-held angle grinders have power ratings ranging from 500 to 2000 W. Prices vary depending on the make, power rating, and battery type (if cordless). The price of the angle grinder can be as low as \[$30 or run up to $900\].


## 2. Bench Grinder


A bench grinder is bolted to whatever surface the user chooses. It is typically mounted on a workbench, hence the name. It usually has two wheels: one coarse wheel for heavy-duty grinding and a finer wheel for polishing. This reduces the time spent changing wheels. A bench grinder works by spinning a grinding wheel at high speed to abrade anything it comes into contact with. Generally, bench grinders have three uses: sharpening cutting tools, shaping metal parts, and smoothing metal prior to welding. The bench grinders come in different sizes, but generally use 6-inch or 8-inch grinding wheels. Bench grinders usually have power ratings of between 150 and 750 W and cost \[$50-$1000\].


## 3. Die Grinder


A die grinder is a small, lightweight handheld grinder that’s usually operated with one hand. Die grinders are used for grinding, sanding, and polishing. They are usually used for fine detailing and light work. Air-powered die grinders are used for heavier work, and electric die grinders are used for the lightest work. Die grinders abrade material using an abrasive disc or other attachment on the spindle. The die grinder usually has a power rating of 200 to 1000 W and weighs 1.5 to 4 lbs. Die grinders cost between \[$24 and $600\].


## 4. Hand Grinder


The term “hand grinder” applies to a broad category that includes all hand-held grinding tools, such as angle grinders and die grinders. Hand grinders are sometimes also referred to as portable grinders, as all hand grinders benefit from some degree of portability, depending on whether they are corded or cordless. They can be used for grinding, sanding, and polishing. Hand grinders are usually used for non-precision work, light jobs, and situations that demand portability. Nearly all of them accept different attachments like grinding discs, wire wheels, and disc sanders. The power output of the hand grinder ranges between 500 and 2000 W, and they cost \[$30-$1,000\].


## 5. Belt Grinder


Belt grinders are similar to bench grinders in that they are fixed to a workstation. However, they use a gritted belt rather than an abrasive disc. The belt moves at high speeds to abrade the surface of any item it contacts. Belt grinding can be used for shaping, sanding, deburring, and polishing. These devices are similar in size to bench grinders and are classified by belt sizes such as 1 x 30 (1 inch in width and 30 inches in length), or 2 x 72 (2 inches in width and 72 inches long). The belt grinders come in power ratings of 500-3000 W, and cost between \[$60 and $4,800\].


## 6. Pedestal Grinder


A pedestal grinder is the same machine as a bench grinder, except it’s mounted to its own dedicated pedestal as opposed to a workbench. Pedestal grinders use abrasive discs to grind materials and usually have a fine and coarse grit disc attached at any one time. The machines can be used for grinding, shaping, cleaning, deburring, and polishing. They usually fit 10- or 12-inch discs, are rated at 500-3000 W, and cost between \[$120 and $5,000\]. The pedestal grinder prices vary depending on the power rating, quality, and whether they come with the pedestal or if it is sold separately.


## 7. Bore Grinder


Bore grinding, which is known as internal grinding, inside diameter grinding, or inner diameter grinding, is a process used to precisely grind the inner circumference of a cylindrical and tapered workpiece. The workpiece is centered in a chuck, which rotates the part at a controlled speed while the abrasive wheel rotates at high speed. The grinding cylinder is then spun in the opposite direction and engaged with the inner surface of the workpiece. Depending on the make, size, and power of the bore grinding machine, machines can cost between \[$8,000 - +$100,000\].


## 8. Precision Grinder


“Precision grinder” is a group term used to describe machines that abrade material from a part with very high precision. There are a number of grinding methods within precision grinding, including cylindrical grinding, surface grinding, jig grinding, creep-feed grinding, and centerless grinding. Since precision grinding is a category rather than a specific type, the machines used vary greatly in power and price. Similarly, the power ratings range from 500 to 24,000 W, and they may cost as little as \[$1,000\] or as much as \[$100,000\]. The precision grinder is not usually used for cutting or deburring, but instead for shaping and polishing materials.


## 9. Universal Grinder


A universal grinding machine is a stationary machine that can be used for both internal and external cylindrical grinding. Every universal grinder features a swiveling wheelhead and workhead to allow for tapered and complex cylindrical geometries. Universal grinders have a range of applications, as the name suggests. However, a common use is for sharpening all types of cutting tools. Universal grinders vary greatly in size from small workbench sizes, as in Figure 8, to much larger stand-alone units. The universal grinders can have power ratings between 500 W and 20 kW and range in price between \[$1,000 and $150,000\].


## 10. Jig Grinder


A jig grinder is a precision grinder for meeting fine tolerances and high-quality surface finishes on parts with complex geometries. A jig grinder uses a high-precision vertical spindle with planetary motion to grind circular, linear, and complex geometries in multiple locations. The price of a jig grinder rice will vary depending on make and model, but you can expect them to cost more than \[$20,000\].


## 11. Gear Grinder


Gear grinders are used specifically for producing gears. Gear grinders cut the gear teeth to improve their dimensional tolerances and surface finish. It’s usually done through one of two methods. For method one, a grinding wheel in the shape of a worm gear is meshed with the wheel gear. As the worm gear rotates, it grinds the teeth of the wheel gear. The second method uses a large grinding disc that grinds between two gear teeth at a time. Gear grinders vary in price, power, and weight, depending on the make, model, and condition. As an example, a Reishauer NZA gear grinder runs at 9 kW and weighs a little over 10,000 lbs.


## 12. Surface Grinder


Surface grinders are used to abrade the surfaces of flat parts to improve their finishes and tolerances. Surface grinders use a stationary rotating abrasive wheel, which is fed to the workpiece using a moving bed. The surface grinder is one of the most common grinding machines in machine shops (other than angle and pedestal grinders). The surface grinders are configured with horizontal wheels, while others have vertical wheels. Surface grinders can vary in price from \[$5,000-130,000\].


## 13. Cylindrical Grinder


A cylindrical grinder, as the name suggests, grinds the surfaces of cylindrical objects that can be mounted on a central axis of rotation. Common targets include cylinders, pistons, rods, bars, and tubes. There are different variations of cylindrical grinders, but the most common work similarly to a lathe. The cylindrical workpiece is placed between the head and tailstock. The cylinder is then rotated, and a grinding wheel rotates to oppose it. The grinding wheel can move in and out toward the axis as well as translate in the axial direction, just like a lathe’s tool. As well as grinding the outside diameter, some cylindrical grinders can also perform inner-diameter grinding, centerless grinding, creep-feed grinding, and plunge grinding. Every cylindrical grinder is very different, so price and size will vary. Prices start at \[$15,000\].


## 14. Flexible Grinder


A flexible grinder is a grinding disc that is powered by a long, flexible rotating shaft powered by a stationary electrical motor. Flexible grinders are used to cut, clean, deburr, and polish parts that are too large to be put into other grinding machines. The grinding wheel may be up to 5 inches in diameter, and the motor provides up to 2,500 W of power. The flexible grinders have a more consistent power output, demand less maintenance, and sell for less than universal grinders.


## 15. Tool and Cutter Grinder


A tool and cutter grinder is specifically made to sharpen cutting tools such as drill bits, milling cutters, reamers, and blades. The machine uses a stationary grinding wheel and a movable spindle that holds the cutting tool to be sharpened. Tool and cutter grinding can be either manually or CNC controlled, which will have a big impact on the price. The prices of tool and cutter grinders start at around \[$1,000\] and exceed \[$250,000\].


## 16. Center Grinder


A center grinder is a machine that restores or creates precision 60° center holes in the ends of a workpiece. Center grinder machines do not grind cylinders that don’t have central axes of rotation, such as camshafts (they require specialized cam grinders). A center grinder is a form of cylindrical grinder and works the same way as many lathes. The part is placed between the head and tail stock and rotated. The grinding wheel is then rotated the opposite way and makes contact with the part to grind the surface. The center grinders start at \[$10,000\] and exceed \[$100,000\]. Their power draw can range from around 5,000 W to over 11,000 W.


## 17. Centerless Grinder


A centerless grinder is used to grind parts that have no set axes of rotation. The centerless grinder uses two wheels that rotate in the same direction. One of the wheels will rotate in a stationary position (it is called the regulating wheel), and the other (the abrasive wheel) will not only rotate but also apply pressure to the part as it grinds. The workpiece is passed between the two grinding wheels so they can create a continuous diameter or a taper. The centerless grinders exceed 30,000 W in power and cost as little as \[$6,000\] or exceed \[$100,000\].


## 18. Form Grinder


A form grinder is a machine that uses the geometry of the grinding surface to impart its specialized dimensions to the part being ground. One example of form grinding is gear grinding, where the disc used to cut the teeth of the gear wheel is shaped with the exact dimensions of the final product. The form grinders exceed \[$250,000\] in price and 24,000 W in power.


## 19. Plunge-Cut Grinder


Plunge-cut grinding (also known as in-feed grinding) is a variation of cylindrical grinding. Prices start at \[$10,000\] and exceed \[$80,000\]. This process grinds the outer surface of an object, which is held between a head and a tailstock. The difference is that while center grinding cuts an object with a uniform diameter, the plunge-cut grinder style only creates a uniform diameter in the portion where the grinding wheel contacts it.


## 20. Wet Grinder


A wet grinder uses water during grinding to reduce heat and control dust. The machine uses a rotating stone or abrasive wheel that stays lubricated with water. The water cools the surface and prevents cracks or burns on the material. The design keeps the grinding temperature stable during long operation. The machine handles hardened metal parts, stone polishing, and tool sharpening tasks.. Operators rely on water flow to maintain smooth contact between the wheel and the material. The grinding action removes material through steady abrasion. The setup reduces friction and extends the life of the grinding wheel. The machine supports continuous operation without overheating issues. Power ratings and sizes vary depending on capacity and application for the wet grinder.


## 21. Disc Grinder


A disc grinder uses a rotating abrasive disc to remove material from a surface. The motor spins the disc at high speed to perform grinding tasks. The flat disc makes steady contact with the workpiece. The machine removes rust, paint, and excess material efficiently. Operators use disc grinders for metalworking and woodworking jobs. The design allows quick material removal on flat surfaces. The abrasive disc wears down as it grinds material. The tool supports sanding and polishing functions with different discs. The machine works in both handheld and stationary forms. Power output depends on the size and intended use of the disc grinder.


## 22. Internal Grinder


An internal grinder grinds the inner diameter of a workpiece. The machine secures the part in a rotating chuck. A small grinding wheel enters the bore and removes material from inside surfaces. The rotation of both the wheel and the part ensures even grinding. The process produces smooth and accurate internal finishes. Machinists use internal grinders for bearings and bushings. The machine handles cylindrical and tapered internal shapes. The setup allows tight tolerance control for precision parts. The grinding action improves surface finish and dimensional accuracy. The machine supports high-precision manufacturing tasks for the internal grinder.

The grinding process removes excess material gradually. The machine produces smooth and round external surfaces. Operators use external grinders for shafts and rods. The setup maintains proper alignment of the crankshaft. The machine ensures roundness and a smooth surface finish. Mechanics use crankshaft grinders in engine repair work. ## What is Grinding Machine?


A grinding machine is a mechanical tool that’s equipped with an abrasive wheel intended to make shallow cuts on metals or other materials. With the right attachments, it’s also capable of polishing or putting other finishing touches on the workpiece. This type of operation is employed to improve the dimensional accuracy or appearance of a previously machined product. Grinding machines are used on workpieces that need highly precise forms, dimensions, or surface qualities. The machines remove relatively little metal, usually between 0.01 and 0.05 mm in depth per pass. To learn more, see our[Grinding Machine](https://www.xometry.com/resources/machining/what-is-grinding-machine/) article.


### How Do Grinding Machines work?


Grinding machines work by using a rotating abrasive wheel to remove material from a workpiece through friction and controlled contact. A motor drives the grinding wheel at high speed, which brings abrasive grains into contact with the surface of the material. Abrasive grains act as cutting edges that shave off small particles during each pass, which results in gradual material removal and surface refinement. Operators control feed rate, pressure, and positioning to maintain accuracy and prevent overheating during operation. Workpieces move against the wheel through manual handling or machine-guided systems, depending on the machine type and application. Cooling fluids reduce heat buildup and maintain surface integrity during continuous grinding cycles. Different wheel types determine cutting action and finish quality based on grit size and bonding material. Precision increases as smaller abrasive particles create finer finishes on the material surface. Consistent rotation and controlled movement ensure uniform results across repeated operations. Applications range from shaping rough parts to achieving high-precision finishes in machining processes, which explains the full process of the grinding machine working.


#### Do Grinding Machines Require Specific Tools or Accessories?


Yes, grinding machines require specific tools and accessories to perform different grinding tasks accurately and safely. Grinding wheels act as the primary component that determines cutting action, surface finish, and material compatibility. Operators select wheel types based on grit size, bonding material, and application requirements (cutting disc, flap disc, diamond wheel). Mounting accessories hold and secure the wheel in position, which includes flanges, adapters, and spindle nuts that maintain stability during rotation. Workholding tools keep the material fixed and aligned during grinding, which includes vises, chucks, and magnetic tables used in surface grinding. Coolant systems manage heat during continuous operation and protect the workpiece from thermal damage. Protective guards and shields control debris and spark direction during high-speed rotation. Additional attachments expand functionality for polishing, deburring, and cleaning tasks. Proper selection of tools and accessories ensures consistent results and reduces wear on the machine.


#### Can Grinding Machines Be Used for Both Hard and Soft Materials?


Yes, grinding machines work on both hard and soft materials when operators select the correct abrasive type and settings. Abrasive wheels handle hard materials through harder grains and stronger bonding that maintain cutting edges during high resistance contact. Soft materials require a different wheel composition and lower pressure to prevent clogging and excessive heat buildup. Wheel grit size changes surface finish and removal rate across different material types (coarse grit for steel removal, fine grit for aluminum finishing). Speed control and feed rate adjustment maintain stability during grinding on materials with different hardness levels. Coolant application protects softer materials from deformation and prevents overheating on harder surfaces. Machine versatility supports work on metals, ceramics, plastics, and composites through proper setup. Correct pairing of wheel type and material ensures consistent performance and surface quality across all grinding tasks.


## What is the Difference Between Surface and Cylindrical Grinding Machines?


The difference between Surface and Cylindrical Grinding Machines lies in the shape of the workpiece and the motion used during grinding. A surface grinding machine produces flat surfaces by moving a rotating wheel across a stationary or reciprocating table. A cylindrical grinding machine shapes round components by rotating the workpiece while the grinding wheel moves along its surface. Surface grinding machines focus on flatness and uniform surface finish across wide areas. A cylindrical grinding machine focuses on diameter accuracy, roundness, and concentricity in cylindrical parts. A surface grinding machine uses linear motion to cover the work area. A cylindrical grinding machine uses rotational motion combined with axial feed for full surface coverage. Surface grinding machines suit flat components such as plates and dies. A cylindrical grinding machine suits round components such as shafts and rods. Each machine type addresses a different geometric requirement in machining processes.


### What Are Surface Grinding Machines?


Surface grinding machines are precision machines that create flat and smooth surfaces by moving a rotating abrasive wheel across a workpiece. A surface grinding machine uses a fixed or reciprocating table to position the material under the grinding wheel. Abrasive grains remove small amounts of material during each pass, which produces a uniform finish and tight tolerances. Magnetic chucks or clamps hold the workpiece in place to maintain alignment during grinding. Operators control feed rate, depth of cut, and wheel speed to achieve the desired surface quality. Surface grinding machine supports horizontal spindle and vertical spindle configurations based on the application. Fine-grit wheels produce smoother finishes, while coarse-grit wheels remove material faster. Cooling fluids reduce heat buildup and protect the workpiece from thermal damage. A grinding wheel moves across the outer surface to remove material in controlled passes and achieve accurate diameters. Abrasive grains cut small amounts of material during each rotation, which produces a smooth and uniform surface finish. Operators adjust feed rate, wheel speed, and depth of cut to maintain tight tolerances and consistent results. Cylindrical grinding machines handle external surfaces, internal bores, and tapered profiles through different configurations. ### How Does a Centerless Grinding Machine Differ From Other Types?


A centerless grinding machine differs from other grinding machines by removing material without holding the workpiece between centers or using a chuck. A centerless grinding machine supports the workpiece on a blade while a grinding wheel and a regulating wheel control rotation and feed. Grinding occurs as the workpiece passes between the two wheels, which creates a continuous and stable material removal process. Traditional cylindrical grinders require clamping and repositioning, while a centerless grinding machine processes parts in a continuous flow. The regulating wheel controls the speed and direction of the workpiece, which allows consistent diameter control across large batches. The setup reduces handling time since no centering or alignment is required for each part. Process supports high-volume production with minimal interruption during operation. Roundness and surface finish remain consistent due to uniform pressure and motion. The machine handles long, thin, or small components that are difficult to clamp in other systems. Manufacturing lines rely on centerless grinding for efficient and repeatable output in mass production.
