# Inconel Metal: Types, Materials and Composition

## What Are the Different Types of Inconel?


Inconel encompasses multiple grades, each engineered for specific thermal, mechanical, and corrosive service conditions. Grade selection depends on the operating temperature range, the corrosive media present, the required mechanical properties, and the fabrication method. The grades range from solid-solution-strengthened alloys (Inconel 600, 625) suited for oxidizing and aqueous environments to precipitation-hardened alloys (Inconel 718, X-750) engineered for maximum strength under cyclic thermal and mechanical loading.


### 1. Inconel 600


Inconel 600 is a solid-solution-strengthened nickel-chromium alloy containing approximately 72% nickel, 14–17% chromium, and 6–10% iron. The grade resists oxidation at temperatures up to 1,175°C and performs reliably in both oxidizing and reducing atmospheric conditions. Primary applications include furnace components, heat exchangers, and chemical processing equipment exposed to organic acids and alkalis. The grade exhibits high resistance to chloride-induced stress corrosion cracking, making it a standard selection for nuclear reactor components and pressurized water reactor (PWR) steam generator tubes. Tensile strength reaches approximately 655 MPa at room temperature, with retained mechanical performance at elevated service temperatures. The alloy is non-age-hardenable and relies entirely on solid-solution strengthening for its mechanical properties. Xometry supplies Inconel 600 in bar, sheet, and tube forms as part of its nickel alloy material offerings for **Inconel 600** .


### 2. Inconel 601


Inconel 601 is a nickel-chromium-aluminum alloy containing approximately 60% nickel, 21–25% chromium, and 1–1.7% aluminum. The aluminum addition promotes the formation of a dense, adherent Al₂O₃ oxide layer beneath the Cr₂O₃ surface film, significantly increasing resistance to high-temperature oxidation and scaling up to 1,230°C. Primary applications include industrial furnace components (muffles, retorts, radiant tubes), gas turbine combustion hardware, and petrochemical processing equipment exposed to sulfur-bearing atmospheres. Tensile strength at room temperature reaches approximately 740 MPa, and the alloy retains meaningful strength at 1,000°C due to the stable austenitic matrix and dual-oxide protection mechanism. Inconel 601 is the preferred grade for cyclic thermal service where repeated heating and cooling accelerate oxide spalling on lower-aluminum alloys.


### 3. Inconel 625


Inconel 625 is a solid-solution-strengthened nickel-chromium-molybdenum alloy containing 58% minimum nickel, 20–23% chromium, 8–10% molybdenum, and 3.15–4.15% niobium. The molybdenum and niobium additions provide exceptional resistance to pitting, crevice corrosion, and intergranular attack in aggressive aqueous environments, including seawater, oxidizing acids, and reducing media. The grade operates across a temperature range from cryogenic conditions to 980°C for high-strength applications and up to 1,095°C for oxidation-resistant service. Tensile strength reaches 930 MPa at room temperature without age hardening. Primary applications include subsea pipelines, offshore risers, aerospace ducting, and marine propulsion components. No post-weld heat treatment is required due to its immunity to sensitization, making it a practical choice for welded assemblies. Xometry offers Inconel 625 material for CNC machining, sheet metal fabrication, and 3D printing services.


### 4. Inconel 718


Inconel 718 is a precipitation-hardened nickel-chromium alloy containing 50–55% nickel, 17–21% chromium, 4.75–5.5% niobium, and 2.8–3.3% molybdenum. Age hardening through γ'' (Ni₃Nb) precipitate formation raises yield strength to approximately 1,035 MPa and tensile strength to 1,240 MPa, placing the grade among the highest-strength Inconel alloys available. The grade performs reliably at temperatures up to 650°C and retains weldability without post-weld cracking, a property that distinguishes it from other high-strength superalloys. Primary applications include jet engine discs, turbine shafts, aerospace fasteners, and downhole oil and gas tooling. Inconel 718 accounts for approximately 35% of all superalloy production by weight, reflecting its balance of strength, weldability, and thermal stability. Xometry provides Inconel 718 as a CNC machining and metal 3D printing material for aerospace and energy components.


### 5. Inconel X-750


Inconel X-750 is a precipitation-hardened nickel-chromium alloy containing approximately 70% nickel, 14–17% chromium, and additions of aluminum (0.4–1.0%) and titanium (2.25–2.75%) that promote γ' (Ni₃(Al,Ti)) precipitate formation. The aging treatment raises yield strength to approximately 690–900 MPa, depending on the heat treatment condition. Primary applications include gas turbine blades, aircraft structural components, springs, and nuclear reactor core assemblies. The grade retains strength and oxidation resistance at temperatures up to 980°C and performs at cryogenic temperatures without embrittlement. Relaxation resistance makes the alloy a standard selection for high-temperature spring applications in jet engine afterburners and turbine casings. Xometry lists Inconel X-750 as a premium nickel superalloy material for precision aerospace machining and high-temperature structural applications.


### 6. Inconel 690


Inconel 690 is a nickel-chromium alloy containing approximately 58% nickel and 27–31% chromium, the highest chromium content in the Inconel family. The elevated chromium level provides superior resistance to aqueous corrosion, particularly in high-temperature water and steam environments with dissolved oxygen, sulfur dioxide, and nitrogen tetroxide. Primary applications include nuclear steam generator tubing, where resistance to primary-side stress corrosion cracking (PWSCC) and secondary-side denting corrosion is critical. The grade replaced Inconel 600 in pressurized water reactor (PWR) steam generators beginning in the 1980s after long-term corrosion data indicated superior performance. Tensile strength reaches approximately 690 MPa at room temperature. The grade is non-age-hardenable and achieves its properties through solid-solution strengthening and cold working.


### 7. Inconel 792


Inconel 792 is a cast precipitation-hardened nickel-base superalloy containing approximately 57% nickel, 12.4% chromium, 9% cobalt, and additions of aluminum (3.1%) and titanium (3.9%) for high γ' volume fraction strengthening. The alloy is designed specifically for investment casting of gas turbine blades and vanes operating in the hottest sections of aero and industrial gas turbines. The grade achieves creep rupture life and oxidation resistance at temperatures from 900°C to 1,050°C, where rotating blade centrifugal stresses and combustion gas temperatures combine under the most demanding service profiles. Cobalt content stabilizes the γ' precipitate phase at elevated temperatures, extending blade service life. Inconel 792 is a directional solidification and single-crystal casting candidate used in high-performance turbine hardware.


### What Makes Inconel 625 Suitable for Marine Applications?


Inconel 625's suitability for marine applications stems directly from its molybdenum, niobium, nickel, and chromium content, which improve resistance to localized corrosion in seawater, suppressing the electrochemical mechanisms responsible for localized corrosion in seawater. Seawater contains chloride ion concentrations of approximately 19,000–20,000 mg/L, which attack the passive oxide films on standard stainless steel grades (304, 316), initiating pitting and crevice corrosion. Molybdenum content (8–10%) in Inconel 625 stabilizes the passive Cr₂O₃ film against chloride attack by increasing the critical pitting potential above the electrochemical potential achievable in seawater. Niobium (3.15–4.15%) prevents sensitization at weld heat-affected zones, where chromium depletion in lower-molybdenum alloys creates intergranular corrosion pathways. The alloy's pitting resistance equivalent number, PREN commonly calculated above 50, but seawater suitability depends on temperature, crevice geometry, flow, biofouling, galvanic coupling, and exposure duration, compared to 25–26 for 316L stainless steel, placing Inconel 625 in a corrosion resistance class suitable for seawater at temperatures up to 60°C without active corrosion protection. Subsea umbilicals, propeller shafts, and mooring components rely on the alloy's combined pitting and crevice corrosion immunity.


#### Is Inconel 718 the Most Widely Used Inconel Grade?


Yes, Inconel 718 is the most widely used Inconel grade, accounting for approximately 35% of global superalloy production by weight as of recent industry estimates. Its dominance traces to the combination of exceptional weldability, precipitation-hardened strength, and reliable performance at temperatures up to 650°C. Weldability is the critical factor that distinguishes Inconel 718 from competing high-strength superalloys (Waspaloy, Rene 41), which are prone to strain-age cracking during post-weld heat treatment. The controlled niobium hardening mechanism in Inconel 718 delays precipitation during welding, allowing fabrication of complex assemblies without cracking risk. Jet engine manufacturers depend on the alloy for turbine discs, combustor cases, and compressor blades. Aerospace fastener applications utilize Inconel 718 for bolts and studs rated to withstand clamping loads at elevated temperatures. The alloy's strength, weldability, and service history across aerospace confirm its position as the reference grade within the Inconel family.


## How Is Inconel Material Manufactured and Processed?


Inconel Material is manufactured and processed through controlled melting, alloying, forming, and heat treatment stages that preserve high-temperature strength and corrosion resistance. Manufacturing Inconel presents significant challenges at every production stage due to the alloy's hardness, work hardening rate, and thermal resistance. Inconel work-hardens rapidly during machining, increasing cutting forces compared with many stainless steels and generating high cutting zone temperatures (700–900°C) that accelerate tool wear. Carbide and ceramic cutting tools wear at rates 5 to 10 times faster when machining Inconel compared to carbon steel. Low cutting speeds (15–30 m/min for turning operations), high-pressure coolant delivery, and rigid machine setups are required to maintain dimensional accuracy and surface finish. Welding requires careful heat input control to prevent heat-affected zone cracking in precipitation-hardened grades. Grinding after machining is common to achieve tolerances within ±0.025 mm on critical aerospace components. The alloy's resistance to deformation and heat dissipation makes manufacturing cost and cycle time significantly higher than standard alloy steels.


### What Are the Primary Methods Used To Produce Inconel?


The primary methods used to produce Inconel are listed below.


- **Vacuum Induction Melting (VIM):** VIM is the primary melting process for Inconel, conducted inside a vacuum chamber to eliminate atmospheric contamination. The process melts precise quantities of nickel, chromium, molybdenum, and other alloying elements in an induction-heated crucible under high-vacuum conditions that minimize atmospheric contamination, preventing oxidation of reactive elements such as aluminum and titanium.
- **Vacuum Arc Remelting (VAR)** : VAR is a secondary refining process applied after VIM to reduce segregation and improve the homogeneity of the ingot. An electric arc melts the VIM electrode under vacuum, allowing controlled solidification in a water-cooled copper mold, producing ingots with refined grain size and reduced porosity.
- **Electroslag Remelting (ESR)** : ESR passes the VIM electrode through a molten slag layer that filters inclusions and refines the solidification structure. The process is used where VAR is not available or where specific inclusion control requirements apply in industrial turbine applications.
- **Hot Working (Forging and Rolling)** : Forging and rolling at temperatures from 900°C to 1,200°C refines grain structure, closes internal porosity, and improves mechanical properties. Hot working produces billet, bar, plate, and sheet product forms for downstream machining and fabrication.
- **Powder Metallurgy (PM)** : PM processes atomize molten Inconel into fine powder, which is consolidated by hot isostatic pressing (HIP) or selective laser melting (SLM) to produce near-net-shape components with uniform microstructure and reduced material waste.


### How Is Inconel Produced Through Vacuum Induction Melting (VIM)?


Inconel is produced through Vacuum Induction Melting (VIM) by melting high-purity nickel and alloying elements inside a sealed vacuum chamber that prevents oxidation and gas contamination. Vacuum Induction Melting is a primary melting process used for many high-performance Inconel grades,, conducted entirely under vacuum to prevent oxidation and atmospheric contamination of reactive alloying elements. The process operates at pressures below 1 Pa (0.01 mbar), eliminating contact between molten metal and atmospheric oxygen, nitrogen, and hydrogen. Raw materials are charged into a ceramic crucible inside the vacuum chamber. An induction coil surrounding the crucible generates electromagnetic fields that heat the charge through eddy current losses, melting the nickel base metal first before adding chromium, molybdenum, niobium, aluminum, and titanium in sequence. Vacuum conditions remove dissolved gases from the melt, reducing hydrogen content to below 1 ppm and oxygen to below 10 ppm, which prevents porosity and oxide inclusions in the final product. The molten alloy is poured into electrode molds for subsequent VAR or ESR processing, or directly into casting molds for precision investment castings. VIM output homogeneity directly determines the consistency of the downstream-processed alloy's mechanical properties.


#### Is Inconel Processed Through Hot Working?


Yes, Inconel is processed through hot working operations, including forging, rolling, and extrusion. Hot working is performed at temperatures from 900°C to 1,200°C, where the alloy's flow stress is reduced sufficiently for plastic deformation without cracking. Forging refines grain structure by breaking up as-cast dendrites and closing internal shrinkage porosity, producing a wrought microstructure with improved tensile strength, fatigue resistance, and fracture toughness compared to cast material. Rolling reduces ingot cross-sections to plate, sheet, and bar forms, introducing controlled deformation that further refines grain size. Extrusion produces seamless tubes and pipes with uniform wall thickness for heat exchanger and pressure vessel applications. Hot working temperatures must remain above the recrystallization temperature to prevent excessive work hardening that would cause cracking. Proper temperature control during hot working is critical to achieving uniform mechanical properties across the full cross-section of the finished product form.


## What Are the Common Applications of Inconel Metal?


The common applications of Inconel Metal are listed below.


- **Aerospace and Aviation** : Inconel is used in various aerospace components, including turbine blades, exhaust systems, and jet engines, where materials must withstand extreme temperatures and pressure variations. It maintains its strength and stability even in high-stress environments, ensuring the safety and efficiency of aircraft systems.
- **Power Generation** : Inconel plays a crucial role in power generation, especially in gas and steam turbines. The metal’s ability to withstand high temperatures and thermal cycling makes it ideal for use in power plants. It maintains structural integrity in components exposed to extreme heat, such as combustors and turbine blades, enabling reliable energy production.
- **Oil and Gas Industry** : Inconel is vital in the oil and gas industry, where it is used in applications such as heat exchangers, piping systems, and valves. Its resistance to corrosion and oxidation in aggressive environments, coupled with its heat resistance, makes it the perfect material for parts exposed to harsh chemicals and high temperatures during oil extraction and refinement.
- **Chemical Processing** : Inconel is widely used in chemical processing equipment, such as reactors and heat exchangers. The material's resistance to aggressive chemicals, high temperatures, and corrosion makes it suitable for environments where other metals would fail. Inconel ensures that equipment remains durable and efficient under demanding conditions.
- **Marine Applications** : Inconel is commonly found in marine environments, particularly in seawater systems and high-performance marine engines. Components like pumps, valves, and heat exchangers are made from Inconel due to its corrosion resistance against seawater and high-temperature stability. It is used in applications that face constant exposure to the ocean’s harsh conditions.
- **Automotive and Motorsports** : Inconel is used in high-performance automotive components, particularly in exhaust systems and turbochargers. The material’s ability to handle extreme temperatures makes it a preferred choice in motorsports, where turbocharged engines generate high levels of heat. Inconel’s durability under intense conditions helps ensure the reliability and performance of racing vehicles.
- **Industrial and Heat Processing Equipment** : Inconel is widely used in industrial applications, especially in heat exchangers, furnaces, and other heat-processing equipment. The metal’s excellent resistance to heat and thermal cycling ensures that it maintains its performance in equipment exposed to fluctuating temperatures. The durability helps keep industrial operations running smoothly.
- **Stainless Steel (SS) Applications** : Inconel is sometimes used alongside Stainless Steel (SS) to combine the advantages of both materials. Inconel offers higher resistance to heat and corrosion in environments where SS might not perform as effectively. The combination of Inconel and[Stainless Steel (SS)](https://www.xometry.com/resources/materials/what-is-stainless-steel/) is used in applications that require enhanced durability, such as in certain heat exchangers or industrial components exposed to extreme conditions.
