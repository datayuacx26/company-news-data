# How to Smooth 3D Prints: PLA, ABS, and FDM Prints

## 10. Cold Welding and Solvent Welding


Solvent Welding (also called Cold welding) is primarily a bonding method for ABS prints that can also provide localized surface smoothing.Cold welding involves applying a solvent (acetone) directly to the surfaces of two parts to bond them together. The solvent softens the outer layers of the parts, allowing them to fuse at a molecular level, creating a smooth connection. The method is used to smooth the surface by softening imperfections and joining rough sections, though it does not eliminate all flaws. Solvent welding uses a similar process but focuses on applying the solvent to a single surface, softening it to reduce imperfections and layer lines. The process helps smooth the surface but does not fully address deep flaws. The methods are effective for improving the appearance and bonding strength of ABS prints when surface smoothing and part joining are needed. Cold welding is beneficial for joining parts, while solvent welding is ideal for refining the surface of a single print. Solvent welding is better suited for smoothing surfaces rather than joining multiple parts. The primary advantage of cold welding and solvent welding is their ability to create a smooth, unified surface or bond without the need for sanding or abrasive methods. The methods do not replace the need for polishing or finishing techniques for a flawless surface. Solvent welding works well for smoothing complex geometries and smaller prints, providing a quick and efficient way to refine the surface. Larger prints require additional methods to achieve a polished finish. The methods require careful handling of solvents and proper ventilation, as the solvents are flammable and harmful if inhaled. Always follow safety guidelines to minimize risks.


## 11. Using 3D Gloop


Using 3D Gloop is an effective method for smoothing 3D prints made from PLA and ABS. 3D Gloop is a specialized smoothing solution designed to improve surface appearance by applying a thin layer to the print’s surface. The process softens the outer layer of the print and smooths out minor imperfections, giving it a smoother, glossier finish. The print is left to dry after application, resulting in a softer surface that reduces visible layer lines and imperfections. The primary advantage of 3D Gloop is its ease of use and convenience, as it does not require complex setups. The process requires careful application to avoid uneven layers. The method is beneficial for prints that need smoothing before painting, as it helps create a more uniform surface for better paint adhesion, although additional surface preparation is necessary. 3D Gloop is suited for PLA and ABS prints, providing a cost-effective solution to improve the finish of prints with minimal effort, though deeper imperfections are visible depending on the print's complexity.


## 12. Using XTC-3D


Using XTC-3D is an effective method for smoothing 3D prints when aiming for a high-gloss, polished surface. XTC-3D is a two-part epoxy resin designed for smoothing and coating 3D printed objects. The resin helps fill in layer lines and minor imperfections when applied, resulting in a smoother, glossier finish. The process does not eliminate deep flaws or highly textured surfaces. The process involves mixing the two parts of the epoxy, applying it using a brush or spray, and allowing it to cure for the specified time. The result is a durable, smooth surface that improves the print's appearance and provides added protection, though proper application is essential to avoid streaks or uneven areas. The method is beneficial for prints that require a professional, polished look (display models or functional items) that need a refined finish. XTC-3D provides a durable coating that smooths the surface and adds protection to the print, though the level of durability depends on the print material and curing process. The process is effective for prints with visible layer lines or rough surfaces, filling in imperfections and creating a smoother finish. Deep flaws are visible. The main advantages of using[XTC-3D Coating](https://www.xometry.com/resources/3d-printing/xtc-3d) are its ability to smooth the surface, ease of application, and glossy finish. ## 13. Using Epoxy Resin Coating


Using epoxy resin coating is an effective technique for smoothing and improving the surface of 3D prints for materials like PLA and ABS. The process involves applying a layer of epoxy resin over the print's surface, which helps fill in imperfections (layer lines and minor defects). The process does not eliminate deeper flaws or complex surface irregularities. The resin cures to form a smooth, glossy, and durable finish, though the final texture depends on application technique and curing conditions. Epoxy resin is applied using a brush, spray, or poured over the print, depending on the desired effect, print size, and complexity. The application method affects the finish, requiring more precision for specific geometries. The technique is beneficial for achieving a professional finish on 3D prints for items that are displayed or used in environments requiring durability and a refined appearance. Epoxy resin provides a hard, protective layer that improves the print's surface, making it more resistant to scratches, water, and wear. Formulation and application thickness determine the resistance thickness. The method provides the print with a polished, high-gloss look, making it suitable for functional or decorative objects. The degree of gloss depends on the resin type and curing conditions. The major advantage of using epoxy resin coating is its ability to create a smooth surface and improve surface durability. The final strength depends on the print material and the resin's properties. The results are worth the effort for projects requiring a high-quality finish, while the process is time-consuming. Patience is needed to ensure proper application and curing.


## 14. Using PolyMaker PolySmooth PVB Filament


Using PolyMaker PolySmooth PVB filament is an effective method for smoothing 3D prints, for makers seeking a high-quality finish with minimal effort. PolySmooth is a specialized filament designed to be smoothed using isopropyl alcohol vapor. The outer layers soften when the printed object is exposed to isopropyl alcohol vapor, allowing imperfections such as layer lines to smooth out and giving the print a glossy, polished surface. The degree of smoothness and gloss depends on exposure time and environmental factors. The vapor process is controlled in a sealed chamber to avoid over-softening or distortion, ensuring a consistent finish. The chamber's conditions (temperature and vapor density) are essential in achieving the desired result. The method is effective for prints made with PolySmooth filament, as the material is engineered to respond to alcohol vapor, creating a smoother surface. The primary advantage of using PolyMaker PolySmooth PVB filament is its ease of use and ability to achieve a high-quality surface finish with minimal post-processing, reducing the need for extensive sanding. The process is an excellent option for achieving a polished, professional finish for decorative or functional items where a refined surface is essential. The finish varies based on exposure time and the intricacy of the print. The result is a smoother, more appealing print with reduced visible defects, giving it a polished appearance ideal for display or presentation. Deeper imperfections are visible, depending on the print’s quality and complexity.


## 15. UV-Curable Coatings


UV-curable coatings are an effective method for smoothing and finishing 3D prints, for improving surface quality and durability. The coatings are liquid resins that cure and harden when exposed to ultraviolet (UV) light. The curing time varies depending on the resin type and exposure conditions. The UV-curable coating helps fill in layer lines and minor imperfections when applied to a 3D print, creating a smoother, glossier surface. The process does not eliminate deeper flaws or complex surface defects. The process involves brushing or spraying the coating onto the print and exposing it to a UV light source to cure the resin. The exposure time must be controlled to avoid under-curing or over-curing. The results in a durable, high-gloss finish that improves the appearance and increases the print's resistance to scratches and environmental wear. The level of durability depends on the resin formulation and curing process. UV-curable coatings are beneficial for prints that require a polished, professional look or improved durability. The final finish depends on the print's material and the coating's application. The main advantage of the method is its ability to quickly smooth and harden the surface of the print, reducing the need for extensive manual post-processing (sanding or painting). Optimal results require additional finishing steps. The UV curing process is fast, making it a time-efficient solution for finishing multiple prints. The curing time varies depending on the print size and UV light intensity. UV-curable coatings are ideal for models requiring a high-quality finish, (decorative pieces, prototypes, or functional) objects that undergo frequent handling. Prints exposed to heavy use require additional protective layers or coatings for enhanced durability.


## 16. Post-Processing with Filler and Paint


Post-processing with filler and paint is a common technique for smoothing and refining 3D prints for materials such as PLA, ABS, or PETG. The process starts with applying a filler material (automotive filler putty) to the surface of the print. Selecting the correct filler for the material is essential to ensure proper adhesion and smoothing. The filler helps fill remaining imperfections, such as small gaps or rough spots from the 3D printing process, though larger flaws require additional post-processing. The print is sanded down to create a smoother, more even surface after the filler has dried. Sanding must be done gradually, starting with coarser grits and moving to finer ones to avoid over-sanding. A coat of paint is applied to provide color and protection once the surface is refined. The effectiveness of the paint depends on the type of paint and surface preparation, including the application of a primer. The method is helpful for prints that need to be visually appealing or functional (display models, prototypes, or functional objects). The combination of filler and paint improves the finish by creating a smoother surface and adding durability. The durability depends on the type of filler and paint used. The main advantage of the method is its ability to improve the surface, transforming a rough 3D print into a smoother, glossier product. Achieving a high-quality finish requires careful application and curing. The technique is effective for prints requiring high-quality appearance, as the filler creates a clean base, and the paint provides the final touch for a polished finish. The longevity of the finish depends on the materials and environmental exposure.

Using the pen provides control over the smoothing process, allowing the pen to fill gaps, smooth layer lines, and repair minor defects. The method's effectiveness in large-scale smoothing or deeper defects is limited. The tool is used in a back-and-forth motion to blend the applied filament with the existing surface, but it must be used to avoid visible lines or uneven surfaces. The method is beneficial for minor surface corrections and fine details, for smaller prints or areas needing precise work. The process is not as effective for large-scale smoothing or major surface flaws. The primary advantage of using a 3D pen smoothing tool is its ability to directly control filament application and the smoothing process, although a steady hand is required to ensure uniformity. A versatile and accessible solution for addressing specific imperfections. The finish requires additional post-processing for a perfectly smooth finish, while it reduces the need for sanding. The result is a softer surface, though the level of polish varies depending on the technique used. ## What is the Purpose of Smoothing 3D Prints?


The purpose of smoothing 3D prints is to improve the surface finish, reduce visible layer lines, and improve paint adhesion during post-processing. The process does not strengthen the part unless combined with additional treatments, and smoothing primarily improves surface finish rather than structural integrity. Smooth PLA prints create a more uniform surface that reduces rough textures and imperfections left by the 3D printing process. The smoother surface makes it easier to apply paint and coatings, as the smoother surface allows for better adhesion. Optimal paint adhesion requires priming. 3D print smoothing helps reduce layer lines, resulting in a more polished and professional look, though achieving a flawless finish requires further post-processing. Smoothing PLA prints or other 3D prints primarily improves aesthetics, with limited impact on mechanical performance. The direct effect on structural integrity is limited without reinforcing methods or materials. The improved surface finish is essential for parts that undergo further processing (painting or functional applications), where surface smoothness impacts the final quality and performance.
