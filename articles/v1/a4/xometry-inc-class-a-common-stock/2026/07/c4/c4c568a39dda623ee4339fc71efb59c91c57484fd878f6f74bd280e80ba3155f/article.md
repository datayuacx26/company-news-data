# Thermoplastic Polyurethane (TPU)

Thermoplastic Polyurethane (TPU) is a thermoplastic elastomer used in wheels, seals, footwear parts, cable jackets, films, hoses, and molded industrial components. Thermoplastic Polyurethane (TPU) combines plastic melt processability with rubber-like elasticity, giving the material flexibility, rebound behavior, abrasion resistance, and shape recovery after repeated deformation. TPU belongs to the polyurethane family and contains hard segments that support strength, plus soft segments that support elasticity. Grade chemistry controls hardness, oil resistance, hydrolysis resistance, and low-temperature flexibility. Covestro lists Desmopan and Texin TPU grades across 60 to 98 Shore A and 30 to 75 Shore D hardness ranges.


TPU production uses injection molding, extrusion, blow molding, film casting, coating, calendaring, and additive manufacturing, depending on the target part geometry. Thermoplastic behavior lets TPU soften during heating, flow into a mold or die, then harden again after cooling, which separates it from crosslinked rubber. Industrial relevance comes from balanced toughness, tear resistance, wear resistance, chemical resistance, and flexible performance under repeated movement. TPU appears in automotive interiors, medical tubing, sports equipment, electronics protection, conveyor belts, gaskets, and precision parts where flexible durability matters. Material selection depends on the hardness target, service temperature, contact fluids, part thickness, and processing route for the finished component.


## What Is Thermoplastic Polyurethane (TPU)?


Thermoplastic Polyurethane (TPU) is a melt-processible thermoplastic elastomer that combines plastic processing behavior with rubber-like flexibility. TPU belongs to Thermoplastic Elastomers (TPE), a material group that delivers thermoplastic behavior and elastomeric properties in one polymer family. TWI defines Thermoplastic Elastomers (TPE) as copolymers or compounds with thermoplastic and elastomeric properties.


TPU works as an engineering plastic because it provides abrasion resistance, tear strength, oil resistance, grease resistance, flexibility, and durable mechanical performance. SpecialChem describes TPU as a melt-processable thermoplastic elastomer with high durability and flexibility. Hard and soft polymer segments give TPU its hybrid structure, where rigid segments support strength and soft segments support elasticity. TPU suits injection molding, extrusion, films, coatings, and 3D printing for parts that require flexible strength under repeated use.


### What Is Thermoplastic Polyurethane (TPU) Material Made of?


Thermoplastic Polyurethane (TPU) is synthesized from three primary raw materials a polyol soft segment, a diisocyanate hard segment, and a short-chain diol or diamine chain extender. Polyols come from polyester or polyether sources, and diisocyanates are commonly MDI, methylene diphenyl diisocyanate, or aliphatic diisocyanates (such as hexamethylene diisocyanate).


The soft segments provide flexibility and low-temperature performance, contributing to elastic recovery and elongation at break. The hard segments form rigid, hydrogen-bonded domains that deliver tensile strength and dimensional stability. The ratio of soft to hard segments directly controls the final Shore hardness, ranging from Shore A 60 to Shore D 80, depending on formulation. Polyester-based TPU gives stronger abrasion resistance and oil resistance for mechanical parts. Polyether-based TPU gives better hydrolysis resistance and low-temperature flexibility for wet or outdoor service conditions. Chain extenders adjust molecular weight, hard segment spacing, and final mechanical performance across TPU grades.


### What Is the full form of Thermoplastic Polyurethane (TPU)?


"Thermoplastic Polyurethane" is the full form of the acronym "TPU," where each word describes a defining chemical or physical characteristic of the material. "Thermoplastic" indicates the material softens reversibly upon heating, and "polyurethane" identifies the urethane linkage, NHCOO, backbone formed during polymerization.


TPU is categorized as a thermoplastic elastomer because it combines the melt processability of thermoplastics with the elastic deformation behavior of vulcanized rubber, without requiring irreversible crosslinking. The material returns to its original shape after deformation, a defining trait of the thermoplastic elastomer classification. Reversible softening allows TPU to be injection molded, extruded, and reprocessed under controlled heating conditions. Polyurethane chemistry gives TPU strong abrasion resistance, tear strength, and bonding ability across films, coatings, and molded parts. The acronym TPU directly reflects the material's dual identity as a heat-processable polymer and an elastic engineering material.
