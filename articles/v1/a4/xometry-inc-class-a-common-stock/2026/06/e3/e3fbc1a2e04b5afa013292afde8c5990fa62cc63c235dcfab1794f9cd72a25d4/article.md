# 5 Types of Hydraulic Fluid

Hydraulic fluid is a liquid medium that transmits power, lubricates internal components, and dissipates heat within a hydraulic system. The hydraulic fluid fills the entire circuit from reservoir to actuator, carrying pressure energy generated by the pump to cylinders and motors performing mechanical work. Selecting the correct fluid type determines system efficiency, component lifespan, and operational safety across every application. Hydraulic fluid is important because it governs pressure transmission, surface lubrication, and thermal regulation simultaneously. A system running degraded or incompatible fluid loses volumetric efficiency, accelerates seal and pump wear, and risks catastrophic component failure. Operating pressures from 1,000 psi to 5,000 psi demand a fluid with adequate viscosity, thermal stability, and chemical compatibility with seals and metals throughout the circuit.


Different hydraulic fluid types interact with system components in distinct ways. Mineral-based fluids provide reliable lubrication in standard industrial equipment, water-based fluids offer fire resistance in high-heat environments, synthetic fluids maintain stability at temperature extremes, biodegradable fluids reduce environmental risk in sensitive areas, and fire-resistant fluids protect personnel and equipment in steel plants and aviation facilities. Matching the fluid type to the operating environment determines whether the hydraulic system performs reliably across its full service life.


## 1. Mineral Based (Petroleum) Hydraulic Fluids


Mineral-based hydraulic fluid is refined from crude oil and represents the most widely used hydraulic fluid type across industrial, construction, and mobile equipment applications. The fluid delivers reliable lubrication at viscosity grades from ISO VG 32 to ISO VG 68 and performs at operating temperatures from 0°C to 90°C. Petroleum-based fluids cost \[$2 to $8\] per liter, making them the most affordable option in the hydraulic fluid category.


**Pros** : Affordable and widely available, with strong lubrication performance and broad compatibility with standard seals, hoses, and pump materials.


**Cons** : Flammable with flash points from 150°C to 220°C, and a petroleum-based composition presents environmental contamination risk in the event of leakage.


## 2. Water Based Hydraulic Fluids


Water-based hydraulic fluids incorporate water as the primary fire-suppression component and appear in three distinct subtypes serving different performance requirements. Water-Glycol Fluids mix water and glycol in ratios from 35% to 50% water content, achieving fire resistance without a measurable flash point while maintaining viscosity suitable for system pressures up to 2,500 psi. Oil-in-Water Emulsions suspend small oil droplets in a water carrier, providing moderate lubrication with water content from 85% to 95% and fire resistance at temperatures above 5 to 50 °C. Water-in-Oil Emulsions disperse water droplets within an oil continuous phase, delivering better lubrication than oil-in-water types with water content from 40% to 45%.


**Pros** : Fire-resistant and effective at cooling system components during high-heat operation.


**Cons** : Lower lubrication capacity than mineral oil, with corrosion risk to ferrous metals if the concentration and pH from 8.5 to 9.5 are not maintained.


## 3. Synthetic Hydraulic Fluids


Synthetic hydraulic fluids are engineered from chemical compounds rather than refined crude oil, designed to perform where mineral-based fluids fail due to temperature extremes or fire risk. Phosphate esters, silicones, and polyalphaolefins (PAO) represent the primary synthetic types, with operating temperature ranges from -54°C to 135°C for PAO grades and up to 250°C for silicone-based fluids. Synthetic fluids cost \[$15 to $60\] per liter, depending on base chemistry and additive package.


**Pros** : Excellent thermal and oxidative stability, high-temperature resistance, and fire-resistant properties in phosphate ester grades used in aviation and steel industries.


**Cons** : High cost relative to mineral and water-based alternatives, with potential incompatibility with standard nitrile seals requiring fluorocarbon or EPDM seal substitution.


## 4. Biodegradable (Environmentally Friendly) Fluids


Biodegradable hydraulic fluids derive from vegetable oils (rapeseed, sunflower, or soybean) or synthetic esters designed to minimize ecological impact in the event of leakage or spill. The fluids biodegrade at rates from 70% to 99% within 28 days under OECD 301B testing standards, compared to less than 30% biodegradability for mineral-based alternatives. Cost ranges from \[$8 to $25\] per liter depending on base oil type and additive content.


**Pros** : Environmentally safe, low toxicity, and preferred for forestry, marine, and agricultural equipment operating near water sources or ecologically sensitive areas.


**Cons** : Shorter service life from 2,000 to 4,000 hours compared to 4,000 to 8,000 hours for mineral oil, and higher susceptibility to oxidative degradation at temperatures above 70°C.


## 5. Fire Resistant Hydraulic Fluids


Fire-resistant hydraulic fluids serve applications where ignition risk from hot surfaces, open flames, or molten metal contact is a primary operational hazard. Steel plants, die casting facilities, mining equipment, and aviation hydraulic systems specify fire-resistant fluids to protect personnel and equipment from fluid ignition in environments where hot surface exposure exceeds 300°C. The category includes water-based fluids (water-glycol and emulsions) and synthetic fluids (phosphate esters and polyol esters), with prices ranging from \[$10 to $50\] per liter.


**Pros** : Significantly safer in high-heat and ignition-risk environments, with phosphate ester fluids achieving self-extinguishing behavior under direct flame exposure.


**Cons** : Higher cost than mineral-based alternatives, with synthetic fire-resistant fluids requiring system compatibility verification for seals, paints, and non-ferrous metals before installation.


## What Is Hydraulic Fluid?


Hydraulic fluid is an incompressible liquid that transmits power through a sealed hydraulic circuit by carrying pressure energy from the pump to actuators performing mechanical work. The fluid fills every component continuously, from the reservoir through the pump, valve block, hoses, and cylinder, maintaining a pressure pathway that converts pump output into usable force at the actuator. Beyond power transmission, hydraulic fluid lubricates all internal moving surfaces, including pump pistons, valve spools, and cylinder rods, forming a hydrodynamic film of 1 to 10 micrometers that prevents metal-to-metal contact at operating velocities. The fluid transfers heat generated by pressure drops and internal friction back to the reservoir and heat exchanger, maintaining system temperature within the acceptable range of 40°C to 80°C for most industrial applications. Fluid properties, including viscosity, bulk modulus, flash point, and additive chemistry, determine compatibility with system components and suitability for the operating environment. Petroleum-based, water-based, synthetic, biodegradable, and fire-resistant formulations each represent a category of[Hydraulic Fluid](https://www.xometry.com/hydraulic-fluids-definition-uses) serving distinct pressure, temperature, and environmental requirements.


### How Does a Hydraulic System Work with Different Hydraulic Fluid Types?


A hydraulic system works by the same fundamental pressure transmission principle regardless of fluid type, but the fluid's viscosity, compressibility, and lubrication capacity directly affect how efficiently the system converts pump output into actuator force. Pascal's Law governs pressure transmission in every fluid type, confirming that pressure applied at the pump transmits equally throughout the circuit to the actuator. Mineral-based fluids at ISO VG 46 maintain viscosity near 46 centistokes at 40°C, providing reliable film thickness across standard industrial pump clearances of 0.0002 inch to 0.0006 inch. Water-based fluids with lower viscosity from 15 to 35 centistokes reduce lubrication film thickness, increasing internal leakage and lowering volumetric efficiency by 3% to 8% compared to mineral oil in the same pump. Synthetic fluids maintain stable viscosity across temperature ranges from -54°C to 135°C, allowing consistent system performance where mineral oil would thicken at cold start or thin excessively at high operating temperature. #### Does Fluid Type Affect How a Hydraulic System Works?


Yes, fluid type affects how a hydraulic system works by influencing viscosity, lubrication film thickness, volumetric efficiency, and thermal behavior throughout the operating cycle. The pressure transmission principle remains constant across all fluid types, but efficiency and component durability change significantly depending on fluid selection. A system designed for ISO VG 46 mineral oil operating on water-glycol fluid at 30 centistokes experiences increased internal leakage across pump and valve clearances, reducing volumetric efficiency and lowering actuator force output at the same pump speed. Water-based fluids also require system pressure de-rating by 10% to 20% compared to mineral oil specifications to prevent accelerated wear on pump and motor components rated for oil lubrication. Synthetic fluids in the same system maintain stable viscosity across a wider temperature range, improving cold-start performance and reducing thermal degradation at sustained high loads. Matching fluid type to system design specifications determines whether the hydraulic circuit meets its rated pressure, flow, and service life targets in actual operation.


## Why Is Hydraulic Fluid Important in Hydraulic Systems?


Hydraulic fluid is important in hydraulic systems because it performs power transmission, lubrication, heat removal, and contamination control simultaneously within a single fluid body. Removing or degrading any one of the functions collapses system performance and accelerates component failure across the entire circuit. Power transmission depends on the fluid's incompressibility, with a bulk modulus from 200,000 psi to 300,000 psi for petroleum-based oils, ensuring immediate pressure response at the actuator. Lubrication depends on maintaining adequate viscosity at operating temperature, with film thickness below 1 micrometer causing boundary lubrication conditions that increase pump wear rates by a factor of 3 to 10. Heat removal capacity determines whether the fluid carries thermal energy from valve pressure drops and pump friction to the reservoir and heat exchanger without exceeding the maximum fluid temperature of 80°C to 90°C for mineral-based grades. Contaminated or degraded fluid causes 70% to 80% of all hydraulic system failures, confirming that fluid condition in[Hydraulic Systems](https://www.xometry.com/hydraulic-systems-definition-components) governs reliability more than component design or operating pressure alone.


### What Happens If the Wrong Hydraulic Oil Is Used in a Hydraulic System?


The reasons if wrong hydraulic oil is used in a hydraulic system are listed below.


- **Accelerated Pump Wear** : An incompatible fluid with viscosity below the pump's minimum specification reduces hydrodynamic film thickness below 1 micrometer, causing boundary lubrication and metal-to-metal contact that shortens pump service life from 10,000 hours to below 2,000 hours.
- **Seal Degradation** : Fluids chemically incompatible with system seal materials cause swelling, shrinkage, or hardening of nitrile, polyurethane, or EPDM seals within 500 to 2,000 operating hours, resulting in internal and external leakage at pressures from 1,000 psi to 5,000 psi.
- **Reduced Volumetric Efficiency:** A fluid with viscosity too low for system clearances of 0.0002 inch to 0.0006 inch increases internal leakage across pump and motor components, dropping volumetric efficiency below 85% and reducing actuator force and speed at the same pump output.
- **Overheating** : Fluids with inadequate thermal stability or viscosity index oxidize at temperatures above 80°C, forming sludge and varnish deposits on valve spools and pump internals that restrict flow and trap heat within the circuit.
- **Corrosion of Metal Components** : Water-based fluids applied outside their specified pH range from 8.5 to 9.5 corrode ferrous and non-ferrous metal surfaces, producing particulate contamination that circulates through the circuit and damages precision components.


#### Can the Wrong Fluid Damage a Hydraulic System?


Yes, the wrong fluid damages a hydraulic system, and the damage progresses rapidly once an incompatible fluid enters the circuit. Seal materials, pump internals, valve spools, and metal surfaces all respond to fluid chemistry and viscosity within hours to days of exposure, depending on operating pressure and temperature. A phosphate ester synthetic fluid introduced into a system with standard nitrile seals dissolves seal material within 24 to 48 hours causing seal failure and uncontrolled leakage at every static and dynamic sealing point in the circuit. A mineral oil with viscosity grade ISO VG 100 installed in a system specified for ISO VG 32 increases flow resistance across valve passages and reduces pump delivery at cold start temperatures below 20°C, causing cavitation-induced damage to pump inlet components within the first operating hours. Flushing the system with the correct fluid after contamination removes residual incompatible fluid, but components already damaged by wear, corrosion, or seal degradation require inspection and replacement before reliable operation resumes.


## How Do Hydraulic Fluid Properties Impact System Performance?


Hydraulic fluid properties impact system performance by determining how efficiently pressure transmits from the pump to the actuator, how effectively internal surfaces are lubricated, and how well the fluid resists degradation across the operating temperature and pressure range. Each property influences a different aspect of system behavior, and no single property operates independently of the others. Viscosity governs internal leakage, pressure drop across valve passages, and hydrodynamic film thickness at pump and motor surfaces. Bulk modulus determines pressure response speed, with higher values producing faster actuator response to control inputs. Flash point and fire resistance determine safe operating environments, with mineral oils limited to non-ignition-risk areas and synthetic or water-based fluids required where open flames or molten metal are present. Oxidative stability, measured by the rotating pressure vessel oxidation test (RPVOT), determines fluid service life from 1,000 hours for vegetable-based fluids to over 8,000 hours for premium synthetic grades. Additive packages including anti-wear, anti-foam, rust inhibitors, and demulsifiers extend individual property performance across the service interval without altering the base fluid chemistry.


### How Does Viscosity Influence How a Hydraulic System Works?


Viscosity influences how a hydraulic system works by controlling internal leakage across pump and valve clearances, pressure drop through circuit passages, and hydrodynamic film thickness at all lubricated surfaces simultaneously. The relationship from viscosity to system performance is direct and measurable at every operating temperature. A fluid with viscosity too low for the pump's internal clearances of 0.0002 inch to 0.0006 inch allows excessive leakage across piston shoes, valve plates, and spool lands, reducing volumetric efficiency below 90% and wasting pump output as heat. A fluid with viscosity too high increases pressure drop across valve orifices and pump inlet passages, raising the minimum starting temperature required for reliable operation and causing cavitation damage at the pump inlet below 0°C for high-viscosity grades. ISO VG 46 at 40°C maintains a kinematic viscosity of approximately 46 centistokes, providing the balance of leakage control and flow resistance required for most industrial hydraulic circuits operating at pressures from 1,500 psi to 3,500 psi and temperatures from 40°C to 80°C. The viscosity index (VI) measures viscosity stability across temperature, with higher VI values from 95 to 150 indicating less viscosity change from cold to hot operating conditions.


#### Does Fluid Thickness Change Hydraulic Efficiency?


Yes, fluid thickness (viscosity) changes hydraulic efficiency in two opposing ways depending on whether viscosity is above or below the system's optimum range. Volumetric efficiency and mechanical efficiency respond in opposite directions to viscosity changes, creating a performance optimum at the specified viscosity grade for each system design. Volumetric efficiency increases with higher viscosity because a thicker fluid reduces internal leakage across pump and motor clearances, keeping more pump output available as useful flow to the actuator. Mechanical efficiency decreases with higher viscosity because a thicker fluid generates greater resistance to flow through valve passages and hose circuits, requiring more pump input power to overcome internal friction losses. Below the optimum viscosity, volumetric efficiency drops due to leakage, while mechanical efficiency improves due to lower flow resistance. Overall hydraulic system efficiency, combining volumetric and mechanical factors, peaks at the viscosity grade where leakage losses and flow resistance losses are balanced. For most axial piston pumps, peak overall efficiency occurs at viscosities from 25 to 36 centistokes at operating temperature.
