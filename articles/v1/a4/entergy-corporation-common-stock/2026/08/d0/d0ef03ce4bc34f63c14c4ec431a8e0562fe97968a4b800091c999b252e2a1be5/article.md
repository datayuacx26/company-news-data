# Busted: Debunking smart technology myths

## Busted: Debunking smart technology myths


Are smart devices expensive and hard to use? Here we clear up these and other common misconceptions.


By now, you’ve probably heard that smart devices can make your life easier and your home more efficient. You may have also heard some of the myths about this technology, potentially holding you back from diving in and creating your very own smart home. Well, it’s time set the record straight and debunk these five smart tech rumors.


### 1. Smart tech is very expensive


Although some smart home devices can come with a hefty price tag, many devices can easily fit into a tight budget. Smart home hubs and speakers are great introductory devices, and you can nab these for under $100. Other devices like smart power strips and lightbulbs are also cheap additions that can make your home more connected and simplify your life.


### 2. Only tech-savvy people can use smart devices


Smart home devices are made to make your life easier and streamline your daily activities. That means even those who can get easily frustrated with technology will be able to take advantage of a connected smart home. Through connected smartphone apps, remote control or voice commands with a smart speaker, you can control smart devices around your home however you’d like.


### 3. All smart devices can work together


One of the biggest roadblocks customers face when trying to set up a smart home is device incompatibility. Different brands of devices may not interact with each other, and others use lesser-known communication protocols like Zigbee and Z-Wave. When you shop for a new smart device, make sure it can work with your existing smart home technology.


### 4. Smart technology leaves your information vulnerable


Many companies that make smart devices have safeguards in place to encrypt and protect any personal information you store with them. However, if privacy is still a concern, you can take some safety precautions, such as using hard-to-guess passwords, keeping devices updated, and using guest Wi-Fi networks for smart devices.


### 5. Smart devices waste energy


Some people may think that adding more electronics to your home will just increase your energy bill, but they can actually help save energy. Smart power strips can turn off electronics when they’re not in use, smart thermostats can adjust the temperature to save energy when you’re away, smart bulbs can turn off at set times, and more.


So, there you have it. Creating your own smart home is easy, safe and won’t put a strain on your wallet.
