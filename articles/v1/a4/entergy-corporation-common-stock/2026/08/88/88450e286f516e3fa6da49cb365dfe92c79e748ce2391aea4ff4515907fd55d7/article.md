# Entergy Arkansas invests in the future workforce through STEM scholarships to Arkansas students

LITTLE ROCK, Ark. – As Arkansas experiences unprecedented economic growth and increasing demand for highly skilled workers, Entergy Arkansas is investing in the future of higher education and workforce development by awarding $32,500 in scholarships to eight outstanding students attending Historically Black Colleges and Universities (HBCUs) and partner institutions in Arkansas.


The scholarships are part of Entergy’s $20 million Power of Prosperity initiative, which supports HBCUs and minority-serving institutions across the Gulf South to help remove barriers to students’ academic success.


The initiative pillars include academic scholarships; workforce development, including student internships, mentoring and faculty externships; generational wealth-building; and competitive facility grants and endowments.


Scholarships are awarded to students pursuing degrees in high-demand fields such as computer science, information technology, engineering and related disciplines that align with the evolving energy workforce.


“These students represent the future of Arkansas. Their talent, determination and ambition will help shape the next generation of innovation, industry and economic development across our state,” said Ventrell Thompson, vice president of customer service. “By investing in their education today, we’re not only opening doors of opportunity, we’re building a stronger, more resilient workforce that will support our communities for years to come.”


The 2026 scholarship recipients are:


**University of Arkansas at Pine Bluff**


- Oluwademillade Ogunbade – Mathematics and Computer Science ($5,000)


- Abel Phiri – Computer Science ($5,000)


- Omarion Tucker – Computer Science ($5,000)


**Philander Smith University**


- Zara Duruji – Computer and Information Sciences ($5,000)


- Olaoluwa James-Owolabi – Computer Science ($5,000)


**University of Arkansas – Pulaski Technical College**


- Kyle Stroud – Mechanical Engineering ($2,500)


- Charles Doerpinghaus – Manufacturing Engineering Technology ($2,500)


- Allison Kinsey – Manufacturing Engineering ($2,500)


These students join 16 others across Entergy’s service area in Louisiana, Mississippi and Texas who are benefiting from the company’s commitment to strengthening education, career preparedness and workforce development.


Entergy Arkansas serves approximately 735,000 customers across 63 counties, making it essential to cultivate a diverse and highly skilled workforce to meet the state’s growing energy needs and supporting the Job Power pillar of its


[Next Generation Arkansas](https://www.entergyarkansas.com/nextgen) roadmap for the future.


“Power of Prosperity reflects our belief that investing in people is one of the most important investments we can make. When our students succeed, Arkansas succeeds,” Thompson said.


The Competitive Facilities Grant portion of the program will provide up to $1 million in funding this year for accredited HBCUs and Minority Serving Institutions in Entergy’s service area to help upgrade classrooms, labs and equipment. Awards will be announced during the fall semester.
