# Mexico’s Emo Superfans Are Not Okay (They Promise)

In the mid-aughts, emo transformed from a fringe underground scene into a cultural phenomenon, launching bands like


[Fall Out Boy](https://open.spotify.com/artist/4UXqAaa6dQYAk18Lv7PEgX) (and accessories like fingerless gloves) into the mainstream. Two decades later, the genre is once again having a moment, and nowhere is that revival more alive than in Mexico.


Between 2019 and 2025, streams of emo and pop-punk in Mexico nearly doubled, outpacing growth in the U.S. Over a similar stretch, from 2022 to 2026, the number of monthly listeners of the genre in Mexico grew by 66%.


What began as an offshoot of American punk has found a home in Mexico. “Sadness and heartbreak are deeply embedded in Mexican culture, from


*rancheras* to


*telenovelas* to the national football team,” said


**Gerardo Mora** , Senior Editor, Spotify Mexico. “As a country, we have always found beauty in suffering, and emo found a land that was ready for it.”


While global giants like


[Linkin Park](https://open.spotify.com/artist/6XyY86QOPPrYVGvF9ch6wz) or


[My Chemical Romance](https://open.spotify.com/artist/7FBcuc1gsnv6Y1nwFtNRCb) are popular across the globe, the surge in streams in Mexico is driven by local artists like


[PXNDX](https://open.spotify.com/artist/5NNlkZBrBpOJ7C11JwVxIj) . Formed in Monterrey, Mexico, in 1996, their audience today is bigger than ever, with more than 3.6 million monthly listeners on Spotify, up 125% between 2019 to 2025.


Their 2006 record


[Amantes Sunt Amentes](https://open.spotify.com/album/3kIvqYvs58ZdunGTOFZebr) is the most-streamed emo album of all time in Mexico, and it features “


[Los Malaventurados No Lloran](https://open.spotify.com/track/0R6s0WCBfgQ2dByPFSBh63?si=5b835185a57c48c5) ,”


the country’s most-streamed emo track.


It makes sense, Gerardo explained, that emo booms in Mexico, and online.


“Emo was the first music scene in Mexico built on the internet, long before that was the norm,” he continued. “That sense of digital community and identity translates directly into today’s hyper-connected world, making this the natural environment for that movement to resurface stronger than ever.”


## **The new emo kids**


Today’s fans are pressing play on the next wave of Mexican artists—including


[Blnko](https://open.spotify.com/artist/1nHfLPLJrZ7mF3vyerDg4d) ,


[cacomixtle](https://open.spotify.com/artist/5BPV50KIHSSKCHuQwh30fP) ,


[Sad Saturno](https://open.spotify.com/artist/4skcssepAWMUywr7Nmng2d) ,


[San Venus](https://open.spotify.com/artist/0I1DNEdOBX3s6eL8UlaskZ) , and


[Moorelo](https://open.spotify.com/artist/0aLrOKg7LwawHHWfvv8ua5) —who are putting their own spin on the genre.


“There’s a global revival of pop punk and Y2K culture happening right now,” said Gerardo. “The emo sound and aesthetic is making a comeback. That opens the door for a new generation of Mexican artists, like Blnko and cacomixtle, to take the genre’s blueprint and make it entirely their own.”


While the fans aged 25 to 34 (the original emo generation) account for more than half of streams in Mexico, a new cohort is proving that the uptick isn’t driven solely by nostalgia. Today, listeners aged 18 to 24—born well after many of the first-wave bands debuted—make up 20% of all streams of emo music in the country. That younger audience is also driving renewed interest in many of the tracks that helped define the genre, which may help explain why, between 2020 and 2025, streams have skyrocketed for plenty of iconic anthems.


We’re also seeing a surge in first-time listeners for many of the big-name legacy bands.


The result is an only-in-Mexico mash-up. “I see Mexico’s emo scene moving toward a moment of celebration between the old and the new,” said


Gerardo


. “Legacy bands are honoring what they built and commemorating a scene that defined a generation. But what’s most exciting is that the new bands recognize the importance of what those artists did, and are carrying that legacy forward in their own voices. In Mexico, new times and old friends exist in the same room. And that’s what makes the emo scene in this country unlike anywhere else.”
