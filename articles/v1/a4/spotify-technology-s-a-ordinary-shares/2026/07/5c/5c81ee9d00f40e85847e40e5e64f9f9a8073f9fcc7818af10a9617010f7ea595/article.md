# Spotify’s Greasy Tunes Nairobi Brings the City’s Soundtrack to Life

What happens when a familiar, everyday space becomes a stage for music, conversation, and community? That’s the idea behind Greasy Tunes, Spotify’s multi-week residency in Nairobi that brings Kenya’s listening culture off the playlist and into the real world.


Inspired by the original concept of transforming a classic “greasy spoon” cafe into a cultural hub,


[Spotify’s Greasy Tunes Nairobi](https://www.instagram.com/spotifygreasytunes/?hl=en) is a 12-day program at Nairobi’s Heltz House, a community events space, with programming running July 15-26. More than anything, Greasy Tunes reminds us that music is best experienced together.


## **From data to IRL**


The lineup for Greasy Tunes was built around how Nairobi listens. Spotify data from June 2026 showed that listeners 18-24 are responsible for more than half of all streams in the city. With listening peaking in the evening, music is clearly a key part of how young Nairobians unwind and connect—and Greasy Tunes is designed to capture that vibe.


“Greasy Tunes brings the energy people experience on Spotify into a shared physical space,” said


**Agnes Opondo** , Artist & Label Partnerships Lead for East Africa. “The program reflects how music already connects Nairobi’s young listeners, whether through food, conversation, creativity, or community.”


## **Week one: A taste of the city**


The first week, themed “Starters,” explored the intersection of sound and culture.


The Greasy Tunes Café turned Heltz House into a daytime hub for work and connection, while the week’s events showed how music is part of the city’s social rituals.


The week kicked off with a Kitchen Opening Party with The BAG, a leading Nairobi music and culture platform, setting the tone by celebrating the links between food, music, and community.


Karibu Night, hosted by Studio 18, a creative collective known for championing the local fashion and cultural scene, translated the sound of Spotify’s


[Made In Kenya](https://open.spotify.com/playlist/37i9dQZF1DX7z7RLIui5KP?si=jZxIp5-ETV6-8j9InT2YPA) playlist into a fashion showcase. The night included a live performance from


[BURUKLYN BOYZ](https://open.spotify.com/artist/4iXOuQsJ5qOOC7HgOIXLFU?si=LMWv-0N2QKi0AtuQv21xwQ) , whose hip-hop and drill sound added another dimension to the Made In Kenya story.


[https://storage.googleapis.com/pr-newsroom-wp/1/2026/07/7.mp4](https://storage.googleapis.com/pr-newsroom-wp/1/2026/07/7.mp4)


The week flowed from fashion into conversation with live podcast recordings from


[Culture Club](https://open.spotify.com/show/3E7ayHrJRfprvfLXlKOJzk?si=bd51ab5cff884950) , Spotify’s editor-led podcast exploring African music and culture, featuring Kenyan songwriter and producer


**Eric Musyoka** , and


[The 30 Percent Podcast](https://open.spotify.com/show/5Um2n9hR6jM779iSrMbvKt?si=a35567938b244b1e) . Live performances also took over Heltz House, including


[The Fisherman’s Experience](https://www.instagram.com/p/Da8nJYSl_Nc/) and


[ADUMA Presents](https://www.instagram.com/p/Da-7w9dl8lw/) . The week closed with Smocha Sundays, which brought together gospel music, community, and a live football watch party, showing the many ways people gather around sound.

## **Week two: The main course**


The second week, “Mains,” expands to focus on the way music fuels creative practice. It kicked off with Gufy’s Room: Poetry, Music & Storytelling, a live performance blending spoken word, music, and storytelling from Kenyan poet and filmmaker


[Gufy](https://open.spotify.com/artist/1xW8m7az6AS77v0CN6V2M7?si=NBtoAakbRvm56e8hOo1vMg) , and continued with a behind-the-scenes look at the creative process with a workshop where


**Kasi Flavour** championed women in music, and a fan event with songwriter and rapper


[Nyashinski](https://open.spotify.com/artist/7KY9NaOVRmptl8vlpVomi6?si=RC7fcNM8SROEU9FKlzeX3w) **.**


Experiences still to come include


an


[EQUAL](https://newsroom.spotify.com/2026-03-19/equal-the-podcast-5-year-anniversary/) workshop and showcase with


[Ongeza Volume](https://open.spotify.com/show/6ZQyLVY5ykZwK6Rbkr0qM5?si=f108e6e7f950485d) , a community platform spotlighting Kenya’s music ecosystem, and a comedy night with The Standup Collective. It all wraps up with the Strictly Soul: Closing Party at the end of the week.


The ongoing event makes one thing clear: for young Nairobians, music isn’t just something they stream. It’s a fundamental part of life and community in their city.
