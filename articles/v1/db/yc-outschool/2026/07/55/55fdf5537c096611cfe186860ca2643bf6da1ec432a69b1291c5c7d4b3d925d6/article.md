# Best online phonics classes for kids: what to look for and where to start

## How much do online phonics classes cost?


Free apps like Teach Your Monster to Read cost nothing. Subscription apps run about the price of a streaming service. Live classes cost more, because you're paying for a trained teacher's time, and prices range widely between a group class, a short decoding boot camp, and 1:1 instruction (which is usually the priciest but most targeted, especially for a struggling reader).


Here's the honest part: you don't have to outsource all of early reading. Many families use a free app for daily practice and add one live class or a few 1:1 sessions for the piece their kid is stuck on. It comes down to your budget and where you actually want the help.


If your family has an education savings account (ESA), live online classes are an approved expense in many states, which can turn 1:1 reading help from out-of-pocket into funded, often what makes targeted phonics tutoring realistic. Our[guide to ESA approved expenses](https://outschool.com/esa-guides/approved-list) walks through what qualifies.


## How to match a class to your kid


Start with where your reader actually is, not their grade.


- **The brand-new reader (ages 3-5):** a playful letter-sounds class that builds phonemic awareness before formal decoding.
- **The kid learning to blend:** a systematic class moving from letter sounds into CVC words and simple[reading practice](https://outschool.com/online-classes/reading) .
- **The kid who's stalled or guessing:** a small-group or 1:1 class that goes back to fill the specific gap, paired with our guide on[how to help a reluctant reader](https://outschool.com/articles/how-to-help-reluctant-reader) .
- **The kid who may have dyslexia:** a class built on structured, Orton-Gillingham-based methods, taught by someone trained in it.


If you're teaching some of it yourself, our[step-by-step guide to teaching a child to read](https://outschool.com/how-to-teach/how-to-teach-a-child-to-read) lays out the science-of-reading sequence so you know what to cover and what to hand off.


## Questions parents ask


### Can I use online classes for all of my kid's phonics instruction?


Yes. Plenty of families run early reading entirely through a live class or 1:1 sessions, especially with a strong teacher and a consistent weekly rhythm. Whether that's the right call is a budget-and-preference question: it's most affordable with ESA funds, and it works well when you want the expertise of a trained reading teacher rather than teaching the sequence yourself.


### My kid already uses a reading app. Do they still need a class?


Not necessarily, but the two do different jobs. An app is great for daily practice and reinforcement. A live class or tutor is what catches the thing an app can't hear, like a kid who's memorizing words instead of actually decoding them. Many families use both.


### How do I know the teacher actually knows how to teach reading?


On a marketplace, check the teacher's profile for structured-literacy or Orton-Gillingham training and read reviews from other parents. For early reading especially, a teacher's method matters more than a polished class description.


### What age should kids start phonics?


Phonemic awareness (hearing and playing with sounds) can start as early as 3 or 4 with rhyming and letter-sound games. Formal, systematic phonics usually clicks around ages 4 to 7, though a struggling or older reader can start structured phonics at any age, it's never too late to fill the gaps.


The goal isn't the single "best" phonics class. It's the class taught in a systematic sequence by someone who can hear your specific kid read and adjust. Start with where your reader is, look for explicit instruction and a trained teacher, and go from there.
