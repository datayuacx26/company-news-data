# 15 life skills every teen should learn before high school

## Running a household


**10. Cooking a few real meals.** Not gourmet, just three or four dishes they can make start to finish, plus safe knife and stove basics. Cooking builds independence faster than almost anything, and beginner[cooking classes](https://outschool.com/online-classes/cooking) or our guide to[teaching kids to cook](https://outschool.com/how-to-teach/kids-to-cook) make it approachable.


**11. Doing their own laundry.** Sorting, water temperature, and not leaving a wet load for two days. One ruined favorite shirt usually cements the lesson.


**12. Basic cleaning and upkeep.** Wiping a counter, taking out trash, keeping a shared space livable. High school group life, from lockers to shared projects, assumes it.


## Communicating like a young adult


**13. Writing a clear, polite email.** Teens will need to message teachers, coaches, and eventually employers. A greeting, a specific ask, and a thank-you go a long way.[Public speaking and communication classes](https://outschool.com/online-classes/public-speaking) help here too.


**14. Self-advocacy.** This may be the most important one. A teen who can say "I don't understand this, can you explain it another way" to a teacher, out loud, will get through high school with far less stress than one who stays silent. Practice it at home first.


**15. Judging what they read online.** Before a research paper and before the group chat spreads a rumor, teens need to ask who wrote this, when, and why. It's a skill, and it's teachable.


## How to teach these without turning it into a chore


You don't need a curriculum or a family boot camp. Pick two or three skills from this list and fold them into normal life over a semester. Hand off one dinner a week. Put your teen in charge of their own laundry. Let them message the coach instead of you. The discomfort of doing it imperfectly is where the learning lives.


Where a skill needs more than kitchen-table practice, like budgeting, communication, or study strategy, a short, focused[life skills class](https://outschool.com/online-classes/life-skills) gives teens structure and a teacher who isn't their parent, which sometimes makes all the difference.


## Frequently asked questions


**Which of these matters most?** Self-advocacy and time management carry the most weight in high school, because nearly everything else depends on them. If you only build two, build those.


**My teen resists all of this. Now what?** Start with a skill tied to something they want: managing money for a purchase they're saving toward, or cooking a meal they love. Motivation follows relevance.


**Isn't ninth grade too late if we haven't started?** No. Teens learn these skills quickly once they're motivated and given real responsibility. The point is to start before the stakes are highest, not to hit a perfect deadline.
