# Best online writing classes for kids: what to look for and where to start

## How much do online writing classes cost?


Game-based apps like Night Zookeeper run about the price of a streaming subscription. Self-paced course platforms cost a bit more. Live classes cost the most, because you're paying for a trained teacher to read and respond to your kid's writing, and prices range widely between an ongoing group writing club, a short essay boot camp, and 1:1 coaching (usually the priciest but the most targeted, especially for a kid with a specific goal like a college essay or a struggling writer who needs one-on-one attention).


Here's the honest part: you don't have to outsource all of writing. Many families teach the basics themselves and bring in one live class or a few 1:1 sessions for the part they can't coach, like giving objective feedback on a teenager's essay. It comes down to your budget and where you actually want the help.


If your family has an education savings account (ESA), live online classes are an approved expense in many states, which can turn 1:1 writing coaching from out-of-pocket into funded. Our[guide to ESA approved expenses](https://outschool.com/esa-guides/approved-list) walks through what qualifies.


## How to match a class to your writer


Start with what your kid actually needs to work on, not their grade.


- **The reluctant writer:** a low-pressure creative writing club that builds enthusiasm before mechanics, paired with our guide on[teaching writing to a child who refuses](https://outschool.com/how-to-teach/how-to-teach-writing-reluctant-writers) .
- **The kid who loves stories:** a[creative writing class](https://outschool.com/online-classes/creative-writing) or club where they draft, share, and revise fiction with peers.
- **The kid heading into essays:** an academic or[essay writing class](https://outschool.com/online-classes/essay-writing) that teaches structure and argument, backed by our[grade-by-grade guide to teaching essay writing](https://outschool.com/how-to-teach/how-to-teach-essay-writing) .
- **The kid who needs a spark:** if the block is ideas rather than skill, start with[writing prompts by grade level](https://outschool.com/homeschool/writing-prompts) and add a class once they're writing regularly.


## Questions parents ask


### Can I use online classes for all of my kid's writing instruction?


Yes. Plenty of families run writing entirely through a live class or ongoing writing club, especially with a strong teacher and a consistent weekly rhythm. Whether that's the right call is a budget-and-preference question: it's most affordable with ESA funds, and it works best when you want an experienced teacher giving objective feedback rather than grading your own kid's writing yourself.


### My kid already writes a lot on their own. Do they need a class?


Volume isn't the same as improvement. A kid can write pages and repeat the same habits every time. What moves a writer forward is targeted feedback and revision, and that's exactly what a class adds that solo writing can't.


### How do I know the teacher can actually teach writing?


On a marketplace, check the teacher's profile for a writing or English-teaching background, a specific method they follow, and reviews from other parents. For writing especially, look for evidence they give real feedback, not just assign prompts.


### What age should kids start writing classes?


Kids can start creative, story-based writing as early as ages 6 or 7, once they're reading and can get words on a page. Structured essay and academic writing usually starts around the upper elementary and middle school years. An older or struggling writer can start targeted instruction at any age, it's never too late to build the skill.


The goal isn't the single "best" writing class. It's a class where a knowledgeable teacher reads your specific kid's writing and coaches the next revision. Start with what your writer needs to work on, look for real feedback and explicit instruction, and go from there.
