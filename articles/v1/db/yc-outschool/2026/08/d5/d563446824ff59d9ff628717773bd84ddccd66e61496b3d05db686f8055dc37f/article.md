# Best online drawing classes for kids: what to look for and where to start

Drawing is one of those skills that looks simple until you try to teach it. A kid who draws for fun will improve on their own up to a point, but there's a ceiling. Learning to see shapes, understand proportion, work with shading, and build composition requires someone who can look at what your kid is doing and say "here's what to try next." That's what separates a good drawing class from a how-to-draw YouTube video.


This guide covers the types of online drawing classes available for kids, what to look for, how the options compare, and how Outschool fits.


## Types of online drawing classes for kids


Drawing classes aren't one-size-fits-all. The right class depends on what your kid wants to draw, how old they are, and whether they want structured instruction or creative freedom.


### Guided/directed drawing


The teacher walks kids through drawing a specific subject step by step: an animal, a character, a landscape. These classes work well for younger kids (ages 5-8) who need structure and enjoy the satisfaction of a finished piece. The risk is that some classes are so rigid they don't build independent skills. The best ones use the guided drawing as a starting point, then encourage kids to make it their own.


### Anime and manga drawing


Consistently one of the most popular categories for kids ages 9 and up. Good anime classes teach proportion, facial expressions, dynamic poses, and character design, not just copying existing characters. The technical skills transfer to other drawing styles.[Browse anime art classes](https://outschool.com/online-classes/anime-art) .


### Digital drawing and illustration


Kids learn to draw using tablets, Procreate, or other digital tools. These classes combine traditional drawing fundamentals (line, shape, shading) with digital-specific skills (layers, brushes, color tools). Typically best for ages 10 and up, and the kid needs access to a drawing tablet or iPad.[Browse digital art classes](https://outschool.com/online-classes/digital-art) .


### Realistic drawing and sketching


Focused on observational drawing: still life, portraits, landscapes. These classes teach kids to see like an artist, breaking subjects into basic shapes, understanding light and shadow, and developing patience with detail work. Best for ages 11 and up, though some classes adapt these concepts for younger kids.


### Cartooning and character design


Kids learn to create original characters, develop visual storytelling, and build consistency in their character designs. This category bridges drawing skills and narrative thinking, which makes it a strong fit for kids who love both art and writing.


## What to look for in an online drawing class


**A teacher who can give individual feedback.** Drawing is a skill that improves through targeted feedback on specific work. If a class has 30 kids and the teacher can't look at each kid's drawing during the session, the value drops significantly. Look for small class sizes (under 10).


**Live instruction, not just recordings.** Recorded tutorials are fine for basics, but they can't adjust when a kid is struggling with a specific concept. A live teacher can demonstrate on camera, look at a kid's work through screen share, and explain the fix in real time.


**Skill progression, not just projects.** A good class builds skills session over session: proportion leads to shading, shading leads to composition. Watch for classes that offer multi-week series or clubs where kids develop over time, not one-offs that start from scratch every session.


**Balance of technique and creativity.** The best drawing teachers give kids enough structure to learn new skills, then enough freedom to apply those skills to subjects they care about. Too much structure kills motivation; too little structure means kids plateau.


**Materials guidance.** A good class specifies what the kid needs before the first session: paper type, pencil grades, specific supplies. Vague materials lists ("bring something to draw with") often signal a less structured class.


## How the options compare


**YouTube tutorials (Art for Kids Hub, Draw So Cute, others).** Free and abundant. Good for casual drawing at home. The limitation: no feedback, no accountability, and kids tend to copy rather than develop independent skills. Useful as a supplement, not a replacement for instruction.


**Self-paced apps (Skillshare Kids, Sparketh).** Pre-recorded video courses organized by skill level. Sparketh is designed specifically for kids and homeschoolers, with a library of ~1,000 video lessons ($15-$25/month subscription). Good for self-motivated kids who will practice independently. No live feedback.


**In-person classes and camps.** Local art studios and community centers offer drawing classes, typically $150-$300 for a multi-week session. Quality depends entirely on the individual instructor. Limited by geography and scheduling.
