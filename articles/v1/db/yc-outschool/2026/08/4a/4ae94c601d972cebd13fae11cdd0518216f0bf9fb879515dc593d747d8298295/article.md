# Best online homeschool curriculum programs: how to choose the right fit

## What "curriculum" means in the homeschool world (and why the word is slippery)


When a parent searches "homeschool curriculum online," they could mean at least three different things: a full-year, all-subjects program that replaces a traditional school (think K12 or Connections Academy); a subject-specific set of textbooks and lessons they teach themselves (think Saxon Math or All About Reading); or a collection of live, teacher-led classes they assemble from multiple sources.


Each model works. None of them is the "best" in every situation. The right choice depends on how much structure your family needs, whether you want live instruction or self-paced materials, how much you want to teach yourself versus outsource, and what your state requires.


This guide compares the major online curriculum options across those dimensions so you can make the decision with real information instead of marketing copy.


## Full-curriculum programs: all-in-one, structured, ready to go


These programs provide a complete K-12 (or near-complete) curriculum across all core subjects. You enroll, and the platform handles scope, sequence, lesson delivery, and often grading. They're the closest thing to "school at home" without a traditional school.


### Time4Learning


Self-paced, multimedia lessons covering math, language arts, science, and social studies for PreK-12. Parents set the schedule; the platform delivers the content and auto-grades most assignments.


- **Cost:** ~$35/month (PreK-8), ~$40/month (9-12)
- **Instruction model:** Self-paced, animated/interactive lessons. No live teacher.
- **Best for:** Families who want an affordable, structured curriculum they can run independently.
- **Watch for:** No live teacher interaction. The social component is entirely on you. Writing feedback is limited to automated grading.


### Power Homeschool (Acellus)


Video-based, self-paced curriculum with over 500 courses. Acellus provides the instruction platform; Power Homeschool is the parent-facing portal. Includes science virtual labs and automated diagnostics.


- **Cost:** ~$25/month per student
- **Instruction model:** Pre-recorded video lessons with embedded quizzes. No live interaction.
- **Best for:** Budget-conscious families who want a structured, video-driven approach.
- **Watch for:** Limited parent customization. The curriculum follows a fixed sequence, which can feel rigid if your child is ahead or behind in specific subjects.


### K12 (Stride)


K12 operates as a tuition-free virtual public school in many states, meaning your child is technically enrolled in a public school and follows its calendar, testing schedule, and attendance requirements. They also offer a private, tuition-based option (K12 Private Academy) for families who want the curriculum without the public school requirements.


- **Cost:** Free (public school option, state-dependent) or ~$5,000-$8,000/year (private academy)
- **Instruction model:** Mix of live online classes and self-paced work, depending on grade and state.
- **Best for:** Families who want a free, structured, accredited option with some live instruction and don't mind following a school calendar.
- **Watch for:** The free option is a virtual public school, not homeschooling. You follow their schedule, take their tests, and your child is enrolled in the public school system. In some states, this means you lose homeschool status. Flexibility is limited.


### Connections Academy


Similar to K12's public-school model: tuition-free virtual public school with a full curriculum, live classes, and certified teachers. Available in roughly 30 states.


- **Cost:** Free (public school enrollment)
- **Instruction model:** Live and recorded lessons, certified teachers, school-issued materials.
- **Best for:** Families who want a full school experience at home with no cost.
- **Watch for:** Same trade-off as K12's free tier. This is virtual public school, not homeschool. State availability varies, and you follow their academic calendar. Withdrawing mid-year can be complicated.


### BJU Press and Abeka


Comprehensive, faith-based curricula with daily lesson plans covering all core subjects plus Bible. BJU Press leans academic with critical-thinking emphasis; Abeka follows a more traditional, drill-based approach. Both offer video instruction options.


- **Cost:** $500-$1,200/year depending on grade and format (textbook vs. video)
- **Instruction model:** Textbook-based with optional video lessons. Parent-taught or video-taught.
- **Best for:** Families who want a faith-integrated, structured curriculum with clear daily lesson plans.
- **Watch for:** Worldview alignment is strong and specific. If you're looking for secular or worldview-neutral content, these aren't it. Curriculum is also fairly rigid, with less room for child-led exploration.


## A-la-carte and supplemental platforms: build your own


These platforms don't try to be everything. Instead, they let you pick the specific subjects, skill levels, and formats your child needs. You're the curriculum architect; they provide the building materials.


### Outschool


Live, teacher-led online classes across 140,000+ topics, from algebra tutoring to creative writing workshops to AP exam prep. Classes are taught by vetted teachers in small groups (averaging 3-4 kids) or 1:1. You pick the classes; there's no fixed sequence or enrollment window.


- **Cost:** Per-class pricing, typically $10-$25/class. No subscription.
- **Instruction model:** Live video classes with real teachers, real-time interaction, and feedback.
- **Best for:** Families who want to assemble a custom curriculum from live instruction, fill gaps in another program, or add enrichment and socialization. Also strong for families using[ESA funds](https://outschool.com/%22https://outschool.com/esa-guides/approved-list/%22) , since Outschool is an approved provider on Odyssey, ClassWallet, and other state ESA platforms.
- **Watch for:** Outschool is not a packaged curriculum. If you want someone else to handle scope and sequence across all subjects, you'll need to pair it with a core program or build your own plan. Our[step-by-step curriculum-building guide](https://outschool.com/%22https://outschool.com/articles/creating-a-top-notch-homeschool-curriculum/%22) walks through how to do that.


### Khan Academy


Free, self-paced lessons and practice across math, science, computing, history, and test prep. Khanmigo (AI tutor) adds a conversational layer for $44/year.


- **Cost:** Free (core platform). Khanmigo: $44/year.
- **Instruction model:** Pre-recorded video lessons with adaptive practice exercises. No live instruction.
- **Best for:** Supplementing math and science. The mastery-based progression is genuinely useful for filling gaps.
- **Watch for:** No live teacher, no writing instruction, and limited humanities content. It's a supplement, not a standalone curriculum.


### IXL


Adaptive, skill-based practice platform covering math, language arts, science, and social studies for K-12. Provides diagnostic assessments and skill plans.


- **Cost:** ~$10-$20/month per subject
- **Instruction model:** Practice problems with immediate feedback. No teaching, just practice.
- **Best for:** Drill and reinforcement after you've taught a concept. Diagnostics are useful for identifying gaps.
- **Watch for:** IXL is practice, not instruction. It doesn't teach concepts. A child who doesn't understand fractions won't learn them from IXL alone.
