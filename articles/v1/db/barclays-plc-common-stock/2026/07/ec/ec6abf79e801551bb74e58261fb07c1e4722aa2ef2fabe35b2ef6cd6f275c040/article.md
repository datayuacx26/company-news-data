# The Burnham era begins

# The Burnham era begins


UK POLITICS


20 July 2026


Luke Mayberry, Investment Strategist

Andy Burnham MP has been appointed prime minister, bringing months of uncertainty to an end. Following Sir Keir Starmer's resignation, Burnham was unopposed in the race for the Labour leadership, with no candidate formally mounting a challenge against him. With the political transition complete, investor attention now turns to policy and what it could mean for markets.


## Policy agenda


Burnham has pledged to pursue a regionally focused growth agenda centred on housing, infrastructure, devolution and skills. His first major policy speech placed devolved decision-making at its core, shifting funding and powers away from Whitehall towards mayors and local authorities, to be done through a new Manchester-based operation he has dubbed "Number 10 North". On housing, he has committed to the largest council housebuilding programme since the post-war period. He has also proposed business rates reform aimed at supporting the high streets. His broader growth agenda points towards regional industrial strategy and a stronger technical and vocational skills offer.


Tax policy is the biggest area of uncertainty. Speculation has centred on potential reforms to property and wealth taxation, including Burnham's previously expressed interest in a land value tax, alongside possible changes to inheritance tax and council tax. However, no proposals have been confirmed, and the political and practical hurdles to major reform remain considerable.


## What should investors watch?


Several developments will be important for investors in the coming months, but the Autumn Budget is likely to be the most significant. It will provide the first detailed insight into how the government intends to balance its growth ambitions with fiscal discipline. Any changes to taxation are most likely to be announced at this point, while the accompanying fiscal forecasts will reveal how much room the chancellor has to manoeuvre. Until then, investors should focus on formal policy announcements rather than political speculation. While headlines and rumours are likely to fuel uncertainty in the months ahead, individuals should be cautious about making impulsive, short-term decisions relating to their wealth and investments based on unconfirmed policy changes.


The run-up to the first Budget will be just as important. Investors will be paying close attention to signals from the Treasury as Chancellor John Healey begins to set out his economic agenda. His appointment came as a surprise to many and leaves markets with relatively little insight into how he will balance the government's growth ambitions with the need to maintain fiscal credibility. Speeches, interviews and Labour's party conference in late September may provide important clues about the direction of tax and spending policy before the Budget itself.


## Market implications


Financial markets have already begun adjusting to the new political landscape. Gilts and sterling have rallied as the shape of the new government became clearer, a notable contrast to the spring when Burnham's emergence as the frontrunner for the Labour leadership prompted concerns over higher borrowing and a looser fiscal stance. That said, investor confidence will ultimately depend on whether the government's fiscal discipline matches its policy ambitions. As a result, volatility is likely to remain elevated in the weeks ahead and any politically-driven rise in gilt yields could present an attractive opportunity for investors looking to add exposure.


For equities, the impact is likely to vary considerably. The FTSE 100 remains relatively insulated, with around three-quarters of its revenues generated overseas, while the FTSE 250's greater domestic exposure means it is likely to be more influenced by developments in fiscal, housing and regional growth policy. At the sector level, construction and housebuilding stand to benefit most directly if the council housing pledge progresses with funding behind it, while regional infrastructure could be a secondary beneficiary of the devolution agenda. Areas facing greater uncertainty include utilities, where investors will be watching closely for any signs of tighter regulation or increased public-sector involvement.


For the Bank of England, the fiscal backdrop adds another layer of uncertainty to the rates outlook. With markets already pricing in hikes through year-end, a credible Autumn Budget could ease that pressure, while any fiscal slippage risks keeping rates higher for longer.


## The bottom line


Political uncertainty has largely given way to policy uncertainty. But until the Autumn Budget, tax speculation is just noise that should be ignored. Instead, investors’ focus should be, in our view, on sector-specific opportunities within equities and on taking advantage of any politically driven entry points in gilt markets.
