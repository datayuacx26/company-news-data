Against the backdrop of a warming planet, the future for which we design our built environment must include responsibility and climate responsiveness. The Royal Institute of British Architects (RIBA) advocates for a radical shift in industry norms: one in which delivered outcomes in completed projects are measured and quantified.


This may not, at first glance, appear so transformative, yet in a profession where sustainability has historically focused on the descriptive and qualitative, emphasising measurable performance is progressive. Furthermore, given the typical disjunct between architects' appointments and post-completion building performance, the importance of getting close to the data to fully understand project outcomes remains relevant and no less revolutionary today.


## Setting industry standards


To facilitate this shift, RIBA has developed industry guidance to support the adoption of standards above those set out in regulation. RIBA’s Sustainable Outcomes Guide is based on the eight UN Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) most relevant to construction and seeks to demystify the breadth of sustainability issues across the environmental, economic and social spectrum. RIBA also developed the 2030 Climate Challenge as a companion tool, focusing on three key areas for considering climate in building design: operational energy, embodied carbon, and water use, issues that are consistently present across all buildings in every geography and locality.


Setting out stepped targets that ratchet downwards over time, the 2030 Climate Challenge creates ambitious but achievable in-use performance goals for project teams to embed. The Challenge demonstrates that business-as-usual approaches are incompatible with a climate-conscious trajectory. Many within the profession use it to benchmark against, and clients as well as developers have adopted its targets into design briefs and business models. References to the Challenge can also be found in manufacturers’ and local authority guidance documents.


## Cross-industry benchmarking


The ability for project teams to benchmark projects at all the design stages against industry targets is game-changing. Through a collaboration of professional bodies, such as RIBA, the Institution of Structural Engineers (IStructE), Chartered Institute of Building Service Engineers (CIBSE) and LETI (the voluntary organisation of built environment professionals who advocate for faster action on climate-related building standards), work has been done on aligning methodology and targets to enable better correlation and benchmarking. This work brought together agreement on scopes, methodologies and targets in whole life and embodied carbon alignment that was published in 2021(1) .


The collaboration on carbon alignment, combined with the UK Green Building Council’s (UKGBC) research that demonstrated a market demand for a single, agreed definition for ‘net zero’, was instrumental to the founding of the UK Net Zero Carbon Buildings Standard (“the Standard”). To date, there has not been a way to robustly demonstrate net zero carbon aligned building performance. The Standard is the industry’s response to this challenge. Led by a coalition of organisations – Better Buildings Partnership (BBP), BRE, CIBSE, IStructE, LETI, RIBA, RICS, and UKGBC – the Standard has been developed with the help of over 350 expert volunteers from across the UK built environment.


## The Net Zero Carbon Buildings Standard


The Net Zero Carbon Buildings Standard is freely available and enables anyone connected with the UK’s real estate industry to robustly prove their built assets are ‘Net Zero Carbon Aligned’ and in keeping with a 1.5⁰C warming scenario. The initiative is driven by purpose, not profit. It is politically neutral and has no regulatory or Government backing, funding or involvement. The inter-organisational collaboration has been unparalleled: many thousands of hours of expertise have been donated pro bono from across all sectors of the UK built environment, as well as over 4,000 data points in response to the Call for Evidence.


The Standard is a rule book, applicable to both new and existing buildings across 13 typologies (from homes to shops to leisure centres and schools). It provides the characteristics of what a building in the UK must meet to call itself ‘Net Zero Carbon Aligned’. These characteristics are set out as mandatory requirements that buildings must evidence with 12 months building performance data (such as energy and upfront embodied carbon, onsite renewable electricity generation, water consumption and others). Crucially, buildings must be fossil fuel free, and all mandatory criteria must be met.


Offsetting is optional and can only be used in addition to the mandatory demand reduction measures. Importantly, the Standard uses building performance metrics that are already familiar to those in industry, and in doing so, mandates a common methodology for energy and carbon reporting.


## Supporting climate policymakers


The UK Net Zero Carbon Buildings Standard is voluntary, but it could be used to support policymakers in setting robust requirements that deliver buildings aligned with a 1.5°C decarbonisation pathway for the UK. One of its very important achievements is to ‘connect’ bottom-up limits i.e. how buildings perform on the ground to ‘top-level’ UK national energy and carbon budgets. This then, for the first time, reflects what the response to a 1.5-degree warming limit needs to be.


The Standard is ambitious but achievable. For example, RIBA put forward its own refurbishment of 66 Portland Place in the UK Net Zero Carbon Buildings Standard Pilot Testing Programme alongside another 204 projects from across the UK.


Users of the Standard must obtain third-party verification before any conformity claims can be made. The robustness of the Standard aims to both reduce spurious claims around net zero carbon and to accelerate the design, construction and use of buildings that deliver lower-carbon outcomes in line with the UK’s legally binding carbon targets. In this way, the Standard amplifies RIBA’s desire to help industry reduce the performance gap between design targets and delivered outcomes.


The UK Net Zero Carbon Buildings Standard is strongly linked to two other current, cross-industry projects: The Built Environment Carbon Database (BECD) and the updated RICS guidance on whole life carbon assessment(2) , both of which further this aim of consistency in measurement methodology.


## The need for continued action


The development of the Standard demonstrates that if we are to reach net zero, deep carbon reductions within the built environment sector are essential. It has also re-amplified the need for the Part Z campaign, calling on Government to introduce embodied carbon regulation, as well as the need for a National Retrofit Strategy.


As a Membership organisation, RIBA’s cross-industry collaboration and campaign work is always mirrored with membership focus. Providing learning and education to all members in sustainability measurement and metrics remains key, and it is inspirational to be able see evidence in case studies and projects where this guidance and the standards have been realised.


## About RIBA


The Royal Institute of British Architects (RIBA) is a global professional membership body driving excellence in architecture. Founded 190 years ago for the ‘advancement of architecture’, the Institute supports an international community of 52,000 members who share in its purpose of ‘make{ing} the future a better place’.


RIBA works in five specific ways to realise this ambition: writing guidance and setting best-practice expectations; engaging with and working on cross-industry standards and collaborations; providing CPD and ongoing support to members through in-person and online events; introducing stringent entry requirements for its Awards programme; and advocating on behalf of the profession and lobbying the Government for change.


RIBA has been a signatory to the UN SDGs and the UN Global Compact since 2015, embedding the SDGs into design guidance and tools.
