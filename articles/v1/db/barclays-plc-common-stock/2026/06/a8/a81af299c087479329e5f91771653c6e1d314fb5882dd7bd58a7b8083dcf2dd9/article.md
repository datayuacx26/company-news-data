# Unlocking veteran talent is the smart solution to the UK's hiring challenge


## Barclays colleagues, Iain Buckle, Nicky Howse and Ed Wynn, write on why hiring military veterans is key to closing the skills gap and strengthening business resilience.

## Iain Buckle, UK Corporate Bank; Nicky Howse, Private Bank and Wealth Management; and Ed Wynn, Human Resources, Barclays


Read time: 4 minutes


June 22 2026


- **UK skills gap is intensifying: 71% of business leaders say hiring skilled talent is impacting performance, rising to 89% in tech and 80% in financial services**
- **Veterans are an overlooked solution: Despite over 85% reporting transferable skills, more than half (52.5%) work below their capability, and only 17% of firms actively recruit them**
- **Built for high-pressure roles: Military experience develops leadership, resilience and decision-making under pressure, exactly the capabilities businesses are investing heavily to build**
- **A faster route to capability: 43% of UK firms spend £50k+ on skills training and 27% spend £100k+ on leadership, competencies veterans already bring from day one**
- **Cybersecurity gap meets real-world expertise: Only 29% of leaders feel confident handling a major cyber incident, yet many veterans have hands-on experience in complex, high-risk operational environments**
- **Proven commercial impact: 91% of organisations that hire veterans report improved performance, particularly in leadership, accountability and team cohesion**
- **Structured pathways unlock value: Programmes like the Military Talent Scheme show that when given the right bridge into corporate life, veterans don’t just transition - they accelerate performance and strengthen organisations**


This Saturday (June 27) marks Armed Forces Day. It is a moment to recognise the service and sacrifice of those who serve, and the families who support them, but it is also an opportunity to look ahead.


As the UK continues to face persistent hiring challenges and structural skills shortages, there is a compelling case to rethink how talent is identified and deployed.


One of the most capable, yet underutilised, talent pools sits within the UK’s veteran community.


Each year, thousands of people leave the Armed Forces. Like the three of us, they don’t leave behind their capabilities, only their uniforms.


Essentially, our military experience shapes how we operate under pressure, giving us the foundations that we rely on daily. Organisations like Barclays are looking for a breadth of roles and skillsets – they aren’t just looking for bankers.


What we, and many like us, bring is a powerful combination of leadership, resilience and adaptability, which was built in complex, high-pressure environments. These are precisely the attributes businesses are seeking as they navigate uncertainty, digital transformation and shifting global dynamics.


Yet the transition to civilian life is often framed too narrowly. It is not simply a change of role, but a shift in identity, networks and language.


Without clear pathways, even highly capable individuals can struggle to articulate how their experience translates into a corporate setting.


The result is a gap between supply and demand and one that comes at a growing economic cost.


## The devil in the data


### 71% of UK business leaders say the struggle to hire skilled labour is impacting their organisation


That disconnect is increasingly visible in the data.


[Barclays’ Business Prosperity Index](https://home.barclays/insights/2026/05/Resilience-Over-Retreat/) shows that 71% of UK business leaders say the struggle to hire skilled labour is impacting their organisation. Our data shows that the challenge is even more acute in high-growth sectors, like technology and financial services.


At the same time, businesses are investing heavily to close these gaps. Across the UK, 43% of firms spend more than £50,000 annually on core skills, while 27% invest over £100,000 each year in leadership development.


Cybersecurity highlights both the scale of the challenge and the opportunity. Only 29% of business leaders feel confident responding to a major cyber incident, prompting 68% to increase cybersecurity investment, with average spend reaching £505,000.


**Ed Wynn, Human Resources, British Army, 2012-2021**


More broadly, cyber, cloud and AI now account for 44% of planned technology budgets. Yet many veterans have first-hand experience operating in precisely these environments, managing risk in real time, working with complex systems, and making decisions under pressure. These are not theoretical capabilities; they are hard-earned skills with immediate relevance to business.


Despite this, veteran talent remains significantly underutilised.[More than half (52.5%) of veterans report working in roles below their skill level](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/employment-analysis-of-uk-armed-forces-veterans/employment-skills-and-volunteering-uk-armed-forces-veterans-uk-veterans-survey-2022) , while only 17% of businesses actively recruit from this group. This is particularly striking given that over 85% of veterans say they possess transferable skills.


The mismatch is clear: businesses are investing heavily to build capability, while a highly trained workforce remains under-recognised.


“Veterans bring a wealth of experience, highly developed skills and diversity of thought. By choosing military talent, businesses can strengthen leadership and invest in the capabilities needed to compete in a rapidly changing economy. Over the past 10 years, Barclays has led the way in demonstrating how veterans can add enormous value to the corporate world, and we’re a better business because of the experience and perspective they bring. I’m proud to call them colleagues and celebrate their contribution this Armed Forces Day."


**Kevin Gartside, Head of Military & Veterans Outreach, Barclays**


## Working in practice


### 86% of businesses say veterans are well suited to management roles, and 85% say they help address skills gaps


Where organisations do engage with veteran talent, the impact is measurable.


Around[91% of employers who hire veterans report a positive effect on business performance, including stronger team cohesion](https://www.gov.uk/government/organisations/office-for-veterans-affairs) , improved accountability and enhanced operational effectiveness.


Additionally, 86% of businesses say veterans are well suited to management roles, and 85% say they help address skills gaps. These outcomes reflect the core strengths developed through military service: leadership, discipline, adaptability and a bias for action.


**Nicky Howse, Private Bank and Wealth Management, Royal Navy, 1998-2021**


## What Barclays is doing


Barclays has long recognised the opportunity within the Armed Forces community, embedding it as part of a broader focus on skills and employability.


A signatory of the[Armed Forces Covenant since 2013](https://home.barclays/news/press-releases/2023/01/barclays-announces-fresh-commitments-to-armed-forces-community-o/) , the bank has combined commitment with practical action.


Central to this is the[Military Talent Scheme](https://search.jobs.barclays/MVO) , the first programme of its kind in UK financial services, designed to help service leavers transition into corporate careers at Barclays.


Over the past decade it has supported more than 300 veterans into roles across the bank, with over 90% securing permanent positions.


The programme’s 7–12-week placements provide a structured route to translate military experience into a business context, while demonstrating the ability of participants to adapt and deliver at pace.


That success has driven wider momentum. Since introducing voluntary military self-identification in 2022, more than 1,000 veterans and military spouses have joined through broader hiring channels.


Partnerships with military charities extend this impact further, supporting hundreds of veterans into employment each year.


Beyond hiring, Barclays is also supporting veteran entrepreneurship, with more than 250 start-ups benefiting from its dedicated[Veteran and Military Spousal Founders](https://labs.uk.barclays/what-we-offer/inclusion-at-eagle-labs/veteran-and-military-spousal-founders/) programme.


This sustained effort reflects a clear reality: unlocking veteran talent is both a social commitment and a smart response to the UK’s skills challenge.


**Iain Buckle, UK Corporate Bank, Royal Navy, 1983-2017**


## What we’ve learned


All three of us are proud alumni of the Military Talent Scheme, and our shared experience points to a common outcome: with the right structure, military capability translates quickly and powerfully into business impact.


For us, the scheme provided a bridge between two worlds, it helped to reframe our military experience in a commercial context, build critical networks and, just as importantly, instil the confidence to operate and belong in a new environment from day one.


What we have in common is not just a successful transition, but a pattern. The qualities we developed in service, qualities like focus, resilience, accountability and execution, map directly onto the needs of modern organisations.


We are passionate believers that, with the right support, veterans can move rapidly from learning to leading, strengthening teams and delivering outcomes at pace.


We have seen this first-hand, which is why we are committed to supporting others as they make the transition from military careers to corporate working life. For us, it's essential that others have the same opportunity we had.


For businesses willing to invest in that bridge, the return is immediate: stronger leadership, sharper performance and a more resilient organisation, built on talent that has already been tested where it matters most.
