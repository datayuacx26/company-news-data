How we pay says a great deal about how we live. Today marks 60 years since Barclays launched the UK’s first credit card, and in the decades since, payments in the UK have been transformed by technology, shifting consumer expectations and growing demand for speed, security and convenience.


From credit cards to chip and pin, contactless cards, mobile wallets and wearables, the story of payments is one of constant reinvention. If there is one lesson from that history, it is this: innovation matters most when it makes everyday life easier for more people.


That shift is clear in how consumers now choose to pay. Barclays research shows that for in-store payments under £100, Gen Z is most likely to use mobile payments, with 54% naming them as their preferred method, ahead of contactless debit or credit cards at 48% and cash at 27%.


Across the wider population, a third of consumers say they do not tend to carry cash when they leave the house. Nearly three in ten often leave home without a physical card, rising to almost half of Gen Z. Yet convenience can sometimes outpace preparedness: 18% say they often find themselves needing a physical card when they do not have one with them.


This tells us something important about the future of payments in the UK. Digital methods are becoming more embedded in daily life, especially among younger consumers, but the picture is not one of simple replacement.


Some methods are fading fast: more than one in five consumers say they have never used a cheque, rising to 44% of Gen Z. Cash, however, remains remarkably resilient. Just 1% of consumers say they have never used cash, and only 3% say they used to use it but no longer do.


The direction of travel is clear, but so is the need to preserve choice. The strongest payments ecosystem will combine innovation with inclusion, ensuring people can pay in the way that works best for them.

## Looking to the future


So what comes next? The future of payments in the UK will not be defined by a single technology, but by how effectively the industry combines innovation with trust, security and accessibility.


Consumers increasingly expect payments to be seamless, whether they are tapping a card, using a phone, travelling on public transport or shopping online. But they also expect reliability, protection and the freedom to choose the method that suits the moment.


At the same time, we are at a turning point for money itself. Digital assets and tokenised forms of money now exist and move around in a different form to money as we have known it.


As they develop, there is an opportunity to make transactions faster, cheaper and more transparent.


But the challenge for the industry is not simply to create the next payment method. It is to build the underlying infrastructure that allows traditional and digital forms of money to work together safely and securely. That will require collaboration across banks, regulators, payment networks and technology providers to connect existing systems with emerging ones.


## An evolving landscape


Barclays has helped shape the UK’s payments story from the very beginning,[from launching the country’s first credit card in 1966](https://home.barclays/insights/2026/06/60-Years-Of-Barclaycard/) to pioneering contactless.


As the landscape continues to evolve, our role remains the same: to keep pushing forward and finding new ways to make payments simpler, safer and more useful in people’s everyday lives, and to help develop the infrastructure that can enable the UK to remain at the forefront of financial innovation.
