# The 10 best budgeting apps in 2026

*Updated 16 July 2026*


The best budgeting apps in 2026 are Finary, Bankin', Linxo and Tricount for aggregating French bank accounts, and YNAB for a strict budgeting method. This comparison sets out their features, pricing and limits so you can pick the tool that suits your situation.


Key takeaways


- Emma, Wallet and Spendee round out the range with targeted subscription tracking or detailed spending charts.
- Most apps offer a free tier; advanced features (unlimited syncing, automatic categorisation) usually require a paid subscription.
- The choice depends above all on your use case: personal tracking, family budgeting or splitting expenses between friends do not call for the same tools.
- Not all of them work with French banks: check that compatibility before choosing, particularly for foreign apps such as YNAB.


## How do you choose a budgeting app?


Before deciding, check a few criteria: whether the free tier is genuinely usable, compatibility with your French banks, how data gets in (automatic syncing or manual entry), the level of data security, the platforms supported (iOS, Android, desktop) and the subscription price for advanced features. Your use case matters too: day-to-day personal tracking, family budgeting or splitting expenses between friends do not call for the same tools.

## Linxo: strong usability and plenty of features


[Linxo](https://www.linxo.com/) is a **budgeting** and **bank account aggregation app** that lets you manage personal and business finances in one place. Launched in 2010, it offers an interface for smartphones and tablets, compatible with iOS and Android. It stands out for its ability to bring a person's banking activity together on a single platform, making account management and budgeting easier.


### Why it is a good app:


- **Bank account aggregation** : Linxo lets you manage transactions from several accounts, personal or business, centralised in one interface, which makes handling your finances easier.
- **Expense tracking and budgeting** : the app categorises spending automatically, offers detailed charts for better financial analysis and lets you add custom notes to expenses. It also provides a forecast budget over more than 30 days, available only on the Premium tier.
- **Data security** : Linxo provides high-level security for banking data, with encrypted servers and advanced encryption to protect users' personal information.
- **Customisation and ease of use** : the app offers considerable freedom to customise and is recognised for its ergonomic, easy-to-use interface.
- **Comprehensive features** : Linxo offers a variety of features, such as making bank transfers straight from the app, customisable real-time notifications, and constantly updated balances across accounts.


### Why you should think twice:


- **Syncing and duplicate issues** : some users have reported slow syncing and duplicated transactions, although those problems seem to have improved recently.
- **Limits in the forecasting** : the app's forecasting may not always match reality, with missing automatic reconciliations and cash-flow projections that are sometimes off.
- **Lack of development and some features** : a few users regret the app's limited development and the absence of certain features, such as splitting expenses or creating additional categories for more advanced project management.
- **Connection problems with some banks** : users have reported difficulties connecting to certain banks, requiring frequent reconnections.
- **Limited technical support** : some users have expressed frustration with technical support, finding it unresponsive on persistent technical issues.

## YNAB: budgeting on macOS and Windows


[YNAB](https://www.ynab.com/) , short for *You Need A Budget* , offers a robust **budgeting** tool built on the zero-based budgeting method. Users assign every dollar to specific categories such as day-to-day spending, savings or paying down **debt** , making financial planning proactive and intentional. The app is aimed at people who want a hands-on approach to their personal finances, and includes **educational resources** to better understand the principles of budgeting and money management.


### Why it is a good app:


1. **Zero-based budgeting method** : YNAB uses this approach to allocate every euro to specific spending categories, which helps prevent overspending.
2. **Ease of use** : suits beginners and advanced budgeters alike, and provides tools to track and monitor financial progress.
3. **Goal setting** : lets you define short- and long-term financial goals, helping users save and plan effectively.
4. **Analysis and reporting** : offers reports and charts to track spending and net worth, giving a clear overall picture of your financial situation.
5. **Bank syncing** : YNAB syncs with more than 12,000 banks, giving an in-depth view of personal finances.


### Why you should think twice:


1. **Language and geographic limits** : the app is available in English only and syncs only partially with French banks, with coverage more limited than dedicated French aggregators.
2. **Cost** : YNAB is one of the most expensive budgeting apps on the market, at $14.99 monthly or $109 annually ($9.08/month).
3. **Learning curve** : it takes some time to get fully to grips with its features.
4. **Possible bugs** : some users have reported bugs in the app, and there is no phone support available.


### How much does it cost?


- **Monthly subscription** : $14.99 per month.
- Annual subscription: $109 per year, or $9.08 per month (around $70 saved compared with the monthly plan).
- Free trial: 34 days, no card required.

## Frequently asked questions


When it comes to managing a personal budget, certain methods and tools have proved their worth in tracking and improving your finances. The questions below give clear answers on specific budgeting topics.


### What is the 50/30/20 rule?


[The 50/30/20 rule](https://finary.com/fr/blog/finance-perso/budget/regle-50-30-20) is a budgeting method that suggests splitting net income into three spending categories: 50% for needs, 30% for wants and 20% for savings or paying down debt. This approach helps maintain financial balance by allocating available resources sensibly.


### How do you keep an eye on your budget?


To monitor your budget, it is worth using tools and apps that track spending in real time. These apps can offer visualisations and alerts to help you stay within preset budgets and avoid overspending.


### Are budgeting apps secure?


Most reputable apps encrypt banking data and have read-only access to accounts through authorised banking connectors. Check that the app displays a security certification (encryption, two-factor authentication) before syncing your accounts.


### Do you have to pay for a budgeting app?


No, most apps such as Finary, Tricount or Linxo offer a free tier that is sufficient for basic use. A paid subscription becomes useful for unlimited syncing, advanced automatic categorisation or managing multiple business accounts.


### Which app should you choose for budgeting as a family or between friends?


For family use, Wallet and Emma offer account sharing between several users. For one-off expenses between friends (a trip, a flat share), Tricount remains the free benchmark thanks to its simple handling of reimbursements and its multi-currency support.
