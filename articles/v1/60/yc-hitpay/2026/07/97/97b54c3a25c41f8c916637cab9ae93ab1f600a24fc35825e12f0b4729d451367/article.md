The Philippine e-commerce market is growing fast. ## What Payment Methods Should a Philippine Invoice Support?


Philippine buyers pay in fragmented ways. A Makati-based wholesale buyer may prefer bank transfer via InstaPay. A retail customer in Quezon City likely pays with GCash. A corporate client may require a card payment for internal expense reporting.


A well-structured invoice payment setup for the Philippines should cover:


-


**E-wallets:** GCash, Maya, GrabPay, ShopeePay


-


**QR-based:** QR Ph (the[Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas](https://www.bsp.gov.ph/) -standardised interoperable QR standard accepted across most banking and wallet apps)


-


**Bank transfer:** InstaPay (real-time, under ₱50,000 per transaction) and PESONet (batch, for larger amounts)


-


**Cards:** Visa and Mastercard, both domestic and international


-


**BNPL:** BillEase and GrabPay PayLater for higher-value orders


-


**Over-the-counter:** Bayad Center, ECPay, Palawan Express — critical for customers without bank accounts


Offering only one or two of these methods causes checkout abandonment. Philippine buyers expect to pay how they already pay.


## How Does Invoice Payment Work for an E-Commerce Store?


An invoice payment link is a URL tied to a specific order amount and reference number. The merchant sends it — via email, Viber, SMS, or Messenger — and the customer clicks through to a hosted payment page.


The flow works like this:


1.


Merchant creates an invoice or payment link in the dashboard, entering the amount, item description, and customer details.


2.


The system generates a unique payment URL tied to that invoice.


3.


Merchant sends the link to the customer via preferred channel.


4.


Customer opens the link and chooses a payment method (GCash, QR Ph, card, InstaPay, etc.).


5.


Payment is processed and the merchant receives a confirmation notification.


6.


Funds settle to the merchant’s bank account the next business day for domestic transactions in PHP.


This flow eliminates the need to manually verify screenshots, cross-reference bank statements, or chase unpaid orders.


## What Are the Reconciliation and Cash Flow Benefits?


Manual payment collection — where customers send proof-of-payment screenshots — creates several operational problems. Confirmations arrive at different times. Screenshots can be forged or reused. Matching payments to specific orders requires staff time.


Payment link invoices solve all three. Each link is tied to one order. The payment either completes or it does not — there is no ambiguity. The merchant dashboard shows real-time payment status per invoice.


For cash flow,[invoice payment best practices for Philippine SMEs](https://hitpayapp.com/blog/invoice-payment) recommend including a clear payment deadline on each invoice link, alongside automatic expiry settings. This reduces the number of outstanding unpaid invoices and shortens the average collection cycle.


Domestic transactions settle the next business day in PHP. Cross-border payments — for example, a Singapore-based buyer paying via PayNow — settle at T+2.

## What Should Businesses Check Before Going Live?


Before activating invoice payments, SMEs should confirm the following:


1.


The payment gateway holds a valid licence issued or recognised by the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP).


2.


The checkout page is PCI DSS compliant — card data must not pass through the merchant’s own server.


3.


InstaPay and PESONet are enabled for customers who prefer direct bank transfer.


4.


Over-the-counter options are active for unbanked customers — relevant for businesses selling to provincial markets outside Metro Manila.


5.


Payout schedule is confirmed: domestic settlements in PHP arrive next business day; cross-border payments arrive T+2.
