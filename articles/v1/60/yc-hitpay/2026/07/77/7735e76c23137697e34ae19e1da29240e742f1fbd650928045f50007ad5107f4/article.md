**Quick Answer:** The best payment solutions for Malaysian retail businesses combine DuitNow QR, FPX, Touch ‘n Go, GrabPay, and Visa/Mastercard cards — covering the full range of how Malaysian customers pay in 2026. Malaysia’s retail payment landscape has shifted decisively toward cashless. Bank Negara Malaysia’s push for a high-value, high-velocity digital payment ecosystem — outlined in the Financial Sector Blueprint and governed through[Bank Negara Malaysia](https://www.bnm.gov.my/) licensing requirements — means retailers that still rely on cash-only or single-method checkouts are leaving revenue on the table. A boutique in Bangsar and a hardware shop in Petaling Jaya now face the same question: which payment methods actually convert, and which provider makes them easiest to manage?


This guide covers what Malaysian retail businesses need to know before choosing a payment solution.


## What Payment Methods Do Malaysian Retail Customers Actually Use?


The methods with the broadest reach in Malaysia fall into four categories.


**QR and instant transfers** — DuitNow QR is the interoperable national QR standard. Any customer with a Malaysian banking app can scan and pay. FPX (Financial Process Exchange) is the bank transfer backbone for online and POS-connected checkouts. Both settle instantly and carry low fraud risk for merchants.


**E-wallets** — Touch ‘n Go eWallet, GrabPay, Boost, and ShopeePay are the four dominant wallets. Touch ‘n Go is particularly strong outside Kuala Lumpur; a petrol station or convenience outlet in Johor Bahru will see heavy Touch ‘n Go volume. GrabPay skews toward urban, app-native shoppers in areas like KLCC and Bukit Bintang.


**Cards** — Visa and Mastercard remain essential for higher-value purchases and older demographics. Retailers that skip card acceptance lose corporate buyers and tourists outright.


**BNPL (Buy Now, Pay Later)** — Atome, Grab PayLater, and SPayLater (ShopeePay’s instalment product) are gaining traction in fashion, electronics, and home goods retail. Enabling BNPL can increase average basket size, as explored in the guide to[adding Atome BNPL to in-store checkout](https://hitpayapp.com/blog/adding-atome-bnpl-to-your-in-store-checkout-integration-steps-fees-and-roi-for-businesses-in-asia) .


For retailers in tourist-heavy areas like Bukit Bintang, Alipay+ and WeChat Pay cover Chinese visitors. Cross-border QR methods — including PayNow from Singapore, QRIS from Indonesia, and PromptPay from Thailand — allow international customers to pay with their home-country apps at no currency exchange friction at the point of sale.


## How Should Retail Businesses Evaluate a Payment Provider?


Four criteria matter most for physical retail.


**1. Payment method breadth.** A provider that supports DuitNow QR and cards but not Touch ‘n Go or GrabPay creates gaps. Customers who can’t pay their preferred way abandon the sale. Check which specific Malaysian wallets and QR schemes are supported — not just a generic “e-wallets” claim.


**2. Payout speed.** Cash flow is operational. Non-card domestic transactions settle at T+2 calendar days; card transactions settle at T+3 business days. Knowing the difference matters for weekly reconciliation.


**3. Fee structure.** Monthly platform fees compound fast for smaller-volume operators. A per-transaction model with no monthly fee scales down in slow months and up in peak ones. For a full comparison of Malaysian gateway pricing and[payout API options](https://hitpayapp.com/blog/best-payout-api-malaysia) , the[Malaysia payment gateway comparison](https://hitpayapp.com/blog/malaysia-payment-gateway-comparison) covers iPay88, eGHL, SenangPay, HitPay, Razer, and others side by side.


**4. Hardware and POS compatibility.** In-person retail needs either a card terminal, a QR display, or both. Some providers require proprietary hardware locked to their ecosystem. Portable card readers that support both DuitNow QR and Visa/Mastercard on one device reduce counter clutter and onboarding complexity.
