For a Philippine SME — whether a Makati boutique, a BGC café, or a Cebu-based online store — cash flow timing is not a minor operational detail. Payout speed varies significantly across Philippine payment processors. So do monthly fees, payment method coverage, and onboarding requirements. This comparison cuts through the noise.


## Why Do Payouts Matter More Than Most Merchants Realise?


A payment gateway that processes a sale on Monday but settles funds on Thursday creates a 3-day gap that compounds across dozens of daily transactions. For SMEs operating on thin margins — a Quezon City food stall, a Davao homewear brand — that gap is working capital sitting idle.


The[Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas](https://www.bsp.gov.ph/) regulates payment service providers operating in the Philippines, setting licensing and consumer protection standards that affect how gateways handle fund flows. Merchants should verify that any gateway they use holds the appropriate Operators of Payment Systems (OPS) licence.


Settlement timelines also differ between domestic and cross-border transactions. A GCash payment from a Manila customer and a PayNow payment from a Singaporean tourist at the same checkout are settled on different timelines — merchants need to account for both.


## What Payment Methods Does a Philippine Gateway Need to Support?


The Philippine digital payments landscape is broad. A gateway that covers only cards misses a significant share of consumer spend. The core methods a Philippine merchant should expect:


-


**QR Ph** — the national interoperable QR standard, accepted across GCash, Maya, and major banks


-


**GCash and Maya** — the two dominant e-wallets with tens of millions of active users


-


**InstaPay and PESONet** — real-time and batch bank transfer rails


-


**Visa and Mastercard** — for card-paying customers, both online and in-person


-


**Over-the-counter** — Bayad, ECPay, and Palawan for unbanked or cash-preferred customers


-


**Cross-border wallets** — PayNow (Singapore), QRIS (Indonesia), PromptPay (Thailand), DuitNow (Malaysia), and others for international visitors


For merchants in tourist-heavy areas or selling to regional customers, cross-border wallet acceptance is a practical revenue consideration, not a luxury add-on. The[alternative payment methods landscape across Southeast Asia](https://hitpayapp.com/blog/alternative-payment-methods-southeast-asia) illustrates how quickly local e-wallet dominance shifts consumer expectations across the region.

### Adyen


Adyen processes over €1.4 trillion annually and serves enterprise retailers globally. In the Philippines, its payment method coverage is limited to card payments. It carries no monthly fee but is designed for businesses with significant transaction volumes and technical integration resources.
