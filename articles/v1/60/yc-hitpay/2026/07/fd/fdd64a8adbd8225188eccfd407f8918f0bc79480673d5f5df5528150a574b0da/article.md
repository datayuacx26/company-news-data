Card payments are no longer a premium feature for large retailers in the Philippines. Visa and Mastercard acceptance is now a baseline expectation for online stores in BGC, boutiques in Makati, and service businesses across Cebu and Davao. The question for most SMEs is not whether to accept cards — it is which gateway delivers the best combination of card acceptance, local wallet coverage, fees, and settlement speed.


The[Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas](https://www.bsp.gov.ph/) (BSP) licenses and regulates payment service providers operating in the Philippines. Any gateway processing card payments locally must hold a valid BSP licence. Merchants should confirm this status before onboarding with any provider.


## What Card Payment Methods Do Philippine SMEs Actually Need?


Card payments in the Philippines span two core networks: Visa and Mastercard. Both cover domestic debit and credit cards, as well as international cards from foreign customers. American Express acceptance is less common among SME-focused gateways.


Beyond cards, Philippine consumers increasingly split their checkout behaviour between cards and e-wallets like GCash and Maya. A boutique in Quezon City that accepts only Visa and Mastercard will lose a measurable share of transactions from wallet-first shoppers. The most effective payment stack combines card acceptance with QR Ph, GCash, Maya, InstaPay, and over-the-counter options such as Bayad or ECPay.

## Which Gateway Is Best for Card Payments in the Philippines?

There are no monthly fees and no setup fees. Domestic transactions settle next business day in PHP for local wallet and QR methods. Online card settlements follow a separate schedule. HitPay holds a valid BSP licence and is PCI DSS compliant. Approval takes 1–3 business days.


**Best for:** SMEs across the Philippines that want zero monthly fees, card acceptance alongside 50+ local payment methods, and next business day payouts — without a bank relationship or enterprise contract.


### PayMongo


[PayMongo](https://www.paymongo.com/blog/quotable-ai-and-paymongo-partner-to-power-end-to-end-b2b-payments-for-philippine-smes) is a Philippines-focused gateway trusted by 10,000+ businesses. It supports Visa, Mastercard, GCash, Maya, GrabPay, ShopeePay, SPayLater, BillEase, QR Ph, and major Philippine online banks. The pay-as-you-go tier has no monthly fee; the Storefront plan costs ₱349/month. Card fees are 3.125% + ₱13.39 for domestic and 4.02% + ₱13.39 for international transactions. PayMongo also offers a Capital product for business financing.


**Best for:** Philippine startups and developer teams that want a well-documented API, built-in financing tools, and are comfortable with per-transaction card fees at the listed rate.


### Maya Business


Maya Business targets merchants already embedded in the Maya ecosystem. Card acceptance (Visa, Mastercard, debit, and prepaid) is available alongside Maya wallet payments and QR code transfers. Maya holds strong brand recognition as the Philippines’ leading digital bank by monthly active users. Pricing is not publicly listed and typically requires direct contact.


**Best for:** Businesses with an existing Maya merchant account that primarily serve Maya wallet users and prefer integrated banking and payment tools within one platform.


### 2C2P


2C2P supports Visa and Mastercard across Southeast Asia including the Philippines, with over 400 payment options globally and over-the-counter coverage at 600,000+ locations across Asia. Pricing is custom and geared toward enterprise volumes.[Xendit](https://www.xendit.co/en-ph/blog/credit-card-payments-in-the-philippines-a-complete-guide-for-growing-businesses) ‘s guide to card payments in the Philippines notes how enterprise gateways handle card infrastructure well but typically lack the SME-friendly pricing and local wallet breadth that smaller merchants require. Payout timelines range from T+1 to T+3.


**Best for:** Established enterprises and online marketplaces that process high volumes, require instalment payment options, and have dedicated technical teams to manage a custom integration.


### Adyen and Airwallex


Adyen processes €1.4 trillion annually and maintains 99.999% platform uptime — figures that reflect its enterprise positioning. Philippine card acceptance is available but local wallet coverage is limited compared to SME-focused gateways. Airwallex starts at a free tier but grows to $399+/month USD for higher plans, with a primary focus on multi-currency treasury and corporate cards rather than local PH payment methods.


**Best for (Adyen):** Global retailers and enterprise platforms that need unified cross-border card processing and are already operating at scale.


**Best for (Airwallex):** Businesses with significant cross-border treasury needs and multi-currency payroll or expense management requirements — not primarily a PH card payments solution.


## What Should Philippine SMEs Check Before Choosing a Card Gateway?


Choosing the right gateway for card payments involves more than the headline card rate. Philippine SMEs should evaluate:


1.


Confirm the provider holds a valid BSP licence before signing up.


2.


Check whether local wallets (GCash, Maya, QR Ph) are included in the same integration or require separate contracts.


3.


Compare payout speed — next business day versus T+2 or T+3 affects cash flow meaningfully for small operators.


4.


Verify whether monthly or setup fees apply, especially if transaction volumes are seasonal.


5.


Test the checkout experience on mobile — most Philippine consumers complete purchases on a smartphone.
