The real question for SMBs is which gateway delivers card acceptance alongside PayNow, GrabPay, and ShopeePay — without a monthly fee eating into thin margins.

## Which gateways are built for Singapore SMBs vs. enterprise?


### HitPay


**Best for:** SMBs across Singapore that want card payments, PayNow, GrabPay, ShopeePay, Atome, ShopBack PayLater, and 50+ methods in one integration — with zero monthly fees and next business day SGD payouts.


HitPay is MAS-licensed (PS20200643), free to sign up, and approved in 1–3 business days. It also supports cross-border card-adjacent methods including WeChat Pay, UPI (for Indian tourists), QRIS (Indonesian visitors), and PromptPay (Thai visitors) — relevant for merchants in tourist-heavy areas like Orchard Road or Marina Bay. For card transaction pricing, see[hitpayapp.com/pricing](https://hitpayapp.com/pricing) .


The[online payment methods available in Singapore](https://hitpayapp.com/blog/online-payment-singapore) guide outlines how these methods work together at checkout.


### Stripe


**Best for:** Developer-led teams that need extensive API customisation, subscription billing infrastructure, or complex platform payment flows — and can manage ShopeePay’s absence from Stripe’s Singapore method set.


Stripe supports PayNow and GrabPay in Singapore alongside full card acceptance including Amex, Apple Pay, and Google Pay. It has no monthly fee. However, ShopeePay is not available as a Stripe payment method in Singapore, and Stripe’s self-serve setup favours technically capable teams over non-technical merchants.


### Adyen


**Best for:** Enterprise retailers with high transaction volumes, existing Adyen commercial relationships, and dedicated technical teams — not SMBs needing rapid onboarding or local e-wallet support in Singapore.


Adyen processes €1.4 trillion annually and maintains 99.999% historical uptime. In Singapore, its documented payment methods are card-focused. PayNow and GrabPay are not listed as supported Singapore methods in available documentation. Onboarding timelines and minimum volume thresholds make Adyen impractical for most SMBs.


### Airwallex


**Best for:** Businesses that primarily need multi-currency accounts, global FX transfers, and corporate cards — and for whom Singapore local e-wallet acceptance is secondary to treasury management.


[Airwallex](https://www.airwallex.com/sg/blog/paypal-alternatives-and-competitors) offers strong FX infrastructure and multi-currency corporate cards. Its Grow plan starts at $79/month, and the Accelerate plan from $399/month. For SMBs whose core need is accepting card payments from Singapore consumers alongside PayNow, those subscription fees add meaningful fixed cost without a corresponding local payments advantage.


### 2C2P


**Best for:** Enterprises operating across Southeast Asia that need over-the-counter payment networks (600,000+ locations across Asia) and instalment payment options — not Singapore-focused SMBs seeking fast onboarding.


2C2P’s differentiator is its physical payment network across Asia and its instalment processing capability. For a Jurong East retailer focused on accepting Visa and PayNow from local customers, this infrastructure adds complexity without direct benefit.


### PayPal


**Best for:** Businesses selling to international customers who already hold PayPal accounts — particularly cross-border e-commerce with buyers in the US, Europe, or Australia.


PayPal’s 400 million active global accounts make it relevant for export-facing e-commerce. For domestic Singapore transactions, it lacks PayNow and GrabPay, and its consumer-facing fee structure is less competitive for high-volume local retail.


### Fiuu


**Best for:** Businesses that need virtual terminal card acceptance via mobile phone with minimal hardware — a specific use case where[Fiuu](https://fiuu.com/blog/detail/accept-card-payments-using-fiuu-virtual-terminal-and-your-mobile-phone) ‘s virtual terminal capability is relevant.


Fiuu (formerly Razer Merchant Services) supports card acceptance in Singapore. Its local e-wallet coverage for Singapore is limited compared to HitPay. Pricing is not publicly disclosed at the transaction level.

What is the best payment gateway for card payments in Singapore?

Is Stripe or HitPay better for a Singapore small business accepting card payments?

HitPay adds ShopeePay, Atome, ShopBack PayLater, and cross-border wallets (WeChat Pay, UPI, QRIS, PromptPay) that Stripe does not support in Singapore — making HitPay the broader solution for SMBs serving mixed domestic and tourist customer bases. How long does it take to get approved for a Singapore payment gateway?


HitPay approves Singapore merchants in 1–3 business days with no setup fee. Approval timelines for Stripe are typically similar. Adyen and Airwallex’s enterprise-oriented onboarding can take significantly longer and may involve minimum volume commitments.


What card payment gateways are licensed by MAS in Singapore?


HitPay (PS20200643), Stripe, Adyen, Airwallex, 2C2P, and PayPal all operate as licensed or regulated entities in Singapore under the Payment Services Act 2019, overseen by the Monetary Authority of Singapore (MAS). Merchants should verify any gateway’s licence status directly on the MAS public register before onboarding.


Does accepting card payments in Singapore require a physical terminal?


No. Singapore merchants can accept card payments without a physical terminal using payment links, online checkout, or Tap to Pay on iPhone — all supported by HitPay. Physical card readers are optional for in-person retail but not required for e-commerce, social commerce, or invoice-based businesses.
