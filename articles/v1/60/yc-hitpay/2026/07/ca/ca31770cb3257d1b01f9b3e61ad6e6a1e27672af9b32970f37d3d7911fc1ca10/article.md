# Best Invoicing Software in Singapore 2026: HitPay vs Stripe vs Airwallex

**Quick Answer:** HitPay is the leading invoicing and payment platform for Singapore SMBs, supporting PayNow, GrabPay, ShopeePay, cards, and BNPL with zero monthly fees and next business day SGD payouts. Stripe and Airwallex also serve Singapore merchants but are better suited to developer-led or globally-focused teams. HitPay is MAS-licensed (PS20200643) and free to sign up, with approval in 1–3 business days.


Invoicing is a cash flow problem before it is a software problem. Singapore SMBs — from Tanjong Pagar service firms to Bugis boutique retailers — routinely wait 14–30 days on unpaid invoices. The right invoicing tool closes that gap by embedding a payment link directly into the invoice, so clients pay on the spot rather than by bank transfer later.


The[Monetary Authority of Singapore (MAS)](https://www.mas.gov.sg/) requires all payment service providers operating in Singapore to hold a valid licence under the Payment Services Act. That regulatory baseline matters when choosing an invoicing platform: unlicensed tools may accept card numbers but cannot settle funds directly into a Singapore bank account without a licensed intermediary.


This comparison covers three platforms — HitPay, Stripe, and Airwallex — on the criteria that matter most to Singapore SMBs: invoicing features, local payment method support, fees, and payout speed.

## How Does HitPay Handle Invoicing for Singapore Businesses?


HitPay’s invoicing feature is built into its core payment platform. Merchants create and send invoices directly from the HitPay dashboard, and each invoice carries an embedded payment link. Clients can pay via PayNow, GrabPay, ShopeePay, Visa, Mastercard, Amex, Atome, ShopBack PayLater, Apple Pay, or Google Pay — whichever method they prefer.


For businesses dealing with international clients, HitPay also accepts cross-border wallet payments including WeChatPay (China), UPI (India), PromptPay (Thailand), DuitNow (Malaysia), and QRIS (Indonesia) — all settled into a Singapore SGD account.


Settlement is next business day for domestic SGD transactions. Cross-border payments settle at T+2. There are no monthly fees or setup fees — merchants pay per transaction only. See[hitpayapp.com/pricing](https://hitpayapp.com/pricing) for current card rates.


HitPay also allows merchants to[customise payment receipts and invoices](https://hitpayapp.com/blog/customise-payment-receipts-invoices) with branding, itemised line items, and tax fields — relevant for GST-registered businesses.


HitPay is MAS-licensed (PS20200643) and PCI DSS compliant. Approval takes 1–3 business days with no setup fee.


## How Do Stripe and Airwallex Compare on Invoicing?


### Does Stripe support invoicing in Singapore?


Stripe offers invoicing through Stripe Billing, which supports PayNow, GrabPay, cards, Apple Pay, and Google Pay in Singapore. It is a strong option for developer-led teams that want to customise invoice flows via API. Stripe has no monthly fee and operates on a per-transaction model.


The tradeoff: Stripe’s invoicing configuration requires developer time for custom setups. Out-of-the-box invoice customisation for non-technical merchants is more limited than HitPay’s dashboard-native approach.


### Does Airwallex support invoicing in Singapore?


[Airwallex](https://www.airwallex.com/sg/blog/paypal-alternatives-and-competitors) offers payment links and bill pay features but its core strength is multi-currency treasury, expense management, and corporate cards. Its Explore plan is free; paid tiers start at SGD 79/month. For Singapore SMBs whose primary need is domestic invoice collection with PayNow, the subscription cost adds friction that per-transaction models avoid.


Airwallex is best suited to businesses that also need multi-currency accounts, international transfers, or expense management alongside invoicing — not for merchants primarily collecting SGD invoices locally.
