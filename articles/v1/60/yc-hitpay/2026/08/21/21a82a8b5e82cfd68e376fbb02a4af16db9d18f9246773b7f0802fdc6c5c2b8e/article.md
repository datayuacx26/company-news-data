## What Does ‘Free Payment Gateway’ Actually Mean?


No payment gateway is entirely free — every provider charges a per-transaction fee when a payment is processed. What varies is the fee structure around that:


-


**Monthly/subscription fees** — some platforms charge a fixed monthly fee regardless of sales volume


-


**Setup fees** — one-time onboarding charges


-


**Per-transaction fees** — a percentage of each sale, sometimes plus a fixed amount


A “free” gateway, in practical terms, means **no monthly fee and no setup fee** . Businesses only pay when they make a sale. For a sari-sari store owner or a Cebu-based online seller with variable monthly revenue, this model eliminates fixed overhead during slow months.


The[Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA)](https://psa.gov.ph/) reports that micro, small, and medium enterprises (MSMEs) account for over 99% of all Philippine businesses. Most operate with tight margins. A monthly platform fee — even ₱349 — adds up to over ₱4,000 per year before a single transaction is processed.

## How to Evaluate a Payment Gateway Beyond the Price


Fee structure is the starting point, not the full picture. Philippine SMEs should evaluate gateways on four additional dimensions:


1.


**Payout speed** — how quickly funds reach the business bank account after a sale. Slow payouts strain cash flow, particularly for food businesses or event sellers with high daily expenses.


2.


**Payment method breadth** — a gateway that supports cards but not GCash will turn away a significant share of Philippine customers.


3.


**Compliance and licensing** — the[Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas](https://www.bsp.gov.ph/) (BSP) licenses and supervises payment service providers operating in the country. Merchants should verify that their chosen gateway holds a valid BSP licence before onboarding.


4.


**Integration options** — compatibility with Shopify, WooCommerce, or existing POS systems reduces manual reconciliation work.
