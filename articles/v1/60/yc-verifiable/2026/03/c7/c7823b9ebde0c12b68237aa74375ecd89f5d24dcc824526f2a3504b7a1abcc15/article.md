# NADP Webinar Recap: How AI is Reshaping Credentialing for Dental Plans

For many dental plans, credentialing is still viewed as a back-office function. In reality, it is much more than that. It affects how quickly providers join the network, how confidently plans manage compliance, and how effectively teams support growth without adding headcount.


In the[recent NADP webinar](https://verifiable.com/videos/beyond-automation-ai-agents-unlock-up-to-10x-productivity-in-credentialing) , Verifiable leaders Rehan Mirza and Brett Dooies explored how AI is about more than speeding up manual work. It is about fundamentally changing how credentialing gets done.
‍


## **The growing pressure on dental plan networks**


Dental plans are facing a more volatile network environment on both the growth and retention sides.[ADA data shows](https://www.ada.org/resources/research/health-policy-institute/dental-care-market/state-of-the-us-dental-economy) that 35% of dentists plan to drop some insurance networks in 2026, raising the stakes for plans already working to maintain provider participation.


At the same time,[64 million Americans live in dental shortage areas](https://bhw.hrsa.gov/sites/default/files/bureau-health-workforce/data-research/State-of-US-Health-Care-Workforce-2025.pdf) , and the provider base is consolidating. As more practices move into larger groups and DSOs, recruitment and contracting shift from individual providers to organizations with more leverage and a greater willingness to walk away.


All of this is happening while dental plans face ongoing margin pressure and limited team capacity. Growth is harder, retention is less predictable, and the operational work required to keep networks stable is increasing. In that environment, a one-practice-at-a-time growth model cannot keep pace.


‍


## **Credentialing’s impact on network performance**


Credentialing is more than an administrative step. It influences how quickly plans can bring providers into the network, how smoothly they manage transitions, and how accurately they maintain provider data and compliance. When the process slows down, providers may not become available to members as quickly as expected, creating friction across operations, provider relations, and network performance.


## **Where the the old credentialing model no longer scales**


Most credentialing teams still operate in a linear model that processes one provider at a time. More volume requires more staff. More complexity requires more manual review.


For dental plans navigating state-by-state requirements, provider variation, and evolving documentation standards, that model creates clear limits on speed, consistency, and scalability.


## ‍ **What agentic AI changes in credentialing**


[Agentic AI](https://verifiable.com/credagent) moves credentialing from sequential work to parallel execution. It launches and advances thousands of tasks at once, then surfaces only the cases that need human judgment.


In the webinar demo, the system handled data validation, document review, work history checks, disclosure review, and license verification. Specialists focused on exceptions, judgment calls, and compliance-sensitive decisions.


That model creates the potential for 10x productivity because it changes how teams spend their time. Instead of moving routine files through each step manually, teams rely on AI to process high-volume work in parallel and focus their effort where expertise matters most. The result is faster throughput, greater capacity, and a more scalable operating model.


## **Credentialing is a practical place to start with agentic AI**


For dental plans evaluating where AI can deliver real operational value, credentialing stands out. It is administrative, highly structured, measurable, and tied to clear business outcomes.


That makes it a practical, lower-risk starting point for organizations looking to prove ROI, improve efficiency, and modernize a critical workflow without compromising oversight.


‍


## **Conclusion**


Credentialing has become a strategic lever for dental plans. It shapes provider access, operational efficiency, and the overall ease of doing business with your organization. As network pressure grows and the traditional linear model becomes harder to sustain, agentic AI gives plans a more scalable way to work. It expands capacity, reduces friction, and allows teams to focus human expertise where it matters most. For dental plans looking for a practical place to apply AI, credentialing stands out. **‍**
