## **What Is Days Sales Outstanding (DSO) in Dentistry?**


Days Sales Outstanding (DSO) measures the average number of days between delivering a dental service and receiving payment capturing how efficiently your revenue cycle converts production into cash.


In dental billing, "days in AR" and "DSO" are often used interchangeably. The subtle distinction: DSO calculates collection velocity on credit sales, while AR days describes the overall aging of your outstanding balances. In practice, both metrics tell the same story about whether your billing cycle is working.


DSO applies differently across practice structures:


- **Solo practices** tracks whether a small team is keeping up with claims and patient follow-up
- **Dental groups** reveals consistency (or inconsistency) across multiple locations
- **DSO organizations** benchmarks centralized billing office performance across the entire portfolio


A DSO under 30 days means payments arrive quickly and your revenue cycle is healthy. A DSO over 45 days means earned revenue is sitting idle in unpaid claims and overdue patient balances that should and can be in your bank account. For context on how DSO intersects with patient experience, see dental[DSO benchmarks and patient experience](https://www.arini.ai/blog/dso-patient-experience-benchmarks) data.

## **How to Calculate Your Practice's DSO**


The dental practice DSO average is calculated with a simple formula, and running it monthly gives you more revenue cycle insight than almost any other metric.


**The formula:**


**DSO = (Accounts Receivable ÷ Total Credit Sales) × Number of Days**


**Worked example:**


If your practice has $75,000 in outstanding AR and $90,000 in total production last month:


DSO = ($75,000 ÷ $90,000) × 30 = **25 days** Excellent


If the same practice had $150,000 in AR, the DSO would be 50 days a clear signal of billing delays or patient balances accumulating.


**Two calculation methods:**


- **Rolling 30-day method:** Use the prior 30 days of collections as the denominator. Best for spotting month-to-month trends quickly.
- **Countback method:** Start from the most recent month and subtract monthly gross sales from the AR balance until it reaches zero, counting the applicable days. More accurate for longer-term benchmarking.


Most practice management software including OpenDental, EagleSoft, Denticon, and CareStack can generate the AR and production figures you need automatically. Run this calculation monthly and compare it against the benchmark scorecard below.

## **Clean Claims: Documentation, Compliance, and Verification**


High DSO is almost always downstream of upstream documentation failures. The industry standard for a clean claim requires specific documentation attached at submission and skipping any element adds days to your DSO.


### **Clean Claim Documentation Requirements**


Every dental claim that is submitted without errors requires:


- **Accurate CDT codes** procedure codes must match the documented treatment and the payer's allowed code list
- **Supporting clinical documentation** X-rays, narrative descriptions, and periodontal charting for procedures that require them (extractions, implants, crowns, periodontal therapy)
- **Pre-authorization confirmation** many plans require pre-authorization for high-value procedures; submitting without it guarantees a denial
- **Correct patient demographics** date of birth, subscriber ID, and payer ID must match exactly what the payer has on file
- **Provider credentials** NPI, tax ID, and group/individual billing configuration must be correct and current


A single missing field can trigger an automatic denial. The most effective prevention is real-time claim scrubbing before submission automated checking that catches missing or mismatched fields before the claim leaves the practice.


### **Real-Time Eligibility Verification**


Real-time insurance eligibility verification is the highest-impact upstream intervention for reducing DSO. It confirms coverage, deductibles, frequency limitations, and benefit maximums electronically at the time of scheduling or check-in. Practices that verify eligibility before every appointment[reduce eligibility-related denials](https://dentalaiassist.com/blog/how-to-reduce-dental-insurance-claim-denials/) by up to 60%.


Best practice: verify 48–72 hours before the appointment. This allows time to correct errors before the patient arrives. Re-verify at check-in for same-day appointments. Modern systems OpenDental, Denticon, and CareStack support real-time eligibility checks through clearinghouse integrations.


### **Compliance Considerations**


Dental billing systems handle protected health information (PHI) and financial data, requiring HIPAA compliance at every layer:


- **EFT enrollment:** Electronic funds transfer with major payers reduces payment turnaround by 5–10 days and eliminates virtual credit card fees that erode collections
- **Secure patient billing:** Automated billing platforms must use HIPAA-compliant encryption for patient statements and payment portals
- **Role-based access controls:** Billing staff should have access limited to the data required for their role a HIPAA requirement and an internal control that prevents errors and unauthorized changes


### **DSO Limitations: What the Metric Doesn't Capture**


DSO is the best single-number summary of revenue cycle performance. But it has real limitations operators need to understand:


- **It averages out location-level variance:** A DSO of 35 days at the portfolio level can mask locations running at 18 days and others at 90 days. Location-level DSO tracking is essential for multi-site groups.
- **It doesn't separate insurance from patient AR:** High insurance AR and high patient AR require different interventions. Track them separately.
- **It can look artificially good after write-offs:** Writing off uncollectable balances improves DSO without actually improving collections. Track write-off rate alongside DSO to get the true picture.
- **It doesn't capture production efficiency:** A practice can improve DSO by reducing production more patients with faster cash cycles. Always benchmark DSO alongside collection rate and total production.

## **How to Reduce DSO: 7 Proven Strategies**


These seven actions address the most common DSO drivers in dental practices. Implement them in order of leverage upstream actions first, downstream cleanup second.


1. **Verify insurance eligibility before every appointment.** Confirming coverage, deductibles, co-pays, and benefit maximums 48–72 hours before the visit prevents the most common source of clean claim failures. Learn[how to automate insurance verification](https://www.arini.ai/blog/how-to-automate-insurance-verification) for every scheduled patient without adding manual steps for your team.
2. **Submit clean claims on the day of service.** Same-day claim submission is one of the highest-leverage billing habits available. Every day a claim sits unsubmitted is a day added to DSO. Use your PMS (OpenDental, EagleSoft, Denticon, or CareStack) to set automated end-of-day claim exports.
3. **Follow up on every claim past 30 days.** No insurance claim should sit without a payer response for more than 30 days. Set a 30-day aging trigger in your billing workflow and assign specific ownership for follow-up.[Automating billing inquiry management](https://www.arini.ai/blog/how-to-automate-billing-inquiries-in-dental-practices) reduces the manual burden without requiring additional staff.
4. **Build a denial management workflow.** Every denial should be categorized by reason code, corrected, and resubmitted within five business days. Track denial reasons by payer patterns reveal systemic issues with how a specific carrier is being billed, not just individual errors.
5. **Send patient statements within 7 days of insurance posting.** Delayed patient billing is a leading cause of patient AR aging. Automated statement delivery via email, text, or paper shortens the patient billing cycle and dramatically reduces follow-up call volume.
6. **Collect at the point of service.** Practices that collect the patient-owed portion at checkout eliminate a significant slice of patient AR before it can age. For larger balances, offer payment plans with automatic recurring payments not monthly manual collection attempts.
7. **Review the AR aging report weekly, not monthly.** Monthly reviews are too slow: a claim can move from 30 to 60 days in a single billing cycle. Weekly reviews catch exceptions before they become write-offs. Assign a specific team member to own the aging report and act on exceptions within 48 hours.
