## **What Is Dental Net Collection Rate?**


Dental net collection rate is the percentage of allowable charges — total production minus contractual insurance write-offs — that a dental practice actually collects. The 2026 benchmark for top-performing practices is 98% or higher. The industry average sits in the mid-80s to low 90s, leaving most practices 6–15 percentage points of legitimate, collectible revenue uncollected every month.


Unlike gross collection rate, which compares total payments to total charges (including non-collectible contracted discounts), net collection rate gives you a true picture of billing efficiency. A practice with a heavy PPO mix may show a gross collection rate of 75–80%, yet have a net collection rate of 97% — meaning they're actually collecting nearly everything they're allowed to charge.


**Why it matters:** Dental net collection rate is the most diagnostic metric in a practice's revenue cycle — a rate below 95% is always a systems problem, never just an insurance problem. Gross collection rate is distorted by payer mix. Net collection rate is not.


A practice whose dental net collection rate consistently hits 98% or above has built airtight processes around eligibility verification, clean claim submission, and denial management. A practice stuck in the low 90s almost certainly has not.


## **How to Calculate Your Net Collection Rate**


The formula is straightforward:


**Net Collection Rate = (Total Payments Received ÷ Allowed Charges) × 100**


Where:


- **Total Payments Received** = all cash collected from patients and insurance in a given period
- **Allowed Charges** = total production minus contractual write-offs (the amounts insurance has pre-negotiated you to write off per your PPO contracts)


**Step-by-step example:**


Net Collection Rate Example Table Item Amount


Total production (charges) $120,000


Contractual adjustments (PPO write-offs) −$22,000


Allowed/adjusted charges $98,000


Total payments collected $95,060


**Net collection rate** **97.0%**


A few important notes on accurate calculation:


- **Use a rolling 3-month average** rather than a single month. Monthly fluctuations caused by holidays, end-of-year insurance rushes, and billing cycles can distort a single month's figure.
- **Exclude non-contractual adjustments from your denominator.** Courtesy discounts and professional write-offs that were elective should not inflate your numerator — they should be excluded from the allowed charges figure.
- **Most practice management software can generate this report automatically.** In Dentrix, Eaglesoft, and OpenDental, the "Collection Ratio" or "Net Collection Rate" report runs this calculation. Reconcile quarterly against your bank deposits to catch discrepancies.

### **What is the difference between gross and net collection rate?**


Gross collection rate compares total payments collected to total fees charged — including contracted insurance discounts that were never collectible. Net collection rate adjusts the denominator to exclude contractual write-offs, leaving only the revenue you were legitimately owed. For PPO-heavy practices, for example, a gross collection rate of 78% and a net collection rate of 97.5% can coexist — the net rate is the meaningful number.
