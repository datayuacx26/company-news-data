## **Top 5 Causes of Dental Claim Denials**


Most dental claim denials trace back to a handful of root causes. Understanding the breakdown helps dental practices target their workflow improvements where they will have the greatest impact on the denial rate benchmark.


According to detailed analysis of dental claim denial patterns, the top five causes by frequency are:


1. **Insurance eligibility errors (~35% of denials)** Expired coverage, wrong subscriber ID, out-of-network status, or benefits that changed since the patient's last visit. This is the single largest driver and largely preventable through real-time verification before treatment is delivered.
2. **Missing or incorrect patient information (~25% of denials)** Wrong date of birth, incorrect subscriber name, or policy number entered incorrectly at the time of booking. Front-desk errors at intake create downstream billing failures that surface weeks later.
3. **CDT coding errors or mismatched procedure codes (~20% of denials)** Incorrect code selection, bundling violations, or procedure codes that don't match the documented clinical findings. Requires ongoing biller training and pre-submission claims scrubbing.
4. **Missing pre-authorization or pre-treatment estimates (~15% of denials)** Major restorative procedures, periodontal therapy, and orthodontic treatment frequently require prior authorization. Major payers including Delta Dental, MetLife, Guardian, Cigna, Aetna, and United Concordia each maintain distinct prior authorization requirements submitting without meeting them guarantees a denial with no path to immediate resolution.
5. **Timely filing violations (~5% of denials)** Each payer sets its own claims filing window. Delta Dental typically allows 12 months; MetLife and Cigna allow 90–180 days. Submitting after that window closes results in a denial with no right to appeal, regardless of the clinical merits of the claim.


**Additional denial triggers** that fall outside the top five include frequency limitation violations (e.g., two cleanings per year limit exceeded), out-of-network provider claims, missing radiographs or periodontal charts, and coordination of benefits errors when a patient carries dual coverage.


Eligibility and patient information errors the top two causes at a combined 60% of all denials both originate from what happens during the first patient contact.[Streamlining new patient intake](https://www.arini.ai/blog/how-to-streamline-new-patient-intake-processes-with-ai) processes is where the largest single reduction in dental claim denial rates becomes available.

## **How to Calculate and Track Your Own Denial Rate**


Your denial rate the share of submitted claims returned as denied is calculated from two numbers in your claims reporting.


**Denial Rate Formula:**


**Denial Rate = (Total Claims Denied ÷ Total Claims Submitted) × 100**


For example: if your practice submitted 400 claims last month and 52 were returned as denied, your denial rate is 13% above the 10% alert threshold and well above the 5% target.


**How to build a tracking workflow:**

- **Track clean claim rate alongside denial rate.** Clean claim rate is the leading indicator when it improves, denial rate follows, confirming upstream changes are working.

## **Common Dental Billing Mistakes to Avoid**


Most dental practices with denial rates above 10% share a predictable set of avoidable mistakes:


- **Collecting insurance information at check-in instead of at booking** - By the time the patient arrives, there is no practical window to verify and correct insurance data before treatment is delivered. Errors at check-in become billing failures that surface weeks later as denied claims.
- **Not categorizing denials by reason code** - Practices that log denials without segmenting by code spend hours chasing individual claims rather than fixing the system-level gaps generating them repeatedly.
- **Letting denied claims age past 60 days** - Most payer resubmission windows are 90–180 days. After 90 days, the percentage of recoverable denied revenue drops sharply. The industry default - roughly two-thirds of denied claims never resubmitted reflects this drift.
- **Skipping pre-authorization for flagged procedure types** - Major restorative work, periodontal therapy, and orthodontic treatment often require prior authorization. Submitting without confirming authorization guarantees a denial with no path to immediate resolution.
- **Under-investing in CDT update training** - Annual CDT code changes create new bundling violations and coverage mismatches. Practices without ongoing biller training inherit new denial sources with each code cycle.
- **Relying on manual eligibility verification at check-in** - Manual verification is too slow and error-prone for a full patient schedule. Real-time eligibility checks integrated with structured intake workflows are the operational standard at below-5% denial rate practices.
