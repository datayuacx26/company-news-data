## **What Is Aged AR in Dental Practices?**


Aged AR in dental practices is accounts receivable that has remained uncollected beyond a defined threshold typically 60, 90, or 120 days from the date of service or initial claim submission. Accounts in the 90+ day bucket are the primary measure of AR health because the probability of collection falls sharply once claims reach that age.


Most dental practice management platforms and billing benchmarks track AR aging in four buckets: 0-30 days (current), 31-60 days (early warning), 61-90 days (action required), and 90+ days (critical). Best-run dental practices keep AR over 90 days below[5% of total AR](https://www.verimedix.com/dental-accounts-receivable-how-to-reduce-ar-aging-and-collect-more-in-2026/) . Practices where this bucket exceeds 22% of total AR experience write-off rates three to four times higher than their peers.


Aged AR has two components in dental: insurance AR (unpaid insurance claims) and patient AR (patient-responsible balances after insurance). Each ages differently and requires a distinct recovery approach.


- **Insurance AR** ages primarily because of denied claims that were not resubmitted promptly, missing documentation, or payer-specific errors in the original submission. These claims have a strict timely-filing window most payers set this at 90-365 days from date of service after which re-submission is no longer possible regardless of the merit of the claim.
- **Patient AR** ages because collection at point of service was not completed, follow-up was inconsistent or delayed, or billing statements were sent by mail with no automated escalation to phone or SMS outreach.


The same coverage gaps that cause practices to[miss patient billing calls](https://www.arini.ai/blog/how-to-stop-missing-patient-calls) also leave patient AR aging without consistent follow-up. Both require different workflows and both are areas where the difference between AI and manual collections is significant.

## **The Manual Collections Process: What It Actually Takes**


Running manual dental AR collections means assigning a billing specialist or more often, a front desk coordinator doing double duty to work through an AR aging report on a schedule. In practice, this is what that workflow looks like:


1. Pull an AR aging report from the practice management system
2. Sort by age (90+ days first) and by dollar value (highest balances first)
3. For insurance AR: identify denial reason from the[EOB or payer portal](https://www.arini.ai/blog/catch-cob-mistakes) , gather any missing documentation, correct coding errors, and resubmit all done claim by claim
4. For patient AR: generate and mail a paper statement, then call the patient if no payment arrives within 10–14 days a cycle that can repeat two to three times before escalation to a collection agency
5. Update the practice management system with each follow-up action so the audit trail is maintained
6. Track timely-filing deadlines manually, because a claim resubmitted after the payer's window closes cannot be recovered regardless of its validity


This process is not flawed in concept. But it has structural limitations that matter when AR aging is the problem:


**Coverage gaps.** Insurance AR denial notices arrive seven days a week, including after hours. Manual workflows only cover business hours and if Friday's denials are not caught until Monday, a claim has already lost two days of its appeal window.


**Prioritization accuracy.** Manual aging report review tends to focus on recent, large-dollar claims. Smaller claims from 90-120 days ago the ones approaching write-off territory often sit at the bottom of the list and never get worked.


**Scalability ceiling.** A single dental billing specialist at[$63,529/year](https://www.salary.com/research/salary/posting/dental-billing-specialist-salary) can typically manage a set number of accounts before productivity drops. As a group adds locations, the headcount required scales linearly and so do the salary costs.


**Clean claim rate.** The industry average for manual billing is[80-85% clean claims on first submission](https://mconsent.net/blog/hidden-cost-manual-dental-billing-eliminate/) . That means 15-20% of claims require at least one round of correction and resubmission each round, adding days to the collection timeline and increasing the probability that the account ages into a higher-risk bucket.

### **What is the timely-filing window for dental claims?**


Most dental insurance payers set timely-filing windows of 90-365 days from the date of service after which claims cannot be submitted or resubmitted regardless of their merit. This makes response speed to denials critical: a claim denied at day 60 that is not resubmitted within 30 days may fall outside the timely-filing window permanently, converting a recoverable claim into a write-off. AI-driven denial management which flags and routes denials in real time directly reduces the number of claims lost to timely-filing deadline expiration.
