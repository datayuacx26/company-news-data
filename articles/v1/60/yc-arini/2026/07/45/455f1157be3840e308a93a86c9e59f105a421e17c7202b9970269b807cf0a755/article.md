## **What Are Dental Write-Offs?**


A dental write-off is the difference between what a practice bills and what it is contractually allowed or realistically able to collect. Write-offs reduce gross production to net collectible revenue and when they run too high, they represent real dollars permanently removed from the practice's income.


Every practice has write-offs. The question is whether yours fall within a healthy range or whether they're silently eroding profitability month over month.


## **The Three Types of Dental Write-Offs**


Not all write-offs are the same, and most practice management software lumps them together. Separating them is the first step to understanding where your revenue is actually going.


### **1. Contractual / PPO Adjustments**


The most common type. When a practice is in-network with a PPO plan, the insurance company sets a maximum fee per CDT code. The difference between your standard fee (UCR) and the PPO fee is a contractual write-off you agreed to it when you signed the contract ([ADA Insurance Guide](https://www.ada.org/resources/practice/practice-management/dental-insurance) ). When these adjustments are posted incorrectly, they compound your write-off rate a pattern covered in depth in the guide to EOB write-off posting errors.


- **Example:** You charge $250 for a filling. The PPO fee schedule pays $140. The $110 difference is a contractual adjustment.
- This is expected and unavoidable if you participate with PPOs.
- The benchmark question is whether your PPO mix is resulting in a write-off rate that's cutting too deep.


### **2. Bad Debt Write-Offs**


These occur when a patient balance goes uncollected despite follow-up phone calls, statements, reminders, or even collections referrals and the practice ultimately removes the balance from accounts receivable ([Dental Economics](https://www.dentaleconomics.com/) ).


- Bad debt is a failure of the collections process, not the insurance contract.
- It typically stems from patients who weren't collected at the point of service, misunderstood their out-of-pocket costs, or simply couldn't be reached after the visit.


### **3. Courtesy Discounts**


Provider-initiated adjustments given to family members, cash-pay patients, hardship cases, or as goodwill gestures. These are discretionary and should be tracked separately so they don't distort your contractual or bad debt metrics ([Dentistry IQ](https://www.dentistryiq.com/) ).


**Pro Tip:** If your practice management software shows one "adjustments" bucket, break it into these three categories. You can't manage what you can't measure.


## **Why Write-Off Rates Are Getting Harder to Manage in 2026**


Most dental billing conversations assume static conditions a fixed PPO mix, stable overhead, and a fully staffed front office. None of those assumptions hold in 2026.


Several structural pressures are pushing dental write-off rates higher across practice types:


- **PPO reimbursements haven't kept pace with inflation.** Many fee schedules were set 3–5 years ago with minimal adjustments. Supply costs, lab fees, and staff salaries have increased significantly in the same window. A procedure with a 30% write-off in 2021 could now carry a significantly higher write-off rate as UCR fees rise faster than carrier updates.
- **High-deductible health plans are shifting more cost to patients.** Employers are moving employees onto HDHPs. More patients arrive with larger out-of-pocket obligations and a higher probability of non-collection if those amounts aren't secured at the point of service.
- **Front desk turnover is creating AR follow-up gaps.** The dental industry has faced persistent staffing challenges since 2020. New or rotating front desk staff produce spikes in aging AR. Bad debt write-offs rise as follow-up protocols slip between staff transitions.
- **Claim complexity is increasing.** Coordination of benefits between dual-coverage patients, mid-treatment plan changes, and frequent payer system updates generate more rejections. Each rejected claim that isn't appealed within the timely filing window becomes a write-off.[COB posting mistakes](https://arini.ai/blog/catch-cob-mistakes) are among the most common sources.
- **Missed calls compound the problem.** Every unanswered call is a patient who never books, never generates production, and never appears in a write-off report but the revenue loss is just as real.


Understanding your dental write-off rate in this environment isn't just a billing exercise it's a practice health check. The benchmarks below give you the context to know where you stand.

## **How to Calculate Your Write-Off Rate**


You don't need specialized software to calculate your baseline write-off rate. Here's the formula:


**Contractual Write-Off Rate:**


(Total PPO Adjustments ÷ Gross Production) × 100


**Bad Debt Write-Off Rate:**


(Bad Debt Write-Offs ÷ Gross Production) × 100


**Net Collection Rate:**


(Collections ÷ Net Production) × 100


Where Net Production = Gross Production minus contractual adjustments.


Pull a 90-day period from your practice management software for a meaningful baseline. One-month snapshots can be skewed by claim timing and seasonal volume.

## **Best Practices**


- **Separate write-off categories in your PMS** don't let contractual adjustments, bad debt, and courtesy discounts roll into a single line item
- **Run a write-off report monthly** and track trends quarter-over-quarter, not just as a year-end exercise
- **Set a bad debt threshold at 1.5%** of production as your internal warning line catching problems at 1.5% is far easier than managing them at 3%
- **Renegotiate PPO contracts every two years** most carriers have a process for this; most practices never use it
- **Collect at the chair** a conversation about financial responsibility during treatment planning reduces bad debt more than any follow-up system
- **Verify insurance before every appointment** not just for new patients
- **Automate reminder sequences** for aging AR practices that automate this typically see meaningful reductions in average AR days and improved collection rates
- **Review your write-off rate against the practice type benchmarks above** a solo practice at 44% may be average; a DSO-affiliated practice at 44% has a serious problem


## **Common Mistakes**


- **Lumping all adjustments into one category** makes it impossible to identify whether the problem is contractual, bad debt, or discretionary
- **Waiting until year-end to review write-offs** by then, 3–4 months of AR have aged past the point of easy recovery
- **Accepting the first PPO fee schedule offered** insurance companies negotiate; practices just rarely ask
- **Skipping financial discussions at treatment planning** patients who don't understand their out-of-pocket costs are far more likely to become bad debt
- **Underinvesting in claim follow-up** a denied claim that's never appealed becomes a write-off that should have been revenue
- **Treating missed calls as a service issue rather than a revenue issue** every missed call is a potential patient who becomes $0 in production and $0 in collections
- **Counting write-off rate without checking net collection rate** a low write-off rate with a low net collection rate means your billing process is the problem, not your contracts


## **What to Do Based on Your Write-Off Rate**


Write-off benchmarks are only useful when they drive action. Here's what to prioritize based on where your practice actually stands:


**If your contractual write-off rate is under 30%:**


You're in excellent shape. Maintain an annual fee schedule audit to hold your position as UCR fees evolve. This range typically reflects strong PPO negotiation or a lean in-network mix protect it by reviewing your PPO participation mix before signing any new carrier contracts.


**If your contractual write-off rate is 30–35%:**


Healthy range. Focus on quarterly monitoring and a scheduled renegotiation cycle with your carriers every two years. Minor improvements on your highest-volume procedure codes can move you toward the lower end of this band over time without changing your insurance mix.


**If your contractual write-off rate is 35–45%:**


Watch zone. Start with a fee schedule audit against current UCR rates in your market particularly on your top 10–15 procedure codes by volume. Request renegotiation on the codes where the gap is widest. Most practices in this range can recover meaningful revenue without dropping PPO plans entirely.


**If your contractual write-off rate is above 45%:**


Critical. For every $1,000 produced, you're keeping less than $550 before overhead. Run a full PPO participation analysis you may have plans generating negative margin after expenses. Consider dropping the lowest-paying plans or negotiating as a condition of continued participation.


**If your bad debt write-off rate is above 2%:**


Your collections protocol needs immediate attention. Start with point-of-service collections and pre-treatment financial consultations. Patients who leave without paying become significantly harder to collect from after 90 days every dollar of bad debt prevented at the chair is worth far more than a dollar chased through AR after 90 days, when collection rates drop sharply and agency fees consume a significant portion of any recovery.


**If your net collection rate is below 95%:**


This is the most urgent scenario. A low net collection rate with normal contractual write-offs means your billing process is the problem: denied claims not being appealed, submissions with errors, or follow-up not happening on aging balances. Audit your 30-60-90-day AR buckets immediately.

A **contractual write-off** is the discount you agreed to when signing a PPO contract it's expected and unavoidable for in-network providers.
