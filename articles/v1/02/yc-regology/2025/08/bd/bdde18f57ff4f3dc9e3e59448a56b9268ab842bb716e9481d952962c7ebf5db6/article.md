# Making Sense of the Noise: Staying Ahead of Compliance Changes

If you work in compliance, you know this moment: you look at your inbox and see a flood of regulatory updates. You skim the subject lines, wondering which ones actually matter, and which ones will quietly pile up until the next team meeting. It’s not that you're unaware of change. It’s that there’s too much of it, too often, and not enough time to respond meaningfully.


That’s the modern compliance challenge in a nutshell. Not a lack of information, but too much of it — without the time, context, or clarity to act. You’re not facing a “storm.” You’re just stuck in a noisy room, trying to pick out the one voice that matters most.


In this post, we’ll show you what you can do manually to improve the noise filtering process, and how you can get a force multiplier in an automated platform.


## It’s Not Discovery—It’s the Deluge


In healthcare alone, hospitals and health systems must comply with[hundreds of discrete regulatory requirements](https://www.congress.gov/118/meeting/house/116166/documents/HHRG-118-SM24-20230719-SD002.pdf) across multiple domains, costing the industry tens of billions a year in administrative compliance overhead, according to the American Hospital Association. These regulations come from HIPAA, CMS, OCR, OSHA, HHS, and state-specific laws, each with its own updates, timelines, and terminology.


In banking and financial services, the noise is just as deafening. A single institution might be subject to overlapping rulemakings from the OCC, FRB, SEC, FinCEN, CFPB, and dozens of state regulators — each monitoring everything from AI risk management and fraud detection to consumer lending practices. One week, it’s an advisory on model validation from the OCC. The next, it’s a proposed update to SAR reporting rules from FinCEN. Meanwhile, your internal stakeholders want to know: does this affect us? Do we need to rewrite anything?


Whether you're in healthcare, finance, energy, insurance, or tech, the pattern is the same: compliance teams are not so much struggling to discover regulations (while timely discovery is also a challenge at times) as they are struggling to *filter, interpret,* and *act on* them with confidence and speed.


To top it off, compliance teams have always been small compared to most other departments in an organization.[Our 2025 survey](https://www.regology.com/blog/state-of-regulatory-compliance-in-2025-survey-results) found that the median size for a compliance team is only four (4) employees, and spreadsheets still dominate compliance tracking, with nearly 80% of teams using them.


The need for automation is glaring, especially looking at the sheer volume of regulatory change and the fact that almost 33% of compliance managers lack confidence in their compliance readiness.


While switching to a compliance management platform may be on your roadmap, what you can do short-term is tighten your manual processes: build a consistent triage method for incoming updates and document your response decisions.


## Categorizing Alerts Like a News Editor


Think of regulatory updates like your own newsroom feed: some stories are front-page material, others belong in the opinion section, and quite a few never make it past the cutting room floor. To reduce noise, you need to categorize alerts by what matters to your specific industry, jurisdiction, and business needs. For teams managing updates manually, it’s important to have a clear framework to prioritize what requires immediate attention versus what’s informational.


Here’s one way to structure your “editorial desk”:


- **Bills in Progress:** These are like early-stage news tips. You might receive hundreds of alerts about proposed legislation each month. But only a small fraction—around 5%—ever become law. An editor wouldn’t greenlight every rumor for publication, and your team shouldn’t chase every bill. Track the ones that are gaining traction and match your legal profile; the rest can be discarded.


- **Regulatory Changes and Rulemaking:** These are your front-pagers; your lead stories. They are critical to day-to-day operations. Finalized changes often require updates to internal policies, procedures, or operations. The challenge is spotting all the relevant updates and doing so in time and correctly identifying which jurisdiction or authority takes precedence—especially when federal, state, and international rules overlap.


- **Agency Bulletins and Updates:** Think of these as editorials and press releases. They don’t always warrant a full read-through but may contain useful indicators of tone or future direction. These are worth a scan, logged in your “industry mood board,” and pulled out when they become more relevant.


- **Enforcement Actions:** These are your watchdog reports. They provide valuable insight into current regulatory priorities and where the scrutiny is heating up. Compliance teams can monitor these actions to stay ahead of emerging focus areas — and make sure their own practices don’t raise red flags.


But even with a solid framework, manually scanning and sorting every update is a heavy lift — especially when regulations span multiple jurisdictions and arrive faster than you can triage them.
