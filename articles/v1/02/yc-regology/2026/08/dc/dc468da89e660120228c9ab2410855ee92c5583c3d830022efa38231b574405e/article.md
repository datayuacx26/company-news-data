# Webinar Recap: How Bloomberg Regology Turns Regulatory Change into Compliance Action

Regulatory change does not slow down for anyone. New rules, amended statutes, and agency guidance keep coming, and for legal and compliance teams already managing hundreds of stakeholders and thousands of policies, knowing what changed is just the beginning. The harder question is: what did your organization actually do about it?


That question sat at the center of a recent Bloomberg Regology webinar featuring Mukund Goenka, Head of Bloomberg Regology and Bridget Kerlick, Head of Sales and Customer Service for Bloomberg Regology. The two walked through how AI-powered regulatory intelligence can take compliance programs from passive observation to active, documented action.


## The Challenge Most Compliance Programs Still Face


Most organizations have some form of regulatory monitoring in place. But the real problem is what happens after an alert lands. Spotting a change is one thing. Routing it to the right owner, assessing its impact on existing policies, and keeping a clear record of everything that followed is where things tend to fall apart.


Goenka put it plainly: compliance teams often find themselves in a position where they can say, "We saw the change." But what regulators, auditors, and leadership actually need to hear is something more complete: "We assessed it, assigned it, acted on it, and can prove it."


That gap between awareness and accountability is where Bloomberg Regology focuses.


## Why Monitoring Alone Falls Short


Regulatory monitoring tools have helped teams track federal and state developments for years. But monitoring is a passive function. It tells you what changed, not what it means for your organization, who should own the response, or how to document the decision trail.


For teams operating across multiple jurisdictions, that limitation gets costly. A single federal rulemaking can trigger different obligations at the state level, affecting separate teams, policies, and workflows all at once. Kerlick pointed out that without a structured system to manage that kind of complexity, compliance becomes reactive rather than strategic.


Bloomberg Regology bridges the gap between the intelligence layer (what changed and why it matters) and the action layer: who responds, how, and with what outcome.


## How Bloomberg Regology Puts AI to Work


The platform brings together several components that move a team from notification to resolution.


The **Smart Law Library** is the foundation. It pulls federal and state regulatory content into a structured, searchable repository so compliance professionals are not hunting across scattered sources. Everything lives in one curated environment.


**Alerts** keep teams informed as developments happen. But these are not generic notifications. Bloomberg Regology's AI engine, **Reggi** , filters incoming updates and surfaces only what applies to the organization's defined regulatory scope. Less noise, faster response.


**Impact assessment** tools help teams evaluate what a new or amended regulation actually means for their policies and operations. This step is where many legacy approaches struggle, relying on slow, manual review processes that get applied inconsistently.


**Workflow ownership** features assign accountability directly within the platform. Once a change is flagged and assessed, it gets routed to the right stakeholder with documented instructions and clear deadlines. That structure removes the ambiguity that quietly creates compliance gaps.


**Reporting** capabilities give compliance leaders something they can stand behind: a documented record of how each regulatory change was handled, who received it, what was assessed, what action was taken, and when.


## A Real-World Example: Managing Compliance Across a Multi-State Health System


The webinar used a multi-state health system to show what this looks like in practice. The organization managed federal and state developments across dozens of jurisdictions, with hundreds of internal stakeholders and thousands of individual policies to keep current.


Before Bloomberg Regology, the team had trouble routing relevant changes to the right people fast enough. They also had no reliable way to show that the organization had responded appropriately. Audits meant manually piecing together compliance timelines, which was time-consuming and often incomplete.


With Bloomberg Regology, the team could track developments through the Smart Law Library and receive targeted alerts from Reggi. When a new state health mandate came through, the platform's impact assessment tools helped identify which policies needed updating. Workflow ownership features assigned those updates to named stakeholders with clear timelines. And the resulting reports gave auditors a complete, accurate record of the response, without the last-minute scramble.


## The Shift That Actually Matters


The biggest takeaway from the webinar is not about any single feature. It is about changing how compliance programs operate and how they prove their value.


"We saw the change" is the starting line, not the finish. What legal and compliance leaders, and the executives and regulators they answer to, need is evidence of a complete, traceable response. Bloomberg Regology makes that evidence something you can actually produce, reliably and at scale.


For teams that have built their compliance programs around monitoring tools and manual follow-up, closing the gap between awareness and accountability can feel like a heavy lift. But as Goenka and Kerlick both made clear, the path forward is connecting intelligence to action in one integrated system.


## Scaling Compliance as Regulatory Complexity Grows


The regulatory environment is not getting simpler. Across healthcare, financial services, manufacturing, and other sectors, the volume and pace of change keeps increasing. Teams that rely on manual processes to bridge the gap between what changed and what was done about it will find it harder to meet the accountability expectations that come with that environment.


Bloomberg Regology gives legal and compliance teams the infrastructure to meet that challenge. Not just to monitor regulatory change, but to assess it, assign it, act on it, and prove it.


If your organization is ready to move from reactive compliance to documented, defensible action, it is worth exploring what Bloomberg Regology can do for your team.


## Frequently Asked Questions


## What Is Bloomberg Regology, and Who Is It Designed For?


Bloomberg Regology is an AI-powered regulatory intelligence platform built for legal and compliance teams. It helps organizations track regulatory changes, assess their impact on existing policies, assign ownership of compliance responses, and generate auditable records of each action taken.


## How Does Reggi Help Compliance Teams Manage Regulatory Change?


Reggi is Bloomberg Regology's AI engine. It filters incoming federal and state regulatory developments and surfaces only those relevant to a team's defined regulatory scope, cutting through the noise so compliance professionals can focus on what actually matters.


## What Is the Smart Law Library?


The Smart Law Library is a structured, searchable repository of federal and state regulatory content at the core of the Bloomberg Regology platform. It gives compliance teams a single, curated source for the legal content they need to track and respond to regulatory change.


## How Does Bloomberg Regology Support Audit Readiness?


Bloomberg Regology's reporting tools generate documented records of how each regulatory change was handled, including who received the alert, what impact assessment was completed, which stakeholder was assigned ownership, what action was taken, and when. That documentation supports both internal reviews and external audits.


## What Types of Organizations Benefit Most from Bloomberg Regology?


Organizations that operate across multiple jurisdictions, manage large volumes of policies, or run compliance programs with many internal stakeholders tend to benefit most. The webinar highlighted a multi-state health system as one example, but the platform applies across healthcare, finance, manufacturing, and any sector subject to complex or fast-moving regulatory requirements.


## How Is Bloomberg Regology Different from a Standard Regulatory Monitoring Tool?


Standard monitoring tools tell you what changed. Bloomberg Regology goes further by connecting those notifications to impact assessment, workflow ownership, and reporting. The result is documented, actionable compliance rather than just awareness.
