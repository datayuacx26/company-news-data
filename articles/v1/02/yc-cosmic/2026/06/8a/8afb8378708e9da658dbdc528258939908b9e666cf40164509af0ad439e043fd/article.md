# Cosmic Rundown: Rocket Lab Buys Iridium, ATS Scoring Chaos, and Age Verification Creep

## Rocket Lab Acquires Iridium


Rocket Lab announced it will[acquire Iridium](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48719485) , the satellite communications company known for its constellation of 66 low-Earth orbit satellites. This is a significant vertical integration play, combining launch capability with an established satellite network.


For the space industry, this consolidation signals that pure-play launch providers are looking beyond rockets. Owning the payload infrastructure changes the economics entirely. The[Hacker News discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48719485) digs into what this means for competition in the satellite communications market.


## HackerRank's ATS Scores Are All Over the Map


HackerRank open-sourced its applicant tracking system, and someone decided to[test how consistent the resume scoring actually is](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48713832) . The same resume scored 90/100, then 74, then 88 on subsequent runs.


This kind of variance in automated hiring tools should concern anyone building or relying on these systems. If a scoring algorithm can't produce consistent results for identical input, what exactly is it measuring? The[discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48713832) raises questions about whether ATS systems are filtering candidates based on signal or noise.


For content teams dealing with AI-assisted workflows, this is a useful reminder: automated scoring and evaluation tools need validation. Consistency matters as much as accuracy.


## Age Verification as a Precursor to Speech Attribution


A widely-discussed post argues that[age verification requirements are laying groundwork for broader identification of online speech](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48714529) . The logic: once infrastructure exists to verify age, it can verify identity for any purpose.


The[discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48714529) examines how these systems could evolve from protecting minors to attributing all online activity to real identities. For platforms and publishers, understanding this trajectory matters for long-term compliance and user trust decisions.


## Supreme Court Rules on Geofence Warrants


The US Supreme Court[ruled that geofence warrants require constitutional protections](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48720924) . These warrants request data on every device present in a geographic area during a time window, essentially casting a digital net.


The ruling establishes limits on how law enforcement can demand location data from technology companies. For developers building location-aware applications, this clarifies the legal landscape around user data requests.


## Tidal's AI Policy Takes a Stand


Tidal published an[AI policy](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48718840) that explicitly addresses how the music streaming service handles AI-generated content and training data. The[discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48718840) debates whether this approach adequately protects artists while acknowledging AI's role in content creation.


As content platforms define their AI policies, Tidal's approach offers one model for balancing creator rights with technological reality.


## Memory Manufacturers Face Price-Fixing Lawsuit


Samsung, SK Hynix, and Micron are being[sued in the US over alleged memory price fixing](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48718102) . The lawsuit claims coordinated pricing in the DRAM and NAND markets.


For anyone who has watched memory prices over the years, this is familiar territory. The three companies control the vast majority of global memory production, making competition dynamics particularly important.


## PlayStation Removes Purchased Movies Without Refund


Studio Canal movies purchased on PlayStation Store are being[removed without refunds](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48718967) . The[discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48718967) revisits the fundamental tension in digital ownership: when you buy something digitally, what do you actually own?


This affects content strategy broadly. If major platforms can revoke purchased content, it changes how consumers think about digital goods.


## Tools Worth Noting


**WATaBoy** demonstrates that[JIT-compiling Game Boy instructions to WebAssembly beats native interpreters](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48720190) . A useful case study in when compilation overhead pays off.


**Cursor for iOS** just launched, letting you[build from anywhere with a mobile coding interface](https://cursor.com/blog/ios-mobile-app) . The AI-assisted coding tool now travels with you.


**HamsterOS** crams a[complete graphical desktop onto a 1.44MB floppy disk](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48721031) . Sometimes constraints produce the most interesting engineering.
