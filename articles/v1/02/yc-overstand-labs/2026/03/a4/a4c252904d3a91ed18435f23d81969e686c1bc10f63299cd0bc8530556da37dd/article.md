# How to make DSAR Compliance simple

Frequently asked questions


A Data Subject Access Request (DSAR) is a formal request made by an individual asking a company to disclose all personal data it holds about them. This right exists under GDPR (Article 15) for EU residents and under CCPA for California residents. Companies must respond within 30 days under GDPR (extendable to 90 days in complex cases) and 45 days under CCPA. The response must be comprehensive — not a partial disclosure.


The Right to Be Forgotten (RTBF) — formally called the right to erasure under GDPR Article 17 — gives individuals the right to request that a company delete all personal data it holds about them. CCPA includes an equivalent deletion right. The obligation applies across every system where the data exists, not just primary databases. Companies must also notify any third parties to whom the data was disclosed.


Under GDPR, fines for failing to respond adequately to a DSAR can reach €20 million or 4% of annual global turnover — whichever is higher. Under CCPA, consumers have a private right of action for data breaches linked to inadequate data management, with statutory damages of $100–$750 per consumer per incident. Regulators assess both timeliness and completeness of responses.
