Unlike conventional AI systems that respond to discrete inputs, world models enable machines to build, maintain, and continuously update internal representations of their environments — allowing them to plan ahead, adapt in real time and act with greater autonomy and precision.


This panel brings together leading voices in spatial AI, embodied intelligence, and autonomous systems to examine how world modeling is moving from research labs to real-world deployment, and what that shift means for enterprises, developers and society.

#### **What Are World Models? A Primer for Enterprise AI Leaders**


[World models](https://www.telusdigital.com/insights/data-and-ai/article/technical-primer-world-models-ai) are AI architectures that learn to build internal simulations of their environments. Rather than mapping inputs directly to outputs, a world model allows an AI system to predict future states, reason about hypothetical scenarios, and plan sequences of actions — capabilities that are foundational to robotics, autonomous vehicles, and advanced AR/VR systems.


#### **Key capabilities enabled by world models include:**


- Spatial reasoning — understanding the geometry and structure of physical environments


- Temporal planning — anticipating how environments change over time and sequencing actions accordingly


- Simulation-based learning — using synthetic environments to safely train agents before real-world deployment


- Adaptive decision-making — updating internal maps in real time as new sensory information arrives


For enterprises, world models represent the next evolution beyond today's generative AI tools. As these systems mature, they will power autonomous agents capable of navigating complex, dynamic environments, from warehouse logistics and field service operations to AI-assisted customer interactions that adapt in real time to conversational context.
