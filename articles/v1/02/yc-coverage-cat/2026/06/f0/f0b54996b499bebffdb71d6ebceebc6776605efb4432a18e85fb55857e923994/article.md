## The Best Texas Homeowners Insurance Depends on Risk, Not Brand Name


No single company holds the title of best homeowners insurance in Texas. The right policy depends on where your home sits, what it costs to rebuild, how you prefer to handle deductibles, and whether you need wind coverage through a standard carrier or a state-backed program.


Texas operates one of the largest and most competitive[homeowners insurance](https://www.coveragecat.com/insurance-types/homeowners-insurance) markets in the country. Roughly 160 companies write policies in the state, and total annual written premium exceeds $19 billion[(Texas Department of Insurance, "Texas Homeowners Insurance Market Overview", 2026)](https://www.tdi.texas.gov/general/texas-homeowners-insurance-market-overview.html) . That competition sounds like good news for shoppers, but Texas also ranks among the most expensive states for home coverage because of hail, hurricanes, tornadoes, and repair-cost inflation[Ibid.](https://www.tdi.texas.gov/general/texas-homeowners-insurance-market-overview.html) .


When you compare homeowners insurance quotes in Texas, focus on five factors:


- **Policy fit** : Does the coverage match your home's reconstruction cost, roof type, and contents value?
- **Wind and hail deductibles** : A 2% deductible on a $400,000 home means $8,000 out of pocket for a hail claim.
- **Replacement cost vs. actual cash value** : Replacement cost pays to rebuild; actual cash value deducts depreciation.
- **Claims service reputation** : Financial strength and customer satisfaction scores from Consumer Reports, J.D. Power, or AM Best matter when you file a claim.
- **Financial stability** : AM Best ratings indicate whether a carrier can pay claims after a major storm season.

## Texas Coverage Issues That Change the Winner


### Wind and Hail Deductibles


Texas wind and hail insurance creates the most confusion for homeowners. Standard policies often apply a percentage-based deductible, typically 1% or 2% of dwelling coverage, instead of a flat dollar amount[(Texas Department of Insurance, "Deductibles", 2026)](https://www.tdi.texas.gov/tips/deductibles.html) . On a $500,000 home, a 2% wind/hail deductible means $10,000 out of pocket before coverage begins.


The Texas Department of Insurance requires insurers to offer policies with both percentage-based and dollar-amount deductibles, but the dollar-amount option usually costs more in premium[Ibid.](https://www.tdi.texas.gov/tips/deductibles.html) . When you compare quotes, ask each carrier for both options so you can see the trade-off.


### Roof Settlement Terms


How a policy handles roof damage matters more than almost any other coverage detail in Texas. Some policies pay replacement cost for roofs regardless of age. Others use an actual cash value formula that deducts depreciation, which means a 15-year-old roof might only receive a fraction of replacement cost after a hail storm[(Kin, "Texas Hail Damage Home Insurance", 2026)](https://www.kin.com/blog/texas-hail-damage-home-insurance/) .


Look for policies that specify "replacement cost" for roofing materials. If your insurer uses actual cash value or a "roof payment schedule" that reduces payouts based on roof age, factor that into your comparison.


### Replacement Cost vs. Actual Cash Value


A standard homeowners policy covered by HO-3 forms typically includes replacement cost coverage for the dwelling and actual cash value for personal property[(Insurance Information Institute, "What Is Covered by Standard Homeowners Insurance", 2026)](https://www.iii.org/article/what-covered-standard-homeowners-policy) . You can usually upgrade personal property to replacement cost through an endorsement.


The difference matters. If a burglar steals your five-year-old laptop, replacement cost coverage pays for a comparable new laptop. Actual cash value pays what a used five-year-old laptop would sell for, often far less.


### Water Backup and Foundation Coverage


Standard Texas homeowners policies exclude flood and typically exclude sewer/drain backup unless you add an endorsement[(OPIC, "Homeowners Insurance", 2026)](https://www.opic.texas.gov/resources/homeowners-insurance/) . Foundation coverage varies by carrier; some policies exclude foundation movement caused by soil conditions, while others offer endorsements for foundation repair.


The Texas Office of Public Insurance Counsel publishes a policy comparison tool that shows which carriers include or exclude foundation coverage[(OPIC, "Policy Comparison Tool", 2026)](https://www.opic.texas.gov/resources/policy-comparison-tool/) . Review it before you finalize your shortlist.


### Coastal Texas and TWIA


Homeowners in the 14 coastal counties and parts of Harris County may struggle to find wind and hail coverage from standard carriers. The Texas Windstorm Insurance Association (TWIA) exists as a residual market for properties that cannot obtain coverage elsewhere[(TWIA, "About Us", 2026)](https://www.twia.org/about-us/) .


TWIA covers wind and hail only. You still need a separate policy for fire, theft, liability, and other perils. TWIA rates increased in recent years to reflect rising reinsurance costs[(TWIA, "Rates", 2026)](https://www.twia.org/rates/) . If your home qualifies for coverage from a private carrier, that option often provides more seamless protection because wind/hail coverage bundles with your main policy.


For homeowners who cannot find private coverage, the[Texas FAIR Plan](https://www.coveragecat.com/reviews/texas-fair-plan) offers fire and extended coverage as another residual option, while[TWIA](https://www.coveragecat.com/reviews/texas-windstorm-insurance-association-twia) handles wind/hail for coastal properties.

### Flood Insurance


Standard homeowners policies do not cover flood damage[(FEMA, "FloodSmart", 2026)](https://www.floodsmart.gov/) . Texas homeowners in flood-prone areas, and increasingly those in areas without prior flood history, should consider coverage through the National Flood Insurance Program or a private flood carrier.


FEMA's FloodSmart program provides federally backed flood policies. Private flood insurers sometimes offer higher coverage limits or lower premiums depending on your location and elevation certificate[Ibid.](https://www.floodsmart.gov/) .


Billion-dollar weather disasters have increased in frequency, with 28 separate events in the United States in 2023 alone[(NOAA NCEI, "Billion-Dollar Weather and Climate Disasters", 2026)](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/access/billions/) . Texas experiences more of these events than most states because of its size and climate exposure.


## High-Value Homes, Umbrella Coverage, and Liability


Homeowners with high-value properties face different calculations. Standard policies max out at certain coverage limits, and liability exposure increases with home value, property features, and net worth.


### Why High-Value Homeowners Should Pair Coverage Reviews


A $2 million home with a pool, a trampoline, and a teenage driver creates liability exposure that extends beyond a standard homeowners policy. If a guest suffers a serious injury at your home or your teen causes a major car accident, a lawsuit can exceed your home and auto liability limits quickly.


[High-value homeowners insurance](https://www.coveragecat.com/blog/guide-to-high-value-home-insurance) from carriers like Chubb, AIG Private Client, or PURE typically offers extended replacement cost (often 125% or more of dwelling coverage), higher liability limits, and coverage for valuables like art, jewelry, and wine collections[(Chubb, "Homeowners Insurance", 2026)](https://www.chubb.com/us-en/individuals-families/products/home/homeowners-insurance.html) .


### Umbrella Insurance in Texas


[Umbrella insurance](https://www.coveragecat.com/insurance-types/umbrella-insurance) adds liability protection above your home and auto policies. A $1 million umbrella policy might cost $150 to $300 per year, depending on your risk profile[(Progressive, "Umbrella Insurance Cost", 2026)](https://www.progressive.com/answers/umbrella-insurance-cost/) .


Before an umbrella carrier writes your policy, they require minimum underlying liability limits on your home and auto coverage. Most carriers require at least $300,000 in home liability and $250,000/$500,000 in auto liability[(Mercury Insurance, "Texas Umbrella Insurance", 2026)](https://www.mercuryinsurance.com/local/texas/umbrella-insurance/) .


Texas households with these exposures should consider umbrella coverage:


- Net worth exceeding $500,000
- Swimming pool, trampoline, or attractive nuisance on property
- Teenage drivers
- Rental properties
- Frequent guests or entertaining
- Dog breeds with higher bite-claim frequency


The[best standalone umbrella carriers](https://www.coveragecat.com/blog/umbrella-liability-insurance-comparison-guide) can write policies that sit above multiple underlying carriers, which gives you flexibility if you switch home or auto insurers later.


### Personal Liability Coverage Basics


Standard homeowners liability coverage (Coverage E) pays for bodily injury or property damage you cause to others, plus legal defense costs[(Travelers, "Personal Liability", 2026)](https://www.travelers.com/home-insurance/coverage/personal-liability) . It also covers incidents that happen away from your home, like accidentally injuring someone on a golf course.


Most standard policies start at $100,000 in liability. Texas homeowners with assets worth protecting should increase this limit to $300,000 or $500,000, which costs relatively little in additional premium, and then add an umbrella policy for catastrophic protection[(Progressive, "Personal Liability Insurance", 2026)](https://www.progressive.com/answers/personal-liability-insurance/) .

### How much does homeowners insurance cost in Texas?


Texas homeowners pay more than the national average because of wind, hail, and storm exposure. Premiums vary widely based on location, home value, roof age, and deductible choices. ### How do wind and hail deductibles work in Texas?


Most Texas homeowners policies apply a percentage-based deductible, often 1% or 2% of dwelling coverage, for wind and hail claims[(TDI, "Deductibles", 2026)](https://www.tdi.texas.gov/tips/deductibles.html) . On a $400,000 home, a 2% deductible means $8,000 out of pocket before coverage begins. Texas law requires insurers to offer dollar-amount deductibles as an alternative, though the premium for that option is usually higher.


### Does homeowners insurance cover flood damage in Texas?


No. Standard homeowners policies exclude flood damage. Texas homeowners need a separate flood policy through the National Flood Insurance Program or a private flood carrier[(FEMA, "FloodSmart", 2026)](https://www.floodsmart.gov/) . Given Texas's exposure to hurricanes, tropical storms, and inland flooding, flood coverage matters for many properties beyond designated flood zones.


### Do I need umbrella insurance in Texas?


Umbrella insurance makes sense if your assets exceed your home and auto liability limits. Texas homeowners with pools, trampolines, teenage drivers, or significant net worth should evaluate umbrella coverage. A $1 million umbrella policy typically costs $150 to $300 per year[(Progressive, "Umbrella Insurance Cost", 2026)](https://www.progressive.com/answers/umbrella-insurance-cost/) .


### How do I compare homeowners insurance quotes in Texas?


Normalize coverage across quotes by matching dwelling limits, deductible types, and endorsements. Use the OPIC policy comparison tool to understand which carriers include or exclude specific coverages[(OPIC, "Policy Comparison Tool", 2026)](https://www.opic.texas.gov/resources/policy-comparison-tool/) . Pay attention to wind/hail deductibles, roof settlement terms, and whether personal property coverage uses replacement cost or actual cash value.
