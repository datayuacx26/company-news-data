## What does homeowners insurance cover (and what it usually excludes)?


Most Fresno homeowners buy an HO-3 policy, which insurers call a "special form." This is the standard policy type for owner-occupied single-family homes. Here is what it protects and what falls outside its scope.


### Coverage components


**Dwelling coverage (Coverage A)** pays to repair or rebuild your home's structure after a covered loss. This includes walls, roof, flooring, built-in appliances, and attached structures like a garage.


**Other structures (Coverage B)** covers detached buildings on your property: a shed, detached garage, pool house, or fence. Most policies set this at 10% of your dwelling limit.


**Personal property (Coverage C)** protects your belongings, from furniture to electronics to clothing. HO-3 policies cover personal property on a "named perils" basis, which means the policy lists specific events it covers, such as fire, theft, or windstorm.


**Loss of use (Coverage D)** reimburses additional living expenses if you cannot stay in your home during repairs. This can cover hotel bills, meals, and temporary rent.


**Personal liability (Coverage E)** protects you if someone is injured on your property or if you cause damage to someone else's property. It also covers legal defense costs.


**Medical payments (Coverage F)** pays small medical bills for guests injured on your property, regardless of fault. Limits are usually $1,000 to $5,000.


### Common exclusions


Standard HO-3 policies exclude several perils that Fresno homeowners should understand:


Exclusion Why it matters for Fresno

Certain wildfire/smoke limitations Some forms exclude smoke damage from nearby fires if your home was not directly affected


For earthquake coverage, Fresno homeowners can purchase a separate policy through the California Earthquake Authority or private insurers. Flood insurance is available through the National Flood Insurance Program or private markets. ## Why California pricing and availability can be volatile (and what Fresno homeowners can do)


California's homeowners insurance market has shifted fast. Major carriers have paused new business, non-renewals have spiked, and the California FAIR Plan, the state's insurer of last resort, has seen applications surge.


### The carrier pullback


Several national insurers have reduced their California exposure since 2023. State Farm, Allstate, and Farmers have all limited new policies in high-risk areas or statewide. The reasons: catastrophic wildfire losses, rising reinsurance costs, and regulatory limits on how quickly insurers can raise rates.


Insurance industry analysts expect this volatility to continue through 2026. According to Deloitte's 2026 insurance outlook, property insurers are reassessing their portfolios in catastrophe-prone states, and California remains a focal point[(Deloitte, "2025 insurance industry outlook", 2025)](https://www.deloitte.com/us/en/insights/industry/financial-services/financial-services-industry-outlooks/insurance-industry-outlook.html) .


For a current list of[companies still insuring homes in California](https://www.coveragecat.com/blog/companies-still-insuring-homes-in-california) , see our carrier guide.


### The FAIR Plan


When admitted carriers decline coverage, homeowners can apply to the California FAIR Plan. This is not a full homeowners policy. It covers fire and certain other perils, but you need a separate "difference in conditions" policy for liability, theft, and other standard protections.


FAIR Plan premiums are often higher than the admitted market, and coverage limits cap at $3 million for dwelling. Fresno homeowners in foothill areas or near wildland-urban interfaces are more likely to need this fallback.


### What Fresno homeowners can do


You cannot control the market, but you can improve your risk profile. Insurers reward homes that are easier to defend and less likely to generate claims.


**Defensible space:** Clear vegetation within 100 feet of your home. The California Department of Forestry and Fire Protection requires this in many areas, and insurers often ask about it during underwriting.


**Roof condition:** A newer roof in good condition can lower premiums. Wood-shake shingles, by contrast, can make coverage hard to find in fire-prone zones. Class A fire-rated roofing materials are preferred.


**Home hardening:** Ember-resistant vents, tempered glass windows, and non-combustible siding can reduce wildfire risk. Some insurers offer credits for Firewise USA certification or completion of home hardening checklists.


**Document upgrades:** Keep permits and contractor invoices for electrical, plumbing, HVAC, and roofing work. Insurers want to know when systems were last updated, and documentation supports higher rebuild cost estimates.


These steps matter for[regional availability of California home insurance](https://www.coveragecat.com/insurance-types/regional-availability-of-california-home-insurance) . A well-maintained, hardened home is more attractive to carriers who remain active in the state.


## How to choose limits and endorsements so you are not underinsured


Underinsurance is one of the most common mistakes homeowners make. Your policy's dwelling limit should reflect what it costs to rebuild your home, not what you paid for it or what Zillow says it is worth.


### Rebuild cost versus market value


Market value includes land, location, and neighborhood. Rebuild cost is only the labor, materials, and permits needed to reconstruct your home to current building codes.


In Fresno, rebuild costs have risen due to material price increases and labor shortages. A home purchased for $400,000 might cost $500,000 or more to rebuild, especially if updated codes require upgraded electrical, plumbing, or energy efficiency features.


Use a rebuild cost calculator or ask your insurer for a replacement cost estimate. Do not simply insure to market value.


### Endorsements worth considering


Standard HO-3 policies leave gaps that endorsements can fill. For a full breakdown, see[which homeowners endorsements are worth the money](https://www.coveragecat.com/insurance-types/which-homeowners-endorsements-are-worth-the-money) .


**Extended replacement cost:** This endorsement pays 25% to 50% above your dwelling limit if rebuild costs exceed your policy limit. After a regional disaster, labor and material costs spike because demand exceeds supply. Extended replacement cost protects against that surge.


**Ordinance or law coverage:** If your home is destroyed, you may need to rebuild to current codes. Standard policies do not always cover the cost of code upgrades. Ordinance or law coverage fills that gap.


**Water backup:** Standard policies exclude sewer and drain backup. This endorsement covers water damage from backed-up sewers, drains, or sump pumps. It is inexpensive and addresses a common claims scenario.


**Higher personal liability limits:** Most policies start at $100,000 in liability coverage. That may not be enough if you face a serious lawsuit. Consider raising your limit to $300,000 or $500,000, or adding an umbrella policy for broader protection.


**Scheduled personal property:** If you own jewelry, art, musical instruments, or other high-value items, standard personal property limits may not cover them fully. Scheduling these items provides higher limits and often removes the deductible.


## How to shop for homeowners insurance in Fresno


The right approach depends on your property and risk profile. Some homeowners can shop directly with carriers. Others benefit from working with a broker who specializes in California's harder-to-place risks.


### Step-by-step quote checklist


Before you contact insurers or brokers, gather these details:


1. **Property address and legal description** for accurate risk scoring
2. **Year built** and any major renovations with dates
3. **Square footage** and number of stories
4. **Roof type, age, and condition** , including any recent inspections
5. **Construction materials** for walls, siding, and foundation
6. **Heating and cooling systems** , including fuel type and age
7. **Electrical panel age and amperage**
8. **Plumbing type** (copper, PEX, galvanized, etc.)
9. **Claims history** for the past five years
10. **Current policy declarations page** if you have existing coverage


When you request quotes, provide the same information to each insurer so you can compare apples to apples.


### When to use a specialist broker


If you receive non-renewals, declinations, or quotes above $5,000, consider working with an independent broker who writes California homeowners business. These brokers have appointments with specialty carriers and excess and surplus lines insurers that do not accept direct applications.


Coverage Cat's quote flow often surfaces specialty carriers such as Lightspeed, Delos, Bamboo, Homesite, and Obie. These carriers focus on California risks that larger admitted insurers have exited.


For more on carrier options, see our ranking of the[best California homeowners insurance carriers](https://www.coveragecat.com/blog/best-california-homeowners-insurance-top-carriers-ranked) .


### Compare policy forms, not just price


A $1,500 policy with a $5,000 deductible, no extended replacement cost, and actual cash value on the roof is not comparable to a $2,000 policy with a $1,000 deductible, 25% extended replacement cost, and replacement cost on all components.


When you compare quotes, check:


- Dwelling limit and whether extended replacement cost is included
- Deductible for all-perils and any separate wind/hail or wildfire deductibles
- Personal property valuation: replacement cost versus actual cash value
- Exclusions and limitations specific to California wildfire or smoke damage
- Endorsements included versus available for additional premium


The cheapest policy is not always the best fit. A slightly higher premium with better terms can save tens of thousands of dollars after a loss.

## FAQs About Homeowners Insurance in Fresno, CA


### How much dwelling coverage do I need in Fresno?


Dwelling coverage should track rebuild cost, not the home's market value. Use a replacement-cost estimator and update it after renovations or major price changes in labor and materials.


### What is an HO-3 policy and why is it common?


HO-3 is the most common homeowners policy form. It typically covers the dwelling on an open-perils basis (covered unless excluded) while personal property is usually named-perils.


### What does homeowners insurance usually not cover?


Flooding and earthquakes usually require separate policies. Wear and tear, maintenance issues, and many earth-movement losses are also commonly excluded.


### When should I consider an umbrella policy?


If you have meaningful assets or higher liability exposure, an umbrella policy can add extra protection above your home and auto liability limits.


### How do I shop for coverage if my home is in a wildfire-risk area?


Start early, be ready to provide detailed property information, and compare private-market options against FAIR Plan plus a companion policy when necessary.
