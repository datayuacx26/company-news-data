## What does homeowners insurance cover (and what it usually excludes)?


A standard HO-3 policy, the most common form for owner-occupied single-family homes, covers six main areas:


**Dwelling coverage (Coverage A)** pays to repair or rebuild your home's structure after covered damage. This includes walls, roof, built-in appliances, and attached structures like a garage.


**Other structures coverage (Coverage B)** protects detached buildings on your property. Think fences, sheds, and detached garages. Most policies set this at 10% of your dwelling limit.


**Personal property coverage (Coverage C)** replaces belongings damaged or stolen from your home or, in many cases, anywhere in the world. Standard policies cap this at 50-70% of your dwelling limit, with sublimits on categories like jewelry, electronics, and firearms.


**Loss of use coverage (Coverage D)** pays additional living expenses if you cannot occupy your home after a covered loss. This covers hotel stays, restaurant meals above your normal food costs, and other expenses you incur while displaced.


**Personal liability coverage (Coverage E)** protects you if someone sues for bodily injury or property damage you caused. Standard limits start at $100,000, but most homeowners should carry $300,000 or more.


**Medical payments coverage (Coverage F)** pays small medical bills for guests injured on your property, regardless of fault. Typical limits run $1,000-$5,000.

### What San Jose homeowners can do


San Jose's[regional availability of California home insurance](https://www.coveragecat.com/insurance-types/regional-availability-of-california-home-insurance) remains better than many inland or foothill communities, but competition has thinned. Practical steps to improve your insurability:


**Create defensible space.** CAL FIRE requires 100 feet of defensible space around structures in State Responsibility Areas. Even if you live in a lower-risk urban zone, maintaining clearance helps your application.


**Document roof condition.** Roof age drives premium calculations more than almost any other factor. If you replaced your roof recently, provide the permit, inspection report, and contractor documentation to every carrier you quote.


**Harden your home.** Ember-resistant vents, Class A roof materials, and enclosed eaves reduce wildfire vulnerability. The Institute for Business and Home Safety maintains Fortified Home standards that some carriers reward with discounts[(IBHS, "FORTIFIED Home", 2026)](https://ibhs.org/fortified/) .


**Keep upgrade records.** Electrical panel upgrades, plumbing replacements, and HVAC installations affect both insurability and rebuild cost estimates. Maintain a file with permits, invoices, and photos.


**Consider the FAIR Plan as a backstop.** The California FAIR Plan provides basic fire coverage when no admitted carrier will write a policy. It covers less than a standard HO-3 and costs more per dollar of coverage, but it keeps mortgage requirements satisfied while you search for alternatives. Homeowners in[high-risk wildfire zones](https://www.coveragecat.com/insurance-types/finding-insurance-in-high-risk-wildfire-zones) may need to layer FAIR Plan coverage with a Difference in Conditions (DIC) policy to approximate standard HO-3 protection.


## How to choose limits and endorsements so you are not underinsured


Underinsurance remains the most common mistake California homeowners make. Market value measures what a buyer would pay for your property. Replacement cost measures what it costs to rebuild your structure after a total loss, often 20-40% higher than market value because it excludes land and includes demolition, debris removal, and current material costs.


### Set dwelling coverage to replacement cost, not purchase price


San Jose's median home sale price hovers around $1.4-$1.5 million[(Realtor.com, "San Jose Housing Market", 2026)](https://www.realtor.com/realestateandhomes-search/San-Jose_CA/overview) , but land accounts for a substantial portion of that value. A home purchased for $1.5 million might need $600,000-$900,000 in dwelling coverage depending on square footage, construction type, and finishes.


Request a replacement cost estimate from your insurer or use a third-party calculator. Several carriers use Marshall & Swift valuation tools that estimate rebuild costs based on local labor rates and material costs.


### Endorsements worth considering


Standard HO-3 policies leave gaps that endorsements can fill.[Understanding which homeowners endorsements are worth the money](https://www.coveragecat.com/insurance-types/which-homeowners-endorsements-are-worth-the-money) helps you allocate premium dollars effectively.


**Extended replacement cost** increases your dwelling limit by 25-50% if rebuild costs exceed your stated coverage. California's post-disaster construction demand often inflates labor and material costs beyond normal estimates.


**Ordinance or law coverage** pays for code upgrades required when you rebuild. A 1970s home rebuilt today must meet current seismic, electrical, and energy codes, which can add 15-30% to rebuild costs.


**Water backup coverage** pays for damage from sewer or drain backups. Standard policies exclude this, but the endorsement typically costs $30-$75 annually.


**Higher personal liability limits** protect your assets. California's tech wealth concentration means many San Jose homeowners have more to lose in a lawsuit than $100,000 will protect. Increase to $300,000 or $500,000, or add an umbrella policy for broader coverage.


**Scheduled personal property** removes sublimits on high-value items like jewelry, art, or collectibles. If you own items worth more than standard sublimits (often $1,500-$2,500 for jewelry), schedule them individually with appraisals.

## How to shop for homeowners insurance in San Jose


Shopping for homeowners insurance in San Jose requires more effort than in states with stable markets. Fewer carriers write new policies, and those that do vary widely in price and coverage terms.


### Quote checklist


Before you request quotes, gather the following:


- Property address and year built
- Square footage and number of stories
- Construction type (frame, masonry, stucco)
- Roof type, age, and last inspection date
- Heating and cooling system types and ages
- Electrical panel type and amperage
- Security system and smoke detector documentation
- Prior claims history (past five years)
- Current policy declarations page (if switching carriers)


### What to tell insurers and brokers


Be specific about upgrades. A carrier that knows you replaced your roof in 2023 will quote differently than one that assumes your original 1978 shingles remain in place.


Disclose claims accurately. Carriers check your CLUE (Comprehensive Loss Underwriting Exchange) report, which lists claims filed at your property regardless of who owned it. Unexplained omissions raise flags.


Ask about discounts. Multi-policy bundling, alarm systems, new roofs, and claims-free histories all reduce premiums. Some carriers offer specific discounts for Fortified Home certification or wildfire mitigation completion.


### When to use a specialist broker


Standard agents who sell for one carrier cannot compare options across the market. Independent agents represent multiple carriers but may lack access to the surplus lines and specialty markets where California homeowners increasingly find coverage.


[Best California homeowners insurance carriers](https://www.coveragecat.com/blog/best-california-homeowners-insurance-top-carriers-ranked) include both admitted carriers (regulated by the California Department of Insurance with guaranty fund protection) and surplus lines carriers (more flexible underwriting but no guaranty fund). A broker with surplus lines access can often find coverage when standard carriers decline.


Coverage Cat's current California quote flow often surfaces specialty carriers including Lightspeed, Delos, Bamboo, Homesite, and Obie. These carriers focus on specific risk profiles or geographic areas where national carriers have retreated.


### Compare policy forms, not just price


Price comparisons matter, but policy form differences matter more. Two policies with identical premiums might offer different coverage:


- **Replacement cost vs. actual cash value** for personal property affects claim payouts significantly. Replacement cost pays to replace items at current prices. Actual cash value deducts depreciation.
- **Named peril vs. open peril** for personal property changes what you can claim. HO-3 policies typically cover the dwelling on an open-peril basis (everything not excluded) but personal property on a named-peril basis (only listed perils).
- **Sublimits and special limits** cap payouts for categories like business property, watercraft, and electronics. Compare these if you have valuable items in affected categories.
- **Deductible structure** varies by carrier. Some apply separate wind or wildfire deductibles in addition to the base deductible.


Request sample policy forms before binding coverage. Read the exclusions section and the definitions of key terms like "sudden and accidental" and "dwelling."

## FAQs About Homeowners Insurance in San Jose, CA


### How much dwelling coverage do I need in San Jose?


Dwelling coverage should track rebuild cost, not the home's market value. Use a replacement-cost estimator and update it after renovations or major price changes in labor and materials.


### What is an HO-3 policy and why is it common?


HO-3 is the most common homeowners policy form. It typically covers the dwelling on an open-perils basis (covered unless excluded) while personal property is usually named-perils.


### What does homeowners insurance usually not cover?


Flooding and earthquakes usually require separate policies. Wear and tear, maintenance issues, and many earth-movement losses are also commonly excluded.


### When should I consider an umbrella policy?


If you have meaningful assets or higher liability exposure, an umbrella policy can add extra protection above your home and auto liability limits.


### How do I shop for coverage if my home is in a wildfire-risk area?


Start early, be ready to provide detailed property information, and compare private-market options against FAIR Plan plus a companion policy when necessary.
