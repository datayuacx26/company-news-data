# Apple Developer Program: Free vs. Paid Explained

If you want to publish an app on the App Store, you need an Apple Developer Program membership. The membership costs $99 per year in the U.S, but you don’t have to pay to start building your apps.


There’s some nuance here, so let’s break down exactly what you get for free, what the paid membership unlocks, and when you should upgrade.


## What can you do with a free Apple developer account?


### Access to tools


A free account lets you download Xcode and start building apps. If you’re not familiar with[Xcode](https://developer.apple.com/xcode/) , it’s Apple’s Mac app for building software for Apple platforms.


You can also use AI to help you build your app in[Bitrig](https://bitrig.com/) . It has the full Xcode toolchain running locally and specializes in Swift, SwiftUI, and Apple frameworks.


So you can get started learning and building your app with the free developer account, but when it’s time to publish on the App Store, you’ll need to join the paid membership.


### Test on your own devices


You can install and run an app on your own Apple devices without paying for the Apple Developer Program. Apple calls this a **Personal Team** in Xcode. It’s useful when you’re learning or working on an early prototype, but it comes with some limitations:


-


You can register up to 10 App IDs at a time.


-


You can register up to three test devices per platform.


-


App IDs, device registrations, and provisioning profiles expire after seven days.


-


You may need to rebuild and reinstall your app after the provisioning profile expires.


That seven-day expiration can be a little annoying, but it’s perfectly workable when you’re learning or testing an idea. What you can’t do with the free account is distribute your beta through TestFlight to friends, family, customers, or other outside testers.


### Beta software access


One of the cooler benefits of a free developer account is access to beta versions of Apple’s software.


Every June, Apple hosts WWDC, the Worldwide Developers Conference. This is where Apple introduces the next major versions of iOS, iPadOS, macOS, watchOS, etc… and all the fun new features.


Developers can use these beta releases to test their apps and start adopting new features before the public software launches (usually in mid-September of that year)

Apple Developer Program?


It includes everything in the free account and what you need to distribute and manage a real app on the App Store.


### App Store Publishing


This is the primary reason to pay for the membership. Membership gives you access to the certificates, identifiers, profiles, and distribution tools needed to publish your app on the App Store.

### Advanced app capabilities


Certain technologies are only available for the paid membership. If you’re on the free developer account, you can’t build with the following:


-


Push notifications


-


In-app purchases and subscriptions


-


Sign in with Apple


-


CloudKit


-

-


Associated domains


-


Certain keychain and app-service capabilities


If your app will use one of these technologies, then you’ll need to pay for the membership well before you submit to the App Store.


### Beta test with TestFlight


It’s best to test your app with real users before you submit it to the App Store. The paid membership gives you access to TestFlight, which is Apple’s way of sending your app out to beta testers.


With TestFlight, you can invite people by email or create a public link that you can share on social media, with your email list, or inside a community. Apple currently allows up to 10,000 external testers per app, along with up to 100 internal testers from your App Store Connect team. Testers install Apple’s TestFlight app, download your beta, and can send feedback or report crashes.


### App Store Connect


Paid members also get access to[App Store Connect](https://developer.apple.com/app-store-connect/) , where you manage your app’s presence on the App Store.


This is where you can:


-


Create and edit your App Store product page


-


Upload screenshots and app previews


-


Set pricing and availability


-


Manage in-app purchases and subscriptions


-


Upload and distribute TestFlight builds


-


Submit your app for review


-


View downloads, sales, revenue, retention, and conversion data

##
Should you enroll as an individual
or an organization?


When you join the paid program, Apple lets you enroll as either an individual or an organization. Both options have the same standard $99 annual membership fee, but there are some important differences.


### Individual enrollment


An individual account is usually the right choice if:


-


You’re building apps by yourself.


-


You’re a sole proprietor or single-person business.


-


You don’t need to manage a larger development team.


-


You’re comfortable having your personal legal name shown as the seller on the App Store.


To enroll, you’ll need an Apple Account with two-factor authentication enabled, and you must be the legal age of majority in your region.


One detail people sometimes miss is that an app published through an individual account is listed under the developer’s personal legal name—not a studio name, nickname, or brand name.


### Organization enrollment


An organization account is generally the better choice if you’re publishing for an LLC, corporation, startup, nonprofit, or other legal entity.


Your organization’s legal entity name will appear as the seller on the App Store. You can also invite additional team members and assign roles such as Admin, Developer, App Manager, Designer, and Marketing.


To enroll as an organization, Apple requires:


-


A recognized legal entity


-


The authority to enter into legal agreements for that entity


-


A work email address associated with the organization’s domain


-


A publicly available, functional website


-


A D‑U‑N‑S Number registered to the legal entity


Apple does not accept a DBA, trade name, fictitious business name, or branch as the legal entity for organization enrollment.


### What is a D-U-N-S Number?


A D‑U‑N‑S Number is a unique nine-digit identifier assigned to a business by Dun & Bradstreet. Apple uses it to verify an organization’s identity and legal status. You don’t need one when enrolling as an individual.


Before requesting a new number, use[Apple’s D‑U‑N‑S lookup tool](https://developer.apple.com/help/account/membership/D-U-N-S/) to see if your company already has one.


If you do need to request it, Apple says to allow up to five business days to receive the number from Dun & Bradstreet. It can then take up to two additional business days for Apple to receive the updated information.


Give yourself at least a week of lead time—and preferably a little more—if you plan to enroll as an organization.


## When should you pay for the Apple Developer Program?


You probably don’t need to pay on day one if you’re still learning Swift, experimenting with an idea, or building an early prototype.


You should consider enrolling when:


-


You’re preparing to publish on the App Store.


-


You want to beta test through TestFlight.


-


You need advanced capabilities such as push notifications or in-app purchases.


-


You need to add team members to the account.


-


You want to configure your App Store listing or distribution workflow.


If you’re just tinkering, stay on the free account until you have a reason to upgrade.


## Frequently asked questions


### Can I build an iOS app without paying Apple?


Yes. You can download Xcode, learn Swift, build an app, and test it on your own devices with a free Apple developer account.


### Do I need the Apple Developer Program to publish an app?


Yes. You need a paid Apple Developer Program membership to distribute an app through the App Store.


### Do I need a paid membership to use TestFlight?


Yes. TestFlight distribution is included with the paid Apple Developer Program and is managed through App Store Connect.


### Is the Apple Developer Program fee monthly or yearly?


The standard fee is $99 per membership year in the United States, or the local equivalent where available.


### Should I enroll as an individual or an organization?


Enroll as an individual if you’re publishing by yourself and are comfortable using your personal legal name as the App Store seller. Enroll as an organization if you’re publishing for a recognized legal entity, want the company’s legal name displayed, or need to manage a team.


## The bottom line


If you want to publish your app on the App Store, beta test with TestFlight, use advanced Apple capabilities, or manage your app through App Store Connect, you’re going to need the paid Apple Developer Program.


If you just want to build, learn, prototype, and tinker with an app on your own devices, the free account should be enough for now.
