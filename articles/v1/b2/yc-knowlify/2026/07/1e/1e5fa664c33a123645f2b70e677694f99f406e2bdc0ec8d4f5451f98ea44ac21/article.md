# What an Unlimited AI Video Plan Should Include

## Quality should not fall behind the unlimited label


Some plans reserve premium models, high-resolution output, faster rendering, expressive voices or specialised avatars for add-ons. Build a “required output” list before comparing subscriptions:


- visual style appropriate to the audience;
- intelligible voice and pronunciation controls;
- brand consistency;
- accurate captions;
- output resolution;
- language coverage;
- factual-review workflow;
- reliable rendering of your typical video length.


Then ask which requirements are included and which are separately metered. If the standard model cannot produce acceptable work, its unlimited allowance has little value.


## Accessibility belongs in the acceptance criteria


For web-delivered prerecorded video with audio, WCAG 2.2 Success Criterion 1.2.2 requires captions except in the stated media-alternative case. W3C explains that captions include dialogue and meaningful non-speech information, not merely a rough transcript.


An unlimited subscription should make caption production and correction operationally manageable. Test:


- timing and speaker identification;
- proper nouns and technical vocabulary;
- meaningful sound cues;
- export as a supported caption file;
- keyboard and player accessibility where the vendor hosts the video.


Automatic captions still require human review. “AI-generated” is not an accessibility acceptance test.


## Security, privacy and governance do not become unlimited


Ask what the service does with uploaded documents, prompts, generated media and user activity. Your review may need:


- data locations and subprocessors;
- retention and deletion controls;
- whether customer content trains models;
- encryption and access controls;
- single sign-on and role-based permissions;
- audit logs;
- incident notification terms;
- data-processing terms;
- controls over public sharing.


The appropriate depth depends on the content and risk. NIST’s voluntary AI Risk Management Framework recommends incorporating trustworthiness into the design, use and evaluation of AI systems. Its Generative AI Profile highlights governance, content provenance, pre-deployment testing and incident disclosure. Use these as prompts for evaluation, not as a claim that buying a subscription creates compliance.


## Support must match production importance


“Support included” might mean community access or a contractual response target. Ask which channels, hours and response expectations apply. For time-sensitive training, establish:


- severity definitions;
- initial response targets;
- escalation route;
- status-page and incident communication;
- onboarding and administrator training;
- help with failed renders or pronunciation issues;
- service availability commitments, if any;
- account and data export assistance on exit.


Do not demand enterprise support if the workflow is low risk. Match the control to the consequence of delay.


## Unlimited-plan scorecard


Score each category from 0 to 2:


- **0, Unclear or unsuitable:** no written definition, or the limit blocks expected use.
- **1, Conditional:** workable with an add-on, process change or accepted risk.
- **2, Included and verified:** documented and demonstrated with your workload.


Apply the score to these ten categories:


1. Standard generation
2. Revisions and rerenders
3. Creator and render concurrency
4. Required quality features
5. Exports and portability
6. Rights and licensing
7. Accessibility workflow
8. Security and privacy
9. Administration and usage visibility
10. Support and exit


A high total is useful only if no must-have category scores zero. Record evidence beside every score: contract clause, product documentation or pilot result.


## Example: two “unlimited” plans


Team A produces 40 short SOP videos monthly with three creators. Plan X permits unlimited standard renders but processes one job at a time and charges separately for required caption-file exports. Plan Y costs more, permits three concurrent renders and includes captions and version history.


Plan X may still suit a solo creator producing evergreen content. For Team A, Plan Y may reduce queue delays and manual caption work enough to provide lower whole-life cost. The correct decision follows the workload, not the adjective.

## FAQ


### Does unlimited mean there should be no fair-use policy?


No. A service can control abuse and automated resale. The policy should define ordinary permitted use, triggers and remedies clearly enough for capacity planning.


### Should unlimited plans include unlimited seats?


Not necessarily. Seats and generated output are separate cost drivers. Require enough seats and permissions for your workflow, and include extra-seat costs in the comparison.


### Are translations normally included?


There is no universal convention. Translation, dubbing, voice cloning and extra language renders may be included, limited or separately metered. Verify each one.


### What happens to videos after cancellation?


Downloaded files may continue to work while hosted embeds or project access may not. Confirm commercial rights, download windows and project portability in writing.


### How do we validate an unlimited claim?


Run a bounded pilot at realistic concurrency and revision volume. Record queue time, excluded features, support outcomes and approved output, not just the number of drafts.
