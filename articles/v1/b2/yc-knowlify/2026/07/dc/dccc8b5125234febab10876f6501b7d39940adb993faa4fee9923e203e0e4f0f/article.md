# Video for the Knowledge Element of Apprenticeship Standards: KSBs Explained

## A five-part decision framework


Use **SCOPE** before commissioning an apprenticeship knowledge video.


### S, Standard


Which occupational standard, version, knowledge statements and assessment outcomes apply? If these cannot be named, the brief is not ready.


### C, Content fit


Will motion, narration, demonstration or a scenario teach this better than text, a diagram or supervised practice? Select the simplest effective format.


### O, Outcome


What should the apprentice be able to explain, distinguish, calculate or decide after the video? “Understand K4” is not precise enough. “Explain three controls and choose an appropriate control for a given risk” is testable.


### P, Practice and proof


What activity follows the explanation? Decide separately how learning and occupational competence will be checked. A quiz may check recall or application; a workplace observation may be needed for performance.


### E, Expert review and expiry


Who is qualified to verify technical accuracy and apprenticeship alignment? What event forces a review: a revised standard, assessment-plan change, policy update, product change or fixed review date?


## Production checklist for KSB-aligned videos


### Before scripting


- Confirm the standard reference, version and learner cohort.
- Name the knowledge statements and assessment outcomes.
- Set one primary learning objective per short asset or coherent module.
- Identify prerequisite knowledge.
- Link every legal or policy claim to a current primary source.
- Choose an appropriately qualified subject-matter reviewer.
- Decide what will be assessed outside the video.


### During production


- Explain specialist terms the first time they appear.
- Show the reasoning behind decisions, not only the final answer.
- Use realistic examples without exposing customer or apprentice data.
- Provide captions and a transcript; check both rather than relying on raw automated output.
- Avoid decorative visuals that conflict with the narration.
- Display version-sensitive information with a “checked on” date.
- Keep knowledge, demonstration and competence claims distinct.


### Before release


- Obtain technical and curriculum approval.
- Test knowledge questions against the stated outcome.
- Check links and accessibility.
- Record the approval, source version and next review date.
- Tell tutors what the video does, and does not, evidence.
- Plan how corrections will be issued to active cohorts.


## Worked example: turning a knowledge statement into video


Imagine a standard requires knowledge of risk-assessment principles. A weak brief says: “Create a five-minute risk-assessment video covering the KSB.”


A stronger brief says:


- **Outcome:** given a simple workplace scenario, the apprentice can identify hazards, distinguish likelihood from severity and justify one proportionate control.
- **Video:** an expert narrates a walk-through, pauses at three hazards and explains how context changes the decision.
- **Check:** the learner analyses a new scenario and explains their choice, rather than repeating the example.
- **Practice:** the tutor discusses the answer; the apprentice later contributes to a supervised workplace task where appropriate.
- **Evidence boundary:** the system records completion and the knowledge-check response. Neither alone proves that the apprentice can conduct risk assessment competently in their occupation.


This structure makes the asset useful without overstating its role.


## Keep assessment language accurate


Be careful with labels such as “EPA-ready,” “compliant” or “proves KSB achievement.” A learning resource can be aligned to a standard, but final judgements depend on the applicable assessment arrangements and valid evidence.


The 2026 reforms also mean providers should not copy assumptions from an older end-point assessment plan into a revised apprenticeship assessment plan. Skills England states that changes are being introduced in phases. Check the plan attached to the relevant standard whenever content is commissioned, reviewed or reused.

## FAQ


### Can video cover skills as well as knowledge?


It can demonstrate a skill and prepare an apprentice to practise it. A video view does not show that the apprentice can perform the skill safely, independently and consistently in the occupational context.


### Does completing a video prove a KSB has been achieved?


No. Completion shows an interaction with the resource. Use appropriate questions, practical activity and workplace evidence to evaluate learning and competence.


### Should every KSB have its own video?


No. Group statements when one coherent scenario genuinely connects them. Use text, discussion, coaching or practice when those methods are more suitable.


### Who should approve an apprenticeship knowledge video?


A qualified subject-matter expert should check technical accuracy, and someone who understands the current standard and assessment plan should check alignment. High-risk topics may also need legal, safeguarding or compliance review.


### How often should KSB videos be reviewed?


Set a risk-based schedule and review immediately when the standard, assessment plan, legislation or operational practice changes. Always check the live official pages before publication.
