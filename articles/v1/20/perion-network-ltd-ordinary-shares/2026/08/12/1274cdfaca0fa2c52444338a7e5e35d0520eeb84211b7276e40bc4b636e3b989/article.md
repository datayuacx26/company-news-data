## **What Is CTV Audience Targeting and Why It Matters**


CTV audience targeting refers to the practice of delivering advertisements to specific viewer segments on connected TV devices based on data-driven criteria such as demographics, behaviors, interests, and geography. Unlike traditional television advertising, which relies on broad program-level ratings, CTV targeting allows advertisers to reach households and individuals with far greater specificity.


### **Why Precision Matters in CTV Advertising**


The shift from linear TV to streaming has created a fragmented viewing environment where audiences consume content across dozens of apps and devices. Without precise targeting, advertisers risk wasting impressions on viewers who fall outside their intended audience. CTV audience targeting solves this by matching ad delivery to verified audience attributes, reducing waste and increasing return on investment.


### **Key Benefits of CTV Targeting**


- **Reduced ad waste:** Impressions are served only to viewers who match predefined audience criteria, eliminating the spray-and-pray approach of traditional TV buys.
- **Measurable outcomes:** CTV campaigns generate impression-level data that can be tied to downstream actions such as website visits, app installs, and purchases.
- **Cross-device reach:** Advertisers can connect CTV exposure to mobile and desktop activity, creating a unified view of the consumer journey.
- **Flexible budget allocation:** Programmatic buying enables real-time optimization, so budgets flow toward the highest-performing audience segments.

## **Linear TV vs. CTV: A Quick Targeting Comparison**


Understanding the differences between linear TV and CTV targeting is essential for advertisers transitioning their budgets to streaming environments. While both channels deliver video ads on a television screen, the underlying mechanics of audience selection, measurement, and optimization differ substantially.


**Dimension** **Linear TV** **CTV**


Audience selection Based on program ratings and estimated demographics (e.g., Adults 18-49) Based on first-party, third-party, and deterministic data at the household or device level


Buying model Upfront and scatter market negotiations Programmatic real-time bidding, private marketplaces, and direct deals


Measurement Panel-based estimates (e.g., Nielsen ratings) Impression-level tracking with attribution to outcomes


Geographic granularity DMA (Designated Market Area) level ZIP code, IP-based, and GPS-level targeting


Creative flexibility One ad per slot, same for all viewers Dynamic creative and sequential messaging by audience segment


Optimization cadence Post-campaign analysis, limited mid-flight changes Real-time bid adjustments and audience reallocation


### **What This Means for Advertisers**


The table above illustrates why CTV audience targeting represents a structural improvement over linear buying. Advertisers gain the ability to define audiences by actual behavior and attributes rather than relying on proxies like program genre. This precision, combined with real-time optimization, makes CTV a more accountable and efficient channel for reaching specific consumer groups.


That said, linear TV still commands significant reach for live events and mass-awareness campaigns. The most effective media strategies often blend both channels, using linear for broad reach and CTV for targeted frequency and measurement.


## **Exploring the Key Types of Audience Segmentation for CTV**


Effective CTV campaigns start with a clear segmentation strategy. The types of audience segmentation for CTV generally fall into several distinct categories, each offering different advantages depending on campaign objectives and available data.


### **Primary Segmentation Categories**


1. **Demographic segmentation:** Targeting based on age, gender, household income, education level, marital status, and household composition.
2. **Behavioral segmentation:** Targeting based on past actions such as purchase history, content consumption patterns, app usage, and browsing behavior.
3. **Geographic segmentation:** Targeting based on physical location, from broad regions down to specific ZIP codes or neighborhoods.
4. **Psychographic segmentation:** Targeting based on lifestyle preferences, values, interests, and attitudes derived from survey data or inferred from behavior.
5. **Contextual segmentation:** Targeting based on the type of content being viewed rather than the viewer’s personal attributes.


### **Layering Segments for Greater Precision**


The real power of CTV segmentation emerges when advertisers combine multiple layers. For example, a luxury auto brand might target households with an annual income above $150,000 (demographic), who have recently researched SUVs online (behavioral), and who live within 25 miles of a dealership (geographic). This layered approach narrows the audience to high-intent prospects while maintaining sufficient scale for campaign delivery.


Platforms such as Perion’s advertising solutions enable advertisers to build these composite audience profiles and activate them across premium CTV inventory, ensuring that segmentation strategy translates directly into media execution.


## **Using Demographic Targeting to Reach Specific Viewer Profiles**


Demographic targeting remains one of the most widely used methods in CTV advertising because it aligns closely with how brands define their core customer base. By filtering audiences based on observable or inferred demographic attributes, advertisers ensure their messages reach the viewer profiles most likely to convert.


### **Common Demographic Variables in CTV**


- **Age range:** Useful for products with generational appeal, such as financial planning services for viewers aged 55 and older or gaming accessories for viewers aged 18-34.
- **Household income:** Critical for luxury goods, premium services, and price-sensitive promotions where purchasing power determines relevance.
- **Parental status:** Brands selling children’s products, family vehicles, or educational services can target households with children of specific ages.
- **Homeownership:** Relevant for home improvement retailers, mortgage lenders, insurance providers, and real estate companies.


### **Where Demographic Data Comes From**


CTV demographic targeting draws from several data sources. First-party data from streaming platforms captures registration information such as age and gender. Third-party data providers like Experian, Acxiom, and TransUnion enrich these profiles with household-level attributes including income, home value, and family composition. Device graphs then link this information to specific CTV devices within a household.


The accuracy of demographic targeting depends heavily on the quality and recency of the underlying data. Stale or modeled data can lead to misattribution, which is why advertisers should prioritize partners who maintain deterministic data connections. Perion, for instance, emphasizes data quality and transparency in its targeting capabilities, helping advertisers minimize wasted impressions caused by inaccurate audience classification.


## **Applying Behavioral Targeting Based on Viewer Interests and Actions**


While demographic targeting tells advertisers who a viewer is, behavioral targeting reveals what that viewer does. This distinction makes behavioral targeting especially valuable for campaigns focused on intent and consideration rather than broad awareness.


### **Types of Behavioral Signals Used in CTV**


Behavioral targeting in CTV draws from a wide range of consumer actions tracked across digital touchpoints. These signals are then mapped to CTV devices through device graphs and identity resolution.


- **Purchase behavior:** Past transactions from loyalty programs, credit card data partnerships, and e-commerce activity indicate brand preferences and spending habits.
- **Content consumption:** The genres, shows, and channels a viewer watches reveal interests and affinities that advertisers can use for contextual alignment.
- **Search and browse activity:** Queries on search engines and visits to specific product pages signal active consideration for a purchase.
- **App usage:** The types of mobile apps installed and frequently used provide insight into lifestyle, fitness habits, financial interests, and entertainment preferences.


### **Behavioral Targeting in Practice**


Consider a travel brand promoting vacation packages. Rather than targeting all adults aged 25-54, the brand can focus on viewers who have recently searched for flights, visited hotel comparison websites, or engaged with travel content on social media. This behavioral targeting approach ensures the ad reaches people who are actively planning a trip, dramatically increasing the likelihood of engagement.


The challenge with behavioral targeting lies in data freshness and cross-device accuracy. A viewer who researched laptops three months ago is far less valuable than one who compared models yesterday. Effective CTV platforms refresh behavioral data frequently and use probabilistic and deterministic matching to connect online actions to the correct household TV screen.

## **The Critical Role of Data in CTV Targeting and Device Graphs**


Data is the foundation upon which all CTV audience targeting is built. Without accurate, comprehensive, and privacy-compliant data, even the most sophisticated targeting strategies will underperform. Understanding the data ecosystem behind CTV is essential for advertisers who want to make informed decisions about their media investments.


### **First-Party, Second-Party, and Third-Party Data**


- **First-party data:** Collected directly by the advertiser or publisher. Examples include CRM records, website analytics, email engagement, and purchase history. This data is the most accurate and privacy-safe but limited in scale.
- **Second-party data:** Another organization’s first-party data shared through a direct partnership. For example, a streaming platform sharing anonymized viewership data with an advertiser under a data-sharing agreement.
- **Third-party data:** Aggregated from multiple external sources and sold by data providers. While broad in scale, third-party data varies in quality and faces increasing regulatory scrutiny.


### **How Device Graphs Connect the Dots**


A device graph is a database that maps relationships between devices, identifiers, and households. When a consumer searches for running shoes on their phone, browses a fitness blog on their laptop, and later watches a cooking show on their CTV device, a device graph connects all three touchpoints to the same individual or household. This connection is what makes cross-device behavioral targeting possible on CTV.


Device graphs use two primary matching methods. Deterministic matching relies on known identifiers such as login credentials shared across devices, producing high-confidence connections. Probabilistic matching uses statistical models based on IP addresses, location patterns, and device behavior to infer relationships, offering greater scale but lower certainty.


### **Data Quality and Its Impact on Campaign Performance**


Poor data quality leads to mistargeted impressions, inflated reach estimates, and inaccurate attribution. Advertisers should evaluate their data partners based on several criteria:


1. **Recency:** How recently was the data collected or refreshed?
2. **Accuracy:** What percentage of records are verified through deterministic methods?
3. **Coverage:** Does the data set cover enough of the target audience to deliver at scale?
4. **Compliance:** Does the data collection process adhere to privacy regulations such as CCPA and GDPR?

## **Leveraging Programmatic Platforms for CTV Ad Automation**


Programmatic platforms for CTV have made it possible for advertisers of all sizes to access premium streaming inventory through automated, data-driven buying. What once required lengthy negotiations with individual publishers can now be executed in real time through demand-side platforms (DSPs) and supply-side platforms (SSPs).


### **How Programmatic CTV Buying Works**


The programmatic CTV workflow follows a structured sequence. When a viewer launches a streaming app and an ad opportunity arises, the SSP sends a bid request containing information about the viewer (anonymized), the content, and the device. DSPs evaluate this request against advertiser-defined audience criteria and submit bids. The winning bid’s ad is served to the viewer, and the entire process occurs in milliseconds.


### **Programmatic Deal Types for CTV**


- **Open auction (RTB):** Any eligible buyer can bid on available inventory. Offers maximum scale but less control over placement quality.
- **Private marketplace (PMP):** Invitation-only auctions where select buyers access premium inventory at negotiated floor prices. Balances scale with quality.
- **Programmatic guaranteed:** A fixed number of impressions at a fixed price, executed through programmatic infrastructure. Combines the certainty of direct deals with the efficiency of automation.
- **Preferred deals:** One-to-one agreements offering first-look access to inventory before it enters an auction. Useful for securing high-value placements.


### **Platform Selection Considerations**


Not all programmatic platforms for CTV offer the same capabilities. Advertisers should assess platforms based on inventory access, audience data integrations, fraud detection, frequency management, and reporting granularity. Perion operates within this programmatic ecosystem, providing advertisers with access to premium CTV supply alongside advanced targeting and measurement tools that streamline campaign management.


A critical consideration is supply path optimization (SPO). Advertisers should work with platforms that provide transparency into the supply chain, reducing unnecessary intermediaries that inflate costs without adding value. Shorter supply paths mean more of the advertiser’s budget goes toward actual media delivery rather than intermediary fees.


## **Best Practices for Optimizing CTV Targeting and Ad Spend**


Building an effective CTV campaign requires more than selecting the right audience segments. Optimizing CTV targeting involves continuous refinement of creative, frequency, measurement, and budget allocation to ensure every dollar contributes to business outcomes.


### **1. Set Clear KPIs Before Launch**


Define what success looks like before activating a campaign. Common CTV KPIs include video completion rate (VCR), cost per completed view (CPCV), reach and frequency, brand lift, website visit rate, and offline sales lift. Aligning KPIs with business objectives prevents the common mistake of optimizing toward vanity metrics that do not translate to revenue.


### **2. Manage Frequency Across Platforms**


One of the persistent challenges in CTV advertising is frequency management. Because viewers use multiple streaming apps, the same household can receive the same ad an excessive number of times across different publishers. Effective frequency capping requires a unified view of exposure across platforms, which is where cross-publisher identity resolution and device graphs become essential.


### **3. Test and Iterate on Creative**


CTV supports A/B testing of creative variants at the audience segment level. Advertisers should test different:


- Ad lengths (15-second vs. 30-second spots)
- Calls to action (QR codes vs. URL prompts vs. branded search terms)
- Messaging angles (emotional vs. rational, product-focused vs. lifestyle-focused)
- Creative formats (standard pre-roll vs. interactive overlays)


### **4. Use Incremental Reach Analysis**


Advertisers already running linear TV campaigns should measure the incremental reach that CTV delivers. This analysis identifies viewers who were not exposed to the linear campaign, quantifying CTV’s unique contribution to total audience coverage. This data justifies CTV budget allocation and informs future media mix decisions.


### **5. Align Targeting with the Purchase Funnel**


Different targeting strategies serve different funnel stages. Broad demographic targeting works well for upper-funnel awareness, while behavioral targeting based on recent purchase intent signals is more appropriate for lower-funnel consideration and conversion campaigns. Optimizing CTV targeting means matching the right segmentation approach to the right campaign objective.


## **The Future of CTV Targeting in a Post-Cookie World**


The deprecation of third-party cookies on web browsers has accelerated the advertising industry’s search for alternative identity solutions. While CTV was never directly dependent on browser cookies, the broader shift toward privacy-centric advertising affects the entire data ecosystem that supports CTV audience targeting.


### **Why CTV Is Better Positioned Than Display**


CTV has a structural advantage over web-based display advertising in a cookieless environment. Streaming platforms collect first-party data through user registrations, subscription accounts, and content preferences. This authenticated, opt-in data provides a more durable foundation for targeting than third-party cookies ever offered for web advertising. IP-based household identification and device-level identifiers such as IFA (Identifier for Advertising) further support CTV targeting without reliance on cookies.


### **Emerging Identity Solutions**


- **Unified ID frameworks:** Industry initiatives such as The Trade Desk’s Unified ID 2.0 and LiveRamp’s RampID create privacy-compliant identity graphs based on hashed email addresses and authenticated logins.
- **Publisher first-party data:** Streaming services are building their own audience segments from subscriber data, offering advertisers direct access to verified viewer profiles.
- **Clean rooms:** Data collaboration platforms allow advertisers and publishers to match audience data without exposing personally identifiable information, maintaining privacy while enabling targeting.
- **Contextual intelligence:** Advanced content analysis enables targeting based on what a viewer is watching rather than who they are, sidestepping identity requirements entirely.


### **How Advertisers Should Prepare**


Forward-thinking advertisers are investing in their own first-party data assets, building direct relationships with publishers, and testing multiple identity solutions simultaneously. The brands that will thrive are those that diversify their data strategies rather than depending on any single identifier or data source.
