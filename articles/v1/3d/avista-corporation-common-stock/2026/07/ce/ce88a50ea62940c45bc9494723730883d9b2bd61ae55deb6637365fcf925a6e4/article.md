# Avista cancels Public Safety Power Shutoff warning in Indian Trail

<< Back[PDF Version](https://investor.avistacorp.com/node/30791/pdf)


*Areas of northwest Spokane’s Indian Trail are no longer at risk of a power outage*


**SPOKANE, Wash., July 25, 2026** — Due to a change in weather causing a decrease in high-fire-risk conditions in northwest Spokane’s Indian Trail area, customers are no longer at risk of a Public Safety Power Shutoff (PSPS). While conditions have improved, wildfire risk remains high, and Avista will continue operating its system at higher sensitivity settings to reduce the risk of wildfire.


Customers who received a previous notification about a potential PSPS in the Indian Trail area are no longer at risk of a PSPS. We understand preparing for the possibility of an outage can be disruptive and we appreciate customers taking steps to be ready.


Weather conditions can continue to change, and Public Safety Power Shutoffs may be needed in the future if wildfire risk becomes extreme. Avista will continue to monitor conditions and provide updates as more information becomes available.


**While weather conditions have improved in Indian Trail, Avista is now evaluating the Kamiah area for a potential Public Safety Power Shutoff, and the Asotin area remains under consideration. Customers in potentially affected areas are receiving direct communication from Avista and should continue preparing for the possibility of an outage later today.**


“As we monitored the potential for a Public Safety Power Shutoff, I want to thank our customers for their patience and preparedness,” said Heather Rosentrater, president and CEO of Avista. “Changing weather conditions allowed us to avoid implementing the shutoff in the Indian Trail area and we’re grateful for that outcome. While we know these notifications can be concerning, preparing ahead of time helps keep our communities safer when wildfire risk is high. We remain committed to keeping customers informed and working closely with our community partners to help protect the people and places we serve.”
