# Securing the Energy Transition: Physical Security for Battery Energy Storage Systems (BESS)

As the world accelerates toward renewable energy adoption, Battery Energy Storage Systems (BESS) have emerged as critical infrastructure for grid stability and energy independence. These facilities store massive amounts of electrical energy, especially from renewable sources, and represent significant capital investments, making them attractive targets for theft, vandalism, and potential terrorist activities. The distributed nature of BESS deployments—from utility-scale installations to smaller commercial and industrial sites—creates security challenges that require robust, resilient protection solutions.


From a[physical security perspective](https://www.sandia.gov/app/uploads/sites/163/2021/09/ESHB_Ch18_Physical-Security_Johnson.pdf) , BESS facilities present both opportunities and challenges. Their relatively compact footprint compared to traditional power plants allows for comprehensive perimeter protection, while their high-value contents and critical grid functions demand multi-layered security approaches. Additionally, the potential for thermal runaway and battery fires creates both environmental and safety hazards that make unauthorized access prevention even more critical for protecting personnel and surrounding communities, necessitating specialized security plans when obtaining local and regional approvals.
