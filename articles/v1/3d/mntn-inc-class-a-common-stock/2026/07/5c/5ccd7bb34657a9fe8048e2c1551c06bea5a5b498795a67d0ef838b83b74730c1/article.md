# What Do We Know About Who Watches Major League Baseball on TV and Streaming?

Major League Baseball is massive. Everybody knows it, and the numbers prove it. The[2024 World Series drew an average of 15.81 million viewers](https://now.temple.edu/news/2025-04-24/reasons-why-baseball-ratings-are-improving) across platforms, the most-watched Fall Classic since 2017. The 2025 regular season posted year-over-year viewership gains across every national broadcast partner. MLB.TV recorded[14.5 billion minutes watched in 2024](https://now.temple.edu/news/2025-04-24/reasons-why-baseball-ratings-are-improving) , then topped it again in 2025 with[streaming numbers up 34% from the prior year](https://www.forbes.com/sites/maurybrown/2025/10/03/mlb-network-continues-trend-of-mlb-viewership-increases-in-2025/) . Scale is not a question here.


But scale is just the opening pitch. The more interesting part of the MLB audience story is about who’s doing the watching now, and the answer has shifted more in the last three years than it did in the previous fifteen.


## **The Audience Everyone Already Suspects Is There**


MLB’s core viewership is broad, loyal, and heavily invested. The sport draws the second-largest total audience of any professional league in the U.S., trailing only the NFL, and its 162-game regular season means that presence isn’t seasonal, but a nightly drumbeat from April through October.


A[January 2024 SBRnet survey](https://www.statista.com/statistics/1471292/mlb-fans-age/) found that the largest single age segment among MLB attendees and viewers was 50-64, which tracks with a generation that grew up watching games as a social ritual, passed it down, and never really stopped. For advertisers, the profile that follows — established careers, dual-income households, strong brand loyalty, discretionary spending on entertainment and lifestyle — is straightforwardly valuable. According to[The Shelf’s analysis of professional sports fan demographics](https://www.theshelf.com/the-blog/sports-viewership/) , MLB fans across the five major leagues are consistently described as middle-class men and women with meaningful disposable income across multiple spending categories.


## **The Part of the Audience That Surprises Most Marketers**


Here’s where the conventional wisdom starts getting complicated.


For years, MLB carried a reputation as a sport with an aging problem. The average age of a fan was trending up, and younger audiences seemed to be drifting away. That reputation is starting to change, thanks in part to the pitch clock era, which began in 2023, and compressed games to under two hours and forty minutes on average, the shortest since the mid-1980s. Shorter games increase accessibility, and when combined with a new generation of genuinely marketable stars, younger audiences have become more inclined to take themselves out to the ballgame from the comfort of their own couch.


According to[MLB](https://www.mlb.com/news/mlb-younger-fans-growing-youth-participation) , the median age of a ticket buyer has dropped from 51 to 45 since 2019 — a six-year shift in under five years. On the streaming side, the movement is clearer still: the average age of an MLB.TV subscriber dropped from 48 to 44 over the same period. Viewership in the 18-34 demographic grew 12% on ESPN, 24% on FS1, and 9% on Fox during the 2024 season. The network-level growth in this demo was broad enough that[Barrett Media noted a 13% gain in 18-34 viewers](https://barrettmedia.com/2025/06/03/major-league-baseball-viewership-up-across-the-board-in-2025/) for the MLB Network Showcase Game series alone in 2025.


What’s driving younger fans toward streaming specifically? Partly platform access —[younger fans, particularly those aged 18-34, are more than twice as likely to stream live sports](https://www.viantinc.com/insights/blog/mlb-streaming-2025-fans-advertisers/) compared to older demographics, according to Viant Technology. And MLB has deliberately expanded its streaming footprint to meet them there. As of 2025, 26 of the league’s 30 teams offer standalone DTC streaming options for local fans. The infrastructure is there; the audience is showing up.


For advertisers, the 18-34 streaming fan is worth separating from the broader “MLB audience” label. This viewer is paying for access, often out-of-market, actively following a specific team across a long season. That level of intent signals a different kind of attention than someone who happens to catch a game occasionally on cable.


## **The Audience Most Brands Are Underestimating**


According to[ACR viewership data from LG Ad Solutions](https://lgads.tv/insights/tv-trends-hit-a-home-run-with-mlb-audiences/) , female viewership of MLB has seen consistent growth. Because of the stereotype that typical sports fans are male, that finding tends to catch people off-guard, but it aligns with something The Shelf’s analysis found: baseball fans are often described as the most family-oriented fan group among the five major American professional sports leagues.


The family dimension of MLB viewership changes how advertisers should think about the audience. A game that runs two and a half hours on a weeknight, in a sport with nearly daily games over six months, tends to pull households into shared viewing in ways that shorter or less frequent sports don’t. It’s background viewing that becomes active viewing when someone hits a grand slam. It’s also a format that holds up across generations in the same room, where a grandparent, a parent, and a teenager can all plausibly have an opinion on who is at-bat.


[Fifty-seven percent of MLB fans report that they like to stream live sports](https://lgads.tv/insights/tv-trends-hit-a-home-run-with-mlb-audiences/) , according to LG Ad Solutions’ 2022 ACR data, a figure that tracks with the broader directional shift toward streaming across all live sports. That distribution matters: an MLB audience that spans broadcast, CTV, and mobile isn’t consolidated in one place, which means brands that show up across platforms have a better shot at reaching the full household than those anchored to a single linear buy.


The female viewer and the co-viewing household don’t always show up in the top-line MLB audience conversation. They should. Both represent reach beyond the assumed sports-fan demographic, and both are watching games in the same high-attention context as the core audience.


## **What This Means for Advertisers**


Major League Baseball offers a live sports placement without the congestion of the NFL window, across a season that runs nightly from April through the World Series. The audience story above points to a mix of loyal older viewers, a younger streaming cohort that’s actively growing, and a household audience with more breadth than the conventional male sports demo would suggest.


For brands in categories like financial services, auto, consumer electronics, retail, food and beverage, or travel, the sustained attention and multi-demographic reach of a full-season MLB buy creates the kind of repeat exposure that builds familiarity, recall, and consideration over time, not just around one event in October.
