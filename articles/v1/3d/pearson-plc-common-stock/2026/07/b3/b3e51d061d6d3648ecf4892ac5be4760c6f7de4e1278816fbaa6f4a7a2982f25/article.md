*Billy Grieco, Product Management Lead, Enterprise Learning & Skills*


Think about the last person your team hired. The resume looked strong and the interview went well.


But how much of that decision was based on verifiable proof, and how much was simply trust?


For most organizations, the honest answer is simple: almost all of it was trust.


For decades, that approach worked well enough. We relied on familiar proxies like university names, years of experience, and a well-polished resume. But the ground has shifted. Generative AI now makes it easy to fabricate credentials and even create synthetic identities. In this environment, a bad hire is no longer just a productivity problem. It can quickly become a security vulnerability or a compliance risk.


That raises a fundamental challenge for employers in an increasingly zero-trust world. How do we verify the basics, and how do we protect our organizations from unverified claims?


Fortunately, we don’t have to invent a solution from scratch. A proven blueprint already exists in global finance. We don’t move money with handwritten IOUs. We rely on secure, interoperable, and verifiable systems of exchange. We trust digital wallets with our currency because they are built on verification and security. It is time to apply that same architecture to the currency of the modern economy: verified human skill.


**From a system of claims to a system of proof**


The opportunity ahead is to move from a hiring model built on claims to one grounded in verifiable proof.


Learning and Employment Records (LERs) are emerging as a trusted way to document a person’s skills, credentials, and experience. A digital skills wallet serves as the secure home where those verified records can be stored, managed, and shared. Together, they create a more trustworthy foundation for hiring, workforce development, and career mobility.


Here’s how this can create value for employers:


1. **It helps establish identity.** A credential in a digital wallet can be cryptographically signed and linked to trusted credential records and validated identity signals. When a candidate shares a record from their wallet, they provide a tamper-evident link between who they are and what they can do. This replaces guesswork with a more secure and reliable check, forming an important layer of defense in a zero-trust world.
2. **It validates capability.** The LERs contained within a digital wallet are not self-reported. They come directly from trusted issuers, whether a university, licensing board, training provider, certification body, or employer. Each record provides verified evidence of skills and achievements. This makes it more difficult to exaggerate experience or invent qualifications while creating a trusted, lifelong record of learning and work.
3. **It’s designed to integrate into your workflow.** Built on open standards, a digital wallet is designed to work with the HR technology you already use. Over time, Applicant Tracking System can more easily incorporate verified skills data, HRIS platforms can better track credentials for internal mobility, and compliance systems can support more automated license checks. This helps shift what is often a slow, manual process toward a more efficient, streamlined workflow.


**Where proof is already delivering value**


Early implementations of this approach are already delivering tangible results.


In healthcare, providers use wallet-based credentials to verify licenses in minutes instead of weeks, ensuring that qualified professionals are in place faster. On construction sites, managers can confirm safety certifications at the start of a shift. Within global companies, teams can make more informed decisions about access to sensitive work based on verified skills.


These examples point to a broader shift: when verification becomes embedded in the system, trust becomes faster, more consistent, and more scalable.


**A call to leadership**


The consequences of unverified hiring are no longer theoretical. A single hire based on false information can expose a company to significant damage.


This is a call to action for leaders who understand that managing talent is inseparable from managing risk. The path forward is not to build higher walls, but to create smarter bridges of trusted, verifiable data.


While some capabilities are available today through Pearson’s credentialing ecosystem, others will continue to evolve as the infrastructure and partnerships needed to support a fully interoperable system mature.


By moving toward a model grounded in proof, organizations can strengthen how they hire, reduce risk, and contribute to a more efficient, equitable market where capable people are connected to the right opportunities.


In a world of uncertainty, verification is a competitive advantage. It is your first line of defense and your fastest path to building a workforce you can truly trust.

**Frequently Asked Questions**


**What types of credentials can be stored in a digital skills wallet?** Pearson's digital skills wallet can hold university degrees, professional licenses and certifications, employer-issued skills assessments, training completions, and continuing education credits — spanning a person's entire career.


**What are Learning and Employment Records (LERs)?** LERs are digital records that document an individual's skills, credentials, education, work experience, certifications, and achievements. They provide verified evidence of learning and work accomplishments and can be securely stored and shared through a digital skills wallet.


**How does a digital skills wallet integrate with existing HR systems?** Pearson's digital skills wallet is built on open standards and is designed to work with applicant tracking systems, HRIS platforms, and compliance tools employers already use, making it easier to bring verified credentials into existing workflows over time.


**Why is credential fraud a growing risk for employers?** Generative AI has made it easier than ever to fabricate resumes, certifications, and even synthetic identities. Pearson's verified credentials address this gap with tamper-proof, source-verified records.


**How is Pearson involved in digital skills wallets and verified credentials?** Pearson is helping to advance the digital skills wallet as a secure, interoperable solution for proof-based hiring.
