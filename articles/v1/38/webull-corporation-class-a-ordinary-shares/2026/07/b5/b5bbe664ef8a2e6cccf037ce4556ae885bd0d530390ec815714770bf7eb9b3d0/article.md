## Part 1: What Is After Hours Trading? Understanding the After Hours Stock Market


**After hours trading is the buying and selling of stocks and ETFs through electronic markets after the regular US session closes at 4:00 p.m. ET, typically running until 8:00 p.m. ET.** Together with the pre-market session, it forms what traders call "extended-hours" trading.


Historically, only banks and hedge funds could participate in after hours market trading. Today, ECNs and commission-free brokerage apps have opened the after hours stock exchange to retail investors, letting them respond to news the moment it breaks instead of waiting for the next opening bell.


**Why traders use the after hours session**


-


**React to earnings:** Most US companies report earnings after 4:00 p.m. ET. After hours share trading lets you respond immediately.


-


**Trade breaking news:** Fed announcements, mergers, and guidance updates often land outside regular hours.


-


**Track after market movers:** Big overnight gainers and losers frequently set the tone for the next day's open.


-


**Fit your schedule:** Working professionals can manage positions in the evening.

### 3.2 The main risks of after hours trading


The after hours stock exchange behaves differently from the regular session in ways that can catch traders off guard. With most institutional desks offline and far fewer participants active, prices can move faster and less predictably on lighter volume, and the quotes you see may not reflect true, executable demand. That combination makes extended-hours trading higher-risk than a typical daytime trade, so it pays to understand what you're stepping into. Know these risks before you place an order in the after hours market:


-


**Lower liquidity:** Fewer buyers and sellers make it harder to enter or exit at your target price.


-


**Wider bid-ask spreads:** Thin volume can push the gap between buy and sell prices much wider.


-


**Higher volatility:** Small orders can move prices sharply, especially around earnings.


-


**Limited order types:** Many brokers only allow limit orders during extended hours.


-


**Price gaps:** An after hours price may differ significantly from the next day's open.


### 3.3 Regulation, safety, and investor protection


Trustworthy after hours share trading depends on where you trade. In the US, reputable brokers are:


-


**FINRA-regulated** members, subject to conduct and reporting rules.


-


**SEC-registered** , operating under federal securities law.


-


**SIPC members** , protecting securities up to $500,000 (including a $250,000 limit for cash) if the brokerage fails. *SIPC does not protect against market losses.*


Before trading the after hours market, confirm your broker's regulatory status on FINRA BrokerCheck and review its risk disclosures. Platform safety features such as two-factor authentication, encryption, and account-activity alerts add another layer of protection.

## FAQs


### **1. What is after hours trading in simple terms?**


After hours trading is buying and selling stocks through electronic networks after the regular US market closes at 4:00 p.m. ET, usually until 8:00 p.m. ET. It lets investors react to news and earnings outside standard hours.


### **2. Is after hours trading a good idea for beginners?**


It can be risky for beginners because the after hours market has lower liquidity, wider spreads, and higher volatility. New traders should start with limit orders, small positions, and ideally practice with paper trading first.


### **3. Can you make money trading after hours?**


Yes, traders can profit by reacting quickly to earnings and after market movers, but the same volatility that creates opportunity also increases the risk of losses and poor fills.


### **4. What time does after hours trading end?**


Standard after hours trading ends at 8:00 p.m. ET. Some brokers, such as Webull, offer overnight sessions that extend stock trading after hours market access well beyond that window.


### **5. Why is the after hours price different from the closing price?**


Because the after hours stock exchange has fewer participants, even modest orders can move prices, causing after hours quotes to differ from both the 4:00 p.m. close and the next day's open.


### **6. Do all brokers offer after hours share trading?**


No. Availability, session length, and order-type rules vary by broker, so confirm extended-hours support and any limit-order requirements before you trade.
