## Part 1: What Is Premarket Trading and How Do Premarket Hours Work?


**Definition:**


[Premarket trading](https://www.webull.com/help/faq/10960-What-are-the-trading-hours-for-stock-and-ETF-orders) is the buying and selling of stocks before the regular US market session opens at 9:30 a.m. ET. It runs through electronic communication networks (ECNs) rather than a traditional exchange floor, letting investors react to overnight news, earnings, and economic reports ahead of the opening bell.


The premarket window is where the day often takes shape. Earnings released before the open, an analyst upgrade, or a jobs report can send a stock premarket price sharply higher or lower before most traders have finished their coffee.

### **3.2 How Premarket Trading Works**


During the premarket session, orders are matched through ECNs instead of the exchange's central auction. Because fewer participants are active, three things change:


-


**Lower liquidity** — fewer buyers and sellers, so orders can be harder to fill.


-


**Wider bid-ask spreads** — the gap between buy and sell prices is often larger.


-


**Higher volatility** — small orders can move stock premarket prices more than they would midday.


Most brokers require **limit orders** in the premarket to protect you from these swings, since market orders can fill at unexpected prices.


### **3.3 Why the Premarket Session Matters**


The premarket is a real-time gauge of sentiment. Watching premarket movers helps you:


1.


Anticipate how a stock may open after earnings or news.


2.


Read index futures for broad market direction.


3.


Plan entries, exits, and stop levels before volume surges at 9:30 a.m. ET.


## Part 2: How to Trade Premarket and Track Premarket Movers Before the Bell


Trading the premarket is less about speed and more about preparation. Because the session runs on thinner volume, the traders who do well are the ones who know exactly what they want to buy or sell, at what price, and why—before the first order ever hits the tape. The steps below walk through how to place a premarket trade the right way, then move into how to read the catalysts and data that separate a fleeting spike from a genuine move.


### 2.1 How to Place a Premarket Trade (Step by Step)


Placing a premarket order looks similar to a regular trade, but a few settings matter more when liquidity is low. Work through these in order the first time you trade before the bell:


1.


**Open a brokerage account** that supports premarket trading. Not every broker offers extended hours, and those that do differ on when access begins.


2.


**Enable extended-hours trading** in your account settings. Many brokers require you to accept a separate extended-hours disclosure that spells out the added risks.


3.


**Fund your account and build a watchlist** of stock premarket candidates the night before, so you're reacting to a plan rather than to a headline.


4.


**Choose a limit order and set your price.** In the premarket, this is your most important control—it caps what you'll pay or accept and protects you from filling into a wide spread.


5.


**Select the extended-hours (premarket) session** when routing the order, since a default order may only be eligible for the regular session.


6.


**Review and confirm carefully.** Double-check the limit price and share count; a quote that looks attractive on light volume can move against you the moment you submit.


A quick note on order types: most brokers restrict premarket trading to **limit orders** and disable market orders entirely. That's a feature, not a limitation. With so few participants, a market order could fill several percent away from the last print. Setting a firm limit price is how you stay in control when the order book is thin.


### 2.2 What Drives Premarket Movers


Premarket movers are the stocks posting the largest percentage gains or losses before the open, and they rarely move without a reason. Understanding the catalyst behind a move tells you whether it's likely to hold into the regular session or fade once real volume arrives. The most common drivers are:


-


**Earnings reports released before market open (BMO)** — the single biggest source of premarket volatility, as results and guidance reset expectations overnight.


-


**Guidance changes, mergers, and acquisitions** — corporate actions that reprice a stock instantly.


-


**FDA decisions and clinical trial results** — frequent, high-magnitude catalysts in biotech and pharma.


-


**Analyst upgrades or downgrades** — rating and price-target changes that shift sentiment before the bell.


-


**Macro data** — CPI, jobs reports, and Federal Reserve announcements that move entire sectors and index futures at once.


-


**Overnight moves in index futures and global markets** — a sharp session in Asia or Europe often carries into US stock premarket pricing.


When you spot a premarket mover, the first question to ask is simple: *what's the catalyst, and is it specific to this company or the whole market?* A stock jumping on its own earnings behaves very differently from one drifting higher only because index futures are green.


### 2.3 How to Find Stock Premarket Data


Good decisions depend on good data, and not every platform surfaces the same detail before the open. To scan premarket movers effectively and confirm what's really happening, look for a platform that provides:


-


**Real-time premarket quotes and volume** — so you can gauge whether a move has conviction behind it or is running on a handful of shares.


-


**Gainers, losers, and most-active lists** — the fastest way to see where attention is concentrated before 9:30 a.m. ET.


-


**Index futures (S&P 500, Nasdaq-100, Dow)** — the backdrop that tells you if a stock is leading or simply following the broad market.


-


**News feeds tied to each ticker** — so you can match every premarket move to its catalyst in seconds.


Volume is the detail new traders overlook most often. A stock premarket price up 8% on a few thousand shares can evaporate at the open, while the same move on heavy volume is far more likely to stick. Reading price *and* volume together is what turns a premarket scan into an actionable edge.


**Risk Disclosure**


Extended-hours and premarket trading carry unique risks. Prices during the premarket session may not reflect the price at the regular market open, liquidity can be limited, and news events can cause rapid, outsized moves. These sessions are not suitable for every investor. Consider your risk tolerance and use limit orders to help manage execution risk.

## FAQs


### **1. What time does premarket trading start?**


Premarket trading typically runs from 4:00 a.m. to 9:30 a.m. ET, though exact hours vary by broker. Some platforms, like Webull, open at 4:00 a.m. ET, while others begin at 7:00 a.m. ET.


### **2. Can you buy stocks in the premarket?**


Yes. You can buy and sell stocks during the premarket session through a broker that supports extended-hours trading. Most brokers require limit orders during the premarket to manage the risk of wide spreads and low liquidity.


### **3. What are premarket movers?**


Premarket movers are the stocks with the largest price changes before the market opens, usually driven by earnings, news, analyst actions, or economic data. Traders watch them to gauge how a stock may open at 9:30 a.m. ET.


### **4. Is premarket trading risky?**


Premarket trading carries higher risk than the regular session due to lower liquidity, wider bid-ask spreads, and increased volatility. Prices before the open may differ significantly from the opening price, so risk management is essential.


### **5.Does premarket price predict the open?**


Premarket prices signal sentiment but don't guarantee the opening price. Liquidity is thin, so a stock premarket quote can shift quickly once regular-session volume arrives at 9:30 a.m. ET.
