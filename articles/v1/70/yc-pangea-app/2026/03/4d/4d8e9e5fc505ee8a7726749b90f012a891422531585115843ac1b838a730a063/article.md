# How Business Operations Consultants Are Using AI to Automate Workflows and Move Faster

Here’s a question hiring teams keep coming back to: can companies actually move faster with AI, or is it mostly noise?


For many business operations consultants, the answer is increasingly clear. AI has become a genuine part of how work gets done. Tasks that once ate up hours of someone’s day–pulling data, building reports, writing scripts, keeping documentation current–can now often be handled in a fraction of the time. The change isn’t hypothetical. It’s showing up in real workflows, on real teams.


Across talent on Pangea, business operations specialists are using AI to fundamentally rethink how they work. Less manual execution, more experimentation. Less time in the weeds, more time on what actually moves the needle.


## What Business Operations Roles Looked Like Before AI Automation


Business operations roles have always sat at the intersection of a company’s systems and processes. These are the people managing reporting pipelines, digging into operational data, building dashboards, and making sure different tools and teams are talking to each other.


But for a long time, a lot of that work meant grinding through repetitive tasks: cleaning messy datasets, extracting information from files, reconciling spreadsheets, writing automation scripts, and generating internal reports. Most teams just accepted this as part of the job.


AI workflow automation is changing that assumption, and sometimes dramatically.


## How AI-Native Business Operations Consultants Work in Practice


The best way to understand what AI-powered operations looks like is through the people doing it. Below are real examples from business operations specialists on Pangea, showing exactly how they’re using AI tools to automate manual work and reclaim time for higher-value projects.


### Case Study: Lukas Blythe, Fractional Business Operations Consultant


**The challenge:** a daily reporting workflow that required downloading and manually processing 70 PDFs, every single day.


Fractional business operations consultant and Pangean[Lukas Blythe](https://pangea.app/profile/paul-lukas-blythe) puts the old way simply: “A lot of analyst work used to be really manual.”


His day-to-day included downloading roughly 70 PDFs from a website, pulling specific numbers from each one, and moving that data into structured reports. It was tedious, time-consuming, and representative of the kind of work that quietly consumes operations teams.


*“Until recently I was doing a lot of it manually, but over the last couple of months, we’ve been really trying to implement AI into these projects where, for example, one of my tasks every day is to download 70 PDFs from a website to pull numbers from, and it’s really annoying. We’ve been trying to figure out a way to basically automate that.”*


**The approach:** instead of building a custom automation from scratch, Lukas used ChatGPT as a development partner, uploading sample files, asking it to generate Python parsing scripts, debugging errors, and refining the logic until the whole process ran automatically.


**The result:** a workflow that used to take an hour each morning now takes five minutes.


*“Now I can download all my stuff in five minutes… Everything is automated now so I just have to run the code in the morning and make sure all the numbers match up and I’m good to go.”*


That single improvement saves more than 20 hours per month, without a developer or a months-long implementation project.


*“It took eight years for this to be automated. It took five hours to perfect it.”*


Beyond PDF automation, Lukas also uses AI to accelerate content-heavy operational work. Managing a product catalog with over 100 SKUs, writing individual product descriptions by hand was a real bottleneck:


*“\[We’re\] having to basically create product descriptions like bullet points for people when they’re looking at the product. That becomes very repetitive, especially when you have over a hundred products... Trying to type those descriptions out for so many things, especially with different parameters, is really really annoying.”*


Now, AI generates structured first drafts that he refines to match his brand’s voice. As he puts it: *“It gets me 60 to 70 percent of the way there. Then I refine it.”*


He’s also using AI image generation, not to publish finished assets, but to explore product presentation concepts quickly:


*“We’ve had to use \[ChatGPT\] for image generation, not really to publish, but, for example, one of the products we sell are these dog bags and we’re asked: how can we give this more of like a luxurious feel? So, we’ll plug in the prompt to Chat and it’ll come up with an image that we can work off of... or just like give us the baseline and then we build on it.”*


## What AI Still Can’t Do: The Limits of Operations Automation


None of this means AI is replacing the judgment that makes operations professionals valuable. Experienced consultants are clear-eyed about that.


*“AI is not going to work on deals or products that we need to come out with and stuff like that.”*


Strategic prioritization, stakeholder alignment, risk evaluation, cross-team decisions: those still require human judgment. What AI does is clear away the repetitive execution work that gets in the way of doing that thinking well.


*“For me, it just makes my very menial tasks a lot easier and take up a lot less time so I can focus on the important things that’ll actually get us business.”*


The most effective AI-native operations consultants aren’t using AI to replace expertise. They’re using it to protect their time so their expertise can go further.


## How AI Helps Operations Teams Learn Faster and Solve Problems Sooner


One of the most underappreciated advantages AI brings to operations work isn’t just automation. It’s speed of learning.


Before AI tools, troubleshooting a technical problem meant Googling documentation, searching Stack Overflow, and reading through forum threads. Now the process is dramatically shorter: “I just upload the file and ask for help.”


That tighter feedback loop lets operations professionals prototype internal tools, write functional scripts, and experiment with new processes, even without deep technical backgrounds. The barrier to trying something new is much lower, which means teams can iterate faster and find what works sooner.
