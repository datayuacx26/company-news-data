# Why Water Quality Matters in HVAC Systems – Key Parameters Explained

Hydronic and other water-based HVAC systems are highly efficient and commonly used to heat and cool large commercial and industrial properties. As water is an excellent conductor of heat and can store large amounts of thermal energy, it is critical to the successful operation of these systems.


The quality of the water within the HVAC network has a direct impact on performance. Continuous monitoring of vital parameters such as pH, dissolved oxygen and chlorine enables managers to gain immediate insight into the health and integrity of their HVAC systems. Identifying early warning signs such as corrosion and scaling allows proactive management to avoid large-scale damage, energy waste and unnecessary downtime.


## What Water Quality Parameters Are Key for HVAC Systems


Water quality monitoring is diverse, and a range of elements can be measured in every application where water is used, providing valuable information and insight into system integrity. Within the HVAC industry, the parameters below are increasingly important:


### pH (Acidity/Alkalinity) & System Chemistry Balance


Monitoring[pH](https://www.badgermeter.com/water-quality-parameters/ph/) is important for the control and prevention of corrosion and scaling. System water with a low pH value (acidic water) can cause oxidation of metal components, whereas water with a high pH value (alkaline water) can increase the presence of calcium carbonate that leads to scaling. Scale deposits and corrosion can affect the efficient transfer of heat, meaning the system must run for longer, significantly increasing energy consumption. By preventing damage from corrosion and scaling, pH monitoring helps to extend system longevity and reduce operating costs.


**HVAC systems impacted by pH:** Closed-loop HVAC systems (hydronic heating and chilled water systems)


### Dissolved Oxygen (DO) & Corrosion Risk


The presence of oxygen is the primary cause of corrosion in closed-loop systems. When oxygen enters the system, it reacts with metals causing rust, which can cause severe damage to components and lead to catastrophic failure. Increased levels of oxygen within the system can also provide optimal conditions for bacterial growth, potentially contributing to public health risks such as Legionella. A steady[DO](https://www.badgermeter.com/water-quality-parameters/dissolved-oxygen/) measurement is an indicator of a healthy and well-maintained water system. By continually monitoring this parameter, managers can protect against corrosion, extend the system's lifespan and maintain a healthy environment for occupants and residents.


**HVAC systems impacted by dissolved oxygen (DO):** Closed-loop HVAC systems (hydronic heating and chilled water systems)


### Conductivity & HVAC Water Chemistry


[Conductivity](https://www.badgermeter.com/water-quality-parameters/conductivity/) measurement provides an indication of the total concentration of dissolved solids (salts, minerals and other ionic compounds) within the water. Water with high levels of conductivity is corrosive and can cause decay in industrial equipment and plumbing systems. Sudden spikes in conductivity could be the result of a leak, a loss of vital water treatment chemicals or contamination from an external source. Monitoring this parameter in real-time and detecting these events in their formative stages is important in preventing conductivity from reaching dangerously high levels and causing serious damage to HVAC systems.


**HVAC systems impacted by conductivity:** Cooling towers, closed-loop HVAC systems (including chilled water systems)


### Turbidity & Water Cleanliness


[Turbidity](https://www.badgermeter.com/water-quality-parameters/turbidity/) focuses on the optical characteristics (clarity and cloudiness) of water and measures the presence of suspended solids. Tracking turbidity within a building provides an overview of the health of the water, with elevated levels suggesting poor quality. High turbidity readings would suggest that water has become contaminated with solids such as rust, dirt and organic matter. These can build up on system components, causing fouling and degradation that can reduce efficiency and increase operating costs, and microbial growth that can contribute to Legionella. Monitoring turbidity enables managers to keep the water in their systems clean and free from contaminants, improving optimization and complying with health and safety regulations.


**HVAC systems impacted by turbidity:** Cooling towers, closed-loop HVAC systems


### Chlorine & Microbial Control


As a powerful biocide,[chlorine](https://www.badgermeter.com/water-quality-parameters/chlorine/) is frequently used within HVAC systems to control microbial growth and keep systems clean. Chlorine helps to prevent the formation of biofilm, a substance that can cause the build-up of scale, creating blockages that prevent water from flowing freely and reduce the system’s ability to transfer heat effectively. Biofilm can also harbor bacteria that can contaminate the water and subsequent cooled air, posing a serious health risk to building occupants. Continuous monitoring of chlorine verifies that adequate levels are always available to control pathogens, enabling improved system performance and compliance with public health regulations.


**HVAC systems impacted by chlorine:** Cooling towers, HVAC make-up water systems (with downstream impact on closed-loop HVAC systems)
