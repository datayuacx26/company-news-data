## Make it legal: the first steps to starting a roofing business


Before you do anything and start advertising your business, you need to make sure you're running it legitimately. That includes things like...


### **Registering your business**


Choose a business structure: LLC, S-Corp, Sole Proprietor, or Inc. An LLC is the most common starting point — it protects your personal assets and is straightforward to set up. Register with your state, get your EIN from the IRS, and open a dedicated business bank account. Mixing personal and business finances creates real accounting and tax problems.


For a current list of state licensing requirements, the[U.S. Small Business Administration](https://www.sba.gov/) is a reliable starting point.


### **Acquire licenses and insurance**


Licensing requirements vary by state. Regardless, carry general liability insurance and workers’ comp from day one. These protect you and your customers. Bonding is worth having, especially if you’re pursuing commercial work.


### **Secure permits**


This may be more on a job-by-job basis, but it still rings true. Make sure you continue to legitimize your work long after you're new and fresh. Each state and area has its own permit rules, so familiarize yourself with yours.


### **Train your staff**


Working-at-heights certificationfall protection courses, first aid training, WHMIS (Workplace Hazardous Materials Information System), and any required training are needed to keep your team safe. These aren't a one-and-done task either. Some of these need to be repeated every year or every few years. Again, keep up with it to stay legitimate.


## Building a roofing business plan


A well-crafted roofing business plan is essential for a successful roofing business.


**The purpose of a business plan is to help you outline your goals, strategies, and financial projections on paper.** It ensures you think about everything you need to kick off your business and can act as a road map for the first 1-3 years. Successful roofing companies don't just happen. They take planning.


Your business plan should be well thought out, and not slapped together over a day or two.


Here's what to include in your business plan:


### 1. Executive summary


The executive summary is a snapshot of your entire business plan, providing an overview of your company, its goals, and how you plan to achieve them. It should act as a brief summary and outline your business. You can write this last.


### 2. Company description


The second part should be a description of who your roofing business is. Include:


- Your mission statement
- Company vision
- Your values


These points can help to guide the rest of your business plan and keep you grounded in WHY you're doing what you're doing, and the type of legacy you want to build.


Include in here the type of roofing you do, or any other services you provide.


### 3. Market analysis


Next, you'll want to look at where you're going to be selling. The more you know about your selling market, the better prepared you'll be to sell in it.


Do this ahead of time to[inform your marketing strategy](https://roofr.com/blog/marketing-101-promoting-your-roofing-company-online) and help you have a strong kick-off. It can even impact the branding you use!


You'll want to look at:


- Homeowner demographics like age, income, household size
- Average age of homes
- Homes sold in your area
- Competitors in your area, as well as their strengths and weaknesses (who is the big guy in your area?)


[US census data](https://data.census.gov/) is a great resource for this, but you can use local data as well.


### 4. Organization and management


Are you hiring admin staff? Do you need any workers? Maybe you want to contract out installers for now. Whatever you decide, include it here.


### 5. Services and products


This is where you want to specify what services you're offering and what products you'll use. call out everything you want to do, like:


- Roof installation
- Repair
- Inspections
- Maintenance


If you want to offer any additional services like gutters, siding, attic insulation, etc. include that too.


For products, will you use GAF, Owens Corning, TAMKO, Atlas, or another shingle manufacturer? Lay all that info out here, including warranties.


### 6. Marketing and sales strategy


You should lay out your initial marketing plans, and then 1+ year plans. You won't want to jump into all marketing channels at once. That'll be too much.


When planning your marketing strategies, think about where your target market is. For example, newer homeowners in a younger demographic are on Instagram while older homeowners would be using Facebook groups more.


Other things to include here is your plan for:


- Branding, colors, name, and logo
- Business cards
- Referral programs
- Email marketing
- Social media strategy
- Website
- Local SEO and Google Business accounts


Outline your sales process and strategies for generating leads, converting them into customers, and fostering long-term relationships.


### 7. Financial Projections


Be realistic in this. Including startup costs, revenue forecasts, and projected expenses in this. This will help you work backwards on what you want to earn down to how many jobs you need to book.


If you're going to look for investors and lenders, they'll want to see that you've thought through your financial plan and have a plan to profit.


### 8. Appendix


If you have any additional supporting documents, include them at the end. This could be market research data, resumes of key team members, legal documents, and supplier agreements.


## **How to hire your first roofing crew**


Your first employees can make or break the business. Bad installations and poor customer interactions damage your reputation fast — especially because roofing runs on referrals.


- **Start with your network.** Your contacts may know good installers looking for a change.
- **Hire for attitude as much as skill.** You can teach technique. You can’t teach work ethic or reliability.
- **Don’t rush.** Being short-staffed is painful. Hiring the wrong person is worse.
- **Pay competitively.** Great roofers have options. Offer fair pay, reasonable hours, and a culture where people actually want to show up.


## **Get the right tools and equipment**


Having the right equipment at your disposal is key to delivering high-quality work. You'll want to look at the tools you'll need — big and small — as well as software.


By tools, we mean...


- Trucks
- Trailers
- Hammers
- Nail guns
- Ladders, including a ladder hoist or something similar for moving shingles
- Safety equipment
- Generators and air compressors
- Shovels
- Pry bars
- Hammer tacker stapler
- Blades and/or utility knifes


Do your research on power tools, such as roofing nailers, to make sure you get the best one for the job. If you're starting off with experience in the industry, you probably have a preferred brand already.

## How to grow your roofing business once you're off the ground


Getting your first 10–20 jobs is a hustle play. Growing past that requires systems.


**Build a referral engine.** Ask every satisfied customer for a referral — most won’t offer without being asked.


**Generate leads online.** Google Business Profile is non-negotiable. Paid Google Ads can accelerate lead generation once you have cash flow.


**Track every lead.** If you’re not tracking where your jobs come from, you can’t double down on what’s working.


**Use your software to look professional.** Homeowners compare 3 quotes before hiring (Roofr 2026 Homeowner Survey). The contractor who sends a clean digital proposal wins more jobs.

## FAQs: starting and growing a roofing business


**How much does it cost to start a roofing business?**


Plan for $10,000–$50,000 to cover tools, equipment, insurance, licensing, a truck or trailer, and initial marketing. Some contractors start smaller by subcontracting out labor, which reduces upfront equipment costs significantly.


**Do you need a license to start a roofing company?**


It depends on your state. Regardless of licensing laws, carry general liability insurance and workers’ comp from day one. Check your state’s requirements through the SBA or your state licensing board.


**How do I find my first roofing customers?**


Start with your personal network. Set up your Google Business Profile immediately. Door-knocking in storm-affected neighborhoods is still a legitimate fast-start strategy in many markets.


**What software do new roofing companies need?**


At minimum: a CRM to track leads and jobs, a way to send professional proposals, and a payment collection tool. Roofr CRM combines all of this in one platform built specifically for roofing contractors.


**What’s the difference between starting and growing a roofing company?**


Starting is about getting the legal, operational, and financial foundation right. Growing is about systematizing lead generation, building referral pipelines, using software to track performance, and making strategic hires. What gets you the first 20 jobs won’t get you the next 200.
