## **What should a roofing proposal include?**


Just listing price and material type isn't enough. A good roofing proposal can achieve a lot. Even experienced contractors often leave money on the table, just by not taking a few minutes to add some basic details into their proposal template.


A complete roofing proposal should include:


- ‍ **Scope of work:** Be specific. Don't just write "reroof." Describe the job, how many layers, whether decking is included, clean up, all of it. The more detailed you are, the fewer surprises for the customer. **‍**
- **Materials breakdown:** List the specific products: shingle brand and line, underlayment type, ice and water shield coverage, ridge cap, ventilation. Homeowners may not know what a synthetic underlayment is, but seeing it listed reassures them you do. **‍**
- **Measurements:** Include[roof measurements](https://roofr.com/measurements) and dimensions in the proposal too. Homeowners love it, it signals accuracy and professionalism, and it stops competitors from lowballing you based on a vague scope.
- **Payment terms:** Clear line-item pricing is better than one big lump sum. Break out labor, materials, disposal, and add-ons. Include your payment schedule. **‍**
- **Warranty info:** List the manufacturer warranty and your workmanship warranty. Be specific about what's covered and for how long. **‍**
- **Project timeline:** An estimated start date and project duration (even if it's a range) sets expectations and shows you're organized. It only takes one bad past experience for a homeowner to be worried about timeline commitments, so it's best get ahead of it. **‍**
- **Terms and conditions:** Protect yourself. Include what happens with unforeseen damage. List cancellation terms and a dispute resolution process. Get a lawyer to draft this once, then use it on every document. **‍**
- **E-signature capability:** The faster a homeowner can say yes, the better. Don't make homeowners print, sign, scan, and email back.[Use e-signature](https://roofr.com/pdf-signer) .

## **Frequently asked roofing proposal questions**


#### **What should a roofing proposal include?**


A complete roofing proposal should include: a detailed scope of work, a specific materials breakdown, roof dimensions, itemized pricing, payment schedule, project timeline, warranty details, and terms and conditions. It can also include photos or mockups, additional business info, e-signature capability, and more.


#### **What's the difference between a roofing estimate and a roofing proposal?**


An estimate provides a rough idea of what a job might cost. A proposal is more complete. It outlines the full scope of work, materials, pricing, and terms. The proposal is usually what the homeowner signs to approve the work in writing. In practice, you'll want to use an estimate to qualify a lead, then a full proposal to close. Roofr's[Instant Estimate](https://roofr.com/estimator) tool helps a lot for that early qualification step.


#### **Is a roofing proposal the same as a roofing contract?**


It's up to you whether you want these to be two separate documents or not. A roofing proposal outlines the work and what it costs. A roofing contract is a legally binding agreement, including the full terms and conditions of the job. Many roofers combine both into one document.


#### **How do I send a digital roofing proposal?**


To send a digital roofing proposal, create the proposal in a platform like Roofr, then send a link via email or SMS text message. The homeowner can view, ask questions, and sign from their phone.


#### **How do I make my roofing proposals stand out from competitors?**


A few things separate a winning roofing proposal from a forgettable one: detailed, specific scope of work, a clear materials breakdown, professional formatting and your company branding, clear warranty info, and an easy signing experience.


#### **How quickly should I send a roofing proposal after a site visit?**


Same day is the gold standard. Response speed is[one of the top factors](https://roofr.com/blog/speed-to-lead-in-roofing) in winning residential roofing jobs. If a homeowner gets three quotes, the first trustworthy-looking proposal they get is most often they one they'll go with. Digital proposal tools make that same-day turnaround very doable.


#### **How to follow up on a digital roofing proposal?**


This is an important step not to miss. Don't just send a proposal and forget about it. Check in with the customer after 12 to 24 hours, or use[automated email/SMS follow-ups](https://roofr.com/masterclass/amazing-automations-user-takeover) after the proposal is sent. That gentle push will often win a roofer the job. Contractors can lose a lot of potential work just by not following up.
