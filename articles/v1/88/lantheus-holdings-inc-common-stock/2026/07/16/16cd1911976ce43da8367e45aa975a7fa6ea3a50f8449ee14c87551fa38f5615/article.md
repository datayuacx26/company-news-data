# Finding a new normal: Leah’s journey with Alzheimer’s

### Before Alzheimer’s entered the conversation, Leah’s life was defined by purpose and supporting others. But just when she felt settled, things started to slip. The independence that was once a hallmark of Leah’s personality began to fade. Something was changing, even before Leah, or her husband Steve, had words for it.


#### **When something felt different**


The first signs were quiet. The changes were easy to explain away as stress or being busy. But Leah knew the difference between a hard day and something more. For Steve, the hardest part was not understanding what he was seeing. The woman who had always kept life moving was asking questions that did not feel like her, and neither of them had a clear answer.


#### Finding answers


The road to answers was long and exhausting. Doctor visits stacked up, referrals led to dead ends and more tests. When imaging finally showed a clearer picture of what was happening, it brought a complicated kind of relief. It was not good news, but it was real. The diagnosis also came with grief, having to step away from the work that had given Leah’s life purpose for so long.


#### Living with purpose


After the diagnosis, Leah had to rebuild her sense of purpose in a life she did not choose. For Leah, this has meant being supported without being overpowered and being seen as a whole person who can still live fully.


Leah’s message is simple: do not be afraid of finding out. Answers can be difficult, but they can also bring a sense of direction, helping people understand what is happening, consider what comes next and begin building support around the life they want to keep living. In that clarity, there can be room for dignity, connection and hope for the future.
