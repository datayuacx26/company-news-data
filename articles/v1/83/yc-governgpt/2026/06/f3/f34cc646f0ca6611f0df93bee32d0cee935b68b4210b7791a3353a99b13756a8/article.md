## What is RFP Automation for Asset Managers?


When a limited partner reviews a fund, they send a questionnaire. It might be an RFP, a DDQ, or an ILPA template. These documents can run hundreds of questions deep, covering investment strategy, compliance history, and firm operations.


For lean IR teams, completing these manually means digging through old submissions, coordinating across departments, and hoping nothing is out of date before it ships. Slow, error-prone work that pulls analysts away from higher-value activity.[AI adoption at institutional asset managers](https://www.cerulli.com/press-releases/ai-adoption-at-institutional-asset-managers-accelerates-as-rfp-teams-seek-greater-efficiency) has accelerated as teams seek faster turnaround in their RFP workflows.


RFP automation software changes that. These tools centralize approved content into a searchable repository, auto-populate responses from past submissions, and keep compliance oversight intact so every answer stays traceable. The better ones also track how data changes over time, preventing outdated figures from appearing in live submissions.

## DiligenceVault


DiligenceVault is a data management and RFP automation tool built for institutional asset managers. It focuses on DDQ and RFP workflows, giving investor relations teams a centralized content library they can draw from when responding to allocator requests.


The tool is well-regarded for its structured data approach, allowing firms to maintain version-controlled answers across multiple funds and strategies. For IR teams managing a high volume of LP requests, this kind of organization reduces the risk of sending outdated or inconsistent information.


That said, DiligenceVault is primarily a content repository with workflow features layered on top. Firms looking for AI-driven draft generation or deep integration with internal investment data may find its capabilities limited compared to more purpose-built RFP automation tools.


## Dasseti


Dasseti is a due diligence and RFP automation tool built for asset managers and allocators. It covers the full DDQ and RFP response workflow, with a content library, version control, and audit trails that help investor relations teams manage complex questionnaires at scale.


Where Dasseti stands out is in its focus on the allocator side of the equation. It's designed for both asset managers responding to RFPs and institutional investors sending them, which makes it a reasonable fit for firms that sit on both sides of the table.


That said, Dasseti is better known for its due diligence workflows than for AI-driven response generation, so firms focused on speed and automation depth may find it less capable than purpose-built RFP tools.


## Responsive


Responsive is an enterprise RFP automation tool built for high-volume response workflows. It uses AI to search your content library, suggest relevant answers, and auto-populate questionnaires based on prior submissions. The tool tracks which team members own which question types and routes drafts accordingly, keeping approval workflows centralized.


Where Responsive works well is in firms that handle hundreds of RFPs annually and need a structured system to prevent duplicated effort. The AI learns from your corrections over time, improving match accuracy as your library grows. Integration with Salesforce and other CRMs keeps your pipeline data synchronized.


That said, Responsive wasn't built for asset managers. It lacks DDQ-focused logic, fund data integrations, and the compliance controls that IR teams managing institutional capital typically require. Firms report needing extensive customization to handle SEC disclosures, performance attribution, and regulatory language that changes quarterly.

## Loopio


Loopio is a well-known RFP response tool built for enterprise sales teams, though asset managers have started adopting it for investor-facing workflows. It offers a content library, response automation, and team collaboration features that help IR teams respond to RFPs and DDQs faster.


The tool works best for firms that handle high volumes of templated requests and need a central repository to manage approved language across teams. Its search and tagging system helps writers pull relevant prior answers quickly.


That said, Loopio's roots in sales instead of investment management mean it lacks purpose-built DDQ logic, compliance controls, and the fund-specific data integrations that asset managers typically require.
