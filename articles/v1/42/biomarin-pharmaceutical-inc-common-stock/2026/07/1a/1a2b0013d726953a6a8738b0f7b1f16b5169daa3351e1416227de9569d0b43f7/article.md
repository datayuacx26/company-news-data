# Megan Bull’s Childhood Empathy Fuels a Career of Impact

## **ASPIRATION**


How did you get your start in the biopharma industry? What do you aspire to accomplish through your work at BioMarin and in your career?


My path to biopharma started with both curiosity and personal connection. I was always drawn to science, whether that meant asking for a frog dissection kit as a birthday gift or testing whether a human pregnancy test would work on my pregnant dog. But my childhood best friend’s experience with cystic fibrosis gave that curiosity a deeper purpose.


I saw the incredible impact physicians and care teams could have, but I also dreamed of contributing to solutions that could reach and help more people. That ambition led me to study pharmaceutical chemistry, with a minor in technology management, at the University of California, Davis.


While at UC Davis, I attended a career fair where I learned about BioMarin and its mission. It closely mirrored my own interest in using science to make a meaningful difference for people living with rare conditions. After graduating in 2018, I set my sights on BioMarin, and in 2019, I officially joined the team.


Today, I support scientific discovery by developing robust assays, creating bioanalytical strategies, validating methods and helping programs move from early research toward regulatory milestones. Across that work, I am motivated by the possibility of helping bring meaningful therapies to people who need them.


My commitment to impact also extends beyond the laboratory. As founder and president of Reclaim Foundation, a nonprofit organization, I help support survivors of trauma as they foster healing, growth and empowerment through connection and community. That work was inspired by my own experience overcoming traumatic events.


At my core, I believe scientific innovation and human compassion are not mutually exclusive. My aspiration is to continue growing as a scientist and leader who can bridge rigorous data, patient empathy and strategic decision-making to support therapies that may improve lives.
