# Cristin Hubbard Reflects on a Career Spent Bringing Innovation to Patients – and Why Working at BioMarin Has Her More Energized Than Ever

## **In your view, where are BioMarin’s greatest strengths, and what is an area of opportunity as the company continues to evolve?**


I think BioMarin sits in a bit of a sweet spot, both in terms of our size and our leadership in rare disease.


We have the resources to make a meaningful impact at scale, while maintaining the agility to move quickly, make decisions, and stay close to patients and the communities we serve. That combination is part of what sets us apart.


One of our defining strengths as a leader in rare disease is our global footprint. We currently reach patients in about 80 countries, with a meaningful presence that often involves navigating complex healthcare systems and enabling access where traditional rare disease infrastructure may not exist. That’s a capability we’ve developed over many years, and it’s highly distinctive.


Another key strength is our ability to help identify people who may benefit from our medicines. Many rare conditions are underdiagnosed or misdiagnosed, making prevalence difficult to understand. We’ve invested in genomics, diagnostics and data analytics, and we’re continuing to explore how AI and machine learning can help identify potential patients earlier. It’s an area where we’ve built deep expertise and continue to raise the bar to create impact across multiple conditions.


That combination of global reach, specialized capabilities, and purpose-driven focus is what I see as BioMarin’s unique proposition today.


At the same time, as innovation in rare disease has advanced, the landscape has become more competitive. It’s not enough to simply be first to market. The standard is higher, and our focus must be on delivering the best possible outcomes for patients.


Our opportunity is to continue leading in rare disease because our work genuinely changes lives. That goes beyond scientific innovation, which is of course critical. But one of our biggest advantages comes from deeply understanding the patient communities we serve and ensuring we’re delivering in the most meaningful way possible.


## **BioMarin completed its acquisition of Amicus Therapeutics at the end of April. How are your teams approaching the integration, and what do you see as the greatest opportunity now that the two companies have come together?**


It’s about maintaining momentum while expanding impact.


First and foremost, we want to continue the strong work already underway and support the Fabry and Pompe communities without disruption. But beyond that, I’m really excited about what we can add. We have capabilities – particularly in terms of identifying patients and leveraging our global footprint – that can help bring these therapies to more people around the world. The opportunity to expand access and reach new communities is incredibly motivating.


## **What’s your approach to leadership?**


My role is to unlock the potential in people.


That means removing obstacles, creating clarity and sometimes reminding people of their own strengths – especially when they don’t see them themselves. It also means putting the right leaders in place at the right time for what we’re trying to achieve.


I often think about the kind of leader I want to be remembered as. For me, it’s someone who is willing to fight to do the right thing. And I say “fights” intentionally, because doing the right thing isn’t always the easiest path.


It’s about having the courage to speak up, to make hard decisions and to stay committed above all else to patients, science, purpose and our people. And just as importantly, it’s about creating an environment where people feel safe to bring their full selves to work. That, to me, is when you really unlock what teams are capable of.
