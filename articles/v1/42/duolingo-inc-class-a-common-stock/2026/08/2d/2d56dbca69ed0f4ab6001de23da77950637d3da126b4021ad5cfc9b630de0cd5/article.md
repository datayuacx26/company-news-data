# How to use the verb “to be” in English

The English verb *to be* is one of the most important verbs for learners to know. It has many different uses, including as a helping verb, and its conjugation is very irregular. There’s a lot to learn!


Here’s a complete guide to using the verb *to be* in English.

## What is the purpose of the verb *to be* ?


In English, to be


is used for a lot of different meanings, such as:


- **Describing:** Lily is serious.


My shirt is blue.


- **Locating:** Eddy is at home.


Your phone is on the table.


- **Identifying:** Junior is Eddy’s son.


My name is Bea.


- and many more!


## How to conjugate the verb *to be*


*To be* is challenging because it’s an[irregular verb](https://blog.duolingo.com/list-of-verbs/) , which means you will need to memorize the different conjugations. Luckily, there are some tips and tricks to make it easier to learn!


### Simple present


The[simple present](https://blog.duolingo.com/simple-present/) forms of *to be* are the first forms you need to know. As you can see in the table below, the verb’s forms are the same for the subjects *you* , *we* , and *they* . However, for the subjects *I* and *he* / *she* / *it* , the conjugation changes:


SIMPLE PRESENT CONJUGATION EXAMPLE


I am


I am happy.


you (singular) are


You are my brother.


he/she/it is


It is Monday today.


we are


We are at school right now.


you (plural) are


You are my friends.


they are


They are on the floor.


When using a present tense form of the verb *to be* , it is common in writing and speaking to combine[the pronoun](https://blog.duolingo.com/subject-pronouns-in-english/) and the verb[to form a contraction](https://blog.duolingo.com/apostrophes-in-english/) :


SIMPLE PRESENT CONTRACTION EXAMPLE


I am ➡️ I’m


I’m happy.


you are ➡️ you’re


You’re my brother.


he/she/it is ➡️ he’s


/ she’s


/ it’s


It’s Monday today.


we are ➡️ we’re


We’re at school right now.


you are ➡️ you’re


You’re my friends.


they are ➡️ they’re


They’re on the floor.


### Simple past


The verb *to be* in the[simple past](https://blog.duolingo.com/simple-past-verbs-in-english/) looks different from the present tense, but it actually follows a similar pattern! In the simple past, *to be* takes the same form for the subjects *you* , *we* , and *they* . Meanwhile, the form changes for the subjects *I* and *he* / *she* / *it* :


SIMPLE PAST CONJUGATION EXAMPLE


I was


I was in Paris yesterday.


you (singular) were


You were hungry before breakfast.


he/she/it was


It was a cold day.


we were


We were at the store.


you (plural) were


You were all bored.


they were


They were asleep at midnight.


💡


💡 ****Memory tip:**** The third person singular ( **he** / **she** / **it** ) conjugations end in **-s** for both present and past: **i** *****s***** and **wa** *****s***** . This is similar to regular verbs, like **he walk** *****s***** and **it say** *****s***** .


### Negation of simple present and simple past


To make a negative sentence with the verb *to be* in the simple present or simple past, add the word not


after the form of *to be* :


SIMPLE PRESENT NEGATION EXAMPLE


I **am not** I am not at home.


you (singular) **are not** You are not sick.


he/she/it **is not** It is not true.


we **are not** We are not best friends.


you (plural) **are not** You are not hungry.


they **are not** They are not my teachers.


SIMPLE PAST NEGATION EXAMPLE


I **was not** I was not at home.


you (singular) **were not** You were not sick.


he/she/it **was not** It was not true.


we **were not** We were not best friends.


you (plural) **were not** You were not hungry.


they **were not** They were not my teachers.


Like affirmative sentences, negative sentences often use a contraction. In the simple past, there’s only one contracted negative form:


CONTRACTED SIMPLE PAST NEGATION EXAMPLE


I **wasn’t** I wasn’t at home.


you (singular) **weren’t** You weren’t sick.


he/she/it **wasn’t** It wasn’t true.


we **weren’t** We weren’t best friends.


you (plural) **weren’t** You weren’t hungry.


they **weren’t** They weren’t my teachers.


In the simple present, however, most sentences have two possible negative contractions. The only exception are sentences with a[first person singular subject](https://blog.duolingo.com/first-person-second-person-third-person/) ( *I* ), which only have one negative contraction ( *I’m not* ):


CONTRACTED SIMPLE PRESENT NEGATION EXAMPLE


I **’m not** I’m not at home.


you (singular) **aren’t** /
**’re not** You aren’t sick.


/
You’re not sick.

**’s not** It isn’t true.


/
It’s not true.

**’re not** You aren’t hungry.


/
You’re not hungry.


they **aren’t** /

### Simple future


The[simple future](https://blog.duolingo.com/future-will-going-to/#the-future-with-will) in English is formed with the[modal verb](https://blog.duolingo.com/types-of-verbs/#modal-verbs) *will* followed by the[infinitive](https://blog.duolingo.com/infinitive-bare-form-english/) *to be* . Since modals don’t ever change based on the subject of the sentence, the simple future of *to be* is the same for all subjects: will be


!


SIMPLE FUTURE CONJUGATION EXAMPLE


I **will be** I will be home tomorrow.


you (singular) **will be** You will be on vacation next week.


he/she/it **will be** She will be here soon.


we **will be** We will be tired after the race.


you (plural) **will be** You will be the winners.


they **will be** They will be busy this weekend.


### Conditional


To form conditional verbs in English, use *would* + the[bare form](https://blog.duolingo.com/types-of-verbs/#infinitives-and-bare-forms-of-verbs) of the verb. So the conditional of *to be* is always would be


! 🎉


CONDITIONAL CONJUGATION EXAMPLE


I **would be** I would be the best dog owner ever!


you (singular) **would be** You would be so happy with a million dollars.


he/she/it **would be** He would be scared of that movie.


we **would be** We would be cold in Antarctica.


you (plural) **would be** You would be in a band.


they **would be** They would be tourists.


## Present perfect


The present perfect is created by using a present tense form of *to have* + the[past participle](https://blog.duolingo.com/uses-of-participles/) *been* . Since *been* is the past participle for all conjugations, you just need to make sure that *to have* agrees with the subject. For the third person singular ( *he* / *she* / *it* ), you’ll get has been


, and all other subjects will get have been


.


PRESENT PERFECT CONJUGATION EXAMPLE


I **have been** I have been to London twice.


you (singular) **have been** You have been here since lunch.


he/she/it **has been** He has been awake for three hours.


we **have been** We have been on a trip all week.


you (plural) **have been** You have been married for a month.


they **have been** They have been gone since yesterday.


💡


💡 ****Memory tip:**** The third person singular ( **he** / **she** / **it** ) form **ha** *****s***** ends in **-s** , just like **i** *****s***** **, wa** *****s***** , and regular verbs, like **he walk** *****s***** and **it say** *****s***** .


### Past perfect


The English past perfect is formed from the past tense of *to have* (which is always *had* ) + the[past participle](https://blog.duolingo.com/uses-of-participles/) *been* . That means you need just one form for all subjects: had been


!


PAST PERFECT CONJUGATION EXAMPLE


I **had been** I had been busy.


you (singular) **had been** You had been to China already.


he/she/it **had been** She had been gone all morning.


we **had been** We had been at work.


you (plural) **had been** You had been on vacation.


they **had been** They had been on a walk.


### Future perfect


The future perfect is another tense where every form of *to be* is the same. 🙌 This tense is made of the[modal verb](https://blog.duolingo.com/types-of-verbs/#modal-verbs) *will* + the[bare form](https://blog.duolingo.com/types-of-verbs/#infinitives-and-bare-forms-of-verbs) *have* + the[past participle](https://blog.duolingo.com/uses-of-participles/) *been* : will have been


.


FUTURE PERFECT CONJUGATION EXAMPLE


I **will have been** I will have been a professor for a year.


you (singular) **will have been** You will have been to the beach every week.


he/she/it **will have been** It will have been windy all day.


we **will have been** We will have been partners for a week.


you (plural) **will have been** You will have been here for two hours.


they **will have been** They will have been students for 10 years.


### Conditional perfect


The conditional perfect in English also uses the same form for all subjects: the[modal verb](https://blog.duolingo.com/types-of-verbs/#modal-verbs) *would* + the[bare form](https://blog.duolingo.com/types-of-verbs/#infinitives-and-bare-forms-of-verbs) *have* +[past participle](https://blog.duolingo.com/uses-of-participles/) *been* : would have been


.


CONDITIONAL PERFECT CONJUGATION EXAMPLE


I **would have been** I would have been so sad.


you (singular) **would have been** You would have been a doctor.


he/she/it **would have been** She would have been famous.


we **would have been** We would have been more excited.


you (plural) **would have been** You would have been richer.


they **would have been** They would have been bored.


### Negation of simple future, conditional, and perfect tenses


For the tenses that are made from multiple words, put *not* after the first word to make the sentence negative.


🙂‍↕️ AFFIRMATIVE 🙂‍↔️ NEGATIVE


She has been in the living room.


➡️ She has not been in the living room.


They will have been here for a year.


➡️ They will not have been here for a year.


I had been to India.


➡️ I had not been to India.


We would have been bored.


➡️ We would not have been bored.


You will be interested in this book.


➡️ You will not be interested in this book.


## Auxiliary verb *to be*


All of the previous examples showed *to be* as a main verb of the sentence, where the word *be* hasa specific meaning (like “to cost” or “to be located”).


However, *to be* can also be used as[an auxiliary verb](https://blog.duolingo.com/types-of-verbs/#auxiliary-verbs) that helps form other tenses. In these cases, *to be* appears together with another verb. You’ll see this happen for progressive tenses and the passive voice. Here are some examples:


Sentences in one of the progressive tenses (the[present progressive](https://blog.duolingo.com/progressive-verbs/) , the past progressive, and the future progressive) contain a form of *be* and a present participle:


SUBJECT ➕ FORM OF *BE* ➕ PRESENT PARTICIPLE


I ➕ am ➕ studying


🟰


I am studying.


SUBJECT ➕ FORM OF *BE* ➕ PRESENT PARTICIPLE


You ➕ have been ➕ studying


🟰


You have been studying.


SUBJECT ➕ FORM OF *BE* ➕ PRESENT PARTICIPLE


They ➕ would have been ➕ studying


🟰


They would have been studying.


Sentences in the passive voice use a form of *be* together with a past participle:


SUBJECT ➕ FORM OF *BE* ➕ PAST PARTICIPLE


She ➕ had been ➕ hired


🟰


She had been hired.


SUBJECT ➕ FORM OF *BE* ➕ PAST PARTICIPLE


You ➕ will have been ➕ hired


🟰


You will have been hired.


SUBJECT ➕ FORM OF *BE* ➕ PAST PARTICIPLE


They ➕ were ➕ hired


🟰


They were hired.
