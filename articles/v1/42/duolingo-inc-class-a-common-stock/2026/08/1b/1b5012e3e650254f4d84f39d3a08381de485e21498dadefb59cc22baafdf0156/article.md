# How does the Duolingo English Test measure reading?

Reading is a complex cognitive process. As we read, we recognize vocabulary and grammar, connect ideas across sentences, interpret unfamiliar words from context, and gradually build meaning from an entire text.


We also adapt our reading strategy depending on our purpose—sometimes skimming for the main idea, sometimes scanning for a specific detail, and sometimes reading carefully to understand a complex argument. In language assessment literature, these are recognized as careful versus expeditious reading strategies, both of which are essential for academic success.


Succeeding in[English-medium higher education](https://bit.ly/3XDzpML?ref=blog.englishtest.duolingo.com) requires students to navigate dense textbooks, evaluate scholarly papers, and digest complex expository prose across multiple academic domains ([Cardwell et al., 2024a](https://utppublishing.com/doi/10.1558/cj.27104?ref=blog.englishtest.duolingo.com) ). Historically, standardized admissions tests measured reading as a monolithic skill - presenting long passages followed by a list of multiple-choice questions.


However, modern applied linguistics has shown us that academic reading is far from passive. It is a highly active, multi-layered orchestration of cognitive processes ([Grabe, W., & Jiang, X. 2024](https://www.wiley.com/en-us/shop/general-introductory-linguistics/the-concise-companion-to-language-assessment-p-9781394179619?ref=blog.englishtest.duolingo.com) ). To capture this reality, the[Duolingo English Test](https://bit.ly/3D7EOoA?ref=blog.englishtest.duolingo.com) (DET) measures reading proficiency through a series of complementary task types.


## What reading skills does the Duolingo English Test measure?


Current research views reading as a collection of closely connected skills rather than a single ability. Successful readers must balance bottom-up linguistic resources with top-down cognitive processes. They recognize vocabulary and grammatical structures, identify how ideas are organized, distinguish main ideas from supporting details, make inferences, and integrate information across an entire passage.


The DET reflects this comprehensive understanding by assessing a broad range of reading[subskills](https://blog.englishtest.duolingo.com/det-subscores/) and processes across two primary focus areas: bottom-up linguistic automaticity and top-down skill mastery, including:


- reading for gist (skimming)
- locating specific information (scanning)
- understanding vocabulary in context
- making inferences from contextual clues
- recognizing text organization and coherence at the discourse level
- using grammatical and lexical knowledge to decode local sentence meanings.


Together, these skills provide a comprehensive picture of reading proficiency.


## Different reading skills require different tasks


Because no single task can capture every aspect of reading, the DET combines four complementary task types that each contribute different evidence about a test taker's reading ability.


### Interactive Reading


Interactive Reading is the cornerstone of the DET's reading assessment, designed to assess general and academic reading comprehension across different communicative purposes.


Rather than asking unrelated questions about multiple passages, the task gradually reveals a single narrative or expository text through a sequence of activities. Test takers complete missing sentences, answer comprehension questions, identify the main idea, and select the best title.


As the test taker completes each sub-task, the passage expands, and correct answers to previous steps are automatically filled in, regardless of whether the test taker answered correctly. This provides implicit corrective feedback, enabling test takers to resolve errors and construct an accurate mental model of the discourse before answering higher-order comprehension and synthesis questions.


Using one passage to measure multiple reading skills creates a more complete picture of how readers develop understanding as they move through a text.


### C-Test


The C-Test consists of a short text representing authentic genres from academic, professional, and public domains. The first and last sentences of the text are fully intact, while alternating words in the intervening sentences are "damaged" by deleting their second half. Test takers must type the missing letters to reconstruct these words.


Completing these missing words requires readers to coordinate vocabulary knowledge, grammar, and contextual understanding at the same time. Research has consistently shown that C-Tests provide strong evidence of overall reading proficiency because they engage multiple language processes simultaneously.


### Vocabulary in Context


[Academic reading](https://blog.englishtest.duolingo.com/what-is-academic-english/) depends on understanding what words mean and how they function within particular contexts.


Vocabulary in Context measures the breadth, depth, and productive fluency of vocabulary knowledge by asking test takers to complete sentences using contextual clues. Success depends on recognizing collocations, grammatical relationships, synonyms, antonyms, and subtle differences in meaning.


### Yes/No Vocabulary


The DET measures receptive vocabulary knowledge through a rapid vocabulary recognition task where test takers see one word at a time on the screen and must select "Yes" (for a real English word) or "No" (for a fake word) within 5 seconds.


These brief items efficiently estimate vocabulary breadth across proficiency levels while also helping calibrate the adaptive portion of the test, allowing subsequent reading tasks to be targeted to each test taker's proficiency.


## Reading tasks should reflect how students actually read


Reading proficiency develops through the interaction of many different skills. Good reading assessments should capture that complexity.


Students in English-medium universities encounter many different types of reading every day. They read textbooks, research articles, lecture slides, assignment instructions, emails, and online resources. Each text requires a different combination of reading strategies.


The DET reflects these academic demands by measuring both careful and expeditious reading, local understanding of words and sentences, and global understanding of complete texts. Its adaptive design also ensures that readers across the proficiency spectrum receive appropriately challenging, academically relevant material while measuring the same underlying construct of reading proficiency.


By combining Interactive Reading, C-Test, Vocabulary in Context, and Yes/No Vocabulary, the DET measures readers’ linguistic resources and skills needed to construct meaning across different kinds of texts and different reading purposes. Together, these tasks provide a richer picture of the reading abilities students need to succeed in English-medium academic environments.
