# 8 slang words you’ll hear in France

It’s always good to know[a little bit of French](https://blog.duolingo.com/french-phrases-for-travel/) before you travel to France, but if you really want to level up and connect with locals, understanding slang is a must!


Here are 8 words you’re sure to hear in France.


## Pote


**Meaning:** friend
**Textbook equivalent:** ami


Pote


is one of the most common ways to say *friend* in everyday French. It’s less ambiguous than copain


and copine


, which can mean *friend* , but also *boyfriend* or *girlfriend* in a romantic sense.


Qu’est-ce que t’as fait hier soir ?


What’d you do last night?


Pas grand-chose, je suis sortie avec quelques potes.


Not much, I went out with a few friends.


## Meuf


**Meaning:** woman, girl
**Textbook equivalent:** femme


Meuf


icomes from French’s unique[verlan](https://blog.duolingo.com/verlan-french-slang-words/) system—in other words, it’s *femme* (woman) backwards!


⚠ Warning: Depending on the context, *meuf* can be perceived as disrespectful, so use this term with care!


Tu aimes ces chaussures ?


Do you like these shoes?


Oui, mais je préfère les baskets de cette meuf.


Yes, but I prefer that woman’s sneakers.


## Mec


**Meaning:** man, guy
**Textbook equivalent:** homme


Although it doesn’t come from verlan, you can think of mec


as the male equivalent of *meuf* , and you’ll often hear them used in similar contexts!


Il y a combien d’élèves dans ton cours de français ?


How many students are in your French class?


On est cinq : deux mecs et trois meufs.


There are five of us: two guys and three girls.


## Bosser


**Meaning:** to work
**Textbook equivalent:** travailler


Who needs *travailler* when you can talk about working in a cooler, more casual way with bosser


? 😎


💡 Pro tip: Use the slang noun, boulot


( *job* or *work* ), along with *bosser* to sound even more in-the-know!


T’as bossé aujourd’hui ? On est samedi !


Did you work today? It’s Saturday!


Je sais, mais j’ai énormément de boulot en ce moment.


I know, but I have tons of work right now.


## Kiffer


**Meaning:** to like, to enjoy
**Textbook equivalent:** aimer


, adorer


The verb kiffer


comes from the Arabic word كيف


(kayf) meaning *pleasure* or *enjoyment* . Due to a long history of contact, colonization, and immigration, French has gotten hundreds of words from Arabic!


T’en as pensé quoi du film ?


What did you think of the movie?


J’ai kiffé, c’était trop drôle !


I loved it, it was so funny!


## Blindé


**Meaning:** crowded (also: rich!)
**Textbook equivalent:** bondé


, rempli


(or riche


)


The literal meaning of blindé


is *armored* or *bulletproof* , like an armored car ( voiture blindée


). In everyday French, it’s often used to describe a place that’s packed with people—like a Parisian park on a summer night!


💡 Pro tip: *Blindé* has another slang meaning: *rich* or *loaded* . So if you ever hear someone use this word in reference to a person, you can assume they’re talking about money, not crowds!


Il y a nulle part pour s’asseoir !


There’s nowhere to sit!


Je sais, le métro est toujours blindé le lundi matin.


I know, the metro is always packed on Monday mornings.


## Bouffe


**Meaning:** food
**Textbook equivalent:** nourriture


You’ll often hear bouffe


, as well as its verb form bouffer


(to eat), in informal conversations among friends


⚠ Warning: While the word isn’t necessarily negative, *bouffe* implies a casual attitude towards the food you’re discussing, so avoid using it when commenting on someone else’s cooking!


On commande quelque chose à bouffer ?


Should we order something to eat?


Oui, la bouffe a l’air bonne ici !


Yes, the food here looks good!


## Crevé


**Meaning:** tired
**Textbook equivalent:** fatigué


The adjective crevé


literally means *burst* or *punctured* , like a flat tire—a perfect metaphor to describe how you might feel after a long day of sightseeing!


Julie fait une fête chez elle ce soir, tu veux y aller ?


Julie is having a party at her place tonight, do you want to go?


Non, je suis complètement crevé !


No, I’m absolutely exhausted!


## You’re gonna *kiffer* French slang!


Keep an ear out for these words in France—with enough practice, your French slang might help you make new French *potes* !
