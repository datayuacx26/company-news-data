## What is the English present tense and when do you use it?


The simple present tense is used for:


**1. Actions that happen regularly or habitually**


I do laundry on Wednesdays.


We watch a movie once a week.


Eddy goes to the gym every day.


**2. General truths and stative verbs (for example *want* , *think* , and *have* )**


They never eat meat.


Junior loves ice cream.


I have a sister.


**3. Scheduled events**


My train arrives at 5 o’clock.


That new bookstore opens this weekend.


The wedding is at noon tomorrow.


## How do you conjugate English verbs in the simple present?


Luckily, verbs in the simple present tense mostly stay the same no matter[the subject](https://blog.duolingo.com/subject-pronouns-in-english/) of the sentence. In fact, for the subjects *I* , *you* , *we* , and *they* , you use the base form of the verb to form the simple present.


However, there is one important exception:


💡


When using the[third person singular](https://blog.duolingo.com/first-person-second-person-third-person/) ( **he/she/it** ), an *****-s***** is added to the end of the base verb.


For example:


SUBJECT VERB
*to work*


I work


you work


he/she/it works


we work


you (plural) work


they work


### Spelling changes for the third person singular


There are special spelling rules that must be followed for certain verbs in the third person singular ( *he/she/it* ).


💡


When a verb ends with **-ch** , **-o** , **-sh** , **-ss** , **-x** , **or -zz** , add *****-es***** instead of just **-s.**


SUBJECT EXAMPLE


I, you, we, they I brush my teeth.


he/she/it He brushes his teeth.


### Pronunciation of the third person singular


The pronunciation of the third person singular *-s* can be tricky because it changes depending on the sound that comes before it. (Remember to think about the last **sound** and not the last letter—they[won’t always be the same in English](https://blog.duolingo.com/why-is-english-spelling-so-tricky/) !)


If a verb ends in a *voiced sound* (one in which you vibrate your vocal folds, such as “b”), the *-s* at the end of the word is pronounced like the “z” in zoo


.


If a verb ends in a *voiceless sound* (one in which you don’t vibrate your vocal folds, like “p”), the *-s* at the end of the word is pronounced like the “s” in sun


.


Finally, if a verb ends in a *sibilant sound* (such as “s”), the third person singular ending is a whole syllable, pronounced “-iz”, like the word is


.

### The verb *to be* in the simple present tense


The verb[to be](https://blog.duolingo.com/how-to-use-the-verb-to-be-in-english/) is irregular in the simple present tense:


SUBJECT FORM OF *TO BE* EXAMPLES


I am


I am happy.


you (singular) are


You **are** my brother.


he/she/it is


It is Monday today.


we are


We **are** at school right now.


you (plural) are


You **are** my friends.


they are


They **are** on the floor.


### Negation of the simple present tense


Negative sentences in the simple present are formed as follows:


Do not


is used for the subjects *I* , *you* , *we* , and *they* , and does not


is used for the subjects *he/she/it* .


💡


****Note:**** In negative sentences, you won’t add **-s** to the base form of the verb for third person singular.


SUBJECT NEGATIVE SIMPLE PRESENT EXAMPLE


I do not + base verb I do not watch TV.


you do not + base verb You do not watch TV.


he/she/it **does not** + base verb She does not watch TV.


we do not + base verb We do not watch TV.


you (plural) do not + base verb You do not watch TV.


they do not + base verb They do not watch TV.


In addition, *do not* is often contracted to form the word don’t


, and *does not* is often contracted to form the word doesn’t


.


SUBJECT NEGATIVE SIMPLE PRESENT WITH CONTRACTION EXAMPLE


I don’t + base verb I don’t like winter.


you don’t + base verb You don’t like winter.


he/she/it **doesn’t** + base verb She doesn’t like winter.


we don’t + base verb We don’t like winter.


you (plural) don’t + base verb You don’t like winter.


they don’t + base verb They don’t like winter.


### Questions in the simple present tense


English has different rules for forming[different types of questions](https://blog.duolingo.com/all-about-english-questions/) , and there are present tense versions of each question type!


**Questions with *to be***


💡


When asking questions with the verb **to be** , use the present tense form of **be** that matches the subject.


In[yes/no questions with to be](https://blog.duolingo.com/english-yes-or-no-questions/#yesno-questions-with-auxiliaries-modals-and-forms-of-the-verb-be) , the subject follows the form of *to be* :


QUESTION Are you hungry?


ANSWER Yes, I’m hungry.


[Wh -questions with to be](https://blog.duolingo.com/what-are-wh-questions/#%E2%80%9Cwh%E2%80%9D-questions-with-auxiliaries-modals-and-forms-of-the-verb-be) begin with a question word, followed by a form of *to be* and the subject:


QUESTION When is the concert?


ANSWER The concert is on Friday.


**Questions with other verbs**


💡


For questions with verbs other than **to be** , use the auxiliary verb *****do***** (for **I** , **you** , **we** , **they** ) or *****does***** (for **he** / **she** / **it** ) along with the base form of the main verb.


[Yes/no questions with other verbs](https://blog.duolingo.com/english-yes-or-no-questions/#yesno-questions-with-a-form-of-do) begin with *do* / *does* , followed by the subject and the base verb:


QUESTION Does Junior like ice cream?


ANSWER Yes, Junior likes ice cream.


[Wh -questions with other verbs](https://blog.duolingo.com/what-are-wh-questions/#%E2%80%9Cwh%E2%80%9D-questions-with-a-form-of-the-verb-do) begin with a question word, followed by *do* / *does* , the subject, and finally the base verb:


QUESTION When does Vikram come home from work?


ANSWER Vikram usually comes home from work at 6 pm.
