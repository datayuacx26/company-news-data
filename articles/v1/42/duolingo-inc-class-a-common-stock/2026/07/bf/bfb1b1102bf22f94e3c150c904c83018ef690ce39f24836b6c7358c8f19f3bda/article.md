# Types of English verbs: modals, auxiliaries, and more

An important part of English grammar is being able to recognize and use different categories of English verbs—there are often different rules for each verb type!


Here are the main types of verbs you’ll find in English and when to use them.

## Modal verbs


Modals are English verbs that are used alongside another verb for actions that are possible or necessary. English modal verbs are special because they never change to[match the subject](https://blog.duolingo.com/first-person-second-person-third-person/) —you don’t have to worry about verb endings or tenses! Here are the most common English modals:


MODAL VERB EXAMPLE


can


I can see the moon.


could


They could run really fast.


may


Oscar may bring a friend.


might


We might see a movie.


must


You must be quiet.


should


I should study tonight.


will


She will cook dinner.


would


They would prefer to stay here.


## Auxiliary verbs


Auxiliaries are also called “helping verbs,” because they are paired with another verb or verbs to form different tenses—they help the main verb show tense!


For example, will


is a helping verb for the[future tense](https://blog.duolingo.com/future-will-going-to) , and the forms of *have* (like have


, has


, and had


) are helping verbs for the present perfect and past perfect, and *will* and *have* are combined to form the future perfect. *To be* can be used as an auxiliary, too!


AUXILIARY VERB EXAMPLE


to be We are writing to our cousins.


to have Bea has seen that movie four times, so it must be good!


will You know the teacher will want us to work in groups.


## Infinitives and bare forms of verbs


All verbs (besides modals) have both[an infinitive and a bare form](https://blog.duolingo.com/infinitive-bare-form-english/#infinitives-vs-bare-forms-in-english) . Bare forms of verbs can be thought of as the basic form of a verb—it's the form you’ll find in a dictionary (including[online dictionaries](https://blog.duolingo.com/how-to-use-online-translators-and-dictionaries/) !).


In English, we mark[infinitives](https://blog.duolingo.com/infinitive-bare-form-english) by adding to


to the bare form ( *to be* , *to go* , *to have* , etc.), while other languages you may know might have a special prefix or suffix for infinitives (like the *-ar* , *-er* , and *-ir* endings for Spanish infinitives).


BARE FORM INFINITIVE EXAMPLES


eat **to** eat My dog can eat all day. He just wants to eat


!


read **to** read I should read more for fun—and I’d like to read fewer work emails!


## Differences between types of verbs


Understanding the different kinds of verbs in English will help you recognize and learn important grammar rules.


For example, modal verbs and other verbs can be followed by another verb. You’ll use the bare form after modals but the[infinitive](https://blog.duolingo.com/infinitive-bare-form-english) with *to* after other verbs.


In English, there are also different rules for how you form questions, depending on whether the sentence uses the verb *to be,* a modal, or an auxiliary—or whether it has any other verb. Here you can see the different patterns for forming[yes/no questions in English](https://blog.duolingo.com/english-yes-or-no-questions/) :


VERB TYPE YES/NO QUESTION


*to be* Are Lily and Zari ready for class?


modal Can you all bring dessert?


auxiliary Has it been raining all morning?


any other verb Does Oscar ever eat fast food?


Didn’t they go on the long tour?


Do I enter on the left?


The same is true for[wh- questions](https://blog.duolingo.com/what-are-wh-questions/) and[tag questions](https://blog.duolingo.com/what-are-tag-questions/) : There are different rules for different types of verbs!
