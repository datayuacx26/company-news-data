# How to Build Supply Chain Resilience Before the Next Disruption

## People


Companies need enough manpower to complete the work, but they also need skilled workers with the knowledge to help their employers tackle tough challenges. According to the[2025 MHI and Deloitte Annual Industry Report](https://og.mhi.org/publications/report) , hiring and retaining qualified workers along with managing the talent shortage remain the top internal supply chain challenges, vexing 52% and 45% of leaders respectively. That finding has held consistently across the MHI/Deloitte research series for years.


As supply chains transform, it's increasingly difficult to find anyone with some — if any — knowledge about the latest and greatest technology or strategies. As they wade into uncharted territory, most companies and employees will have to learn as they go.


This means companies should address talent acquisition through the lens of preventing future supply chain disruptions and seek out employees with the potential to support their organizations. Scout for knowledgeable engineers, procurement specialists, and supply chain managers who can both supplement existing talent and help the company navigate a difficult business environment. Concurrently, look for people with market knowledge who can help the company stay ahead of market trends. Manufacturing will never slow down, so you need the right people to help you keep up with the pace of business.


Once you have the talent you need, structure your organization for success. Organize a commodity management team and a supplier relationship team. Commodity managers focus on short-term goals and should be experts in their specific commodities markets. This means they’re constantly aware of new products, supply chain developments, pricing changes, and other market activities. Such awareness can help mitigate issues related to cost increases and component shortages.


Supplier relationship managers work with a long-term technology roadmap. These individuals don’t handle price negotiations or the short-term business of their commodity counterparts. Instead, they’re focused on building relationships with suppliers at the executive level by understanding their core business strategies and proactively approaching these partners with mutually beneficial opportunities.


Companies are recognizing the value of these relationships to their overall supply chain strategy.[Deloitte's 2025 Global Chief Procurement Officer Survey](https://business.amazon.com/en/blog/supplier-relationship-management) found that 61% of CPOs rated enhancing supplier collaboration and information sharing as the most effective risk mitigation strategy available to them.


At the same time, it’s important to remember that your employees are more than just workers. They’re people, too. Just as you want to build relationships with your suppliers and be a customer of choice, you want to be an employer of choice for your employees. Still, some companies may have very little wiggle room in their operations and budgets to extend more benefits or hire an ideally sized team to support all operations with extra breathing room. In those cases, companies should consider partnering with a manufacturing solutions provider, which can help enable supply chain resilience.
