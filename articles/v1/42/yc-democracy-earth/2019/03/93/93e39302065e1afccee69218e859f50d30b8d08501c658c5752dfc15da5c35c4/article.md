Blockchain networks opened the possibility of new institutional models built with open source code, able to resist censorship and scale participation globally. However, Bitcoin’s original white paper description of “one CPU one vote” shaped the entire crypto-industry to think governance centered around machines, not people. Although a fundamental right to privacy bent early blockchain design toward anonymity, this property significantly limits the governance capacity of protocols. A simple example of that pertains to[Quadratic Voting](https://economics.rice.edu/sites/g/files/bxs876/f/Weyl%20(paper)%20-%20Feb%202017.pdf) : one of the best governance models that can be implemented digitally, yet its execution depends on ensuring no participant votes more than once on the same issue — an impossibility under a fully anonymous solution.


### Formalizing humans on the blockchain


The relevance of formalizing identities online can be inferred from centralized networks: major internet platforms such as Facebook and Google established themselves globally by achieving a sufficient level of consensus over their identity credentials, thus creating a trust layer on top of which a myriad of social applications could be built. The only former alternative able to reach such widespread use pertained to nation-states: our passports and national ID cards.


The creation of a global identification system outside of the strict control of nation-states increased communication and knowledge creation. It formed a networked social infrastructure that facilitated the emergence of multiple borderless political movements. However, being highly vulnerable to Artificial Intelligence exploits such as addictive algorithms and bots generating false identities & content, the underlying architecture of current centralized protocols exposes society to political manipulation and data theft. Not coincidentally, we are currently living the age of[digital authoritarianism](https://freedomhouse.org/report/freedom-net/freedom-net-2018/rise-digital-authoritarianism) , and democracy is in global recession for the 12th consecutive year.


The rules applying to the protocol of a consensus layer for identities have wide-ranging implications, fundamentally shaping the entire structure of applications built on top of it. Those applications in turn determine the ways in which individuals and society are able to organize and develop — and because they are designed for a purely digital context (vulnerable to privately-owned algorithms, bots and sybils), the current model inevitably results in a post-truth environment ruled by the algorithmic optimization logic the apps are built with.


As we continuously work researching and implementing different forms of digital democracies, our experiences highlight that any such endeavor is undermined by both artificial intelligence exploiting the identity protocol, and corruption by third parties controlling a voter registry (legacy systems). This led us to understand that for democracy to grow anywhere with an Internet connection, a distributed consensus on human identities is a fundamental requirement. Determining who has the right to participate and who doesn’t cannot be an afterthought of democracy: it is its elemental task. In that sense, **governance and identity are the same thing.** If we are to enable a humane society — in the very literal sense of it, a society capable of giving precedence to human logic ahead of artificial intelligence — then the next consensus layer for identities must place human consciousness at the center of the network’s governance.


Democracy Earth Foundation's goal is to model a “one human one node” graph, providing protocol specifications able to serve as a source of legitimacy in the governance of digital networks. Looking at identity as a spectrum where at one end we have the identities of the people we know well and in person, and on the other completely encrypted anonymity — everything else falling on a continuum — our model is a probabilistic one. To inform this probabilistic model, our indicators will be built over the social aspect of identity, involving a bi-directional process — on one side, an identity claim and on the other, its subsequent endorsement by validated entities.

### Turing Impossible Proofs


A machine’s perception threshold can be measured using Turing Tests, tasks designed to distinguish robots from humans. Thus, to achieve a *human-based* validation of identities requires by definition a Turing Impossible Proof, i.e. a claim that requires processing capability that goes beyond pattern recognition and puts validation control with humans rather than artificial intelligence.


Perhaps the first Turing Impossible Proof of record is[the video birth certificate of Roma Siri](https://www.coindesk.com/meet-the-dad-who-registered-his-daughters-birth-on-the-blockchain) , daughter of Democracy.Earth cofounders Santiago Siri and Pia Mancini — made in a simple format that is easy for a fellow human to decode, but presents a high difficulty target for machine processing. A proof can remain private and secret — only a hash goes on-chain, able to certify the contents and timestamp the original proof — enabling node validation without broadcasting the entirety of the information contained in the proof.


As artificial intelligence increases capabilities to mimic real human beings, the Proof of Identity format must always be open for debate.

### Modeling a humans-first political economy


Governance and identity also have a symbiotic relationship from an economic point of view: a vote only has value when it comes from a legitimate voter. Thus, the matter of legitimizing digital entities is fundamental to building a working political economy. While strictly financial transactions can be completely anonymous, political transactions only have value if able to ensure that the entities involved in it are legitimately entitled to be a part of the decision in question. Our three-token architecture composed of the ERC 725 SELF token, the ERC 20 $HOUR token, and the ERC 20 $VOTE token is designed to encompass this process.


In order to grant participation rights to all humans, Democracy Earth is developing a protocol based on consensus (voting), and able to generate a probabilistic model that can measure if any given digital entity attempting to join the network is living (not an AI or bot), and singular (connected to a single individual with no replicants in the network).


The proof of identity protocol with its three-token foundation (outlined below) will be built on the Sovereign layer, employing reputation algorithms, cryptographic lotteries and zero-knowledge proofs to protect the network against Sybil attacks and collusion while ensuring privacy. Although details will change as the network adapts to new challenges over time, its fundamental building blocks are as follows:

## ERC 20 $HOUR


Quadratic voting has been the subject of intense research as an optimal governance model, able to prevent majority abuse, speculation and extremism. Its purely mathematical structure makes it a scalable and market friendly version of democracy able to be deployed over the Ethereum blockchain.


In order to allow for a quadratic function to effectively govern the supply of $VOTES, we split the original $VOTE token functionality described in[The Social Smart Contract](http://paper.democracy.earth/) into two tokens: **the $VOTE token and the $HOUR token.** Every $HOUR will correspond to one hour of time — an evenly distributed and scarce resource — with Ethereum’s blockchain serving as a universal clock. At a block creation rate of 15 seconds, the token distribution mechanism will grant the right to 1 new $HOUR token at every ~240 blocks, or 1 hour of Elapsed Time since identity generation.


The $HOUR token, distributed as a Universal Basic Income awarded to every verified identity in the network, quadratically converts to $VOTES as a means of taxing intense preferences and in effect bringing a desirable rationalization to the allocation of $VOTES. Both are ERC 20 tradable tokens: while there is no limit to how many tokens one can own, the Democracy Earth political economy ensures political liquidity to every participant and exponentially taxes extreme preferences by design.

## ERC 20 $VOTE


The principal utility of the $VOTE token is identity validation. It will be used as the vehicle to legitimatize claims of identity (Turing Impossible Proofs) on different TCRs. When the $VOTE score for a given hash related to a Turing Impossible Proof reaches the consensus threshold, a check on the claim “Are you Human?” is issued for a provided SELF[ERC-725 identity](https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/blob/master/EIPS/eip-725.md) (described below). Although the full protocol for joining the Democracy Earth network is being constructed to protect it against sybils, bots, centralization and collusion, the quadratic supply of $VOTEs makes it a deliberately robust vehicle for identity validation.


In addition, the $VOTE token will act as a means of signaling preferences on our blockchain-based, social-media-like governance platform[Sovereign](https://democracy.earth/) . Whereas a ‘like’ on Facebook becomes part of an algorithmic computation designed to corrupt your attention, a ‘like’ on a blockchain is instead an accountable, transparent, and incorruptible $VOTE. Again the symbiotic nature of governance and identity is in evidence, with a participant’s actions on the platform adding to the information available regarding its originating identity.

## ERC 725 SELF


A non-fungible ERC 725 SELF token will join the Democracy Earth network when a given Turing Impossible Proof reaches the $VOTE score threshold and a check on the claim “Are you Human?” is issued.[ERC 725](https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/blob/master/EIPS/eip-725.md) is a non-fungible identity token specification on the Ethereum blockchain. Being built upon a permissionless network, this new identity credential places controls back with users, instead of having them locked into particular registrars. Its specifications enable owners to unlock transactions, certifications, voting and proxy representation on the blockchain — activities owned by the users, and which can add to their own portable reputation.


### Looking forward


It is difficult to overstate the profound implications of the model described above. Formalizing identities is the crucial component for blockchain networks to finally unlock their transformative powers for society through tangible social applications. By creating a layer of trusted credentials that protects privacy, we envision the construction of incorruptible digital democracies, peer-to-peer lending, Universal Basic Income (starting with our own $HOUR), among countless of profoundly innovative solutions being enabled over blockchain networks.


As a common thread, such initiatives can foster human development by enabling disintermediated individual agency, independent of the main current identity providers, i.e. nation states and major internet monopolies. They can enable human logic to prevail over artificial intelligence.


When John Perry Barlow wrote “[A Declaration of the Independence of Cyberspace](https://www.eff.org/es/cyberspace-independence) ” in 1996, he ended with a plea for a *“…more humane and fair civilization of the mind.” Humane* being a powerful choice of word, being used to describe the aspirations of an age giving rise to digital governance. Decentralizing democracy matters as the nation-state keeps failing a growing global society. It is worth remembering[the last words published](https://www.washingtonpost.com/opinions/global-opinions/jamal-khashoggi-what-the-arab-world-needs-most-is-free-expression/2018/10/17/adfc8c44-d21d-11e8-8c22-fa2ef74bd6d6_story.html?utm_term=.33af6a42266c&noredirect=on) by Saudi journalist Jamal Khashoggi:


> *“Through the creation of an independent international forum, isolated from the influence of nationalist governments spreading hate through propaganda, ordinary people in the Arab world would be able to address the structural problems their societies face.”*


The real risk of formalizing humans on the blockchain is not doing it.


As we launch Sovereign on Mainnet, this is the long-term thinking that will guide our development. It’s a challenging path, but if half the battle is knowing the direction forward, we are well-equipped. Join us!
