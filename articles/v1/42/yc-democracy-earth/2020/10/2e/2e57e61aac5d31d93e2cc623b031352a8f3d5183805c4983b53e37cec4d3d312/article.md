# Presenting our Pilots Report

### Presenting our Digital Decision-Making Pilots Report


Democracy Earth Foundation — Digital Decision-Making Pilots Report by Sofía Cossar & Paula Berman


**Hi there!**


Over the past few years we've learned a lot about with digital decision-making, but had never before formally presented our results. Here's the correction to that. Today we introduce a report with some learnings on the most meaningful pilots that we have conducted throughout the years:


- Peace Plebiscites, Colombia, 2016
- Referendum for the Chief Executive in Hong Kong with Civic Data Hong King and Pop Vote, 2017
- Participatory Budgeting, Blockstack.org, 2018
- Appropriation Bills, Democratic Caucus of the Colorado House of Representatives—United States, 2019


The democratizing promise of the Internet has succeeded to transform the cultural layer of humanity, while its potential to improve the institutional layer remains mostly unrealized. Despite increasing global connectivity, democracies around the world have failed to leverage technology to increase societal participation in its processes and decisions. On the contrary: the past 14 years recorded global declines in political rights and civil liberties with participation rates reaching all-time lows, whereas ‘digital authoritarianism’ — governments utilizing automated surveillance systems against their citizens — is on the rise.


The response to the COVID-19 health crisis has seen Eastern and Western governments doubling-down on their digital overreach, which is detrimental to the citizen’s right to privacy, the right to participate in public affairs, and the right to access public data. In parallel, parliaments around the world have been forced to venture into digital democracy and have shifted to video-conferencing to run their debates.


Democracy Earth Foundation is a U.S. (CA) 501 © (3) non-profit founded in 2015 building open source & censorship-resistant democracies able to be deployed anywhere where there’s an internet connection. Throughout the years, four pilots were carefully chosen and executed to test assumptions about digital democracies and gain new information, in partnership with private, public, and third sector organizations. These case studies took place within three governance systems (direct democracy, representative democracy, and liquid democracy) and three voting principles (plural voting, one person-one vote, and quadratic voting).


In the following report, we look at five indicators to measure the performance of the pilots: participation, delegation, pluralism, and support. Although more pilots are needed to verify our findings, we have extracted important conclusions and identified key challenges standing in the way of e-voting mass adoption.


We hope that readers can learn from these examples, make more informed decisions regarding digital democracy implementations for their communities, and continue advancing the available knowledge on those matters!
