# Beyond the Transaction: Where Retail Media's Next Chapter Begins | Fluent

Retail media has spent years expanding its footprint. More channels, more placements, more ways to reach customers along the path to purchase. But after a day with some of retail's sharpest operators at Brand Innovators' Future of Retail and Commerce Summit, hosted with Lowe's in Charlotte, one thing was clear. The next chapter will not be defined by how much more inventory the industry can create. It will be defined by how intelligently we use the signals already there.


Transaction history. Loyalty behavior. Purchase intent. Real-time context. Retailers and brands have more information than ever about what customers value and how they behave. The question is whether we are designing for it.


## Signals only matter if the experience reflects the moment


A loyalty member completing a purchase and a first-time shopper connecting in-store are both valuable. They are not in the same mindset. A shopper who drove in specifically for the garden center on a Saturday is telling you something a banner impression never will. Each person has a different reason for being there, a different level of intent, and a different next need.


The best operators use those signals to decide not only what to show, but whether the moment calls for an offer at all. That is the difference between having access to data and knowing how to use it.


## Experience is the driver, not the tech behind it


Here is the detail that stuck with me. In an audience obsessed with AI, AI barely came up on the panels. What came up, again and again, was experience. Not the tech stack, not the targeting model, not the creative. The actual human experience of shopping. Does the interaction make the customer's life easier? Does it reduce effort? Does it help them find something useful, or does it interrupt the moment?


That is also why the model only holds up when three parties win at once. The customer gets an experience that is useful and relevant. The retailer strengthens the relationship and creates incremental value. The advertiser reaches the right audience and can prove the result. Take the customer out of that equation and you can still generate short-term revenue, but you will not build anything durable.


## Retail media is having two conversations that belong together


The industry tends to treat customer experience and business performance as separate subjects, and they are usually happening in separate rooms.


One conversation is about why a moment matters. It is warm and human. It is the in-store expert, the reason a customer chose to make the trip, the interaction that earns loyalty. The people in this room understand that a store visit is not a digital failure to convert. It is a deliberate, customer-owned choice, which makes it one of the strongest intent signals a brand will ever get.


The other is about proving that any of it works. It is rigorous and technical. Incrementality, new-to-brand growth, measurement a brand can actually defend. The operators on stage were blunt that reporting a conversion is not the same as proving impact, and that when measurement is not standardized across partners, brand teams end up grading their partners' homework for them.


Both conversations are right. Almost no one is connecting them. That is the opportunity.


The teams that will define the next chapter treat in-store moments as intent signals worth measuring, not just experiences worth delivering. They build full-funnel thinking from day one instead of starting with lower-funnel performance and backfilling brands later. And they hold their commerce media to a real bar. Not "here is a placement," but "here is what changed because of it."


## The transaction is a beginning, not only an outcome


A transaction has always marked the end of the journey. The conversion, the completed purchase, the result everyone was working toward. It is also one of the clearest expressions of customer intent we ever get. Someone just said yes. They are engaged, they trust you, and they are open to what comes next.


Most brands let that moment disappear. The opportunity is not to crowd the confirmation page with more offers. It is to recognize what the moment is worth and design for it, then measure it with the same rigor the rest of the funnel already gets. What would make the purchase more valuable? What would this customer actually appreciate next? Those are the questions that turn a transaction into a relationship.


## What comes next will define the channel


Retail media does not have to choose between customer experience and commercial performance. Its next chapter depends on connecting them. The businesses that lead will read the customer's signals, respect the context around each interaction, and prove whether the experience changed behavior.


The purchase is complete. The relationship is just beginning.
