# GoLinks vs. Open Source: The Real Cost of “Free” Go Links

The licensing fee for a GoLinks open source alternative is $0. The total cost of ownership is not.


When teams evaluate GoLinks against open source alternatives, the conversation starts with licensing and ends with a spreadsheet that does not account for engineering time, security gaps, downtime risk, or the compounding cost of a tool that gradually stops getting maintained. Most teams only run that calculation after something breaks.


Here’s what that calculation actually looks like — and why so many engineering and IT teams eventually make the switch.


## What Is a GoLinks Open Source Alternative?


A GoLinks open source alternative is a self-hosted URL shortener that replicates the` go/` shortlink experience using publicly available code. Typically, engineers build the functionality for internal use, publish it to GitHub, and then other teams adopt it to avoid software licensing costs.


The appeal is straightforward: no licensing fees, complete infrastructure control, and full ability to modify the codebase. For a small team with a dedicated engineer and a narrow use case, that tradeoff can make sense — at least at the start.


In practice, “at the start” is a shorter window than most teams expect.


## The Hidden Costs Add Up Fast


Free to license. Expensive to run. The real price — engineering hours, security overhead, maintenance debt — isn’t on the invoice. Here’s where those costs actually land.


**Engineering time.** Someone has to build it, maintain it, and handle every bug, outage, and security patch. Setup, ongoing maintenance, and feature work[can cost upward of $160,000 annually](https://www.golinks.io/open-source.php) — a line item that never appears in a licensing comparison but always appears in engineering headcount.


**Single point of failure.** Most open source go link tools have exactly one owner. When that engineer leaves, so does the knowledge of how it works — the tool stalls, and adoption erodes behind it.


**Downtime risk.** If a catastrophic incident occurs — a bad deployment, a security breach, data corruption — recovery isn’t guaranteed. And when that system goes down, the whole company loses its shortcut to internal tools and docs at the same moment — hours of collective productivity gone while people hunt for links manually.


**Security gaps.** With open source, security is your team’s job — our engineers build and maintain SSL, access control, compliance certifications, and audit logging in-house. GoLinks includes them out of the box: SOC 2 Type 2, 2048-bit SSL encryption, SSO, SAML (Okta, OneLogin, Azure AD, Ping), GDPR compliance, fault-tolerant AWS infrastructure, and a 99.9% uptime SLA. For a deeper look at what’s at stake, see[the hidden risks of open source go links](https://www.golinks.com/blog/the-hidden-risks-of-open-source-go-links/) .

Open source tools handle the basic use case: create a short link, point it at a URL. That’s enough to get a demo working. It’s rarely enough to drive company-wide adoption.

## When Open Source Go Links Actually Make Sense


Open source go link tools aren’t categorically wrong. They’re the right call for organizations with dedicated engineering resources, a compliance requirement that mandates self-hosting, or a real operational reason to control the infrastructure directly. For those teams, the overhead is a fair tradeoff.


However, for everyone else — teams that chose open source to avoid a licensing fee rather than to gain control — the math almost always flips over time.
