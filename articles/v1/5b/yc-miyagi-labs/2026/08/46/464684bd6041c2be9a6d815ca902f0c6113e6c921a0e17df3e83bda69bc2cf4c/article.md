## What Are SAT Scores? The Basics


The SAT is scored on a scale of 400 to 1600. That total, called your composite score, is the sum of two section scores: Evidence-Based Reading and Writing (200 to 800) and Math (200 to 800).


For the class of 2025, the national average composite was[1029](https://blog.prepscholar.com/good-sat-score-2026) , with an average Reading and Writing score of 521 and an average Math score of 508. Over 2 million students took the test, the first class to clear that mark since 2020.


Here’s something that matters more than the average: only[39% of test-takers met both college readiness benchmarks](https://research.collegeboard.org/reports/sat-suite/understanding-scores/sat) , down from 45% before the pandemic. The typical SAT score is lower than most families assume.


### How the Digital SAT’s Adaptive System Affects Your Score


Since the SAT went fully digital in 2023, it uses a multistage adaptive format. You complete Module 1, and based on your performance, the system routes you to either a harder or easier Module 2. This matters because if you’re routed to the easier second module, your maximum possible section score is effectively capped around 600 to 650. The test rewards strong starts.

## Glossary of SAT Score Terms


These are the terms you’ll encounter when researching SAT scores for colleges. Each definition includes a brief explanation of why it matters for admissions.


### Composite Score


Your total SAT score, the sum of your Reading & Writing section score and your Math section score. Range: 400 to 1600. This is the number colleges cite most often and the one you’ll compare against published score ranges.


### Section Score


Your individual score on either the Reading & Writing section or the Math section, each ranging from 200 to 800. Some engineering-heavy programs weigh Math more heavily, while humanities programs may pay closer attention to Reading & Writing. Knowing your section breakdown helps you[target weak areas](https://miyagilabs.ai/sat/question-bank) more efficiently.


### Percentile Rank


The percentage of test-takers who scored at or below your score. A 75th percentile score means you outperformed 75% of students. But here’s a confusion point most guides skip: there are actually two percentile scales on your score report.


**User Percentile** compares you only to students who actually sat for the SAT. **Nationally Representative Percentile** compares you to the entire U.S. high school population, including students who never took the test. A composite of 1200 sits at roughly the[74th Nationally Representative percentile but only the 57th to 59th User percentile](https://www.kollegio.ai/blog/sat-scores-percentiles) . That’s a meaningful gap. When a college says “75th percentile,” they’re typically referring to admitted-student score distributions, not either of these scales.


### Middle 50% (Interquartile Range)


The score band between a college’s 25th and 75th percentile of admitted students. If a school lists a middle 50% of 1350 to 1520, that means half of admitted students scored in that range. The other half is split: 25% scored below 1350, and 25% scored above 1520.


Students constantly mistake the 25th percentile as a hard minimum. It isn’t. One in four admitted students scored below it. The middle 50% is a targeting tool, not a cutoff.


### Superscore


The combination of your highest Reading & Writing score from one test date and your highest Math score from another. Most colleges, including most test-required ones, accept superscores. This changes the calculus on retaking the SAT. If you scored 720 Math but only 650 Reading & Writing on your first attempt, you can retake the test focused primarily on improving Reading & Writing, and the college will combine your best sections. For more on building a retake strategy, the[SAT study schedule guide](https://miyagilabs.ai/blog/sat-study-schedule-guide) breaks down how to plan focused prep.


### Score Choice


A College Board policy that lets you choose which test date’s scores to send to colleges. If you took the SAT three times and bombed the second sitting, you can send only your first and third scores. Note: some schools (like Georgetown) require all scores, so always check individual policies.


### College Readiness Benchmarks


Thresholds set by the College Board (480 for Reading & Writing, 530 for Math) indicating a 75% likelihood of earning at least a C in first-year college courses. These aren’t admissions standards. They’re predictive indicators. Most competitive colleges expect scores well above these thresholds.


### Equating


A statistical process that ensures a 1200 earned in March means the same thing as a 1200 earned in October, even though the questions are different. You don’t need to worry about “easier” or “harder” test dates. The College Board adjusts for difficulty so scores remain comparable across administrations.


### Test-Required vs. Test-Optional vs. Test-Free


Three distinct admissions policies:


-


**Test-required** : You must submit SAT or ACT scores. No exceptions.


-


**Test-optional** : You choose whether to submit scores. The college considers them if sent but won’t penalize you for withholding.


-


**Test-free (or test-blind)** : The college won’t consider test scores even if you send them. Very few schools operate this way.


The category a school falls into directly shapes your application strategy, which we’ll cover in detail below.

## SAT Score Ranges by College Tier: 2026 Benchmarks


When people search for SAT scores for colleges, they usually want a simple answer: what score do I need for schools at X level? The table below organizes the answer into five tiers, based on published middle 50% data from the most recent admissions cycle.


### Tier 1: Ivy League and Top 10 (Middle 50%: 1490 to 1580)


These are the schools where almost every admitted student scores 1500 or above.[Harvard admits students in the 1500 to 1580 range](https://empowerly.com/applications/sat-ranges-top-colleges/) , MIT clusters between 1520 and 1570, and Stanford between 1510 and 1570. Yale, Princeton, Columbia, Penn, and Northwestern fall between 1470 and 1570. Caltech and Rice sit in similar territory.


Only about[7% of all test-takers score between 1400 and 1600](https://www.thethinkacademy.com/blog/whats-a-good-sat-score-understand-compare-and-aim-for-your-best/) , which gives you a sense of how concentrated the competition is at this level.


### Tier 2: Top 20 to 50 Selective (Middle 50%: 1350 to 1530)


Schools like UCLA (1290 to 1510), UC Berkeley (1340 to 1540), Georgetown, Vanderbilt (1510 to 1560), and similar universities. A 1400 puts you solidly in range for most of these schools, and a 1500+ makes you competitive at the top end.


### Tier 3: Competitive State Flagships (Middle 50%: 1200 to 1400)


Ohio State, Penn State, University of Michigan, UT Austin, and similar public universities. A score of 1200 or above, which is roughly the[75th percentile nationally](https://research.collegeboard.org/reports/sat-suite/understanding-scores/sat) , makes you competitive at most of these schools.


### Tier 4: Mid-Range Four-Year Colleges (Middle 50%: 1050 to 1200)


Most state universities and many private colleges.[Towson University, for example, admits students between 1020 and 1320](https://www.compassprep.com/college-profiles/) . At this tier, a score near or above 1100 is typically solid.


### Tier 5: Open or Broad Admission (Below 1050)


Community colleges and open-enrollment universities. Many don’t require the SAT at all.


**Important caveat about score inflation** : Published middle 50% ranges at test-optional schools are inflated. Why? Because only students with high scores tend to submit them. Practitioners on Reddit’s r/ApplyingToCollege frequently point this out, noting that the reported ranges overstate the actual competitiveness floor. A user-compiled thread ranking colleges by highest median SAT (which ranked in the top 15 of Google results for this query) makes the same observation. The published mid-50% SAT range at most Ivy League schools has[climbed roughly 50 points at the 25th percentile since 2018-2019](https://orieladmissions.com/colleges-require-sat-act/) , partly because of this self-selection effect.

## Test-Optional vs. Test-Required: Does Your SAT Score Still Matter?


The short answer: yes, for most applicants.


### The Reinstatement Wave


The test-optional era is narrowing at the top.[More than 90% of ranked four-year colleges still don’t require scores for fall 2026](https://www.collegetransitions.com/blog/test-optional-admissions) , but the list of elite schools bringing testing back grows every few months.


For 2026 admissions, test-required schools include MIT, Harvard, Yale, Dartmouth, Stanford, Brown, Caltech, Georgetown, Purdue, the University of Tennessee, the University of Florida system, the University of Georgia, and all military service academies.


Columbia, which had been the only Ivy publicly committed to permanent test-optional admissions,[reversed course in June 2026](https://orieladmissions.com/colleges-require-sat-act/) and will require the SAT or ACT starting with the 2027-2028 cycle. Princeton remains test-optional for current applicants but has already announced that scores will be required again starting with the Fall 2028 entering class.


The rationale behind these reversals is worth noting. Dartmouth’s reversal was one of the most consequential because it argued that test-optional admissions had actually made it easier to overlook strong applicants from less-resourced backgrounds, students whose test scores would have been an asset when considered in context. Ohio State cited higher GPAs and retention rates among test submitters as justification for its own reinstatement. These are equity and predictive-validity arguments, not just selectivity plays.

### When to Submit vs. Withhold


“Optional” describes the submission decision, not the weight. At selective test-optional schools, a submitted score above the school’s 75th percentile is a meaningful positive signal.


A practical rule: submit if your score falls within or above a school’s published middle 50% range. Strongly submit if you’re at or above the 75th percentile. Withhold if you’re significantly below the 25th percentile.


If you’re an international, homeschooled, or non-traditional student, submitting is generally more helpful because admissions committees have fewer standardized data points to evaluate your academic preparation.


Most schools accept either the SAT or ACT, so consider whether a different test format suits you better. If the ACT’s structure feels more natural,[ACT prep resources](https://miyagilabs.ai/act/prep) can help you compare.

## How to Find SAT Score Ranges for Any College


You don’t need to rely on third-party lists. Here’s how to find the data yourself:


1.


**Check the school’s Common Data Set (CDS).** Search “\[School Name\] Common Data Set” and look for Section C (First-Time, First-Year Admissions). The 25th and 75th percentile SAT scores are listed in a standard format.


2.


**Visit the school’s admissions page.** Most publish a “class profile” or “admitted student profile” with score ranges.


3.


**Use College Board’s BigFuture.** Search any school and see reported score distributions.


When reading these reports, look for the 25th and 75th percentile columns. The 25th percentile is the score below which 25% of admitted students fell. The 75th percentile is the score above which only 25% of admits scored.


And remember the inflation warning: at test-optional schools, these numbers reflect only students who chose to submit. The actual score distribution of enrolled students is broader than what’s published.

## Frequently Asked Questions


### What is the average SAT score?


The national average for the class of 2025 was 1029 out of 1600. By section, it was 521 Reading & Writing and 508 Math.


### What SAT score do I need for Ivy League schools?


Most Ivy League schools admit students in the 1470 to 1580 range (middle 50%). Harvard, Yale, and Princeton have 25th percentiles at or above 1500. A score below 1480 is now below the 25th percentile at all three.


### Do all colleges require the SAT in 2026?


No. Over 90% of ranked four-year colleges remain test-optional for fall 2026 admissions, and over 1,000 colleges on the Common App are test-optional. However, a growing number of elite schools (including Harvard, MIT, Yale, Stanford, Brown, Dartmouth, Caltech, and Georgetown) now require scores.


### Should I submit my SAT score to a test-optional school?


Submit if your score is within or above the school’s published middle 50% range. If your score falls well below the 25th percentile, withholding is usually the better strategy.


### What is a superscore, and do colleges accept it?


A superscore combines your highest Reading & Writing score from one test date with your highest Math score from another. Most colleges accept superscores, which makes retaking the SAT strategically valuable even if only one section needs improvement.


### Can I retake the SAT to improve my score?


Yes. The SAT is offered 8 times per year. There’s no limit on retakes, and with Score Choice, you can control which scores colleges see. Most admissions counselors recommend two to three attempts.


### How is the digital SAT scored differently from the old paper test?


The digital SAT uses multistage adaptive modules. Your performance on Module 1 determines whether Module 2 is harder or easier. If you’re routed to the easier Module 2, your maximum section score is capped around 600 to 650. The scoring scale (400 to 1600) and section structure (two sections, 200 to 800 each) remain the same.


### Does a 1300 SAT score get me into a good college?


It depends entirely on your college list. A 1300 is roughly the 87th percentile nationally, which is above average at many strong public universities and private colleges. It would be below the middle 50% at most top-25 schools. The only meaningful definition of a “good” score is one that meets or exceeds the expectations of the specific colleges you’re applying to.
