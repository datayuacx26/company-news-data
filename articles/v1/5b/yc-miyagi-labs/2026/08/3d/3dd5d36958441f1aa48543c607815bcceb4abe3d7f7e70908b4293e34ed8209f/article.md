# What Is a Good SAT Score? 2026 Guide & Percentiles


## TL;DR


A good SAT score depends on where you want to go to college. Generally, 1200+ is above average and competitive at most four-year colleges, 1350+ puts you in the top 10% nationally, and 1500+ makes you competitive at Ivy League schools. The national average for the class of 2025 is 1029 out of 1600. Your target score should be at or above the 75th percentile of admitted students at your top-choice school.


---


A “good” SAT score is not a single number. It’s the score that gets you into the college you want to attend and earns you scholarship money if you need it. That might sound like a dodge, but it’s the most honest answer available, and admissions counselors who have led hundreds of information sessions say the same thing.


Still, you came here for concrete numbers. Here they are: scoring above 1200 places you ahead of roughly three-quarters of all test-takers. Scoring above 1350 puts you in the top 10%. And scoring 1500 or higher lands you in the top 2%, competitive at virtually every university in the country.


The rest of this guide breaks down exactly what those numbers mean, how percentiles work, what different college tiers actually expect, and how to figure out the right target for your situation.

## SAT Score Range and Structure


The SAT is scored on a 400 to 1600 scale. It has two sections: Reading and Writing (200 to 800) and Math (200 to 800). There is no penalty for wrong answers, so you should answer every question.


The current version is the digital SAT, which is section-adaptive. You complete a first module in each section, and your performance determines the difficulty of the second module. Two students can see different questions and still receive scores on the[same 1600-point scale](https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/k12-educators/about/understand-scores-benchmarks/benchmarks) . The class of 2025 was the first majority-digital cohort, with 97% taking the digital format.


If you’re unfamiliar with how the digital SAT works, a[full-length practice test](https://miyagilabs.ai/sat/practice-tests) is the fastest way to experience the format and timing firsthand.


## Average SAT Score in 2025


Understanding the national average gives you a baseline for evaluating any score.


According to the[College Board’s official data](https://newsroom.collegeboard.org/sat-participation-class-2025-surpasses-2-million-test-takers-first-time-2020) , the average SAT score for the class of 2025 was **1029** , broken down as 521 for Reading and Writing and 508 for Math. More than 2 million students took the SAT at least once, and only 39% met or exceeded both the Reading/Writing and Math college readiness benchmarks.


These averages are slightly higher than the prior year but still well below pre-pandemic levels. For context, the class of 2019 averaged 531 in Reading/Writing and 528 in Math, giving a combined average of 1059.


What this means in practice: if you’re scoring above 1029, you’re already outperforming the majority of test-takers. But “above average” and “good enough for your goals” are often very different things.

### User Percentile vs. Nationally Representative Percentile


This is a detail most guides skip, but it shows up right on your score report.


The College Board reports two percentile numbers. The “user percentile” compares you to actual SAT test-takers from the past three graduating classes. The “nationally representative percentile” compares you to all U.S. 11th and 12th graders, including those who never took the SAT.


Because the SAT-taking population skews more college-bound than the general high school population, your[nationally representative percentile](https://www.larrylearns.com/blog/sat-percentiles) is always higher. For example, a 1200 corresponds to roughly the 76th percentile among actual test-takers but the 81st percentile in the nationally representative sample.


When comparing your score to college admissions data, use the user percentile. That’s the pool you’re actually competing in.


## What Is a Good SAT Score by College Tier?


The phrase “good SAT score” only becomes meaningful when you attach it to a specific set of schools. Here’s how score expectations break down across different college tiers.


### Ivy League and Top 20 Universities


At the most selective schools in the country, the middle 50% of admitted students falls between roughly[1480 and 1580](https://www.bestcolleges.com/blog/good-sat-score-ivy-league/) . Specific ranges include:


-


Harvard: 1500 to 1580


-


MIT: 1520 to 1570


-


Stanford: 1510 to 1570


-


Yale, Princeton, Columbia, Penn, Northwestern: approximately 1470 to 1570


-


Cornell: 1430 to 1550 (the broadest range among Ivies)


A 1500+ is competitive. Below 1470, you’re in the bottom quartile of admits, and most students in that range have significant hooks (recruited athlete, legacy, underrepresented background, institutional priority).


Practitioners on Reddit’s r/Sat discuss this dynamic frequently. One popular thread on what counts as a “good” score captured this well: a top comment explained that 1450+ is competitive at most top schools, but 1530+ is the score “where you stop worrying.” The consensus is that score expectations feel like a moving target, pushed upward by social media comparison and rising competition.


### Top 21 to 50 Universities


For schools ranked roughly 21st to 50th nationally, the middle 50% typically spans[1380 to 1530](https://empowerly.com/applications/sat-ranges-top-colleges/) . Scoring 1400 or above puts you in a solid position. A 1450+ makes you a strong academic candidate at nearly all of these institutions.


### Selective State Flagships


Schools like UCLA (1290 to 1510), UC Berkeley (1340 to 1540), University of Michigan, and UVA generally admit students in the 1300 to 1500 range. A 1400+ is a strong position for most flagship state universities.


### Most Four-Year Colleges


A 1200 is above the national average and opens the door at the majority of four-year colleges. For schools with a median around 1100 to 1200, this score makes you a competitive applicant. However, for selective schools where the median sits at 1400+, a 1200 falls below the competitive range.


### The “Effective Floor” for Unhooked Applicants


This is a critical insight that most score guides leave out. When a college publishes a 25th percentile SAT score of, say, 1450, it doesn’t mean that’s the realistic minimum for all applicants. Many students admitted below the median are recruited athletes, legacies, children of major donors, or students from dramatically underrepresented backgrounds.


If you don’t fall into one of those categories, the effective SAT floor is higher than the published 25th percentile. Unhooked applicants should aim for the median or above. This is something experienced admissions counselors emphasize, and it comes up repeatedly in student communities online.

## Good SAT Section Scores


Section-level performance matters, especially depending on your intended major.


The College Board’s college readiness benchmarks are 480 for Reading and Writing and 530 for Math. Students who hit both benchmarks have a 75% chance of earning at least a C in first-semester, credit-bearing college courses. A combined 1010 is roughly the “college ready” floor, yet only 39% of the class of 2025 met both benchmarks.


Beyond readiness, your section balance matters strategically:


-


**STEM applicants** should prioritize a strong Math score. Engineering and computer science programs at selective schools often have admitted-student Math averages of 750+.


-


**Humanities and social science applicants** benefit from a high Reading and Writing score, though Math still matters for overall competitiveness.


If you know which section is holding you back, targeted practice makes the biggest difference. Working through[SAT practice questions](https://miyagilabs.ai/sat/practice-questions) organized by skill area lets you focus where it counts.


## Good SAT Scores for Scholarships and Merit Aid


Here’s an angle that most “what is a good SAT score” guides miss entirely: your score directly affects how much college costs.


Many institutions tie merit scholarships to specific SAT thresholds:


-


**1200+** qualifies for entry-level merit aid at many schools


-


**1300 to 1450** opens the door to substantial merit awards


-


**1450+** puts you in range for prestigious, high-value scholarship programs


The connection between scores and dollars is often explicit. At the University of Alabama, for example, moving from the 1200 to 1250 range to the 1260 to 1290 range raises the annual out-of-state merit award from $6,000 to $8,000. Reaching the[1300 to 1320 range bumps it to $10,000](https://www.mysatcoach.com/guide/sat-merit-scholarships-2026) . These are automatic awards based purely on your score and GPA.


The most reliable strategy for maximizing merit aid: aim for an SAT at or above a school’s 75th percentile of admitted students. You can find this number in any school’s Common Data Set, which is publicly available.


One important exception: Ivy League schools (Harvard, Yale, Princeton, MIT, Columbia, Penn, Brown, Dartmouth) offer zero merit aid. Their financial aid is 100% need-based.


## Does Your SAT Score Still Matter? The Test-Optional Reversal


The test-optional trend that expanded during COVID is reversing, fast. The 2025 to 2026 admissions cycle has seen a wave of top colleges[reinstating standardized testing requirements](https://www.crimsoneducation.org/us/blog/colleges-that-require-sat-act) .


Schools now requiring SAT or ACT scores include Harvard, MIT, Stanford, Brown, Georgetown, Caltech, Purdue, and Penn. Princeton has announced it will return to requiring scores starting with the 2027 to 2028 cycle.


Even at schools that remain test-optional, submitting a strong score helps. Admissions data consistently shows that applicants who submit scores above a school’s median are admitted at higher rates than those who withhold. And as noted above, merit scholarships at many institutions require a score regardless of whether the admissions process is test-optional.


The practical rule: if your score is at or above a school’s middle 50%, submit it. If it’s below, weigh whether withholding helps your overall application.


## SAT Superscoring: How It Affects Your Target


A superscore combines your best section scores from different test dates into one higher composite. If you scored 650 in Reading and Writing on one attempt and 700 in Math on another, a college that superscores evaluates you as a 1350.


Most colleges superscore, but the specifics matter. Many institutions will only combine scores within the same test format, meaning they’ll superscore across multiple digital SAT sittings but won’t mix digital and paper SAT scores. Always check each school’s policy individually.


Superscoring is one reason retaking the SAT often makes sense. You only need to improve one section to raise your composite.


## How to Set Your Target SAT Score


Instead of asking “what is a good SAT score” in the abstract, use this step-by-step process to find *your* target number.


**Step 1: List your target colleges.** Include reaches, targets, and safeties.


**Step 2: Look up each school’s middle 50% SAT range.** This data is in each school’s Common Data Set or on their admissions page.


**Step 3: Aim for the 75th percentile of your top-choice school.** This gives you the strongest academic positioning and the best shot at merit aid.


**Step 4: Find your baseline.** Take a timed, full-length[SAT practice exam](https://miyagilabs.ai/sat/practice-exams) under realistic conditions. Your starting score tells you how much ground you need to cover.


**Step 5: Identify skill gaps and build a study plan.** Focus on the specific topics and question types where you’re losing the most points. If you need a structured roadmap, an[SAT study schedule](https://miyagilabs.ai/blog/sat-study-schedule-guide) can help you allocate your prep time effectively.


### The Percentile Acceleration Zone


Here’s a motivating data point that few guides mention. The fastest percentile gains come in the 1200 to 1400 range. Every 50-point improvement in that band moves you through roughly 8 to 10 percentile points. Below 1200, the gains per point are smaller because the percentile distribution is denser. Above 1400, each point is harder to earn because you’re competing with a smaller, higher-performing group.


If you’re sitting around 1200 right now, the return on investment for additional study is enormous.


### Retaking the SAT Works


College Board research shows that[students who retake the SAT improve 68% of the time](https://www.kollegio.ai/blog/is-1200-a-good-sat-score) . Additionally, 20 hours of structured practice correlates with an average 115-point improvement. That’s not a trivial gain. Moving from 1200 to 1315 could shift you from the 74th percentile to roughly the 88th.

## The Middle 50% Concept, Explained


You’ll see the phrase “middle 50%” everywhere in college admissions. It refers to the 25th to 75th percentile range of SAT scores among admitted students at a given school.


If your score is above the 75th percentile, you’re in strong academic shape for that school. If you’re within the middle 50%, you’re competitive. If you’re below the 25th percentile, your score is a weakness in your application (unless you have a significant hook).


This range is the single most practical benchmarking tool available. Use it for every school on your list.


## Frequently Asked Questions


### Is 1200 a good SAT score?


Yes. A 1200 places you around the 74th percentile, meaning you scored higher than about three-quarters of test-takers. It’s competitive at most four-year colleges and qualifies you for entry-level merit scholarships at many institutions. However, it falls below the competitive range for selective schools where the median is 1400+.


### Is 1400 a good SAT score?


A 1400 is excellent. It puts you at approximately the 94th percentile, in the top 6% of all test-takers. This score is competitive at many top-50 universities and opens the door to substantial merit aid. For the most selective schools (top 20), you’d want to push toward 1450 or 1500.


### Is 1500 a good SAT score?


Outstanding. A 1500 reaches the 98th percentile or higher. It makes you academically competitive at every university in the country, including Ivy League schools. At this level, other parts of your application (essays, extracurriculars, recommendations) become the differentiating factors.


### What is the highest possible SAT score?


The maximum is 1600: 800 in Reading and Writing plus 800 in Math.


### How many times can you take the SAT?


There is no official limit. Most students take it two or three times. Since many colleges superscore, retaking gives you the opportunity to improve individual sections without risking your best scores.


### Does the digital SAT score differently than the old paper SAT?


No. The digital SAT uses the same 400 to 1600 scale. The section-adaptive format means question difficulty adjusts based on your first-module performance, but the final scoring range and percentile benchmarks remain consistent.


### When should I withhold my SAT score from a test-optional school?


If your score falls below a school’s 25th percentile for admitted students, withholding is usually the safer strategy. If you’re within or above the middle 50%, submitting almost always helps.


### How much can I realistically improve my SAT score?


College Board research indicates that 20 hours of structured practice correlates with an average 115-point gain, and 68% of retakers improve. With a focused study plan targeting your weakest skills, gains of 150 to 200+ points are achievable over two to three months of consistent work.
