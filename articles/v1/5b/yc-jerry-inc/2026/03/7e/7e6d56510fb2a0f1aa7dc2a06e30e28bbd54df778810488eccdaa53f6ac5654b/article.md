## Factors that affect home insurance costs in Oregon


From wildfire seasons that stretch longer each year to the ever-present earthquake risk along the coast, **where you live and what you own in Oregon plays a big role in what you pay** . Here are the biggest factors driving home insurance costs in the state and how to stay protected.


**🔥 Wildfire risk**


Wildfire is the **single biggest factor** impacting Oregon’s home insurance market right now. Homes near forests, grasslands and the wildland-urban interface in areas like **Central Oregon, the Rogue Valley and the Santiam Canyon** may face higher premiums or difficulty finding coverage.


**🌋 Earthquake risk**


Oregon sits along the **Cascadia Subduction Zone** , one of the most significant earthquake fault lines in North America. A major quake could cause severe damage across the Willamette Valley and the coast. Standard homeowners policies **do not cover earthquake damage** , so a separate earthquake policy is worth considering, especially for homeowners west of the Cascades.


**🌧️ Heavy rainfall and flooding**


Western Oregon’s wet climate brings **heavy rain, river flooding and landslides** , particularly during the fall and winter months. Standard homeowners policies cover water damage from burst pipes, but **flooding from rivers, heavy rain or rising water levels requires a separate flood insurance policy** . Even homes outside of designated flood zones can be at risk in Oregon’s heavy rain season.


**🌬️ Windstorms and winter storms**


Oregon’s coastal areas and the Columbia Gorge are prone to **powerful windstorms** that can down trees, damage roofs and knock out power for days. Winter ice storms can also cause widespread damage in the Willamette Valley. Standard policies generally cover wind damage, but **older roofs and trees close to your home** can increase your risk and your rate.


**🏠 Older homes and construction type**


Oregon has a wide range of home ages and construction types, from **turn-of-the-century Craftsman bungalows in Portland** to newer builds in the suburbs. Older homes with **outdated electrical wiring, plumbing or roofing** can be more expensive to insure because they carry a higher risk of fire, water damage and other costly claims.


**Key takeaway:** Oregon’s wildfire seasons, earthquake exposure and heavy rainfall all factor into your rate. Understanding what drives your premium helps you find the right coverage.

### Jerry Agent Tip 2: Stack discounts for a cheaper rate


From investing in fire-resistant home upgrades to paying your annual premium upfront, **most homeowners qualify for at least a few discounts** , and stacking them could add up to serious savings. But insurers don’t always apply all of them, which is why Jerry automatically finds the discounts you qualify for.


**Besides bundling, here are some common Oregon home insurance discounts:**


**🔥 Wildfire mitigation discount**


Oregon has partnered with the Insurance Institute for Business and Home Safety on the[Wildfire Prepared Home](https://wildfireprepared.org/) **certification program** , according to a 2025 article from[Oregon Public Broadcasting](https://www.opb.org/article/2025/05/06/oregon-wildfire-prevention-insurance-house-homeowners-fire-premiums/) . Homeowners who create defensible space, install fire-resistant roofing and siding and take other home hardening steps may qualify for lower premiums from participating carriers. If you live in a fire-prone area, this is one of the most impactful discounts you can pursue.


**🔒 Security system discount**


Installing safety devices like alarms, smart home systems and deadbolts can qualify you for a lower rate. Many carriers offer protective device **discounts of up to 15% when you combine security systems** like advanced burglar protection and automatic water leak valves.


**🔧 Home renovation discount**


Updated your electrical, plumbing or heating systems? Some carriers offer **discounts when these systems have been replaced within the last 10 to 15 years** , since modern systems reduce the risk of fire and water damage. For Oregon’s many **older Craftsman and mid-century homes** , these upgrades can make a real difference.


**🆕 New home discount**


Newer homes are less likely to have costly claims, and insurers reward that. Depending on the carrier, **homes built within the last 10 to 15 years may qualify for savings of up to 15%** for the first year you’re with an insurance company.


**🎓 Claims-free discount**


The longer you go without filing a claim, the more you save. Most insurers offer this discount after three or more claims-free years, with some offering **savings of up to 20%** .


**💳 Pay-in-full discount**


Paying your full annual premium upfront instead of in monthly installments can earn you a discount with many carriers, **typically between 5-10%** .


**⏳ Loyalty discount**


Staying with the same insurer year after year can lead to increasing savings. **Some carriers start rewarding loyalty after just one year** , with discounts growing the longer you stay. But it never hurts to shop around when your policy is up for renewal.


**📋 Quote-in-advance discount**


Shopping early can pay off. Some insurers offer a **discount of up to 15%** when you get a quote before your previous policy expires.


**🚭 Non-smoker discount**


Since smoking increases the risk of house fires, **some carriers offer lower rates to non-smoking households** .


**Key takeaway:** Most homeowners qualify for multiple discounts, from bundling and wildfire mitigation to claims-free credits. But insurers don’t always apply them automatically. Jerry can help you find all discounts you’re eligible for to maximize your savings.


### Jerry Agent Tip 3: Pick the right deductible


Your deductible — the amount you pay out of pocket before insurance kicks in — has a direct impact on your premium. **According to the**[Insurance Information Institute](https://www.iii.org/article/12-ways-to-lower-your-homeowners-insurance-costs) **, raising your deductible from $500 to $1,000 can reduce your premium by 10 to 25%** , depending on your location, insurer and home’s replacement cost.


The trade-off is simple: a higher deductible means a lower premium, but more out of pocket if you file a claim. To find the right balance:


- **Check what you can afford out of pocket.** If a $2,000 expense after a storm would be manageable, a higher deductible could save you hundreds a year. If it would strain your budget, stick with a lower one.
- **Consider how often you’d actually file a claim.** Most homeowners only file a claim once every 8 to 10 years, so the premium savings can add up long before you ever pay that deductible.

### Jerry Agent Tip 4: Improve your credit score


In Oregon, insurers have **restrictions on how they can use credit information** . Under[Oregon law](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_746.661) , insurers **cannot cancel or refuse to renew your policy based on your credit** , and they can only use credit to lower your rate on an existing policy, never to raise it. Insurers also cannot consider medical collections, your total available credit line or multiple mortgage inquiries within 30 days when calculating your insurance score.


Credit can still be a factor when you’re **applying for a new policy** , so improving your score can help you access better rates when shopping around. Focus on **paying bills on time** , keeping **credit card balances below 30%** of your limit and **avoiding new credit applications** before shopping for insurance.


If your credit isn’t where you want it to be, don’t worry. **Some insurers weigh credit less heavily than others** , and Jerry can show you which carriers offer the best rates for your situation.


**Key takeaway:** Oregon gives homeowners strong credit protections, but a better credit score can still help when applying for a new policy. Shopping around with Jerry can help you find a competitive rate regardless of your credit.

## FAQ


- **💰** How much is homeowners insurance in Oregon?


Costs vary widely based on your home’s age, location, rebuild value, claims history and credit score. Jerry’s data shows the average Oregon homeowner pays around $1,050


**a year** , but your actual rate could be higher or lower. Comparing quotes from multiple carriers with Jerry is the best way to see what you’d actually pay.


- 🔥 Does homeowners insurance cover wildfire damage in Oregon?


Yes. Standard homeowners policies in Oregon typically cover **damage caused by wildfire** , including damage to your home’s structure and personal belongings. Oregon law also requires insurers to allow at least **two years for rebuilding** after a wildfire loss. However, if your home is in a high-risk wildfire zone, you may face higher premiums. Comparing quotes through Jerry can help you find the most competitive rate for your area.


- 📊 Does Oregon use credit scores for home insurance?


Oregon restricts how insurers can use credit information. Insurers **cannot cancel or refuse to renew your policy** based on your credit. They also cannot use credit to increase your rate on an existing policy. However,[credit can still be a factor](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_746.661) when you’re applying for a new policy. Oregon also limits what credit data insurers can consider, excluding things like medical collections and multiple mortgage inquiries made within 30 days.


- 🌋 Is earthquake insurance required in Oregon?


No, earthquake insurance is not required in Oregon. However, standard homeowners policies **do not cover earthquake damage** . Given Oregon’s location along the Cascadia Subduction Zone, a separate earthquake policy is worth considering, especially if you live west of the Cascades or in the Portland metro area.


- 💧 Does home insurance cover flood damage in Oregon?


No. Standard homeowners policies **do not cover flood damage** . If you live near a river, in a low-lying area or in a region prone to heavy winter rainfall, a separate flood insurance policy is recommended. You can purchase flood insurance through the[National Flood Insurance Program](https://www.floodsmart.gov/) (NFIP) or a private insurer.


- 🏠 Is home insurance required in Oregon?


Oregon does not legally require homeowners insurance. However, if you have a mortgage, your lender will almost certainly require you to carry coverage. Even if you own your home outright, having a policy protects your investment from fire, storms, theft and liability.


- 🌲 What is the Wildfire Prepared Home program in Oregon?


The[Wildfire Prepared Home program](https://wildfireprepared.org/) is a partnership between the Oregon State Fire Marshal and the[Insurance Institute for Business and Home Safety](https://ibhs.org/) . It certifies homeowners who take specific steps to reduce wildfire risk, like creating defensible space around their property and installing fire-resistant building materials. Participating insurers may factor this certification into their rate-setting decisions, which could help lower your premium.


- 💡 How can I lower my home insurance in Oregon?


There are several ways to reduce your premium: **bundle your home and car insurance** , raise your deductible, invest in wildfire mitigation and home hardening, install security and safety devices, maintain a claims-free record and compare quotes from multiple carriers.
