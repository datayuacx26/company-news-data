# Best AI Video Generators [2026]

**AI video generator** is one phrase covering four different technologies, and picking the wrong category costs far more than picking the wrong brand. Avatar tools turn *a script* into a synthetic presenter. Generative video models turn *a prompt* into footage that never existed. Assembly tools cut *existing material* into something watchable. Explainer tools turn *a question* into a narrated walkthrough. Almost every roundup on this keyword mixes all four into one ranked list, which is why so many people buy a subscription and then find it does not do the thing they wanted.


This guide maps the four categories, names the tools leading each one, and puts verified 2026 prices against them, including what a finished minute of video actually costs.


## What Does "AI Video Generator" Actually Mean?


An AI video generator is any tool that produces video from a non-video input, which in practice covers four unrelated technologies with different inputs, costs, and failure modes.


The categories, in the order most people meet them:


1. **Avatar video.** You write a script, a synthetic presenter delivers it on camera. Built for corporate training, product explainers, and translating one video into thirty languages. Synthesia and HeyGen lead here.
2. **Generative video models.** You write a prompt, the model invents footage frame by frame. Built for creative, cinematic, and effects work where the shot cannot be filmed. Runway, Google Flow, and Pika lead here.
3. **Assembly tools.** You supply a recording, a stock library, or an article, and the tool cuts it into something watchable. Built for podcasts, social clips, and repurposing. Descript, InVideo, and Pictory lead here.
4. **Explainer video.** You ask a question, you get a narrated walkthrough of the answer. Built for onboarding, documentation, and study, where the point is that someone understands the subject afterwards.


The fourth category is the one roundups leave out, and its absence is not a ranking judgment. It is a difference of input. The other three all start from material you already have or already imagined. This one starts from something you do not know yet.


> If you cannot say which of the four you need, no amount of tool comparison will help you. The category is the decision. The brand is a detail.

## Avatar Video: Synthesia and HeyGen


Avatar video is the most commercially mature corner of the category, because the job is narrow: read this script convincingly, on camera, in any language.


**Synthesia** prices by minutes of generated video. Its[plan page](https://www.synthesia.io/pricing?ref=scrimba.com) lists a free tier at 10 minutes a month, Starter at $19 a month billed monthly, and Creator at $89 a month or $804 a year for 30 minutes a month. Enterprise removes the minute cap. Note what the free tier withholds: downloading your videos and removing the Synthesia logo both start at Starter, so the free plan makes video you can watch but cannot export.


**HeyGen** prices by credits instead. Its[plan page](https://www.heygen.com/pricing?ref=scrimba.com) lists a free tier of three videos a month capped at one minute each, Creator at $29 a month with 600 credits and watermark removal, and Pro at $49 a month with 1,000 credits and 4K export. Length and resolution are gated alongside volume, so the tier decides what you can make as well as how much.


Both share a quality ceiling. A synthetic presenter reading a script to camera is convincing. The same presenter demonstrating a physical action or carrying real emotional range is not. Avatar tools are excellent at *talking head* video and nothing else, which is fine, because talking head video is most of corporate video.


## Generative Video Models: Runway, Google Flow, and Pika


Generative video models produce footage that never existed, frame by frame, from a text prompt or a reference image.


**OpenAI has discontinued Sora.** According to[OpenAI's help centre](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001152-what-to-know-about-the-sora-discontinuation?ref=scrimba.com) , the web and app experiences shut down on 26 April 2026, the API follows on 24 September 2026, and user data is permanently deleted once the export window closes. The best known name in generative video is gone, and anyone who stored work there has a deadline. The most famous tool in a category is not automatically a safe place to keep your work.


**Runway** publishes exact credit costs, which makes it the easiest tool here to reason about. Its[pricing page](https://runwayml.com/pricing?ref=scrimba.com) puts Gen-4.5 at 60 credits per five seconds. Standard is $15 a month, or $12 billed yearly for 7,500 credits a year. Pro is $35 a month, or $28 yearly for 27,000 credits. Max is $95 a month, or $76 yearly for 114,000 credits. The free tier is 125 one time credits, and watermark removal starts at Standard.


**Google Flow** is not sold on its own. It is the studio wrapping Veo 3.1, Gemini Omni, and Nano Banana, and access is bundled into[Google AI plans](https://one.google.com/about/google-ai-plans/?ref=scrimba.com) at $4.99, $19.99, or from $99.99 a month.[Google's support page](https://support.google.com/flow/answer/16526234?ref=scrimba.com) puts non subscribers at 50 Flow credits a day, AI Plus at 200 a month, AI Pro at 1,000 a month, and AI Ultra at 10,000 or 25,000. A Veo generation costs roughly 10 credits in Lite mode, 20 in Fast, and 100 in Quality, so $19.99 a month buys about ten Quality generations.


**Pika** is the cheapest way in. Its[pricing page](https://pika.art/pricing?ref=scrimba.com) lists a free tier of 80 credits a month restricted to 480p and image to video, with Standard at $8 a month billed yearly. Unusually, commercial use and watermark free downloads are both included on that free tier, which is not true of most free plans in this article.


Every model in this category shares the same limits:


- Output is measured in seconds. Long form means stitching many short generations together.
- Physics, hands, and text inside the frame still fail regularly.
- Continuity across shots is unreliable, so the same character rarely survives three cuts unchanged.
- Your hit rate is not 100 percent. Most generations get discarded, and every discarded one was paid for.


That last point puts real money behind prompt quality, which makes[prompt engineering](https://scrimba.com/articles/best-prompt-engineering-courses-2026/) a practical skill here rather than a novelty.


## Assembly Tools: Descript, InVideo, and Pictory


Assembly tools do not invent footage. They cut it. That single difference changes the economics completely, because processing an hour of existing video costs a fraction of generating ten seconds of new video.


**Descript** treats video as a text document: edit the transcript, the video edits itself. Its[pricing page](https://www.descript.com/pricing?ref=scrimba.com) lists a free tier at 60 media minutes a month and 720p export, Hobbyist at $16 a month billed yearly for 10 media hours and watermark free 1080p, and Creator at $24 a month yearly for 30 media hours and 4K.


**InVideo** has no free plan at all. Its[pricing page](https://invideo.io/pricing/?ref=scrimba.com) starts at Plus, $17 a month or $200 yearly for 75 credits a month, and every paid tier exports without a watermark.


**Pictory** turns long text into video, and its plan structure shows the split between the two kinds of work. According to its[pricing page](https://pictory.ai/pricing?ref=scrimba.com) , Starter costs $25 a month billed annually and includes 2,400 minutes of media a year alongside 1,200 AI credits a year. Those credits are worth about 12.5 minutes of generated video. One product, two resources, and the generated one is roughly two hundred times scarcer.


## Explainer Video: The Category Roundups Leave Out


Explainer video starts from a question rather than a script or a prompt, and returns a narrated walkthrough of the answer instead of a produced clip.


This is a different product shape. The other three categories assume you already know what the video should say, and the tool's job is production. Here the tool's job is the explanation itself, and the video is how the explanation gets delivered. The use cases are onboarding, documentation, and studying, where the measure of success is that someone understood something.


**Scrimba Explain** sits in this category. It works as an MCP plugin: you ask your coding agent a question, the agent researches it against the files and conversation it already has, and Explain turns the answer into a narrated video walkthrough. It runs with Claude Code, Codex, or any agent supporting[MCP](https://scrimba.com/articles/best-mcp-tutorials-and-courses/) , and it works beyond code, with its FAQ naming biology, mathematics, philosophy, and language learning. It is free during open beta.


What it is not: no avatars, no stock footage, no brand templates, so it is not an alternative to Synthesia or HeyGen for marketing video. And like any AI tool it can get things wrong, so anything that matters should be double-checked.

## Watermarks, Rights, and the Rest of the Fine Print


Three constraints decide whether a tool survives a real workflow, and none of them lead the marketing page.


- **Export rights.** Most entry paid tiers exist mainly to remove a watermark. Synthesia goes further and gates the download itself.
- **Commercial use.** Not uniform. Pika grants it on the free tier while others restrict resolution or volume, and stock library licensing is separate again. Read the plan before building a business process on a free account.
- **Platform risk.** Sora's shutdown is the reference case. Keep local copies of anything you would be unhappy to lose, and prefer tools that let you export at full quality on the plan you actually pay for.


## Which AI Video Generator Do You Actually Need?


Pick the category first, then the tool. Match your input to one of these five cases:


1. **A person on screen saying your words, in several languages, without filming.** Avatar video. HeyGen for a low commitment monthly plan, Synthesia if you need minute quotas and enterprise controls.
2. **Footage that does not exist and cannot be filmed.** A generative video model. Start with Pika to learn what prompts work at low cost, then move to Runway or Google Flow when quality matters. Budget for discarded takes.
3. **A recording, footage, or an article that needs to become a watchable cut.** An assembly tool. Descript for anything with a spoken track, InVideo for social output, Pictory for long text.
4. **Someone needs to understand something.** Explainer video, the category to reach for when the deliverable is comprehension rather than a produced asset.
5. **You are not sure yet.** Take the free tier in the right category over the best reviewed tool in the wrong one. A limited free plan doing the correct job teaches you more in an afternoon than a month of the wrong subscription.

## Frequently Asked Questions


### What is the best AI video generator in 2026?


There is no single best one, because the term covers four different technologies. Synthesia and HeyGen lead avatar video, Runway and Google Flow lead generative video models, Descript leads assembly and editing, and explainer tools handle question to video. Identify your category first, then compare within it.


### Is there a genuinely free AI video generator?


Yes, with real limits. Pika's free tier includes 80 credits a month, commercial use, and no watermark, capped at 480p. Google Flow gives non subscribers 50 credits a day. Descript allows 60 media minutes a month at 720p. Synthesia's free plan cannot download videos at all.


### What happened to Sora?


OpenAI discontinued Sora. The web and app experiences shut down on 26 April 2026, and the API is scheduled to shut down on 24 September 2026. User data is permanently deleted after the final export window closes. Anyone who created work in Sora should export it before the API deadline.


### How much does AI video cost per minute?


It depends entirely on category. At list prices, generative model output runs roughly $6 to $14 per finished minute, avatar video around $3 per minute, and assembly tools a fraction of a cent because they bill media hours rather than generated seconds. Treat all of these as floors, since unusable takes still cost credits.


### Can I use AI generated video commercially?


Sometimes on free tiers and usually on paid ones, but the terms differ per vendor. Pika includes commercial use on its free plan. Other tools restrict resolution, watermarks, or export volume instead. Always read the specific plan page, because commercial rights and stock library licensing are handled separately.

- **AI video generator** describes four distinct technologies: avatar video, generative video models, assembly tools, and explainer video. Choosing the wrong category is the expensive mistake.
