# Best AI Video Summarizers [2026]

The best **AI video summarizer** for you depends on something no vendor page leads with: whether your video has captions. Most tools in this category never touch the video. They pull the *transcript* and hand it to a language model, which is why Google states plainly that only the text transcript of a YouTube video gets imported into its own notebook product.


Once you know that, the category sorts itself. This roundup covers YouTube's built-in tools, Gemini Notebook, NoteGPT, Eightify, Notta, and Fireflies, with prices verified in August 2026, plus a note about one well-known tool that is offline.


## What Does an AI Video Summarizer Actually Do?


An AI video summarizer converts a video's spoken audio into text, feeds that text to a language model, and returns a condensed written version of what was said.


That is the entire pipeline, and it runs in three steps:


1. **Get a transcript.** Either reuse captions the platform already generated, or run automatic speech recognition (ASR) on the audio.
2. **Send the transcript to a language model** with an instruction like "summarize the key points with timestamps."
3. **Format the output** as bullets, a mind map, an outline, or chat answers.


Steps two and three are near identical across every tool here. Step one is where they differ, and it is the expensive one: ASR costs the vendor real money per minute, which is why it appears as a metered limit while caption-based summarizing gets advertised as unlimited.


Captions are also less reliable than most people assume.[YouTube's automatic captions](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6373554?ref=scrimba.com) cover more than 90 languages, and Google is candid about the trade-off: captions "might misrepresent the spoken content due to mispronunciations, accents, dialects, or background noise."


Google lists six reasons captions may not appear at all, among them poor sound quality, an unsupported language, and several people speaking at once. Any one of those turns a summarizer into an error message on a video that plays perfectly.


> A summarizer is only ever as good as the transcript it never shows you. When the summary comes back thin, the transcript is usually thinner.


If you are using video to build a skill rather than to skim one,[AI tools and courses for learning to code](https://scrimba.com/articles/best-ai-tools-and-courses-for-learning-to-code/) is a better starting point. Summaries are for triage, not practice.

## YouTube's Own Tools Are Free and Already Installed


Two YouTube features get confused with each other, and only one is AI. The first is the transcript panel: free, available on any captioned video, and it hands you plain text you can copy. It is not a summarizer, but it is the raw material every summarizer runs on.


The second is[Ask YouTube on the watch page](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14110396?ref=scrimba.com) , which "lets you interact with AI to learn more about the content" of whatever you are watching. Google's documentation puts it at signed-in users aged 13 and over, across more than 190 countries, in 42 languages, on Android, iOS, the web, and TV.


Google is unusually direct about the limits. Quality and accuracy "may vary," the tool "may sometimes produce inaccurate or offensive information," and answers are not medical, legal, or financial advice.


The verdict: Ask YouTube is excellent at *asking* and useless at *exporting* . It will tell you whether a 90 minute talk deserves your evening, but it hands you nothing you can paste into your notes, and does nothing for video hosted elsewhere.


## Gemini Notebook Is the Best Free Option If Your Video Has Captions


The constraint comes first, because it disqualifies a lot of videos. Google's documentation states that "only public YouTube videos with captions, either user-uploaded or auto-generated, are supported," and that "only the text transcript of the video is imported as a source."


A second constraint bites harder in practice. Videos[uploaded less than 72 hours earlier](https://support.google.com/gemininotebook/answer/16215270?hl=en&co=GENIE.Platform%3DDesktop&ref=scrimba.com) may not be importable yet, so summarizing a conference talk the morning it goes live often fails.


Where Gemini Notebook beats every dedicated summarizer is scale sideways. The free tier holds 50 sources per notebook, each up to 500,000 words or 200MB. A whole conference track, or a semester of recordings, sits in one notebook you can query as a whole rather than video by video.


For files you own it also solves the caption problem, since uploaded audio and video are transcribed at import. A lecture recording on your laptop works, even though the same file uploaded uncaptioned to YouTube would not.


## NoteGPT and Eightify: The Dedicated YouTube Summarizers


These are the browser-first tools most people mean by "AI video summarizer," and they differ in one place that matters.


**NoteGPT** is the only tool here that publicly prices the uncaptioned case. On Pro, YouTube videos with subtitles are unlimited, videos without subtitles are capped at 120 minutes each, and local uploads at 1GB. It also batches up to 20 videos at once, plus whole playlists and channels.


One inconsistency worth flagging: NoteGPT's[YouTube summarizer page](https://notegpt.io/youtube-video-summarizer?ref=scrimba.com) advertises videos "up to 150 minutes" without subtitles, while its pricing page sets the Pro limit at 120. Where marketing and pricing disagree, pricing is the page you are buying.


From its[pricing page](https://notegpt.io/pricing?ref=scrimba.com) : Pro is $9 per month billed annually ($108 a year) or $9.99 month to month, Unlimited is $19.92 per month annually, and Max is $69 per month annually.


**Eightify** is the lighter option: a browser extension and mobile app producing timestamped key points, with summaries and translations in 40 or more languages on videos of any length.


Its pricing is harder to pin down, which is itself a fact about the product. The` /pricing` path returns Not Found, so the only verifiable numbers sit on[Apple's App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/eightify-ai-youtube-summarizer/id6467562658?ref=scrimba.com) : $9.99 monthly and $59.99 annually. A tool that will not show its price on its own website is telling you something.


Between the two:


- Choose **NoteGPT** for volume, playlists, or videos with no usable captions.
- Choose **Eightify** for a lightweight skim button inside the browser, when you never need to export a batch.

## Meeting Recordings Are a Different Product Category


Consumer YouTube summarizers fail on meetings for structural reasons: several people talk over each other, there are no captions, and the recording lives inside Zoom, Teams, or Meet rather than at a public URL.


**Fireflies** joins the call as a participant. Its free tier gives unlimited transcription but only 400 minutes of storage per team and 20 AI credits, and storage is the limit that actually bites. Pro at $10 per seat per month billed annually raises storage to 8,000 minutes and unlocks downloads. Business at $19 removes the cap; Enterprise at $39 adds private storage and a transcript-only mode.


**Notta** imports rather than attends. Its free plan covers 120 transcription minutes a month, three minutes per conversation, 50 uploads and 10 AI summaries. Pro at $8.17 per month billed annually raises that to 1,800 minutes and five hours per recording; Business at $16.67 makes transcription unlimited. Notta supports 58 languages, integrates with Zoom, Teams, and Meet, and offers a 50% education discount.


One thing deserves thought before you switch either on: a bot that joins every call records other people, not only you. Fireflies keeps private storage and its transcript-only mode on Enterprise, so on cheaper plans your recordings sit in a vendor's system in full. Under an NDA, that is a *procurement* decision, not a preference.


## Just Paste the Transcript Into a Chatbot


Nobody sells you this option, which is why it stays underrated. Open the transcript panel, copy the text, paste it into whichever assistant you already pay for, and ask for exactly the shape you want.


The advantages are real: no new subscription, no extra company holding your data, total control of the format. So are the costs: you lose clickable timestamps, the process is manual, and a three hour transcript can overflow a model's context window.


Four instructions worth keeping, in rough order of usefulness:


1. **"List only the claims the speaker supports with evidence, and name the evidence."** Separates argument from assertion faster than any summary.
2. **"List what the speaker asserts without support."** The inverse, and often the more interesting half.
3. **"Produce a timestamped outline, one line per section."** Rebuilds the navigation the transcript threw away.
4. **"Rewrite this as a checklist for someone who has not watched it."** Forces concrete steps instead of themes.


Getting more out of this route is a prompting skill that transfers everywhere else, and Scrimba's roundup of[prompt engineering courses](https://scrimba.com/articles/best-prompt-engineering-courses-2026/) covers the technique.


One caveat applies to every tool here.[FaithBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.13210?ref=scrimba.com) , a benchmark built from summaries produced by 10 modern language models across 8 model families, found that even the best hallucination detectors reach roughly 50% accuracy at spotting an *unfaithful* summary. Nothing will catch it for you, so when the stakes are real, spot-check against the transcript.


## Which AI Video Summarizer Should You Use?


Choose on two variables: where the video lives, and whether it has captions. Everything else is preference.


### The student summarizing lecture recordings


If the recording is on YouTube with captions, use Gemini Notebook. It is free, and the 50-source limit means a whole module lives in one notebook you can query across rather than video by video.


If it is a downloaded file with no captions, use NoteGPT, or Notta, whose education discount makes it cheaper across a full term of recordings.


One honest note: a summary is a revision aid, not a substitute for the work. If you cannot reconstruct the argument without rereading it, you have not learned it.


### The professional summarizing meetings


Use Fireflies when your meetings are recurring calls on Zoom, Teams, or Meet and you want them captured without thinking about it. Use Notta when you record first and upload afterwards, or when a bot in the participant list would be awkward with clients. Either way, check your local consent rules and tell people they are being recorded. That is basic courtesy, and in some places it is also the law.


### The developer summarizing conference talks


Start with Ask YouTube to settle whether a talk deserves 45 minutes, then paste the transcript for anything you need to retain. What you want from a dense talk is usually *specific* , which is where a custom instruction beats a fixed template. If the talk covers tooling you are evaluating,[AI coding assistants](https://scrimba.com/articles/best-ai-coding-assistants-2026/) is a faster read.


One adjacent tool is worth naming, precisely because it does the opposite job. Summarizing compresses a video that already exists. Generating an explainer produces a video for a question that has no video yet. **Scrimba Explain** does the second: an MCP plugin that turns a question into a narrated video walkthrough, free during open beta. It is not a summarizer and does not belong in the table above, and like any AI tool it can make mistakes, so double-check anything important. If MCP is new to you,[MCP tutorials and courses](https://scrimba.com/articles/best-mcp-tutorials-and-courses/) covers the protocol.


## Frequently Asked Questions


### What is the best free AI video summarizer?


Gemini Notebook, if your video is a public YouTube URL with captions. Its free tier holds 50 sources per notebook and answers across all of them at once. For a single video you are watching, YouTube's own Ask feature is free and instant, but exports nothing.


### Can AI summarize a video without captions or subtitles?


Yes, but only tools running their own speech recognition, and they meter it. NoteGPT caps uncaptioned YouTube videos at 120 minutes on Pro. Gemini Notebook transcribes audio and video files you upload, but will not process an uncaptioned YouTube link. Notta and Fireflies transcribe audio directly.


### Are AI video summaries accurate?


Accurate enough for triage, not reliable enough for anything consequential without checking. Summaries inherit every caption error beneath them, and Google warns automatic captions can misrepresent speech given accents or background noise. FaithBench found the best automated hallucination detectors catch unfaithful summaries only about half the time.


### Can ChatGPT or Claude summarize a YouTube video directly?


Not reliably from a link alone. The dependable method is opening YouTube's transcript panel, copying the text, and pasting it into the assistant with your own instructions. You lose timestamps and it stays manual, but you gain full control of the output format.


### Is it legal to summarize someone else's video?


Summarizing a video for your own study or notes is generally uncontroversial. Republishing a full transcript, or a substantial summary presented as your own content, depends on your jurisdiction and the platform's terms. For recorded meetings, consent rules are the more pressing issue.


## Key Takeaways


- Most AI video summarizers never process video. They read the transcript, so captions decide which tools can help you.
- Google documents six reasons automatic captions fail to generate, among them poor audio, overlapping speakers, and unsupported languages.
- Gemini Notebook is the strongest free option for captioned YouTube videos, at 50 sources per notebook.
- NoteGPT is the only tool here that publicly prices uncaptioned video, capping it at 120 minutes on Pro.
- Meetings need a different product. Fireflies joins calls, Notta imports recordings, and both transcribe audio rather than captions.
- Pasting a transcript into an assistant you already pay for costs nothing and gives the most control.
