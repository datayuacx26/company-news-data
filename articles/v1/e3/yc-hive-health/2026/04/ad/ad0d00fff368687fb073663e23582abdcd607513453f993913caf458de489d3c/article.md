# ABL vs MBL in HMO Plans: What HR Teams Need to Know

# **What Is an ABL (Annual Benefit Limit)?**


An **Annual Benefit Limit (ABL)** is the **maximum total amount an HMO will cover for a member within a single year** .


All approved medical services – such as consultations, laboratory tests, and hospitalizations – draw from this **one annual pool of benefits** . For example:


If an employee has a plan with a ₱150,000 ABL and seeks care:


- Consultation and diagnostics: ₱10,000
- Hospital confinement: ₱90,000
- Follow-up treatment: ₱20,000


Total used: ₱120,000


The employee would still have **₱30,000 remaining coverage for the rest of the year.**


Once the ABL is fully used, additional medical expenses would need to be paid out-of-pocket until the benefit limit resets the following year.


Many modern HMO plans use ABL because:


- It is simpler for employees to understand
- HR teams can easily track annual utilization
- Coverage can apply across **multiple types of medical services**


This model is especially helpful for companies that want straightforward, transparent healthcare benefits.


### **Advantages of ABL:**


- **Simple and transparent** - **** Employees can easily understand how much coverage they have left.
- **Flexible usage** - **** Coverage can be used across different healthcare services without complex rules.
- **Easy for HR to monitor** - Companies can track annual utilization and healthcare trends more clearly.


### **Consideration of ABL:**


- **Limiting experience for employees:** If one major illness consumes most of the benefit limit early in the year, remaining coverage may be limited until the next renewal cycle.


# **What Is an MBL (Maximum Benefit Limit)?**


A **Maximum Benefit Limit (MBL)** works differently. Instead of one annual pool of coverage, **MBL sets limits per illness, confinement, or medical condition** .


This means that each illness may have its **own coverage cap** .


An employee might have a plan with an ₱100,000 MBL per illness.


If the employee is hospitalized for pneumonia:


- Hospital bill: ₱85,000
- Covered under MBL: ₱85,000


Later that year, the same employee develops appendicitis. Because it is a separate medical condition, another MBL may apply, allowing coverage again up ₱100,000.


This structure can sometimes provide multiple layers of protection across different illnesses within the same year.


### **Advantages of MBL:**


- **Separate protection for multiple illnesses** - Coverage may apply independently for different medical conditions.
- **Strong hospitalization protection** - Many MBL plans are designed around hospital-based care.


### **Considerations of MBL:**


- **More complex benefit rules** - Employees may find it harder to understand how coverage works due to more conditions and rules
- **Coverage may vary per condition** - Certain illnesses may have different limits or restrictions.

# **Choosing the Right Model for Your Company**


When HR teams evaluate ABL vs MBL plans, the decision often depends on a few factors:


- **Workforce demographics** - Younger workforces may benefit from preventive care-focused plans, where ABL works well.
- **Risk tolerance and healthcare usage** - Companies prioritizing hospitalization protection may consider MBL structures.
- **Administrative simplicity** - Simpler plans tend to improve employee understanding and reduce HR administrative questions.
