The moment arrives for every successful host. It might be a late-night message about a faulty Wi-Fi password. It could be the third trip to the store this week for more paper towels. Or it’s the realization that you haven’t had a real vacation in years because you can’t step away from your phone. You’ve built a[successful short-term rental business](https://www.guesty.com/blog/secrets-to-running-a-successful-airbnb/) , but now the business is running you.


Getting help is the first step to scaling. For many hosts, the most accessible option is an Airbnb co-host. A co-host is a trusted partner who shares the responsibilities of managing your listing and your guests. It’s a way to lighten your operational load without handing over the keys to your entire business.


This guide covers the when, why, and how of hiring a co-host. We’ll explore their responsibilities, the signs you need one, and how the role compares to a full-service property management company.

- An Airbnb co-host is a partner who manages specific hosting tasks on your behalf.

## What does an Airbnb co-host do?


A co-host’s duties are flexible and defined by your agreement. They can take on a few specific pain points or manage nearly the entire guest experience. You decide how much responsibility to delegate.


### Listing and calendar management


Your co-host can manage your listing details, photos, and pricing. They also keep your calendar up to date, sync availability to prevent double bookings, and adjust rates based on seasonality and local demand.


### Guest communication and support


This is the most common reason to hire a co-host. They handle all guest messaging, from initial inquiries and booking confirmations to in-stay questions and post-trip follow-ups. A great co-host acts as the responsive, professional contact for your property. To succeed, they need tools like a[Unified Inbox](https://www.guesty.com/features/unified-inbox/) , which centralizes all messages from Airbnb, email, and SMS. This gives a co-host full context on every conversation, leading to faster, more accurate replies.


### Check-in and check-out coordination


A co-host[ensures a smooth arrival](https://www.guesty.com/blog/guide-to-airbnb-key-exchanges-and-self-check-in/) and departure for every guest. This can involve sending check-in instructions, greeting guests in person, or being the point of contact for any issues.


### Cleaning and turnover management


Between stays, your co-host manages the entire[turnover process](https://www.guesty.com/blog/cleaning-checklist-for-airbnb-hosts/) . They schedule cleaners, conduct quality inspections, and report any damages.


While a co-host can coordinate this via text messages and phone calls, a professional operation uses a dedicated system. A central[Task Management](https://www.guesty.com/features/tasks-management/) tool provides a reliable way to assign, track, and get photo verification for every cleaning, ensuring your standards are met every time.


### Restocking supplies and minor maintenance


Is the coffee running low? Did a guest report a burned-out lightbulb? Your co-host handles restocking essential supplies and coordinating minor maintenance to keep the property in perfect condition.


### Writing guest reviews


Timely, personalized reviews encourage guests to leave their own feedback, which boosts your listing’s visibility. A co-host can take on the task of writing and submitting reviews after each stay.


## 5 signs it’s time to find a co-host


How do you know when you’ve hit your operational limit? These five signals indicate it’s time to get professional help.


### 1. You’re spending too much time on guest messages


If your phone has become a 24/7 guest support hotline, you’re on the fast track to burnout. Constant messaging interrupts your day, fragments your focus, and makes it impossible to disconnect.


### 2. You live far from your rental property


Managing a property remotely introduces a new layer of complexity. You can’t easily drop by to fix a small issue or meet a contractor. A local co-host acts as your on-the-ground representative, giving you peace of mind.


### 3. You want to take a vacation without closing your calendar


Your rental business shouldn’t prevent you from living your life. A co-host keeps your business running and revenue flowing while you’re on vacation, dealing with a family emergency, or simply taking a weekend off.


### 4. Your response rates and review scores are slipping


As you get busier, it’s easy for small things to fall through the cracks. A delayed response to an inquiry or a forgotten detail can be the difference between a 5-star review and a 4.2-star rating. If your performance metrics are trending down, it’s a clear sign you’re at capacity.


### 5. You’re ready to add a second property


The operational workload doesn’t just double when you add a second property; it multiplies. The systems that worked for one listing often break with two. Hiring a co-host for your first property can free up the time and mental space you need to acquire and launch your next one.

The biggest operational difference is the technology. A co-host typically works inside your Airbnb account. ## How to find and hire the right co-host


Finding someone you trust with your property and your guests requires a structured approach.


### Start with your personal network


Your best candidate might be someone you already know. Ask for recommendations from friends, family, or local real estate contacts. A trusted referral is often the safest bet.


### Use Airbnb’s Co-Host Services Platform


Airbnb has an official platform where experienced co-hosts offer their services. You can browse profiles, read reviews from other hosts, and filter by services offered in your area.


### Vet candidates carefully


Once you have a shortlist, conduct a thorough interview. Treat it like a real job interview, because it is. Ask questions that reveal their experience, problem-solving skills, and communication style.


- What is your experience with short-term rentals in this area?
- Describe a time you handled a difficult guest situation. What was the outcome?
- How do you ensure a property is perfectly clean and ready for every guest?
- What is your availability for guest communication and emergencies?


### Create a clear co-hosting agreement


Don’t rely on a verbal agreement. A written contract protects both you and your co-host. It should clearly outline responsibilities, compensation, performance expectations, and the process for ending the partnership.


## How much do co-hosts charge?


Co-host compensation typically follows one of two models.


### The percentage model


This is the most common structure. The co-host earns a percentage of each booking’s revenue, usually between 10% and 25%. The exact percentage depends on the scope of their responsibilities. If they only handle guest messaging, the fee will be on the lower end. If they manage the entire guest experience from booking to review, it will be higher.


### The flat-fee model


Some co-hosts charge a flat fee per booking or a fixed monthly retainer. This model provides more predictable income for the co-host and predictable expenses for you. It’s often used when the co-host is only responsible for a specific, limited set of tasks, like managing turnovers.


## How to add a co-host to your Airbnb listing


Once you’ve hired someone, adding them to your listing is a straightforward technical process within Airbnb.


1. Log in to your Airbnb account and switch to your hosting dashboard.


2. Go to the **Listings** tab and select the property you want to add a co-host to.


3. Click on the **Co-hosts** tab in the listing editor.


4. Click **Invite a co-host** and enter their email address or phone number.


5. Your invited co-host will receive a notification to accept the role. Once they accept, they will appear on your listing.
