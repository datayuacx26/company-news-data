## **What is trust accounting, and why does it matter for short-term rental managers?**


Trust accounting is the legally mandated practice of keeping owner funds separate from your own business revenue. In Australia — governed by legislation like Queensland’s Agents Financial Administration Act 2014, which regulates how agents establish, manage, and audit[their trust accounts](https://www.reiq.com/articles/property-management/trust-account-principles-for-principals) — licensed property managers must maintain strict records of every dollar that flows through trust accounts. That includes guest payments, owner disbursements, commissions, and expenses.


Get it wrong, and you’re not just dealing with angry owners. Failure to comply can result in[penalties, fines, or licence suspension](https://outbooks.com.au/guide-to-real-estate-trust-account-audits/) . The stakes are high, and the margin for error is zero.


For short-term rental operators juggling dozens or hundreds of properties across multiple channels, the complexity multiplies fast. Australia’s short-term rental market is valued at approximately AUD 3.4 billion and is projected to reach[AUD 6.4 billion by 2030](https://blog.madecomfy.com.au/resources/research/brisbane-short-term-rental-market-report) . As of February 2026, STR listings alone reached[170,000 across Australia](https://switchsolutions.com/short-term-rental-growth-australia-forecast/) . Every one of those bookings generates transactions that need to be tracked, categorised, and reported in regulatory format — accurately and on time.

## **Why was this built specifically for Australia and New Zealand?**


This isn’t a global accounting module with a few regional settings toggled on. Guesty’s trust accounting solution was developed in collaboration with one of Australia’s top trust accounting audit firms, combining nearly 30 years of local expertise with global product development capabilities.


That expertise matters because every state handles trust accounting differently. NSW and Victoria run their audit period from 1 July to 30 June with reports due by 30 September, while Queensland operates on a 1 April to 31 March cycle with reports due by 31 May, and Western Australia runs a calendar year with a 31 March deadline. Guesty is built to meet QLD trust accounting compliance — one of the most demanding regulatory frameworks in the region. From ABA file generation to pre-formatted audit report packages, every ANZ-specific feature is built to match how compliance actually works on the ground.
