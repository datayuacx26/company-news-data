**TL;DR:** Guesty and OwnerRez both cover the core bases — channel management, direct bookings, unified inbox, and reporting. But OwnerRez focuses primarily on those core capabilities. Guesty adds task automation, dynamic pricing, multi-unit management, advanced analytics, an owners portal, AI-powered messaging, and 24/7 guest communication services. If you’re running a growing portfolio, the feature gap matters.


## **What both platforms offer**


Guesty and OwnerRez share a meaningful set of core capabilities:


- Channel manager with real-time sync
- Direct booking tools
- Unified inbox and multi-calendar
- Automated messaging
- CRM
- Trust accounting
- Reporting tools
- Rental agreements with e-signature
- Open API
- User roles and permissions
- Automated reviews
- Guest app


For a solo operator with a handful of listings and simple workflows, either platform covers the essentials.

### **Channel reach**


[Guesty connects to 60 booking channels](https://www.guesty.com/features/channel-manager/) through direct integrations — Airbnb, Booking.com, Vrbo, Expedia, Google Vacation Rentals, Marriott, and more — plus 200+ marketplace partners. OwnerRez supports 23 channels and 100+ integrations. More distribution means more bookings and less platform dependency.


### **Automations and workflows**


Guesty includes full task automation and custom workflows — from housekeeping triggers to post-stay reviews to maintenance scheduling. OwnerRez has no task automation. Manual coordination across a growing team adds up fast.

### **Dynamic pricing**


Guesty has a native AI-driven dynamic pricing tool built into the platform. OwnerRez offers no native dynamic pricing — you’d need to layer in a third-party tool, then manage the integration separately.


### **Multi-unit management**


Guesty handles complex portfolio structures: buildings, sub-units, amenity management, and bulk updates at scale. OwnerRez has no multi-unit management capability. For managers running apartment buildings, condo complexes, or any portfolio with shared infrastructure, this is a hard stop.


### **Guest experience and communication**


Guesty offers AI-powered messaging tailored for hospitality, WhatsApp and SMS integration, 24/7 guest communication services, and a full guest portal. OwnerRez has none of these. Guest communication gets harder to scale manually as your portfolio grows — and guest expectations aren’t getting lower.


### **Analytics and reporting**


Both platforms offer reporting, but the depth is different. Guesty provides advanced analytics with forecasting — RevPAR, ADR, occupancy trends, and performance dashboards. OwnerRez reporting covers booking records and transactional data. Guesty helps you understand your business, with forecasting, RevPAR, ADR, and occupancy trends; OwnerRez tells you what happened.


### **Financial tools and payments**


Guesty includes a native payment processor alongside third-party options, plus[liability insurance](https://www.guesty.com/features/guesty-liability-coverage/) ,[damage protection](https://www.guesty.com/features/damage-protection/) , and a[funding solution](https://www.guesty.com/features/guesty-card/) . OwnerRez relies entirely on third-party payment processing and doesn’t offer native financial add-ons.


## **Which platform fits which operator?**


**OwnerRez** suits independent hosts and small managers who want solid booking fundamentals — channel management, direct bookings, trust accounting — without much complexity. The open API and CRM make it a reasonable choice for tech-comfortable operators who are willing to build out their stack with third-party tools.


**Guesty** is built for property managers running — or planning to run — a professional operation. Guesty Lite™ supports portfolios of 1–3 listings, giving smaller operators access to Guesty’s core technology and continuous product updates. Guesty Pro™ serves larger and more complex portfolios with advanced multi-unit management, custom workflows, and enterprise-grade analytics. Both come with dedicated onboarding support.


The real question is whether you want to assemble a stack of third-party tools to fill the gaps — or run everything from one platform that was built to handle the whole operation.
