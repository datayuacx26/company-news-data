# 12 Key Strategies to Improve Student Retention for Your Institution

**When considering student retention initiatives, higher education administrators often examine how a student is integrated into the campus community both academically and socially.**


Student retention strategies that focus on these two key areas, academic interventions and a sense of belonging, are critical to student success. Developing actionable plans to identify students in need and implement supportive programs improves student retention rates by helping them get back on track and engage with campus communities.


Why is retention such a critical need? Students who don’t graduate often head down a path that leads to fewer professional opportunities. By understanding the challenges students face, campus leaders have a unique opportunity to boost overall retention rates and ensure individual students progress from admission to graduation. We’ll explore 12 retention strategies to help improve student retention and engagement.


## **1.** **Define Student Success**


Understand your institution’s vision of


[student success](https://www.readyeducation.com/articles/powering-student-success-through-connection-and-community/) , and communicate it to students. It’s much easier to identify with and work toward goals when students know what’s expected of them. Some students may become discouraged when they don’t understand the steps to take to reach undefined goals, and could be unfamiliar with the resources available to help achieve them. Lacking clear goals also makes it harder for your institution to measure success or optimize program effectiveness.


Clearly setting and communicating academic expectations, combined with supportive programming and resources, is an effective strategy for increasing graduation rates and preparing students for their future careers. Teach students good habits and offer a variety of opportunities for success by sharing expectations and breaking down goals into attainable steps.


**Use technology to guide students.** Help students keep track of their progress by guiding them through intentional pathways via interactive tracks and checklists in your student retention software system. This is a great way for students to see what they’ve accomplished and what they still need to do at a glance. This can also help administrators identify students who are falling behind (and provide support and resources) as well as incentivize and reward students who complete mandatory tasks.


## **2.** **Connect and Engage, Early and Often**


For incoming students, design orientation programming with virtual components that are accessible, engaging, and sustainable over the entire welcome process. Use your institution’s student engagement platform to help students start building important relationships with peers, student leaders, staff, faculty, and advisors before they set foot on campus!


For at-risk and non-traditional students in particular, this is a great opportunity to learn about campus services and academic expectations, and to start developing a strong bond with members of the campus community. Identify populations that might need specialized support, such as transfer and


[first-generation students](https://www.readyeducation.com/articles/empower-first-generation-students/) , and create targeted onboarding checklists outlining requirements students must complete. This might include asynchronous training, videos, and live event attendance. Collect participation data along the way to make sure everyone stays on track.


## **3.** **Cultivate Relationships with Advisors, Staff and Faculty**


Many students do not reach out to staff or faculty members for assistance with academic or social needs, and some may not know how to reach the right people. This can leave them feeling isolated, and receiving support and guidance from a professor or advisor can make the difference between a student learning to navigate obstacles or feeling out of place in college.


Connect students with a strong mentoring program that teaches them important life skills and where to ask questions, so they can easily reach out for help when needed.


Use technology to increase resources for academic advising. Make it easy to connect with and book a meeting with an advisor, faculty member, or other essential support staff to help students learn about and access available resources. For example, a meeting scheduler tool allows a student to find the right person and book a time slot right from their phone!


## **4.** **Use Data to Track Warning Signs**


Collecting data on key indicators of students who may be struggling, such as poor attendance or not earning enough credits, is a good way to address low college student retention rates by gaining a deeper understanding of which students are most likely to drop out.


Pay attention to warning signs and use your engagement platform to configure automated alerts based on behavioral triggers that will inform you of a student’s persistence level and assign tags or specific account types to target your retention efforts to specific students (e.g., first generation, second year, transfer, academic probation).


Use communication tools to check in and follow up with students in need: use email marketing software with tracking metrics to see who is opening and clicking your email content, send out reminders and check-ins with push notifications, use a community feed for discussions and resource sharing, and assign checklists of tasks to guide students on a path to success with action items they must complete. Consider incentivizing task completion with prizes, giveaways, or virtual badges.


## **5.**


**Implement Intervention Processes**


Early intervention is key to retaining students. Once you have identified at-risk students, have teachers, staff, and families work together to help them turn things around. Developing an ongoing assessment and intervention plan to identify these students and equip them with increased support and services is one of the most efficient ways to increase student retention.


Academic advisors and faculty can implement strategies that use engagement data analytics to provide timely, proactive outreach to students experiencing academic, personal, financial, or social issues, informing them about school resources and workshops that can help them address their specific needs.


Through these early interventions, faculty and advisors also begin to build relationships and trust with their students, and sometimes, that relationship and connection can make all the difference in a student’s decision to stay or go.


## **6.** **Build Community & A Sense of Belonging**


For many students, large school environments can mean getting lost rather than finding their path. It’s critically important to focus on personalizing learning, building a campus community, and cultivating a sense of belonging for all students. Establishing community both in and out of the classroom helps build a strong network for students and supports good study habits to improve academic performance.


For hybrid and virtual students navigating online learning and events, some may struggle to find and build community. Many colleges, community colleges, and universities know that disconnected students may leave school because they feel alone and isolated, and are working on supportive programs and marketing campaigns specifically focused on increasing a sense of belonging.


Start small. Developing deeper connections in small groups and learning communities, such as Residence Life, student organizations, Greek Life, volunteer programs, or by creating a network of peer mentors, makes students feel like an integral part of the campus community.


## **7.** **Engage and Partner with Parents**


Parents can be among the most important members of a student’s support network. Offer guidance on how to support students in virtual and blended settings, and share information about mental health counseling, tutoring, academic advising, and other services your institution offers. Host webinars to help parents identify and intervene when their student shows signs of academic, mental, or social struggles.


## **8.** **Gather Feedback Regularly**


Measure student happiness and program effectiveness. One of the best ways to keep students from leaving school and to prevent issues before they arise is to collect feedback regularly. Student engagement polls and surveys help you to get to know your students, how they really feel, what their needs and struggles are, what they’re happy with and excited about, and more. These insights empower you to act quickly, address issues right away, and optimize ongoing programming efforts.


## **9.** **Encourage Autonomy**


Encouraging autonomy is important in a student’s development and overall retention, but can be very challenging at times. Students need intrinsic motivation, and campus administrators are uniquely positioned to encourage individual autonomy in many ways.


Student retention software features that help students become more autonomous include:


- **Guided pathways.** Create success tracks with action items to help students become more engaged and successful both inside and outside the classroom. This can include goal-setting tracks that guide students through setting goals, tracking their progress, and celebrating wins. These pathways could also feature complementary (and even mandatory) events and workshops to provide further information and support.


- **Connection to campus resources.** Students are often afraid to ask for help or just don’t know who to turn to. Administrators can enhance their marketing and promotion of a tutoring, counseling, or mentoring program to raise awareness of support resources. Developing autonomous students also means normalizing asking for help. Highlighting and boosting the visibility of campus resources can be an effective student retention strategy, as it empowers students to seek assistance on their own.


- **A community feed.** Administrators can also partner with students and alumni to create motivational content for their community feed. It can be very powerful for a student to hear directly from peers who overcame similar challenges during their college years. Hearing others’ stories might even inspire students to get the help they need to accomplish their goals.


## **10. Help Students Discover Enriching Experiences, Outside the Classroom**


Connecting students with opportunities for involvement and leadership roles outside of the classroom makes learning relevant and enriches the overall student experience. Boredom and a lack of engagement are two common reasons students disengage, stop attending class, or even drop out of school.


Help at-risk students browse student organizations and check out activities happening around them on your campus-wide calendar, directly from their phone or laptop. Encourage them to get involved in campus life as an important part of their educational and professional journey.


A great way to help students learn about and join groups is by hosting an involvement fair. Student groups and campus departments set up customizable booths for students to visit, ask questions, and learn more about their organization.


## **11. Offer Flexible Scheduling**


For some students, including those enrolled in


[community college](https://www.readyeducation.com/articles/what-actually-works-for-community-college-student-engagement/) , workplace demands and family responsibilities present major obstacles to attending classes during typical hours or getting involved on campus. Offer flexible schedules, such as weekend and evening classes, to accommodate work schedules, as well as affordable child care options for students who are parents.


Implement accessible, virtual student life programming and groups that accommodate non-traditional students, giving them the opportunity to get involved and unlock their potential in the same way their counterparts in morning classes do.


A commuting student who drives back and forth to class with no other interactions may not think campus life is relevant or accessible to them, but by connecting them to opportunities in which they are able to participate, they are also empowered to enhance their


[co-curricular transcript](https://www.readyeducation.com/articles/co-curricular-transcripts-an-important-compass-of-college-life/) with transferable skills developed through involvement and leadership roles on campus.


## **12. Connect Students with Financial Resources**


Finances can be a major concern for students at risk of dropping out of college, especially those trying to balance their education with supporting family members. Higher education costs have been rising for decades, and some students lack access to financial aid or family support to manage the burden.


Keep students informed about jobs, scholarships, and grant opportunities in your communication outreach and community news feed, and help connect students with professional opportunities on campus and in the community. A centralized job board enables students to browse open positions, submit applications online, and schedule interviews, all in one place.


## **Student Retention FAQs**


### **What is the biggest factor affecting student retention in higher education?**


No single factor determines whether a student stays or leaves, but research consistently points to a combination of academic support and a sense of belonging as the most influential drivers.


Students who feel connected to their campus community, whether through student organizations, peer relationships, or faculty mentorship, are far more likely to persist. Students who participate in at least one student organization have a 90% retention rate, compared to 74% for those who are not involved.


### **How can institutions identify at-risk students before they drop out?**


Institutions should use early warning systems that track engagement data to give administrators a clearer, earlier picture of which students may be struggling. Indicators like low event attendance, incomplete checklists, or limited platform activity can signal disengagement before it leads to dropout. Configuring automated alerts and using engagement dashboards allows advisors to reach out proactively rather than reactively.


### **What role does technology play in student retention strategies?**


A student engagement platform can centralize communication, track co-curricular involvement, surface at-risk students through real-time data, and connect students to services all in one place. Rather than asking students to navigate a patchwork of disconnected tools, institutions that offer one unified campus experience make it easier for students to stay informed, find their place, and get help when they need it.


### **How do student retention strategies differ for non-traditional students?**


Commuter students, online learners, transfer students, and student parents face barriers that traditional on-campus students may not. These challenges include limited time on campus, fewer organic opportunities to build relationships, and competing responsibilities outside of school. Effective retention strategies for these populations prioritize flexible scheduling, virtual programming, targeted communication, and making campus resources visible and accessible from any device.


### **How long does it take to see results from retention initiatives?**


Some interventions, like early outreach to at-risk students, can show results within a semester. Broader cultural and structural changes, such as building a stronger sense of belonging or consolidating fragmented technology systems, tend to take longer to show up in retention metrics. Institutions that track engagement data consistently are better positioned to measure progress and adjust their approach over time.
