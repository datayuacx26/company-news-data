# Lex Fridman and Jason Calacanis


### Lessons from the Calacanis and Fridman Podcast


[Gerald Bate](https://substack.com/profile/14686850-gerald-bate)


Apr 22, 2021


**J. Cal Nuggets:**


What would the world look like, if the business you want to invest in works? This seems to be a huge aspect of Jason’s investing framework, regardless of the odds said business will thrive.


You get no points for ideas, execution matters.


In order to win in today’s landscape we have to be better learners, and Capitalism has led to wonderful technological advances that allow us to learn all sorts of new things on YouTube and podcasts for free.


The best skill to pick up is the ability and willingness to learn skills quickly


**Capitalism and Competition:**


Competition is good, and when businesses compete the customers win. Tesla rolled into the car industry and competed, and now their competition intends to go electric before 2030. Today, we’re living in a better world for it.


We have to do a better job defending and supporting Capitalism, because currently we are selling entrepreneurs down the drain


China has created a weird pseudo-capitalistic system, and we can't allow China to win the war on Capitalism. Would we want to live in a world where entrepreneurs could be forced into re-education camps for having faith, or making spicy comments?


Covid-19 a.k.a the Panoramic has shown that when folks care, and regulations loosened, entrepreneurs will race to find better like we’ve seen with the vaccine.


**RobinHood and GameStop:**


RobinHood is a crazy idea, and attempted to democratize and make it easier for millenials -who aren’t doing particularly well financially- to invest in the public markets


Those same folks on Reddit collaborated and did extensive research reminiscent of hedge funds from back in the day, and discovered that GameStop had more shares shorted than were available in the market.RobinHood succeeded in their goal and folks flocked to RobinHood to buy GameStop


RobinHood was growing at an unexpected rate, and the risk of insolvency grew. This is where the mistakes happen.


RobinHood had to get in front of the story fast, and put out all the relevant information for their customers, without triggering alarms with regulatory groups. This led to people filling in the blanks with conspiracy theories, which grew like wildfire.


Let’s not forget that while they were dealing with this, they also managed to raise well over $3 billion in one week.


**Relationships and Loyalty:**


The whole ordeal with GameStop led to a very public spat between Chamath and Jason. How do we deal with disagreements with friends?


Sit down and TALK to your friends. Listen for NUANCE and don’t ascribe negative INTENT


Loyalty is important, and we need to remember that in the end, we are just a series of memories we’ve made with those we love.


Tesla almost failed during the financial crisis. Elon said Tesla had 2 weeks of money left, and Space-X is failing too. Elon is functionally bankrupt and is living off loans. Elon and Jason go out and talk about this, and Jason asks for positive news, and Elon shows him the full-size clay model for the Model S. Jason believes that this dream is too important, and that this car will matter. Jason goes home and writes two $50,000 checks for the car although he didn’t think he’d be getting said car. Elon pulls it out. Entrepreneurship is super hard.


**Startup Leadership and Culture:**


Qualities of a good founder include charisma -yes, introverts can be charismatic too-, and the ability to take a fresh look at problems each time. Leaders set the standard. Remember it won’t be easy, and you will suffer.


Great founders align their companies to solve one problem, and this goal will keep you motivated when all hell breaks loose.


To build a successful startup you must build a great product -great teams usually don’t build bad products- that connects with customers. Measure connection with customers by tracking engagement. How often do customers use your product? How much time do they spend using your product? Etc


Only raise money when you want rocket fuel, because with the money comes the pressure to grow and scale quickly and this can ruin good companies.


If you’re chasing a big goal, and taking on a Herculean effort, there will be no work-life balance. This will trickle down to staff, and remember not everyone is cut out for working in a startup. This is an Olympic-level task, and Olympian-esque efforts will be demanded.


Focus on removing friction on products and services, but beware because sometimes it can go too far, like when Facebook let folks add friends to groups, without their consent.
