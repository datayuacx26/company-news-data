An operating agreement outlining the ownership structure, management rules, and other vital company information is a key document for your LLC. Creating a comprehensive plan can help prevent future disputes and maintain smooth operations within your LLC. The document must adhere to the California Revised Uniform Limited Liability Company Act's requirements, ensuring that the rights and responsibilities of LLC owners are clearly defined.

### **Step 5: Get an employer identification number (EIN)**


Any California LLC fundamentally requires an[employer identification number (EIN)](https://www.legalzoom.com/articles/how-to-get-an-ein-number) , also known as a Federal Tax Identification Number. It is an essential tool to validate your LLC and start working on business operations. This unique number, issued by the Internal Revenue Service (IRS), allows you to handle tax filings, open a business bank account and business credit card, and hire employees. Applying for an EIN is a straightforward process, with the IRS offering a free online application.


Even if your California LLC is a single-member entity with no employees, obtaining an EIN can still be beneficial. Using an EIN instead of your personal Social Security number for business purposes can help protect against identity theft. With an EIN in hand, you'll be prepared to manage your LLC's finances and comply with tax requirements.


### **Step 6: Comply with California licenses and permits**


The next step in figuring out how to form an LLC in California involves obtaining the appropriate business licenses and permits.


To operate your California LLC legally, you must fulfill federal, state, and local government regulations. This includes obtaining necessary health permits, building permits, and signage permits, among other requirements. Adherence to these regulations ensures the smooth operation of your LLC and helps to avoid potential legal issues.


Information about business licenses can be found at your local county clerk's office or the Governor's Office of Business and Economic Development's website, CalGold.


### **Step 7: Maintain good standing with the California Secretary of State**


For the continued success of your LLC, it's important to maintain good standing with the California Secretary of State and the California Franchise Tax Board. This involves filing required documents, such as the Statement of Information, and paying annual franchise taxes and other fees to the California Franchise Tax Board.


The[Statement of Information](https://www.sos.ca.gov/business-programs/business-entities/statements) contains supplemental contact information and details regarding your company's finances and must be filed within 90 days of formation. You must submit an updated Statement of Information with the California Secretary of State every two years and pay the $20 filing fee.


Prompt submission of necessary documents and payment of fees ensures that your California LLC continues to maintain good standing with the Secretary of State. This will help you avoid potential penalties and maintain a positive reputation for your business.
