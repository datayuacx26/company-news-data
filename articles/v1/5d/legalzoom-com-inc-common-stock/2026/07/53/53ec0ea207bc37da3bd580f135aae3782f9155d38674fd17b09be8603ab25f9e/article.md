# Form your Ohio LLC today

## The Buckeye State business advantage: Why start an Ohio LLC?


The primary benefit of making your business an LLC is asset protection. If your business incurs debts or faces legal action, your personal assets are generally shielded from any settlements that arise.


An LLC is a separate legal entity from its members. It can sign contracts, buy property, and take out loans in its own name. This helps you separate your professional and personal life, both for legal reasons and to increase the credibility of your business.


Ohio is a business-friendly state, with favorable laws for small business owners. Here are some of the ways Ohio works to help businesses thrive.


- **No annual reports.** Unlike most states, Ohio doesn’t require businesses to file an annual report. In fact, the state has[no ongoing state fee for most LLCs](https://www.ohiosos.gov/businesses/information-on-starting-and-maintaining-a-business/frequently-asked-questions/) , which makes long-term compliance more affordable.
- **No franchise tax.** Ohio repealed its franchise tax in 2014. Instead, the state has a[commercial activity tax (CAT)](https://tax.ohio.gov/commercial_activities.aspx) with a threshold of $6 million in annual gross receipts, giving small businesses a chance to grow.
- **Tax flexibility.** Ohio allows pass-through entities—such as an[LLC or S corp](https://www.legalzoom.com/articles/s-corp-vs-llc) —to elect to pay state income taxes at the entity level rather than pass those taxes to the individual owners. This helps owners choose the best tax method for their business.
- **Operational flexibility.** Lastly, Ohio only requires LLCs to have at least one member and appoint a[statutory agent](https://www.legalzoom.com/business-glossary/statutory-agent) before filing articles or organization. As long as you maintain compliance, how you operate your LLC is up to you.


## Before you file: Essential Ohio LLC requirements


It’s important to know the requirements for an LLC in Ohio before you start your[articles of organization](https://www.legalzoom.com/business-glossary/articles-of-organization) . This helps ensure your forms are error-free and that the Ohio SOS will accept them with no delays.


### Follow Ohio LLC name rules


The Ohio Secretary of State won’t allow you to register a business if its name doesn’t comply with state requirements. Your business name must:


- Include the words or abbreviations “limited liability company,” “LLC,” “L.L.C.,” “limited,” or “ltd.” You can always[file a doing business as (DBA)](https://www.legalzoom.com/articles/file-a-dba-in-ohio) name if you want to operate under a name without these words, but the formal entity name must include one of them.
- Be unique—that is, different from existing business names registered with the Ohio SOS and distinguishable enough that it can’t easily be confused. You can[conduct a name search](https://www.legalzoom.com/articles/how-to-conduct-an-ohio-business-search) on the SOS website to determine if your desired business name is available.
- Avoid words used to name a government agency (i.e., State Department, CIA, FBI, Treasury, etc.)
- Stay away from restricted words implying unauthorized association with regulated industries, including words like bank, lawyer, attorney, credit union, or insurance. Terms related to these industries may require additional documentation and proof of licensure paperwork.


See[Ohio Revised Code Section 1706.07](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-1706.07) for a complete listing of the state's naming rules.


### Identify a statutory agent


Ohio law ([Section 1706.09](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-1706.09) ) requires you to appoint a statutory agent for your LLC (also called a registered agent in other states). A statutory agent is the person or entity that receives service of process and other formal legal notices on behalf of your LLC. This role is required because it ensures that you can be informed of formal legal notices about your business.


A statutory agent can be a person (including yourself or an employee of your LLC) or an entity that offers a statutory agent service. Your agent must:


- Be resident of Ohio (if an individual)
- Have a physical address in the state (if an entity)
- Maintain physical presence on-site during regular business hours


### Obtain Ohio business licenses and permits


Although Ohio doesn’t have a general operating license, LLC owners may need a business license to operate in the state depending on the location and nature of their business.


For example, if your LLC sells tangible personal items or taxable services, the LLC must obtain a vendor's license from the Ohio Department of Taxation. This also means you’ll need to collect and pass on sales tax, which ranges from[6.5% to 8%](https://taxfoundation.org/location/ohio/) across Ohio.
