# How to Start an LLC in Pennsylvania

## Step-by-step guide to forming an LLC in Pennsylvania


Ready to form your new business? Follow these detailed steps to start your LLC in Pennsylvania.


### 1. Name your Pennsylvania LLC


Selecting an appropriate[name for your business](https://www.legalzoom.com/tools/business-name-generator) is the first step in forming a limited liability company in Pennsylvania, and the name must comply with Pennsylvania naming requirements. The following are the most important requirements to keep in mind:


- Your business name must include the words Limited Liability Company, LLC, L.L.C., Limited, or Ltd.
- Your name must be different from all existing businesses in the state.
- The business name cannot contain words used associated with a government agency (e.g., State Department, CIA, FBI, Treasury, etc.)
- Certain restricted words (bank, lawyer, attorney, credit union, etc.) may require additional documentation and licensure paperwork.


For more guidance, see the complete listing of[Pennsylvania LLC naming rules.](https://www.legis.state.pa.us/cfdocs/legis/LI/consCheck.cfm?txtType=HTM&ttl=15&div=0&chpt=2)


We also recommend conducting a[business entity search on the Secretary of State's website](https://www.legalzoom.com/articles/how-to-conduct-a-pennsylvania-business-search) to ensure your business name isn't already taken. This database lets you see the names of registered businesses so that you can select a unique and distinct name for your LLC.


If you aren't ready to register your LLC but are concerned your name might be taken by someone else, you can reserve it for up to 120 days for a fee. To reserve your business name, navigate to the[Pennsylvania Department of State website](https://file.dos.pa.gov/forms/business) , fill out the Name Reservation form, and pay the $70 filing fee.


### 2. Choose your registered office


Pennsylvania, unlike other states, doesn’t require a registered agent; instead, it requires a registered office address or a commercial registered office provider. The commercial registered office provider will act as a registered agent, as it receives correspondence on your LLC's behalf.


When filling out your Pennsylvania LLC paperwork, you must provide a registered office address (which may or may not be your business address). You can provide your own office or hire a commercial registered office provider that meets the following criteria:


- The registered office must have a physical street address in Pennsylvania.
- The address cannot be a P.O. box.
- The registered office must have someone available during business hours to receive correspondence.


LegalZoom provides registered agent services that provide a registered office Pennsylvania street address meeting state requirements and ensure timely and seamless delivery of important legal notices.


### 3. Prepare and file a certification of organization


To officially register your LLC in PA, you'll need to file a certificate of organization. This document (commonly called articles of organization in other states) officially establishes your LLC by laying out basic information about it.


Prepare the certificate of organization and file it with the Pennsylvania Department of State, Bureau of Corporations and Charitable Organizations. This can be done by mail or filing online through Pennsylvania Business One-Stop Shop. You’ll also pay the $125 filing fee.


To prepare your certificate, you'll need the following information:


- The LLC’s registered office address or the name and address of your commercial registered office provider and county of venue
- Name of each organizer of the LLC
- The effective date of your certificate (this could be the filing date or later)
- The signatures of all of the LLC's organizers


Along with your certificate of organization, you must file a new entity docketing statement with the Pennsylvania Department of State, Bureau of Corporations and Charitable Organizations. The docketing statement is just a supplemental form that you must attach if completing the certificate of organization on paper. You don't have to pay a filing fee with it, but you must include the following information on the statement:


- Your LLC's name
- The name and address of the person preparing for initial tax reports
- The description of the LLC's business activity
- The LLC's federal employer identification number
- The LLC's fiscal year-end


Once you file your Pennsylvania certificate and docketing statement, the secretary of state will review the filing. If the articles are approved, your Pennsylvania LLC becomes a legal entity.


### 4. Create an operating agreement


Now, it's time to draft an[operating agreement](https://www.legalzoom.com/articles/how-to-draft-an-llc-operating-agreement) , which is a document that outlines the way your LLC will conduct business.


Pennsylvania doesn't require LLCs to file operating agreements, but they can be very helpful to your business, especially when settling disputes that may arise over financial agreements and other potential litigation. Without an agreement in place, Pennsylvania courts make determinations based on state law, not necessarily what is in the best interest of the LLC and its members.


Your LLC's operating agreement should include basic details about the company as well as information about the way profits and losses will be divided, the procedure for admitting new members, the LLC’s management structure, indemnification and liability clauses, and dissolution instructions.


You can create your documents using an[operating agreement template](https://www.legalzoom.com/templates/t/llc-operating-agreement) or turn to LegalZoom’s convenient[operating agreement services](https://www.legalzoom.com/business/business-operations/operating-agreement-overview.html) for additional guidance.
