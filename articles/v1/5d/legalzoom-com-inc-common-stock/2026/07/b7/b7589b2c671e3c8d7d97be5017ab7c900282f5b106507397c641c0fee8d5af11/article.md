# Start Your LLC in Michigan: A Step-by-Step Guide

## Michigan LLC tax and legal considerations


There are some key things to keep in mind regarding the tax and legal environment in the Great Lakes State.


### Federal and state taxes


As a pass-through entity, the income and losses of the LLC are reported on the individual members' personal federal and state income tax returns. In Michigan, the flat income tax rate is 4.25%.


Michigan also offers a state-specific tax election for pass-through entities, including LLCs, known as the Flow-Through Entity (FTE) tax. This was created in 2021 as a response to the federal 2017 Tax Cuts and Jobs Act’s state and local tax (SALT) deduction cap of $10,000. (Note that the 2025 One Big Beautiful Bill Act increased the limit to $40,000, but it is set to decrease to $10,000 by 2030.)


The purpose of the FTE is to allow certain business owners to pay tax at the entity level, essentially reducing their amount of pass-through income and subsequent federal taxes owed. Eligible entities who can apply for this FTE tax credit are:


- S corporations
- Partnerships
- LLCs that file federal income tax returns as partnerships


Businesses who choose to elect into the FTE tax must do so through the[Michigan Treasure Online portal](https://mto.treasury.michigan.gov/eai/mtologin/authenticate?URL=/) , typically by March 15 of the tax year, and reelect every three years.


### Local taxes


Along with state income tax, LLC members may also have to pay local income tax depending on where they live. There are 24 cities in Michigan that levy income tax, including East Lansing, Flint, Pontiac, and more. The tax rate varies by municipality; for example, East Lansing income tax is 1% for residents and 0.5% for nonresidents.


### Local business licenses


Some cities and counties require business licenses to be able to operate there. Even if the city in which you conduct business doesn’t require you to obtain business licenses, you may still be required to obtain an industry-specific license or permit. For instance, in Bay City, Michigan, companies don’t need a general business license, but those that operate food trucks or pedicabs must apply for separate licenses.


## Common mistakes to avoid


The following are some common pitfalls that should be avoided when forming your LLC in Michigan:


- **Failing to file the annual statement.** To stay compliant and in active status, LLCs in Michigan must regularly file an annual statement. The statement is due by February 15 every year, and the filing fee is $25.
- **Picking the wrong business name.** Adhere to Michigan’s specific LLC naming requirements (noted earlier).
- **Mixing personal and business finances.** Open a separate bank account to track all business expenses and make payments.
- **Not having an operating agreement.** Having a readily accessible, written operating agreement can help you settle disputes that may arise over financial agreements and other potential litigation. Otherwise, the courts make determinations based on Michigan state law, not necessarily what is in the best interest of the LLC.
- **Not fulfilling all licensing requirements.** Follow your specific industry and profession’s licensing requirements, and ensure that if you form a PLLC, all members are licensed in the specific service.


## How much does it cost to form an LLC in Michigan?


The[cost to form an LLC in Michigan](https://www.legalzoom.com/articles/how-much-is-an-llc-in-michigan) is only $50 to file the articles of organization, making it a very affordable place to start a business. However, many LLC owners choose additional services. Here’s the complete breakdown.


- **Name reservation:** $25 filing fee
- **Formation cost:** $50 for filing the articles of organization
- **Certificate of good standing:** $50 filing fee
- **Business licenses and permits:** Varies by profession, business activity, and area
- **Registered agent service:** $249 per year on average
- **Operating agreement:** $99 and up for an online service
- **Business insurance:** Varies depending on the insurance company and type of insurance, but generally between $40 and $70 per month, per policy


The total cost to start an LLC in Michigan ranges from $50 to more than $400 if you take advantage of all the professional services available. Once you set up your LLC, you’ll also need to file your annual statement for a filing fee of $25 each year.


## Is a Michigan LLC right for you?


The legal business entity you choose should fit your business and financial needs. It’s important to consider all that goes into forming and maintaining an LLC to determine if it’s right for you.


### When a Michigan LLC is the best choice


When you operate as a sole proprietorship vs. LLC, you take on more personal liability, meaning your personal assets could be used to settle business debts and obligations. While an LLC requires submitting formal LLC formation documents to the Michigan Corporation Division and annual legal requirements, you can still be a single-member LLC and safeguard your personal assets.


Operating as an[S corp vs. LLC](https://www.legalzoom.com/articles/s-corp-vs-llc) can be beneficial if you need investors to help grow the business or want to issue stock. However, LLCs have fewer requirements and regulations, enjoy more flexible management structures and profit distributions, and have greater flexibility with tax elections. Even if you form an LLC, you can elect to be taxed as an S corporation.


### When another state might be better


If you decide not to form an LLC in Michigan, there are other states that could be good picks for various reasons:


- [Delaware](https://www.legalzoom.com/articles/how-to-start-an-llc-in-delaware) : Business-friendly tax and legal environment, no franchise tax, option for series LLCs, privacy on formation documents
- [Arizona](https://www.legalzoom.com/articles/how-to-start-an-llc-in-arizona) : No annual franchise tax, no annual report requirements, and low-cost formation fees
- [Florida](https://www.legalzoom.com/articles/how-to-start-an-llc-in-florida) : No personal income tax and low costs of formation
