# Levi’s® Jeans: Putting the Blue in Red, White and Blue

[Unzipped Staff](https://www.levistrauss.com/author/lbrinsfield/)
Levi Strauss & Co.


July 1, 2026


Ingenuity. Individuality. Enduring. These are some of the themes that emerge when you look back at the past 250 years of American history. They also describe Levi’s® jeans, the quintessential American garment.


It’s hard to talk about America without mentioning denim and Levi’s®. Denim’s story started, as many great American stories do, with immigrants and an idea. Following the California Gold Rush, Levi Strauss, an immigrant from Bavaria, and Jacob Davis, a Latvian tailor, came together with a simple idea: reinforce points of strain on work pants with[copper rivets](https://www.levistrauss.com/2025/03/24/evolution-of-riveted-denim/) . They received U.S. patent #139,121 for their riveted “XX waist overalls,” and the[modern blue jean was born](https://www.levistrauss.com/2019/07/04/the-history-of-denim/) . After the patent expired, Levi assigned the “XX overalls” a three-digit lot number: 501. That number is still used today, more than[150 years later](https://www.levistrauss.com/2023/01/23/celebrating-150-years-of-the-iconic-levis-501-fit/) , to identify our iconic five-pocket, straight-fit, button-fly jean.


The 501® Original revolutionized workwear and turned Levi’s® jeans into the pants that people have reached for at key moments in their lives for more than a century.[Cowboys](https://www.levistrauss.com/2025/08/07/levis-western-wear-heritage/) wore them in the American West. They became[symbols of rebellion](https://www.levistrauss.com/2018/12/06/levis-bag-became-symbol-rebellion/) for teenagers. Activists have turned them into canvases of self-expression and patriotism. Celebrities like[Bruce Springsteen](https://www.levistrauss.com/2026/05/27/bruce-springsteen-levis/) and Beyoncé have donned Levi’s®, as have[WWII-era women pilots](https://www.levistrauss.com/2026/03/24/levis-betty-jacket-mystery/) , astronauts like Sally Ride and revolutionary minds like[Einstein](https://www.levistrauss.com/2016/07/29/sold-the-story-behind-the-albert-einstein-auction/) and[Steve Jobs](https://www.levistrauss.com/2016/10/14/the-jean-ius-style-of-steve-jobs/) .


Simple, tough and endlessly adaptable, our jeans have become so much more than just a good pair of pants — they are now a symbol of American identity.


That arc — from reinforced working pants to cultural touchstone — is a uniquely American story that’s now on display at The Vault’s newest exhibit, “An American Original: From Innovation to Icon.” Open now through December 2, the exhibit features artifacts that span the full sweep of this story, including one of the earliest pairs of riveted denim work pants from 1879; the Trucker jacket owned by[Al Smith](https://www.levistrauss.com/2021/07/22/levis-klamath-jacket-added-to-san-francisco-exhibit/) , who argued at the Supreme Court for his right to use peyote in religious ceremonies; and the story of José Hernandez, who crossed the border into California wearing his 501® jeans and whose family still calls him “Levi” to this day. There are even some surprises, like a[custom Jeep CJ/5 Renegade](https://www.levistrauss.com/2016/10/20/off-roadin-in-an-orange-tab-1970s-levis-jeep/) in “jeans.”

Because some things don’t need to be reinvented. They just need to be worn.
