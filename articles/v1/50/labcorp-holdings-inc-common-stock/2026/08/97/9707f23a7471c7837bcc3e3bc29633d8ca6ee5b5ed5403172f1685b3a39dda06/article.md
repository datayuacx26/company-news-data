# What Pathologists Say Is Defining Cancer Diagnostics in 2026

*Cancer diagnostics are becoming more complex, and with that complexity comes new challenges for pathologists.*


To better understand the trends shaping pathology today, Labcorp surveyed 148 U.S. pathologists about the realities of practicing in an increasingly demanding environment for the newly release **Pulse of the Pathologist report** . They point to rising cancer volumes, workforce shortages, evolving technologies and ongoing reimbursement pressures that are straining laboratory operations and influencing how diagnostic services are delivered.


Pathologists continue to look for solutions that improve workflow efficiency, increase diagnostic capacity and reduce operational friction. From digital pathology and remote slide review to practical applications of AI, the findings highlight opportunities to strengthen pathology operations and support timely, high-quality patient care.


**Top takeaways**


- **90%+** believe cancer rates are rising, with many expecting multicancer early detection (MCED) to further increase pathology volumes.
- **59%** identify digital pathology as the most impactful solution for addressing workforce shortages.
- **49%** point to remote slide reading as an important way to expand diagnostic capacity.
- **66%** cite the expense of AI without reimbursement as a top barrier to adoption.
- **66%** expect comprehensive genomic profiling to account for at least 75% of their testing portfolio within three years, up from 55% today.
- **44%** expect PAMA-related reimbursement changes to have a significant impact on laboratory operations.
