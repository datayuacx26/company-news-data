# Pulse of the Pathologist

### Opportunity


Expand digital and remote workflows to increase capacity and reduce pressure on pathology teams.


## Trend 3: Careful adoption of artificial intelligence (AI)


AI is most commonly used for low-risk tasks such as archiving, indexing, and case flagging. Adoption in primary diagnosis remains limited due to cost and risk concerns.


### AI in digital pathology: Where adoption stands today


Pathologists generally view four use cases for AI in digital pathology as possible, and among those, Archiving and Scoring are most commonly already in use. We saw no material difference in adoption preferences by critical segment.


Specific use cases Total Limited Outsourcers


1. Archiving/indexing cases 30% 61% 9%


2. Flagging for follow-up 61% 68% 11%


3. Scoring cases 9% 64% 11%


4. Training new pathologists 21% 64% 22%


5. Second opinion 68% 47% 41%


6. Primary diagnosis 11% 33% 61%


### Opportunity


AI applications that reduce workload and improve consistency.


## Trend 4: Workflow models


Pathology practices are balancing in-house testing and outsourcing to manage volume. Each model presents trade-offs, with in-house workflows often reporting more friction.


> “Outsourcing helps in some areas, but it doesn’t solve the root issues.”


### Opportunity


Build more connected workflows that reduce friction across testing models.


## Trend 5: Innovation in pathology


Pathologists expect a shift toward comprehensive genomic profiling. However, the pace of innovation is creating uncertainty in implementation.


### Movement to comprehensive profiling


Pathologists expect their test portfolio will shift towards more comprehensive profiling over the next 3 years.
