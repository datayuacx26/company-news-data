# Alliance for Water Stewardship Forum 2026: Key Insights and Takeaways

## What resilience actually means


The word that kept surfacing was *resilience* . It's the obvious buzzword, and it's fair to be skeptical of any term that's suddenly everywhere. What's more interesting is that nobody means quite the same thing by it. When I asked people what it meant to them, some pointed to redundancy and hard infrastructure, others to adapting faster than a problem evolves, and others still to relationships and institutions that hold under stress.


One answer stuck with me. Resilience depends first on *knowledge* , on establishing the basic facts before reaching for models and projections. Where does the water come from? What's the storage volume, the recharge rate, the year-to-year variability? Who else relies on the same source? It's a useful corrective. Predictions have their place, but resilience built on speculation instead of facts can send you confidently in the wrong direction.


Another useful framing is that resilience isn't just about responding when something breaks. It's about recovering stronger, anticipating the next shock, and becoming better prepared with each cycle. It's an iterative practice, where trust and relationships matter just as much as the technical work. That contest over one word is worth paying attention to. A whole field reaching for the same term while meaning different things by it is a sign we're mid-shift, not settled.


That shift shows up in *where* water sits inside a business. The language has moved from corporate responsibility toward something more operational. The business case is being reframed. It's less about the savings from using less water, and more about whether there will be enough water to keep operating, secure supply, or justify permitting a new facility at all. That's a different conversation, and a more serious one.
