# How AI is Revolutionizing Corporate Water Sustainability

# Measure


## Bridging Local and Global Data Through AI


### The Challenge


Global datasets—like country-level climate projections or broad hydrologic models—often fall short when it comes to reflecting on-the-ground realities such as localized water availability, local regulations, and site-specific water quality concerns. Relying solely on these datasets can lead to inaccurate evaluations of site-specific risks and, therefore, misallocation of resources. This challenge is particularly poignant for sustainability managers who must assess water risks across multiple facilities worldwide, often without the time or resources to understand the nuance of each region.


### How AI Helps


-


**Data Integration** : AI tools pull information from a variety of sources—remote sensing, on-ground measurements, local community reports—and integrate them with global datasets to build a consolidated perspective that still reflects local realities.


-


**Enhanced Accuracy** : By combining granular, high-resolution data (e.g., sensor readings) with broader climate and hydrologic datasets, AI refines water risk models to reflect site-specific realities rather than one-size-fits-all assumptions.


-


**Focused Reporting** : Automated systems combine these datasets and flag emerging risks in dashboards, making global trends easier to interpret at the site level.


“Many businesses rely on global datasets for risk assessments, but this data often lacks the granularity needed for site-specific insights. AI can help bridge that gap,” explains Waterplan’s Water Scientist, Hemant Servia **.**


**Key Takeaway** : Local context is essential. By integrating micro-level data with broader datasets, AI ensures that water strategies reflect on-the-ground conditions, leading to better resource allocation and stronger alignment with site stakeholders. This approach not only drives smarter investments but also builds trust through evidence-based narratives that are clearer for all parties involved.


## Measuring Volumetric Water Benefits (VWBs) at Scale


### The Challenge


Companies often struggle to quantify the impact of water interventions, be it a wetland restoration or improvements in irrigation efficiency. Additionally, traditional reporting can be fragmented, making it difficult to compare projects or to justify new investments.


### How AI Helps


-


**Leveraging high quality datasets in VWBA Calculations** : While local data is always preferred in VWB quantification, access to digital tools and datasets with global/national geographic coverage can support impact assessment.Datasets include[ERA5](https://cds.climate.copernicus.eu/datasets/reanalysis-era5-single-levels?tab=overview) ,[NASA SMAP](https://smap.jpl.nasa.gov/) ,[OPENET](https://etdata.org/) , and others.


-


**Transparent Reporting** : Robust dashboards log every project’s data inputs and how final volumetric benefits are calculated, simplifying stakeholder reviews and third-party audits.


-


**Continuous Information Loop** : As more companies publicly report volumetric outcomes, AI learns from these data sets, refining future interventions and highlighting which strategies offer the best return on investment.


**Key Takeaway** : Moving away to data-based, volumetric metrics lends credibility to corporate water stewardship claims - always bolstered by on-the-ground data from implementation partners.. AI-driven tools based on scientifically credible data can ensure calculations are consistent, transparent, and easily communicated to internal and external stakeholders. This quantification also enables companies to track progress against replenishment targets and other sustainability commitments.


## Turning Complex Water Data into Actionable Insights


### The Challenge


Water data comes from many sources—on-ground sensors, weather stations, public databases, and satellite imagery. Sorting through these disparate data sets can be overwhelming, especially for companies running multiple projects across diverse geographies.


### How AI Helps


-


**Data Integration and Standardization** : AI-powered platforms consolidate hydrologic, climatic, and operational data into a central dashboard. By ensuring each data set is formatted and analyzed consistently, sustainability teams can more easily see the “big picture” of water risks and opportunities across multiple sites.


-


**Satellite and Remote Sensing** : Advanced algorithms leverage satellite imagery to monitor changes in land cover, soil moisture, and surface water extent. This scaling up of local observations is particularly useful for remote or data-scarce regions.


-


**Real-Time Analytics** : Automated monitoring systems provide continuous updates on key metrics—like water withdrawal or replenishment—offering immediate insight into whether a project is hitting its targets.


**Key Takeaway** : AI eliminates guesswork by translating valuable raw data into timely, actionable information. This robust, science-based foundation allows companies to focus on measurable outcomes—such as how much water is truly being restored or conserved in a region.


It also dramatically reduces the time sustainability managers currently spend on manual data gathering. Traditionally, external consultants, spreadsheets, and decentralized reporting formats slow down risk analysis and hinder standardization across facilities. AI platforms overcome this by structuring data inputs automatically and integrating them into intuitive dashboards that visualize water risk at local, regional, and global scales.
