# Using Technology for Volumetric Water Benefit Accounting in Water Stewardship

# Transforming VWBA Implementation with Tech


A significant hurdle in implementing VWBA is the complexity of collecting data, monitoring progress, and ensuring accurate, transparent reporting. Below are four common challenges and how technology can address them:


### 1. Gathering High-Quality Local Data


VWBA improves with reliable local data—such as groundwater recharge rates, runoff patterns, and pollutant load. In regions lacking established databases, data may be sparse or scattered.


**How Technology Helps**


-


**Satellite Imagery & Remote Sensing** : Tools like satellite imagery can track changes in land cover over time, helping practitioners measure pre- and post-project conditions at scale.


-


**AI-Based Analytics** : Machine learning models can detect data patterns from historical hydrologic information and predict trends, such as potential water availability or risks (e.g., flooding, drought).


-


**Integration of Multiple Sources** : Cloud-based platforms can integrate data from sensors, public databases, and even on-ground teams. This consolidation cuts down on the guesswork and manual collection efforts.


[Procter & Gamble (P&G)](https://www.waterplan.com/case-studies/pg) uses technology to include estimated and actual VWBs to their project portfolio, with the supporting documentation for reported VWBs linked to each project profile. Waterplan’s platform allows P&G to instantly access relevant project documentation for use in reporting, communication or program planning).


*For more information on VWB data consolidation, read*[our case study with P&G](https://www.waterplan.com/case-studies/pg) *.*


### 2. Monitoring Progress


As corporate water targets rise, the number and complexity of water projects also grow. Sustainability managers can be inundated with data sets in varying formats, making it difficult to maintain a coherent view of progress or standardize reporting.


**How Technology Helps**


-


**Automated Monitoring & Reporting** : AI-powered platforms allow users to set up real-time tracking of key metrics—such as water withdrawal or recharge—in a centralized dashboard.


-


**Digital Fact Sheets** : Instead of relying on fragmented spreadsheets, technology can turn site-by-site data into a digital “storyline” for each project, which is automatically updated.


-


**Global-to-Local Consistency** : Standardizing data formats and reporting styles across regions ensures that each location’s water interventions can be compared consistently while also capturing important local context


For instance,[Coca-Cola Europacific Partners](https://www.waterplan.com/case-studies/ccep) (CCEP) leverages technology to manage **over 50** water replenishment projects, estimating, measuring and monitoring progress towards their VWB replenishment targets in **near-real time** . Waterplan’s AI-powered platform grants CCEP visibility into each project’s individual contribution to their VWB targets, including an activity log that records data updates and edits, ensuring **transparency** in how progress is documented.


*For more information on VWB tracking, read*[our case study with CCEP](https://www.waterplan.com/case-studies/ccep) *.*


### 3. Innovative Capabilities


Many organizations lack deep in-house hydrological knowledge, which can make it intimidating to calculate volumetric water benefits or analyze sophisticated water models.


**How Technology Helps**


-


**Predictive Modeling** : AI-powered simulation tools can forecast changes in precipitation or groundwater levels, assisting decision-makers in identifying the most effective projects.


-


**Instant Access to Expert Frameworks** : Platforms designed for VWBA often embed hydrological equations, volumetric calculations, and best practices from frameworks like VWBA 2.0, reducing the learning curve.


-


**Automation of Complex Analyses** : Automated land-use classification algorithms can show conditions before and after interventions, highlighting where and how interventions are most effective.


By streamlining these complex calculations, teams can focus on strategy and stakeholder engagement rather than wrestling with unwieldy data sets.


Amazon used satellite imagery and machine learning for project monitoring to gain high-resolution, up-to-date monitoring of land cover changes for VWB calculations. The project aimed to achieve full restoration of a 177 ha area that had been burned and deforested. By leveraging Waterplan’s monitoring approach with high-resolution satellite imagery, Amazon accessed a clearer picture of “pre-project” and “with-project” land cover conditions for the assessment of the project’s performance.


*For more information on VWB monitoring, read this*[white paper](https://www.waterplan.com/case-studies/amazon) *co-authored with Amazon.*


### 4. Stakeholder Alignment and Coordination


VWBA often involves multiple departments internally (procurement, operations, sustainability) plus external stakeholders like NGOs, community groups, and third-party auditors. Coordinating input from all sides can be time-consuming.


**How Technology Helps**


-


**Role-Based Access** : Modern water stewardship platforms can assign specific tasks and data fields to each user. For instance, site managers can input data, while auditors can review and approve final metrics.


-


**Transparent Data Validation** : Built-in workflows ensure that any edits to project data—such as water withdrawal figures—are tracked and verified, improving accountability.


-


**Clear Communication Channels** : Instead of juggling email threads or spreadsheets, stakeholders collaborate in one system, leading to fewer errors and faster approvals.


[Coca-Cola Europacific Partners](https://www.waterplan.com/case-studies/ccep) leverages Waterplan’s platform and data to strategically **prioritize project investments** based on factors like anticipated VWB, fostering collaboration with internal and external stakeholders and allowing for data-driven decision making.
