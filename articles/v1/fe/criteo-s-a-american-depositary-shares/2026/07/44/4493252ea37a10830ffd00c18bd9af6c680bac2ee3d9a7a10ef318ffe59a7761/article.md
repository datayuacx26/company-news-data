If you’ve spent any time reading about digital marketing lately, you’ve probably come across two seemingly contradictory ideas.


The first is that the marketing funnel is one of the most fundamental concepts in advertising. The second is that the funnel is dead.


Both statements can’t be true.


The reality sits somewhere in the middle. The marketing funnel hasn’t disappeared, but it has changed. Consumers no longer move neatly from awareness to purchase in a predictable sequence. They discover products on social media, compare options on retailer websites, ask AI assistants for recommendations, watch product reviews on YouTube, and revisit brands across multiple devices before deciding to buy. The path has become far more dynamic than the simple diagrams found in marketing textbooks.


That doesn’t make the funnel irrelevant. If anything, it makes it more important.


Rather than serving as a rigid map of how every customer behaves, the marketing funnel has become a framework for understanding the different moments that influence a purchase. It reminds marketers that someone discovering a brand for the first time needs something very different than someone who’s already comparing products or deciding whether to buy.


Understanding those moments is what separates campaigns that simply generate impressions from campaigns that generate measurable business results.


## What is a marketing funnel?


At its core, a marketing funnel is a framework for understanding the different stages people move through before making a purchase. It helps marketers think beyond individual campaigns and instead focus on the entire customer journey, from the moment someone first discovers a brand to the interactions that keep them engaged over time.


Traditionally, marketers think about this journey in terms of awareness, consideration, conversion, and retention. Those stages remain a useful way to understand customer behavior, but they shouldn’t be viewed as a rigid sequence. Today’s shoppers move back and forth between channels, revisit products multiple times, and often discover brands in unexpected places before deciding to buy.


Think about your own shopping habits. You might notice a product while scrolling social media, research it on a retailer’s website later that evening, ask an AI assistant for recommendations the next day, and finally complete your purchase after seeing another ad a few days later. The journey isn’t linear, but it still moves through recognizable moments of discovery, evaluation, decision-making, and continued engagement.


That’s what the marketing funnel is designed to help marketers understand.


## Why the funnel still matters


It’s easy to see why some marketers argue that the funnel no longer reflects reality. Today’s shoppers jump between channels constantly. They might start researching on TikTok, continue on Google, browse a retailer’s website, ask ChatGPT for recommendations, and eventually convert after clicking a display ad. Trying to force that journey into a straight line feels overly simplistic.


But that’s not what the funnel is trying to do.


The value of the marketing funnel isn’t in predicting every click. It’s in recognizing that customers have different needs depending on where they are in their decision-making process.


Someone who’s never heard of your business needs a very different message than someone who has already added products to their cart. A shopper comparing brands is looking for reassurance. A returning customer is looking for a reason to come back. Treating those audiences as if they’re all at the same stage almost always leads to wasted media spend and lower engagement.


The funnel gives marketers a way to organize those moments and build campaigns that reflect how people actually make decisions, even if every individual journey looks a little different.


## Understanding how customers move through the funnel


Every customer relationship begins with discovery. At this point, people aren’t necessarily searching for a specific product or even thinking about a particular brand. They may be researching a problem, exploring a new hobby, or simply browsing content that catches their attention.


For marketers, the goal is to earn visibility where potential customers are already spending time. That might happen through display advertising, social media, online video, retail media, creator content, or increasingly through AI-powered discovery experiences. Success at this stage isn’t measured only by immediate sales. It’s about introducing your brand to the right audience and creating familiarity before purchase intent fully develops.


As shoppers become more interested, they naturally begin comparing options. They read reviews, visit retailer websites, ask more specific questions, and evaluate whether one product or brand is a better fit than another. Marketing during this stage becomes less about attracting attention and more about building confidence. Helpful product information, relevant messaging, and personalized recommendations all work together to reduce uncertainty and move shoppers closer to a decision.


Eventually, that interest turns into measurable intent. Shoppers revisit products, compare prices, return to websites, and often narrow their choices to just a handful of options. This is where performance marketing excels. Dynamic product ads, personalized creative, and relevant reminders help remove friction and encourage shoppers to complete a purchase they were already considering.


The journey doesn’t stop after someone checks out. Existing customers remain one of a business’s most valuable audiences because they’re often more likely to purchase again, explore complementary products, and build lasting relationships with brands they trust. Continued engagement through thoughtful recommendations and relevant follow-up marketing helps strengthen those relationships over time while creating opportunities for future growth.


## Where performance marketing fits in


Performance marketing is often described as marketing that’s focused on measurable outcomes. That’s true, but it only tells part of the story.


The real strength of performance marketing is that it allows brands to tailor campaigns to where shoppers are in their journey. Instead of showing everyone the same advertisement, marketers can adapt messaging based on behavior, intent, and previous interactions.


Someone discovering your brand for the first time may respond best to educational content or inspiring creative. A returning visitor might need product comparisons or customer reviews. Someone who abandoned a shopping cart yesterday may simply need a reminder about the items they already considered.


Each campaign serves a different purpose, but together they support one connected objective: helping customers move naturally from discovery to purchase.


That’s why the marketing funnel and performance marketing aren’t competing ideas. They’re complementary ones. The funnel provides the strategic framework. Performance marketing brings that strategy to life through campaigns that can be measured, optimized, and continuously improved.


## Why full-funnel marketing has become the new standard


For years, digital advertising was often managed channel by channel.


Search campaigns were planned separately from display. Social operated independently from retail media. Brand awareness lived in one budget while performance lived in another.


Consumers, of course, never experienced those divisions.


They encountered brands across multiple platforms, often within the same day, expecting every interaction to feel connected. The gap between how marketers organized campaigns and how shoppers actually behaved became increasingly difficult to ignore.


That’s why full-funnel marketing has become such an important strategy.


Rather than optimizing individual campaigns in isolation, full-funnel marketing recognizes that every interaction contributes to business growth. Discovery creates future demand. Consideration builds confidence. Conversion turns intent into sales. Ongoing engagement strengthens customer relationships and creates opportunities for repeat business. Every stage supports the next, creating a more consistent experience across the customer journey.


This approach has become even more important as AI introduces new ways for people to discover products. Consumers are increasingly asking conversational questions instead of typing product names into search bars. Discovery and evaluation are beginning to happen simultaneously, making consistency across channels more important than ever before.


The marketers who succeed won’t necessarily be the ones spending the most. They’ll be the ones creating the most connected customer experiences.


## The future of the marketing funnel


The marketing funnel isn’t disappearing. It’s becoming more flexible.


As customer journeys continue to evolve, marketers will rely less on rigid channel strategies and more on understanding intent. They’ll think less about whether a shopper came from social, display, retail media, or AI, and more about what that shopper needs in that particular moment.


That’s ultimately what the funnel has always been about.


Not forcing customers into a predictable sequence, but helping marketers meet people with the right message, at the right time, wherever they happen to be in their journey.


Technology will continue to change. New advertising channels will emerge. AI will reshape how consumers discover and evaluate products. But the fundamental challenge remains the same: understanding people well enough to deliver marketing that’s genuinely useful.


The funnel is simply the framework that helps make that possible.

# Frequently asked questions


## What is a marketing funnel?


A marketing funnel is a framework that helps marketers understand the stages customers move through before making a purchase. While the journey isn’t always linear, the funnel provides a useful way to align marketing strategies with customer intent from discovery through ongoing engagement.


## What is full-funnel marketing?


Full-funnel marketing is an approach that supports customers throughout their journey rather than focusing only on conversions. It connects brand awareness, customer consideration, conversion efforts, and ongoing engagement into one cohesive strategy.


## How does performance marketing relate to the marketing funnel?


Performance marketing uses measurable campaigns to support different moments in the customer journey. By tailoring messaging based on shopper intent, marketers can improve campaign performance while creating more relevant customer experiences.


## Is the marketing funnel still relevant?


Yes. Although today’s customer journey is far more dynamic than it once was, the marketing funnel remains one of the most useful frameworks for understanding customer behavior and building effective marketing strategies across multiple channels.


## What’s the difference between the marketing funnel and full-funnel marketing?


The marketing funnel describes how customers typically move toward a purchase. Full-funnel marketing is the strategy of creating connected campaigns that support every stage of that journey rather than optimizing only for the final conversion.


## Why is full-funnel marketing important?


Customers rarely discover a brand and make a purchase through a single interaction. Full-funnel marketing helps businesses create consistent experiences across multiple channels, supporting long-term growth by driving discovery, building consideration, encouraging purchases, and strengthening customer relationships.
