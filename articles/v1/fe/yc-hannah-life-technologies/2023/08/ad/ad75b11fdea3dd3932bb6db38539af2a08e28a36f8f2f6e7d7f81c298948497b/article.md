## **What Is Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM)?**


For the uninitiated, traditional Chinese medicine (TCM) is a type of alternative treatment that’s increasingly popular among people who are trying to conceive (TTC).


Unlike Western medicine, TCM holistically considers the body, mind, and environment. It views one’s health as a harmonious state achieved by balancing one’s vital forces.


At its core, this includes Qi and the Yin-Yang balance:


- **Qi (气):** The body’s vital life force


- **Yin (阴):** Cold, moist, feminine energy


- **Yang (阳):** Hot, try, masculine energy


Yin and Yang are key opposite elements that coexist with each other. A balanced Yin and Yang is ideal for optimal health, including fertility levels.


In the following sections, let’s explore infertility through the lens of TCM.


## **Infertility in TCM**


When your body’s vital energy (aka Qi) is disrupted, or there’s an imbalance of Yin and Yang, this can lead to health issues like subfertility and unexplained infertility.


### **What Does Infertility Mean in TCM?**


In Chinese medicine, fertility is often viewed as a reflection of the body's overall energy balance and harmony, including the Yin and Yang forces.


As such, TCM practitioners often describe infertility as the inability to conceive or sustain a pregnancy due to disruptions in the body’s vital energies.


These imbalances can be caused by various factors, such as:


- [Emotional stress](https://blog.twoplusfertility.com/blogs/mental-health/manage-stress-when-ttc) , anxiety, fear, and other negative emotions


- An


[unhealthy diet](https://blog.twoplusfertility.com/blogs/prenatal/foods-to-avoid-pregnant) or poor dietary choices, like skipping meals


A competent TCM practitioner can help identify and address the imbalances in your body to restore it to its natural equilibrium, thereby improving your fertility health and chances of conception.


### **What Could Cause Infertility From the Lens of TCM?**


As mentioned earlier, the main culprits of infertility are:


1. Disruptions in your body’s vital life force (Qi)


2. An imbalance in your body’s Yin and Yang energies


Some examples include:


- **Kidney Yin deficiency:** The kidneys play a vital role in reproduction, and a deficiency in kidney Yin may result in fertility issues.


[A study](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3627412/) in the Journal of Alternative and Complementary Medicine highlighted symptoms like a


[dry vagina](https://blog.twoplusfertility.com/blogs/fertility/vaginal-ph-and-fertility) , insomnia, and early or delayed periods.


- **Liver Qi stagnation:** Stress, frustration, resentment, and other negative emotions can cause


[liver Qi stagnation](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/medicine-and-dentistry/qi-stagnation) . ICYDK, the liver is a key organ in fertility health as it regulates the menstrual cycle and supports egg implantation and early pregnancy. A liver imbalance can, therefore, negatively affect your conception efforts.


- **Spleen Qi deficiency:** The spleen is another star player in fertility. According to science, the spleen


[influences sperm and egg quality](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/medicine-and-dentistry/qi-deficiency) and supplies nutrients to the growing baby after an embryo transfer. Spleen Qi deficiency manifests as early menses and heavy bleeding. It could also lead to repeated implantation failure and


[miscarriages](https://blog.twoplusfertility.com/blogs/mental-health/miscarriage-and-loss) for those undergoing fertility treatments like in-vitro fertilization (IVF).


## **Does TCM Work for Fertility?**


TCM has gained significant attention for its potential to enhance fertility. While TCM alone may not guarantee pregnancy, it can complement a healthy lifestyle and Western-based treatments like IVF, offering a holistic approach to improving your odds of conception success.


Ahead, we share key insights from the scientific community about TCM’s benefits on fertility health.


### **How TCM Can Complement Fertility Treatments Like IVF and IUI**


Research suggests incorporating TCM practices, like acupuncture and herbal remedies, alongside assisted reproductive technologies like IVF and intrauterine insemination (IUI) may lead to more favorable pregnancy outcomes.


Case in point:


[A young couple aged 28 years old](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30337989/) was treated with acupuncture when they could not get pregnant after trying for a year. Within 4-7 treatments, the couple experienced better sperm quality and the absence of ovarian cysts. They also got pregnant at the end of the treatment period.


A


[larger-scale 2022 study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35222669/) of over 7,000 women undergoing IVF found that:


- Acupuncture improved the rate of embryo implantation, pregnancies, and live births.


- The number of acupuncture treatments may influence the likelihood of success in managing female infertility.


However, other research has shown that doing “acupuncture around the time of embryo transfer”


[didn’t significantly impact IVF outcomes](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33040570/) .


It’s important to note that individual responses to alternative treatments like acupuncture may vary. As such, consulting a qualified TCM practitioner for personalized guidance is highly advisable.


### **How Can I Restore My Fertility Naturally?**

### **What Chinese Herbs Are Good for Egg Quality?**


Chinese herbs have been studied for their potential to enhance egg quality and ovarian function. These herbs are believed to promote an optimal reproductive environment to get pregnant more easily or quickly.


According to a


[2017 study](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5597078/) , the following Chinese herbs may positively impact female fertility, including egg quality:


- **Dang Gui (Angelica sinensis):** May enhance your ovarian function by improving blood supply.


- **Tu Si Zi (Cuscutae chinensis):** May boost egg quality for better fertility.


- **Sang Shen Zi (Fructus mori):** Potentially promote better egg quality.


- **Ba Ji Tian (Morindae officinalis):** Positively impacts ovulation.


- **Dan Shen (Salvia miltiorrhizae):** Encourages blood circulation to the reproductive organs.


Remember that it’s recommended to consult a TCM expert before incorporating herbs into your diet.

## **How Long Does TCM Take to Work To Improve My Fertility?**


Sure, TCM has seen success in helping some TTC folks get pregnant with or without fertility treatments like IVF. But it’s definitely not an overnight miracle.


The timeline for TCM’s effectiveness in improving fertility can vary depending on personal factors such as age, overall health, and specific fertility issues.


Some people may experience positive changes within a few months of consistent TCM treatment, while others may require a longer duration. For example, in the abovementioned 2017 study, a 43-year-old woman underwent six months of TCM treatment before getting pregnant naturally.


In essence, patience and adherence to the recommended treatment plan are essential.
