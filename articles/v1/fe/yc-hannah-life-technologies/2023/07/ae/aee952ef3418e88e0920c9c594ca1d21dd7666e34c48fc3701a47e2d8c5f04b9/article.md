## **All About the** **Vaginal pH** **💦**


The vaginal tract is an important part of the


[reproductive system](https://blog.twoplusfertility.com/blogs/pregnancy/reproductive-system-infertility) for people who ovulate and want to get pregnant.


In a


healthy vagina


, here’s what the


pH levels


look like, according to a


[2021 study](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7909242/) :


- **Lower vaginal tract:** The lower portion of the vagina is the closest to the vaginal opening. It has an


acidic environment


with a


pH level


of 3.9-4.5.


- **Upper vaginal tract:** As you move higher up the vaginal tract, the


pH balance


gradually increases to 5.7.


- **Near the** **cervix** **:** The nearer you get to the


cervix


and


ovaries


, the more the


vaginal environment


becomes


alkaline


as its


pH levels


increase to ≥7.7.


The reason why your lower vaginal tract sports an


acidic pH level


is due to the presence of


lactobacilli


, aka “good bacteria.”


According to research, a healthy


vaginal microbiome


is “


[dominated by lactobacillus](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fcimb.2021.631972/full) .” This bacterial strain produces


lactic acid


, which creates an


acidic environment


in the lower to upper vaginal tract.


While these make for some fun facts at your next Trivia game, how does the above info help you on your quest to procreation?


Here’s what a 2018 study had to say about the various


[fertility-focused benefits of lactic acid](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5904085/) :


- Kills


harmful bacteria


and viruses to minimize the risk of vaginal and


yeast infections


(more on that later!)


- Helps vaginal cells survive for as long as possible


- Stops inflammation that might otherwise negatively affect your unborn child


As you can see, the ideal amount of


lactobacilli


in your vay-jay-jay can potentially maximize fertility success rates for those who are trying to conceive. Let’s learn more in the next section 🙂


## **How Do My** **Vaginal pH Levels** **Affect My Fertility and Pregnancy Chances? 🤔**


The million-dollar question (for couples who are trying to conceive, at least): How does your vaginal pH affect your fertility and pregnancy chances?


The answer boils down to one crucial factor: sperm survivability.


### **At What** **pH Level** **, Can Sperm Survive?**


It might surprise you to hear this: Sperm actually don’t survive well in


acidic environments


— like in the lower vaginal tract! (Yes, we were mindblown by this fact, too 🤯)


Per a 2015 scientific paper,


[sperm thrives in slightly alkaline environments](https://www.scielo.br/j/medical/a/qjRg5mV765Dvs5tYjCtBwyC/) :


- For optimal sperm viability and motility, your


vaginal pH


needs to be 7.0-8.5.


- When the


pH level


drops below 6.0,


[sperm motility](https://blog.twoplusfertility.com/blogs/fertility/low-sperm-motility) (how efficiently your swimmers move toward the egg) declines.


- After ejaculation into the female body, semen’s


alkaline


pH can temporarily protect sperm. But eventually, it will succumb to the too-acidic environment in your intimate area.


A too-acidic


vaginal pH balance


works against sperm, so it’s harder for you to reach your fertility goals.


To complicate matters, if your partner has abnormal sperm parameters like low semen volume*, this means their ejaculate is now less resistant to vaginal acidity. As a result, their semen is even more likely to fall prey to the acidic lower vaginal tract.

## **Does My** **Vaginal pH** **Change Throughout My** **Menstrual Cycle** **? 🩸**


TLDR; your


vaginal pH


falls and


surges


throughout your


menstrual cycle


, courtesy of fluctuating


hormone levels


:


- For most of your cycle, your


vaginal pH


remains acidic, around 3.8-4.5.


- From the 2nd to the 14th day of your


menstrual cycle


, your body’s


estrogen levels


increase before


[ovulation](https://blog.twoplusfertility.com/blogs/pregnancy/ovulation) . This causes your


vaginal pH


to become more acidic from 6.3-6.6 to 4.0-4.4, give or take.


- During


ovulation


, your body produces more


luteinizing hormone


. This helps to keep your


vaginal pH levels


within the


alkaline


range of 7.0-14.0.


If you recall, an


alkaline


vaginal environment


is non-toxic to sperm.


Procreative sex during ovulation means that your partner’s swimmers now have a higher chance of swimming through the


[cervical mucus](https://blog.twoplusfertility.com/blogs/fertility/cervical-mucus-ovulation-discharge) toward the


fallopian tubes


in order to reach their dream destination: ✨


Ovaries


✨


## **Does My** **Vaginal pH** **Change During Sex? 👩‍❤️‍👨 👩‍❤️‍👩 💑**

- This

## **What Negatively Affects Your** **Vaginal pH** **Levels? ⛔️**


Now that you’ve learned the importance of maintaining the right

### **1. Douching**


Many of us know the perils of


douching

Introducing scented

and hygiene washes into your private parts can disrupt your


vaginal flora


, altering its


pH levels


unfavorably.


In turn, this could elevate the risk of


vaginal infections


. Think


bacterial vaginosis,


that’s characterized by bacterial


overgrowth

overgrowth

Cue the nasty-looking


vaginal discharge


and other uncomfortable symptoms that means sex isn’t on the cards tonight.


### **2. Hormonal Imbalances**


When your body’s hormonal production doesn’t sync up, it spells trouble for your


vaginal pH levels


as you cycle through your period.


For example, suppose your


estrogen levels


rise before


ovulation,


but your body doesn’t secrete enough


luteinizing hormone


during your fertile window. In that case, your


vaginal pH


remains acidic during this prime babymaking period.


Many factors can contribute to hormonal imbalances, such as an


[unhealthy diet](https://blog.twoplusfertility.com/blogs/prenatal/foods-to-avoid-pregnant) , a lack of sleep, or chronic stress. To learn more, read our detailed post on


[hormone imbalance](https://blog.twoplusfertility.com/blogs/fertility/hormone-imbalance) .

Case in point: Aquagel, marketed as “non-spermicidal,” is actually one of the most sperm-toxic lubes — at concentrations as low as 5%, Aquagel demolished sperm motility.
