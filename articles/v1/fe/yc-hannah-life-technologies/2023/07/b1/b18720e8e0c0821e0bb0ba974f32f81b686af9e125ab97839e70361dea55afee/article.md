## **Are Fertility-Friendly Lubricants Better Than Ordinary Lubricants?**


Not all lubricants are created equal, especially when you’re trying to conceive (TTC). Conventional water-based, silicone-based, and oil-based lubricants may, in fact, adversely impact sperm motility and viability (read: sperm vitality).


If you’ve previously mistaken a non-spermicidal lubricant as safe for sex-to-conceive, the following section will probably turn your world upside down.


### **Most Vaginal Lubricants Are Sperm-Toxic 😳**


A


[2019 study](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6516666/) warned that “most vaginal lubricants are sperm toxic and therefore should not be used by couples trying-to-conceive.”


In fact, the study proclaimed “Aquagel is one of the most sperm toxic ‘non-spermicidal’ lubricants currently available in the marketplace” — the results showed that Aquagel lowered sperm motility, even at low concentrations of 5%!


To complicate matters, lubricants labeled as “non-spermicidal” don’t necessarily mean “a product is sperm-safe or that it will not harm or kill sperm.”


Meanwhile, the U.S. Food & Drug Administration (FDA) has


[classified certain fertility-friendly lubricants like Conceive Plus as PEB](https://www.vcarefairhavenhealth.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/04/gamete-fertilization-embryo-compatible-lubricants-peb.pdf) .


In human-speak, PEB is a Product Code for lubes that are “gamete, fertilization and embryo compatible.”


No wonder PEB-cleared lubricants — like Conceive Plus — are identified as lubes safe for procreative sex!


### **Fertility Lubes Are Tested Against Toxins**


One key factor that sets fertility lubricants apart from their non-fertility-friendly counterparts is their rigorous screening process.


Fertility lubricants are thoroughly tested to ensure they do not contain a dangerously high amount of endotoxins.


ICYDK,


[endotoxins](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/earth-and-planetary-sciences/endotoxin) are produced by gram-negative bacteria.


To ensure the safety and well-being of both sperm and eggs, Conceive Plus is carefully screened for endotoxins.


Meanwhile, using lubricants not designed for conception may introduce ingredients or pH levels that can harm sperm.


For example, conventional lubes — such as those commonly found in your local drugstore — may contain additives or substances that could negatively affect sperm motility and vitality.


Plus, most regular vaginal lubricants can have a pH level below 7.0. Their too-acidic formula isn’t compatible with that of sperm, which prefers a neutral to slightly alkaline environment of


[pH 7.0 to 8.5](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8083857/) .
