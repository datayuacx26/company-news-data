## Why Hunting Ear Protection Is Different from Range Protection


The biggest mistake new hunters make is assuming range ear protection works the same way in the field, which consist of very different environments depending upon what game is being hunted (forests, grasslands, swamps, etc…).


At the shooting range, the goal is simple: block as much noise as possible while interfering with training as little as possible. Loud gunfire is constant and predictable at the range, with the report usually coming from multiple directions. Hearing can be beneficial during range training, for instance, being able to hear commands from the range officer and other communication from staff and other range users, with hunting, hearing can be imperative.


The forest can be very quiet, often with only 20–40 dB of ambient sound. That quiet environment is where hunters detect movement, locate animals, and communicate with partners or dogs. A shot from a rifle, however, spikes to 140–170 dB, which is loud enough to cause permanent hearing damage even in an outdoor environment.


This creates a predicament:


Hunters need hearing enhancement, not just noise-blocking protection.


Electronic ear protection solves this predicament by:


-


Amplifying safe environmental (and sometimes critical) sound.


-


Instantly compressing gunshots and other hazardous noise.


-


Maintaining situational awareness and enhancing hearing makes urgent communication easier and more intuitive..


This is why modern in-ear hearing protection for hunting and electronic muffs have become standard equipment among experienced hunters and shooters of all pedigrees.

### Low-Profile Design


Hunters shooting rifles or shotguns need a clean cheek weld. In other words, what is referred to as “shouldering”. Shouldering a long gun consists of putting the stock firmly into the area where the arm and chest meet and cocking one's head to place their cheek against the stock, putting the shooter's eye in line with the sights.


Bulky protection can shift the rifle stock and disrupt accuracy, and more importantly, dislodge the protection, exposing the shooter to potential hearing damage. That’s why many experienced hunters prefer in-ear hearing protection for hunting rather than large over-ear muffs.

## FAQ


### Do you need ear protection for hunting?


Absolutely. A single rifle shot can cause permanent and irreversible hearing damage. Even occasional exposure accumulates over time.


[Many hunters develop hearing loss after years of shooting without protection.](https://goaxil.com/blogs/axil/shooter-s-ear-protecting-your-hearing-from-gunshot-noise)


### What ear protection do hunters use?


Many modern hunters use electronic in-ear protection or best electronic ear muffs for hunting that amplify environmental sound while protecting hearing.


In-ear electronic systems are especially popular because they don’t interfere with the shouldering of long-gun and won’t be moved or dislodged when shooting.


### Can you hunt with hearing aids?


Yes, but traditional hearing aids are not designed for gunshots.


They amplify sound but typically lack the compression circuitry needed to protect against impulse noise.


Electronic hunting protection is a better option.


### What NRR do I need for hunting?


For most hunting scenarios which always occur in an outdoor environment,


[specific NRR numbers](https://goaxil.com/blogs/axil/nrr-rating-for-shooting-everything-you-need-to-know-axil) should be as follow:


-


22+ NRR for rifle hunting


-


18+ NRR for suppressed firearms


-


Higher NRR for indoor shooting environments


The key is balancing protection with environmental awareness.
