# The Complete Backcountry Elk Hunting Gear Guide

Packing for an elk hunt means building an elk system that works in rugged and even potentially dangerous terrain and environments. This gear list walks newer hunters through every essential item, why it matters, and how to make gear choices that support comfort, durability, and success in elk harvesting. Mobility and a degree of stealth should be the top priority when choosing the best gear. After consulting avid and active elk hunters, this list was compiled based on recommendations from their years of experience in the field.


# Backpacking and Expedition Essentials for Elk


## Multi-Day Pack Selection and Fit


Quality multi-day packs can easily be the backbone of a successful excursion into elk country. A pack must carry a camp, a rifle or bow, and eventually any harvested meat, so weight distribution and frame size matter. Brands like


[Kifaru](https://kifaru.net/collections/frames?srsltid=AfmBOorBMX6wZDrUjF3HJvsCqhugmddsYRLdpJQRwwEZe0axLTQoQA6s) and


[Exo Mtn Gear](https://exomtngear.com/) build strong, reliable packs with substantial load space, and were also the two name brands that kept coming up repeatedly when asking experienced elk harvesters about their packs of choice.


As a rule of thumb:


-


3000 cubic inches works for 1–3 day trips


-


5000+ cubic inches works for multi-day or late-season hunts


Pro Tips:


-


Training and conditioning in advance to understand physical capabilities and conditioning can help new hunters understand how much weight they can carry and for how long.


-


Add orange or reflective tape to packs for safety and recognition by friends and other hunters.

Like socks and merino materials, boots can make or break a hunt. Elk can reside in steep, rocky terrain, and a hunter may have to cover miles under heavy weight.

-

-


Wearing the same merino socks during a boot break-in period that will be worn in the field is recommended. Some hunts will spend many hours and even possibly days in the field, the more used to apparel a hunter is, the better.


## Rain Gear and Weatherproofing Tactics


Mountain storms can appear quickly and be severe, scuttling any sense of ease or comfort a hunter is trying to achieve. A proper rain jacket and rain pants protect and provide insulation layers from saturation. Add


[waterproof gaiters](https://www.kuiu.com/collections/boot-gaiters?utm_source=google&utm_medium=paid&utm_campaign=748497286&utm_content=746697489312&utm_term=&gad_source=1&gad_campaignid=748497286&gbraid=0AAAAADg1feuQzxGnzrjl7vQbZ1piuunJu&gclid=CjwKCAiAlfvIBhA6EiwAcErpyffytB5YteGt2lLJpHtSyMi3jifl2q62DsbwJRsqXflwN1vv2m7WgBoCTJgQAvD_BwE) ; they keep snow, sticks, and debris out of boots, and also add a layer of shin protection when pushing through dense brush or foliage. Maintaining dry inner layers is a critical function and can make the difference between a successful excursion or a very short one..


Pro Tips:


-


Refresh waterproofing with


[DWR spray](https://www.gore-tex.com/support/care/dwr) before the season. This will keep the water-resistant quality of outer layers performing at maximum efficiency.


-


Store rain gear at the top of a pack for instant access, which seems like a small thing, however, in a torrential downpour, having quick access to essential equipment and being able to get to it easily is a game-changer for a successful hunt.


# The Tools of the Trade: Rifles, Bows & Optics


## Rifle Caliber and Ammunition for Elk


A reliable elk rifle balances power and shootability. Common elk calibers include:


[.308, .30-06, 7mm Rem Mag, .300 Win Mag](https://www.hornady.com/ammunition/precision-hunter#!/) . Larger, field calibers with proven track records of ballistic performance in the field. Manuals for firearms should be checked and rechecked to ensure compatibility with certain loads and grain weights. Many hunters and marksmen swear by custom reloads; however, experienced shooters will stick to factory loads in most cases and refrain from using rounds they didn’t load or purchase themselves.


Pair a rifle with a clear, dependable scope, and remember, a quality optic is oftentimes more expensive, even MUCH more expensive than the rifle itself.


[Swarovski,](https://www.swarovskioptik.com/us/en/hunting?gad_source=1&gad_campaignid=139681997&gbraid=0AAAAADuZMWuilzXXHIFu6fD1OxXge04Gx&gclid=CjwKCAiAlfvIBhA6EiwAcErpyWJL5Q3qL5whvd0QLZbJP2PO9ltTHQOaTXdYhjkvL2dUYzH2R-7VWBoCUHoQAvD_BwE)


[Zeiss](https://www.zeiss.com/photonics-and-optics/us/home.html) ,


[Leupold](https://www.leupold.com/) , and


[Vortex](https://vortexoptics.com/) all offer excellent options and are the choice of many top professionals and experienced hunters. Make sure the ammunition type works well with a particular rifle system, test it before the season, not during it. Zeroing a rifle and optic (syncing a rifle and scope for accuracy at a specific distance) should be done well in advance of the hunt. Many ranges used for zeroing start to fill up rapidly in the weeks leading up to hunting season.


Pro Tips:


-


Zero a rifle at hunting altitude for accurate DOPE (Data on Previous Engagement). Research the optimum distance for the caliber and barrel length of the rifle and cartridge being used. For elk hunting, a 200-yard zero is considered by most to be optimal for maintaining shots within the vital zone (approximately four to six inches high or low) out to 250 to 300 yards without dialing (adjusting), which is ideal for quick shots from larger calibers (30 and higher). However, a 100 zero (often two to three inches high at 100 yards) works well for smaller calibers .223, which shouldn’t be used for elk).


-


Pack at least 10 extra rounds in a waterproof kit, as corroded ammo can affect accuracy and performance, and also cause damage to a firearm.

## High-End Optics: Spotting Scopes & Binoculars


Glassing is where success begins and generally refers to using high-quality binoculars or spotting scopes from a vantage point to meticulously scan terrain for elk, gaining intelligence before stalking for a closer encounter.


High-end optics can assist with finding, signing, judging animals, and planning stalks. Pair a spotting scope with stable binoculars and a sturdy tripod.


Pro Tips:


-


Wrap tripod legs with tape for warmth and noise reduction. Wrapping tripod legs with tape or foam covers is a great idea for providing insulation against freezing metal or carbon fiber, making them comfortable to carry over a shoulder, and protecting the legs from dings.


-


Clean lenses nightly using manufacturer-approved methods to improve clarity in excessively dry or wet conditions.

# Navigation, Safety & Emergency Gear


## Satellite Communication Devices (InReach, SPOT)


Satellite messengers can provide instant access to SOS features and keep hunting parties updated. Use tools like


[OnX Maps](https://www.onxmaps.com/t-mobile-satellite) , download ahead of time, and always bring an offline backup in case of emergency.


Pro Tips:


-


Set preset messages before the trip.


-


Bring a power bank for storms and long days, and plan accordingly.


## Bear Safety: Sprays & Storage Methods


Elk habitat can oftentimes overlap with bear habitat. Keep spray on a hip belt and store food away from camp in scent-proof bags or bear-safe canisters. Also, research the different bear species in the area, as different types of bear require different reactions for the safest results.


Pro Tips:


-


Understand that sprays can lose potency over time and mark a spray’s expiration date on a gear list.


-


Keep a cooking area 100 feet away from tents. Also, as a precaution, never bring food into a tent or any other sleeping shelter in the field.


## First-Aid Kit and Survival Essentials Checklist


A proper first-aid kit includes bandages, medications, blister care, fire starters, and an emergency blanket. Quick application tourniquets have also become staple offerings in most modern first-aid field kits.


Pro Tips:


-


Trauma shears can add another level of capability to an IFAK (individual first-aid kit).


-


Check a kit each year for expired or worn-out items.


# Field Processing and Meat Preservation


## Game Bags and Quartering Techniques


Breathable game bags protect meat from insects and dirt. Practice quartering before a hunt so work can be quick and avoid spoilage. Newer hunters should have a guide or experienced hunter with them to demonstrate proper game bag usage and quartering.


Pro Tips:


-


Hang quarters in shade to cool fast.


-


Paracord can be an excellent option for creating hanging points.


## Specialized Knives and Bone Saws


Knives are small, but they matter a lot. Many hunters carry both a fixed blade and a replaceable-blade knife, like


[Havalon](https://www.havalon.com/) or


[Outdoor Edge.](https://www.outdooredge.com/?srsltid=AfmBOoqTr-ilFvPQtsJWeOkAAh54CuDA1el1WfrqQqdDxq0-ETT8NF1Y) As a survival instrument and required hunting tool, a high-quality knife or two is essential elk hunting gear.


Pro Tips:


-


Bringing extra blades in a sealed kit is a common recommendation from experienced elk hunters.


-


Wipe knives frequently to avoid corrosion and maintain sharpness. A dull knife is often more dangerous to a hunter than a sharp one due to an increased chance of slippage or malfunction.


## Hauling Methods: Backpacking vs. Pack Animals


Whether hauling quarters with a pack’s load shelf or hiring pack animals, plan routes before taking the shot.


Saving weight and staying organized make the entire process more efficient and safe.


Pro Tips:


-


Cache non-essential items to reduce weight on the next trip out.


-


Plan routes ahead of time for smooth travel and stick to them for safer travel. Also, making sure other people (family, friends, etc) know the route and what areas will be traveled is a critical safety precaution in the event of injury or other unplanned emergency in the field.


# The Unique Advantage: Staying Mobile and Silent


## Physical Training and Acclimatization Tips


Hydrate well and train often with a pack, and spend time at elevation if possible. Even one day of acclimatization improves comfort, performance, and the chance of success on an elk hunting trip.


Pro Tips:


-


Do stair workouts with weight to prepare for steep basins as well as other conditioning exercises.


-


Spend time hiking longer distances as preparation for a longer excursion.


## Wind Direction and Scent Management


Wind dictates elk movement.


[Use wind powder to check direction often](https://deaddownwind.com/wind-detector) , and store clothes in sealed bags to reduce scent. Move only when gusts rise to stay quiet. Also keep in mind, wind and other elements can affect bullet trajectory and play a role in long-distance accuracy.


Pro Tips:


-


Keeping powder easily accessible on a chest harness or belt can ensure proper and timely usage.


-


Shifting positions as wind swirls to avoid being detected is a common practice.


# Final Thoughts: Build a System That Works


A successful elk hunt comes from a gear system built with intention: items that work together, fit a specific physique, match terrain, and reduce worry. Hunters often advise against focusing on simply getting the “best” gear; rather, consider what the “best” gear is for each individual and customize accordingly. Updating gear lists, staying organized, and subscribing to gear updates or seasonal sales, enabling buying upgrades when they matter most is a vital component to having the most suited gear for the hunt. Every step, including first order of the year, final pack-outs, and everything in between, works better when a system is built for quiet movement, comfort, and success.
