# Best Ear Protection for Shooting in 2026 — Range, Competition & Tactical

A short training session or recreational trip to an indoor range without effective and properly worn ear protection can easily cause permanent or even short-term hearing damage. Firearms produce impulse noise between 140 and 175 decibels, far[exceeding the 85 dB threshold](https://goaxil.com/blogs/axil/nrr-rating-for-shooting-everything-you-need-to-know-axil)[considered safe for human hearing.](https://goaxil.com/blogs/axil/nrr-rating-for-shooting-everything-you-need-to-know-axil)


Whether you're a first-time range visitor, a recreational/competitive shooter, or training in a tactical environment, choosing the correct hearing protection is critical. What makes hearing protection critical can require a substantial amount of research. The good news? There are options for every scenario including, from inexpensive foam ear plugs to advanced electronic systems that let you hear conversations while blocking hazardous noise levels.


In this guide, we'll take a deep dive into the best ear protection for shooting, explain how much you actually need, and assist in choosing the right setup for your specific use case.

~165 dB

Indoor ranges are even worse due to sound reflecting off of walls, increasing noise levels by an additional 5–10 dB, making proper protection even more critical, particularly with rifles and larger calibers.

## Best Ear Protection by Shooting Context


#### Indoor Range


Foam plugs + electronic muffs


The most hazardous environment due to sound reflection. Doubling up is strongly recommended. Even if muffs are knocked off during training, plugs provide a last line of defense.


#### Outdoor Range


Electronic muffs or plugs NRR 22+


Sound dissipates more naturally, but gunshot reports remain potentially damaging. Electronic options give protection while maintaining communication and spatial awareness.


#### Competition Shooting


Electronic in-ear plugs


Speed and awareness are top priorities. Rapid physical movement and sweat moisture are additional factors. Electronic in-ear provides secure fit, low profile, and clear hearing of range officer commands.

Communication and situational awareness are critical. These systems allow seamless team coordination while protecting hearing and enabling directional awareness for early threat detection.

## NRR Ratings Explained — How Much Protection Do You Actually Need?


The Noise Reduction Rating (NRR) tells you how much sound a device can block, but real-world performance can be lower than advertised.


NRR Rating Formula Real-World Reduction


NRR 22 (22 – 7) ÷ 2 ~7.5 dB


NRR 25 (25 – 7) ÷ 2 ~9 dB


NRR 28 (28 – 7) ÷ 2 ~10.5 dB


NRR 33 (33 – 7) ÷ 2 ~13 dB


This is why higher NRR and sometimes doubling up can be necessary and a best practice.

## Doubling Up — When and How to Wear Plugs + Muffs


Doubling up means wearing ear plugs + ear muffs together.


### When to Do It


- Indoor ranges
- Shooting high-caliber rifles
- Long shooting sessions


**Important:** NRR values don't stack directly. Instead, add about +5 dB to the higher-rated protection.


Also consider that hearing protection can be uncomfortable and there is an adjustment period when someone is new to shooting. New shooters should make it a point to wear hearing protection before going to the range to get used to the slight discomfort in a non-hazardous noise environment.

## FAQ


#### What is the best ear protection for shooting?


This depends greatly on the environment. Indoor ranges require maximum protection (doubling up), while outdoor and competition shooting benefit from electronic options. Hearing protection should be tested off the range beforehand and phased into gradually, starting with smaller caliber firearms. Consideration should be made for the level of training, type of firearm, and environment.


#### How much ear protection do I need for shooting?


Outdoor: Minimum NRR 22. Indoor: NRR 28+ (preferably doubled). The more hazardous the environment, the higher the protection required.


#### Can shooting damage hearing with ear protection?


Yes, if your protection is insufficient for the noise level or not worn correctly. Damage can also occur if hearing protection is unintentionally moved out of place during live-fire training or competition.
