In 2024, it’s hard to find anyone in the crypto space who hasn’t heard of stablecoins or isn’t aware of their significance. These digital currencies are designed to maintain a stable value by pegging themselves to stable assets like fiat currencies or commodities, serving as a critical bridge between traditional finance and the burgeoning crypto ecosystem. In this article, we will discuss some new establishments in the stablecoin ecosystem and how it is changing the DeFi ecosystem.


## Types of Stablecoins


Before delving into the details of new establishments, let’s briefly look into some of the major types of stablecoins out there in the market:


- **Fiat-Backed Stablecoins** : Pegged directly to fiat currencies, ensuring a 1:1 value.
**Examples** :[Tether (USDT)](https://tether.to/en/) ,[USD Coin (USDC)](https://www.circle.com/usdc) .
- **Crypto-Collateralized Stablecoins** : Backed by other cryptocurrencies, providing a decentralized approach to stability.
**Example** :[Dai (DAI)](https://makerdao.com/en/) .
- **Algorithmic Stablecoins** : Utilize algorithms and smart contracts to maintain their peg, adjusting supply based on demand.
**Example** :[Ampleforth (AMPL)](https://www.ampleforth.org/) .

## Increasing Usage of Stablecoins for Money Laundering


In recent years, stablecoins have emerged as a favored choice for hackers following cyberattacks. Their inherent stability and high liquidity make them an attractive option for laundering stolen assets. Once a breach occurs, converting ill-gotten gains into stablecoins like DAI or USDT helps maintain value and facilitates easier laundering through decentralized exchanges. The ability to quickly move these assets without the volatility often associated with other cryptocurrencies adds to their appeal in illicit activities.


However, centralized stablecoins like USDT and USDC are critical in combating money laundering and illicit activities. Issuers of these stablecoins have the authority to freeze specific wallet addresses, providing a powerful mechanism for compliance with law enforcement and regulatory mandates. This ability to blacklist and immobilize stolen or suspicious funds acts as a strong deterrent and an essential tool in the fight against financial crime.

## Why do Hackers Prefer DAI Over USDT?


While Tether (USDT) boasts the largest market cap among stablecoins, hackers frequently lean towards Dai (DAI) for several reasons. DAI’s decentralized structure, governed by smart contracts and a decentralized autonomous organization (DAO), provides a layer of anonymity and reduces the risk of regulatory scrutiny. In contrast, USDT, a centralized stablecoin, is more susceptible to government intervention and potential asset freezes, as discussed in the last section. This makes DAI a more attractive option for those wishing to obscure the origin of their funds.


> USDT being a centralized stablecoin, is controlled by Tether, allowing it to ban addresses holding illicit USDT, to comply with compliance and regulations.

## Algorithmic Stablecoins vs. Centralized Stablecoins


The debate between algorithmic and centralized stablecoins continues to gain traction. According to *Michael Egorov, founder of*[Curve Finance](https://curve.fi/#/ethereum/swap) *,* algorithmic stablecoins present a decentralized alternative that mitigates regulatory risks and asset seizures commonly associated with centralized options.


> [“If you have something totally decentralized, then it is just software running onchain autonomously, so you cannot really do anything to it, and, in principle, it’s still fully trackable …… For \[the US dollar\], keys are never yours. So, that’s a problem”- Egorov stated.](https://cointelegraph.com/news/centralized-stablecoins-pose-risk-defi-curve-finance-founder)


Algorithmic models adjust the supply based on market demand, maintaining stability without relying on a centralized issuer. This resilience is particularly important in a landscape increasingly marked by regulatory scrutiny and enforcement actions against centralized stablecoin providers.


One event that exposed the fragility of algorithmic stablecoins was the dramatic failure of[TerraUSD (UST) in May 2022. UST, an algorithmic stablecoin, maintained its peg through a mint-and-burn mechanism with its sister token, LUNA which lost confidence and triggered a massive sell-off, causing UST to depeg and LUNA to hyperinflate, wiping out billions in value.](https://www.coindesk.com/learn/the-fall-of-terra-a-timeline-of-the-meteoric-rise-and-crash-of-ust-and-luna) This serves as a cautionary tale, prompting developers to prioritize transparency, collateralization, and robust stability mechanisms in future designs.


## New Players in the Stablecoin Market


The stablecoin market is witnessing an influx of new entrants, reflecting the growing demand for stable digital currencies[. Notable examples include Ethena Labs’ UStb, backed by financial giant BlackRock](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2024/09/26/ethena-announces-ustb-stablecoin-backed-by-blackrocks-buidl/) , and established players like Robinhood and Revolut, which are exploring their stablecoin offerings. These new initiatives aim to enhance transaction efficiency and capitalize on the increasing adoption of stablecoins across various sectors, further shaping the future of digital finance.


## Conclusion


As the use of stablecoins continues to rise, their implications extend beyond traditional finance. With the increasing involvement of hackers, the preference for decentralized options like DAI, and the emergence of innovative players, stablecoins are poised to play a significant role in the evolving cryptocurrency landscape. As we move forward, understanding these dynamics will be crucial for stakeholders across the blockchain ecosystem.
