## 2024 in Review


The end of 2024 marks a remarkable year for cryptocurrency markets. Bitcoin surpassed $100,000 for the first time, the SEC approved Bitcoin and Ethereum ETFs, and the EU launched the MiCA Regulation. However, amidst these milestones, there was a troubling surge in criminal activity.


[In 2024, a staggering $2.2 billion worth of cryptocurrency was stolen in 303 separate hacking incidents, marking a 21.07% increase in funds stolen and a 7.4% rise in hacks compared to 2023.](https://en.cryptonomist.ch/2025/01/10/crypto-hack-chainalysis-counts-2-2-billion-of-stolen-funds-in-2024/) The graph below represents the number of incidents and total value stolen all across 2018–24.

## Trends in Hacking Activity


The first half of 2024 was particularly volatile, with $1.58 billion stolen between January and July — an 84.4% year-over-year (YoY) increase compared to the same period in 2023. While theft slowed down in the latter half of the year, the cumulative impact remained significant.


Interestingly in 2024, exploiters’ focus shifted from decentralized finance (DeFi) platforms to centralized services. The top exploited categories were Tokens, followed by DEX and, Lending and Staking protocols.

## Crypto Operations Linked to North Korea


North Korea-affiliated hackers were responsible for a substantial share of the year’s exploits.[They amassed $1.34 billion across 47 incidents, representing 61% of the total value stolen and 20% of the total number of hacks in 2024. This marked a 102.88% increase in value stolen compared to 2023.](https://www.orfonline.org/research/2024-a-jackpot-year-for-north-korea-s-cyber-criminals) The tactics used by these actors include advanced malware, social engineering, and insider threats, often targeting cryptocurrency exchanges and DeFi platforms.

## Trends in Money Laundering


Hackers laundered stolen funds through various methods. Private key compromises often involved bridges and mixing services like Tornado Cash. Exploited funds were often moved through some of the most popular DEXs as part of laundering efforts and to convert to tokens that could be funneled through mixers.


[Despite OFAC sanctions, Tornado Cash](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/jy0916) remained a preferred mixer, and[wallet drainers such as Pink Drainer compounded the challenges posed by malicious actors.](https://bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/cryptocurrency-wallet-drainers-stole-494-million-in-2024/)


## Recommendations


To combat laundering and enhance security, governments, law enforcement agencies, and companies should work collaboratively. Key recommendations include:


- **Enhancing KYC Protocols:** Strengthen Know Your Customer (KYC) measures for DEXs and mixing services.
- **Collaboration with Blockchain Analytics Firms:** Improve traceability through partnerships with analytics providers.
- **Real-Time Monitoring:** Implement tools to detect suspicious transactions promptly.


Countries like the UAE and the EU have made progress with clarified crypto taxation laws and the MiCA Regulation, respectively. The US introduced tailored regulations, but further clarity is needed to support compliant business operations.
