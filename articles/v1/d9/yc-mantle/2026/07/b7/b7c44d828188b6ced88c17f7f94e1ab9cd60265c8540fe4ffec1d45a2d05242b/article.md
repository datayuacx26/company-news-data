# Stop Manually Data-Entering SAFEs: The AI-Native Approach to Equity Management

If you’re currently managing your startup’s equity on a spreadsheet, nobody can blame you.


Excel and Google Sheets are great. They’re flexible, free, and great for financial forecasting. When you’re just setting up your corporate entity, tracking a couple of co-founder splits and an early advisor on a spreadsheet makes total sense.


But as you scale, a subtle shift happens. You start raising capital via[Simple Agreements for Future Equity](https://blog.withmantle.com/how-safes-work/) (SAFEs). You issue an equity offer letter to a new engineer. You bring on a strategic advisor.


Suddenly, your spreadsheet stops being a *modeling tool* and starts acting as a *database* .


And that is exactly when things get dangerous. Because manually transcribing high-stakes legal PDFs into a manual grid in 2026 is an open invitation for expensive, catastrophic data errors.


---


## The Tipping Point: Where Spreadsheets (and Legacy SaaS) Break


Most founders assume the natural upgrade path from a spreadsheet is to buy into the big brand cap table software platform. But when you look under the hood of those traditional platforms, you realize a frustrating truth: **they still rely on the exact same manual data-entry workflows as your spreadsheet.**


The old process of logging a SAFE or an option grant looks like this:


1. You close a SAFE on DocuSign (another SaaS bill).
2. You open the finalized PDF document.
3. You manually copy the Valuation Cap.
4. You manually copy the Discount Rate.
5. You manually type the investor’s full legal name and email address into your software database.


If you misplace a decimal point, mix up a pre-money versus post-money cap, or mistype a name, your source of truth is broken. When it’s time to convert those SAFEs during a preferred priced round, a single manual entry error can alter your ownership percentages by hundreds of thousands of dollars.


Why are founders paying legacy software companies thousands of dollars a year just to act as manual data-entry clerks?
