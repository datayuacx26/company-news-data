# Have Your Business Back-to-School Ready with Less Waste


***July 17, 2026***


The back-to-school season isn’t just a rush for fresh supplies—it’s a signal that businesses must rethink how they handle resource use, efficiency, and sustainability. Two forces converge at once: families squeezing in last-chance vacations before the school bell rings, and the full weight of back-to-school preparation that follows. Together, they drive a concentrated surge across retail, hospitality, transportation, grocery, and beyond—creating an outsized spike in packaging, product, and material discards at the same time. The smartest organizations already understand that this challenge isn’t about quick fixes or one-off initiatives. It’s about examining the entire system and making it work better, smarter, and more sustainably.


Doing more with less requires a practical approach: data-driven oversight, integrated solutions, and a partner that sees your operation holistically—not one that simply cleans up individual messes. Below, we explore how forward-thinking businesses across the industries Quest serves are shifting from piecemeal efforts to strategic, system-based solutions that deliver lasting results.


Scroll to find your industry!


## **Retail: Tackling Complexity with Clarity**


Retailers know the pressures of back-to-school all too well.[Recent figures from the National Retail Federation](https://nrf.com/media-center/press-releases/majority-back-class-shoppers-have-already-begun-purchasing-school-items) place K–12 back-to-school spending near $43.3 billion, one of the highest totals on record, with back-to-college spending pushing the combined figure substantially higher. That demand translates into an enormous influx of inbound freight, display materials, and packaging that flows through stores and distribution centers in a matter of weeks.


While take-back programs and recycled-content products show real progress, true impact comes from examining the entire chain—from inbound packaging to reverse logistics. The retailers finding meaningful, lasting reductions are those working with partners who provide end-to-end visibility, actionable data, and seamless program management across every location and department.


## **Grocery: Managing Perishables and Peak Volumes**


Grocery operations feel the back-to-school surge in a distinct way. Lunchbox staples, single-serve snacks, and convenience packaging fly off the shelves, while perishable inventory turns faster than at almost any other time of year. That combination creates two pressure points at once: organic food waste and a spike in cardboard, film, and plastic packaging.


The stakes are significant.[The ReFED 2026 food waste report](https://refed.org/food-waste/refed-us-food-waste-report-2026/) estimates roughly 70 million tons of food goes unsold or uneaten across the U.S. supply chain each year—a loss that hits margins as hard as it hits the environment. Grocers making measurable gains are those that connect food donation, organics diversion, and recycling into one coordinated system. When you unify these streams, you turn a seasonal spike into an opportunity for cost recovery and stronger diversion performance.


## Automotive Industry: Moving from Compliance to Optimization


Back-to-school momentum extends into the automotive sector as families prepare vehicles for busy fall schedules and service bays fill up. The industry continues to make impressive progress—swapping single-use packaging for reusable containers, recycling parts, and adopting greener processes. But here’s the hard truth: compliance alone isn’t a strategy, and simple substitutions don’t solve underlying issues when each part of the operation runs in a silo.


Used oil and scrap tires remain persistent challenges, with EPA data showing that recovery rates still fall short of their potential and improper handling continues to pose real environmental risk—a single gallon of used oil can contaminate up to two million gallons of water. Managing these streams works best when you treat the operation as an interconnected system rather than a checklist. By integrating compliance, tracking, reporting, and training under one unified platform, organizations catch inefficiencies before they become problems—and adapt as regulations and materials change.


## **Transportation & Logistics: Turning Movement into Efficiency**


Few industries feel the back-to-school surge more directly than transportation and logistics. E-commerce now accounts for roughly 16.9% of total U.S. retail sales, with online sales growing 9.7% year over year in the first quarter of 2026. That growth translates into more shipments, more packaging, and more pressure on distribution networks during peak season.


Every mile, every pallet, and every packaging decision carries a cost. Fleets generate used oil, tires, filters, and batteries. Warehouses accumulate shrink wrap, corrugated cardboard, and wood pallets at high volume. The operators who thrive are those who coordinate these material streams across facilities—recovering value where others see only disposal. A connected approach reveals hidden savings in routing, consolidation, and diversion that isolated programs simply can’t uncover.


## **Hospitality & Restaurant: Connecting the Dots for Maximum Impact**


From campus dining halls to catering companies and hotels hosting family move-in weekends, hospitality and restaurant operations face intense back-to-school demand. Food waste and packaging waste climb sharply during high-traffic periods, and with the industry already contributing meaningfully to the nation’s food loss, the financial and environmental costs add up quickly.


Leading operators have rolled out food donation programs, switched to compostables, and invested in smarter purchasing software. Yet isolated programs too often stall amid changing volumes and complex logistics. The real game-changer is a bigger view—unifying waste diversion, procurement, and inventory tracking within a connected system that responds dynamically to data. The businesses making measurable gains partner with specialists who consolidate information from multiple touchpoints, identify actionable savings, and tie sustainability targets directly into daily workflows.


## **Manufacturing: Building Efficiency into Every Line**


Manufacturers ramping up production to meet back-to-school demand generate substantial industrial byproducts—scrap metal, plastics, wood waste, packaging, and process residuals. When these streams are managed reactively, they represent pure cost. When managed strategically, many become recoverable commodities that offset expenses and support circular goals.


The most competitive manufacturers treat waste as a data problem, not a disposal problem. By capturing accurate stream-level information across facilities, they identify where materials can be reduced, reused, or resold. This visibility drives both environmental performance and bottom-line results—and it scales seamlessly as production volumes rise and fall throughout the year.


## **Construction: Managing Materials at the Source**


Late summer marks a busy building season, with schools, retail spaces, and multifamily developments racing to complete projects before the academic year begins. Construction and demolition activity generates one of the largest waste streams in the country, dominated by concrete, wood, drywall, metals, and cardboard.


The good news: much of this material is highly recoverable when handled correctly. Contractors who plan diversion from the outset—rather than sorting through commingled debris at the end—capture greater value and reduce hauling costs. A knowledgeable partner helps design source-separation strategies, secure the right equipment, and document diversion for compliance and green-building credits. The result is a cleaner jobsite and a stronger project margin.


## **Healthcare: Balancing Safety, Compliance, and Sustainability**


As students return to classrooms, campus clinics, pediatric offices, and urgent care centers see a seasonal rise in visits for physicals, vaccinations, and back-to-school checkups. Healthcare waste is uniquely complex, spanning regulated medical waste, pharmaceutical waste, hazardous drugs, sharps, and everyday recyclables—all governed by strict handling requirements.


Agencies including National Institute for Occupational Safety and Health (NIOSH) continue to emphasize rigorous protocols for hazardous drug exposure and safe disposal. Getting this right protects staff, patients, and the environment while avoiding costly compliance failures. Healthcare organizations succeed when they consolidate these varied streams under a single, well-managed program that ensures every material is tracked, documented, and diverted where possible—without adding burden to clinical teams.


## **Multifamily Properties: Streamlining Turnover and Everyday Waste**


Back-to-school season coincides with one of the busiest leasing periods of the year. As renters move in and out around the academic calendar, multifamily properties confront a wave of turnover waste—furniture, appliances, packaging, and bulk discards—on top of ongoing resident recycling and trash.


Turnover patterns and new renter segments continue to shape how properties operate, and waste management is a growing part of that equation. Property managers who implement clear recycling programs, right-sized equipment, and reliable service see cleaner communities, happier residents, and lower operating costs. A coordinated approach across a portfolio turns a recurring headache into a predictable, well-managed process that supports both resident satisfaction and sustainability goals.
