# Don’t Let Your Restaurant Get Greased: A Guide to Grease Traps


**– Mar. 04, 2026 –**


Your restaurant kitchen is a symphony of controlled chaos. Sizzling pans, clattering plates, and the constant hum of activity all contribute to creating memorable dining experiences.


But beneath the surface, an unsung hero is working tirelessly to keep your operation running smoothly: the grease trap. Ignoring it can lead to foul odors, costly repairs, and even shutdowns. So, let’s talk about how to keep this crucial component in top shape.


### **Best Practices: Your First Line of Defense**


The best way to manage grease trap issues is to prevent them from starting. It all begins with simple, consistent habits in the kitchen. The number one rule is to keep fats, oils, and grease (FOG) out of your drains in the first place.


- **Scrape, Don’t Rinse:** Before washing, make sure all food scraps, sauces, and thick grease are scraped from plates and cookware directly into the trash.
- **Install and Maintain Strainers:** Every sink drain should have a strainer to catch solid materials that could cause clogs. Clean them regularly throughout the day.
- **Avoid Boiling Water:** Pouring boiling water directly down drains might seem like a good way to clear them, but it can actually melt the grease in your interior pipes, only for it to solidify further down the line, creating a bigger problem.


Some restaurants consider using chemical or enzyme treatments to break down grease. However, these are often ineffective as they don’t remove solids and can simply move the problem downstream. Many municipalities even prohibit their use, so it’s best to avoid them altogether.


### **Warning Signs of a Failing Grease Trap**


How can you tell if your grease trap is crying out for help? Your senses are often the first to know.


- **Foul Odors:** A persistent rotten egg or sewage smell in your kitchen or bar area is a classic sign that your trap is full or not functioning correctly.
- **Slow Drains:** Are your back-of-house sinks taking forever to drain? If multiple drains are slow, the issue is likely your main grease trap. If it’s just one sink, it might be a localized plumbing clog.
- **Pest Infestations:** Old food and grease buildup is an open invitation for pests. An increase in unwelcome critters could be linked to an overflowing or poorly maintained trap.


### **Plumber vs. Grease Trap Partner: Who Do You Call?**


Knowing who to call when a problem arises can save you time and money.


**Call your Grease Trap Partner if:**


- Odors return shortly after a service.
- Sinks or your dishwasher are backing up.
- You can see visible grease buildup on the trap walls.
- The trap is near its 25% FOG capacity or you’ve received a notice from the health department.


**Call a Plumber if:**


- You have wastewater or grease flooding the kitchen floor.
- Floor drains are making gurgling noises.
- You need a trap replacement or have a broken baffle.
- You’ve had multiple service calls in a short period for lingering issues.


### **The Importance of a Maintenance Schedule**


Preventative maintenance is non-negotiable. A quarterly service schedule is the industry standard and is even required by many municipalities. Delaying service can cause FOG to solidify, leading to eroded pipes and severe plumbing damage. For a truly deep clean, consider hydrojetting services once or twice a year. This high-pressure water cleaning removes hardened buildup that regular pumping can’t, restoring your system’s flow and efficiency.
