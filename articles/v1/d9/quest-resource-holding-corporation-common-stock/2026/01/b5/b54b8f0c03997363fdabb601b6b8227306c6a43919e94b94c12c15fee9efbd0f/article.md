# Set and Achieve Your 2026 Resource Goals: Essential Strategies for Industry Leaders Using Data & AI


**– Jan. 29, 2026 –**


The new year is an ideal time to set better resource goals. For many companies, the focus is shifting from broad sustainability claims to measurable impact, cost control, and operational reliability. As we enter 2026, the big question is: How do you hit your resource, waste, and recycling targets without getting buried by complexity?


Let’s break down the biggest roadblocks—and how you can overcome them with a more streamlined, data-driven approach.


## **What’s Holding Businesses Back?**


These challenges consistently limit visibility, efficiency, and cost control.


**1. Scattered Data = Blind Spots** Imagine trying to steer a ship without a working compass. Many companies still rely on separate spreadsheets, bits of paper, and different reporting systems at each site. For example, a national retailer may find that their invoices, diversion reports, and recycling volumes are tracked differently at every distribution center. The result? Missed trends, hidden costs, and no easy way to answer basic questions like “Where can we cut expenses or improve efficiency with our waste and recycling?”


**2. Rising Costs Erode Margins** It’s not just about sustainability—resource inefficiency can hit the bottom line. Consider the impact of unpredictable haul rates, landfill fees, and fuel surcharges. A regional grocer could discover, by analyzing centralized data, that switching recycling vendors for two locations saved over $30,000 a year. But most businesses can’t spot these hidden opportunities without first organizing their data.


**3. Navigating a Maze of Regulations** No one wants to face penalties for missing an ESG disclosure or failing to recycle required materials. In 2025, for example, new state-level food waste bans required quick adaptation for food service companies. For many, manual paperwork and fractured data collection turned compliance into a full-time headache.


## **Data and Tech: Your Secret Weapons**


At Quest, we see resource oversight as an ongoing process—not just a service. Once your data is organized and your goals are clear, it’s much easier to pivot, improve, and show results. Here’s how companies are leveraging better tools:


- **Centralized Dashboards:** Clients using a unified, cloud-based portal can instantly view diversion rates, costs per site, vendor performance, and more—all in one place. This empowers teams to make quick, effective decisions.
- **AI-Powered Insights:** Smart analytics are game-changers. Imagine saving thousands by AI flagging a pattern of recurring overcharges on cardboard recycling at several depots—something that went unnoticed for months.
- **Holistic, Systemwide Monitoring:** Efficiency shouldn’t happen in a vacuum. For example, after optimizing waste pickup routes with tech, a manufacturing client could notice a spike in water usage due to a new rinse process. With a full-suite monitoring approach, they caught the issue early, recalibrated their process, and avoided added costs.
- **Reporting That Simplifies Compliance:** With ESG and CSR reporting under increased scrutiny, timely, accurate, and centralized data makes compliance easier. For instance, with the rise of AI-driven “playbooks” for corporate reporting, companies that already have clean, reliable data are years ahead in fulfilling both current and future requirements.


## **2026 Action Plan: How to Simplify Success**


Ready to up your resource game in 2026? Here are proven steps:


- **Do a Resource Audit:** Start by getting a complete view. Map out every stream—solid waste, organics, recyclables, water, energy—across all locations. Engage your site teams for insights they might have on inefficiencies or improvement points.
- **Centralize Your Data and Reporting:** Choose a system or partner that can bring all your data together. This often yields “quick wins,” like identifying bills that don’t match contract terms or flagging missed vendor pickups.
- **Make Smart Tech Choices:** Don’t rely solely on manual tracking. While automation—like extracting data from invoices, disposal reports, and equipment sensors—unlocks efficiency and insight, it matters how and why you deploy new tools. Be sure you fully understand how these tools work and what it means for your operations. It’s easy to get swept up by the latest tech trends, but not every solution will be a true fit. That’s why Quest stands behind a systems-based approach. We look at the big picture, from operational savings to environmental impact.
- **Set Realistic, Systemwide Goals:** Select 2–3 key metrics, like landfill diversion rates, cost per ton, or energy use per site. Make sure goals are specific, measurable, and tracked over time. A retail client, for example, could achieve a 10% cost reduction by zeroing in on just two high-impact KPIs for six months.
- **Think Beyond the Bin:** Don’t stop with waste and recycling. Include energy management and water use in your targets. Look for patterns—like whether process changes that improve recycling also increase energy consumption or water needs—so you can optimize holistically instead of shifting problems from one area to another.
