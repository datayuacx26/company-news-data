## How Industrial Cardboard Recycling Works


While each operation is different, most industrial recycling systems follow a similar process.


### Collection Within the Facility


Cardboard is gathered from key areas such as receiving docks, packing stations, and production floors. Designated collection zones help keep materials organized.


### Preparation and Handling


Boxes are typically flattened to save space. Larger operations may use baling equipment to compress cardboard into dense bundles that are easier to store and transport.


### Scheduled Pickup


Recycling providers collect cardboard based on the facility’s volume and needs. Regular pickups make sure materials do not accumulate or disrupt operations.


### Processing and Reuse


Once collected, cardboard is sorted and processed into new fiber. This fiber is then used to create new corrugated packaging and paper products.


Through Carrollton recycling programs, businesses become part of a system that keeps materials moving forward.


## Choosing the Right Recycling Approach


Selecting the right setup depends on how your facility operates. Businesses should consider a few key factors when evaluating recycling options.


**Volume of material**
Facilities generating large amounts of cardboard may benefit from baling or compactor systems or more frequent pickups.


**Available space**
Space constraints can influence whether equipment like balers, compactors or designated storage areas are needed.


**Workflow integration**
Recycling should fit naturally into existing processes without slowing down operations.


**Scalability**
As your business grows, your recycling system should be able to grow with it.


By aligning recycling solutions with operational needs, businesses can create a system that works consistently over time.
