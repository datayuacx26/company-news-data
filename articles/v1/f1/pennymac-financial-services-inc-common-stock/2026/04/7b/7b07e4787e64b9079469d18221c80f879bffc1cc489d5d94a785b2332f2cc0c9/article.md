Taking the first step toward buying a home feels even better when you understand the process. Learn how mortgages work, what impacts approval and how to prepare financially for your purchase.


Buying your first home is one of the most exciting milestones in life. It marks the beginning of a new chapter where you have the freedom to paint the walls any color you choose, plant a garden or simply put down roots in a community you love. The path to homeownership involves several steps, and having the right information can help you navigate it all.


For many[first-time buyers](https://www.pennymac.com/blog/first-time-homebuyer-loan) , the financial side of things, specifically the mortgage, is the biggest factor to consider. Understanding how home loans work, what lenders look for and which programs suit your needs is essential. This guide can help you move forward with confidence.


## Are You Ready to Buy Your First Home?


Deciding to transition from renting to owning is a significant lifestyle change. While building equity is a smart financial move, you want to ensure the timing is right for your personal situation.


Being ready isn't just about having the money for a down payment; it is also about your long-term goals and your readiness to handle the daily responsibilities that come with owning property.


### Lifestyle Considerations


Housing experts generally suggest that you should ideally plan to stay in your home for at least five years. This timeframe gives you a chance to recover closing costs and start paying down your principal balance. Before you start browsing listings, ask yourself:


- **Stability:** Are you ready to commit to living in one location for several years?
- **Maintenance:** Are you prepared for tasks like mowing the lawn, fixing leaky faucets or managing contractors for repairs?
- **Career:** Do you expect to maintain or increase your income?


### Financial Readiness Questions


If you feel settled in your career and lifestyle, the next step is evaluating your financial health. A mortgage is a long-term commitment that includes both upfront costs and ongoing monthly payments, taxes and insurance. Ask Yourself:


- Do I have a reliable income stream?
- Have I saved enough for initial costs like a down payment and closing costs, as well as an emergency fund for unexpected expenses?
- Is my current debt manageable enough to take on a new mortgage payment?
- Am I prepared for other homeownership expenses, such as property taxes, homeowners insurance and potential HOA fees?
- Have I budgeted for routine costs like HVAC maintenance and unexpected repairs?


Taking an honest look at these factors will help you determine if now is the right time to apply for a loan.


## Key Financial Factors That Affect Mortgage Approval


When you apply for a mortgage, lenders review several core components of your financial profile to determine eligibility and loan terms. These typically include:


- **Credit score:** Your credit history shows how you've managed debt over time. A stronger score may improve your chances of approval and help you qualify for more competitive terms
- **Debt levels:** Lenders review your existing debt obligations to understand how much you already pay each month. Lower overall debt may make it easier to qualify for a mortgage.
- **Income stability:** Lenders look for consistent, verifiable income. Steady employment history can strengthen your application, while self-employed or variable-income borrowers may need to provide additional documentation.
- **Assets and cash reserves:** Available funds for your down payment and closing costs are reviewed, along with any required reserves that demonstrate financial stability after closing.


## How Your Credit Score Impacts Your Mortgage


Let’s take a closer look at your credit score and history, since they have a major impact on your mortgage application.


Your credit score is a three-digit number that reflects how you’ve handled debt over time. It's based on factors such as:


- Payment history
- Amount of debt you carry and available credit
- Length of your credit history
- Types of credit accounts
- Recent credit activity


Your credit report includes detailed information about your accounts, balances and payment history, along with any negative marks such as late payments or collections.


Lenders review both your score and your full credit report to assess risk. In general, a higher score may help you qualify for more competitive rates and loan terms. A lower score could limit options or increase borrowing costs.


Before[starting the mortgage application](https://www.pennymac.com/blog/explaining-the-home-loan-process-part-2-the-application) process, review your credit report carefully. You’re entitled to one free copy every 12 months from each of the three nationwide credit reporting companies at[AnnualCreditReport.com](https://www.annualcreditreport.com/index.action) . Checking your own credit does not affect your score and gives you time to correct any errors.


Generally, lenders look for a minimum credit score between 620 and 640 for many loan programs, though some government-backed loans allow for lower scores. If your score needs work, you can take steps to[increase your chances of getting approved](https://www.pennymac.com/blog/increase-your-chances-of-getting-approved) , by paying down balances and making all bill payments on time.


## Down Payments, Closing Costs and Upfront Expenses


Buying a home comes with several financial considerations, and understanding these upfront costs is key to planning your budget effectively.


### Down payment


A down payment is the upfront amount you pay toward the purchase price of your home, typically expressed as a percentage. This payment reduces the total loan amount you need to borrow.


A common misconception is that you need a[20% down payment](https://www.pennymac.com/blog/down-payment-options) to buy a home. While putting 20% down helps you avoid private mortgage insurance (PMI) on conventional loans, it is not a requirement for homeownership. Many first-time buyers successfully purchase homes with much less. For example:


- **Conventional Loans:** Minimum down payment as low as 3% for first-time buyers.
- **FHA Loans:** Minimum down payment of 3.5% for qualifying borrowers.
- **VA Loans:** 0% down payment option for eligible service members and veterans.


### Closing costs


In addition to the down payment, you must be prepared for closing costs. These are fees paid to third parties to facilitate the transaction. Closing costs typically range from 2% to 6% of the total loan amount. Common closing costs may include:


- Appraisal fees to determine the home’s market value
- Title search and title insurance to verify ownership and protect against claims
- Loan origination fees charged by the lender
- Attorney or escrow fees depending on state requirements
- Prepaid property taxes and homeowners insurance


In some cases, borrowers may be able to roll certain closing costs into the loan amount or negotiate seller concessions, depending on the loan program and transaction terms. Your real estate agent can help you understand what options may be available.


### Other upfront expenses


There are additional out-of-pocket costs to plan for before and around closing. Building the following potential expenses into your budget early can help you avoid last-minute surprises and feel more financially prepared on closing day.


- Home inspection fees (often paid before closing, though some buyers arrange to pay at closing)
- Earnest money deposit submitted with your offer, which is paid upfront but typically applied to your total cash to close
- Moving expenses (packing, movers, truck rental, storage)
- Utility deposits or service transfer fees
- Immediate repairs or minor updates you may want to complete before moving in


## How Much Can You Afford?


So, how large of a mortgage can you afford? The answer depends on two things: what a lender will approve and what feels manageable for your budget.


### How Lenders Measure Affordability


Lenders rely on a calculation called your "[debt-to-income ratio (DTI)](https://www.pennymac.com/blog/what-is-dti-debt-to-income-ratio) " to evaluate how much home you may be able to afford. DTI compares your total monthly debt payments to your gross monthly income. This includes:


- Your projected mortgage payment (principal and interest)
- Property taxes and homeowners insurance
- Mortgage insurance, if applicable
- HOA dues, if required
- Existing debts such as credit cards, auto loans and student loans


As a general rule, many loan programs require that your total monthly debt obligations fall between 45% and 55% of your gross monthly income, depending on the loan type and your overall financial profile.


### What You’re Comfortable Affording


Determining your budget is about more than just what a lender says you qualify for. It is about what you are comfortable paying every month without stretching your finances too thin.


Even if you qualify within lending guidelines, you may choose a lower price point to leave room for savings, investments or long-term goals like retirement. Finding that balance is key to making a confident and informed decision.


A Pennymac Loan Expert can review your income, debts and goals to help determine a housing price that fits both lending guidelines and your personal comfort level.


You can also use online tools to run the numbers yourself. A[mortgage calculator](https://www.pennymac.com/mortgage-calculators/how-much-can-i-borrow) is an excellent resource for estimating your monthly payment under different scenarios, helping you see how interest rates and down payment amounts impact your bottom line.


## The Importance of a Mortgage Pre-Approval


Another way to determine how large a mortgage you can afford is by getting **pre-approved** for a loan. A pre-approval not only gives you a clear understanding of your budget but also demonstrates to sellers that you’re a serious buyer.


During the pre-approval process, lenders review your financial information, such as your income, debts, and credit score, to provide a detailed estimate of the loan amount you may qualify for. This step helps streamline your home search and ensures you’re focusing on properties within your financial range.


## Documents You’ll Need to Apply for a Mortgage


Once you are ready to move forward with your loan application, you will need to provide documentation to verify your financial status. Lenders require this information to ensure you have the ability to repay the loan. Gathering these documents early can facilitate the process and prevent delays.


#### To[prepare for the home loan process](https://www.pennymac.com/blog/explaining-the-home-loan-process-part-1-getting-prepped) it helps to have the following items ready:


- **Proof of income:** Recent pay stubs, W-2s from the past two years, and federal tax returns. (1099s and P&L statements if self-employed).
- **Asset verification:** Bank statements for the last two months, plus records of retirement accounts, stocks, or bonds.
- **Other financial documents:** Documentation for child support, rental income, or financial gifts.
- **Debt information:** A list of current monthly debts, like car payments or student loans.


## First-Time Homebuyer Assistance Programs


Many buyers are surprised to learn there are programs available to help with upfront homebuying costs. First-time buyers, in particular, may be eligible for options such as down payment assistance (DPA) or help with closing costs, making it easier to move forward with their purchase.


### Down Payment Assistance


Down payment assistance programs are designed to help eligible buyers cover some or all of their down payment. These programs may come in several forms, including deferred-payment loans, low-interest second mortgages or forgivable loans that do not require repayment if certain conditions are met.


In some cases, assistance may be structured as a grant that does not need to be repaid. Availability and eligibility requirements vary by state, local government and program guidelines, so it’s important to review the options in your area.


### Closing Cost Assistance


Closing costs may also be offset through loans or grants offered by government or private programs, depending on your location. Eligibility often depends on income and the home’s purchase price. These costs can include fees such as attorney services and title insurance. In some cases, sellers may contribute through negotiated seller concessions.


### Federal and Nonprofit Options


Certain government-backed programs are designed to lower the barrier to entry. For example, the Good Neighbor Next Door program, sponsored by the U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development (HUD), provides substantial discounts on homes for law enforcement officers, teachers, firefighters and EMTs. Nonprofits like Habitat for Humanity and the Neighborhood Assistance Corporation of America (NACA) also offer pathways to affordable homeownership for qualifying low-to-moderate income families.


In addition to these targeted programs, certain government-backed loan options may also offer flexible qualification requirements for eligible buyers.


## Common Mortgage Types for First-Time Homebuyers


Choosing the right loan type is just as important as finding the right house. Each mortgage product has unique benefits and requirements.


### Conventional Loans


Conventional loans are mortgages not insured by the federal government. They are a popular choice for buyers with good credit scores and stable income.


- **Best for:** Buyers with credit scores of 620 or higher and with strong cash reserves.
- **Key benefits:** Potential for no mortgage insurance with a 20% down payment and flexible term options.
- **Requirements:** Typically require a minimum 3% down payment for first-time buyers.


### FHA Loans


Insured by the Federal Housing Administration,[FHA loans](https://www.pennymac.com/fha-home-loans) are designed to help buyers who might not qualify for conventional financing.


- **Best for:** Buyers with lower credit scores or smaller down payments.
- **Key benefits:** Broader qualification requirements and down payments lower than 20%.
- **Requirements:** A minimum down payment of 3.5%, must live in the property they are purchasing and have a debt-to-income ratio no higher than 50-55% (depending on their credit history).


### VA Loans


Backed by the Department of Veterans Affairs,[VA home loans](https://www.pennymac.com/va-home-loans) are exclusively for eligible service members, veterans and surviving spouses.


- **Best for:** Qualified military borrowers looking to minimize upfront costs.
- **Key benefits:** No down payment requirement, more relaxed credit requirements, typically lower interest rates and no monthly mortgage insurance.
- **Requirements:** A Certificate of Eligibility (COE) is required to prove service history.


### Jumbo Loans


A[jumbo loan](https://www.pennymac.com/jumbo-home-loans) is a non-conforming loan used to finance amounts above the standard conforming loan limits (currently $832,750 in most areas).


- **Best for:** Buyers purchasing higher-value homes in expensive markets.
- **Key benefits:** Allows you to borrow more than standard limits without needing secondary financing.
- **Requirements:** Rigorous credit and income requirements compared to conforming loans.


## FHA vs. Conventional Loans: A Side-by-Side Comparison


First-time buyers often find themselves deciding between FHA and conventional loans. Here is a quick comparison to help you understand the differences.


Criteria Conventional Loans FHA Loans


Credit Score


You need a credit score of 620 or higher.


You can qualify with a credit score as low as 580 if you make a 3.5% down payment.


Mortgage Insurance


If your down payment is less than 20%, you'll need Private Mortgage Insurance (PMI). However, you can stop paying PMI once you've built enough equity in your home.


FHA loans require an upfront mortgage insurance premium (UFMIP) and monthly mortgage insurance (MIP). If your down payment is less than 10%, you'll likely pay this insurance for the life of the loan. Refinancing into a conventional loan to remove mortgage insurance is a good option.


Property Standards


The home must meet standard appraisal requirements.


The home must meet stricter safety and habitability standards, as required by FHA guidelines.


## Frequently Asked First-Time Homebuyer Questions


### What is mortgage insurance?


Mortgage insurance is a policy that protects the lender in case you stop making payments. It is typically required on conventional loans with less than 20% down and on all FHA loans.


### How does mortgage insurance affect monthly payments?


It is an additional cost added to your monthly mortgage bill. The exact amount depends on your loan type, credit score and down payment size.


### What is the difference between mortgage insurance and homeowners insurance?


These are two different things. Mortgage insurance protects the lender against default. Homeowners insurance protects you against financial loss from damage to the home (like fire or storm damage) and liability.


### When can mortgage insurance be removed?


For conventional loans, you can request to cancel PMI once your loan balance drops to 80% of the home's original value and you are current on your payments. It is automatically canceled at 78%. For FHA loans, mortgage insurance is generally permanent unless you put down 10% or more (in which case it lasts 11 years) or you refinance into a conventional loan later.


### Can first-time buyers qualify with lower credit?


Yes. FHA loans are specifically designed to help borrowers with less-than-perfect credit.
