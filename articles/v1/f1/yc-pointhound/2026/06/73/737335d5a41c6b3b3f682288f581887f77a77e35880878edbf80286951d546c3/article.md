# Where Your Points Go Furthest: The 12 Best Award Flight Deals for 2026


The gap between a lazy points redemption and a great one is enormous. Run your points through your credit card's travel portal and you get about 1 to 2 cents each. Transfer them to the right airline and the same points can be worth 2 to 20 times more, often 10 times more in business class and up to 20 times in first.


That $3,000 business class ticket to Europe? With the right transfer, you might book it for fewer points than some programs charge for economy. The catch is that every airline prices awards differently, and the best deals move. A sweet spot that was wide open last year can get more expensive overnight.


So we did the work. Below are 12 specific award flight deals that are bookable in 2026, ordered from easiest to most aspirational. Each one lists the points cost, the cash fare it beats, and the single move that unlocks it. These are live as of mid-2026, and availability shifts constantly, which is exactly why Pointhound tracks award space across 21 loyalty programs and 200+ airlines so you can see what is actually open right now.


## How We Picked These Award Flight Deals


We picked these deals on three things: value per point, real bookability, and how easy they are for a beginner to pull off.


Value per point matters most. A redemption is only good if it beats the roughly 1 to 2 cents per point you would get cashing out through your card portal, according to[The Points Guy's June 2026 valuations](https://thepointsguy.com/loyalty-programs/monthly-valuations/) . Cents per point, or cpp, just measures how much value you squeeze from each point you spend.


We also kept this list to deals you can book with transferable points (Chase, Amex, Capital One, Citi, Bilt), since those are the points most beginners actually hold. And we flagged where pricing recently changed, because some charts moved in the last six months and older guides still quote the old numbers.

### 1. Short U.S. Hops for 4,500 Points


If you barely travel, start here. Alaska's Atmos Rewards keeps a distance-based partner chart, and short economy flights on American Airlines start at just 4,500 points one way, scaling up by distance to 7,500, 12,500, and 17,500 points for the longest domestic routes,[according to Alaska](https://www.alaskaair.com/content/mileage-plan/use-miles/partner-airlines) .


A $250 weekend hop for 4,500 points is an easy first win that proves the whole system works. Atmos points transfer 1:1 from Bilt, and most transfers land instantly.


### 2. Economy to London for 6,000 Points


This is the cheapest full-service way across the Atlantic. Virgin Atlantic Flying Club prices its Saver economy seats from select U.S. cities to London from 6,000 Virgin points one way,[per Virgin Atlantic](https://www.virginatlantic.com/flying-club/spending-miles/flight-reward) . Even at that price you still get a checked bag, a meal, and seat selection.


Virgin points are among the easiest to collect because they transfer 1:1 from Chase, Amex, and Capital One. Watch for the taxes and fees on flights leaving the U.K., which apply on every award.


### 3. Economy to Europe for 18,750 Miles


Few discounts are as reliable as Air France-KLM Flying Blue's monthly Promo Rewards, which knock up to 25% off saver awards on rotating routes. Long-haul economy to Europe starts at 18,750 miles each way during the promo,[according to The Points Guy](https://thepointsguy.com/deals/flying-blue-special-promo-rewards/) , with routes and dates refreshed every month on the[Flying Blue site](https://www.flyingblue.us/en/flights/promo-rewards) .


Flying Blue partners with every major transferable currency, so almost any points balance can reach it. The deals change monthly, so check the current list before you transfer.


### 4. Lie-Flat to London for 29,000 Points


Here is where the math gets fun. Virgin Atlantic's Upper Class lie-flat seats to London start at 29,000 Virgin points one way,[per Virgin Atlantic](https://www.virginatlantic.com/flying-club/spending-miles/flight-reward) . That is a business class bed for fewer points than many programs charge for an economy seat to the same city.


A cash Upper Class ticket often runs $3,000 to $6,000, so a transfer like this is the clearest example of points being worth 10 times their portal value in a premium cabin.


### 5. Premium Economy to Europe for 30,000 Miles


If lie-flat is more than you need but economy feels tight, the middle cabin is a strong value. Flying Blue's Promo Rewards price long-haul premium economy to Europe from 30,000 miles each way,[according to The Points Guy](https://thepointsguy.com/deals/flying-blue-special-promo-rewards/) .


Premium economy cash fares to Europe commonly land between $1,500 and $3,000, so 30,000 miles for extra legroom, better food, and priority boarding is an easy upgrade to justify.


### 6. Hawaii from the Mainland for 25,000 Miles Each Way


A note of honesty on this one. Turkish Airlines Miles&Smiles used to price mainland-to-Hawaii flights on United at just 10,000 miles each way, and you will still see that number in older guides. It is gone. Turkish[devalued these awards on December 3, 2025](https://thepointsguy.com/news/turkish-miles-smiles-domestic-hawaii-devaluation/) , and Hawaii now costs about 25,000 miles in economy or 40,000 in business each way,[per Turkish Airlines](https://www.turkishairlines.com/en-int/miles-and-smiles/redeem-miles/redeem-miles-on-flights/turkish-airlines-award-tickets/) .


Even at the new price, 25,000 miles for a flight that often sells for $400 to $700 is a fair deal. Miles&Smiles transfers 1:1 from Capital One, Citi, and Bilt, usually instantly. We flag the change because booking on stale numbers is how people get caught short at checkout.


### 7. Business Class to Europe for 63,750 Miles


The Flying Blue Promo Rewards discount reaches the front of the plane too. Long-haul business class to Europe starts at 63,750 miles each way during the monthly promo,[according to The Points Guy](https://thepointsguy.com/deals/flying-blue-special-promo-rewards/) .


Set that against a cash business fare of $5,000 to $10,000 or more, and the value is dramatic. This is the deal that turns "I'll never have enough points for business class" into a one-trip reality.


### 8. Japan in Business Class for 60,000 Miles


American Airlines AAdvantage keeps a generous fixed price for partner flights, and Japan Airlines business class from the U.S. to Tokyo is one of the best. It costs 60,000 AAdvantage miles one way,[per American Airlines](https://www.aa.com/i18n/aadvantage-program/miles/redeem/award-travel/award-chart.jsp) .


A cash seat on the same flight often runs $7,000 to $15,000. AAdvantage miles also rank as the most valuable airline currency in[The Points Guy's June 2026 valuations](https://thepointsguy.com/loyalty-programs/monthly-valuations/) at 1.6 cents each, and you can reach them by transferring Citi ThankYou points.


### 9. Hong Kong in Business Class for 75,000 Points


Cathay Pacific's new Aria Suite business cabin is one of the most talked-about products in the sky, and Alaska's Atmos Rewards is the way in. Cathay business class to Hong Kong starts at 75,000 Atmos points one way,[according to Alaska](https://www.alaskaair.com/atmosrewards/content/partners/airlines/cathay-pacific) .


Atmos also allows a stopover on a one-way award, so you can break the trip in Hong Kong before continuing on. Cash for this cabin typically runs $5,000 to $10,000.


### 10. Japan in First Class for 80,000 Miles


For only 20,000 more miles than its business class deal, AAdvantage will put you in Japan Airlines first class. The price is 80,000 AAdvantage miles one way,[per American Airlines](https://www.aa.com/i18n/aadvantage-program/miles/redeem/award-travel/award-chart.jsp) , against cash fares that routinely top $10,000.


Availability is the hard part, not the price. Search by month on the AAdvantage site, and if you can, aim for a flight on Japan Airlines' newer Airbus A350.


### 11. Cathay First Class for 70,000 Points


This is the single best premium redemption in the points world, and it comes with a quirk: Cathay Pacific first class from the U.S. to Hong Kong costs 70,000 Atmos points one way,[according to Alaska](https://www.alaskaair.com/atmosrewards/content/partners/airlines/cathay-pacific) , which is actually fewer points than Cathay's own business class.


Cash for a Cathay first class seat can exceed $20,000. At 70,000 points, you are getting better than 25 cents of value per point. If you book only one aspirational trip from this list, make it this one.


### 12. ANA First Class to Tokyo for 145,000 Points Round Trip


The bucket-list seat. Virgin Atlantic prices ANA first class round trips by route, starting around 145,000 Virgin points from Chicago and 170,000 from New York or Houston,[per Virgin Atlantic](https://www.virginatlantic.com/flying-club/spending-miles/flight-reward) . You can book one way for half if you only find space in one direction.


A round-trip ANA first class ticket can cost $10,000 to $20,000 or more in cash. Older roundups still list this deal near 120,000 points, so confirm the current route price before you transfer. ANA first class space is rare, which makes a live alert the difference between booking it and missing it.

## Frequently Asked Questions


### What does "cents per point" mean and why does it matter?


Cents per point, or cpp, measures how much value you get per point redeemed. Cash out through a card portal and you get about 1 to 2 cents per point,[per The Points Guy](https://thepointsguy.com/loyalty-programs/monthly-valuations/) . Transfer to an airline for one of these deals and you can get 10 cents or more.


### Do these award flight deals change?


Yes, and sometimes overnight. Award pricing is dynamic, and charts get devalued without much warning. Turkish Airlines raised its Hawaii award prices by up to 167% in December 2025,[according to The Points Guy](https://thepointsguy.com/news/turkish-miles-smiles-domestic-hawaii-devaluation/) . Always confirm the current price before you transfer points.


### Which credit card points can I use for these deals?


Most of these deals use transferable points from Chase, Amex, Capital One, Citi, or Bilt. You move points to the airline's loyalty program, then book. Each deal above notes which programs transfer to the airline you need, and most transfers complete instantly.


### Do I need a lot of points to start?


No. Several deals on this list are within reach of a single welcome bonus or a few months of spending. Short U.S. flights start at 4,500 points and economy to London starts at 6,000 Virgin points, so you can get real value long before you have enough for first class.


### Will I earn miles when I book an award flight?


Sometimes. Award bookings do not always earn miles, but some programs do credit miles on award tickets, so it is worth checking your specific airline. Either way, the value comes from the discount versus the cash fare, not from earning on the award itself.
