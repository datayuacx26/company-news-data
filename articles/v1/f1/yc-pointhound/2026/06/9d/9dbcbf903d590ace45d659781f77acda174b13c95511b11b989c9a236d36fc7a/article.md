# Last-Minute Award Flights: How to Book Premium Seats on Short Notice (2026 Guide)


For cash, booking last-minute is a penalty. Buy a ticket within six days of departure and you will pay about 59% more than you would in the ideal window, according to[CheapAir's analysis of more than 917 million airfares](https://www.cheapair.com/blog/the-best-time-to-buy-flights/) . For points, the math can run the other way. That business class seat priced at $7,000 in cash three days before departure? It might be sitting there for 60,000 miles. Same seat, same flight, a fraction of the cost.


The reason is simple: airlines would rather release an unsold premium seat as an award than fly it empty. So while everyone else is bracing for a brutal cash fare, the points traveler is looking at one of the best redemption windows of the year.

## Why Last-Minute Is Actually a Good Time for Award Flights


Cash fares and award prices move in opposite directions as departure approaches. Understanding why is the whole game.


On the cash side, prices climb through the final weeks.[CheapAir's 2024 study](https://www.cheapair.com/blog/the-best-time-to-buy-flights/) found that the last 0 to 6 days before departure cost about 59% more than the prime booking window of 74 to 21 days out, and the 7-to-13-day stretch costs about 26% more. Airline pricing systems assume late buyers are business travelers and emergency flyers who will pay almost anything.


Award seats follow a different schedule. Airlines load most of their award inventory close to a full year ahead, with calendars[opening roughly 330 to 362 days before departure](https://upgradedpoints.com/travel/airlines/when-do-airlines-release-award-seats/) . But there is a second wave. When premium seats have not sold and departure is closing in, carriers release that unsold space as award availability rather than send a half-empty business cabin down the runway. This leftover space is sometimes called "distressed inventory," and it gets[released as last-minute award seats](https://onemileatatime.com/guides/last-minute-airline-awards/) .


So the picture flips. On a Tuesday morning you might see a New York to Frankfurt business class ticket at $6,000 in cash. That same seat could be bookable for 55,000 miles through a transfer partner, which works out to more than 10 cents per point, or roughly 10 times the value you would get redeeming through a credit card portal.


One honest caveat: this is an opportunity, not a guarantee. It works best in premium cabins on international routes that rarely sell out. It does not apply to every airline, and as you will see next, some programs price awards in a way that keeps last-minute seats expensive. Treat it as a genuine edge, not a sure thing.


## Which Airlines Release Last-Minute Premium Award Seats?


Not every carrier drops premium award space at the last minute. These are the names worth watching, based on[One Mile at a Time's tracking](https://onemileatatime.com/guides/last-minute-airline-awards/) of release patterns:


- **American Airlines (First and Business):** Releases reasonably priced awards in the weeks leading up to departure. Transatlantic business class typically runs 60,000 to 70,000 miles one-way, and connecting itineraries are often priced better than nonstops.
- **Emirates (Business, sometimes First):** Most business class award seats tend to open around the two-week mark. First class is stingier and usually appears only when the cabin is staying largely empty.
- **United Airlines (Business):** Frequently opens last-minute business class space, especially on transatlantic and transpacific routes. Availability varies, so it is worth checking inside the final two weeks.
- **British Airways (Business):** Releases business class space close to departure, but watch the high carrier-imposed surcharges. They can add hundreds of dollars in cash on top of the miles, so always check the total before you commit.
- **Swiss and Austrian (Business):** Both have historically released extra business class award seats as departure nears. Treat them as worth checking rather than dependable, since their parent Lufthansa Group has been tightening partner access across the board.


### One big change: Lufthansa is off the list


For years, Lufthansa first class was the canonical last-minute award, released to partner programs about two weeks out. That is over. Since[June 1, 2026, Lufthansa has stopped releasing first class award space to partner programs entirely](https://onemileatatime.com/insights/lufthansa-first-class-awards-partner-miles/) , and[a month later none had reappeared](https://thepointsguy.com/news/lufthansa-first-awards-partner-programs/) . Partner business class space has been cut sharply as well, and close-in availability took the hardest hit.


If you are reading an older guide that tells you to watch for Lufthansa first class three days before departure, that advice is out of date. It is also the clearest possible reminder of how this game works: award rules change without notice. Search live, and never plan a trip around a redemption you have not confirmed is actually there.


One important shift: since the pandemic, airlines tend to hold inventory longer than they used to. The 10-to-14-day advance window that was common before 2020 has compressed, and most last-minute drops now happen in the final 3 to 5 days before departure. That tighter window is exactly why an alert beats refreshing airline websites by hand.


## The Pricing Trick: Dynamic vs. Fixed Award Charts


Here is the catch that trips up most beginners. Some airlines price their awards *dynamically* , meaning the number of miles required tracks the cash fare. Delta SkyMiles, JetBlue TrueBlue, Southwest Rapid Rewards, and United MileagePlus all[work this way](https://www.nerdwallet.com/travel/learn/a-comprehensive-guide-to-airline-and-partner-award-charts) . Book a dynamically priced award at the last minute and the points cost can spike right alongside the cash price. A seat that was 25,000 miles a month ago might be 80,000 the week of departure. This is why the "last-minute awards are cheap" idea falls apart for so many people on their first try.


The fix is to use a **fixed award chart** . Programs with fixed charts, including Air Canada Aeroplan, Alaska, ANA, British Airways, and Cathay Pacific, charge the same number of miles regardless of when you book or how high the cash fare has climbed. A business class seat from the U.S. to Europe costs the same whether you book it 11 months out or 4 days out.


The move that ties it all together: you can often book the *same flight* through a fixed-chart **partner** program instead of the operating airline's own dynamic program. A "transfer partner" is just a loyalty program your credit card points can move into. United, for example, prices its own awards dynamically, so a last-minute business class seat can balloon past 100,000 miles. That same United flight booked with Air Canada Aeroplan miles is priced off Aeroplan's fixed chart instead, and the number does not move because departure is close. American business class to Japan can run[around 60,000 miles plus $5.60](https://thepointsguy.com/loyalty-programs/best-times-book-business-class-award-flights/) even close to the end of the schedule.

## Frequently Asked Questions


### Can you really book premium award seats at the last minute?


Yes, and often more easily than far in advance for business and first class. When premium seats have not sold, airlines[release them as award space](https://onemileatatime.com/guides/last-minute-airline-awards/) rather than fly them empty. It is not guaranteed on every route, but on international premium cabins it is a real and repeatable pattern, especially on carriers like American, Emirates, and British Airways.


### How last-minute is too last-minute?


Some carriers release space down to a few days out, and most drops now land in the final 3 to 5 days. The practical limit usually is not the airline, it is your transfer speed. Most transfers are instant, but if yours runs slower, you need a few days of cushion to be safe. One caveat worth knowing: Lufthansa first class, long the go-to last-minute redemption,[is no longer released to partner programs at all](https://onemileatatime.com/insights/lufthansa-first-class-awards-partner-miles/) .


### Will I pay more miles for booking last-minute?


On a fixed award chart, no. The cost is[the same whenever you book](https://www.nerdwallet.com/travel/learn/a-comprehensive-guide-to-airline-and-partner-award-charts) . On a dynamically priced program like Delta, United, JetBlue, or Southwest, often yes, because the mile cost tracks the cash fare. That is exactly why booking through a fixed-chart partner program is the move that keeps last-minute awards affordable.


### Which programs are best for last-minute awards?


Fixed-chart partner programs such as Air Canada Aeroplan, Alaska, ANA, Avianca LifeMiles, and British Airways let you book premium seats on other airlines without dynamic markups. They are the difference between a 55,000-mile business class seat and an 80,000-plus-mile one on the same flight.


### Do I need to transfer my points before I find a seat?


No, and you should not. Confirm the award is actually bookable first, then transfer. Transferring on speculation risks stranding your points in a program with no seat to use them on, and you cannot reverse the transfer.
