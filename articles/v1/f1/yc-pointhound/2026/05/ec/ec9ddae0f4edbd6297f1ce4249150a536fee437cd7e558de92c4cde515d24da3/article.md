# Amex Membership Rewards: How to Get the Most Value from Your Points


The American Express Gold and Platinum are two of the most popular travel cards in the country. Their welcome offers run as high as 100,000 and 175,000 Membership Rewards points. Most people who earn those points spend them the easy way: booking a flight through American Express Travel for about 1 cent per point.


That works. It also leaves most of the value behind.


Membership Rewards points have a hidden gear. American Express lets you move them to 17 airline loyalty programs, and a point spent through the right airline can be worth far more than the travel portal gives you. The hard part is the airline side, where things get confusing fast. Which program? How many points? Is this a good deal or not?

## What Is Amex Membership Rewards (and Why Transfers Beat the Portal)?


Amex Membership Rewards is American Express's flexible points currency, worth the most when transferred to airline partners rather than spent through the Amex Travel portal.


You earn Membership Rewards points on a handful of American Express cards. The two most common are the[American Express Gold Card](https://oc.brcclx.com/t?lid=26748625&s1=usr_a63TvZbFJq%3Aph&utm_campaign=usr_a63TvZbFJq%3Aph) , with a $325 annual fee, and the[American Express Platinum Card](https://oc.brcclx.com/t?lid=26748623&s1=usr_a63TvZbFJq%3Aph&utm_campaign=usr_a63TvZbFJq%3Aph) , at $895 a year. One nice feature: Membership Rewards points do not expire as long as you keep an active enrolled card in good standing, according to[American Express](https://www.americanexpress.com/en-us/credit-cards/credit-intel/american-express-points-value/) .


Here is where the value question comes in. American Express publishes what points are worth by redemption method. Flights booked through Amex Travel return about 1 cent per point. Prepaid hotels return 0.7 cents, and covering charges on your statement returns just 0.6 cents ([American Express](https://www.americanexpress.com/en-us/credit-cards/credit-intel/american-express-points-value/) ).


Transfers to airlines are a different story. Move your points to the right program and they can be worth 2 to 20 times the portal rate, with the biggest gaps in premium cabins, roughly 10 times the portal value in Business Class and up to 20 times in First Class.


The reason is simple. The portal prices your flight in dollars, then converts at a fixed rate. An airline prices an award seat in miles, and that mile count often has little to do with the cash fare. A Business Class seat that sells for $6,000 might cost the same miles as one that sells for $3,000. When you transfer, you capture that gap.

## Every Amex Airline Transfer Partner (and When to Use Each One)


American Express has 17 airline transfer partners, the widest network of any major U.S. card program. Most transfer at a 1-to-1 ratio, with a few exceptions, according to[American Express](https://www.americanexpress.com/en-us/credit-cards/credit-intel/american-express-points-value/) .


Before you pick a partner, two notes on ratios. A few programs do not transfer 1-to-1. Cathay Pacific, Emirates, and JetBlue give you fewer airline miles than the Amex points you send, so they are rarely the smart move. Aeromexico runs the other way and gives you more. Always check the current ratio on the[Amex transfer partners page](https://www.americanexpress.com/us/rewards/membership-rewards/redeem/airline-partners/) before you move points.

### Best for North America


**Air Canada Aeroplan** is one of the most flexible partners. It can book flights on a large group of partner airlines, including United, so it covers most of North America and reaches well beyond it. Short economy flights can start around 6,000 miles one-way.


**Delta SkyMiles** is useful if you fly Delta often and live near a Delta hub. Award prices move with demand, so they vary, but transferring Amex points to Delta is one of the few ways to top off a SkyMiles balance.


**JetBlue TrueBlue** works for East Coast and transcontinental routes. Keep in mind the transfer ratio is not in your favor, so only use it when a specific JetBlue award makes sense. **Aeromexico Rewards** has a favorable ratio and is handy for Mexico and Latin America.


### Best for Europe


**Air France-KLM Flying Blue** is a standout. It runs monthly Promo Rewards that cut the miles needed on selected routes, often by a quarter to a half. Economy to Europe regularly starts in the 20,000 to 30,000 mile range one-way, and Promo Rewards can push business class lower than you would expect.


**British Airways Club, Aer Lingus AerClub, and Iberia Plus** all use the same currency, called Avios, and you can move Avios between them. Avios prices short flights cheaply by distance, which makes them strong for quick hops and for connecting around Europe once you land.


**Virgin Atlantic Flying Club** is best for transatlantic routes to London and for redemptions on its partner airlines.


### Best for Business and First Class


This is where transfers pay off most. **ANA Mileage Club** and **Singapore KrisFlyer** are prized for long-haul Business and First Class at reasonable mile counts. **Qatar Airways Privilege Club** books one of the most awarded Business Class products in the world. **Cathay Pacific** and **Emirates Skywards** reach Asia and the Middle East, though the unfavorable transfer ratios mean you should run the numbers first.


### Other Partners, and One That Is Leaving


**Avianca LifeMiles** is useful for flights in Latin America and on partner airlines, and **Qantas Frequent Flyer** covers Australia and the South Pacific.


One important change: Etihad Guest will no longer be an Amex transfer partner after June 30, 2026, according to[American Express](https://www.americanexpress.com/us/rewards/membership-rewards/redeem/airline-partners/) . Do not transfer points to Etihad expecting to use them later.


## How to Transfer Amex Points to an Airline (Step by Step)


Transferring Amex points takes four steps. The order matters, because once points leave American Express they cannot come back.


### Step 1: Find and Confirm the Flight First


Search for the award seat before you do anything else. American Express states that all point transfers are final ([American Express](https://www.americanexpress.com/us/rewards/membership-rewards/redeem/airline-partners/) ). If you transfer first and the seat is gone, your points are stuck in a program you may not want.


This is the step Pointhound is built for. Search with Amex Membership Rewards selected as your points currency, and you see which airlines have award seats on your route, how many miles each one needs, and the value you are getting. You confirm the deal is real before committing.


### Step 2: Open the Airline Loyalty Account


Sign up directly with the airline program you chose. It is free. Use a name that matches your American Express account exactly, since a mismatch is the most common reason a transfer or booking gets held up.


### Step 3: Link the Program and Transfer


In your American Express account, link the airline loyalty program, then transfer your points. The minimum is 1,000 points for most partners, though JetBlue allows 250 and Qantas allows 500 ([American Express](https://www.americanexpress.com/us/rewards/membership-rewards/redeem/airline-partners/) ).


Be aware that Amex transfers are not always instant. Many post within minutes, but some partners take longer, anywhere from a few hours to a few days. If you are chasing a specific seat, do not cut it close.


### Step 4: Book on the Airline's Website


Once the miles land, book the award directly on the airline's site. Expect to pay taxes and fees in cash. These are small on most U.S. routes and larger on some international tickets, so check the total before you confirm.


## Amex Travel Portal vs. Transfer Partners: When to Use Which


The Amex Travel portal is not useless. It is just the wrong default for most flights worth booking with points. Here is how to decide.


**Use the portal when:**


- The cash fare is low, so the gap between portal value and transfer value is small.
- You want full flexibility, since portal bookings are easier to change or cancel than award tickets.
- No transfer partner has a good price on your route.
- You are short on time and the math is close enough that hunting for an award is not worth it.


**Transfer to an airline when:**


- You are booking Business or First Class, where the value gap is largest.
- The cash fare is high relative to the miles an award costs.
- You are flexible on dates, routing, or airline, which is when award seats open up.


A quick example. Say a domestic flight sells for $180. Through the portal that costs about 18,000 points at 1 cent each. Not terrible. Now take a Business Class seat to Europe that sells for $5,000. A transfer partner might book it for around 60,000 miles. That is more than 8 cents per point, several times the portal rate, on a single ticket.


One more reason not to sit on points: airlines can raise award prices. The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau lists devaluation, when a program quietly increases the miles needed for a redemption, as one of the most common rewards complaints it receives ([CFPB](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/data-research/research-reports/issue-spotlight-credit-card-rewards/) ). Points are worth the most when you use them.

## What Can 100,000 Amex Points Book?


Transferred to the right airline, 100,000 Amex points can book a round-trip economy ticket to Europe, a one-way international Business Class seat, or a string of short flights, redemptions often worth several thousand dollars in cash.


The Amex Gold's welcome offer runs as high as 100,000 points after you spend $8,000 in the first six months ([American Express](https://www.americanexpress.com/us/credit-cards/card/gold-card/) ). That is a common starting balance, so it is a useful yardstick. Here is the kind of thing it can do once transferred.


- **Round-trip economy to Europe.** Transferred to Flying Blue or Aeroplan, 100,000 points can often cover a round-trip economy ticket across the Atlantic with miles to spare, against cash fares that frequently run $800 or more.
- **One-way Business Class to Europe.** A single lie-flat seat that sells for $4,000 to $6,000 can often be booked in the range of 50,000 to 70,000 miles, leaving part of the balance for the return.
- **Several short flights.** Moved into an Avios program, 100,000 points stretches across multiple short economy hops, since Avios prices short distances cheaply.
- **A Promo Rewards deal.** Catch a Flying Blue Promo Rewards month and a business-class redemption to Europe can land well below its normal mile count.


Award prices move constantly, so treat these as the shape of what is possible, not fixed quotes. The point stands: a balance worth about $1,000 in the portal can book flights worth several thousand dollars when you transfer it well.

## Frequently Asked Questions


### How many Amex points do you need to book a flight?


It depends on the route and cabin. Short economy flights can start around 6,000 miles one-way. Economy to Europe often runs 20,000 to 30,000 miles one-way, and Business Class to Europe commonly falls between 50,000 and 90,000 miles, depending on the program and availability.


### Can you transfer Amex points to any airline?


No. You can only transfer to one of Amex's 17 airline partners, and you need a card that earns Membership Rewards points, such as the Gold or Platinum. American Express is not a partner of Southwest or several other carriers, so check the partner list before counting on a transfer.


### How long do Amex point transfers take?


Many Amex transfers are instant or post within minutes, but some partners take longer, from a few hours to a few days. Because timing varies, confirm your award seat is still available and transfer well before you plan to book.


### Can you reverse an Amex point transfer?


No. American Express states that all point transfers are final. This is why you should always find and confirm the award flight first, then transfer only the number of points that specific booking needs.


### Do Amex Membership Rewards points expire?


No. American Express states that Membership Rewards points do not expire as long as you have at least one active enrolled card in good standing. That said, airline miles can lose value over time as programs raise award prices, so it is still smart to use points rather than hoard them.
