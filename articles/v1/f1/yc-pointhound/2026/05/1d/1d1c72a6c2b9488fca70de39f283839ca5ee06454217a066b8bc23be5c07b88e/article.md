# How to Fly to Europe With Points: The Complete Guide

# How to Fly to Europe With Points: The Complete Guide


A round-trip economy flight to Europe in summer routinely costs $900 to $1,400 in cash. The same seat can book for roughly 20,000 to 40,000 points one-way through an airline transfer partner. A lie-flat business class seat that retails at $5,000 to $10,000 or more books for around 60,000 points.


Most travelers never see those numbers because they only ever search cash fares. Americans earned more than $40 billion in credit card rewards in 2022, and card issuers openly assume a chunk of those points will never be redeemed ([CFPB](https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/cfpb_credit-card-rewards_issue-spotlight_2024-05.pdf) ). Europe is the single most common trip those points could cover. The hard part is not the flight. It is knowing which airline to transfer to, what a fair price is, and how to find an open seat.


This guide walks through all of it: which transfer partners fly to Europe, what economy and business class realistically cost in points, when award seats open, and how to book one step by step. Pointhound searches award availability across 200+ airlines and 21 loyalty programs, so you can see which Europe flights your existing points can book, and at what value, before you transfer anything.


## Why Use Points to Fly to Europe Instead of Cash?


Using points to fly to Europe means transferring credit card rewards to an airline program and booking an award seat, which is worth far more than redeeming those same points for cash through a card portal.


Here is the core gap. Your credit card portal gives you about 1 to 1.5 cents per point. Transfer the same points to an airline and they can be worth 2 to 20 times that. The multiplier climbs with the cabin: roughly 10x in business class and up to 20x in first class.


Transatlantic routes are where the gap is widest. Premium-cabin cash fares to Europe are among the most expensive a regular traveler will ever see, so a points booking erases the biggest line item on the trip. One more thing worth knowing: award bookings can sometimes earn miles, depending on the program, so a points ticket is not always a dead end for future travel.

The same 60,000 points spent through a card portal would cover roughly $600 to $900 of airfare. Transferred to the right airline, they book a seat that sells for ten times that.


A common worry holds people back here: "I'll never have enough points for business class." In practice, a single welcome bonus on a mainstream travel card often lands you 60,000 points or more. That is one transatlantic business class seat from one bonus. You do not need years of spending to cross the Atlantic up front. You need to know where the points go furthest, which is the entire point of this guide.


## Which Credit Card Points Can You Use for Europe Flights?


Five transferable points programs feed airline travel: Chase Ultimate Rewards, American Express Membership Rewards, Capital One miles, Citi ThankYou Rewards, and Bilt Rewards. Each moves points to multiple airlines, mostly at a 1:1 ratio.


You do not fly on "Chase" or "Amex." You transfer those points into an airline loyalty program, then book the airline's seat. That distinction trips up almost every beginner, and it is the whole game.


Most transfers are now instant. A few exceptions exist, mainly some Amex partners, where a transfer can take up to a few days to land. The practical rule is simple: never transfer points until you have confirmed the exact award seat you want, because transfers cannot be reversed.


The good news for Europe is coverage. Air France/KLM Flying Blue accepts transfers from all five programs ([Upgraded Points](https://upgradedpoints.com/travel/best-ways-to-fly-to-europe-with-points/) ). Virgin Atlantic Flying Club does too. That means almost any cardholder has at least one strong path across the Atlantic. If you want the full breakdown of one program, our[Chase Ultimate Rewards guide](https://www.pointhound.com/blog/chase-ultimate-rewards-how-to-use-your-points-for-flights) covers every Chase partner in detail.


## Best Airline Programs for Europe Award Flights


Four airline programs do most of the work for Europe trips. Each one has a different strength, a different points cost, and a different set of cards that feed it. Here is when to reach for each.


### Air France/KLM Flying Blue


Flying Blue is the best all-around starting point for beginners. Standard pricing runs from 25,000 miles one-way in economy and 60,000 miles one-way in business class for US to Europe flights ([The Points Guy](https://thepointsguy.com/loyalty-programs/ultimate-guide-flying-blue/) ).


The real draw is Promo Rewards. Every month, Flying Blue discounts a rotating set of routes, and economy fares have dropped as low as 18,750 miles one-way ([The Points Guy](https://thepointsguy.com/loyalty-programs/ultimate-guide-flying-blue/) ). It takes transfers from all five major programs at 1:1, so whatever points you hold, Flying Blue is reachable.


The catch to know about is dynamic pricing. Flying Blue has no fixed award chart, so the same route can be cheap on one date and expensive on another. The 25,000-mile economy figure is the floor, not the ceiling. This is a program where searching specific dates pays off, because two flights a day apart can differ by tens of thousands of miles.


### Virgin Atlantic Flying Club


Virgin Atlantic is the program to use when your trip starts or ends in the UK. Economy seats between the US and London can start from around 6,000 points one-way ([Upgraded Points](https://upgradedpoints.com/travel/best-ways-to-fly-to-europe-with-points/) ).


Flying Club accepts transfers from American Express, Chase, Capital One, Citi, and Bilt, and it runs frequent transfer bonuses that stretch your points even further. When a bonus is live, a 25,000-point transfer can become 35,000 Flying Club points, which can be the difference between an economy seat and a premium one. For a London-bound trip, check Virgin Atlantic first, then time your transfer to a bonus if you can.


### Air Canada Aeroplan


Aeroplan is the choice for premium cabins across a wide range of European airlines. Business class from North America to Europe starts at 60,000 points one-way for routes under 4,000 miles, rising to 75,000 points for the 4,001 to 6,000 mile band as of June 1, 2026 ([Upgraded Points](https://upgradedpoints.com/news/air-canada-aeroplan-award-charts-june-2026/) ).


Economy on Aeroplan is less of a bargain, starting around 32,500 to 35,000 points one-way for the shortest transatlantic routes ([Upgraded Points](https://upgradedpoints.com/news/air-canada-aeroplan-award-charts-june-2026/) ). Use Aeroplan when you want a lie-flat seat, not when you want the cheapest economy ticket.


Aeroplan's strength is reach. It can book a long list of European airlines under one program, so a single transfer of Chase, Amex, or Capital One points opens dozens of business class options across the continent. If you are eyeing a lie-flat seat and your dates are firm, price the route on Aeroplan before you assume business class is out of budget.


### Avios (British Airways and Iberia)


Avios is the points currency shared by British Airways and Iberia. Iberia is the one to watch: off-peak business class between the US East Coast and Spain has run from around 34,000 Avios one-way, though Iberia adjusts its award chart periodically ([The Points Guy](https://thepointsguy.com/loyalty-programs/iberia-sweet-spot-off-peak-europe/) ).


Avios works best when your trip touches Spain directly. Watch the fees on British Airways-operated flights, which can add several hundred dollars per ticket. ## When Should You Book Award Flights to Europe?


Award seats to Europe open in two windows: when airlines first load their schedules, about 11 to 12 months before departure, and again in the final few weeks before takeoff as carriers release unsold seats.


Both windows are worth watching. The early window gives you the widest choice of dates and cities. The late window rewards flexible travelers who can move fast when a seat appears.


Season matters too. Shoulder season, meaning late spring and early fall, has both lower cash fares and more open award space than peak summer. If your dates are fixed in July or August, book as early as you can.


The single biggest lever is flexibility on your arrival city. Major Western European hub airports tend to have far more award space than smaller secondary airports, so being open to flying into a large gateway and connecting onward can turn a sold-out search into an easy booking. ## How to Book a Europe Flight With Points, Step by Step


Booking an award flight to Europe comes down to four steps: confirm the seat exists, match it to the right program, transfer your points, and book directly with the airline.


1. **Search the route and dates first.** Before you touch your points, confirm that an actual award seat exists on the days you want to fly. A great points price is meaningless if no seat is open.
2. **Match the award to a program you can reach.** Identify which airline program prices the seat best, then check which of your cards transfers to it. Flying Blue and Virgin Atlantic are reachable from all five major programs.
3. **Transfer the exact number of points needed.** Most transfers are instant. Move only what the booking requires. Do not transfer extra "to be safe," because the points cannot come back.
4. **Book directly with the airline program.** Complete the booking on the airline's site and pay the taxes and fees in cash. Those fees are normal and usually modest in economy, larger in business class.


The order matters. Never transfer points before confirming the seat. Every Pointhound search result includes step-by-step booking instructions for that specific award, so you are following a checklist instead of guessing.

## Frequently Asked Questions


### How many points do you need to fly to Europe?


A one-way economy award to Europe usually costs 25,000 miles at standard rates and as little as 18,750 miles during airline promotions. Business class typically starts around 60,000 miles one-way. Round-trip economy commonly lands in the 40,000 to 50,000 point range.


### Can you fly to Europe in business class with points?


Yes. Business class to Europe starts around 60,000 points one-way through programs like Air France/KLM Flying Blue and Air Canada Aeroplan. Those lie-flat seats often retail for $5,000 to $10,000 or more in cash, which makes them one of the highest-value points redemptions available.


### Which credit card is best for booking flights to Europe?


There is no single best card. What matters is that your points transfer to an airline that flies your route. Chase, American Express, Capital One, Citi, and Bilt all transfer to Air France/KLM Flying Blue and Virgin Atlantic Flying Club, two of the strongest programs for Europe.


### Do you pay taxes and fees on an award flight to Europe?


Yes. Award flights still carry taxes and carrier fees, paid in cash at booking. In economy these are usually modest. In business class, and especially on some British Airways flights, they can run into the hundreds of dollars, so check the total before you transfer points.


### Is it cheaper to fly to Europe with points or cash?


Points are almost always the better value for Europe, especially in business class. A 60,000-point business class seat can replace a cash fare of $6,000 or more. Even in economy, a 25,000-mile award beats a typical $600 to $700 one-way cash fare.


## Key Takeaways


- Standard economy awards to Europe cost about 25,000 miles one-way, dropping to roughly 18,750 miles during airline promotions.
- Business class to Europe starts around 60,000 points one-way and can replace a cash fare of $5,000 to $10,000 or more.
- You do not fly on credit card points directly. You transfer them to an airline program, then book that airline's seat.
- All five transferable programs (Chase, Amex, Capital One, Citi, Bilt) reach Air France/KLM Flying Blue and Virgin Atlantic, two of the best Europe programs.
- Award seats open about 11 to 12 months out and again in the final weeks before departure; shoulder season has the most space.
- Always confirm an award seat exists before transferring points, because transfers are instant but cannot be reversed.
