## What Are Credit Card Points (and Why Do They Matter for Flights)?


Credit card points are rewards earned on everyday spending that can be transferred to airline loyalty programs to book flights at 2 to 20 times their portal value. Really.


If you carry a Chase Sapphire, Amex Gold, Capital One Venture, or similar card, you're already earning them. More than 90% of general-purpose credit card spending in the U.S. happens on rewards cards ([CFPB](https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/cfpb_credit-card-rewards_issue-spotlight_2024-05.pdf) ).


The key concept here is **transferable points** . Six major programs let you move points from your credit card into airline loyalty accounts: Chase Ultimate Rewards, Amex Membership Rewards, Capital One Miles, Citi ThankYou, and Bilt Rewards. This is different from fixed-value cashback.


Why does this matter? Because there are two ways to use points for flights, and one is significantly better than the other.


**Option 1: Book through your credit card portal.** Your card issuer has a travel booking site. You "pay" with points at a fixed rate, usually 1 cent per point. A $500 flight costs 50,000 points.


**Option 2: Transfer your points to an airline.** You move your credit card points into an airline loyalty program (like United MileagePlus or Air France Flying Blue) and book an award flight. The same 50,000 points might book a flight worth $3,000 or more.


The difference is real. Here's how the major programs compare when you transfer:


- **Chase Ultimate Rewards** cards: Sapphire Preferred, Sapphire Reserve, Ink Business Preferred. 11 airline partners. Typical transfer value: 2.0 cpp ([Upgraded Points](https://upgradedpoints.com/travel/points-and-miles-valuations/) ).
- **Amex Membership Rewards** cards: Gold, Platinum, Business Platinum. 18 airline partners. Typical transfer value: 2.2 cpp ([Upgraded Points](https://upgradedpoints.com/travel/points-and-miles-valuations/) ).
- **Capital One Miles** cards: Venture X, Venture, Savor. 15+ airline partners. Typical transfer value: 1.8 cpp ([Upgraded Points](https://upgradedpoints.com/travel/points-and-miles-valuations/) ).
- **Citi ThankYou** cards: Premier, Custom Cash, Double Cash. 16 airline partners. Typical transfer value: 1.6 cpp ([Upgraded Points](https://upgradedpoints.com/travel/points-and-miles-valuations/) ).
- **Bilt Rewards** card: Bilt Mastercard. 14 airline partners. Typical transfer value: 2.0 cpp ([Upgraded Points](https://upgradedpoints.com/travel/points-and-miles-valuations/) ).


The bottom line: transferring your points to airlines typically gives you 2x or more the value compared to using the credit card portal. For premium cabin flights, the multiple can reach 10x in Business Class to 20x in First Class.


## How to Book a Flight with Points in 5 Steps


Booking a flight with points follows the same basic process regardless of which credit card or airline you use. And[Pointhound](https://www.pointhound.com/) makes the booking process easy with simple step-by-step guides shown during your booking flow. Here's the complete walkthrough.


### Step 1: Check Your Points Balance and Transfer Partners


Log into your credit card account and check your points balance. Then look at your transfer partner options. Each credit card program connects to different airlines.


[Chase](https://www.chase.com/personal/credit-cards/education/basics/chase-transfer-partners-everything-you-need-to-know) transfers to 11 airlines and 3 hotel programs. All transfers happen at a 1:1 ratio, meaning 50,000 Chase points become 50,000 airline miles. Eligible cards include the Sapphire Reserve, Sapphire Preferred, and Ink Business Preferred.


[Amex](https://www.americanexpress.com/us/customer-service/faq.transfer-membership-reward-points.html) has the largest network with 18 airline and 3 hotel partners. Most transfer at 1:1, though some ratios differ (Hilton transfers at 1:2, meaning 1 Amex point becomes 2 Hilton points).


[Capital One](https://www.capitalone.com/learn-grow/money-management/venture-miles-transfer-partnerships/) offers 15+ partners, most at 1:1. There's a minimum transfer of 1,000 miles, and a few partners have different ratios (Emirates at 2:1.5, JetBlue at 5:3).


You don't need to memorize every partner. Know which credit card you hold, then search for flights across all available programs at once.


### Step 2: Search for Award Availability


Before you transfer a single point, you need to confirm that award seats exist on your desired route and dates. This is the most important step in the entire process.


Airlines set aside a limited number of seats on each flight that can be booked with points. These are called "award seats." They're separate from the cash fare, and they can sell out. If you transfer points without checking first, you might end up with airline miles and no flight to book.


[Pointhound](https://www.pointhound.com/) searches all 21 airline programs and 200+ airlines simultaneously, so you can compare award availability across programs before committing your points. You can see exactly how many points each airline charges for the same route, then decide which transfer makes the most sense.


### Step 3: Create a Loyalty Account with the Airline


Once you've found a flight you want to book, you'll need a free frequent flyer account with that airline. If you're booking through United, create a United MileagePlus account. If you're booking through Air France, join Flying Blue.


This takes about two minutes. Sign up on the airline's website using the same name and email address associated with your credit card account. You'll need the loyalty account number to complete the transfer.


Do this before you transfer your points, not after. Having the account ready means you can move quickly once you're ready to book.


### Step 4: Transfer Your Points


Now transfer your credit card points into your airline loyalty account. Log into your credit card account, find the transfer section, select the airline, enter your loyalty number, and choose how many points to send.


Transfer speeds vary. Most transfers are instant. Some other transfers take 1 to 3 business days. Plan accordingly.


A few things to know:


- **Transfers are one-way.** Once you send points to an airline, you cannot send them back. This is why Step 2 matters so much.
- **Most transfers are 1:1.** Your 50,000 credit card points become 50,000 airline miles in most cases.
- **Minimum transfers apply.** Capital One requires a minimum of 1,000 miles per transfer ([Capital One](https://www.capitalone.com/learn-grow/money-management/venture-miles-transfer-partnerships/) ).


Only transfer the exact number of points you need for the booking. Don't round up "just in case."


### Step 5: Book Your Flight


Once your miles land in your airline loyalty account (check the balance to confirm), go to the airline's website and search for your flight. Select the award option, pay with your miles, and confirm.


You'll still owe taxes and fees. For U.S. domestic flights, this is typically $5.60 per person. International flights range from $50 to $200 depending on the airline and departure country. Some airlines (especially British Airways departing the UK) charge significant fuel surcharges on award tickets.

## Which Points Program Is Best for Flights?


The best points program for flights is whichever one you already have, since all six major transferable currencies offer strong airline transfer partners.


If you hold a Chase Sapphire, your best transfer options for flights include United (strong for domestic and transatlantic), Air Canada Aeroplan (excellent international coverage), and British Airways Avios (good for short-haul flights). Chase's partner list covers most major routes a beginner would search for ([Chase](https://www.chase.com/personal/credit-cards/education/basics/chase-transfer-partners-everything-you-need-to-know) ).


Amex Gold or Platinum cardholders get access to standout partners like ANA (some of the best business class redemptions to Japan), Air France/KLM Flying Blue (frequent promo awards to Europe), and Singapore Airlines KrisFlyer. With 18 airline partners, Amex offers the widest selection ([Amex](https://www.americanexpress.com/us/customer-service/faq.transfer-membership-reward-points.html) ).


Capital One rounds out the group with Turkish Miles&Smiles and Avianca LifeMiles, two of the most cost-effective ways to book Star Alliance flights on the Venture X or Venture cards.


Here's a quick comparison:


- **Chase UR** - Best for domestic flights and transatlantic. Standout partners: United, Aeroplan, Hyatt. Key advantage: instant transfers to United.
- **Amex MR** - Best for international premium cabins. Standout partners: ANA, Flying Blue, Singapore. Key advantage: largest airline partner network.
- **Capital One** - Best for low-cost international awards. Standout partners: Turkish, Avianca LifeMiles. Key advantage: growing network, strong value partners.
- **Citi TY** - Best for flexible international options. Standout partners: Turkish, Air France, JetBlue. Key advantage: often overlooked, solid value.
- **Bilt** - Best for earning on rent + strong partners. Standout partners: Amex MR-like partner list. Key advantage: only card that earns points on rent.


Don't overthink it. You can always earn into a second program later once you've booked your first award flight and seen the value firsthand.

## Common Mistakes Beginners Make (and How to Avoid Them)


1. **Transferring before confirming availability.** This is the number one beginner mistake. Once you transfer points to an airline, they're gone. Always complete Step 2 (search for award availability) before Step 4 (transfer). If the award seat disappears before you can book, you're stuck with airline miles.
2. **Booking through the credit card portal without checking transfer value first.** The portal is convenient, but you're paying 1 cent per point for something that might be worth 2 to 5 cents through a transfer. For a deeper comparison, see our guide on[portal vs. transfer value](https://www.pointhound.com/blog/credit-card-portal-vs-transferring-points) .
3. **Ignoring taxes and fees.** Award flights aren't "free" in the literal sense. You still pay taxes and fees. For most flights, this is minor ($5.60 domestic, $50 to $100 international). But some airlines, especially British Airways on UK departures, charge substantial fuel surcharges that can reach $500+ on premium cabin awards.
4. **Hoarding points instead of using them.** Points can lose value over time. Airlines raise award prices, programs devalue their currencies, and the[CFPB has flagged rewards devaluation](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/compliance/circulars/consumer-financial-protection-circular-2024-07-design-marketing-and-administration-of-credit-card-rewards-programs/) as a consumer protection concern. Use your points for trips you're planning. Don't wait for a "perfect" redemption that never comes.
5. **Only looking at one airline.** Your Chase points might get you a great deal on United, but an even better deal on Aeroplan or Singapore Airlines for the same route. Search across programs before deciding where to transfer.


## Frequently Asked Questions


### Can you really book flights for free with credit card points?


Yes, but "free" means no cash for the ticket itself. You'll still pay taxes and fees. For U.S. domestic flights, that's typically $5.60 per person. International flights range from $50 to $200. The flight itself, including the seat, the cabin, the service, is covered entirely by your points.


### How many points do you need to book a flight?


It depends on the route, airline, and cabin class. Economy flights within the U.S. start around 4,000 to 15,000 miles. Economy to Europe runs 6,000 to 40,000 miles. Business class to Europe can range from 40,000 to 120,000 miles. The exact number varies by airline program and availability.


### Is it better to use points through the portal or transfer them to an airline?


Transferring almost always gives you more value. The portal typically values your points at 1 cent each. Transferring to airlines yields 2.0 to 2.2 cents per point on average, and premium cabin redemptions can reach 5 to 15 cents per point ([Upgraded Points](https://upgradedpoints.com/travel/points-and-miles-valuations/) ). The exception: very cheap domestic flights under $200 where the portal might cost fewer points.


### How long does a points transfer take?


It varies by program and partner. Many are instant. Chase to United is instant, for example. Most other Chase and Amex transfers complete in 1 to 3 business days. Some Amex partners can take up to 5 days. Capital One transfers are typically instant or within a few hours. Always allow enough time before your planned booking date.


### What happens if award availability disappears after I transfer my points?


You're stuck with the airline miles. You can still use them for other flights on that airline or its partners, but you cannot send them back to your credit card. This is why confirming award availability before transferring is the single most important rule in award travel.
