# Credit Card Portal vs. Transferring Points: Why You're Leaving Money on the Table


100,000 Chase points through the portal: roughly $1,000. The same 100,000 points transferred to United or Air France/KLM: $5,000 to $10,000 or more in value. That's not a hypothetical. It's the gap between the two ways you can use your credit card points for travel.


Most people default to their credit card's travel portal because it's familiar. One interface, no loyalty accounts, no research. But that convenience has a cost. The portal gives you the minimum possible value for your points, typically 1 cent each.


It used to be slightly better for Chase cardholders. Before June 2025, Sapphire Preferred earned 1.25 cents per point in the Chase Travel portal, and Sapphire Reserve earned 1.5 cents. Those guaranteed rates are gone.[NerdWallet's analysis](https://www.nerdwallet.com/travel/news/chase-points-boost-is-a-points-bust) found that only about 9.5% of Chase portal flight inventory now qualifies for the replacement "Points Boost" rates. For the other 90%, your points are worth 1 cent each.


The gap between portal and transfer is wider than ever. This guide shows you exactly when the portal still makes sense, when transferring is the clear winner, and how to decide in about 60 seconds. ## How Do Credit Card Portals Actually Work?


A credit card portal is your card issuer's own travel booking website where you pay with points at a fixed rate, typically 1 cent per point.


Chase has Chase Travel. Amex has Amex Travel. Capital One has Capital One Travel. These portals work like any online travel agency: you search for flights, pick one, and "pay" with points instead of cash.


The catch is the exchange rate. Your points have a fixed value in the portal, and that value is almost always 1 cent per point. A $500 flight costs 50,000 points. A $200 flight costs 20,000 points. No exceptions, no workarounds.


There are real advantages to portals. You don't need airline loyalty accounts. The booking experience is familiar. Portal bookings often earn airline miles (which award bookings typically don't). And you can combine points with cash if you're short.


But you're accepting the floor value for your points every time.


- **Chase Ultimate Rewards** - 1 cent per point baseline; up to 2 cpp with Points Boost. Points Boost applies to ~9.5% of flights ([NerdWallet](https://www.nerdwallet.com/travel/news/chase-points-boost-is-a-points-bust) ).
- **Amex Membership Rewards** - 1 cent per point. Flat rate across all bookings.
- **Capital One Miles** - 1 cent per point. Flat rate ([Capital One](https://www.capitalone.com/learn-grow/money-management/ways-to-redeem-venture-miles/) ).
- **Citi ThankYou** - 1 cent per point. Flat rate via Citi Travel.
- **Bilt Rewards** - 1.25 cent per point. Through Bilt Travel.


## How Does Transferring Points to Airlines Work?


Transferring points means moving your credit card points into an airline loyalty program, where they buy award flights at 2 to 20 cents per point.


Instead of booking through your card issuer's portal, you send your points to an airline partner. Your 50,000 Chase points become 50,000 United miles. Your 60,000 Amex points become 60,000 Air France/KLM Flying Blue miles. Most transfers happen at a 1:1 ratio.


The value difference comes from how airlines price award flights. A round-trip business class ticket from San Francisco to Tokyo might cost $5,000 in cash but only 75,000 to 88,000 miles. That's 5.7 to 6.7 cents per point, compared to 1 cent in the portal.


Multiple independent sources confirm this gap:


- **Chase Ultimate Rewards** - Portal: 1.0 cpp / Transfer: 2.0 cpp ([Upgraded Points](https://upgradedpoints.com/travel/points-and-miles-valuations/) ) / 2x value.
- **Amex Membership Rewards** - Portal: 1.0 cpp / Transfer: 2.2 cpp ([Upgraded Points](https://upgradedpoints.com/travel/points-and-miles-valuations/) ) / 2.2x value.
- **Capital One Miles** - Portal: 1.0 cpp / Transfer: 1.8 cpp ([Upgraded Points](https://upgradedpoints.com/travel/points-and-miles-valuations/) ) / 1.8x value.
- **Citi ThankYou** - Portal: 1.0 cpp / Transfer: 1.6 cpp ([Upgraded Points](https://upgradedpoints.com/travel/points-and-miles-valuations/) ) / 1.6x value.
- **Bilt Rewards** - Portal: 1.25 cpp / Transfer: 2.0 cpp ([Upgraded Points](https://upgradedpoints.com/travel/points-and-miles-valuations/) ) / 1.6x value.


[Bankrate's independent analysis](https://www.bankrate.com/credit-cards/travel/points-and-miles-valuations/) confirms similar figures, valuing Chase UR at roughly 2.0 cpp via transfer compared to 1 cpp through the portal.


One critical rule: always confirm award availability before transferring. Transfers are one-way. Once you send points to an airline, you cannot send them back to your credit card.


## Portal vs. Transfer: Side-by-Side Comparison


Here's how the two methods compare across every factor that matters.


### The Decision Table


- **Value per point** - Portal: 1 cpp (fixed). Transfer: 2–20+ cpp (varies by route and cabin).
- **Effort** - Portal: low (book like any travel site). Transfer: medium (find availability, create loyalty account, transfer, then book).
- **Flexibility** - Portal: can combine points with cash. Transfer: must have enough miles for full booking.
- **Best for** - Portal: cheap domestic flights under $200. Transfer: international flights, premium cabins.
- **Risk** - Portal: none (standard booking). Transfer: points stuck as airline miles if availability disappears.
- **Earning miles** - Portal: can earns airline miles on the ticket. Transfer: award bookings can sometimes earn miles.
- **Time to book** - Portal: instant. Transfer: instant to 5 days (transfer time varies).


### Real Examples with Dollar Amounts


The comparison table tells one story. Real flights tell a clearer one.


**Cheap domestic flight (NYC to LAX).** Cash price: $200. Through the Chase portal, that's 20,000 points. Transfer to United: 12,500 miles, but you'd need a United loyalty account and 1-2 days for the transfer to process. For a $200 flight, the portal is simpler and costs a similar number of points. **Portal wins on convenience.**

That's 2.7 cents per point. You save 50,000 points, which is enough for another domestic round-trip. **Transfer wins clearly.**


**Business class SFO to Tokyo.** Cash price: $5,000 or more. Through the portal: 500,000+ points. Transfer 75,000 to 88,000 Amex points to ANA and book the same lie-flat seat. The value per point: 5.7 to 6.7 cents. You're using roughly 85% fewer points for the same flight. **Transfer wins by a factor of 6x.**

## When Is the Portal Actually Worth Using?


The portal is worth using for cheap domestic flights under $200, when award seats are sold out, or when you need to mix points with cash to cover a booking.


Being honest about when the portal makes sense makes the transfer case stronger.


**Cheap domestic flights under $200.** This is the threshold rule. If the cash price is low enough, the portal might cost fewer points than an award booking. A $150 flight costs 15,000 points through the portal. The same route might require 12,500 miles via transfer, but after accounting for the effort, the savings don't justify the extra steps.


**When award availability is sold out.** Airlines release a limited number of award seats per flight. If your dates aren't flexible and no award seats exist, the portal is your only points option.


**When you're short on points.** Portal bookings let you mix points and cash. If you have 30,000 points and the award flight costs 45,000 miles, the portal lets you use what you have.


**When you want to earn elite status.** Portal bookings count as revenue flights, which earn airline miles and elite qualifying credits. Award bookings typically don't.


**When Chase Points Boost applies.** If your specific booking shows a Points Boost rate of 1.5 to 2x, the portal can approach transfer value. But this applies to only about 9.5% of flight inventory ([NerdWallet](https://www.nerdwallet.com/travel/news/chase-points-boost-is-a-points-bust) ), so don't count on it.


## When Should You Always Transfer?


You should transfer points instead of using the portal for international flights, premium cabins, and any booking where the cash price exceeds $300.


The pattern is straightforward: the more expensive the cash ticket, the more you benefit from transferring.


**International flights.** The longer and more expensive the flight, the wider the gap between portal and transfer value. A $1,200 round-trip to Europe at 1 cpp through the portal costs 120,000 points. The same route via a transfer might cost 20,000 to 40,000 miles.


**Premium cabins.** Business and first class is where transfers deliver the most dramatic value. Lie-flat seats that cost $4,000 to $8,000 in cash can book for 50,000 to 100,000 miles. The math is not close.


**When transfer bonuses are active.** Card issuers periodically offer 15 to 30% bonus miles on transfers to specific airlines. A 25% bonus on 80,000 points gives you 100,000 airline miles. These promotions amplify an already strong value proposition.


**When you have flexible dates.** Award availability varies by day. If you can shift your trip by a day or two, you're more likely to find the pricing you want.

## The Chase Points Boost Change: What It Means for You


In June 2025, Chase replaced its guaranteed portal rates with a new system called[Points Boost](https://www.chase.com/travel/guide/trips/chase-sapphire-points-boost-benefits-guide) . This matters because Chase Sapphire is the most popular transferable points card, and the change made portal bookings worse for the majority of users.


**Before June 2025:** Sapphire Preferred cardholders got a guaranteed 1.25 cents per point on every portal booking. Sapphire Reserve cardholders got 1.5 cents per point. These rates applied to every flight, every hotel, every time.


**After June 2025:** The baseline is 1 cent per point for everyone. Chase now offers "Points Boost" on select inventory, where rates can reach 1.5 to 2x for Reserve cardholders. The problem: only about 9.5% of flight inventory qualifies for Points Boost ([NerdWallet](https://www.nerdwallet.com/travel/news/chase-points-boost-is-a-points-bust) ).


**Grandfathering provision:** If you earned Chase points before October 26, 2025, you can still redeem them at the old guaranteed rates (1.25 or 1.5 cpp) through October 26, 2027 ([Doctor of Credit](https://www.doctorofcredit.com/chase-sapphire-reserve-points-boost-removes-1-5-travel-redemption-adds-boost-feature-for-2-on-select-travel/) ).


The bottom line: for most Chase cardholders, the portal went from "decent but not great" to "barely worth considering." If you were on the fence about learning to transfer, Chase made the decision for you.


## Frequently Asked Questions


### Is it always better to transfer points instead of using the portal?


No. For cheap domestic flights under about $200, the portal can be simpler and cost a similar number of points. Portal bookings also earn airline miles and count toward elite status, which award bookings don't. For international flights and premium cabins, though, transferring almost always gives you more value.


### What if I transfer points and can't find a flight?


You're stuck with the airline miles. You can still use them for other flights on that airline or its partners, but you cannot send them back to your credit card. This is the number one beginner mistake. Always confirm award availability before transferring.


### How much more are my points worth when I transfer them?


Typically 2x or more versus the portal. Chase UR averages 2.0 cpp via transfer compared to 1 cpp in the portal. Amex MR averages 2.2 cpp. On premium cabin international flights, values can reach 5 to 15 cents per point ([Upgraded Points](https://upgradedpoints.com/travel/points-and-miles-valuations/) ).


### Did Chase make portals worse in 2025?


Yes. Since June 2025, the guaranteed 1.25/1.5 cpp rates are gone for newly earned points. Chase replaced them with "Points Boost," a dynamic system where higher rates apply to select bookings, but only about 9.5% of flight inventory qualifies. The baseline for everything else is 1 cent per point.
