# How to Fly to Hawaii With Points: A Beginner's Guide

# How to Fly to Hawaii With Points: A Beginner's Guide


A round-trip economy flight to Hawaii runs $400 to $1,000 from the West Coast in cash, and more from the East Coast or during peak weeks. The same seat can book for as few as 12,500 to 15,000 points one-way through the right airline program. A lie-flat first class seat that sells for $1,200 or more one-way can go for around 35,000 points.


Most travelers never see those numbers because they only ever search cash fares. Americans earned more than $40 billion in credit card rewards in 2022, and card issuers openly assume a large share will never be redeemed ([CFPB](https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/cfpb_credit-card-rewards_issue-spotlight_2024-05.pdf) ). Hawaii is one of the most common trips those points could cover. The hard part is not the flight. It is knowing which airline to transfer to, what a fair price looks like, and how to find an open seat.


This guide walks through all of it: which programs fly to Hawaii, what economy and first class realistically cost in points, when award seats open, and how to book one step by step. Pointhound searches award availability across 200+ airlines and 21 loyalty programs, so you can see which Hawaii flights your existing points can book, and at what value, before you transfer anything.


## Why Use Points to Fly to Hawaii Instead of Cash?


Using points to fly to Hawaii means transferring credit card rewards to an airline program and booking an award seat, which is worth far more than redeeming those same points for cash through a card portal.


Here is the core gap. Your credit card portal gives you about 1 to 1.5 cents per point. Transfer the same points to an airline and they can be worth 2 to 20 times that, with the multiplier climbing in premium cabins, roughly 10x in business class and up to 20x in first class.


Hawaii has a twist that makes points especially useful. Cash fares swing hard with the season, doubling or tripling in peak summer, the winter holidays, and spring break. Award prices on fixed-chart programs barely move. That means points pay off most at exactly the times cash fares are worst.

A common worry holds people back: "I'll never have enough points for a trip like that." In practice, a single welcome bonus on a mainstream travel card often lands 60,000 points or more, enough for two or three round-trip economy seats to Hawaii. And award bookings can sometimes earn miles, depending on the program, so a points ticket is not always a dead end for future travel.


## Which Credit Card Points Can You Use for Hawaii Flights?


Five transferable points programs feed airline travel: Chase Ultimate Rewards, American Express Membership Rewards, Capital One miles, Citi ThankYou Rewards, and Bilt Rewards. Each moves points to airlines that reach Hawaii, mostly at a 1:1 ratio.


You do not fly on "Chase" or "Amex." You transfer those points into an airline loyalty program, then book the airline's seat. That distinction trips up almost every beginner, and it is the whole game. If this is your first award booking, our[beginner's guide to booking flights with credit card points](https://www.pointhound.com/blog/how-to-book-flights-with-credit-card-points) covers the mechanics start to finish.


Most transfers are now instant. A few exceptions exist, mainly some Amex partners, where a transfer can take up to five days to land. The practical rule is simple: never transfer points until you have confirmed the exact award seat you want, because transfers cannot be reversed.

## Best Airline Programs for Hawaii Award Flights


Four airline programs do most of the work for Hawaii trips. Each has a different strength, a different points cost, and a different set of cards that feed it. Here is when to reach for each.


### Alaska Atmos Rewards


Atmos Rewards is the home program for Hawaii. It absorbed Hawaiian Airlines' HawaiianMiles in October 2025, so a single program now books both Alaska and Hawaiian nonstops across the Pacific. Economy from the West Coast starts around 12,500 miles one-way, and as low as 10,000 miles on the shortest routes ([The Points Guy](https://thepointsguy.com/loyalty-programs/points-and-miles-to-hawaii/) ).


The standout is the premium cabin. Hawaiian flies lie-flat seats on its widebody jets, and Atmos prices that first class from around 35,000 miles one-way, for a seat that often sells for $1,200 or more in cash. Among the five transferable programs, Bilt is the only one that transfers to Atmos, at 1:1 ([NerdWallet](https://www.nerdwallet.com/travel/learn/bilt-transfer-partners) ).


One caveat: Atmos uses dynamic pricing on Alaska and Hawaiian flights, so the starting numbers are a floor, not a ceiling. Longer routes from the East Coast often run 20,000 miles or more one-way in economy, so search your specific dates.


### United MileagePlus


United has the widest reach to Hawaii of any single program, with nonstops from more mainland cities than anyone else. It uses dynamic pricing, so there is no fixed chart. West Coast economy typically runs 22,500 to 25,000 miles one-way, and round trips have gone for as little as 30,000 miles when saver space is open ([The Points Guy](https://thepointsguy.com/loyalty-programs/points-and-miles-to-hawaii/) ).


From the East Coast or the middle of the country, plan closer to 50,000 miles round trip. One perk worth using: holders of a United co-branded credit card get at least 10% off award flights booked with miles ([NerdWallet](https://www.nerdwallet.com/travel/learn/complete-guide-redeeming-united-mileageplus-miles) ). United takes transfers from Chase and Bilt at 1:1, so Chase cardholders can reach Hawaii directly through it.


### Avianca LifeMiles


LifeMiles is the quiet bargain. It books the same United flights to Hawaii, but often for fewer miles: around 15,000 one-way in economy from Los Angeles or San Francisco, and about 20,000 from other cities ([The Points Guy](https://thepointsguy.com/loyalty-programs/points-and-miles-to-hawaii/) ).


LifeMiles also charges zero fuel surcharges on partner awards, so the cash you pay at booking stays low ([The Points Guy](https://thepointsguy.com/loyalty-programs/everything-you-need-to-know-avianca-lifemiles/) ). It takes transfers from American Express, Capital One, Citi, and Bilt at 1:1 ([NerdWallet](https://www.nerdwallet.com/travel/learn/avianca-airlines-lifemiles-guide) ), which makes it the go-to path for Amex cardholders, who cannot transfer to United or Atmos directly. The catch is that LifeMiles can only book a United seat that United has released, so its availability tracks United's saver space.


### Turkish Miles&Smiles


Turkish is the predictable option. It also books United flights to Hawaii, at a flat 25,000 miles one-way in economy, 40,000 in United's business or premium cabin, and 50,000 in first, no matter the date ([The Points Guy](https://thepointsguy.com/news/turkish-miles-smiles-domestic-hawaii-devaluation/) ).


Know the history, though. Turkish charged just 10,000 miles for the same economy seat until December 2025, when it raised Hawaii rates by 150%. It is no longer the runaway deal it once was, but the fixed price still beats United's dynamic rate on expensive dates. Turkish takes transfers from Capital One, Citi, and Bilt at 1:1.


A couple of other options round things out. Southwest Rapid Rewards books West Coast nonstops for as few as 16,500 points one-way, all economy with no lie-flat cabin, and takes transfers from Chase and Bilt ([NerdWallet](https://www.nerdwallet.com/travel/learn/guide-to-southwest-transfers-and-partnerships) ). American AAdvantage flies to Hawaii too, but it no longer accepts transfers from the major flexible-points programs, so it is not a practical path for most cardholders. ## When Should You Book Award Flights to Hawaii?


Award seats to Hawaii open in two windows: when airlines first load their schedules, about 11 to 12 months before departure, and again in the final few weeks before takeoff as carriers release unsold seats.


Season matters more for Hawaii than almost anywhere else. Summer, the winter holidays, and spring break are the hardest times to find award space, and the times cash fares spike the most. If your dates fall there, book at the 11-month mark. Shoulder months such as late spring and early fall have lower cash fares and more open award space, and they reward travelers who can grab a late-release seat, the same pattern behind[cheap last-minute flights](https://www.pointhound.com/blog/how-to-find-cheap-last-minute-flights) .


Two levers open up a stubborn search. First, be flexible on which island you fly into: Honolulu usually has the most award space, so flying there and connecting onward can turn a sold-out search into a booking. Second, be open on which program you use. If United prices your dates high, Atmos or LifeMiles may have the same week wide open. Beginners often lock onto one airline and conclude there is "no availability," when really there is none on that one program.


Checking several programs at once is what separates a frustrating search from a booked trip. ## How to Fly to Hawaii With Points, Step by Step


Booking an award flight to Hawaii comes down to four steps: confirm the seat exists, match it to the right program, transfer your points, and book directly with the airline.


1. **Search the route and dates first.** Before you touch your points, confirm that an actual award seat exists on the days you want to fly. A great points price means nothing if no seat is open.
2. **Match the award to a program you can reach.** Identify which program prices the seat best, then check which of your cards transfers to it. A West Coast nonstop on Hawaiian points best through Atmos; a United flight often prices lower through LifeMiles.
3. **Transfer the exact number of points needed.** Most transfers are instant. Move only what the booking requires, not extra "to be safe," because the points cannot come back.
4. **Book directly with the airline program.** Complete the booking on the airline's own site and pay the taxes and fees in cash. On domestic Hawaii routes those fees are small, often under $12.


The order matters. Never transfer points before confirming the seat. Every Pointhound search result includes step-by-step booking instructions for that specific award, so you are following a checklist instead of guessing.

## Frequently Asked Questions


### How many points do you need to fly to Hawaii?


A one-way economy award to Hawaii usually costs 12,500 to 25,000 miles depending on the program, with lie-flat first class starting around 35,000 miles. Round-trip economy from the West Coast commonly lands between 25,000 and 40,000 points, and more from farther east.


### What is the cheapest way to fly to Hawaii with points?


For most travelers, Alaska Atmos Rewards from around 12,500 miles one-way, or Avianca LifeMiles from about 15,000 miles for United flights out of Los Angeles or San Francisco. Both beat United's own dynamic rate, especially on peak-season dates when cash fares spike.


### Can you fly to Hawaii in first class or lie-flat with points?


Yes. Hawaiian Airlines flies lie-flat seats on its widebody jets, and Alaska Atmos Rewards prices that first class from around 35,000 miles one-way. That replaces a cash fare that often runs $1,200 or more one-way, making it one of the best-value premium redemptions on a domestic route.


### Which credit card points are best for Hawaii flights?


It depends on your route. Bilt is the most flexible because it reaches Alaska Atmos, United, LifeMiles, and Turkish. Chase points transfer to United and Southwest, while Amex, Capital One, and Citi points reach United flights through Avianca LifeMiles.


### Is it hard to find award seats to Hawaii?


It can be during summer, the winter holidays, and spring break, when both award space and cash fares are tightest. Booking 11 to 12 months out, staying flexible on your island and program, and setting a flight alert are the three things that turn a sold-out search into a booking.


## Key Takeaways


- Economy awards to Hawaii start around 12,500 miles one-way through Alaska Atmos Rewards and about 15,000 through Avianca LifeMiles, versus $400 to $1,000 round-trip cash fares.
- Lie-flat first class on Hawaiian Airlines books from around 35,000 miles one-way through Atmos, for seats that sell for $1,200 or more.
- You do not fly on credit card points directly. You transfer them to an airline program, then book that airline's seat.
- Bilt reaches the most Hawaii programs, including Alaska Atmos, which no other transferable currency can access; Chase reaches United and Southwest.
- Turkish Miles&Smiles raised its Hawaii economy rate to a flat 25,000 miles in December 2025, so it is predictable but no longer the cheapest option.
