# How to Fly to Asia with Points: Business Class for Less


A round-trip business class seat to Asia routinely sells for $5,000 to $8,000 or more in cash. That same lie-flat seat, on the same plane, can book for around 105,000 to 120,000 points round-trip. The cheapest single-program path starts at just 60,000 miles one-way.


Most travelers never see those numbers because they only search cash fares. A transpacific business class fare is often the single most expensive line item on a trip, which is exactly why booking it with points erases the most money. The hard part is not the flight. It is knowing which airline to transfer to, what a fair price looks like, and how to find a seat that is actually open.


This guide covers all of it: which transfer partners unlock Asia business class, what a seat realistically costs in points, when award space opens, and how to book one step by step. ## Why Use Points to Fly Business Class to Asia?


Using points for business class to Asia means transferring credit card rewards to an airline program and booking an award seat, which is worth far more than redeeming the same points for cash through a card portal.


Here is the core gap. Your credit card portal gives you about 1 to 1.5 cents per point. Transfer those same points to an airline and they can be worth 2 to 20 times that. The multiplier climbs with the cabin: roughly 10 times the portal value in business class and up to 20 times in first class.


Asia is where the gap gets dramatic. Premium transpacific cash fares are among the highest a regular traveler will ever see, so a points booking wipes out thousands of dollars in one move. And award bookings can sometimes earn miles, depending on the program, so a points ticket is not always a dead end for future travel.

A common worry stops people before they start: "I'll never have enough points for business class." In practice, a single welcome bonus on a mainstream travel card often lands you 60,000 points or more, and two of them can cover a round-trip lie-flat seat across the Pacific. You do not need years of spending. You need to know where the points go furthest.


## Which Credit Card Points Can You Use for Asia Business Class?


Five transferable points programs feed airline travel to Asia: Chase Ultimate Rewards, American Express Membership Rewards, Capital One miles, Citi ThankYou Rewards, and Bilt Rewards. Each moves points into airline programs, mostly at a 1-to-1 ratio.


You do not fly on "Chase" or "Amex." You transfer those points into an airline loyalty program, then book the airline's seat. That one distinction trips up almost every beginner, and it is the entire game.


Every cardholder has at least one strong path. Chase and Amex reach the widest set of programs, while Capital One, Citi, and Bilt each feed at least two of the programs below. Most transfers are instant. The one slow exception to plan around is American Express to ANA, which takes two to four business days to land, according to[ANA](https://www.ana.co.jp/en/us/amc/amex-membership-rewards-program/) . If your points sit with Amex, our[Amex Membership Rewards guide](https://www.pointhound.com/blog/amex-membership-rewards-how-to-get-the-most-value) covers every partner in detail. Whatever program you use, never transfer until you have confirmed the exact award seat, because transfers cannot be reversed.


## Best Airline Programs for Asia Business Class Awards


Five programs do most of the work for business class to Asia. Each has a different strength, a different points cost, and a different set of cards that feed it. Here is when to reach for each.


### Virgin Atlantic Flying Club (to fly ANA)


Virgin Atlantic Flying Club is the cheapest well-documented way into ANA business class, the Japanese carrier's celebrated "The Room" suite. Business runs 52,500 points one-way from the U.S. West Coast and 60,000 from the East Coast, and first class ("The Suite") starts at 72,500 points one-way, according to[ThriftyTraveler](https://thriftytraveler.com/guides/points/book-ana-flights-virgin-atlantic-points/) .


The draw is accessibility: all five major programs transfer to Flying Club, and most transfers are instant. Two catches to know. You cannot book ANA online through Virgin, so you call Virgin Atlantic and an agent finds the seat, and ANA passes on fuel surcharges of roughly $180 to $350. Even so, a five-figure cash seat for 52,500 points is one of the best deals in the awards world.


### Turkish Miles&Smiles


Turkish Miles&Smiles is the cheapest single program for reaching Asia broadly, pricing business class on Star Alliance partners at about 60,000 miles one-way from the U.S., according to[ThriftyTraveler](https://thriftytraveler.com/guides/points/turkish-airlines-miles-and-smiles/) . Star Alliance is a group of airlines that book each other's flights, so those Turkish miles can put you on ANA, EVA Air, or Asiana.


Turkish takes transfers from Capital One, Citi, and Bilt, which makes it the go-to if your points are not with Chase or Amex. Two honest caveats: the Turkish website is glitchy and you may need to call to ticket a partner award, and some airlines add fuel surcharges. EVA Air does not, which makes it a favorite here.


### Air Canada Aeroplan


Aeroplan is the most flexible option because it prices by distance and books a long list of Asian carriers, including ANA, EVA Air, Singapore Airlines, and Asiana. Business class from North America to Japan or Korea runs 85,000 points one-way, rising to 102,500 for Southeast Asia, as of the June 1, 2026 award chart, according to[Upgraded Points](https://upgradedpoints.com/news/air-canada-aeroplan-award-charts-june-2026/) .


It takes transfers from Chase, Amex, Capital One, and Bilt. The quieter advantage is that many partner awards carry no fuel surcharges, so your out-of-pocket fees stay low even when the mileage price is higher than Virgin or Turkish. Reach for Aeroplan when your dates are firm and you want the widest choice of airlines under one program.


### Singapore KrisFlyer


KrisFlyer is how you fly Singapore Airlines' own business class, consistently rated one of the best in the sky. A saver business seat from the West Coast to Singapore runs about 112,500 miles one-way, according to[The Points Guy](https://thepointsguy.com/loyalty-programs/ultimate-guide-singapore-krisflyer/) . "Saver" means the lowest published mileage price, which is also the hardest to find.


KrisFlyer takes transfers from Chase, Amex, Capital One, and Citi. It costs more miles than the other programs here, so treat it as the splurge when the experience is the point. Saver space is scarce, so early searching matters more with Singapore than with any other program on this list.


### ANA Mileage Club (booking ANA direct)


If your points are with Amex, you can book ANA through its own program instead. ANA Mileage Club prices round-trip business class to Japan at around 110,000 miles and allows one-way awards on ANA's own flights, according to[ANA](https://www.ana.co.jp/en/us/amc/partner-flight-awards/) . American Express is the only flexible-points program that transfers in, alongside Marriott, and that transfer takes a few days.


For most people, Virgin Atlantic is the cheaper route onto the exact same ANA plane. Book ANA direct mainly when Virgin shows no space and your Amex points are ready to move well ahead of time.

## When Does Asia Business Class Award Space Open?


Business class award seats to Asia open in two windows: when airlines first load their schedules, about 330 to 355 days out, and again in the final one to three weeks before takeoff as carriers release unsold seats.


Both windows reward planning. The early window gives you the widest choice of dates and cities. The late window rewards travelers who can move the moment a seat appears.


Here is the honest part. Premium award space to Asia is genuinely scarce. ANA often releases only one or two business class seats per flight, and they get snatched up fast. No search tool can conjure a seat that does not exist, so realistic expectations matter. The biggest lever you control is flexibility: being open on your gateway city, your dates, and which program you use turns a sold-out search into a booking.

## How to Book an Asia Business Class Flight With Points, Step by Step


Booking an Asia business class award comes down to five steps: confirm the seat, match it to the cheapest program you can reach, transfer your points, book with the airline, and pay the fees in cash.


1. **Search the route and dates first.** Confirm an actual business class award seat exists on the days you want to fly. A great points price means nothing if no seat is open.
2. **Match the seat to a program you can reach.** The same ANA flight might be 52,500 points through Virgin Atlantic or 85,000 through Aeroplan. Pick the cheapest program your cards transfer to.
3. **Transfer only what you need.** Most transfers are instant, but Amex to ANA takes a few days, so move those points early. Never transfer extra "to be safe," because points cannot come back.
4. **Book directly with the airline program.** Complete the booking on the airline's site, or by phone for ANA-via-Virgin awards.
5. **Pay taxes and fuel surcharges in cash.** These run from modest to a few hundred dollars depending on the carrier, so check the total before you commit.


The order matters more than anything else. Never transfer points before confirming the seat. ## Frequently Asked Questions


### How many points do you need for business class to Asia?


Business class to Asia starts around 52,500 to 60,000 points one-way through the cheapest programs, such as Virgin Atlantic on ANA or Turkish Miles&Smiles. Premium carriers or longer routes run 85,000 to 112,500 miles one-way. Round-trip commonly lands between 105,000 and 120,000 points.


### What is the cheapest way to fly to Asia in business class with points?


The cheapest documented path is Virgin Atlantic Flying Club, which books ANA business class from 52,500 points one-way on the West Coast. Turkish Miles&Smiles is close behind at about 60,000 miles one-way and reaches more of Asia on Star Alliance partners like EVA Air.


### Can you fly ANA business class ("The Room") with points?


Yes. The cheapest way is transferring points to Virgin Atlantic Flying Club, which books ANA business class from 52,500 points one-way. You call Virgin to confirm the seat rather than booking online. You can also book ANA directly through ANA Mileage Club using American Express points.


### Do you pay fuel surcharges on Asia award flights?


Sometimes. ANA passes on fuel surcharges of roughly $180 to $350, and Turkish awards on some carriers carry them too. EVA Air and many Star Alliance partners booked through Air Canada Aeroplan have no fuel surcharges, so you pay only modest taxes. Always check the total before transferring.


### Which credit card is best for business class to Asia?


There is no single best card. What matters is that your points transfer to a program that prices your route well. Chase and Amex reach the widest set of Asia programs. Capital One, Citi, and Bilt each feed Turkish or Virgin Atlantic, two of the strongest programs for Asia business class.
