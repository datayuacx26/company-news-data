# What can utility-scale renewable energy companies learn from disturbed energy companies? Portfolio Financing.

“Engineers at Tesla Inc. found a quality problem earlier this summer with a cast aluminum auto part that was taking hours to diagnose and fix. They were stumped, so they called in the rocket scientists -- literally.” - Bloomberg, *Tesla and SpaceX Share More Than Musk.*


Innovation in material stress testing is a must-have function for space exploration, but it can also be a competitive advantage for the car industry.


‍


Knowledge cross-pollination can also happen between utility-scale renewable energy and distributed energy resource (DER) project businesses, even though they operate very differently, almost like construction vs. manufacturing business models. DER asset managers and investors must deal with large quantities of smaller assets to scale and reach profitability. Portfolio financing is a must-have function. They have already invested a lot of resources into building the right tools, processes, and expertise. They can provide lessons learned on why and how to do portfolio financing, which can be a competitive advantage for large utility-scale investors and independent power producers (IPPs).


‍


Portfolio financing consolidates multiple renewable energy assets—operational or in development—into a single financing structure. This approach offers numerous benefits over traditional single-asset project financing, including risk diversification, economies of scale, and enhanced flexibility:


1. **Risk Diversification:** Portfolio financing allows for the distribution of risk across various projects. By incorporating assets that differ in geography, technology, and revenue profiles, the underperformance of one project can be offset by the success of others. This diversification reduces the likelihood of default for lenders and provides sponsors with more stable returns.
2. **Economies of Scale:** Managing multiple projects under a single financing structure can lead to cost efficiencies. Shared due diligence, negotiation, and documentation processes lower transaction costs per project. Additionally, the collective procurement of equipment and services can result in better pricing and terms, further enhancing the financial viability of the projects.
3. **Flexibility:** A portfolio approach provides greater flexibility in managing individual projects. Cash flows from different assets can support debt repayment and covenant compliance, allowing for more adaptable financial management. This flexibility is particularly beneficial in the dynamic renewable energy market, where project performance can vary due to factors like weather conditions and local energy market dynamics.

### Key Considerations for Portfolio Financing


While promising, portfolio financing requires careful planning:


1. Correlation Analysis: Identifying and quantifying correlations across variables such as commodity prices or regulatory impacts are critical for minimizing portfolio volatility.
2. Flexibility for New Assets: Flexibility to add or refinance assets is critical for adapting to market changes and scaling the portfolio.
3. Portfolio Optimization: By combining projects with distinct revenue streams—such as solar power with PPA and energy storage for arbitrage opportunities — investors can achieve greater stability and higher returns.
4. Borrowing Base Management: The availability of capital depends on each project’s viability and contribution to the portfolio, accounting for factors like operational status, asset type, and revenue models.
5. Balancing Greenfield and Operational Projects: It is crucial to manage the higher risks associated with greenfield construction projects, which often involve separate financing during early phases.
6. Worst-case Scenario Planning: Rare but impactful events like natural disasters or equipment recalls can significantly disrupt project performance. Geographic diversification is built into portfolio recommendations to reduce exposure to localized events. Counterparty and supplier analysis tools ensure that systemic risks from defaults or equipment issues are minimized.


### Portfolio Optimization vs. Asset Aggregation


Traditionally, the only way to construct a portfolio has been to "templatize" the projects and make them replicable. Standardization can be beneficial in speeding up asset aggregation. However, more is needed to maximize a portfolio's risk return. DER companies have effectively leveraged IT and AI-enabled platforms (like Perl Street) to construct high-performing portfolios by making the portfolio's financial asset management agnostic to hardware and data sources. These technologies integrate the following pillars into its risk management framework:


1. **Understanding Risks:** By aggregating and analyzing project-level data, users can pinpoint the root causes of financial, operational, and regulatory risks.
2. **Modeling Risks:** Advanced probabilistic techniques on the platform help model interconnected variables and project realistic outcomes.
3. **Portfolio Construction:** The platform’s tools facilitate the creation of diversified portfolios with uncorrelated projects, enhancing return stability while minimizing risk.
