### Are there any specific regulations for pool installations in Phoenix?


Phoenix has several specific regulations for residential pool construction and installation. As outlined in the


[Arizona Revised Statutes 36-1681](https://www.azleg.gov/ars/36/01681.htm) , residential swimming pools must be entirely enclosed by a wall, fence, or barrier of at least five feet, measured on the exterior side. These laws apply to swimming pools or any contained body of water with more than 18 inches depth and that is wider than eight feet at any point.


Additionally, the city of


[Phoenix requires two separate sets of inspections](https://www.phoenix.gov/content/dam/phoenix/pddsite/documents/trt/external/dsd_trt_pdf_00144.pdf) before a pool can be filled. While these inspections are called “pre-plaster” and “pre-gunite,” they may still apply to pools that do not include concrete in their construction, such as fiberglass and vinyl liner pools. Phoenix Building Construction Code requires that in addition to Arizona state-mandated fencing of at least five feet high, pool fencing must separate the pool from neighboring properties and there must be a physical barrier between a house and a swimming pool located on the same property. One approved option is the addition of a motorized safety cover that meets compliance with ASTM F 1346.


Maricopa County also has strict


[barrier requirements](https://www.maricopa.gov/DocumentCenter/View/69465/Pool-Barrier-Requirements) . All pools must be set back at least three feet from surrounding property lines and pool barriers must conform to specific measurements. Requirements dictate that barriers must contain “no openings in the wall, fence or barrier through which a spherical object four inches in diameter can pass” and must be located at least 20 inches from the water’s edge.

### What are the typical challenges homeowners face when installing a pool in Phoenix?


Phoenix is located in the Sonoran Desert — a region of the U.S. where extreme heat, intense UV exposure, and


[seasonal monsoons](https://www.azwater.gov/news/articles/2024-05-28) create year-round challenges for pool owners. Summer temperatures


[regularly spike above 110°F](https://www.12news.com/article/weather/extreme-weather/surprising-comparison-between-phoenixs-extreme-heat-other-big-cities-america/75-25a728bd-3fc5-4dcd-bbe7-396722d8c44e) , and the city averages over 300 days of sunshine annually. Without quality materials or proper maintenance, intense heat and UV exposure can fade pool finishes, accelerate chlorine breakdown, and contribute to rapid water evaporation.


Another challenge is that many residential properties in Phoenix sit on either hard caliche layers or expansive clay soils. Clay soil can shift seasonally, requiring pool wall reinforcement during construction, while yards with caliche may need specialized excavation equipment to properly situate your pool.
