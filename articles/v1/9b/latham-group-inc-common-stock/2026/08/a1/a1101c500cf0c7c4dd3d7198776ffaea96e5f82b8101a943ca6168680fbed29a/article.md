## Inground Pool Options


Inground swimming pools come in a variety of sizes, shapes and materials – each with their own unique attributes. Three of the most popular types of inground pools are:


- **Fiberglass pools.** The ultimate in beauty, durability and low-maintenance enjoyment, Latham fiberglass pools are made with a scratch-resistant, algae-resistant gel coat finish that minimizes upkeep and delivers a smooth-to-the-touch feel in a range of dazzling colors.


- **Vinyl liner pools.** For a beautifully blank canvas that lets you customize nearly every aspect of your pool, vinyl liner pools are ideal. From the color of your liner to an array of custom features, your pool takes shape according to your preferred aesthetics to bring your vision to life.


- **Plunge pools.** Ideal for smaller backyards or when you want a pool that doesn’t dominate the entire space, plunge pools leave you with more room for an outdoor grilling station, a shady pergola or even a homegrown veggie garden.


No matter what type of inground pool you choose, Latham offers a range of options to help you create a bespoke pool that works for your lifestyle. Features like variable-speed pumps, algae-resistant finishes, and eco-minded pool covers not only offer a more beautiful backyard experience, but put water conservation and ease of maintenance top-of-mind.


Many Latham pool models are autocover-ready, making it easy to add an automatic safety cover that provides both protection and sustainability. Historically, San Diego has been


[prone to drought and water shortages](https://thinkpic.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/PIC_The-Story-of-San-Diegos-Water.pdf) , making water a precious commodity. Not only can an autocover prevent unauthorized access and protect pets and children from falling in when your pool is unsupervised, but it can also prevent water evaporation, saving natural resources.

## FAQs


###


The average cost to install a pool in San Diego depends on several factors. The size of your pool, custom features and the layout and geographical features of your backyard all play a role. For instance, if you live in an area with clay soil like Rancho Santa Fe, your builder will need to prepare the site with backfill and drainage features to prevent water from expanding the soil around your pool. Specialized solutions to ensure your pool is properly installed can add to the cost of labor and installation.


Custom features like an specially-cut autocover for a freeform pool, LED pool lights, or a Baja ledge can also add to the total cost of your pool. Typically, building a new pool in San Diego can start as low as $50,000 and run upwards of $150,000 for bigger pools with more elaborate features.


###


Building a new residential pool in San Diego


[requires](https://www.sandiego.gov/sites/default/files/dsdib712.pdf) a building permit and the project must comply with the California Building Code, the California Swimming Pool Safety Act, local zoning requirements and applicable city or county rules. Within the City of San Diego, private pools also need to meet local setback requirements, barrier details, and permitting requirements.


As per


[California’s Title 24](https://poolpromag.com/the-solar-solution/) , gas-only pool heaters are no longer allowed for new pools as part of a statewide effort to reduce emissions and energy consumption. Instead, pools must meet specific energy standards that require heat pumps, solar systems, or a hybrid system that incorporates renewable energy.


California also requires compliant pool barriers and other drowning-prevention measures. State law specifies that enclosures must be


[at least 60 inches high](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=HSC&sectionNum=115923.) , gates must open away from the pool and gates must be self-closing and self-latching. Any openings in pool gates or fencing must not be wide enough for a four-inch diameter sphere to pass through. Additionally, compliant fencing must not have any features that would easily allow a toddler to climb over it and access the pool area without adult supervision.


Some properties may also need added review beyond the pool permit itself. In parts of San Diego County, grading, drainage, slope conditions and


[septic or onsite wastewater systems](https://www.sandiegocounty.gov/content/dam/sdc/pds/ceqa/Soitec-Documents/Final-EIR-Files/references/rtcref/ch3.1.9/2014-12-19_DEH2010.pdf) can affect what is required and which agency must sign off. Additionally, San Diego may also request a geotechnical investigation report prior to breaking ground on a new pool to


[evaluate soil conditions](https://www.sandiego.gov/sites/default/files/dsdib712.pdf) , including expansive soil, unstable ground, fill materials and more.


###


Some of the most common hurdles San Diego homeowners encounter when building a pool are due to geographic features and ensuring a pool meets regulatory requirements. Sloped lots, limited backyard access, retained soil, setback requirements and proximity to adjacent properties can all influence pool design and construction timelines. The


[City of San Diego](https://www.sandiego.gov/sites/default/files/dsdib712.pdf) also notes site-specific considerations such as native soils, structural design, and retained soil conditions for certain pool types.


San Diego’s diverse terrain is one reason why geotechnical reviews of soil and yard conditions are often a big part of the pool permitting and approval process before construction begins. Areas like Rancho Santa Fe have expansive


[clay soil](https://www.foundationsonthelevel.com/blog/southern-california-soil-issues/) that can swell when wet and contract when it dries (like pottery). In other parts of the greater San Diego area, Oceanside and Carlsbad have loose, sandy soil that may require reinforcement and retaining walls to prevent shifting.


For some homeowners, the biggest challenge is coordination. Depending on the property, a pool project may involve separate reviews for building, grading and onsite wastewater systems rather than a single batch approval.
