## 1. The Data Saturation Challenge


### 1.1 Traditional data sources reaching alpha saturation (social media, credit card, geo-location)


The investment landscape is experiencing diminishing returns from conventional data sources as shown in Figure 1. Internet data, social media signals, credit card transactions, and geo-location information, once considered cutting-edge alternative data, are now mainstream.

Figure 1. Social media and other sources show decreasing alpha generation potential as adoption increases


As more firms integrate these data sources into their strategies, the alpha advantage continues to shrink. This democratization of data access has created a paradox: **when everyone has the same information, the competitive edge disappears** .


### 1.2 The untapped potential of satellite imagery analysis

Figure 2. Satellite imagery use-cases when combined with Artificial Intelligence (i.e., Visual Intelligence)


While traditional alternative data sources become saturated, satellite imagery emerges as a frontier with vast unexplored potential as illustrated on Figure 2. From counting vehicles in parking lots to monitoring oil storage tanks and agricultural production, the applications are diverse and growing.


The key difference lies in the **complexity of extraction** — while almost anyone with a budget can purchase credit card data, deriving insights from satellite imagery requires sophisticated visual intelligence capabilities, creating a natural barrier to entry.


## 2. Democratization of Visual Intelligence


### 2.1 Historical barriers: High costs ($300k) and extensive expertise requirements


The journey of visual intelligence began with significant barriers to entry. Organizations needed to invest around **$300,000** only in data labelling during a 12 months project. This substantial financial commitment made satellite imagery analysis a privilege of large institutions.


Press enter or click to view image in full size


Figure 3. Break down of costs for a typical 12-month implementation of a visual intelligence project


Beyond the monetary investment, the expertise requirements were equally daunting. Organizations needed PhD-level professionals in AI and computer vision to develop and maintain these systems. The technical complexity required deep understanding of machine learning algorithms, image processing techniques, and domain-specific knowledge.


### 2.2. The dramatic shift: Cost reduction and accessibility in minutes versus months


Today’s visual intelligence landscape has undergone a radical transformation (see Figure 4). The advent of pre-trained models and cloud computing has slashed some of the costs by up to 300 times, making the technology accessible to organizations of all sizes.


Press enter or click to view image in full size


Figure 4. Visual intelligence costs have been declining amid the rise of AI


This acceleration comes from automated model training pipelines, transfer learning capabilities, and intuitive interfaces that abstract away technical complexity.


The democratization extends to expertise requirements as well. User-friendly platforms now enable analysts without PhD-level knowledge to leverage visual intelligence effectively, opening up new possibilities for market research, competitive analysis, and strategic planning.


## 3. Real-World Applications and Case Study


### 3.1 Data center expansion and energy consumption insights


Market intelligence from satellite imagery reveals unprecedented expansion in data center construction and power infrastructure development. This signals NVIDIA’s strategic preparation for sustained AI computing demand.


Satellite analysis shows hyperscalers are rapidly increasing their GPU compute capacity, with power consumption projections suggesting the AI industry could consume energy equivalent to the Netherlands by 2027. This trend aligns with NVIDIA’s reported data center revenue growth but provides earlier insights into capacity expansion.


### 3.2 Google’s global data center construction tracking and implications


Google’s global data center expansion provides a concrete example of visual intelligence’s analytical power. Satellite imagery analysis reveals active construction in eight new countries, with particular insights into key projects like the ~$650 million Netherlands facility and the $200 million Santiago development (see Figure 5).

Water consumption patterns, visible through multispectral satellite imagery, indicate operational sustainability challenges. The analysis reveals that data centers require millions of liters of water annually for cooling, with usage patterns providing early indicators of capacity utilization.


This comprehensive monitoring of Google’s infrastructure development, shown in Figure 6, offers insights into both geographical expansion strategy and operational constraints. Construction progress rates, when combined with water usage patterns, help predict facility commissioning timelines and potential bottlenecks.

## 4. Conclusion


The democratization of visual intelligence marks a pivotal moment in investment research.


As shown in this post, the dramatic reduction in implementation costs and expertise requirements has made satellite imagery analysis accessible to a broader range of market participants. The transformation from complex, expensive systems to accessible tools suggests we’re entering a new era of visual data-driven investment decision-making.
