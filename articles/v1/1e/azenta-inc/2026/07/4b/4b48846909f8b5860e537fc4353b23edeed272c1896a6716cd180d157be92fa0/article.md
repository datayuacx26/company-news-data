# Lab Chemical Relocation: Hidden Risks and How to Avoid Them

Lab chemical relocation is common when facilities expand or research operations consolidate and can even be a routine step in a multi-site pharmaceutical workflow. The process may seem straightforward at first glance, but it’s more challenging than simply moving bottles from origin to destination. Every lab chemical relocation carries serious operational, safety, and regulatory risks like chemical spills, regulatory violations, compromised material integrity, and personal injury.


These risks can significantly impact research continuity and quality compliance. Because of this, lab chemical relocation isn’t simply a logistics matter. Nor is it a suitable task for general moving companies who lack the knowledge and capabilities to proactively mitigate these risks. Instead, trust your chemicals and lab assets to lab relocation specialists.


Moving lab chemicals is a complex and multi-layered process that can be governed by federal and state laws, institutional policies, and even basic chemistry principles. On top of this, cold chain material relocation includes the extra criteria for consistent temperature control. Errors and deviations from guidelines and plans can range from timely to costly to a worst-case catastrophic consequence.


Understanding the hidden risks involved in chemical material and lab relocation projects is the first step toward protecting valuable laboratory assets and ensuring a straightforward move.


## Risk #1 Regulatory Compliance


Safety data sheets (SDS) and material safety data sheets (MSDS) are well known to anyone working in the lab, but once that chemical leaves the facility bound for a new destination, different regulations are added to the equation.


Depending on the material, lab relocation specialists may need to be trained on and compliant with regulations from U.S. governmental agencies like the Occupational Safety and Health Administration (OSHA), the Department of Transportation (DOT), and the Environmental Protection Agency (EPA)1. Penalties for non-compliance can range from fines to civil action in federal court, and even imprisonment2. State and international regulations may introduce further complexity that must be navigated.


These combined regulations underscore the need to work with trained lab chemical relocation experts that understand how to safely and efficiently move your chemical materials while adhering to multiple guidelines.


## Risk #2 Chain-of-Custody


When moving laboratory chemicals, maintaining traceability and accountability is essential. This includes clearly documenting incoming and outgoing handoffs, the truck’s route, actions taken by individual lab relocation specialists, and precise location of each container.


During a cold chain material relocation, tracking consistent and precise temperature is vital. Any excursions during the move can alter chemical properties, degrade compounds, and compromise experimental reliability.


Lab relocation specialists manage temperature sensitive material moves using vehicles with multiple power and freezer redundancies, on-board generators, and other precautions to strengthen the chain-of-custody. This provides confidence that chemicals and sensitive materials are fully protected throughout the lab chemical relocation process.


## Risk #3 Packaging and Chemical Compatibility


Improper packaging can easily lead to a chemical transport incident. Chemical containers may appear sealed, undamaged, and properly labeled, yet when subject to normal vehicle vibrations and pressure changes, these cumulative stressors might elevate the risk of leaks, spills, and contamination.


At the same time, if incompatible chemicals, like acids and bases or flammables and oxidizers, are packed together or insufficiently contained during a lab chemical relocation, a serious breach could result in catastrophic and life-threatening consequences for the movers and the surrounding communities.


A chemical and laboratory relocation company with deep experience and chemical knowledge will use packaging specifically designed for hazardous and laboratory chemicals, and segregate incompatible and reactive materials. They will also ensure that containers are properly stabilized to minimize movement during the trip.


## Risk #4 Unknown or Legacy Chemicals


As lab operations evolve or researchers change roles, some reagents, samples, and other chemicals may be pushed to the side, never to be used again. The same can happen when a label is compromised. These unknown “legacy chemicals” present a risk because the material cannot be properly classified, packaged, or documented.


Simply moving an unknown material along with everything else is not advisable, and in fact, attempting to transport an unknown chemical may violate federal law and increase safety risks.

## Risk #5 Time Delays


Time is a constant pressure, and unfortunately, this pressure may be an incentive to cut corners. When it comes to lab chemical relocation or a full laboratory relocation, the consequences of rushing steps like vetting qualified laboratory relocation specialists, documenting inventory, and more in the interest of time can result in productivity, safety, financial, and legal ramifications that ripple throughout the company.


Early planning is the best way to reduce time pressures. Take the time to align all internal stakeholders on a coordinated logistics plan. Share the plan details with external vendors like a lab relocation specialist so that they have ample opportunity to provide early-stage guidance and begin preparations.


## Benefits Beyond Protecting Chemicals


Lab chemicals are a foundation of critical research, testing, development, and manufacturing activities. Efficiently relocating them while sustaining safety and compliance requires skills and expertise beyond what a general mover can offer.


By identifying and mitigating hidden risks early in a relocation project, laboratories are in a better position to protect sensitive materials, maintain compliance, and avoid costly disruptions.
