# How to keep a Mac timesheet without manual entry

Every manual timesheet fails the same way: it depends on the person with the least spare attention - you, mid-work - remembering to log time. The fix is timekeeping software for Mac where the timesheet fills itself in, and the right tool depends on who the timesheet is for.


## If the timesheet is for you


When nobody is billing anybody - you just need a truthful record of your working time - a work journal beats a time-logging grid.[Dayflow](https://www.dayflow.so/) (free, open source) records your Mac’s screen at one frame every 10 seconds and has AI write each day into a readable timeline: tasks, times, durations, in sentences. It is a timesheet in the only format you will actually reread, and it needs zero entry: no projects to set up, no categories to maintain. Everything stays local on your Mac.


That covers the two commonest personal uses:[standup updates](https://www.dayflow.so/blog/automatic-standup-updates/) (“what did I do yesterday”) and the end-of-week reckoning (“[where did this week go](https://www.dayflow.so/where-did-my-day-go/) ”).


## If the timesheet is for clients


Billable hours need project buckets, rates, and export to invoices - a real timesheet product with automatic capture underneath:


- **[Timing](https://timingapp.com/)** (Mac, from ~$10/month) - records apps, documents, and sites automatically, then lets you drag activity into client projects with per-project rates. The deepest Mac-native reporting; the standard pick for consultants.
- **[Memtime](https://memtime.com/)** (Mac/Windows/Linux, paid) - captures your activity in minute increments locally, then syncs finished time entries into Jira, ClickUp, and other project tools. Built for agencies reconstructing billable time.
- **[Toggl](https://toggl.com/) /[Clockify](https://clockify.me/)** - timer-first team tools; their Mac apps add some auto-tracking assists, but you are still fundamentally operating timers ([details here](https://www.dayflow.so/compare/toggl-for-mac/) ).


## If the timesheet is for your employer


That is monitoring software territory (Hubstaff, DeskTime, Insightful) - a category with screenshots-for-your-boss and activity scores. If you landed here looking for the opposite of that, you want[time tracking without surveillance](https://www.dayflow.so/blog/time-tracking-without-surveillance/) .


## The five-minute setup


For the personal case: download[Dayflow](https://www.dayflow.so/) (macOS 14+, MIT-licensed), grant screen-recording permission, and pick where the AI runs - fully local via Ollama/LM Studio, or your own API key. There is no step two; the timesheet starts writing itself. For billing, take Timing’s 30-day trial and see whether its project reports match how you invoice.
