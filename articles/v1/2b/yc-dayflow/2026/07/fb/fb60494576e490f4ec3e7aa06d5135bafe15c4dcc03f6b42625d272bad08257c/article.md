# Time tracking without surveillance: tracking for yourself, not your boss

Search for “time tracking software” and most of what ranks is employee monitoring: admin dashboards, activity scores, screenshots-for-your-manager, idle detection. Useful to somebody, maybe - but if you wanted to understand your own time, you were looking for a different product category wearing the same name.


The line between the two is simple: **who sees the data.**


## Surveillance tools, briefly


Hubstaff, DeskTime, Insightful, Time Doctor and the rest are bought by employers and installed on workers’ machines. Their defining features - random screenshots, keyboard/mouse activity rates, team leaderboards, admin consoles - only make sense when the viewer is not the person being tracked. Whatever you think of that category, one fact matters here: architecture follows the buyer. These tools are built to report upward, because that is what they are for.


## Self-tracking tools


A tracker built for the person being tracked inverts every design decision:


- **The data stays with you.**[Dayflow](https://www.dayflow.so/) stores recordings and its timeline database only on your Mac; with a local AI model, nothing leaves the machine at all.[ActivityWatch](https://activitywatch.net/) is likewise local-first. There is no admin view because there is no admin.
- **Honesty is safe.** A record only you can see can afford to include the YouTube detour and the 40-minute slump. That honesty is the entire value - you cannot fix a day you cannot see, and you will not let a tool see it if your manager can too.
- **No score, no judgment.** Self-tracking tools describe (“Researching competitors, 2:10-2:55”); monitoring tools rate (activity: 61%). Descriptions inform; scores perform.
- **You can turn it off.** Pause before a sensitive session, quit any time, delete everything locally. Try that with software your employer installed.


Open source is the strongest version of the guarantee: Dayflow is MIT-licensed with the[code public](https://github.com/JerryZLiu/Dayflow) , so “there is no upward reporting” is a checkable property, not a promise. The[full safety breakdown is here](https://www.dayflow.so/blog/is-dayflow-safe/) .


## Why this distinction is worth a whole post


Because the surveillance category poisons the well. People who would genuinely benefit from seeing their own time - anyone who has hit 6pm wondering[where the day went](https://www.dayflow.so/where-did-my-day-go/) - bounce off the idea because “time tracking” evokes the boss’s screenshot bot. If that was your hesitation: the tool you were avoiding and the tool you need share a search term and nothing else.


Pick something local, open source, and built to show the data only to you. Dayflow (free, Mac, writes your day up in sentences) and ActivityWatch (free, cross-platform, raw activity data) are both exactly that - the[comparison is here](https://www.dayflow.so/compare/activitywatch-vs-dayflow/) .
