# How to review your deep work without a distraction blocker

Distraction blockers work while they are on. The moment you disable one to “quickly check something,” you are back where you started - and there is no record of what happened. A deep-work review is the other approach: instead of policing yourself in the moment, you look at an honest record of your day and adjust. Blockers are enforcement; reviews are feedback.


## What a deep-work review needs


To review your focus you need to know, truthfully, three things:


1. **When were the long unbroken stretches?** Deep work shows up as 60-plus-minute blocks on a single task.
2. **What actually broke them?** Slack, email, a “quick” YouTube detour - the breaks you do not remember are the ones that matter.
3. **What did the fragmented time cost?** Six hours at the desk that produced two hours of real work is the pattern to catch.


Manual timers cannot answer these questions because you do not start a timer before getting distracted. Nobody logs “aimless twitter, 2:15-2:40.”

[Dayflow](https://www.dayflow.so/) records your Mac’s screen at one frame every 10 seconds and has AI write the day into a readable timeline - including the parts you would not have logged yourself. It can tell “researching on YouTube” from “watching cat videos,” which is exactly the distinction a focus review needs. It is free, open source, and 100% local: recordings never leave your computer.


The weekly review, ten minutes on Friday:


1. Scan the week’s timelines for your longest unbroken blocks. When did they happen? (Most people find a consistent window - often mid-morning.)
2. Count the interruptions inside what you intended as focus time. Name the top offender.
3. Compare intention to record: the meeting-free Tuesday afternoon you planned - what did it actually contain?
4. Set one change for next week. Just one: “Slack closed until 11am.”


## Why this beats blocking


A blocker treats the symptom in the moment and teaches you nothing about the pattern. A review compounds: after three weeks you know your genuine focus windows, your actual top distraction (rarely the one you would have guessed), and whether your one-change-per-week is working. Some people run both - a blocker during their protected window, the review to decide where the window should be.
