# Your Travel Nursing Career in 2026: What to Know, Where to Go, and How to Grow

You didn't become a nurse to stay in one place — not professionally, and maybe not geographically either. Whether you're a seasoned traveler mapping out your next assignment or an RN who's been eyeing the travel life and finally feels ready, one thing is clear: the road ahead is wide open.


The travel nursing landscape continues to evolve, and staying informed is one of the most powerful things you can do for your career. Here's what you need to know heading into 2026.


### **The Market Is Strong — and It's Not Slowing Down**


The demand for travel nursing soared during the pandemic, and while the spike has stabilized, the profession remains steady due to ongoing workforce challenges. The U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics estimates that nearly 200,000 new RN jobs will be created annually from 2023 to 2033. Travel nurses are uniquely positioned to benefit because of their adaptability and willingness to relocate.


### **Where the Demand Is Hottest**


Geography matters.[States](https://www.amnhealthcare.com/careers/nursing/locations/) with booming healthcare industries, like California, Texas, and Florida, are expected to see higher demand due to growing populations and expanding infrastructure. Facilities are still navigating staffing shortages, especially around peak seasons and in high-turnover units, giving experienced travelers more options and leverage when selecting assignments.


Timing plays a role, too. While summer months tend to be popular, considering assignments in fall or winter can open doors to less competitive, higher-paying opportunities.


### **What to Expect in Pay**


The era of pandemic-level crisis pay is behind us, but the compensation picture is far from bleak. Pay has stabilized at competitive levels, usually higher than pre-pandemic, and in late 2024,[average travel RN pay](https://www.amnhealthcare.com/careers/nursing/salary/) saw a slight increase. For 2025 and into 2026, expect generally stable rates with modest growth as demand holds.


Pay rates continued to rise in critical specialties, such as ICU, ER, and OR. And remember, don't just look at hourly rates. Consider housing stipends, travel reimbursements, and tax-free allowances when evaluating the full picture.


### **The Specialties Facilities Are Asking For**


ICU,[ER](https://www.amnhealthcare.com/careers/nursing/travel/specialties/er/) , telemetry, med-surg, operating room, and rural home health currently generate the most requests. With the growing complexity of healthcare needs, travel nurses with specialized training and certifications will remain in high demand. Whether you're an[L&D](https://www.amnhealthcare.com/careers/nursing/travel/specialties/labor-delivery/) specialist, a[NICU](https://www.amnhealthcare.com/careers/nursing/travel/specialties/nicu/) RN, or a PACU pro, sharpening your skills in a sought-after area can open doors to exclusive, high-paying assignments.


### **Licensing: Get Ahead of the Paperwork**


One of the most common things that slows a travel nurse's momentum is licensing delays. Ensure certifications like BLS, ACLS, and PALS are current, and be ready to secure state-specific licenses through the[Nurse Licensure Compact](https://www.amnhealthcare.com/careers/nursing/license-and-renewal/) or individual state boards.


As more states join the NLC, nurses licensed in a member state enjoy increased mobility and flexibility. Planning to work in a non-compact state? Start those applications four to six months ahead.


### **Set Goals That Move Your Career Forward**


Travel nursing isn't just about the next contract; it's about building a career that's intentional and fulfilling. The SMART framework helps you turn big ambitions into real milestones: refining clinical skills, earning new certifications, managing time in fast-paced environments, and building confidence for new challenges. Your goals don't have to be set in stone. Sometimes just the act of setting them leads to an unexpected and rewarding path.


### **Making the Most of Every Assignment**


Landing the contract is just the beginning. During orientation, don't hesitate to ask questions. Working across different facilities, specialties, and cultures fuels both professional and personal growth. And when it comes to well-being, don't treat it as an afterthought — protecting your energy between assignments isn't a luxury, it's a career strategy.
