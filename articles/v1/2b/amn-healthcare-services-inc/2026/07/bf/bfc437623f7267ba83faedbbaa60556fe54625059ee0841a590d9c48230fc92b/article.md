# Seasonal Travel Nurse Jobs: Why Nurses Move Fast on These High-Pay Opportunities

Seasonal nursing jobs are short-term, high-demand assignments tied to predictable surges in patient volume, and they're among the fastest-filling[opportunities in travel nursing](https://www.amnhealthcare.com/apply/c/Nursing/?filters=JobType:Travel) . Each year, registered nurses who prepare early secure the best locations, preferred shifts, and the most[competitive pay](https://www.amnhealthcare.com/careers/nursing/benefits-and-salary/) . Here's everything you need to know to be first in line.


## What Are Seasonal Nursing Jobs?


Seasonal nursing jobs are temporary RN assignments activated when healthcare facilities anticipate a significant increase in patient census during specific times of year. That window runs roughly from October through March and is driven by flu season, cold and respiratory illness, holiday travel injuries, and the general strain the winter season has on the healthcare system.


During this period, hospitals in high-demand markets bulk-upload hundreds of open positions at once to meet urgent staffing needs. These roles span the most in-demand specialties: Medical-Surgical, Telemetry, PCU,[Emergency Room](https://www.amnhealthcare.com/careers/nursing/travel/specialties/er/) , and[ICU](https://www.amnhealthcare.com/careers/nursing/travel/specialties/icu/) . Because facilities plan months ahead, the nurses who are already prepared, with updated profiles and an active recruiter relationship, are the ones who land the roles they actually want.


## Why Are Seasonal Registered Nurse Jobs So Popular?


Seasonal registered nurse jobs consistently attract travel nurses for a few
straightforward reasons:


- **Higher pay rates:** Contracts issued during peak-demand periods tend to carry competitive and, in many cases, above-market weekly pay rates, which reflects the urgency facilities face when census spikes.
- **Desirable locations:** The bulk of seasonal openings concentrate in highly sought-after markets like California, Florida, New York, Arizona, and Hawaii.
- **Shift and holiday preference:** Nurses who book early gain first access to preferred shift schedules, and in many cases can request up to five days off during the holiday season, an advantage that disappears once positions are filled.
- **Exclusive, time-limited openings:** Unlike standard travel assignments, seasonal roles aren't replenished once they're taken. When they're gone, they're gone.

## How Long Do Seasonal Nursing Jobs Last?


Most seasonal travel nurse assignments run 8 to 13 weeks, aligned with the core of peak patient volume periods. However, select locations also offer 26-week contract options, an advantage for nurses who want stability across the entire fall-to-spring window without the uncertainty of renegotiating mid-season.


A 26-week contract locks in your rate, your location, and your schedule before the competition arrives. It also simplifies housing arrangements, makes holiday planning more predictable, and allows nurses who start in August or September to transition seamlessly into the heart of winter season without breaking stride. For nurses weighing a 13-week versus a 26-week term: a shorter contract gives flexibility, while a 26-week commitment works best when location and rate certainty matter most.


## Where Can You Find Seasonal Nursing Jobs?


Seasonal RN opportunities concentrate in states that experience the sharpest population swings during fall and winter. California and New York consistently lead in volume, followed by Georgia, Pennsylvania, and Iowa. Warm-weather states that attract seasonal residents also see significant demand spikes, with seasonal nursing jobs in Florida standing out as one of the most sought-after destinations of the season.


### Why Florida Is a Top Destination for Seasonal Nursing Jobs


Florida's appeal is dual-layered: its population surges as snowbirds and retirees migrate south for winter, driving a predictable and significant increase in hospital census. Seasonal nursing jobs in Florida are available across a broad range of specialties, particularly in Medical-Surgical, Telemetry, and ICU. Travel nurses seeking sun, a lower cost of living, and competitive pay consistently rank Florida among their top placement preferences, which also means these roles attract strong competition and fill quickly. Starting your search and getting your profile ready early is especially important if Florida is on your list.
