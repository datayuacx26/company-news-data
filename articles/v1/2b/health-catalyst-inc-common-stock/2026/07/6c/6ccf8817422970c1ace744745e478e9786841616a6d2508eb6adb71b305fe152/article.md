# Why Patient Retention Is Healthcare’s Untapped ROI Opportunity

The healthcare industry is bracing for steep challenges ahead—from market disruptions to the growing cost of patient retention. As systems confront tighter margins and shifting expectations, stemming patient leakage is emerging as a critical survival strategy.


[Revenue is expected to shrink](https://www.mha.org/newsroom/new-pwc-report-warns-of-rising-hospital-costs-and-mounting-financial-pressure-on-u-s-healthcare-system/) due to macroeconomic pressures affecting the total insured population, changes in reimbursement, and the cost of capital required to invest in growth. As a result, competition is increasing. Retaining existing patients is a crucial first step in minimizing revenue loss and managing expenses. It’s[cheaper to keep a patient](https://bmcprimcare.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s12875-021-01593-8) than to acquire a new one.


Further, the competitive landscape has shifted. Health systems aren’t just competing with the organization down the street anymore; they’re vying against every other demand on a consumer’s time, attention, and discretionary dollars, amid rising inflation and financial strain.


## The Rising Importance of Patient Retention in Healthcare


To compete, health systems must offer a differentiated consumer experience—from digital communication to in-person care—that captures attention, earns trust, and inspires loyalty. The shift from viewing patients solely as patients to understanding them as empowered consumers is no longer theoretical but a strategic necessity.


Beyond financial pressures driving an increasing need to prioritize patient retention, societal forces are also at play. For instance, young people are getting diagnosed with cancer at[an alarming rate](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2808381?) . As more patients require timely, coordinated care, the healthcare system is becoming more complex, and issues like insurer hurdles, limited payer guidance, and fragmented provider networks make it harder to navigate.


This complex environment often results in inconsistent follow-up appointments and delayed treatments. Care falls through the cracks, conditions worsen, and missed referrals become avoidable hospitalizations. This breakdown isn’t just a patient experience improvement problem; it’s a major driver of disease progression and rising costs.


## Understanding Patient Leakage—and Its Cost


The business of healthcare is innately human and personal. The following story grounds this idea into something tangible.


Primary care physicians at a large healthcare organization (“System A”) recommended a patient undergo testing for a concerning symptom. System A didn’t follow up to schedule that test, and, as is often the case, the patient asked friends and family for suggestions. The patient scheduled the test at another healthcare institution (“System B”) and was then referred to a specialist within the same group.


Unfortunately, System B misdiagnosed the patient. The patient’s disease progressed, which compromised their outcomes. When the patient reappeared in System A’s primary care, they were correctly assessed. But due to the lapse in correct treatment, the care was more burdensome for the patient; their healthcare costs were higher and the collateral damage greater.


It’s this personal story—not just the financial figures—that the health system should keep top of mind as it works to improve patient retention and continuity of care.


## Why Patients Churn: Experience Gaps and Fragmented Communication


When patients disengage, it’s often because their[digital experiences feel hollow](https://www.mdpi.com/3126282) , leaving them feeling forgotten and tempted to walk away. Some of the confounding factors include:


- Despite having patients’ most sensitive information, messages from their health systems are often irrelevant or generic.


- Patients often report not feeling seen or known after sharing endless personal details, such as family health history, financial barriers, and intimate details of their symptoms.


- Siloed digital technologies hinder healthcare organizations from surfacing the right insights at the right time to guide patient care at scale.


- Multiple service lines have overlapped or have misaligned KPIs, resulting in over-messaging and confusion—creating abrasion rooted in a lack of visibility into what each entity is communicating.


Healthcare systems simply aren’t converting the vast amounts of unique data points into an experience that eases a patient’s way through a complicated and highly complex healthcare system.


By and large, organizations have the right intentions: to close care gaps, improve quality measures, reduce readmissions, etc. Still, fragmentation erodes patient trust, leading to lackluster healthcare experiences that drive even satisfied patients away.


Solving this challenge is nuanced and requires more than a single tool or department. It demands a coordinated, cross-functional, data-driven approach—one that reflects the complexity of the patient journey and enables health systems to deliver timely, personalized, and quality care that engages patients and encourages return visits.


## Patients Expect More: What Healthcare Can Learn from Consumer Brands


Patients don’t evaluate their healthcare experience in isolation. They expect to take care of their health with the[same convenience, transparency, and personalization they’ve come to know](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9597013/) from other digital experiences, such as booking travel, streaming digital content, or shopping with online retailers.


Their expectations have shifted to real-time updates, proactive communication, the application of previously expressed preferences, and suggested (and easily executable) next steps.


Consumer brands have set this new standard—leveraging copious amounts of data to understand their customers deeply. These expectations now extend to every interaction consumers have, including healthcare.


Today’s patient experiences must include:


- Simple, digital-first access to care orchestration.


- Timely, relevant communication via their preferred channel that speaks to their motivations, needs, and preferences.


- A perceived sense of feeling seen and heard—that their provider knows and respects them.
‍
- Bonus: An overall experience that recognizes and uses their longitudinal data and prior interactions to personalize their care.


When health systems fail to meet those expectations, patients disengage. And when they do, they don’t always return.


## Building a Data-Driven Patient Retention Strategy: Here’s How


Stopping patient leakage through a consumer-driven approach begins with understanding patients—what they need, what motivates them, and where they fall off the care path. That starts with a strong data foundation.


Healthcare leaders need solutions that fit into their current systems without adding complexity, too. They need a way to combine clinical, operational, and financial data and insights, including patient behavior, to derive a deeper understanding of patients and meet their needs throughout the care journey.


These data and analytics ecosystems must help orchestrate care, forecast patient engagement, and prioritize care needs, while honing the nudge frequency and messaging channel, ultimately optimizing it for conversion versus abrasion.
