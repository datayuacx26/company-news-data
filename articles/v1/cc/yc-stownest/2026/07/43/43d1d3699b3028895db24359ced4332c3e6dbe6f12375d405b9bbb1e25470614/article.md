The arrival of[Ashada Maasa](https://stownest.com/?utm_source=blog&utm_medium=Websiteblog&utm_campaign=ashada_maasa_plannings+) marks the beginning of the monsoons in India and the start of a month when Housewarmings, Weddings, and Relocations are restricted in many homes across the country. ## **What is Ashada Maasa and Why is it Important?**


Ashada Maasa is a special time for Indian families, usually falling between June and July. It's a month many people set aside for prayer, temple visits, and spending time with family. It's less about big celebrations and more about slowing down and reflecting.Because of this, most families choose to hold down big life events during Ashada things like weddings, moving into a new home, or housewarming.


But here's the thing: it's not just about tradition. There are plenty of reasons that fall outside the box as well, like the July-August monsoon season that makes relocating a challenge. There are also those that prefer to proactively relocate when it feels like the best time to start fresh and settle down. When you look at the emotional and the practical sides, it is easier to make a decision that is best for you and your family.

## **The Real Reasons People Avoid Moving During Ashada**


### **1. Monsoon Season & Moving**


The rain during Ashada, the Indian monsoon season, creates a lot of problems when relocating, Loading and unloading can damage electronics and furniture. Documents may also suffer serious damage. Muddy, water-soaked access roads can cause delays and make moving even more difficult. Slippery steps and doorways can cause injuries to those moving.


### **2. High Winds Add to the Risk**


Along with rain, several parts of India see strong winds during this period. This makes handling large or fragile items such as glass panels, mirrors, appliances, tall furniture noticeably riskier during loading and unloading.


### **3. Cultural Belief in Inauspicious Timing**


For many households, the reluctance is not related to logistics, but to tradition. Housewarmings or marriages have traditionally been avoided during Ashada. Because of the length of the summer monsoon, Ashada is thought to be an unlucky month. This belief is undying and is kept with great honor from generation to generation.


### **4. Income Uncertainty Discourages Large Expenditures**


A more serious issue relates to the economy. Many self-employed citizens, traders, and owners of small businesses experience a slowdown of activity during this time of year due to a cultural slowdown on the demand side. People become more careful about discretionary expenses when income becomes uncertain. As a result, the relocation is postponed.


### **5. Moving Becomes Logistically More Difficult**


Moving during the month of Ashada actually becomes even harder to do well. Combine the factors of wet roads and a belief system with the greater risks of damaged goods in transit and all the other unpredictable elements, and the friction of what should normally be an easy relocation becomes much more challenging.
