## 1. The Quick Selection tool


The Quick Selection tool uses Adobe’s Sensei AI technology to detect edges in your image after you’ve painted a selection, making it easier to separate your subject from the background.


**Best for:** Making quick selections, images with more complex details, and when there’s a high contrast between your subject and background.


### How to remove backgrounds in Photoshop using the Quick Selection tool


1.


Sign in to Photoshop and upload your image by clicking **Create +** on the menubar to the left or select **Upload file** in the welcome screen.


2.


Click on **Select** in the toolbar to open some options and choose **Quick Selection** .


3.


Toggle the **Detect objects** switch on and choose the image subject (which is the chair, in the image below).


4.


Photoshop will place a pink highlight on it with wiggly lines so you know that you have selected the right thing.


5.


Press **Ctrl + C** (or **Cmd + C** ) on your keyboard to make a copy of the selected area


6.


Navigate to the **Layers** menu on the right and select the **+** sign to make a new layer.


7.


In the new layer, press **Ctrl + V** (or **Cmd + V** ) to paste the selected area.


8.


Click on the previous layer that has the whole image and click the eye icon next to it to hide it.


9.


Now, you are left with the layer that has only the image subject on a transparent background.

## 3. The Eraser tool


The Eraser tool gives you the most control over your edits, but requires patience and practice. It allows you to manually erase the background around your subject, but you have to be careful to not erase your image subject as well.


**Best for:** When you need to erase specific parts of your background, or are working on images with irregular shapes.


### How to delete backgrounds in Photoshop with the Eraser tool

2.

3.
