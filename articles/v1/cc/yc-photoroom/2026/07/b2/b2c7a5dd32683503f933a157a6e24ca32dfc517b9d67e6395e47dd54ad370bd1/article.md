## Amazon requirements for product imagery


The core[Amazon image requirements](https://www.photoroom.com/blog/amazon-product-images) apply across every category, food included. Main product photography images face the toughest standards and have the biggest impact, because they populate search results and determine your initial click-through rate.


Every seller needs to meet these non-negotiable Amazon photography requirements for the main image:


-


**Pure white background:** The background must have RGB color values of 255, 255, 255, so it blends into Amazon’s search and detail pages.


-


**Product fill:** Your product has to fill 85% or more of the image frame.


-


**No illustrations or mockups:** The image must accurately and realistically represent the product.


-


**No text or graphics:** The main image cannot contain text, watermarks, logos, borders, or inset images.


-


**No props or accessories:** Only the product being sold can appear in the frame; for food photography, that means no cooking utensils or serving dishes can be used.


-


**Image dimensions:** The image needs to be at least 500 pixels on its longest side, although if you want Amazon’s hover-to-zoom feature to work, you should aim for at least 1,000 pixels.


-


**File format:** Amazon accepts JPEG, PNG, TIFF, and non-animated GIF files, with JPEG being the preferred format.


## Types of food product shots for your Amazon listings


Once you’ve perfected the main product image, it’s time to prepare your secondary visuals.[Amazon](https://www.photoroom.com/use-cases/sell-on-amazon) recommends including six images in total (one primary and five secondary) along with one[product video](https://www.photoroom.com/tools/video-generator) .


The requirements for secondary images are less strict, which gives you more creative freedom. Strong food brands use these slots to answer buyer objections, show texture, and tell visual stories.


Let’s look at a few[product photography ideas](https://www.photoroom.com/blog/product-photography-ideas) that translate well to grocery and pantry items.


### Packaging and nutrition shots


Shoppers often care deeply about ingredients and allergens, so use[high-resolution](https://www.photoroom.com/glossary/resolution) secondary images to display nutrition facts panels and ingredient lists clearly. That transparency builds trust and helps shoppers make fast, informed decisions without leaving the listing.


### Ingredient list callouts


Full ingredient lists can be dense, so bring the key selling points to the front with a dedicated visual. To do that, build a clean infographic that shows the raw, high-quality ingredients. If you sell organic honey, for example, you might show the jar next to callouts highlighting its raw, unpasteurized character. Keep the text minimal and benefits-focused, so it doesn't distract too much from the image.


### Texture and detail shots


Food is a sensory purchase, so a closeup that highlights the texture of the item outside the packaging is important for showing customers what the product is actually like. Show the creamy pull of a spread, the texture of a snack bar, or the deep color of a spice blend: These fine details make the food look fresh and appetizing.


### Lifestyle and serving suggestion shots


[Lifestyle shots](https://www.photoroom.com/glossary/lifestyle-image) place your food products in real-world settings, so buyers can envision how they fit into their day to day. These shots both sell the experience and tell a story. You might display a pasta sauce tossed through fresh noodles on a rustic table, or a protein powder blended into a morning smoothie.


## Common food photography mistakes


A single slip in your product photography workflow can lead to rejected images, suppressed listings, and a wasted budget. Food styling is hard, because fresh food loses its appeal fast under warm lights, and small framing errors are magnified at thumbnail size. Knowing where most sellers trip up helps you sidestep costly reshoots and keep listings consistent.


The most common food product photography mistakes on Amazon include:


-


**Non-white backgrounds for main images.** Off-white, light grey, and textured backdrops can trigger automatic suppression or removal.


-


**Raw ingredients in main images.** Fresh fruits or nuts next to a packaged product is a smart secondary image move, but it’s banned in the main slot.


-


**Poor lighting and color casts.** Yellow or blue color casts from an incorrect white balance can make food look stale instead of fresh.


-


**Heavy, unnatural shadows.** Hard shadows read as harsh and amateur, while soft, diffused light keeps food looking tender and appetizing.


-


**Mismatched colors.** If the on-screen color drifts from the real product because of sloppy editing, returns and negative reviews tend to climb.


-


**Low-resolution images.** Blurry files block the zoom tool, which hurts conversions on mobile devices (where[most Amazon traffic happens](https://www.semrush.com/website/amazon.com/overview/) ).
