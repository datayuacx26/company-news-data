Thousands of sports players’ headshots processed via API

Cut editing time for background removal from days to minutes

-

Hit launch deadlines, even with last-minute asset deliveries


Every sports season begins the same way for Sorare: with thousands of player headshots arriving from football, basketball, and baseball clubs around the world. For Jessica Assouline who has spent four years at Sorare—first in business development signing licensing deals, then shifting to operations to bring those partnerships to life—these images represent far more than just photos. They're the foundation of everything Sorare does.


> **"Visuals are really important here at Sorare, specifically player headshots. They're at the core of our product.”**
>
>
> **— Jessica Assouline, Partnership Operations Lead at Sorare**


[Sorare](https://sorare.com/) is a global fantasy sports game where users buy, sell, and trade digital player cards, then use them to build teams just like real-life coaches. The real-world performance of players directly impacts game results, unlocking rewards ranging from signed jerseys to once-in-a-lifetime experiences, such as meeting Zinedine Zidane, the French football player. At the heart of this experience are the player cards themselves, and at the heart of those cards are the headshots Jessica's team processes each season.


But before Photoroom, getting those images ready for launch was anything but simple.


## **The hidden costs of relying on photo agencies**


Before Photoroom, Sorare worked with external photo agencies to edit player headshots. The process seemed straightforward on paper: clubs and leagues would shoot media days at the start of each season, send the images to Sorare, and the agency would handle background removal and retouching. Then Sorare's tech team could create the player cards.


In reality, it was a constant source of stress.


"We’re never really certain of when these media days happen," Jessica shares. "Sometimes they happen a month before the launches, which is great because this gives us enough time for editing. But sometimes we receive them only a couple of days, if not hours, before launch."


When images arrived with just hours to spare, the agency workflow became a bottleneck. I **t took 24 hours to several days to get edited photos back** . Even in the best cases, **turnaround time was 12 hours minimum** . To top it off, the process was "outrageously expensive," as Jessica puts it.


> **"The biggest challenges we had before Photoroom on the photo editing side were costs at first. And then the second was the time frame that it took us to get those photos edited. Depending on the agency it would take a few days to 12 hours for the shortest turnaround."**
>
>
> **— Jessica Assouline, Partnership Operations Lead at Sorare**


With an entire card creation process waiting on the other side of photo editing, any delay rippled through the whole launch timeline. For a platform where player cards are the centerpiece of the product, missing a launch deadline wasn't an option.


## **A chance discovery leads to an AI solution**


Jessica discovered[Photoroom](https://www.photoroom.com/enterprise) through a friend who was using it for social media, a classic consumer use case that caught her attention.


When Sorare began exploring alternatives to its expensive agency setup, **Jessica's first instinct was to look at other agencies** offering lower prices. But after some evaluation, she decided to explore AI automation and its value for product photography.


**She reached out to the Photoroom team, and the conversations revealed a compelling opportunity:** the potential to save significant time and money through AI-powered automation.


Photoroom understood Sorare's needs from the beginning and, more importantly, had the technology to meet them.


> **"We were very happy with the onboarding experience at Photoroom. As soon as we signed our contract, we onboarded on a call to understand our needs and image standards. We have an account manager, and they have engineers that help us use the API."**
>
>
> **— Jessica Assouline, Partnership Operations Lead at Sorare**


### **Building a new workflow using Photoroom's API**


Sorare's editing process is now much simpler compared to the old agency workflow:


-


**Sorare's team notifies their freelance editor** when partner clubs send player headshots.


-


**The freelance editor uploads large batches** of photos to[the Photoroom API](https://www.photoroom.com/api) .


-


**Photoroom's AI automation handles**[background removal](https://www.photoroom.com/tools/background-remover) and reframing in minutes.


-


**The freelancer does final color retouching** externally to harmonize the images, which typically have inconsistent lighting, tones, and jersey colors from Sorare’s 300+ club partners.


-


**Sorare receives edited images** within a few hours instead of days.


The company’s image quality remains consistently high, which is a crucial factor on a platform where visuals are central to the user experience.


> **"We're beyond satisfied with the quality of Photoroom. One of the greatest powers of Photoroom is that it enables background editing in real time in just a few minutes. Our process is much more optimized now, and it has allowed us to save cost while maintaining the quality of our pictures."**
>
>
> **— Jessica Assouline, Partnership Operations Lead at Sorare**


Now when clubs send images just hours before a launch, the team can handle it without panic. The entire downstream card creation process moves forward quickly, ensuring launches happen on time.

**Scaled image production** using the API to process large image volumes.

> **— Jessica Assouline, Partnership Operations Lead at Sorare**
