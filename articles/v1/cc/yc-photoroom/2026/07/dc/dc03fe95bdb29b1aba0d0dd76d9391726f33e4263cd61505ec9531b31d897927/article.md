# Shopify for small sellers: the complete guide to building your product catalog

***Shopify gives small sellers the infrastructure to run an online store. Photoroom helps them create and manage the visuals that fill it.***


Shopify gives small sellers the technology needed to run an online store. It provides the storefront, product catalog, checkout, payments, inventory tools, and order-management system without requiring you to build your own e‑commerce platform.


What Shopify does not provide is the finished store customers see.


You still need to create your brand, attract shoppers, photograph your products, write your listings, and present your catalog consistently. Shopify gives you the fields and infrastructure; you decide what fills them.


This guide explains what Shopify handles, what remains your responsibility, and how tools such as Photoroom can help you prepare and publish product content without building a traditional photography and editing workflow.

## What is Shopify?


Shopify is a hosted commerce platform that lets businesses sell online and in person. Sellers can use it to create a storefront, add products, manage inventory, accept payments, process orders, and connect additional sales channels.


Because Shopify is hosted, you do not need to install the platform on your own servers or maintain the underlying commerce software yourself. The store is managed through Shopify’s online admin and mobile app.


Shopify is not the same as a[marketplace](https://www.photoroom.com/glossary/marketplace) like Amazon or Etsy. A marketplace places products from many sellers in front of an existing audience. A Shopify store is your own storefront, so you generally need to bring customers to it through channels such as search, social media, email, advertising, partnerships, or marketplaces.


In simple terms:


> **Shopify gives you the infrastructure to run a store. You still need to create the products, content, and customer experience that make it yours.**

A complete Shopify product may need a title, description, images, videos, price, inventory details, variants, specifications, search information, and image alt text.


Creating those assets can become a significant workload for a small seller. It involves more than taking one product photo: images need to be selected, edited, resized, adapted for variants, matched with accurate listing information, and uploaded to the correct product.


The same process then repeats across every SKU, collection, campaign, and sales channel.
