## 6 types of product photography for e‑commerce


Etsy reported that[90% of surveyed customers](https://www.etsy.com/seller-handbook/article/147451496051) find image quality to be very or extremely important to their purchase decisions. Some images are meant to inspire emotional reactions, while others present fine product details. By using these six product photography shot types, e‑commerce sellers can present customers with all the information they need to feel informed and ready to purchase your product.


### 1. Packshot


A packshot isolates your product against a plain, usually white, backdrop. It gives potential buyers an accurate, distraction-free representation of the item that includes the branding and packaging.


[Amazon](https://www.photoroom.com/ai-product-photography/amazon) requires this type of shot as the main image, and they're strict about this criteria. Because the packshot can gatekeep[marketplace imagery](https://www.photoroom.com/blog/visual-commerce) , sellers tend to budget the most retouching time here, making them expensive to produce without the help of an AI visual solution for e‑commerce like Photoroom. The[Background Remover](https://www.photoroom.com/tools/background-remover) and[White Background](https://www.photoroom.com/tools/white-background) tools solve the biggest problem of packshots by turning any background into the pure white canvas Amazon requires.


### 2. Detail


[Detail shots](https://www.photoroom.com/glossary/detail-image) zoom in on product features that are hard to judge from a distance, such as textures, stitching, and hardware. These images matter most when craftsmanship and intricate details are a major product differentiator, like[apparel](https://www.photoroom.com/ai-product-photography/clothing) and[home goods](https://www.photoroom.com/ai-product-photography/furniture) .


Detail shots are usually found in marketplace listings’ secondary image slots because they don’t typically catch a potential customer’s eye from the search page. Instead, they’re useful as a way to establish trust with buyers who are curious about your listing.


This shot type is one of the cheaper additions to a photo shoot, since the photographer doesn’t need a particular product set up to capture it. It’s also straightforward (and even less expensive) to zoom in on a very high resolution image and crop a detail shot, then sharpen any blurriness using tools like Photoroom’s[AI Photo Enhancer](https://www.photoroom.com/tools/photo-enhancer) .


### 3. Scale


Scale shots give shoppers something familiar to compare your product to, like a hand holding a mug. Specs and dimensions only go so far, and a photo that clarifies the item’s actual size answers potential buyers’ questions, like “where will this go?” and “will this fit my needs?”


Scale shots work great for really any products where the size is easy to misjudge from a flat product photo, like a keychain or work of art. Like detail shots, they’re a relatively quick addition to a shoot list, since it often means just adding one more prop or model to an existing setup.


### 4. Lifestyle photography


[Lifestyle shots](https://www.photoroom.com/glossary/lifestyle-image) put your product into a real scene to help shoppers picture it in their own life. For example, a purse might be shown on someone's shoulder mid-stride, or a facial cleanser on a bathroom countertop.


Lifestyle photography is one of the more expensive shot types to produce well. The shoot often calls for a stylist, props, and a specific location or background. Because of this, lifestyle shots have a longer production time than a tabletop studio session. The high cost is why many e‑commerce sellers reserve these shots for hero products and best sellers rather than their full catalog. (Or, you can[stage your image](https://www.photoroom.com/tools/product-staging) in a realistic background with Photoroom, all without getting up from your desk.)


### 5. Flat lay


[Flat lay shots](https://www.photoroom.com/blog/flat-lay-photography) are taken from directly above the product, which is styled on a flat surface below the camera. It's a popular format on social media and in fashion and beauty catalogs because it leaves room for creative layout choices and prop pairings.


Flat lays are easier to standardize than lifestyle shots. They generally use consistent lighting and composition across images, so it’s a practical option for catalogs that need a consistent look without reshooting the whole scene each time. However, these setups take a lot of time and manual adjustments to perfect. To save time and resources, you can use Photoroom’s[Flat Lay](https://www.photoroom.com/tools/flat-lay) tool to turn standard images of clothing, accessories, and soft goods into clean, structured shots.


### 6. Packaging


Packaging shots show the box, label, or container your product ships in. These images are important when the packaging itself carries information shoppers need, like ingredient lists on food containers and beauty products, or when the product inside is hard to photograph on its own, like supplements.


Packaging shots set customer expectations. A buyer can match what they see online to what arrives at their door, which cuts down on expectation-based returns.


## 3 specialized and high-intent e‑commerce product photo types


Some e‑commerce product photography is intended for a specific platform, like Instagram, or for a moment in the buying decision, like comparing details between similar products. These images take more production effort to pull off, but the results are often worth it, offering higher engagement and better conversions at the consideration stage.


Here are three specialized product shots to consider:


1.


**Infographics** visually represent information and data using text snippets and graphics. Sellers use these in marketplace listings’ secondary slots because they're a quick way to answer questions shoppers otherwise have to hunt for in the product description.


2.


**Social media shots** are vertical, visually bold images designed to match how people scroll on platforms like TikTok. They’re meant to grab attention in under a second, like a product spinning in an Instagram Reel, so the potential customer doesn’t swipe away before they see your product. You can use Photoroom tools like[Social Media Templates](https://www.photoroom.com/use-cases/social-media) ,[AI Instagram Post](https://help.photoroom.com/en/articles/11550049-create-an-ai-instagram-post-design) , and[Campaign Assets](https://www.photoroom.com/use-cases/marketing-campaigns) to quickly turn images into social selling assets.


3.


**360-degree shots** typically come from a collection of 24 to 36 product shots taken at small rotational intervals, linked together with interactive animation so a shopper can spin the product on screen as if they’re holding it. This interactive format works especially well if an item’s shape and dimension are important, like shoes, bags, or electronics.
