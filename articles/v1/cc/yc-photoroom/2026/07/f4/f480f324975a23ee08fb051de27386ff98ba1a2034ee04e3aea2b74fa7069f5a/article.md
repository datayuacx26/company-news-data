## What causes a blurry photo?


Blurry photos can happen for a variety of reasons. Knowing what’s behind the blur makes it easier to fix and, even better, prevent it from happening in the first play.


Here are five common causes of blurry images.


### 1. Camera or subject movement


Even the slightest shake or movement while taking a photo can cause motion blur, creating ghosting or a double image effect. This often happens in low light or when capturing fast-moving subjects.


###


2. High ISO levels


Using a high ISO in dark settings can introduce grain and reduce sharpness. While it helps brighten photos, too much noise can make images look blurry rather than crisp.


###


3. Shallow depth of field


A wide aperture (low f-stop) results in a narrow focus area, making only part of the image sharp while the rest is blurred. This is great for portraits but problematic if key details end up out of focus.


###


4. Missed focus


If the wrong part of the image is sharp while the subject is soft, the autofocus likely missed its target. Or, you might have manually set the wrong focus point with your camera.


This is common in portraits or action shots when the camera locks onto the background instead of the subject.


###


5. Poor lens quality


Some lenses struggle to maintain sharpness across the frame, especially at extreme focal lengths or high apertures. Dust or smudges on the lens can also soften an image.

Tips for preventing blurry photos


While tools to fix blurry images are aplenty, the best tactic is always to avoid them in the first place. With a few simple techniques, you can capture sharp, high-quality images every time.


Here are five tips:


-


**Prioritize image quality.** A good photo starts with the right settings. Use high-quality lenses, adjust your camera settings, and position your subject for the best clarity.


-


**Master manual focus.** Autofocus isn’t perfect, especially in low light. Switching to manual focus lets you control exactly where the sharpest details land.


-


**Use focus assist.** Many cameras highlight sharp areas in real-time, making it easier to ensure perfect focus, especially in tricky lighting or fast-moving scenes.


-


**Stabilize your shot.** A tripod or image stabilization prevents camera shake, while a faster shutter speed freezes moving subjects.


-


**Watch the environment.** Haze, humidity, and poor lighting can soften your photos. Adjust settings accordingly to maintain crisp details.


By fine-tuning your technique and using the right tools, you’ll reduce blur and capture stunning, clear images every time.


## Bonus tips for editing blurry images


If you took all the steps above and still end up with some blurry photos, don’t worry—we got you. In addition to the Photoroom tools outlined above, here are five tips for editing blurry images.


-


**Convert to black and white.** Removing color can reduce the appearance of blur, making textures and contrasts stand out more.


-


**Enhance texture with grain.** Adding a slight film grain effect can make an image look more intentional and mask minor blur.


-


**Stack multiple images.** If you have several similar shots, use image stacking to combine the sharpest parts of each.


-


**Use selective blur.** Intentionally blurring the background or edges can make the main subject appear sharper by contrast.


While these tricks won’t fix extreme blur, they can help improve clarity and make the most of a soft image.
