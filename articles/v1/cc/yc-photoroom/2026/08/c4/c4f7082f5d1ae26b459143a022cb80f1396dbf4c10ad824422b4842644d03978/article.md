## What is ChatGPT Shopping?


ChatGPT Shopping is not a separate marketplace that shoppers need to visit. ChatGPT Shopping is not a separate marketplace that shoppers need to visit. A shopper makes a request, and ChatGPT finds products that match their specifications. Depending on the query and product category, ChatGPT can show visual product results, compare options, ask follow-up questions, and link to merchants where shoppers can learn more or make a purchase.


Because this all happens inside a conversation, shoppers can refine their search the way they'd refine a request to a knowledgeable friend: adding constraints like budget, size, material, or delivery timing, ruling out unsuitable options, and asking for alternatives as they go. What starts as "a black pair of pants" can turn into "a black pair of wool trousers, size medium, with free shipping, under $100." These searches are highly specific and high-intent, and traffic from them[converts at twice the rate of traditional e‑commerce journeys](https://www.usegrowthos.com/blog/chatgpt-shopping-retail-strategy) .


OpenAI's own research puts shopping-related queries at roughly[2% of all ChatGPT activity, or an estimated 50 million requests a day](https://www.nber.org/papers/w34255) . For e‑commerce sellers, that means products are increasingly getting discovered and compared inside ChatGPT itself, not just on marketplaces or brand websites. That makes ChatGPT Shopping readiness a strong competitive advantage, and small businesses can start optimizing for it today.


## How does product discovery work inside ChatGPT?


OpenAI hasn’t shared a public formula that explains why one product might appear for a search query and another might not. But they do describe the process at a high level: ChatGPT considers the shopper's request and context, then uses product information to identify relevant options.


[Unlike Google](https://www.photoroom.com/blog/shopify-seo) , ChatGPT doesn't rely on crawling alone. It also pulls from structured feeds that approved merchants push directly to OpenAI, along with publicly available product information, metadata, and retail sources like reviews


When selecting a seller, ChatGPT may also consider factors such as availability, price, quality, and whether the merchant is the maker or primary seller. The exact mix can vary by product and shopping experience.


Together, these sources make up what ChatGPT actually sees of your catalog. That includes your product images, which don't require a click-through the way they would on a traditional search result. A photo is part of the answer itself, shown alongside price, specs, and reviews.

## How can e‑commerce sellers connect their product feeds to ChatGPT Shopping?


[OpenAI's Agentic Commerce Protocol (ACP)](https://www.photoroom.com/blog/agentic-commerce-protocols-ecommerce) lets any merchant share product data directly with ChatGPT. For a small business, this usually means asking whoever manages your website or a developer to set it up. It's a technical project, but not one that requires a large team or a big budget to get started.


A direct feed is useful because product catalogs change continuously. Prices, stock, delivery estimates, promotions, and variant availability can quickly become inaccurate. A structured, always-refreshed feed can help reduce the lag between your actual catalog and the product information ChatGPT receives.


This integration isn't a substitute for a[strong product page](https://www.photoroom.com/blog/what-makes-a-great-product-detail-page) . ChatGPT's shopping research can use both merchant-provided data and publicly available information. The feed and the page should reinforce one another, rather than becoming two competing versions of the product.


At a high level, here's what it takes to connect your product feed to ChatGPT:


1.


**Apply** :[Submitting a feed](https://chatgpt.com/merchants) is free and open to any merchant.


2.


**Format your catalog** : Organize your product information (titles, prices, stock, images, and shipping details) into[the structure OpenAI expects.](https://developers.openai.com/commerce/specs/file-upload/products)


3.


**Send a starter version** : Share an initial feed so OpenAI can confirm it's set up correctly.


4.


**Keep it updated** : Refresh your feed regularly so pricing, stock, and availability stay accurate as your catalog changes.


For most small businesses, the technical setup is a one-time lift. The bigger, ongoing job is making sure the information in your feed, product catalog, and website stays accurate and complete.

## How can e‑commerce sellers get their products discovered in ChatGPT Shopping?


ChatGPT can only recommend what it can clearly understand. That starts with a product catalog that's specific, complete, and consistent across every field and image. Here's where to focus.


### 1. Upload your entire product catalog


If a product is absent from your catalog (or unavailable to the relevant integration), ChatGPT can’t retrieve it from the source—even if it’s relevant.


### 2. Give each product a specific title


Lead with the actual product type and the attributes that distinguish the variant. A title like 'women's waterproof hiking jacket, navy, size medium' supports more matching criteria than a brand-led slogan or creative product name.


### 3. Record specific and relevant attributes


Record material, color, dimensions, capacity, compatibility, fit, condition, and other category-specific facts in the appropriate fields.


### 4. Write for conversational product constraints


Shoppers rarely search ChatGPT using one or two keywords. Instead of asking for a suitcase, dress, or coffee maker, they describe a situation: a durable carry-on for a budget airline, a formal dress that works in hot weather, or a compact espresso machine for a small kitchen.


Product data should cover the attributes that make those requests answerable. That doesn’t mean stuffing every imagined use case into the title. Keep core facts in structured fields, use the description to explain practical benefits, and add category-specific information that helps the shopper understand fit.


### 5. Make sure variants are accurate


Each size, color, bundle, or configuration should have the correct availability, price, identifier, landing page, and image. A product card is only useful when it sends the shopper to the version of the product they actually selected.


Images should align with variants, too: if a shopper asks for the sage green version of a linen throw pillow, ChatGPT shouldn't surface a product card showing the cream or terracotta version just because that was the only photo on file. Photoroom can help close that gap by[generating consistent images across every color and size variant](https://www.photoroom.com/use-cases/product-variant-image-generation) , so every version of a product has an accurate, high- quality image attached.


### 6. Keep prices, stock, shipping, and merchant information current


[OpenAI has identified availability and price](https://openai.com/index/buy-it-in-chatgpt/) as factors that can affect merchant ranking, along with shipping, returns, policies, and the maker’s identity. Outdated information can also lead to poor handoffs and undermine consumer trust. The product card should match what shoppers find when they continue to your store.


### 7. Highlight useful reviews and product evidence


ChatGPT shopping experiences can compare reviews and key features. Valid, visible reviews, FAQs, detailed specifications, and clear supporting content help shoppers evaluate the recommendation after it appears.


### 8. Give shoppers enough visual information to evaluate the product


Show the product clearly, keep the item recognizable, and avoid backgrounds or compositions that obscure important product details. Use additional views or images when they help communicate shape, material, texture, fit, features, or other details that matter to the purchase.


### 9. Maintain visual consistency across your catalog


Consistent[framing](https://www.photoroom.com/tools/ai-expand) ,[scale](https://www.photoroom.com/tools/image-resizer) ,[backgrounds](https://www.photoroom.com/tools/instant-backgrounds) , and image quality make products faster to scan when they appear beside competing options.

## How to audit your product catalog for ChatGPT Shopping


ChatGPT doesn’t provide sellers with a complete organic-shopping performance dashboard. The steps below help you identify the strengths and weaknesses in your catalog and measure their improvement over time.


-


Choose 10 to 20 priority products with strong margin, healthy stock, and complete catalog data.


-


Write three to five conversational-style prompts for each product using relevant constraints such as category, use case, budget, material, size, delivery, or compatibility. Test both broad discovery prompts (e.g., "a cozy throw blanket for a couch, under $60") and prompts that closely match the product (e.g., "a chunky knit oatmeal-colored throw blanket, 50x60 inches, machine washable").


-


Record whether the product appears, which image and details are shown, the merchant and URL represented, nearby competitors, and any inaccurate information.


-


Repeat the same prompt set on a fixed cadence.


This audit is most helpful when you repeat it regularly and look for patterns:


-


**If products are continuously absent** , inspect your catalog coverage and eligibility first


-


**If the products appear with incorrect details** , that’s your signal to compare the feed, product page, and variant data.


-


**If products appear but are difficult to distinguish from competing products** , revisit the product image, visible title, and overall presentation.
