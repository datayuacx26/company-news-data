# HungerRush POS Review: What Operators Should Know About the Pizza-First System

## Key takeaways


- HungerRush is a cloud POS built for pizza, quick service, and delivery-heavy operations. More than 18,000 restaurants run it, including Hungry Howie's and Jet's Pizza locations.
- The reason to pick it is the menu engine: half-and-half toppings, quarters, slices, and size-based pricing that generalist systems handle with workarounds.
- Pricing starts at $199 a month before hardware, processing, and add-ons, which lands above most of the mainstream category.
- This review is in two parts: the POS on its own merits, then OrderAI, its phone AI, compared straight with Certus. Pizza is still a phone business - 39% of pizza customers order by phone call, more than any other channel.


At Certus we work with hundreds of restaurants across every major POS, and phone AI is our whole business, so read this knowing where I stand. This review runs in two parts. Part one is the POS, judged on its own. Part two is the phone: HungerRush's OrderAI next to our Certus, compared straight. I will keep the facts and my opinions clearly separated in both.


## Part one: the POS


## What HungerRush is


HungerRush, formerly known as Revention, is a cloud-based restaurant POS aimed squarely at pizza, quick service, and delivery operations. More than 18,000 restaurants use it, and the marquee names are pizza chains: Hungry Howie's and Jet's Pizza among them. This is not a general-purpose till stretched to fit pizza. It is a pizza system first, and reviewers score it well for exactly that fit.


## The pizza test: why not just use Clover?


Here is the question that actually decides this purchase. Any modern POS can ring up a pepperoni pie. The test is Friday night's real orders: half pepperoni, half mushroom, extra cheese on one half only. A large that costs $2 more than the medium, but $3 more with stuffed crust. Slices at lunch, whole pies at dinner, quarters for the family deal.


A generalist like[Clover](https://www.certus-ai.com/blogs/clover-pos-review) or[Square](https://www.certus-ai.com/blogs/square-pos-review) can be configured to handle most of this, but you are bending the system: modifiers stacked on modifiers, and your staff pay for it in taps and workarounds during the rush. I have reviewed both, and they are excellent systems for the restaurants they were built for. Pizza is not that restaurant.


HungerRush builds its menu structure around exactly these orders. That is the single strongest reason it wins pizza shops. If your menu lives on customization - toppings by the half and quarter, size-dependent pricing, slice sales - the menu engine is the difference between a 40-second order and a 3-minute one, hundreds of times a week.


## What the platform covers


HungerRush sells itself as one system for the whole operation, and the platform covers a lot:


- **Online ordering** from the POS vendor itself, so web orders land in the same queue as everything else.
- **Delivery management** with driver tracking and dispatch, the logistics layer most mainstream POS systems leave to third parties.
- **Loyalty and marketing** built in, aimed at the repeat-order economics of pizza.
- **Reporting** across locations, which matters once you run more than one shop.


For a delivery-first operation, that bundle is the pitch: one vendor owns the till, the website, the drivers, and the phone.


## HungerRush pricing: what you will pay


Published pricing starts at $199 a month. That is the entry point, and it sits noticeably above the mainstream category, where software tiers start at $0 to $69 at[Toast](https://www.certus-ai.com/blogs/toast-pos-review) and $29.99 at[SkyTab](https://www.certus-ai.com/blogs/skytab-pos-review-what-operators-should-know-before-switching) . Independent review sites make the same point: itqlick's expert review scores HungerRush 7.5 out of 10 and calls it excellent for pizza and delivery but pricey against competitors.


As with every POS I have reviewed, the sticker is the start of the math. Hardware, payment processing, and module add-ons land on top, and quotes are custom. Pin the full number down in writing, ask specifically what OrderAI costs on top of the base system, and check the support terms while you are at it, because user reviews on support responsiveness are mixed.


## Where it struggles


Price is the first flag. The $199 floor lands before hardware, processing, and add-ons, and itqlick's verdict of excellent but pricey matches what custom quotes tend to look like in practice.


Support is the second. User reviews on responsiveness are mixed, which weighs heavily in a business where the till going down at 7pm on a Friday is an emergency, not a ticket.


The third is the shape of the platform itself. Everything is built for everything else, which is efficient right up until you want to take one piece somewhere else. Nothing in the stack, OrderAI included, comes with you to another POS. All-in-one platforms work this way, and it is fair design. Just price the exit before you sign.


## The POS verdict


If you run a pizza or delivery-first shop with real customization, HungerRush belongs at the top of your shortlist, ahead of the generalists. You are buying the menu engine and the delivery logistics, and both are genuinely built for you. If you are rate-sensitive, get the full quote in writing before you commit, because the gap between $199 and the real monthly number is where this decision gets made. And if you run counter service without delivery, start with the mainstream reviews linked above instead.


## Part two: the phone


## If pizza is your business, the phone is too


Pizza is still a phone business. CivicScience survey data from February 2026 shows more pizza customers order by phone call, 39%, than any other channel, ahead of the restaurant's own website at 34%. If you run the kind of shop HungerRush is built for, your phone rings all night, and you will end up evaluating a voice AI whether you planned to or not.


If you land on HungerRush, you have two real options: OrderAI, the phone AI that comes from your POS vendor, or Certus, which is purpose-built for restaurant phones. Here is that comparison, played straight.


## What OrderAI is


OrderAI is HungerRush's own ordering AI, and it comes in two flavors: Talk, which answers phone calls, and Text, which handles orders by SMS. The numbers are real. HungerRush's own page counts more than 10 million AI orders, and Jet's Pizza runs it across more than 350 locations with a 92% order success rate, meaning the AI completes roughly 9 in 10 orders it starts without a human stepping in. Unlimited simultaneous calls means the Friday rush cannot bury it.


Credit where due: HungerRush understood before most of the industry that the phone is a revenue channel, and built for it. If you are already on their POS, OrderAI is the default choice, and a reasonable one. It comes from your vendor, on one support line, built for your till. So the fair question is what to compare before you sign. Two things.


## The first thing: menu truth


Every voice AI sounds perfect in a demo, because demos use easy orders. The expensive failure is quieter: the AI agrees to things that do not exist. A half-and-half with a topping you dropped last month. A large at a price from two menus ago. The industry politely calls this hallucination. Your kitchen calls it a refund, a remake, and a one-star review, and on an allergen question it is far worse than that.


We built Certus so that mistake cannot happen. The agent is locked to the live menu in your POS. Every item, size, modifier, and price is validated against your till before the ticket fires. If it is not on your menu, Certus cannot sell it, no matter how nicely the caller asks.


OrderAI integrates with the full HungerRush menu, which is a real start. What its pages do not publish is the validation mechanism. So ask the question I would put to any voice AI vendor, us included: what stops the agent from selling something I do not serve, and what does it do when a caller asks an allergen question the menu data cannot answer? Listen for a specific answer.


## The second thing: where payment happens


A delivery order is not real until it is paid. HungerRush's own OrderAI page says checkout runs through secure mobile payments via a texted link. A texted link assumes a smartphone in the caller's hand and a caller willing to finish the job after the call ends. The regular ordering from a landline cannot click one at all.


Certus takes payment on the call itself, or by link, or at pickup, your choice per order type. An order paid on the call gets made, collected, and never ghosted. For a delivery-heavy shop, that difference compounds every single night.
