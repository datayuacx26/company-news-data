# Yelp Host vs Certus AI: An Honest Comparison for Restaurant Operators

## Key takeaways


- Yelp Host is real. It launched in October 2025, has handled more than 1 million calls, is growing 38% month over month, and added takeout ordering and OpenTable reservations in July 2026.
- The bill runs $249 to $599 a month depending on plan, and Spanish costs $99 a month extra on the Standard plan.
- Yelp Host's ordering is takeout for pickup only, with payment at pickup or by text link. Delivery orders are not part of the product.
- Certus takes delivery orders with the address and payment captured on the call, is locked to the live menu in your POS, books reservations through OpenTable, and includes English and Spanish in one flat monthly price.


Yelp built the site your guests use to judge you. Now it wants to answer your phone. Yelp Host launched with real momentum, a sales team that knows your name, and a price on the page. If you have a claimed Yelp listing, the pitch has probably already found you.


I am not a neutral referee here. Certus is my company, and Yelp is now a competitor, so I will not pretend you should take my word for anything. Here is the deal instead: every Yelp number in this piece comes from Yelp's own pages, linked at the bottom. Where I do not know something about their product, I will say so and tell you exactly what to ask them. Then judge both products the way you would judge a line cook: by how they handle the rush.


## Your phone has four jobs


Forget the AI for a minute and listen to what your phone actually does on a Friday.


1. **It sells food.** Takeout, delivery, the $180 office order. The money calls.
2. **It books tables.** Party of six, seven thirty, name for the table.
3. **It catches the big fish.** Catering and private events, the most expensive calls of the month and the easiest to lose to voicemail.
4. **It absorbs questions.** Parking, hours, gluten, dogs on the patio. Two minutes each, forty times a day.


Any phone AI that wants your money should be judged on all four jobs. So let us judge.


## What Yelp Host is


Yelp Host is Yelp's voice AI for restaurant phones. It launched in October 2025 alongside Yelp Receptionist, its sibling for service businesses, and by Yelp's own numbers it has handled more than 1 million calls while growing 38% month over month. In July 2026 it added takeout ordering for pickup with no commission, real-time OpenTable reservation sync, 16 new languages for 17 total, and custom voice options. It requires a claimed Yelp business listing, and its deeper reservation features run through Yelp Guest Manager.


Credit where due: this is a real product with real adoption, and Yelp's distribution means your neighbors will hear about it. Which is exactly why the job-by-job look matters.


## Job 1: selling food, and getting paid for it


Both products now take phone orders. That sentence hides the whole difference, so meet three callers every restaurant knows.


**The delivery caller.** Friday night, raining, and a family two miles away wants dinner brought to the door. Yelp Host cannot take this order. Its ordering, added in July 2026, is takeout for pickup only, per Yelp's own announcement. Certus takes the delivery order, captures the address, takes payment on the call, and fires the ticket into your POS like any other order. If delivery is real revenue for you, this one job may end the comparison by itself.


**The landline caller.** The regular who has ordered the same two pizzas every Friday for fifteen years and has never sent a text. Yelp Host's payment happens at pickup or through a text-message payment link. A payment link assumes a smartphone in the caller's hand. Certus takes the card on the call. No link, no app, no assumptions about the phone on the other end.


**The big order.** The $180 office lunch, called in Thursday for Friday noon. Every operator has cooked one of these and watched nobody show up to pay for it. An order paid on the call gets collected. An unpaid order is a hope with your food cost attached. Certus lets you require payment on the call for exactly these orders, your choice per order type.


The phone is still where this money lives. In a Mail Shark survey of 9,165 consumers, 52% said they still prefer to phone in their orders, ahead of the restaurant's own website at 49% and third-party apps at 30%. Those callers are your highest-margin channel, with no commission and no aggregator in the middle. The only question is whether the thing answering can finish the sale.


## The part nobody demos: menu truth


Every voice AI sounds perfect in a demo, because demos use easy orders. The expensive failure is quieter: the AI agrees to things that do not exist. A large gluten-free deep dish you never sold. A party platter at a price from two menus ago. Extra crab on a sandwich that has no crab. The industry politely calls this hallucination. Your kitchen calls it a refund, a remake, and a one-star review.


Now run the same failure on an allergen. A caller asks if the pad thai can be made without peanuts, and a guessing AI wants to be helpful. A wrong price costs you a refund. A wrong allergen answer can put a guest in the hospital. Guessing is the one thing a restaurant phone AI must never do.


We built Certus so the off-menu mistake cannot happen. The agent is locked to the live menu in your POS. Every item, size, modifier, and price gets validated against your till before the ticket fires. If it is not on your menu, Certus cannot sell it, no matter how nicely the caller asks.


I cannot tell you how Yelp Host handles this, because Yelp's pages do not say. So ask them the two questions that matter before you buy any voice AI, ours included: what stops your agent from selling something I do not serve, and what does it do when a caller asks an allergen question the menu data cannot answer? Then listen for a specific answer.


## Job 2: booking tables


A year ago this section would have decided the comparison in Yelp's favor. Yelp Host runs waitlists through Guest Manager and, since July, syncs reservations with OpenTable in real time. That is genuinely strong.


It is also no longer a gap. Certus books reservations through OpenTable too, and routes the large-party calls that need a human decision straight to your team. Call this job a draw, and notice what a draw does: it moves the decision back to the jobs where the products differ.


## Job 3: the big fish


A 60-person event inquiry is worth more than a week of takeout, and it almost always arrives by phone, mid-service, when nobody can talk. Certus captures the details, asks the follow-up questions, and hands the inquiry to your team as a triaged lead instead of a voicemail you find on Sunday.


Yelp's materials pitch Host on reservations and takeout. If events and catering are real revenue for you, ask where a catering inquiry lands in their flow, and what your team receives.


## Job 4: the question treadmill


Both products answer routine questions. Yelp's page says Host handles substitutions, specials, and dietary questions; Certus answers anything your menu and policies cover, in English or Spanish. This job is table stakes now, which is good news for everyone's front of house.


## Now the bill


Yelp Host Standard runs $249 to $399 a month. Premium runs $399 to $599. There is a pay-per-use option at 59 cents per conversation, and Guest Manager customers get a $199 rate. The trial is free for up to 30 days.


Then there is the line that made me read the pricing page twice: on the Standard plan, Spanish is a $99-a-month add-on. Yelp markets 17 languages, and the one your order ticket is most likely to need costs extra. In most American restaurant markets, Spanish is the second language of the kitchen, the counter, and the caller. We built Certus with English and Spanish included, automatic detection, no add-on, because a bilingual phone line is not a premium feature. It is the job.


Certus runs on flat monthly pricing. One number, no usage tiers, no language surcharges, on a 30-day rolling contract with a 30-day money-back guarantee. Our Trustpilot rating is 4.5 stars.
