## 1. Affil’s AI solution leads in contextual analysis


Performline primarily incorporates rules-based detection when implementing AI technology to its platform. Rules-based monitoring is limited in that ​​it doesn’t truly solve the users’ compliance burdens as it produces a slew of false positives.


The results are similar to Example 1 below, where the AI flags perfectly acceptable content just because it technically matches a rule, even when the actual risk is nonexistent or low.


### Example No. 1: Incorrect Product Detection


> *“When a customer tries to pay with an inactive card, the system will automatically* ***block*** *the attempt.”*


Let’s say we want the AI to flag all mentions of the fintech company Block. Rules-based monitoring solutions will flag this quote as a “product detection,” unable to distinguish whether the word “block” is referring to the verb (e.g. to *block* a transaction), the noun (e.g. a *block* of data), or the proper name of a fintech company, Block.


In order to actually save customers’ time and be easy to use, a compliance solution must properly utilize contextual analysis to prevent false positive outputs. Affil not only raises less false alarms but is also able to produce customized, concise explanations for easier remediation.


### Example No. 2: Incomplete Explanations


> *“This card earns 3x miles on dining purchases on up to $2,000 in quarterly spending.”*


Let’s say "this card" earns up to 3x miles on gas stations and dining purchases on up to $2,000 in combined purchases each month.


In order for the affiliate, content creator, or marketing team to remediate easily, the generated explanation must go beyond mere categorization of the compliance issue. Contextual AI analysis makes a meaningful difference here. Below is a sample Affil explanation:


Explanation: *This rewards description is inaccurate. The product information specifies that the 3x miles applies to combined gas-station AND dining purchases, and the cap applies monthly, not quarterly.*


This level of specificity prevents teams from wasting time deciphering vague or incomplete feedback.

## 3. Screenshots vs. snapshots: which is better for compliance documentation?


The importance of documentation and record keeping cannot be overstated in the realm of compliance. When thoroughly documented, the insight an audit trail provides can eliminate guesswork and simplify workflows across all teams involved in the marketing campaign. Some examples:


- Brand Marketing – Can verify whether an affiliate was using correct branding, promotional offers, and required disclosures at a given time.
- Brand Legal – Can utilize audit trails as evidence that the brand has been taking preventive measures to monitor affiliate claims and stop incorrect information from being presented.
- Brand Data Analyst – Gains insight into each marketing campaign by analyzing messaging copies, content layout, assets used, etc., identify high-performing patterns.
- Affiliates – Can build trust with brands by maintaining records of their marketing campaigns and compliance efforts.


As such, marketing compliance solutions typically include some kind of audit trail feature. Performline, for example, boasts record keeping as one of its capabilities. Performline’s limitations for this key feature lie in the apparatus they use for record keeping: screenshots.


### How are screenshots and snapshots different?


In essence:


- A screenshot captures what something looked like.
- A snapshot captures what something was.


A screenshot captures the visual representation of what was displayed on a marketing campaign at a given time. In other words, a screenshot gives you a photo of the book, but not the contents of the book itself. For audit trails, full insights are necessary.


A screenshot doesn’t provide info on:


- Metadata – You can’t see when the content was published, last modified, or taken down.
- Hidden UI elements – Modern web pages commonly use dropdowns, expandable sidebars, hovers, etc. to display information. Screenshots exclude these elements.
- Links/URLs – Screenshots show text links and CTA buttons (“Apply Now,” “Learn More”) but don’t show the actual URL, creating an information gap of whether the correct landing page was used or whether the link led to a correct product.


Snapshots, by contrast, capture the exhaustive digital footprint of a particular marketing campaign at a moment in time. They preserve not just the visual appearance, but the hidden elements, and functional details that compliance and marketing teams need.


For example, a snapshot can reveal that a product’s CTA actually linked to a different product, catching a bait-and-switch tactic that a screenshot couldn't detect. This level of documentation transforms audit trails from visual references into defensible evidence that can withstand regulatory review.


Affil leverages snapshot technology to capture complete audit trails, going beyond the limitations of basic screenshots.
