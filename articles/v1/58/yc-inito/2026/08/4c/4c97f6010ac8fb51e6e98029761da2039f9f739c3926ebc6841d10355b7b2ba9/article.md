# Understanding Your hCG Levels by Week in Pregnancy

Human chorionic gonadotropin, also known as hCG, is one of the earliest and most important hormones during pregnancy. hCG levels fluctuate by week in pregnancy.


hCG first appears in your blood about[6 to 8 days after fertilization](https://www.ovid.com/jnls/njcp/fulltext/10.4103/njcp.njcp_124_25~the-predictive-value-of-early--hcg-levels-after-embryo) . hCG levels in early pregnancy rise rapidly,[doubling](https://blog.inito.com/hcg-doubling-in-pregnancy/) every[48 hours](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10940029/) . By week 10, hCG peaks, then gradually declines and stabilizes for the next two trimesters. Once you give birth, your levels return to baseline after a few weeks.


Understanding your hCG levels by week in pregnancy can help you make sense of your pregnancy test results. Here’s what you should know about typical patterns of hCG.


## Key Takeaways


- hCG is the hormone that’s detected in pregnancy tests.
- hCG plays an essential role in maintaining early pregnancy.
- hCG peaks around 10 weeks, and then gradually declines before stabilizing for the rest of pregnancy.
- Blood tests are more sensitive than urine pregnancy tests done at home.
- Normal hCG levels vary widely between pregnancies.
- Trends over time, instead of a single value, tell more about your pregnancy.
- Contact your healthcare provider if you notice bleeding, abdominal pain, or dizziness after getting a positive pregnancy test.


## What Is hCG and Why Does It Matter?


hCG is known as the pregnancy hormone. Before[implantation](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8835849/#) , the embryo makes hCG. Later, the developing placenta takes over hCG production. hCG supports early pregnancy. It maintains the corpus luteum, a temporary structure in the ovaries that produces progesterone. Progesterone is another hormone essential for the uterine lining and[implantation](https://blog.inito.com/implantation/) .


Pregnancy tests detect hCG in your blood or urine. There are[different forms](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/1477-7827-10-24) of hCG. Each form is produced by certain cells at different stages of pregnancy.


hCG has two parts, known as alpha and beta subunits. The structure of hCG’s alpha subunits is the same as in other hormones, including:


- FSH (follicle-stimulating hormone)
- LH (luteinizing hormone)
- TSH (thyroid-stimulating hormone)


**hCG’s beta subunits are what set hCG apart from other hormones.** Beta hCG is more specific, so it’s what pregnancy tests are looking for.[Beta hCG tests](https://blog.inito.com/hcg-doubling-in-pregnancy/) can provide qualitative or quantitative measurement. A qualitative test, like a home urine test, checks if hCG is present. A quantitative test, like a blood test, measures the actual amount of hCG.

## How Do Pregnancy Tests Detect hCG?


Urine pregnancy tests use a method called antibody-based immunoassay. Antibodies are proteins that bind to the hCG in urine. Urine tests use antibodies to make a “[sandwich complex](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK532950/) .” This complex includes hCG antigen and antibodies. It forms a visible color line on the test strip, meaning the pregnancy test is positive. A[positive result](https://blog.inito.com/what-is-a-positive-beta-hcg-test/) shows two lines. A negative result (not pregnant) shows only the control line.


Most home urine tests detect hCG when levels are over[20 to 25 mIU/mL](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK532950/) . This is the usual amount of hCG at about[12 to 14 days past ovulation](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK532950/) .


**Know more:[How Long After Implantation Does hCG Rise?](https://blog.inito.com/how-long-after-implantation-does-hcg-rise/)**


[Blood pregnancy tests](https://www.invitra.com/en/can-pregnancy-tests-fail-false-positives-and-negatives/) are generally more sensitive than urine pregnancy tests. hCG appears in the blood first. It takes time for hCG to be filtered from the blood into the urine. A blood pregnancy test detects the amount or quantity of hCG.


**Know more:[When to Take a Pregnancy Test After Implantation?](https://blog.inito.com/pregnancy-test-after-implantation/)**

hCG blood levels rise from week to week in the first trimester. The general trend increases each week. But there’s a big range of “normal” values. If your hCG levels were very high during pregnancy or some placental tissue remains in the uterus, hCG may stay high for longer. If hCG levels don’t fall as expected, your healthcare provider may check for pregnancy tissue that was left behind or a rare condition called[gestational trophoblastic disease](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3752097/) .

## How Do hCG Levels Change Each Week?


Here’s what you should know about shifting hCG levels by week in pregnancy.


### hCG in Early Pregnancy: Weeks 3 to 4


hCG production begins shortly around embryo implantation, around the[3rd week](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/1477-7827-10-24) . During this early stage, hCG levels start to rise rapidly as the pregnancy develops.Levels are still low but increasing quickly. Changes over time matter more than one single measurement.


### Rapid Rise: hCG Levels in the 5th Week of Pregnancy


During the first few weeks of pregnancy, hCG rises[dramatically](https://www.researchgate.net/profile/Ellen-Lokkegaard/publication/334683505_Early_pregnancy_reference_intervals_29_serum_analytes_from_4_to_12_weeks'_gestation_in_naturally_conceived_and_uncomplicated_pregnancies_resulting_in_live_births/links/5fe99db245851553a0fb47cf/Early-pregnancy-reference-intervals-29-serum-analytes-from-4-to-12-weeks-gestation-in-naturally-conceived-and-uncomplicated-pregnancies-resulting-in-live-births.pdf) . One[study](https://www.researchgate.net/profile/Ellen-Lokkegaard/publication/334683505_Early_pregnancy_reference_intervals_29_serum_analytes_from_4_to_12_weeks'_gestation_in_naturally_conceived_and_uncomplicated_pregnancies_resulting_in_live_births/links/5fe99db245851553a0fb47cf/Early-pregnancy-reference-intervals-29-serum-analytes-from-4-to-12-weeks-gestation-in-naturally-conceived-and-uncomplicated-pregnancies-resulting-in-live-births.pdf) of 203 women with uncomplicated pregnancies found that serum hCG levels increased from 32,660 units/liter (U/L) (1 mIU/mL = 1 IU/L or U/L) at 4–6 weeks.Another[study](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/nursing-and-health-professions/chorionic-gonadotropin-beta-subunit) reported that hCG can go up[66% or more within 48 hours](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/nursing-and-health-professions/chorionic-gonadotropin-beta-subunit) in early viable pregnancies.


### hCG Levels in the 6th Week of Pregnancy and Beyond


hCG continues to increase rapidly during weeks 6 to 8. One[study](https://www.researchgate.net/profile/Ellen-Lokkegaard/publication/334683505_Early_pregnancy_reference_intervals_29_serum_analytes_from_4_to_12_weeks%27_gestation_in_naturally_conceived_and_uncomplicated_pregnancies_resulting_in_live_births/links/5fe99db245851553a0fb47cf/Early-pregnancy-reference-intervals-29-serum-analytes-from-4-to-12-weeks-gestation-in-naturally-conceived-and-uncomplicated-pregnancies-resulting-in-live-births.pdf) found that in some women hCG levels doubled every 48 to 72 hours. Another[study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31343977/) found median hCG levels rising to 15,571 U/L at 6-8 weeks, although individual values varied widely.


### Peak hCG Levels at Weeks 8 to 10


hCG reaches its highest levels around 9 to 10 weeks of pregnancy.[Studies](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10654-015-0039-0/tables/1) show that average hCG levels peak to 75,000 mIU/mL at 9 weeks and 74,655 mIU/mL at 10 weeks. In one[study](https://www.researchgate.net/profile/Ellen-Lokkegaard/publication/334683505_Early_pregnancy_reference_intervals_29_serum_analytes_from_4_to_12_weeks'_gestation_in_naturally_conceived_and_uncomplicated_pregnancies_resulting_in_live_births/links/5fe99db245851553a0fb47cf/Early-pregnancy-reference-intervals-29-serum-analytes-from-4-to-12-weeks-gestation-in-naturally-conceived-and-uncomplicated-pregnancies-resulting-in-live-births.pdf) , hCG concentrations got up to 200,000 mIU/mL by 8 to 10 weeks.


### hCG Decline Begins: Weeks 10 to 12


After reaching its peak, hCG levels in pregnancy gradually go back down.[Average](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10654-015-0039-0/tables/1) levels decrease from 74,655 mIU/mL at 10 weeks to 62,493 mIU/mL at 11 weeks. By 12 weeks, levels may be down to 56,004 mIU/mL. In other[studies](https://www.researchgate.net/profile/Ellen-Lokkegaard/publication/334683505_Early_pregnancy_reference_intervals_29_serum_analytes_from_4_to_12_weeks'_gestation_in_naturally_conceived_and_uncomplicated_pregnancies_resulting_in_live_births/links/5fe99db245851553a0fb47cf/Early-pregnancy-reference-intervals-29-serum-analytes-from-4-to-12-weeks-gestation-in-naturally-conceived-and-uncomplicated-pregnancies-resulting-in-live-births.pdf) , average levels decreased to 38,585 mIU/mL by 10 to 12 weeks. This decline is normal. The placenta develops and becomes fully functional around the end of the first trimester. Ultimately, the placenta takes over hCG hormone production.Once the placenta can independently support the pregnancy, the body no longer needs such high levels of hCG. After this transition, hCG levels typically stabilize at a lower range. hCG remains relatively steady for the rest of pregnancy.


### Continued Gradual Decrease in Weeks 12 to 16


hCG levels continue to decrease after the first trimester. Different studies show different values, but they all reflect the same trend.One[study](https://www.researchgate.net/profile/Ellen-Lokkegaard/publication/334683505_Early_pregnancy_reference_intervals_29_serum_analytes_from_4_to_12_weeks'_gestation_in_naturally_conceived_and_uncomplicated_pregnancies_resulting_in_live_births/links/5fe99db245851553a0fb47cf/Early-pregnancy-reference-intervals-29-serum-analytes-from-4-to-12-weeks-gestation-in-naturally-conceived-and-uncomplicated-pregnancies-resulting-in-live-births.pdf) showed hCG levels around 22,314 mIU/mL after 12 weeks. Another[study](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10654-015-0039-0/tables/1) found average hCG levels fall to:


- 52,367 mIU/mL at 13 weeks
- 47,267 mIU/mL at 14 weeks
- 37,303 mIU/mL at 15 weeks
- 29,614 mIU/mL at 16 weeks


More[research](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/1477-7827-10-24) states that at 15 weeks, hCG levels are at 30% of their peak. By 20 weeks, levels stabilize around 18% of peak levels. Then, they’re relatively steady until delivery.


## Why Does hCG Vary for Different Women?


Although hCG follows a general pattern, normal levels can vary significantly. That’s why it’s important to remember that a single hCG reading doesn’t provide enough information about pregnancy.
Healthcare providers usually also consider:


- [Gestational age](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10654-015-0039-0) (pregnancy in weeks)
- The rate of hCG increase over time
- Ultrasound findings
- Clinical symptoms


One study looked at about 8000 women and found that these[factors](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10654-015-0039-0) affected hCG levels:


- **The type of test or laboratory method used** : Different tests and laboratories may use different assays, leading to slight variations in hCG results.
- **Differences between individuals and pregnancies:** Normal hCG levels vary widely from one person and one pregnancy to another.
- **BMI:** Higher maternal BMI is associated with lower circulating hCG levels in early pregnancy.
- **Smoking:** Smoking has been linked to lower hCG concentrations during pregnancy in some studies.
- **Ethnicity:** Average hCG levels may differ slightly among different ethnic populations.
- **Number of previous pregnancies:** Previous pregnancies (parity) may influence hCG levels, although the effect is generally small.
- **Weight of the placenta:** Larger placentas tend to produce more hCG than smaller placentas.
- **Severe pregnancy nausea (hyperemesis gravidarum):** Women with hyperemesis gravidarum often have higher-than-average hCG levels.
- **Sex of the baby:** Pregnancies with a female fetus tend to have slightly higher hCG levels than those with male fetuses.


hCG results should be considered as one part of the bigger picture of your pregnancy journey. While reference ranges can be helpful, you shouldn’t compare your results to others.


## Why Are hCG Levels Important to Know?


hCG is an important marker of early pregnancy. It helps confirm pregnancy and monitor early development. Abnormally high or low hCG levels or slow-rising hCG have been linked with pregnancy complications, including pregnancy loss, preeclampsia, preterm delivery, and poor fetal growth.


High levels may happen with:


- A mistake in calculating how far along you are
- [Multiple pregnancy (twins or triplets)](https://blog.inito.com/twins-hcg-levels/)
- Molar pregnancy (a rare condition)


**Know more:[hCG Levels Twins: Key Indicators for Twin Pregnancy](https://blog.inito.com/twins-hcg-levels/)**


Low levels may happen with these conditions:


- [Ectopic pregnancy](https://blog.inito.com/ectopic-pregnancy-hcg-levels/)
- [Impending miscarriage](https://www.fertstert.org/article/S0015-0282(01)02552-3/fulltext)
- Anembryonic pregnancy


However, a low hCG value may still be normal if levels are rising appropriately. It’s always best to reach out to a healthcare provider to understand what’s happening before jumping to conclusions.


**Know more:[Understanding Ectopic Pregnancy hCG Levels](https://blog.inito.com/ectopic-pregnancy-hcg-levels/)**


## How Does hCG Change After InVitro Fertilization (IVF)?


In IVF pregnancies, early beta hCG levels can be helpful to know. One[study](https://www.ovid.com/jnls/njcp/fulltext/10.4103/njcp.njcp_124_25~the-predictive-value-of-early--hcg-levels-after-embryo) looked at 755 women undergoing IVF. Researchers found that higher beta hCG levels 12 and 14 days after embryo transfer was associated with better pregnancy outcomes. The same was true if hCG increased at a faster rate. The study mentioned these beta-hCG thresholds for higher chances of live birth:


- Day 12 after embryo transfer: hCG at or above 205.5 mIU/mL
- Day 14 after embryo transfer: hCG at or above 535 mIU/mL


However, beta hCG values alone can’t accurately predict pregnancy success. Your results should always be viewed alongside other findings.


## When Should You See a Doctor?


Most variations in hCG levels are normal. But it’s important to contact your healthcare provider if you have a positive pregnancy test and any of the following[symptoms](https://www.statpearls.com/point-of-care/29399) :


- Severe belly pain
- Heavy vaginal bleeding
- Dizziness or fainting


These signs may indicate an ectopic pregnancy or miscarriage.


You should also seek medical advice if your hCG levels aren’t rising as expected or are unusually high. If you receive conflicting pregnancy test results, like false positive or false negative results, see your doctor. You may need a repeat blood hCG test and an ultrasound to see what’s going on. Reach out to your doctor if you’re concerned. You can get peace of mind and help, if needed.


## FAQs


How long after implantation does hCG rise?


hCG starts rising around the time of implantation. The hormone can usually be detected in the blood about 6–8 days after fertilization. It then rises rapidly during early pregnancy, often doubling every 48–72 hours in viable pregnancies.


What's a good hCG level for early pregnancy?


There’s no single “good” hCG level for early pregnancy. Ideal hCG ranges vary, depending on how many weeks pregnant you are. By the third week of pregnancy, a normal blood hCG level can be anywhere between 5 and 72 mIU/mL. By week ten, the normal range is from 44,186 to 170,409 mIU/mL. As long as the trend is increasing at a good rate, the actual value isn’t always as important.


Can hCG levels fluctuate in early pregnancy?


Yes, but in a healthy early pregnancy, hCG should generally continue to rise. If hCG levels go down in early pregnancy, there might be a problem. Overall, hCG levels in early normal pregnancies should be rising consistently.


What hCG level is least likely to miscarry?


There’s no specific hCG target level. The most important thing is that hCG continues rising as expected. Sudden drops or leveling off too soon are a possible sign of miscarriage.


How to increase hCG level?


There’s nothing you can do to increase hCG yourself. hCG production depends on how the pregnancy is progressing. Following healthy habits and staying in touch with your healthcare team increase your chances of pregnancy success.


What are signs your hCG is rising?


The only way to know for sure if your hCG level is rising is through repeated blood hCG tests. Some women also feel symptoms, like nausea, breast tenderness, or fatigue, but these can vary. As a result, you can’t rely on your symptoms to predict if hCG is rising appropriately.


Can hCG levels indicate ectopic pregnancy?


hCG levels can’t tell if you have an ectopic pregnancy. hCG that rises more slowly than expected or doesn’t match ultrasound findings may raise suspicion and requires further evaluation.


Can hCG levels go down and not miscarry?


A decrease in hCG during early pregnancy isn’t normal. It should be seen as a serious issue that needs to be checked by your doctor. Falling hCG levels in the first trimester could mean a pregnancy loss or another complication. But diagnosis requires repeat hCG testing and an ultrasound. After the first trimester, hCG levels naturally decline as the placenta takes over hormone production. hCG naturally declines later as pregnancy progresses beyond the first trimester.


When does hCG stop doubling?


hCG usually stops doubling every 48 hours, as pregnancy progresses. During early pregnancy, hCG may double every 48-72 hours. The rate of increase gradually slows as total hCG levels rise. Around 9 to 10 weeks of pregnancy, hCG reaches its peak. Then, levels naturally decline and stabilize.
