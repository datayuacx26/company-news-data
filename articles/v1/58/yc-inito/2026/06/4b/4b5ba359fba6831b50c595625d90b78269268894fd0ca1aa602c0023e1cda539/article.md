# Traditional Chinese Medicine and Fertility​

Traditional Chinese medicine and fertility have been linked for centuries. But more people are turning to complementary therapies while trying to conceive. So, it’s worth asking: what does the evidence say?


Around[1 in 6 people](https://www.who.int/news/item/04-04-2023-1-in-6-people-globally-affected-by-infertility) around the world experience infertility at some point in their lives. And many of them are looking beyond conventional treatment for additional support.


A study of almost 33,000 infertility patients found that about[26% use some form of complementary or alternative medicine](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11589981/) while TTC.


TCM is one of the most widely used. This article breaks down what it is, how it may support fertility, and what you should know before trying it.


*Disclaimer: While some research suggests TCM may support reproductive health, it is not a substitute for medical care. Always consult your healthcare provider before trying acupuncture, herbal medicine, or other complementary therapies.*


## Key Takeaways


- TCM and fertility have a long history, and a growing body of research to support the connection.
- Traditional Chinese medicine believes in restoring balance in the body rather than treating isolated symptoms. It takes a holistic approach to reproductive health.
- Key TCM practices for fertility include: acupuncture, Chinese herbal medicine, diet, and stress reduction techniques.
- Acupuncture may help regulate hormones, improve blood flow to the reproductive organs, and support ovulation.
- Chinese herbal medicine uses tailored combinations of herbs to address individual patterns like yin deficiency, yang deficiency, or blood stasis.
- Some studies show that women who received acupuncture had higher pregnancy rates than those who did not.
- TCM works best as a complementary approach alongside evidence-based fertility care. It is not a replacement.
- Always work with a licensed traditional Chinese medicine practitioner. Discuss any herbal treatments with your doctor, especially if you’re on fertility medications.


## What is Traditional Chinese Medicine?


Traditional Chinese medicine (TCM) is a healthcare system that focuses on restoring balance within the body rather than targeting isolated symptoms.


The kidneys play a central role in TCM. They are considered the root of reproductive energy. And they store the vital essence that governs fertility and development.


**Traditional Chinese medicine is built on a few key principles:**


### Yin and Yang


In TCM, health depends on the balance between yin and yang.


Yin represents:


- Coolness
- Stillness
- Nourishment


Yang represents:


- Warmth
- Activity
- Movement


Fertility depends on the balance between these two forces. Yin deficiency and yang deficiency are two of the most common patterns treated in fertility patients.


### Qi and Blood


Qi is the body’s vital energy. And blood provides nourishment to tissues throughout the body.


Reproductive health in TCM depends on the smooth flow of both. When that flow is disrupted, it can affect:


- Menstrual cycle
- Ovulation
- Hormones
- Overall well-being


The kidneys are also seen as the foundation of both yin and yang. This makes kidney health *central* to reproductive function.


The main traditional Chinese medicine practices include:


- Acupuncture
- Chinese herbal medicine
- Dietary guidance
- Mind-body techniques like Tai Chi and meditation


## How Can Traditional Chinese Medicine Support Fertility?


In[TCM](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8000828/) , your fertility reflects your overall health and internal balance.


The goal is to strengthen the body from the inside out to create a supportive environment for conception. This is done through:


- Treatment
- Food
- Herbs
- Lifestyle


[Infertility](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6182526/) in TCM might happen when the flow of Qi and Blood becomes disrupted. Or, when yin and yang fall out of balance. This can affect the kidneys, liver, and the whole reproductive system.


**Traditional Chinese medicine is used to address[three main patterns](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6182526/) :**


#### Deficiency syndrome


Disrupts sexual and reproductive function in both men and women. Is often linked to kidney or blood deficiency.


#### Stagnancy syndrome


Restricts blood flow from circulating to the reproductive organs. It affects the menstrual cycle and fertility.


#### Heat syndrome


Connected to inflammation that can affect egg quality and gynecological health.


**Depending on the individual symptoms, a[TCM treatment plan](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/article-abstract/2781637) might focus on:**


- Nourishing yin
- Replenishing blood
- Supporting kidney yang
- Improving blood circulation
- Promoting the smooth flow of Qi


Traditional Chinese medicine for female fertility → targets the[kidneys and liver](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6182526/) as the root of reproductive health.


Traditional Chinese medicine for male infertility → focuses on[kidney yang deficiency and blood stasis](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6182526/) to improve sperm quality.


## What Are the Key TCM Practices for Fertility?


### Acupuncture


[Acupuncture](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6182526/) involves inserting tiny, sterile needles into specific points on the body. In traditional Chinese medicine, this helps regulate the body’s energy and support reproductive health.


Research suggests acupuncture may:


- Help balance hormones and hormone levels
- Improve blood circulation to the uterus and reproductive organs
- Reduce stress and support emotional well-being
- Regulate the menstrual cycle and support ovulation
- Improve sperm quality to support male fertility


A[review of 27 randomized controlled trials](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8865966/) involving nearly 8,000 women had a major finding. Those who received acupuncture had *higher pregnancy rates and live birth rates than those who did not* .


This makes acupuncture one of the most studied TCM practices for fertility.


Combined with Chinese herbal medicine, acupuncture might also help:


- Boost ovarian function
- Support follicle development
- Enhance blood circulation to the endometrium


All of these improve conditions for pregnancy.


Acupuncture may be helpful complementary therapy for women with fertility issues. But more large, well-designed studies are still needed.


### Chinese Herbal Medicine


[Chinese herbal medicine](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1071750/) (CHM) uses herbs tailored to each person’s symptoms and health history.


Traditional Chinese medicine uses[four main categories of herbs](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/bcpt.70214) to support fertility:


#### Kidney nourishing herbs


Examples of kidney-nourishing herbs:


- Cuscuta Semen (Tusizi) → known as the key herb for balancing yin and yang
- Epimedii Folium (Yinyanghuo)
- Lycii Fructus (Gouqizi)


These herbs are traditionally used to:


- Nourish the kidneys
- Support reproductive health
- Balance yin and yang


Some[studies](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12015-024-10679-6) suggest they may help regulate the HPO axis. Also, they contain antioxidant compounds that support reproductive function.


Kidney yin deficiency can manifest as symptoms like:


- Vaginal dryness
- Hot flashes
- Irregular cycles


And that kidney yin deficiency is one pattern these herbs commonly address.


#### Blood-Activating Herbs


Examples of blood-activating herbs:


- Angelicae sinensis Radix (Danggui)
- Diels (Apiaceae)
- Salvia miltiorrhizae (Danshen)


[Danggui](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0944711321001951) is used to:


- Nourish blood
- Improve blood circulation
- Regulate menstruation
- Alleviate pain


[Danshen](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13048-025-01727-x) may improve pelvic circulation and support uterine flow.


#### Liver-Qi Soothing Herbs


Examples of liver-Qi soothing herbs:


- *Bupleuri Radix (Chaihu)*
- *Cyperio Rhizoma (Xiangfu)*


These herbs are used to promote the smooth flow of Qi and support emotional well-being.


Some[studies](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1936523325000294?via%3Dihub) suggest they may help restore menstrual regularity, though evidence is limited.


#### Phlegm-Resolving Herbs


*Examples of phlegm-resolving herbs:*


- *Poria (Fuling)*


Traditional Chinese medicine uses these herbs to address “dampness.”


This dampness is linked to fluid retention and metabolic imbalances.


[Studies](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11950336/) suggest that Poria might support metabolic health and insulin sensitivity. This may be relevant for conditions like PCOS.


A[2015 study](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0965229914001915) found that women using Chinese herbal medicine had higher pregnancy rates and improved ovulation rates. (This is when compared with those receiving conventional treatment alone.)


***An important note: Many of the proposed benefits of these herbs come from traditional use, laboratory studies, animal studies, or small clinical studies. More high-quality human research is needed to understand their effects on fertility and reproductive outcomes.***


### Diet and Lifestyle


The TCM foundations of reproductive health are a balanced diet and moderate physical activity.


And food choices are tailored to support each person’s specific traditional Chinese medicine pattern.


Here’s what general dietary guidance often includes:


- **Root vegetables** like yams, beets, and carrots. These foods support blood and kidney health.
- **Sesame seeds and[healthy fats](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11019195/)** nourish yin and[support hormonal balance](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12156535/) .
- **Warm and cooked foods** are easier for digestion and better for maintaining blood flow.
- **Dark leafy vegetables and whole foods** support liver health,[reduce inflammation](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13023996/) , and enhance overall health.
- **Vegetables and legumes**[regulate blood sugar](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13023996/) and hormonal balance.
- **A balanced diet** that[avoids processed foods, added sugars, and alcohol](https://blog.inito.com/diet-and-fertility/) , which can[disrupt hormones and reduce fertility](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12195172/) .
- **Foods rich in antioxidants**[help protect eggs](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13023996/) and support the menstrual cycle.


**Know more:[11 Natural Ways and Diet Tips to Balance Your Hormones](https://blog.inito.com/how-to-balance-hormones-ti/)**


Many TCM dietary principles align with modern nutritional guidance. This is especially true when it comes to:


- Reducing inflammation
- Supporting liver and kidney health
- Maintaining a healthy weight to boost fertility outcomes


Eating the right foods for your specific pattern is a key part of any traditional Chinese medicine treatment plan.


### Stress Reduction and Mind-Body Practices


TCM incorporates practices like[Tai Chi](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8437614/) , meditation, and controlled breathing to reduce stress and support emotional health.


*(Tai Chi is a gentle and safe comprehensive exercise that combines the idea of wholeness in TCM and can help regulate balance in the body.)*


A[2024 study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11291950/) found that mind-body interventions can reduce stress, anxiety, and depression in people experiencing infertility. They can also improve overall quality of life during treatment.


It’s no secret that chronic stress can:


- Disrupt hormones
- Affect ovulation
- Reduce progesterone levels in the luteal phase


Practices that help regulate the nervous system aren’t just good for your well-being. They might also support the body’s fertility signals.


## Can TCM Improve IVF Success Rates?


Many people use traditional Chinese medicine during IVF (especially acupuncture). This is to manage stress and feel more supported during treatment.


In one[study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12041984/) of women with previous IVF complications who combined TCM with a subsequent IVF cycle:


- 51% achieved a clinical pregnancy
- 42% went on to have a life birth


These findings are encouraging, though the study was small and didn’t include a control group.


More studies are needed to confirm whether traditional Chinese medicine directly improves IVF outcomes.


## In Conclusion: Can Traditional Chinese Medicine Help With Fertility?


For women dealing with fertility issues, TCM offers a holistic approach that addresses the whole body.


Acupuncture, Chinese herbal medicine, food choices, and stress reduction may all play a supporting role in your fertility journey.


But traditional Chinese medicine works best alongside (not instead of) evidence-based fertility care


If you’re interested in TCM for fertility, work with a licensed practitioner who has experience with fertility patients. Talk to your doctor before starting any kind of herbs. And keep tracking your cycle so you can see what’s changing in your body. Also, so you can understand how your hormones, menstrual cycle, and ovulation respond to any changes you make.


Remember that fertility outcomes depend on a variety of factors. Not just one single therapy works for everyone.


## FAQs


What is the success rate of Chinese Medicine for fertility?


Results vary widely depending on the person, the condition being treated, and how traditional Chinese medicine is used.


One study found that women using Chinese herbal medicine had higher pregnancy rates than those on conventional treatment alone. But TCM works best as a complement to medical care, not as a standalone solution.


What helps you conceive faster naturally?


The most evidence-backed natural steps are:


- Maintaining a healthy weight
- Eating a nutrient-dense diet
- Reducing stress
- Tracking your cycle accurately using a fertility monitor like[Inito](https://www.inito.com/?srsltid=AfmBOor2XaRQ4JqmTh5KppUFlgpYm9xAcKfXvAf4DlpOFYV6GGVdBIfe)


What Chinese Medicine is good for fertility?


Acupuncture and Chinese herbal medicine are more studied traditional Chinese medicine approaches for fertility.


Acupuncture may help regulate hormones and improve blood flow to the reproductive organs.


Herbal formulas are tailored to individual patterns like yin deficiency, yang deficiency, or blood stasis.


What is the most successful treatment for infertility?


It depends on the cause.


Conventional treatments like IVF, IUI, and ovulation induction have the strongest evidence base.


TCM (especially acupuncture and herbal medicine) might offer additional support when used alongside medical care. But they should not replace a diagnosis and treatment plan from a fertility specialist.


What Chinese herbs help you get pregnant?


Commonly used herbs include:


- Cuscuta Semen (Tusizi) and Lycii Fructus (Gouqizi) to nourish kidney yin and support reproductive function
- Danggui to help activate blood and regulate the menstrual cycle
- Chaihu to promote the smooth flow of Qi


All herbal treatments should be prescribed by a licensed TCM practitioner and discussed with your doctor.


What Chinese tea is good for fertility?


Some TCM practitioners recommend teas made from herbs like red raspberry leaf, nettle, or goji berries to support general reproductive health.


However, there is limited clinical evidence linking any tea to improved fertility outcomes.


Always check with your healthcare provider before taking herbal teas, especially if you’re on fertility medications.


Which Chinese herbs increase fertility?


TCM herbs and typically prescribed in combinations based on your individual pattern, not as single herbs.


Some of the most commonly used herbs to support fertility are:


- Kidney nourishing herbs like Tusizi and Gouquizi
- Blood activating herbs like Danggui
- Qi-moving herbs like Chaihu


A qualified traditional Chinese medicine practitioner will tailor the formula to your specific needs.


What is Chinese medicine for male fertility?


TCM addresses male infertility by targeting patterns like kidney yang deficiency, blood stasis, and heat syndrome. All of these can affect sperm quality, count, and motility.


Acupuncture and herbal formulas containing kidney-tonifying and blood-activating herbs are the most commonly used approaches for male fertility in TCM.


What is the best herb for fertility in men?


Epimedii Folium (Yinyanghuo) and Cuscuta Semen (Tusizi) are among the most widely studied TCM herbs for male fertility.


Both are used to tonify the kidneys and support reproductive function. Some evidence suggests they might help regulate the HPO axis and reduce oxidative stress in sperm.


What is the Chinese herb like Viagra?


Epimedii Filum (commonly known as Horny Goat Weed of Yinyanghuo) is often referred to this way in TCM.


It’s been widely used in traditional Chinese medicine for centuries to support kidney yang, libido, and male sexual function.


Some research suggests its active compound, icariin, may improve blood circulation. But evidence in humans is still limited, and it is not a medical substitute for any prescribed treatment.
