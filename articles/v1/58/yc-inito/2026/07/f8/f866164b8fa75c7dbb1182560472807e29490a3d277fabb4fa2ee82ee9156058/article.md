# How Can You Use Inito in Your Follicular Phase?

Your follicular phase is where a new cycle (and a new chance to conceive) begins. While your body is busy preparing for ovulation, your hormone levels are constantly changing behind the scenes.


With Inito, you can follow these changes in real time to better understand your fertile window, recognize your unique patterns, and make more informed decisions throughout your cycle.


**Disclaimer:** Inito is intended for tracking your fertile window and confirming ovulation. It is not designed to diagnose medical conditions, detect or track pregnancy, or replace medical advice.


## Key Takeaways


- Follicular phase starts on the first day of your period and ends with ovulation.
- It’s the most variable part of the menstrual cycle since ovulation may not happen on the same day every month.
- Tracking estrogen during your follicular phase can help identify your right fertile days.
- Tracking beta-LH helps capture your unique LH pattern instead of relying only on fixed thresholds, and helps reduce cross-reactivity seen with some traditional ovulation tests.
- Comparing hormone trends across multiple cycles can help you learn what’s typical for your body and spot meaningful changes over time.
- Logging symptoms alongside your hormone data can help you identify patterns leading up to ovulation and better understand your cycle.
- You can also keep important fertility information, such as blood test results, follicle scans, medications, and IUI details, in one place in the Inito app.
- If you notice persistent changes like highly irregular cycles, repeated delayed ovulation, or no clear LH surge across multiple cycles, it’s a good idea to discuss them with your healthcare provider.


## What is the follicular phase?


The follicular phase is the first part of your menstrual cycle. **It begins on the first day of your bleed and goes on until you ovulate.**


During this time, follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH) encourages several follicles in your ovaries to grow. As one follicle becomes dominant, it produces increasing amounts of estrogen, preparing your body for egg release.


Unlike the[luteal phase](https://blog.inito.com/luteal-phase/) , which is relatively consistent at around 12-14 days, the follicular phase can vary significantly from cycle to cycle. In a study of more than 600,000 menstrual cycles, the[average follicular phase lasted 16.9 days](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41746-019-0152-7) , with many healthy cycles falling anywhere between 11.6 and 22.2 days.

## What hormones does Inito track during the follicular phase?


Inito measures 4 fertility hormones in urine during your follicular phase as well as throughout the cycle: FSH (follicle-stimulating hormone), LH (luteinizing hormone), E3G (estrogen), and PdG (urine metabolite of progesterone).


- **FSH (follicle stimulating hormone):** Supports follicle growth early in the cycle.
- **E3G (urine metabolite of estrogen):** Estrogen starts rising as follicles mature, peaking about 3-4 days before ovulation. Inito uses this rise from your baseline to identify your High Fertility days.
- **LH (luteinizing hormone):** Surges shortly before ovulation and signals that the egg is likely to be released within the next 24-36 hours. Inito identifies a rise in your LH from your baseline to give you Peak Fertility.
- **PdG (urine metabolite of progesterone):** PdG becomes more relevant after ovulation and helps confirm if ovulation has actually occurred.

## How does Inito detect High Fertility?


Inito detects High Fertility by identifying when your estrogen (E3G) levels begin to rise from your baseline. As your follicles mature, estrogen typically increases around 3-4 days before the LH surge. It’s one of the earliest signs that ovulation is approaching.


**Why this matters**


Sperm can typically survive in the female body for up to five days. That means **having intercourse before ovulation can improve your[chances of conception](https://blog.inito.com/chances-of-getting-pregnant-on-ovulation-day/) .** In fact,[having sex before ovulation](https://www.fertstert.org/article/S0015-0282(19)30432-7/fulltext) leads to the highest chances of pregnancy!

## Why should you track your LH surge?


LH rises shortly before ovulation and typically signals that **ovulation is likely within the next 24-36 hours.**


Identifying this surge helps narrow down your most fertile days and can improve timing for intercourse or insemination. However, LH patterns are often more complex than many people realize.
Research shows that not everyone experiences a single sharp surge. Approximately[48% of women have one distinct LH surge](https://www.fertstert.org/article/S0015-0282(12)02135-8/fulltext) , while others experience other types of LH surge patterns, like plateau surges, biphasic surges, or multiple LH rises[before ovulation](https://blog.inito.com/lh-surge-and-ovulation/) .


**Know more:[What Does a Normal LH Level Look Like?](https://blog.inito.com/normal-lh-level/)**


Additionally, LH concentrations during a surge vary widely between individuals. In one study, ovulation occurred across a broad range of LH values–from around[6.5 mIU/mL to over 100 mIU/mL](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/272175944_Development_of_the_first_urinary_reproductive_hormone_ranges_referenced_to_independently_determined_ovulation_day) –meaning there is no “universal” threshold that applies to everyone.


However, traditional ovulation predictor kits often rely on population averages and commonly detect alpha-LH, which may be more susceptible to cross-reactivity. Inito measures beta-LH and gives you results based on your unique baseline, providing a more personalized picture of your LH pattern and cycle trends.

## Can tracking multiple cycles help you understand your body better?


Yes. One of the biggest advantages of hormone tracking is recognizing trends over time rather than focusing on a single cycle.
Your follicular phase may naturally vary from cycle to cycle. You might notice:


- Earlier or later estrogen rises
- Longer or shorter follicular phases
- Multiple LH surges before ovulation
- Delayed ovulation in some cycles

## What if your follicular phase looks different from previous cycles?


Variation from one cycle to another is common, especially in the follicular phase. However, you may want to pay closer attention if you consistently notice:


- Very short or very long menstrual cycles (<21 days or >35 days)
- Highly irregular cycle lengths
- No LH surge across multiple cycles
- No ovulation confirmation over several cycles
- Symptoms that interfere with your daily activities


These patterns do not automatically indicate that something is wrong. However, if they persist (especially when you’re trying to conceive), they can provide useful information to discuss with your healthcare provider.

## FAQs


What hormones does Inito measure during the follicular phase?


Inito measures four key fertility hormones during the follicular phase as well as throughout your cycle: LH, estrogen, PdG, and FSH. During the follicular phase, estrogen, LH, and FSH are especially important for tracking follicle development and identifying your fertile window.


Can Inito predict ovulation before an LH surge?


Kind of. Inito tracks rising estrogen, which typically increases 3-4 days before your LH surge. This helps identify the start of your fertile window before ovulation is expected.


Why is my follicular phase longer this month?


Don’t worry, this is more common than you think! The follicular phase is the most variable part of the menstrual cycle, so it’s normal for its length to change from one cycle to the next. Things like stress, illness, travel, sleep, and natural biological variation can all affect when you ovulate.


Can I have more than one LH surge?


Yes! Not everyone has the classic single LH peak. You may see a double surge or even multiple LH surges before ovulation. That’s why it’s more helpful to look at your hormone trends rather than relying on a single positive ovulation test.


Should I use Inito during my period?


Yes, you can. Inito recommends testing from CD (cycle day) 6 onwards. This helps establish your baseline hormone levels early in the cycle, making it easier to detect meaningful hormone changes as your fertile window approaches. If your reference day test overlaps with your period, it’s totally okay to test! Just make sure you’re using a tampon or menstrual cup to avoid blood mixing with your urine and altering your results.


What else can I track in the Inito app?


Along with hormone testing, you can log symptoms, blood test results, follicle scans, fertility medications, trigger shots, insemination details, pregnancy tests, and other cycle information to keep everything in one place.
