# AI Alone is Not Enough for Reliable Title Search Automation and Insurable Title Decisioning at Scale: New Study from DataTrace

New analysis compares public-record-only AI search with title plant-supported search to evaluate completeness, accuracy and insurability.


---


July 29, 2026, AGOURA HILLS, Calif.


[DataTrace®](https://www.datatracetitle.com/) Information Services LLC, the nation’s largest provider of property and ownership data and title automation solutions, today released a new analysis “[AI Title Search Tested in the Real World: What Accuracy, Risk, and Readiness Really Look Like](https://www.datatracetitle.com/title-search-automation-reality-risk-and-responsibility-of-ai) ,” examining whether public-record-only AI can deliver the completeness, confidence and insurability required to support insurable title decisions at scale.


As interest in AI-powered title search continues to grow, the central question is no longer whether AI can retrieve public records. The question is whether AI operating on public records alone can consistently produce the complete, validated information required to support insurable title decisions. The analysis examines how public-record-only AI title search performs in practice and why trusted data infrastructure remains essential to supporting reliable, insurable title production. Together, the findings reinforce how often meaningful misses occur and why those misses matter for insurability.


“Our analysis demonstrates that AI performs best when it operates on a foundation of structured, validated title data, rather than fragmented county-level public records,” said Annette Cotton, chief data officer at DataTrace. “The speed of AI creates value, but only when paired with the confidence, completeness and accuracy needed for insurable title decisioning. The future isn't AI versus title professionals. It's AI powered by trusted title data and guided by experienced title experts. That's how the industry scales automation without sacrificing confidence or insurability.”


Among the paper’s key findings:


- In a review of 200 residential title files, public-record-only AI search missed at least one meaningful matter in 40.8% of searchable files. When compared with title search supported by DataTrace plant data, these misses highlighted significant gaps in accuracy.
- The most significant gaps occurred in high-risk categories, such as involuntary liens, which had an issue fail rate of more than 36%.
- An underwriting review also illustrates how seemingly small search omissions can translate into significant financial exposure when repeated across transaction volume. Using a simple illustrative extrapolation across annual existing-home sales, the analysis estimates approximately $489 billion in maximum potential liability and $148 billion in probable liability associated with missed title matters.
- Of the 200 residential title files evaluated, AI was unable to search 16 files (8%) because they lacked the title plant data or comparable normalized datasets needed to complete the search, highlighting an important limitation of public-record-only AI title search.


The report distinguishes between two separate, but related challenges in modern title production: search completeness and insurability. While AI may successfully retrieve many public records, producing an insurable title decision requires connecting related documents, validating ownership history, identifying missing information, and applying underwriting judgment. The search analysis shows how often meaningful misses occur, while the underwriting review illustrates why those misses matter.


Through its title plant network, standardized datasets, and cross-source validation, DataTrace transforms fragmented public records into structured, validated, property-centric datasets that support more accurate title production and responsible AI deployment at scale. The company currently delivers normalized title plant datasets across more than 1,850 U.S. jurisdictions and maintains a document library of 9 billion recorded document images to support title production and automation at scale.
