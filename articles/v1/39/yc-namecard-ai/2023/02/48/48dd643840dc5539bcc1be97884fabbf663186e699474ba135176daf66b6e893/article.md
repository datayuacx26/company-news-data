# The Rise of NFT Scams: Emily’s Story and Tips for Staying Safe

LEFT: Profile photo of Emily. Right: Diagram of scammer generated by DALL·E 2


The NFT market has become a hotbed for scams, with 90% of respondents in a recent survey by PrivacyHQ reporting falling victim to an NFT scam. This high level of uncertainty is concerning for a relatively new marketplace currently generating billions of dollars.


One victim of an NFT scam is[Emily](https://www.linkedin.com/in/emily-carrig/) , the founder of the NFT project True Stars. She fell for a crypto scam after connecting her wallet to a malicious website that promised her an airdropped NFT. Unfortunately, the scammer made off with her prized “World of Women” series and other valuable NFTs.


Thankfully, Emily didn’t give up hope. She shared her story online, which gained the crypto community's attention. Chainsight helped her track the flow of the fraudster’s illicit profits from selling the NFTs on Opensea. We discovered that the money went to Fixedfloat.


The tracing diagram.


With the crypto community's help, Emily could connect with a law enforcement officer willing to take her case. They successfully subpoenaed data from Fixedfloat, which allowed them to trace an IP address and the destination of the stolen ETH after it was swapped for Bitcoin. Although the BTC was sent to a mixer service called WasabiWallet, the investigation is ongoing.


Emily’s story is a powerful reminder that the crypto world can be risky, but there are steps you can take to protect yourself from scams. Here are some tips:


1. Don’t give in to FOMO (fear of missing out). Always double-check the website URL before connecting your wallet.
2. Create a new wallet for any new projects you’ve never engaged with.
3. Use a cold wallet, which gives you more time to think about connecting your wallet.
