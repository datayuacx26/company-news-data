# What Does Botox Cost Per Unit in August 2026?

## Per-Unit vs. Per-Area Pricing: What the Two Models Mean


Two pricing models dominate the Botox market, and understanding them matters before any dollar figure makes sense.


Per-unit pricing charges patients based on exactly how many units of neurotoxin were injected. If your forehead required 20 units at $14 per unit, you pay $280. The math is visible, and patients can compare quotes across providers on an apples-to-apples basis.


Per-area pricing bundles everything into a flat fee per treatment zone, such as "$350 for the forehead" regardless of how many units that actually takes. It obscures the real cost per unit: a patient using 15 units in that zone pays more per unit than one who needs 25, and neither one knows it.


Per-unit pricing is generally considered more transparent. It rewards providers who are precise with dosing and gives patients visibility into exactly what they're receiving. Per-area pricing can benefit injectors who use less product per patient but creates opacity that makes comparison shopping nearly impossible.


For[med spa owners](https://decodahealth.com/who-we-serve/med-spa) setting prices, the choice of model shapes revenue and patient trust alike.


## How Much Does Botox Cost Per Unit in 2026


National per-unit Botox pricing in 2026 runs roughly $10 to $20 for most markets, with the sweet spot landing between $11 and $18 per unit. Premium urban providers in cities like New York, Los Angeles, and Miami routinely charge $15 to $25 per unit or higher, reflecting real estate costs, injector credentials, and market demand.


A single-area treatment like the forehead alone (10 to 20 units) runs between $150 and $400 depending on location and provider. Treating the full upper face, typically forehead lines, glabellar lines, and crow's feet combined, uses 40 to 60 units, putting the[total treatment cost](https://facialesthetics.org/how-much-does-botox-cost/) between $480 and $1,200 at average rates. The American Society of Plastic Surgeons' most recently published statistics peg the average botulinum toxin treatment in the $435 to $530 range, aligning with a mid-market full upper-face session.


If a provider in a mid-tier market is quoting $25 per unit with no premium credentials to back it, ask why.

AbbVie's published wholesale acquisition cost is $656 for a 100-unit vial of Botox Cosmetic and $362 for a 50-unit vial, per the company's own[WAC disclosure](https://www.allerganaesthetics.com/content/dam/aa-corporate/us/en/disclosure/co/CO_WAC_BOTOX_COSMETIC_FEB_2024.pdf) . WAC is a list price, not what anyone actually pays. Most clinics land below it through authorized distributors, with real acquisition cost depending almost entirely on volume.

## What Drives Botox Price Variation Between Providers


Four variables account for most of the pricing spread you see between providers, and none of them are inherently suspicious on their own.


### Injector Credentials


A board-certified plastic surgeon with a decade of injection experience charges more than a newly licensed RN at a starter med spa. Both may deliver good results, but the skill floor, liability coverage, and overhead costs differ.


### Geography


A provider in a midsize Midwestern city paying $4,500 per month in rent can price at $11 per unit and still run profitably. That same math falls apart in Manhattan or Beverly Hills.


### Patient Anatomy


Muscle mass determines how many units a treatment requires. Men typically need 20 to 30% more units than women in the same area because male facial muscles are denser, so a man and a woman visiting the same clinic for forehead treatment may pay very different totals at identical per-unit rates.


### Clinic Type


Plastic surgery offices and dermatology practices carry higher overhead and often charge accordingly. Med spas operate leaner and tend to price more competitively, though injector backgrounds vary widely across that category.


## How Long Does Botox Last by Treatment Area


Duration varies by area because muscles with more repetitive movement break down the neurotoxin faster.


Forehead lines typically hold for 3 to 4 months. Crow's feet, which sit around the constantly moving eye area, often fade sooner, closer to[7 to 12 weeks](https://www.medicalnewstoday.com/articles/drugs-does-botox-wear-off) . Glabellar lines (the "11s") land in the middle at 3 to 5 months. Masseter treatments last longest, often 4 to 6 months, because jaw muscles see less constant micro-movement than expressive facial zones.


Most patients return two to three times per year. At $480 to $1,200 per upper-face session, that is roughly $1,000 to $3,600 annually. Repeated treatments over time can extend intervals slightly, as muscles gradually lose some mass and require less frequent dosing to stay relaxed.


## Neurotoxin Brand Alternatives: Dysport, Xeomin, and Daxxify


Four FDA-approved neurotoxins compete in the same market: Botox, Dysport, Xeomin, and Daxxify. Each carries a distinct dosing profile, wholesale cost, and duration story.


Units are not interchangeable across brands, which is where per-unit price comparisons mislead. Dysport requires roughly 2.5 to 3 units to match the effect of 1 Botox unit. A clinic quoting Dysport at $5 per unit is not offering a bargain. At that conversion rate, a 20-unit Botox treatment would need 50 to 60 Dysport units, making the true cost comparable or higher.


Xeomin is sometimes called "naked Botox" because it contains no complexing proteins, which may reduce antibody buildup over repeated treatments. Both Dysport and Xeomin wholesale roughly 10 to 20% below Botox, giving practices more margin flexibility without requiring higher per-session volume.


Daxxify is the newest entrant and the most clinically distinct. Phase 3 trial data showed many patients holding effect near six months, longer than the 3 to 4 month average for the other three brands. It commands a higher wholesale price, but for practices targeting patients who want fewer annual visits, the duration story is a real differentiator in consultation.


Offering two or three neurotoxins lets you serve different price sensitivities and treatment goals without discounting your anchor product.


## Red Flags When Botox Pricing Looks Too Low


Unusually low per-unit pricing deserves scrutiny. The explanation is usually mundane, not sinister.


The most common culprit is dilution. Botox comes as a powder that providers reconstitute with saline before injection. More saline per vial means lower concentration per unit, so the same declared unit count delivers less effect. A patient paying $8 per unit may be getting half the product they expect.


A related pattern is underdosing, where providers advertise a low rate but cap treatments at an artificially low unit count, requiring follow-up appointments to achieve the desired result. The annual cost frequently matches or exceeds a higher-priced provider once visit frequency is factored in.


Grey-market product is rare but real. Sourcing outside authorized distributors voids manufacturer safety guarantees and puts patients at legal and medical risk. If a provider cannot name their distributor, that warrants a direct question.


The "starting from" tactic is also worth calling out. Advertising "$10 per unit" is technically accurate, but a full upper-face treatment at 40 to 60 units still costs $400 to $600. Patients comparing quotes should always ask for an estimated unit count, not merely a rate.


## How Med Spas Should Structure Botox Pricing in 2026


Pricing Botox sustainably starts with understanding what the product actually costs you, not the number on your distributor invoice.


Raw wholesale product cost runs roughly $7 to $12 per unit depending on your volume tier. That number looks comfortable against a $15 rate, until you account for everything else: waste from partial vials, syringes and needles, injector labor, room time, front desk overhead, and no-show appointments that ate a slot without generating revenue. Practices that price against product cost alone often find themselves selling Botox near breakeven, and without solid[medspa billing](https://decodahealth.com/products/billing) , even correct pricing leaks revenue.


A workable 2026 framework for most independent med spas:


- $15 to $17 per unit for standard treatments requiring 20 or more units
- $18 to $20 per unit for small single-area sessions under 20 units, where waste and fixed per-visit overhead hit harder relative to revenue generated


The minimum-order premium reflects real economics. A 6-unit lip flip uses a freshly opened vial, occupies injector time, and generates a fraction of the revenue of a full upper-face treatment.


Competing on the lowest per-unit rate in your market tends to attract patients whose primary loyalty is price, and those patients leave when a competitor posts a Groupon. Practices that compete on injector skill, outcomes, and patient experience retain patients longer and build higher lifetime value per chart.
