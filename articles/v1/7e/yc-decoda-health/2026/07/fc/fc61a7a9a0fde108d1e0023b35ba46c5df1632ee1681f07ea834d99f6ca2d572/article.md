## Do Appointment Reminders Reduce No-Shows? What the Research Shows


There's no shortage of opinions on no-shows — but a handful of findings have real studies behind them, and they're worth knowing:


**Reminders work.** A[meta-analysis of digital notifications](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5093388/) found automated reminders significantly improve attendance, and one[randomized controlled trial](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5227159/) cut no-shows from 38.1% to 23.5% with text reminders alone.


**The wording does surprising work.**[Two randomized trials](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0137306) found that stating the appointment's cost in the reminder beats a generic one — "Your $400 Botox appointment is tomorrow at 2 PM" outperforms "You have an appointment tomorrow." A[large A/B study](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7310733/) dropped no-shows from 21.1% to 14.2% by changing nothing but the message.


**Money changes behavior.** Deposits and card-on-file turn a soft promise into a small contract. Same principle as the reminder studies: when the slot has visible value, clients protect it.


## How to Reduce No-Shows at Your Med Spa: Match the Policy to the Appointment


The mistake is one policy for everything. Instead, scale the friction with the ticket:


- **Under ~$150 (consults, touch-ups):** reminders only — email at 3 days, text at 24 hours with one-tap confirm, text at 2 hours. A deposit on a $99 service scares off exactly the new clients you're trying to win.
- **$150–$400 (injectables, facials):** reminders + card-on-file at booking. Nothing gets charged upfront — but a stored card quietly reduces casual no-shows on its own, and it turns your cancellation fee from theoretical into enforceable.
- **$400+ (laser packages, full-face filler):** reminders + a 20–50% deposit, credited toward treatment. At this ticket size, a no-show is too expensive to leave to goodwill — and serious clients don't blink at it.
- **Repeat offenders:** prepay to book. Two no-shows is a pattern, not bad luck.

Here's a cancellation policy you can copy and paste and integrate into your booking flow:


> We hold your appointment time exclusively for you. Cancellations or reschedules within 24 hours, and missed appointments, are subject to a fee of \[50% of the booked service / $X\], charged to the card on file. Deposits are applied to your treatment and are refundable with more than 24 hours' notice. By booking, you agree to this policy.


Three rules make it stick: clients agree to it at booking (not in a buried FAQ), it's restated in the reminder ("reply R to reschedule free until tomorrow at 2 PM"), and it's enforced every time. Waive it for a genuine emergency — but say you're waiving it, so the exception proves the rule exists.

## Frequently Asked Questions


What is a good no-show rate for a med spa?


Under 5% is strong; 5–10% is typical with reminders and card-on-file in place. Above 10% points to a systems gap — usually no financial commitment at booking, or reminders that don't ask for confirmation.


Should a med spa charge a no-show fee?


Yes, for anything beyond low-cost consults — research shows that even communicating the cost of a missed appointment changes behavior. The fee matters less as revenue than as a signal. 50% of the booked service or a flat $50–$100 are the common structures.


How much should a med spa deposit be?


20–50% of the service price for appointments over ~$400, always credited toward treatment. Below that, card-on-file with a clear cancellation fee gets the same commitment with less booking friction.


Can a client dispute a no-show fee?


They can attempt a chargeback — which is why documentation matters. Capture explicit agreement at booking, restate the policy in the reminder, and keep confirmation logs. That paper trail wins the overwhelming majority of disputes.


Do text reminders actually reduce no-shows?


Yes — it's one of the better-documented findings in healthcare operations. Randomized trials show double-digit reductions, and results improve when the message states the appointment's value and asks for active confirmation.
