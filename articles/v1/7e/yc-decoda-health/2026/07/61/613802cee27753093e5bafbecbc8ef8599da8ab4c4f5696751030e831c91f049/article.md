## What Is Medspa EHR Software?


Medspa EHR software is a practice management and clinical documentation system built for medical aesthetic practices. It covers the full practice layer: scheduling, patient health records, consents, clinical notes, billing, inventory, and memberships.


What separates it from a general hospital EHR or a salon booking tool is the hybrid nature of the work. A medspa functions as both a medical practice and a consumer service business. On the clinical side, you need[HIPAA-compliant charting](https://www.hipaajournal.com/hipaa-compliance-for-medical-spas/) , consent tracking, and detailed treatment records. On the consumer side, you need[automated online booking](https://decodahealth.com/products/scheduling) , package sales, and membership billing. General-purpose tools tend to handle one half reasonably well and leave the other half to spreadsheets, workarounds, or a second piece of software. Purpose-built medspa EHR software is designed to carry both without compromising either.

## Boulevard


Boulevard is a scheduling and business management tool built primarily for salons and spas, with some med spas using it for appointment booking and staff management. It offers a polished client-facing booking experience and solid point-of-sale functionality.


However, Boulevard lacks the clinical depth that med spas require. It offers limited charting through its Professional app, but there is no dedicated consent form management and no full treatment documentation. Practices that need HIPAA-compliant clinical records will need to bolt on separate tools, adding cost and workflow friction.


For med spas weighing Boulevard, the core question is whether a booking-first feature set is enough, or whether the missing consent management and treatment documentation will create problems down the line.

## Frequently Asked Questions


How do I choose between Decoda Health, Zenoti, and AestheticsPro for my medspa?


Start with your practice size and priorities: AestheticsPro works well for smaller independent medspas focused on aesthetics-specific charting at a lower price point, Zenoti suits multi-location group practices that need centralized reporting across clinics, and Decoda Health is built for practices that want clinical documentation and business management — scheduling, memberships, and patient communications — in one system without bolting on extra tools.


Is Boulevard a viable medspa EHR, or does it work better as a salon booking tool?


Boulevard is strong on client-facing booking and point-of-sale, but it lacks built-in charting, consent management, and HIPAA-compliant clinical documentation — which means med spas using it will need separate tools for the clinical side of their practice, adding both cost and friction.


When should a medspa owner consider switching from AestheticsPro or Zenoti to a different system?


If your practice is running multiple disconnected tools to cover what one system should handle, if support response times are costing your team hours each week, or if membership and package billing requires manual workarounds, those are signals that your current software is holding your growth back rather than supporting it.


What is the difference between a general EHR and a purpose-built medspa EHR like Aesthetic Record or Decoda Health?


General EHRs were designed for primary care and hospital workflows, so features like before-and-after photo management, aesthetic consent tracking, package sales, and membership billing are either missing or require expensive add-ons. Purpose-built medspa EHRs like Aesthetic Record and Decoda Health are built around elective-care workflows from the start, though they vary in how well they handle the business management side versus the clinical side.


Which medspa software works best for a solo injector versus a multi-location practice?


Solo providers or single-location practices tend to get more value from lighter-weight options like Vagaro or Jane App at lower monthly costs, while practices with two or more locations — or those running memberships, packages, and structured follow-up programs — need a system like Zenoti or Decoda Health that can handle the added complexity without requiring a separate CRM or billing tool alongside it.


What happens to my patient data if I decide to leave my medspa EHR platform?


Your patient data should always be yours to take with you — look for a vendor that provides a full data export at no cost and in a portable format before you sign any contract. Some legacy systems charge fees to release data or make export difficult, which is worth asking about directly during the sales process.


Can I run a medspa membership program in Vagaro or do I need more specialized software?


Vagaro supports basic recurring billing but lacks the membership configuration depth that most medspas need — things like per-service rollover credits, tiered discounts, and multi-frequency billing cycles. Practices running structured membership programs typically find they need a purpose-built medspa EHR like AestheticsPro, Zenoti, or Decoda Health to avoid manual workarounds.


How does an AI scribe work inside a medspa EHR, and is it different from a regular note template?


An AI scribe listens to the provider-patient conversation in real time and drafts a clinical note automatically, rather than requiring the provider to fill out a form after the visit. The difference from a standard SOAP template is that the scribe captures context from the actual appointment, which saves charting time and creates a more detailed, accurate record without extra clicks.


What should I look for in medspa booking software if I want patients to be able to self-schedule online?


Look for a system that supports omnichannel booking — website, Instagram, and text — along with the ability to set provider-specific scheduling links and deposit collection at the time of booking. The best med spa booking software also handles room and equipment availability automatically so double-booking is not something your front desk has to manage manually.


Does medspa EMR software need to be HIPAA-compliant, and how do I verify that?


Yes — any software storing or transmitting patient health information at a medical spa must comply with HIPAA. To verify compliance, ask the vendor directly for a signed Business Associate Agreement (BAA), and confirm that data is encrypted both in transit and at rest; a reputable med spa EMR vendor will provide this documentation without hesitation.


Zenoti vs. Boulevard for a two-location medspa — which one actually handles memberships better?


Zenoti handles memberships more fully for medical aesthetic practices, including recurring billing, loyalty tiers, and multi-location tracking. Boulevard's membership functionality is more limited for clinical settings and lacks the ability to link memberships directly to checkout in the way most med spas need, which often pushes owners to look at Zenoti or a purpose-built medspa EHR instead.


How does telehealth work inside a medspa practice management system, and can I keep virtual visits off the patient self-booking page?


Most modern medspa EHR platforms let you configure virtual visit types within the same scheduling and charting environment used for in-person care, so clinical notes and patient records stay in one place. You can typically restrict telehealth appointments to staff-initiated booking only, keeping them off the public scheduling page for services like post-treatment follow-ups that you want to control directly.


Can my medspa use GoHighLevel for marketing funnels alongside a dedicated EMR, or do I have to pick one?


Most practices run GoHighLevel for top-of-funnel marketing and lead nurturing while keeping a dedicated medspa EHR for clinical documentation, scheduling, and billing — the two tools serve different purposes and are not mutually exclusive. The key question to ask your EMR vendor is whether their built-in patient communication and booking tools can reduce how much you depend on external CRM tools over time, since fewer disconnected systems means less data fragmentation.


What is AestheticsPro pricing per month, and are there hidden add-on costs I should know about?


AestheticsPro's published pricing starts around $149 to $199 per month depending on tier, but users frequently report that features like text reminders, additional provider seats, and support upgrades carry separate fees that push the real monthly cost higher. Before signing, ask for a line-item quote that includes all the features your practice will actually use day one.


How do I get fast support after going live on a new medspa software platform?


The best medspa software companies provide a dedicated support channel — ideally direct Slack or text access to your account team — rather than a generic ticketing system where responses take days. When evaluating vendors, ask specifically what the support path looks like after onboarding ends, and whether urgent issues go to the same team that set you up or get routed to a general help queue.
