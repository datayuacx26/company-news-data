# Wisconsin Sales Tax: Rates, Rules & Filing Guide

Wisconsin Sales Tax Quick Facts


**Wisconsin sales tax rate** 5%


**Max combined state and local sales tax rate** 7.9%


**Economic nexus threshold** $100,000 in gross sales in the previous or current calendar year


**What sales count toward the threshold** Gross sales, including marketplace sales


**Tax authority**[Wisconsin Department of Revenue](https://www.revenue.wi.gov/)


**Tax portal**[My Tax Account](https://tap.revenue.wi.gov/mta/_/)


**SaaS taxable?** No


**Administration difficulty** 2/5


Wisconsin charges a 5% state sales tax rate on most retail sales. Most counties add a 0.5% county tax on top of that, bringing the standard combined rate to 5.5%. The City of Milwaukee stacks its own 2% city tax on top of Milwaukee County's 0.9% rate, pushing the combined total to 7.9%, the highest rate anywhere in the state.


SaaS and remotely accessed software are not taxable in Wisconsin. That's good news if your business sells subscription software to Wisconsin customers, but it's just one piece of the compliance picture.


This guide walks through Wisconsin sales tax rules from what's taxable, when you’re on the hook, how to register, how to calculate the right rate, and how to file and pay on time.


## Wisconsin Sales Tax at a Glance


The state rate is 5%. Most Wisconsin counties add a 0.5% county sales tax, so most areas of the state land at 5.5%. A handful of counties, including Waukesha and Winnebago, don't impose any county tax at all, so purchases there are taxed at the base 5% state sales tax rate.


Milwaukee County is the exception to the exception. Its county rate jumped from 0.5% to 0.9% effective January 1, 2024, and the City of Milwaukee layered a new 2% city sales tax on top that same day. That combination brings the City of Milwaukee to a 7.9% combined rate, making it the most expensive place in Wisconsin when it comes to sales tax.


Two more counties joined the local tax list in 2025. Manitowoc County added its 0.5% county tax effective January 1, 2025, and Racine County followed on April 1, 2025. Both counties cited a need for local revenue to fund infrastructure and public services without raising property tax bills.


Wisconsin uses destination-based sourcing. That means the sales tax rate you charge is based on your customer's delivery address, not your business location. If you ship a product to a customer in Kenosha versus Green Bay, you may owe different local sales tax rates depending on the county and city involved, even though both fall under the same statewide 5% base rate.


Here's how the combined rate breaks down by location type:


Location State County City Combined


**City of Milwaukee** 5% 0.9% 2% 7.9%


**Most WI counties** 5% 0.5% 0% 5.5%


**County with no local tax (e.g. Waukesha)** 5% 0% 0% 5%


Because rates vary by address, don't rely on zip code averages. Use the[Wisconsin DOR's Sales Tax Rate Lookup tool](https://www.revenue.wi.gov/Pages/Apps/strb.aspx) to confirm the exact combined rate for any delivery address before you calculate tax on a sale.


Wisconsin sales and use tax is administered entirely through[My Tax Account](https://tap.revenue.wi.gov/mta/_/) , the DOR's online portal for registration, filing, and payment.


## Taxable Categories: What Wisconsin Sales Tax Applies To


Wisconsin taxes tangible personal property and a specific, limited list of services. If something isn't on that list, it generally isn't taxed.


### Tangible Personal Property (TPP)


Physical goods sold at retail in Wisconsin are taxable at the combined state and local rate. This is the default rule, and most of what your business sells as a physical product falls under it.


Grocery food meant for home consumption is exempt from Wisconsin sales tax. Prepared or heated food sold for immediate consumption, however, is taxable, along with candy, dietary supplements, and soft drinks. Prescription drugs and certain medical devices are also exempt from sales tax.


Motor vehicles follow their own set of rules. County tax on motor vehicles, boats, snowmobiles, trailers, and similar items is generally based on the buyer's county of registration rather than the standard destination-sourcing rule used for other goods.


### SaaS and Digital Products


[SaaS](https://www.getsphere.com/blog/are-subscriptions-taxable) is not taxable in Wisconsin. Wisconsin’s tax code generally requires a transfer of tangible personal property for software to be taxable, and SaaS doesn’t fall into that categorization. This applies whether your customer is an individual or a business.


However, some digital goods are taxable. Wisconsin taxes specified digital goods like downloaded music, digital video, and e-books. The difference is that the digital products are downloaded and thus constitute a transfer of property.


On that note, prewritten software delivered on physical media such as a disc or flash drive is also considered taxable. Also be cautious about bundled transactions. If your[SaaS](https://www.getsphere.com/blog/sales-tax-software) transaction also includes something physical, such as a paper instruction manual, then it may become taxable. Mixed transactions involving taxable and nontaxable products should be reviewed individually because Wisconsin's bundled transaction rules may apply.


### Taxable Services


Wisconsin does tax some services, but only those that are specifically named in state law. Anything not on that list is exempt by default.


Taxable services in Wisconsin include:


- Landscaping
- Cable TV
- Parking
- Laundry & dry cleaning
- Hotel and lodging stays
- Certain repair and installation work tied to taxable goods


If your business provides a service that isn’t precisely enumerated in Wisconsin’s tax law, then you likely don’t have to charge sales tax for it. It is a good idea to periodically track Wisconsin tax law, since they can add new services from time to time to their list of taxable items.


## Monitoring: When Wisconsin Sales Tax Applies to You


When you have[sales tax nexus](https://www.getsphere.com/blog/nexus-tax-software) in Wisconsin, you’re required to register for a sales tax permit and begin charging sales tax to customers.


### Economic Nexus Threshold


A remote seller triggers economic nexus in Wisconsin once gross sales into the state hit $100,000 in the current or previous calendar year. Wisconsin eliminated its 200-transaction threshold effective February 20, 2021, so transaction count no longer matters here. Only the revenue figure does.


Every sale counts toward that $100,000 threshold, including exempt sales. Both taxable and exempt sales of goods, and both taxable and exempt sales of services, get added together when the DOR calculates whether you've crossed the line. Marketplace sales also count toward this threshold even if the marketplace itself handles the sales tax on those transactions.

### Physical Nexus


Physical presence in Wisconsin creates nexus immediately, regardless of how much revenue you generate in the state. Having employees, an office, inventory, owned real property, or business representatives physically present in Wisconsin all establish physical nexus.


### Marketplace Facilitators


If you sell through a marketplace facilitator like Walmart or Amazon, that platform is generally required to collect and remit Wisconsin sales tax on your behalf. That said, if you also make direct sales outside the marketplace, you may still have a separate filing obligation for those transactions.


## Registration: How to Register for Wisconsin Sales Tax


### How to Register


Register online for a Wisconsin sales tax permit through[My Tax Account](http://tap.revenue.wi.gov/mta) . The application is entirely online and processing is typically immediate once you submit your Business Tax Registration (BTR) application. Some businesses receive their permit number the same day.


### Registration Fees


There is an initial $20 fee for the Business Tax Registration. This fee stays the same no matter how many locations within Wisconsin you are registering. This fee covers a two-year period, and after that a $10 renewal fee applies.


The $20 fee is applicable to most businesses, though certain nonprofits are exempt.

## Calculation: Wisconsin Sales Tax Rates


### State and Local Rate


When collecting sales tax in Wisconsin, you’ll charge the 5% state rate plus the applicable county and local rates. Most counties levy a .5% rate, except Milwaukee County, which is .9%. Further, right now only Milwaukee levies an additional 2% rate, making its 7.9% rate the highest in the state.


Always confirm sales tax rates using[Wisconsin’s Sales Tax Lookup Tool](https://www.revenue.wi.gov/Pages/Apps/strb.aspx) or automate calculation every time with a service like[Sphere](https://www.getsphere.com/) .


### What the Rate Applies To


The combined rate applies to taxable tangible personal property, specified digital goods, and the enumerated list of taxable services. Wisconsin uses destination-based sourcing for out-of-state sellers, meaning the rate is set by the customer's delivery address.


## Filing: How and When to File Wisconsin Sales Tax Returns


### Filing via My Tax Account


File Wisconsin sales tax electronically through My Tax Account using Form ST-12. Electronic filing is mandatory for all sales and use tax registrants.
Note that you must file a return every period, even if you have no sales tax to remit. This is called a “zero return.” If you don’t file, even if you have nothing to turn in, you’re still subject to fines and penalties.


### Filing Deadlines


The sales tax due date for standard filers is the last day of the month following the reporting period. A monthly filer’s January return is due February 28. A quarterly filer’s Q1 return is due April 30. Annual filers owe their sales tax return by January 31 the following year.


There is one exception, and that’s early monthly filers. These high-volume filers, who have sales tax remittances of $3,601 or more per quarter, are required to pay sales tax more frequently, and their returns are due on the 20th of the month after the filing period.


### Filing Frequency Thresholds


Wisconsin assigns your filing frequency based on your estimated tax liability. The more tax you owe, the more often you’ll be required to file and remit. The state doesn’t publish the exact calculations it uses, but does reevaluate your sales tax remitted yearly and may assign you a new filing frequency should your tax liability change significantly. Always be on the lookout for communications from the state to ensure you’re still filing and paying at the right frequency.


### Late Filing Penalties


Filing late in Wisconsin triggers a flat $20 late filing fee. On top of that, a penalty of 5% of the total tax due applies for each month or partial month the return is late, capped at 25%. Interest on unpaid tax accrues at 1.5% per month, or 18% annually, from the original due date. That rate drops to 1% per month if you've received an approved filing extension.
