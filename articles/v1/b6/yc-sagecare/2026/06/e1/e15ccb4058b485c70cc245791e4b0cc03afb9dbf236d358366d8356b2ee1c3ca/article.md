## What Is a Non-Medical Home Care Intake Form?


A non-medical home care intake form is the document an agency uses to collect essential information about a prospective client before services begin. It typically captures the client's personal information, health background (non-clinical), care needs, living situation, emergency contacts, insurance or payment details, and any special instructions for caregivers.


This form is the starting point of your intake pipeline. It shapes how you staff a case, what questions you ask in the assessment, and how quickly you can move from inquiry to signed agreement. A thorough form means fewer follow-up calls and faster care plan development. A vague or incomplete one means delays, missed details, and frustrated families.

## What a Good Intake Form Should Include


Before we get to the templates, here is what a complete non-medical home care intake form should cover. Use this as a checklist when reviewing your current forms.


### **Client Information**


-


Full legal name, date of birth, and preferred name


-


Home address and directions if needed


-


Primary phone number and preferred contact method


-


Email address (important for follow-up and document delivery)


### **Emergency Contacts and Authorized Decision Makers**


-


Name, relationship, and phone number for at least two contacts


-


Who is authorized to make care decisions on the client's behalf


-


Any legal documents in place (healthcare proxy, POA)


### **Living Situation**


-


Type of home (house, apartment, assisted living)


-


Who else lives in the home


-


Pet information (for caregiver matching)


-


Home entry instructions


### **Care Needs and ADL Assessment**


-


Activities of daily living the client needs help with (bathing, dressing, meal prep, mobility, medication reminders)


-


Frequency and hours of care needed


-


Any behavioral considerations or preferences


### **Medical Background (Non-Clinical)**


-


Primary diagnoses relevant to care coordination (e.g., Alzheimer's, Parkinson's, post-surgical recovery)


-


Physician name and contact


-


Current medications list (for reference, not clinical management)


-


Known allergies


### **Insurance and Payment**


-


Payer source (private pay, long-term care insurance, Medicaid waiver, VA benefits)


-


Insurance policy number and contact


-


Billing contact if different from emergency contact


### **Caregiver Preferences**


-


Gender preference


-


Language preferences


-


Any specific experience required

## Why Paper Forms Are Costing You Clients


Free templates are useful. But if you are printing these forms, handing them to families at assessments, and then manually transcribing the information into your records, you are likely losing clients you do not even know you are losing.


Here is where it breaks down.


### **The Response Time Problem**


Speed is one of the most underrated factors in home care conversion. Research on consumer behavior consistently shows that the first agency to follow up after an inquiry is significantly more likely to win the client. According to data from a[survey of 500-plus home care consumers](https://www.sagecare.ai/blog/home-care-business/what-home-care-consumers-actually-want-insights-from-500-survey-responses) , families often contact two to three agencies simultaneously. Whoever responds first and follows up consistently tends to win the placement.


When your intake process relies on paper forms and manual data entry, there is almost always a gap between when you collect the information and when you act on it. An assessment happens on Tuesday afternoon. The form sits on a desk. You transcribe it on Wednesday morning. The follow-up email goes out Wednesday afternoon. By then, the family may already have signed with another agency.


### **Manual Entry Introduces Errors**


Every time information is transcribed from handwritten notes into a software system, there is a risk of error. Wrong phone number. Misspelled name. Missing care hours. These errors create downstream problems with scheduling, billing, and caregiver matching. For small agencies, one bad data point can mean a failed service visit and a lost client.


### **Paper Forms Do Not Fit Your Sales Process**


A good intake pipeline does not just collect data. It qualifies leads, tracks follow-up activity, and surfaces which prospects are close to converting. Paper forms cannot do any of that. If you are trying to manage more than a handful of active prospects, paper creates blind spots. You forget to follow up. You lose track of where a family is in the decision process. You find out they went elsewhere after the fact.


This is the point where many agencies start looking at moving from manual tracking tools into a more structured[CRM system built for home care operations](https://www.sagecare.ai/blog/home-care-services-ai-tech/spreadsheets-to-crm-systemizing-home-care-operations) . The shift is not just about software. It is about having a process that reliably moves every inquiry toward a decision.
