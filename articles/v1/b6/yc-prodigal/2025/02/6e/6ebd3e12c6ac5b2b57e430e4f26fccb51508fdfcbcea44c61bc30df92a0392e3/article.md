# If your IVR could talk, it would apologize for lost payments -

Interactive Voice Response (IVR) systems have been a cornerstone of debt collection operations for decades. Initially implemented to streamline collections, reduce operational costs, and improve the efficiency of debtor communications, these systems are now showing their age in today's AI-driven landscape.


In today's digital-first world, where consumers expect instant, frictionless payments through apps like Venmo and Apple Pay, traditional IVRs create frustrating barriers to collection.


With average wait times of 79 seconds just to reach an agent, debtors are forced to navigate lengthy menu trees and press multiple keys - a process that often results in abandoned calls and missed payment opportunities.


This misalignment with modern consumer expectations doesn't just create operational inefficiencies - it directly impacts the bottom line by deterring willing payers from completing transactions. When consumers are used to speaking naturally to Alexa and Siri, forcing them through robotic IVR menus feels archaic and frustrating.


## **Lost revenue from payment processing limitations**


Here's a painful truth: every day, collection agencies are losing thousands in potential payments simply because their IVRs can't process them. Think about it - a debtor calls, ready to pay, only to abandon the call after struggling with confusing menu options or being unable to set up a payment plan that fits their needs. That's real money walking out the door.


During critical payment periods like tax season, when debtors have cash in hand, outdated IVRs become a bottleneck that literally costs you money. While your competitors are automatically processing payments 24/7 with virtual agents, your IVR is:


- Forcing willing payers to wait until business hours to make payments
- Rejecting partial payments when debtors can't pay in full
- Unable to quickly modify existing payment plans when debtors can pay more
- Missing opportunities to capture recurring payment commitments
- Failing to process digital wallet payments that modern consumers prefer


[Virtual agents](https://prodigaltech.com/proagent) turn these limitations into opportunities by enabling:


- Instant payment processing through any method, anytime
- Flexible payment arrangements based on real-time ability to pay
- Smart negotiation of payment terms to maximize collection potential
- Automated payment plan adjustments to capture more revenue
- Digital-first payment options that match consumer preferences


The math is simple: Every payment your IVR fails to process is money lost. In an industry where every percentage point in collection rates matters, can you afford to let willing payers slip away because your technology won't let them pay?

## **Ineffective authentication leading to wasted workforce hours**


In today's collection environment, agents are spending precious time on tasks that should be automated. According to the 2024 US Contact Center Decision-Makers' Guide, an average of 26 seconds per call is wasted on manual verification processes - time that could be spent on actual collection activities. This inefficiency translates to a staggering $19.8 billion annually in agent-handled verification costs.


Despite FDCPA requirements making proper identification mandatory, collection agencies are still relying on human agents to perform these repetitive verification tasks. This misallocation of workforce hours is particularly painful in an industry where agent productivity directly impacts recovery rates.


While agents spend their time asking for basic identifying information, they could instead be negotiating payments or handling complex disputes that truly require human intervention.


## **The path forward**


The true cost of IVRs extends beyond immediate financial impact. In an era where consumer experience drives success, outdated IVR systems aren't just inefficient - they're actively harmful to your bottom line.


Every robotic interaction, every lengthy menu tree, and every frustrated debtor represents lost revenue and potential compliance risks, especially when handling sensitive issues like medical debt or time-barred debt disclosures.
