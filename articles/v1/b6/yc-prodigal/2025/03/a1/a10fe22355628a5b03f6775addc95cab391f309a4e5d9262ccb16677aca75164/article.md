# Are virtual agents ready to handle collections on their own? -

The collections industry is at a technological crossroads. AI voice agents promise game-changing automation, but do they truly deliver?


By analyzing hundreds of millions of collections calls, we see where virtual agents excel and where they fall short. Understanding their real impact helps define the best use cases and where human expertise remains essential.


## **Where Virtual Agents fall short**


### **Recognizing conversational nuances**


Collections calls are high-stress, with customers often signaling intent indirectly through tone, hesitation, or deflection. Current voice agents struggle with understanding these nuances - missing sarcasm, intonation, modulation, or implicit meaning. While they process basic intent, they lack the context awareness needed to accurately gauge willingness or ability to pay, limiting their effectiveness in sensitive interactions.


### **Payment processing**


Unlike e-commerce, where payment links drive high completion rates, collections require real-time payment processing. Virtual agents struggle here, often redirecting customers to portals or collecting card details for later use—both prone to drop-offs. Lacking deep integration, they can’t validate cards or ensure seamless on-call payments, making this a major industry bottleneck.


### **Managing multiple accounts**


Virtual agents can identify and list different accounts belonging to a customer, but they falter when handling multiple payment methods. For instance, if a customer wants to use different cards for different accounts, a human collector usually needs to step in.


### **Consumer expectations**


While industries like retail and hospitality have embraced automation, collections remains in early adoption. Customers expect human assistance, so virtual agents must deliver low-latency, highly accurate responses while understanding conversational nuances. Any lag or misinterpretation amplifies frustration, making it critical for AI to minimize friction and maintain a seamless, human-like experience.


## **Where Virtual Agents stand out**


Despite these limitations, virtual agents provide undeniable value in the right scenarios:


### **Compliance adherence**


They adhere strictly to regulatory requirements, avoiding deviations or emotionally driven responses that could lead to compliance risks.


### **24/7 availability**


Unlike human collectors, virtual agents operate around the clock, giving customers the flexibility to engage on their own time.


### **Cost efficiency**


By handling routine interactions, virtual agents reduce operational costs and allow human collectors to focus on complex cases.


### **Consistent quality**


They follow up consistently without experiencing burnout, ensuring no opportunity is missed.


## **Where Virtual Agents shine**


Rather than replacing human collectors, virtual agents work best in specific roles that enhance collection efforts. Here’s where they provide the most impact:


### **After-hours coverage and overflow management**


- Capturing consumer intent and scheduling callbacks outside business hours
- Handling basic account inquiries over weekends
- Managing peak-hour overflow, reducing hold times and call abandonment rates


### **Third-party and late-stage collections**


- Verifying right-party contact before transferring calls to human agents
- Presenting pre-approved settlement terms for aged debt


### **Early-stage collections**


- Reaching out after a first missed payment when resolution likelihood is highest
- Sending automated courtesy payment reminders
- Following up on payment commitments made to live agents


## **Limitations in payment processing and arrangements**


**‍** Most virtual agents cannot process payments directly due to compliance restrictions. Instead, they:


- Direct customers to secure payment portals
- Schedule callbacks for agent-assisted transactions
- Send payment links (though secure, unique link generation is often limited)


Payment plan setup also presents challenges, as virtual agents struggle with real-time calculations and negotiation. They often rely on predefined templates or human intervention.


## ‍ **Finding the right balance**


**‍** The key to successful virtual agent implementation is understanding their best use cases. A tiered approach works best:


- Virtual agents handle initial outreach, basic inquiries, and simple payments
- AI systems flag cases requiring human intervention
- Human collectors take on complex negotiations and emotional conversations
- Virtual agents maintain routine follow-ups and communications


## **Looking ahead**


**‍** Virtual agents in collections follow a trajectory similar to automation in other industries. While still evolving, their capabilities are improving rapidly. Success depends on:


- Setting realistic expectations about their role
- Choosing the right use cases for implementation
- Maintaining a balance between automation and human expertise
- Continuously refining performance based on real-world outcomes


## **Conclusion**


**‍** Virtual agents may not be ready to collect entirely on their own, but they can play a significant role in enhancing efficiency and compliance. The real question isn’t whether they should replace human collectors—it’s how to best integrate them into collection strategies to maximize effectiveness while maintaining customer engagement.A hybrid approach, where virtual agents handle routine tasks and human collectors focus on high-value cases, offers the best path forward. This balance ensures a more efficient, compliant, and customer-friendly collections process while reducing operational costs.
