## **The Challenge After Placement**


An ATS like Bullhorn gets you to the offer letter. But once a candidate says “yes,” a new set of challenges begins — and this is where many staffing companies stumble.


**Delayed Onboarding:** Workers get lost in paperwork or sit idle for days while admins chase signatures and I-9s. For high-volume roles, this delay means candidates simply move on to another company with faster start times.


**Broken Payroll** : Generic systems struggle with multiple pay rates, overtime rules, or per diems. Workers expect to be paid correctly and on time, but instead face missed checks or confusing pay stubs, leading to frustration and turnover.


**Late or Error-Filled Invoices:** When billing doesn’t match client contracts or invoices go out late, cash flow slows. A single mistake in a rate card or overtime calculation can erode client trust instantly.


**Compliance Gaps:** Wage laws, tax rules, and worker classifications shift constantly. Without purpose-built tools, companies rely on spreadsheets or manual workarounds — a recipe for costly audits and penalties.


These aren’t minor back-office issues. They’re the difference between being a staffing company that grows — or one that loses workers and clients to someone with faster, smoother operations.

## **Two Wrong Paths Staffing Companies Often Take**


The operational challenges that staffing companies face aren’t really their fault. While Bullhorn has created front office software, specifically for staffing companies, the same thing for middle and back office hasn’t existed. When staffing companies for solutions to improve their middle and back office operations, they have historically only had two options. Neither of which is a complete solution, built for staffing.


### 1. The “Generic Payroll Provider”


These systems are built for any business, not the unique complexity of staffing. On paper, they can cut checks and file taxes. In reality:


- **Manual corrections for state-compliant paystubs** : States like CA, NJ, and IL require every client, rate, and set of hours to be listed. Generic payroll providers are incapable of handling this out-of-the-box, forcing staffing companies to custom build paystubs (and risk penalties if it’s generated incorrectly).


- **Gaps in wage & hour compliance** - Overtime, minimum wage, and sick leave must be calculated on a per work-location basis. Generic systems push this burden back onto admins.


- **Inefficient multi-location taxation** - When workers clock in one state on a Monday and another on a Wednesday, admins have to update worker profiles by hand. Zeal dynamically taxes based on actual work location, including nexus and reciprocity rules.


- **Missed or delayed filings** - If tax IDs come in late, many providers won’t backdate filings, leaving staffing companies exposed to penalties. Zeal handles backdated filings automatically.


- **Hidden fees and slow support** - Amended filings, off-cycle runs, or compliance corrections often trigger surprise charges and weeks-long delays. Zeal includes these in-platform, with dedicated success managers, tax experts, and fast turnaround.


- **Messy onboarding processes** - Legacy technology results in a lack of cohesion and automation and as a result, onboarding becomes a patchwork of documents, emails, and manual data entry.


- **Duplicate data entry** - Generic payroll systems aren’t deeply integrated with Bullhorn, which means recruiters and admins end up re-keying worker information across systems — introducing errors and wasting hours every week.


For a small local business with one location and a handful of employees, a generic provider might work. For a staffing company juggling hundreds of workers across multiple clients, it’s a costly slowdown.


### 2. The “All-in-One Platform”


These vendors promise the world: ATS, onboarding, payroll, invoicing, compliance — all under one roof. But here’s the reality:


- **Mediocre front-office tools** - ATS and CRM features lag behind Bullhorn, making it harder to win talent and clients in competitive markets.


- **Deprioritized middle and back office functionality** - Onboarding, payroll, and billing are tacked on, no purpose-built for staffing.


- **Rigid workflows** - One-size-fits-all functionality forces your team to adapt to the software rather than the tools adapting to your business needs.


- **Incomplete solutions that require patchwork** - When an all-in-one lacks critical functionality (and can’t prioritize it because product teams are spread thin), staffing companies end up bolting on new tools anyway — defeating the purpose of an “all-in-one.”


- **Locked in contracts and slow innovation** - To soften the sticker shock when buying new software, all-in-one vendors bundle discounts into multi-year contracts. That guaranteed revenue locks staffing companies in and allows the platform to rest on their heels rather than delivering meaningful updates.


- **Weak worker experience** - White-label mobile and web apps are rarely included, leaving workers stuck in vendor-branded portals instead of your agency’s brand.
