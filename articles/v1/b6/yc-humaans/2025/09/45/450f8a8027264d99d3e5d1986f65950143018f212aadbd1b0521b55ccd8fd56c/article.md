# AI & HR Readiness Report 2026

Artificial Intelligence has officially entered the HR mainstream. What was once considered a distant possibility has quickly become a defining force for how organisations recruit, support, and manage their people. According to our *AI & HR Readiness Report 2026* , HR leaders worldwide are shifting from experimentation to real-world deployment, embedding AI into their strategies and operating models.


### Familiarity and Confidence Are Growing Rapidly


Only a year ago, less than half of HR professionals described themselves as “very familiar” with AI. Today, that figure has soared to 80%. This leap signals more than just awareness. HR teams are developing a deeper, more practical understanding of AI’s applications, giving them the confidence to evaluate solutions, work with vendors, and implement new systems at speed.


### Adoption Has Become the Norm


The data makes it clear: adoption is no longer optional. A striking 93% of HR teams have now used AI tools, up from 80% in 2024. Even more encouraging, none of this year’s respondents reported negative experiences, compared to 8% the year before. Positive results are creating a flywheel effect, where successful pilots give leaders the confidence to scale further.


### Budgets Are Following Strategy


Organisations are backing intent with investment. More than 80% of respondents are either already investing in AI or planning to in the next 12 months, and nearly 70% have committed budgets over $100K annually. A quarter of HR teams are now dedicating half a million dollars or more, showing that AI in HR is no longer a line item for experimentation but a core part of operating budgets.


### Where AI Delivers the Most Value


While AI is being applied broadly, HR leaders are zeroing in on the areas where it creates measurable business impact. Data management and analytics are emerging as the top priority, cited by 25% of respondents, doubling from last year. By turning people data into actionable insights, AI is helping organisations improve decision-making across recruiting, performance, and workforce planning.


The benefits are already visible. Over half of HR professionals report faster decision-making, almost 50% highlight cost and time savings, and many are seeing improvements in employee engagement and personalised experiences. These early wins suggest AI is not only reducing administrative burden but also enhancing the quality of HR services.


### The Rise of Agentic AI


2025 also marks the moment when autonomous, or “agentic,” AI enters the HR conversation in a significant way. More than half of HR leaders report that they are already using agentic AI in some capacity, with talent acquisition leading the way. Functions like workforce planning, onboarding, and performance management are also seen as ripe for automation.


However, this enthusiasm comes with caution. Around 45% of HR professionals express concern that AI could replace significant parts of their role. This tension underscores the importance of approaching adoption with a focus on augmentation, upskilling, and clear communication about AI’s role in supporting rather than replacing HR.


### Challenges on the Road Ahead


Despite the progress, barriers remain. Data privacy and security are the top concerns, cited by more than half of respondents. Ethical considerations, once a fringe issue, have jumped to 40%. Skills gaps and resistance to change are also slowing progress. These concerns highlight the need for robust governance frameworks, bias checks, and continuous investment in AI literacy.


### Looking to 2026 and Beyond


HR leaders are optimistic about the future. Nearly 40% expect productivity gains of 41 to 60% from AI adoption, with some anticipating even higher improvements. The most desired capabilities for the next two years include fully autonomous recruiting, AI-driven career coaching, and advanced analytics dashboards.


The message is clear: AI is no longer just about efficiency. It is about enabling HR to move faster, make better decisions, and unlock growth across the organisation.


### Keeping the Human in HR


As AI adoption accelerates, one principle must guide the journey: keeping the human in HR. AI can automate repetitive tasks, generate insights, and even operate autonomously in some areas, but it cannot replace the empathy, judgment, and relationships that define great HR. The challenge and opportunity for leaders is to balance technological progress with human-centred values.
