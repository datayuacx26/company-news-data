# OnPrem Power: How Onsite Energy is Rewriting the Rules of Growth

Three compounding forces are driving energy demand in logistics real estate: electrification of transportation fleets, automation and robotics on warehouse floors, and surging demand from digital infrastructure, including data centers. U.S. electricity demand is expected to grow 25 percent by 2030 and nearly 80 percent by 2050, according to ICF. Onsite power generation is increasingly how operators are responding to that gap.


The average wait for additional power from a utility is close to five years, according to the Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory, with nearly twice the capacity of the entire U.S. grid currently stuck in utility approval queues. OnPrem Power systems can be deployed significantly faster, enabling operators to move on their own schedule rather than the grid's.


A microgrid is a localized energy system that can operate independently of or in coordination with the main utility grid. For warehouses and logistics facilities, microgrid solutions draw on gas generation, fuel cells or solar, paired with onsite energy storage and management systems. The result is steady power that runs on the operator’s schedule, not the grid’s. Peak-hour pricing volatility becomes a manageable variable rather than an uncontrollable cost.


Grid outages, peak demand surges and interconnection delays all threaten to halt logistics operations entirely. Onsite power generation, microgrid systems and onsite energy storage reduce that risk by building power resilience directly into the facility, providing an independent layer of power that keeps operations running regardless of grid conditions. The result: power built in, not bolted on.


Onsite power generation and microgrid systems can be deployed within both existing and new warehouse and logistics facilities, with energy design, deployment and management built into the facility relationship, not parceled out to multiple vendors. For operators, that means a single point of accountability across real estate, energy infrastructure and day-to-day operations, with a path to power that doesn't hinge on utility timelines.
