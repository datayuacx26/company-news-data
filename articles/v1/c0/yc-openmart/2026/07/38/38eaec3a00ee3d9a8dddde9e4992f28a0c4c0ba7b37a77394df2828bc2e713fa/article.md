# Best physician email list providers (2026): verified databases compared

Finding accurate physician contact data is harder than it looks. Doctors move between practices, open independent clinics, and change specialties — and most email databases can't keep up. This guide breaks down the top physician email list providers, what each is best for, and how to pick the right one for your outreach goal.


## What is a physician email list?


A physician email list is a database of verified contact information — primarily email addresses — for licensed medical doctors. Quality lists include the physician's practice name, specialty, location, phone number, and NPI (National Provider Identifier). Lists can be segmented by specialty (cardiologist, pediatrician, surgeon), geography, practice type (independent vs. hospital-employed), or practice size.


Who buys physician email lists?


- Medical device and pharmaceutical sales reps
- Healthcare software vendors (EHR, billing, practice management)
- Continuing medical education (CME) providers
- Staffing and locum tenens agencies
- Suppliers selling to independent medical practices

### 2. Specialized medical databases (Definitive Healthcare, IQVIA, SK&A) — best for enterprise healthcare sales


Specialized medical databases aggregate physician data from NPI registries, claims data, hospital credentialing records, and prescribing data. They offer deep segmentation by specialty, procedure volume, prescribing behavior, and health system affiliation.


Best for: Pharmaceutical companies, large medical device manufacturers, and enterprise healthcare vendors who need to target physicians at specific hospital systems or by clinical behavior.


Limitations: High cost (typically five-figure annual contracts), complex procurement, and minimal coverage of independent practice owners. These platforms are built for enterprise buyers, not SMB sales teams or individual reps.


### 3. General B2B databases with healthcare filters — best for broad physician list building


General B2B contact databases include physician records alongside millions of other professional contacts. They typically source from business registrations, LinkedIn profiles, and third-party data aggregators.


Best for: Teams that want a single platform for healthcare and non-healthcare outreach, or need to build a broad physician list by specialty and geography.


Limitations: Coverage of independent practice physicians is inconsistent. Data freshness varies, and physicians who do not maintain a strong public professional profile are often missing or outdated. These platforms are optimized for corporate employee contacts, not practice owners.


### 4. NPI registry + email enrichment — best for compliance-first teams building their own list


The NPPES NPI registry is a free federal database of all licensed healthcare providers in the US. It includes physician names, specialty, practice address, and phone number — but no email addresses. Teams can export NPI data and enrich it with emails using a tool like[Openmart's business owner finder](https://www.openmart.com/products/business-owner-finder) .


Best for: Teams with compliance requirements who want to build a first-party physician list from a government source and enrich it with verified contact data.


Limitations: Requires a multi-step workflow and technical comfort with data exports and enrichment APIs.


### 5. Medical association and specialty society lists — best for niche specialty targeting


Many medical specialty societies (AMA, ACS, ACC, AAP, etc.) sell or license their member email lists for approved commercial purposes. These lists are highly accurate for the specific specialty but are narrow in scope, expensive per record, and come with strict usage restrictions.


Best for: CME providers, specialty-specific medical device reps, or pharmaceutical teams targeting a single specialty at scale.


Limitations: Usage restrictions, high per-record cost, and limited geographic or practice-type filtering.


### 6. Healthcare staffing databases — best for locum tenens and physician recruiting


Healthcare staffing and recruiting platforms maintain physician databases focused on employment status, specialty, licensure, and availability. These are distinct from sales-outreach databases and are not suitable for product or service sales.


Best for: Locum tenens agencies, hospital recruiting teams, and physician placement firms.


Limitations: Not designed for sales outreach — contact data is often restricted to recruiting use cases.

## What to look for in a physician email list provider


### 1. Independent practice vs. hospital-employed coverage


Most enterprise databases are built around health system affiliations and hospital-employed physicians. If the target is an independent cardiologist running a private clinic or a pediatrician who owns their practice, a local business database like Openmart will have significantly better coverage than an enterprise medical platform.


### 2. Data freshness and verification


Physician contact data decays fast. Doctors complete residencies, join and leave group practices, and open independent clinics regularly. Look for providers that verify data on a rolling basis rather than publishing static annual snapshots. Openmart verifies contact data at 97–99% accuracy.


### 3. Specialty and geography segmentation


A useful physician email list is not just a flat file of email addresses. Effective segmentation by specialty (see Openmart's[dermatologists](https://www.openmart.com/databases/dermatologists) ,[orthopedists](https://www.openmart.com/databases/orthopedists) , and[otolaryngologists](https://www.openmart.com/databases/otolaryngologist) databases), geography, and practice type makes targeting precise and reduces wasted outreach spend.


### 4. Compliance


Physician email outreach in the US must comply with CAN-SPAM. Lists sourced from business registrations, NPI records, and publicly available practice information are generally compliant for B2B commercial outreach. Always confirm the data sourcing methodology with any provider before purchasing.


### 5. Export and integration options


Most outreach workflows require a CSV export or API access to push contacts into an email sequencing tool. Openmart supports direct CSV export from every database page and offers a[local business API](https://www.openmart.com/products/local-business-database) for programmatic access.
