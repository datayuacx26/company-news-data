# 6 Best Privacy.com Alternatives for Secure Virtual Payments in 2026

Online payment fraud is getting harder to ignore. U.S. data breaches reached a record high in 2025, with 3,322 reported incidents, representing a 4% increase over the previous year. For everyday consumers, that translates to one very practical question: is your real card number floating around in a merchant database somewhere, just waiting to be stolen?


Virtual cards were built to answer that question. Privacy.com popularized the concept, but its US-only availability, strict card limits on the free plan, and minimal budgeting integration push many users to look elsewhere.[42% of U.S](https://www.pymnts.com/digital-payments/2025/payment-security-concerns-are-pushing-us-consumers-to-use-virtual-cards-online/) . consumers used a virtual card in the past six months, and 65% say they're likely to use one in the next year, so the demand for strong alternatives has never been higher. If you're one of those consumers, this guide covers the six best Privacy.com alternatives in 2026, ranked by overall usefulness for the general user.


### Key Takeaways


-


**Privacy.com has real limitations:**[The free tier caps you at 12 cards](https://ramp.com/blog/privacy-dot-com-alternatives) per month, and single-use card numbers are not included. If you need more volume or richer controls, you need an alternative.


-


**Virtual cards are going mainstream:**[Virtual card adoption is particularly](https://www.cuinsight.com/why-virtual-cards-are-reshaping-member-expectations/) strong among tech-savvy millennials and Gen Z, who value the privacy and security benefits of virtual cards for online transactions. Act accordingly and protect yourself now.


-


**Budgeting-integrated cards beat standalone privacy cards:** Tools like[Envelope](https://envelopebudgeting.com/) connect virtual card spending directly to your budget categories, offering protection and financial clarity in a single tool.


-


**International users must look beyond Privacy.com:**[Privacy.com's top complaints](https://buvei.com/blog/privacy-com-virtual-card-review-2025/) center on its US-only availability, requirement for a Social Security number, and lack of international access, meaning global users need alternatives built for their market.


-


**Fraud is the real driver:** The FTC reported that more than 2.3 million consumers in the U.S. experienced fraud in 2022, equating to nearly $9 billion in losses. A virtual card is one of the most effective shields you can add to your digital financial life.

### 1. Envelope, Editor's Pick and Best for Budget-Connected Virtual Cards


Most virtual card tools hand you a digital card number and call it done.[Envelope](https://envelopebudgeting.com/) takes a fundamentally different approach: it connects each virtual card directly to a spending envelope in your budget, so your payment protection and your financial planning live in the same place.


#### How Envelope Works


Envelope is a budgeting app with built-in checking and debit cards. It helps you organize money into digital envelopes, control spending before it happens, and manage household finances with more clarity. Envelope also lets you create virtual debit cards tied to specific envelopes.


The practical impact of that design is significant. For example, you could create a "Netflix" virtual debit card that only has $20/month available. If the envelope does not have enough money, the card does not have extra money to pull from. You can also close the card anytime.


This makes Envelope different from a simple privacy card tool. It is not only protecting your card number; it is connecting the card to your actual budget. That distinction matters for anyone who has ever been surprised by an auto-renewal charge or tried to cancel a subscription only to find the merchant keeps billing anyway.


> **Pro Tip:** Use one Envelope virtual card per subscription service. Set the envelope's monthly cap to the exact subscription price. If the merchant ever tries to charge more, a price hike, a surprise fee, or an unauthorized renewal, the card simply declines. You stay in control without lifting a finger.


**Pros:**

Virtual debit cards tied directly to individual budget envelopes


-


Built-in checking account, so no separate bank account needed


-


Envelope-level spending caps prevent overcharges and unwanted renewals


-


[Early paycheck access: get paid up](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/envelope-budgeting-banking/id6444296251) to two days early with Envelope's early paycheck feature

**Cons:**


-


Primarily US-focused, so international currency support is limited

Fewer standalone privacy masking features (email aliases, phone masks) compared to IronVest


**Best for:** Individuals and households who want virtual card protection with real budgeting power baked in.


### 2. Revolut, Best for International Shoppers and Travelers


Revolut has grown into one of the most versatile fintech platforms globally, and its virtual card capabilities make it a strong Privacy.com alternative for anyone who spends across borders.


#### What Sets Revolut Apart


[One of Revolut's biggest](https://ucompares.com/services/virtual-cards/) advantages is its disposable virtual card technology. After each online transaction, the card details automatically change, reducing the risk of fraud and unauthorized purchases. This feature is particularly valuable for online shoppers who frequently buy from unfamiliar websites or international merchants.


[The app includes two-factor](https://financer.com/review/revolut/) authentication, biometric login, instant card freeze, and transaction notifications. You can also create disposable virtual cards for safer online shopping on Premium and Metal plans.


On pricing, Premium costs $9.99 per month and includes travel insurance, higher exchange limits, and priority support. Metal costs $16.99 per month and adds the highest savings APY and a stainless steel card. The Standard (free) plan includes a basic virtual card but without the auto-rotating disposable number feature.


> **Pro Tip:** If you sign up for free trials on websites you don't fully trust, use Revolut's disposable virtual card on Premium. The card number changes after the trial, making it impossible for the merchant to charge you again, even if they've stored your details.


**Pros:**


-


Disposable virtual card numbers that auto-rotate after each transaction (Premium/Metal)


-


Multi-currency wallets with real interbank exchange rates


-


Strong fraud monitoring with instant in-app card freezing


-


Budgeting tools and expense tracking built into the same app


**Cons:**


-


Disposable virtual cards require a paid plan ($9.99/month)


-


The main risk factor is the occasional account freeze that some users report during large or unusual transactions


-


[Revolut is not yet a fully](https://financer.com/review/revolut/) licensed U.S. bank; it operates as an e-money institution and relies on banking partners for deposit services


**Best for:** Frequent international shoppers or travelers who want a single app for spending, currency exchange, and virtual card security.


### 3. Wise, Best for Multi-Currency Virtual Spending


Wise (formerly TransferWise) built its reputation on transparent international transfers, and its virtual card extends that philosophy to everyday spending across 40+ currencies.


#### How the Wise Virtual Card Works


[The Wise Virtual Card is designed](https://halocard.co/blog/best-virtual-card-providers) to offer convenience and security. Users can hold over 40 currencies at once, converting them in real-time using the mid-market exchange rate, a feature that minimizes hidden fees.


[You can have up to 3 virtual cards](https://www.thecurrencyshop.com.au/reviews/transferwise-debit-card) at any one time, which can make it easier to manage your budget by assigning different spending types to different cards. Wise virtual cards have different card details to your physical card, and can be frozen instantly whenever you're not using them, offering a handy added layer of security.


[Banks and traditional travel cards](https://liveyourlifeitsyours.com/articles/wise-card-review-best-travel-card) typically apply a spread; they buy currency at the mid-market rate and sell it to you at a worse rate, pocketing the difference. That difference is usually 2-5% and it's invisible because it's baked into the rate, not listed as a fee. Wise does not do this. That alone can save meaningful money for anyone who shops internationally.


**Pros:**

Mid-market exchange rates with no markup on supported currencies


-


Up to 3 virtual cards included, free to create


-


Works across 160+ countries with no foreign transaction fees when paying in a held currency


-


Strong regulatory backing and transparent fee structure


**Cons:**


-

-

-


[Does not support cryptocurrency](https://ucards.uk/learn/en/privacy-com-alternative) and has standard card features


**Best for:** International users who want real exchange rates and a reliable virtual card for cross-border shopping.

With Eno, you can easily lock or unlock virtual card numbers anytime, preventing unwanted charges and giving you full control over your online spending. Even if you get a new credit card, your virtual card numbers remain active and automatically update behind the scenes.


The rewards angle is an important differentiator: all purchases made using these virtual numbers link directly to your Capital One account, so you continue to earn rewards and benefits seamlessly. Privacy.com's debit-based virtual cards earn nothing. With Eno, you keep your rewards while adding a security layer.

### 5. IronVest, Best for Full Identity Masking Beyond Just Cards


For users who want to go further than card-number protection, IronVest (formerly Blur by Abine) offers the most comprehensive privacy toolkit on this list.


#### What IronVest Covers


[IronVest focuses heavily on online](https://ucompares.com/services/virtual-cards/) privacy and identity protection. While many virtual card providers emphasize business spending or international payments, IronVest's primary goal is helping users protect sensitive financial information during online transactions. The platform enables users to create virtual card numbers that shield real payment details from merchants. This reduces the risk of data breaches, unauthorized transactions, and identity theft.


But it goes beyond payments. IronVest is a password manager platform that creates and manages user passwords on different websites. It offers masked email addresses, private phone numbers, and masked single-use debit cards. It's decentralized and offers 2FA authentication.


On pricing:[the free version of IronVest](https://www.allthingssecured.com/identity-protection/9-best-alternatives-to-privacy-virtual-credit-cards/) allows you to test two virtual cards, which they call "masked cards," after which you'll have to upgrade to their Plus ($6/mo) or Ultimate ($15/mo) plans for more.


> **Pro Tip:** Use IronVest's masked email feature alongside its virtual cards. Every website gets a unique forwarding address. If that address starts receiving spam, you know exactly which site sold your data, and you can kill that alias instantly, while your real inbox stays clean.

### Common Mistakes to Avoid When Choosing a Virtual Card Tool


#### Ignoring the Card Limit Fine Print


[Privacy.com's free tier caps you](https://ramp.com/blog/privacy-dot-com-alternatives) at 12 cards per month. Many competing tools also impose limits. Before committing, count how many subscriptions and recurring charges you manage. If it's more than a dozen, you need a plan, or a tool, that can scale with you.


#### Picking a Card Tool Without Budgeting Integration


A virtual card protects your card number. A tool like[Envelope](https://envelopebudgeting.com/) protects your card number AND enforces your spending limits at the envelope level. If you only solve the privacy problem but not the overspending problem, you've only done half the job.


#### Assuming Every Tool Works Internationally


[Privacy.com's top complaints](https://buvei.com/blog/privacy-com-virtual-card-review-2025/) include its US-only availability and lack of international access. The same limitation exists for several other tools. If you shop from international merchants or hold accounts in multiple currencies, Wise and Revolut are the two options built for that use case.


> **Pro Tip:** Before signing up for any virtual card tool, run through your last three months of online transactions. Note which merchants are international, which are subscription-based, and whether you've ever had an unauthorized charge. That audit tells you exactly which features you need, don't pay for tools that don't match your actual spending patterns.


### Frequently Asked Questions


#### What is a virtual card and how does it protect me?


A virtual card is a digital payment card with its own unique number, expiration date, and CVV that you use in place of your real card number when shopping online.[A virtual card is a digital](https://ucompares.com/services/virtual-cards/) payment card that generates a unique card number, expiration date, and security code for online transactions. It functions like a regular debit or credit card but exists only in digital form. Virtual cards help protect your actual banking information and reduce the risk of fraud during online purchases.


#### Is Privacy.com available outside the United States?


No. Privacy.com's top complaints are its US-only availability, requirement for a Social Security number, and no support for international access. If you are outside the US, Wise and Revolut are the strongest alternatives to evaluate, as both support dozens of countries and multiple currencies.


#### Can I still earn credit card rewards when using a virtual card?


It depends on the tool. If you use a standalone tool like Privacy.com or IronVest, those are debit-based and carry no rewards. If you use a card-issuer's own virtual feature, like Capital One Eno or Amex's virtual card tool, all purchases made using those virtual numbers link directly to your credit card account, so you continue to earn rewards and benefits seamlessly.


#### What makes Envelope different from other Privacy.com alternatives?


Most virtual card tools stop at card-number masking. Envelope is different from a simple privacy card tool. It is not only protecting your card number; it is connecting the card to your actual budget. Each virtual card is tied to a spending envelope, so a subscription card for Netflix literally cannot charge you more than your Netflix envelope contains. That combination of protection and proactive budgeting is unique among consumer-facing tools.


#### How many virtual cards do I actually need?


In my experience, most households need one virtual card per active subscription, plus one or two general-purpose "burner" cards for one-off purchases from unfamiliar merchants. That typically works out to somewhere between 5 and 20 cards.[Virtual cards are generally](https://ucompares.com/services/virtual-cards/) considered safer for online transactions, and many providers offer features such as disposable card numbers, spending limits, merchant-specific cards, and instant card freezing, so the right number depends on how many merchants you want to isolate from each other.
