# SAT vs ACT: How Do You Make the Best Choice for You?

For high school sophomores and juniors, one of the first big questions in the college application process journey is, “Which test should I take?” For years, the choice was SAT vs. ACT, a classic dilemma. But the game has changed.


This is the new era of standardized tests: the Digital SAT from the College Board and the Enhanced ACT.


Both exams are taken at a test center (and some during school test days), and both are critical tools in the college admissions process. While many schools are test-optional, a strong test score can be a game-changer for your college application, unlocking thousands in merit scholarship money and proving your college readiness.


But these two admissions tests are fundamentally different. The Digital SAT is a new, adaptive test built from the ground up. The Enhanced ACT retains its linear, non-adaptive format but has undergone significant structural changes, including a shorter core test and both digital and paper testing options.


So, ACT vs. SAT? Here’s a breakdown of the new choice.


## SAT vs ACT: A Comprehensive Overview


When it comes to college admissions, students are inevitably confronted with this question: “Which test should I take? The SAT or the ACT?”. While both exams cover similar subjects and test the same skill sets, each standardized test is unique and stands on its own. This is why students must know what makes each test special and which is best given a student’s strengths, weaknesses, and preferences.


The core[American College Test (or the ACT)](https://www.act.org/content/act/en-california.html) has been streamlined to include three required sections: English, Mathematics, and Reading. The Science and Writing test sections are now optional add-ons. Meanwhile, the[Scholastic Aptitude Test (SAT)](https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/sat) is divided into two major test sections: Reading and Writing, and Mathematics. The ACT was originally designed to test a student’s grasp of high school concepts, while the SAT was designed to assess a student’s reasoning and problem-solving skills. These days, though, the lines are blurred. Universities and colleges treat a student’s SAT or ACT score the same.


The ACT Composite score is now based only on the required sections: English, Mathematics, and Reading. The SAT separates the score reports into two scores (Reading & Writing, and Math). Both the SAT and ACT offer a superscore, which combines the highest score from each section across multiple test administrations. The ACT Superscore is also calculated using only the three required section scores.

## Understanding the Difference Between the ACT and SAT Structures


Aside from the differences between their contents, the ACT and SAT also differ in how they are structured. The duration for each section also differs for the ACT and SAT. Students choosing between the two tests must know this so they can decide which one suits best for their test-taking capabilities best.


With the enhanced changes, the required core ACT is now significantly shorter. It takes approximately 125 minutes (English, Math, Reading) for the core test, which is much closer to the Digital SAT’s 2 hours and 14 minutes. The test has fewer questions overall, which means students now get more time per question than on the previous ACT format, allowing for slightly improved pacing. The SAT also allows for more pacing per question, but that doesn’t make it an easier test because of that. For the ACT, students still need sharp decision-making, while the SAT requires students to sharpen their time management skills due to the adaptive nature of some questions.
