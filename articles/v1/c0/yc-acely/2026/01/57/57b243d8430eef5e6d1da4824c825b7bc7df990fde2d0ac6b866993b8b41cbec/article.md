# Parent Guide to Supporting Digital SAT and ACT Prep

Preparing for the digital SAT and ACT looks different from what it did even a few years ago. Tests that once relied on paper and pencils are now delivered on screens with built-in tools.


This parent guide explains what has changed, why parental support matters, and how families can help students prepare with structure and realistic expectations.


## Why parental support matters


Digital testing introduces new factors beyond academic content:


- comfort with testing software
- device readiness
- screen-based stamina
- stress and confidence


Parents can reduce avoidable friction by helping students build routines and practice in realistic testing conditions.


## Understanding digital SAT and ACT formats


Both exams assess core college-readiness skills, but they differ in pacing, structure, and delivery.


### Digital SAT highlights


- Two main sections (Reading and Writing, and Math)
- Adaptive modules
- Built-in tools like calculator and question flagging
- Shorter total test duration compared to the old paper SAT


### ACT highlights


The ACT keeps fixed section pacing and includes science-style data interpretation. Depending on region and administration, the test can be paper-based or digital.


## Helping students choose SAT vs ACT


Families should focus on fit rather than prestige.


The SAT can be a strong fit for students who prefer shorter adaptive modules. The ACT may fit students who are comfortable with fast pacing and data interpretation.


A practical approach is to complete one full-length SAT practice test and one ACT practice test before committing.


## How parents can support daily prep


### Create a stable study environment


Use a consistent study space, a charged device, and minimized distractions. Encourage students to practice on the same device setup they are likely to use on test day.


### Build a realistic schedule


Two to four sessions per week (30-60 minutes each) is often more effective than marathon cramming.


### Keep motivation healthy


Praise effort and consistency, not just outcomes. Avoid score comparisons and constant pressure conversations.


## Final thoughts


Parents do not need to be test experts to make a meaningful difference. Calm structure, realistic planning, and consistent support can significantly improve a student's experience and outcomes.
