# Accommodations for the Digital SAT & ACT: Extra Time & Support

## Understanding Accommodations for the Digital SAT & ACT


Stepping into standardized tests like the Digital SAT and ACT can feel like preparing for a marathon. Testing accommodations help students with documented disabilities or learning differences remove barriers so scores reflect true knowledge and skills.


## Why Accommodations Matter


Standardized tests are designed to be uniform, but that can create hurdles for students with certain conditions. Accommodations provide equity by supporting how a student accesses the exam, not by lowering standards.


## Common Types of Accommodations


### Extended Time


Extended time is the most commonly requested accommodation. Students may receive +50% time or +100% time, depending on approved needs.


Students approved for extended time in the SAT Suite often receive additional breaks as well.


### Break Supports


Common break-related supports include:


- **Extra breaks:** Two additional 5-minute breaks.
- **Extended break:** One 20-minute break instead of a standard 10-minute break.
- **Breaks as needed:** Students can pause when needed within approved guidelines.


### Reading and Visual Supports


Students may qualify for supports such as:


- Magnification tools
- Braille or refreshable braille support
- Text-to-speech tools
- Paper testing in approved exceptional cases


### Writing and Motor Supports


- Dictation/speech-to-text
- A scribe for recording responses
- Four-function calculator use where approved for disability-related need


### Physical Accommodations


Physical accommodations can include preferential seating, wheelchair access, private rooms, and permission for medical devices or snacks.


## Who Qualifies?


Eligibility is based on documented need and disability impact.


- [SAT eligibility criteria](https://accommodations.collegeboard.org/how-accommodations-work/who-is-eligible)
- [ACT eligibility criteria](https://www.act.org/content/act/en/products-and-services/the-act-educator/states-and-districts/accommodations.html)


### Documentation


Strong documentation from qualified professionals is central to approval.


### IEPs and 504 Plans


Students with active IEPs or 504 Plans are often easier to verify because they already have a documented history of receiving support in school.


## How to Apply


### College Board (SAT)


1. Talk to your school SSD Coordinator.
2. Gather all relevant documentation.
3. Have the coordinator submit your request in the SSD portal.
4. Review the decision and keep your eligibility letter.
5. Practice with approved settings in the Bluebook app.


For official instructions, visit[How to Request Accommodations](https://accommodations.collegeboard.org/request-accommodations) .


### ACT


1. Talk with your school official/counselor.
2. Complete required consent forms.
3. Have the school submit through TAAS.
4. Wait for ACT's decision notification.


For official instructions, visit[ACT Test Accommodations and English Learner Supports](https://www.act.org/content/act/en/products-and-services/the-act/registration/accommodations.html) .


## Timelines and Deadlines


Start early. Missing registration or accommodation deadlines can result in testing without approved supports.


## If a Request Is Denied


Both ACT and College Board provide appeal processes. Appeals usually require updated documentation or added professional detail about why the requested support is necessary.


## Final Encouragement


Using accommodations is not a shortcut. It is an accessibility support that helps ensure your results reflect your actual ability and preparation.
