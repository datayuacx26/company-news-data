## What should you look for in an ACT prep platform?


### Score guarantee


The clearest signal of a program's confidence in its own results is whether the guarantee is specific or vague. A specific guarantee, like Acely's[5-point ACT guarantee](https://acely.com/score-improvement-guarantee) , commits to a measurable outcome. A vague "higher score" guarantee, common among legacy brands, can technically be satisfied by a one-point improvement.


### Content volume


More practice material means more chances to build pattern recognition without memorizing answers. For students targeting a competitive score, a platform with only a couple thousand questions will run out of unique content quickly.


Look for:


- At least 3 to 5 full-length ACT practice tests, ideally more
- At least 1,500 to 2,000 ACT practice questions, ideally several thousand


### Content quality


Volume alone isn't enough. Two platforms can both advertise thousands of questions and deliver very different prep experiences depending on how that content was made and how current it is.


**Expert-created vs. generated on the fly.** Some platforms, and general AI tools like ChatGPT, generate practice questions in real time with no expert review, no validation against official test standards, and no consistency check on format or difficulty. That introduces real risk: incorrect answers, flawed reasoning, or questions that don't resemble what a student will actually see on test day. Look for a question bank that's been written and verified by educators with subject-matter expertise, not assembled algorithmically as students go.


**Updated for the enhanced digital format.** The ACT has moved to an enhanced digital, adaptive format, and older prep materials haven't always kept pace. Practice tests and content should reflect the actual enhanced digital ACT: the interface, the section structure, and the pacing conditions students will face on test day. Prep built for the old paper test doesn't fully prepare you for the one you're taking.


### Adaptive personalization


A platform that adjusts what you study next based on how you've actually performed is more efficient than one that assigns the same content to every student regardless of their starting point.


### Free trial


A free trial lets you evaluate fit before paying. Acely offers a 3-day free trial; Kaplan offers none; Princeton Review's free option is a single practice test.
