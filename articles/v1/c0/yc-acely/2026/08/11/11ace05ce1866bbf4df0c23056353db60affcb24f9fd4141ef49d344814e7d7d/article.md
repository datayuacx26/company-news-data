## What should you look for in an SAT prep platform?


### Score guarantee


The clearest signal of a program's confidence in its own results is whether the guarantee is specific or vague. A specific guarantee, like Acely's[200-point SAT guarantee](https://acely.com/score-improvement-guarantee) , commits to a measurable outcome. A vague "higher score" guarantee, common among legacy brands, can technically be satisfied by a one-point improvement.


### Content volume


More practice material means more chances to build pattern recognition without memorizing answers. For students targeting a competitive score, a platform with a few hundred questions will run out of unique content quickly.


Look for:


- At least 4 to 5 full-length practice tests, ideally more
- At least 1,500 to 2,000 practice questions, ideally several thousand


### Content quality


Volume alone isn't enough. Two platforms can both advertise thousands of questions and deliver very different prep experiences depending on how that content was made and how current it is.


**Expert-created vs. generated on the fly.** Some platforms, and general AI tools like ChatGPT, generate practice questions in real time with no expert review, no validation against official test standards, and no consistency check on format or difficulty. That introduces real risk: incorrect answers, flawed reasoning, or questions that don't resemble what a student will actually see on test day. Look for a question bank that's been written and verified by educators with subject-matter expertise, not assembled algorithmically as students go.


**Updated for the digital format.** The SAT has moved to a digital, adaptive format, and older prep materials haven't always kept pace. Some legacy providers still reference paper-test conventions, like timing built around the discontinued SAT with Essay, in their current materials. Practice tests and content should reflect the actual digital SAT: the interface, the adaptive section structure, and the pacing conditions students will face on test day.


### Adaptive personalization


A platform that adjusts what you study next based on how you've actually performed is more efficient than one that assigns the same content to every student regardless of their starting point.


### Free trial


A free trial lets you evaluate fit before paying. Acely offers a 3-day free trial; Khan Academy is always free; Kaplan offers none; Princeton Review's free option is a single practice test.
