# How to Make Your Annual Health Checkup Count

Most corporate health checkups follow the same pattern.


An email goes out announcing the annual screening. Some employees sign up right away, others procrastinate until the deadline, but eventually they book their slot through the app or the company portal and show up at the diagnostic center. Blood gets drawn, vitals get checked, forms get filled.


Two weeks later, a 15-page PDF lands in their inbox. They open it during lunch break, coffee in hand, and scan through the numbers. Most look fine—green checkmarks, "within normal range." Then they spot the red flags: Cholesterol at 245 mg/dL, Vitamin D deficient, HbA1c at 6.3—borderline pre-diabetic.


*"I should probably look into this."*


They make a mental note to Google what HbA1c means, think about booking a doctor's appointment, and maybe start walking in the evenings. But then work gets busy. A project deadline hits, someone on the team quits, and the quarterly review is coming up.


The report gets minimized, then closed, mentally filed under "deal with this later."


Later never comes.


By the time next year's checkup rolls around, the cholesterol is still high, the vitamin D deficiency persists, and that pre-diabetic warning has turned into full-blown diabetes.


This is what most annual health checkups deliver: data that employees receive but rarely act on—not because they don't care, but because there's no infrastructure to help them follow through. No explanation of what those numbers really mean, no plan for what to do next, and no one is checking in to see if they did anything about it.


The checkup happened. The report arrived. But nothing changed.

## **Why Your PDF Report Changes Nothing**


The traditional annual checkup works on a simple premise: give employees their health data, and they'll know what to do with it.


But that premise has a flaw. Not because employees don't care about their health, but because a 15-page lab report written in medical jargon, delivered with no explanation and followed up by no one, doesn't actually lead to behavior change.


Here's what usually happens: You get your blood work done, and two weeks later the PDF arrives. You open it, see your HbA1c is 6.2, and immediately Google what that means. The search results tell you it's in the pre-diabetic range. You feel a flash of concern, tell yourself you should probably eat better, maybe cut back on the sweets.


Then life continues. Work piles up, the concern fades, and the report stays in your downloads folder. By the time your next checkup comes around, that 6.2 has crept higher.


> The problem isn't the checkup itself, it's the gap between receiving information and knowing what to do with it. Between seeing the numbers and actually changing them.

## **Three Critical Gaps in Your Health Report**


Your report arrives with flagged values and medical terminology. It assumes you can answer three critical questions on your own:


**1. What your numbers actually mean**


Your report says your HbA1c is 6.4. What does that mean? That you're pre-diabetic. What does *that* mean? How close are you to diabetes? Is 6.4 concerning or manageable? Should you see an endocrinologist, or can you reverse it with diet? Can it even be reversed?


The report won't tell you. ChatGPT will give you sixteen conflicting answers. Your company's insurance won't clarify anything until you're sick enough to file a claim.


**2. What to actually do about it**


Let's say you understand that 6.4 HbA1c means you're pre-diabetic, and yes, it's reversible with lifestyle changes. Great.


*What changes? How much exercise? Which foods exactly? Should you cut carbs or just sugar? Do you need medication? How do you know if it's working? When should you retest?*


The report offers zero guidance. You're handed a problem with no instructions for solving it.


**3. Who will follow up with you**


This might be the biggest gap of all.


Even if you understand your numbers and know what to do, who's checking that you're actually doing it? Who's monitoring your progress? Who's adjusting the plan when it's not working? Who's celebrating when your numbers improve?


Traditional annual checkups are a snapshot, not a video. They show you where you are, not where you're going. And they definitely don't help you get anywhere better.
