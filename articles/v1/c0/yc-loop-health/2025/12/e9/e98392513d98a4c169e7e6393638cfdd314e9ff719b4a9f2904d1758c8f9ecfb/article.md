# Introducing Care Tab: One Timeline for Your Entire Health History

*Your mom calls. Her joints have been hurting for a week. "It's probably nothing," she says.*


*But you're in Bangalore, and she's in Delhi, and probably nothing doesn't feel like enough. So you book a doctor's appointment. The doctor refers her to a specialist. The specialist orders tests.*


*Now you need her last prescription to show the specialist. You can't find it. She sends you a blurry photo of some paperwork—turns out it's the wrong document. You finally locate it in a WhatsApp thread from two months ago.*


*You book the tests. The lab emails her the results, but she doesn't forward them to you for three days. When the next appointment comes up, you've forgotten what the first doctor said because you didn't write it down.*


*Coordinating someone's healthcare remotely isn't hard because medicine is complicated. It's hard because the information lives in ten different places, and you're always one missing document away from starting over.*

## **The Cost of Scattered Health Records**


Most of us aren't just managing our own health. We're juggling appointments for aging parents, making sure our partners follow up on that thing the doctor mentioned, tracking our kids' vaccination records.


And every time someone in your family sees a doctor, you become an accidental medical archivist:


- Digging through old WhatsApp messages for that prescription from March
- Trying to remember which specialist you saw for this exact issue last year
- Explaining the same medical history to three different doctors because nobody has the full picture
- Hoping you don't miss a follow-up appointment because you forgot to set a reminder


You're not a healthcare professional, you're just trying to make sure the people you care about get the right care at the right time.


But without a single place where everything lives, you're constantly playing catch-up.

## **Why Continuity of Care Changes Everything**


Healthcare fails when information doesn't follow the patient.


You see a specialist who doesn't know what your primary care doctor recommended. You get tests done but forget to share the results with the doctor who ordered them. You start a medication and can't remember if the dosage changed at your last visit.


These gaps—small on their own—add up to misdiagnoses, duplicate tests, delayed treatment, and medications that don't work because nobody knew you were already taking something else.


The Care Tab closes those gaps. Not by adding more work to your plate. But by making sure the information follows you automatically.
