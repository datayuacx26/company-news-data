# Couples Coaching - Working Together To Build A Successful Relationship


Creating and maintaining fulfilling relationships takes hard work and commitment. And it doesn't always come naturally to us.


If you’ve ever been in a relationship, you’ve experienced relationship problems. And you’re probably familiar with what happens when those problems don’t get resolved. As time goes on, unresolved problems may lead to grudges, insults, resentment, jealousy, insecurity, cheating, and eventually breakup or divorce.


If you’re struggling with your relationship or trying to salvage a broken one, you may decide to turn to professionals for guidance. That’s where a **Couples Coach** comes in.


I know what you’re thinking:


> **Don’t you mean a couples therapist?**


Well, not necessarily. Maybe couple’s therapy is indeed what will work best for you, but there’s a good chance it may not apply to you. Maybe your partner/ex would not be open to "therapy" even if it was sufficiently applicable to you.


> **What about a therapist just for me, not couples therapy?**


Yes, therapy in general is going to apply to a lot more situations than couples therapy. But depending on your goals, couples coaching may still be a better fit for you than therapy.


To be clear, couples coaching isn’t a substitute for therapy - a couple's coach does not help treat mental ailments. Therapy focuses on mental health, whereas couples coaching focuses on your interpersonal and relationship goals.

## HOW DOES COUPLES COACHING WORK?


During couples coaching, you and your partner will talk with a relationship/couples coach to go over your relationship. They will help with amplifying your relationship's strengths and minimizing the negatives. Depending on what you need, the process can take some time. It's crucial to be patient, committed, and work together.


### Starting at square A


The first few sessions will be focused on getting to know both of you and your relationship - your backgrounds, how you met, how long you have been together, how things have progressed, what issues you want to work on, etc. While you may feel a bit intimidated at first, it's important to be patient and speak your mind. Honest communication is the best way to progress and pretty soon the sessions can start feeling casual.


### Fostering open communication


Coaches encourage their clients to openly discuss things that are on their minds during these sessions. Reviewing previous tasks and topics discussed in the past together are great segues into meaningful conversation. A lot of couples are more open to sharing things with each other when they have a third party in the room. And coaches do their best to focus these conversations - to foster relationship growth.


### Setting tasks for future sessions


Setting goals and keeping track of them is an important part of going through couples coaching. Towards this, couples are often given tasks or assignments to work on between sessions. These can range from journal exercises to combined breathing exercises. Your coach will often discuss what you're learning from these tasks in order to gauge your progress and what to work on next.


### Common Exercises


There are a few common exercises that coaches employ to foster open communication, cooperation, empathy, conflict resolution, etc. Couples are also encouraged to collaborate on lists of new, fun activities they can try together. The goal isn't just to bring some novelty into the relationship, but also to enjoy quality time together. Couples may even work on individual lists where they write down things they appreciate about their partner, or positive messages, etc.

## How To Make The Most Of Couples Coaching


Just because you sign up for couples coaching doesn't automatically mean all your relationship problems will disappear immediately. You must do your part to actually get the most out of it.


### Make a conscious effort


One of the most common reasons for couples wanting o break up is that they think it's too far beyond fixing or that it will take too much work. But it's important to remember that this work will nurture you, regardless of what happens with the relationship. Even if things don't work out after you try your best, you will have learned crucial information that will guide you in your future relationships. If nothing, it'll help you to avoid similar mistakes.


### Be patient


A successful relationship requires a strong foundation, which takes time to build. If it could be done in a day, no one would have any relationship issues ever. Couples coaching will require a lot of emotional labor on your part - you'll have to open up and be vulnerable, be honest about your needs and your dislikes, but also be willing to hear criticism of yourself. It can hurt and be very uncomfortable. Saving a relationship can take some time and trying to hurry it can often lead to frustration that just feeds into the negativity.


### Listen to your coach


Your coach will tell you what to do, but ultimately it's up to you to actually follow through. Most couples know that arguments can be futile, hurtful, and often just make things worse. But it doesn't stop them from having arguments. A coach will work with you but you have to work with them or it's not going to go anywhere.


### Building healthy patterns


Building healthy patterns will build the solid foundation you need for your relationship to succeed. Your coach will help you identify negative patterns that you and your partner engage in, and then help you change these patterns. Anything that is directly impacting your relationship can be addressed. They will help you to replace these negative patterns with healthy, positive patterns that will help you reach your relationship goals.


### You don't have to be unhappy to get coaching


You don't need to wait until things are bad. It can certainly be scary to ask your partner to go coaching with you when things are just going fine. In fact, it might even make them worry that things are going bad. In those situations, it might be better to get individual coaching first and your coach can recommend the best way for you to approach couples coaching with your partner.

# Therapy vs Relationship Coaching


Therapists focus on past traumas and issues to change self-destructive habits, repair and improve relationships and work through painful feelings. In this sense, therapy focuses on the past, introspection, and analysis, with the hope of resolving past issues and creating a happier, more stable future. Therapy is a long term process that involves regular sessions with a fixed time limit.


Couples coaches focus on advancing the clients' potential and helping them achieve their goals. They focus on the present and the future, helping clients bridge the gap between their partners and them, as well as where they are and where they want to be. Couples coaches help clients identify obstacles and problematic behaviors in order to create action plans to help them achieve the results they want. They set clients up with a process that may be long or short-term and can involve a combination of on-demand and regular sessions at varying lengths.

## SO WHICH SHOULD I CHOOSE?


# Therapy and coaching have many overlaps. Some therapists will offer you actionable advice and some relationship coaches will help you overcome emotional challenges. When deciding whether to get relationship help from a therapist or a relationship coach, think about how you’ll answer the first question a therapist or relationship coach will ask you:


> **Why brought you here? What is your goal?**


If you’re seeking help with your emotions, like you want to learn how to control your relationship anxiety or get over your fear of commitment, therapy is a good option. If you’re seeking help with an external goal, like reconnecting with your ex or getting more dates from online dating, a relationship coach might be a better option.


If you're interested in hearing on this topic from a psychologist, check out this article on *Psychology Today* -[The Difference Between Coaching and Therapy Is Greatly Overstated](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/what-is-he-thinking/200904/the-difference-between-coaching-and-therapy-is-greatly-overstated)
