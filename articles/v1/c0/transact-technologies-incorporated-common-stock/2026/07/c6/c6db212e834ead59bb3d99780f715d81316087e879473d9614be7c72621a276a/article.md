# Digital HACCP Records That Prove Compliance | BOHA! Temp

## **When a health inspector walks in, the paperwork is the whole conversation**


A health inspector rarely doubts that a kitchen wants to serve safe food. What they need to see is evidence: that the checks were done on schedule, by someone accountable, with a documented response every time a reading came back out of range. A confident answer and a clean-looking kitchen are a great sign but count for very little if the records behind them are thin.


For most operators, those records still live on a clipboard or in a binder somewhere near the walk-in. Handwriting is rushed or paper wanders off, gets rained on by a leaky reach-in, and turns illegible by the third shift. Even at its best, a stack of handwritten logs is a weak way to prove a kitchen has been running safely for weeks on end.


[BOHA! Temp](https://www.transact-tech.com/software/boha-temp) replaces that stack with a guided digital workflow. Staff are directed through each food safety check in turn, the right corrective action appears on screen the moment a finding lands out of range, and every one of those actions rolls up into a digital HACCP record that is ready long before anyone from the health department shows up.


After years of building back-of-house tools, the pattern keeps coming back to is this: temperature is never the hard part of food safety. Anyone can read a probe. What breaks down, shift after shift, is the trail of evidence around the step taken, who took it, when, and what actions were taken when things went wrong. In short, proof that not only was the job done, but done right. Kitchens do not fail inspections because their staff do not care. They fail because detailed notes are not the main priority of a busy kitchen; delivering the highest quality food and service to patrons is the goal. The truth is it’s difficult to deliver at the highest level while also keeping track of every little nuanced, repetitive step taken to prove the highest level was products correctly. When relying on manual process, the honest work from that kitchen has a hard time making it onto paper in a credible form for posterity. By addressing this difficulty, compliance stops being a scramble and starts being a byproduct of doing the job well, giving time back to staff to focus on delivering at the highest level.

## The real output is a HACCP report, not just a temperature log


A temperature reading is one data point. A[HACCP report](https://www.fda.gov/food/hazard-analysis-critical-control-point-haccp/haccp-principles-application-guidelines) is the whole picture: every critical control point a kitchen monitors, every reading taken, every corrective action logged, and proof that each one was handled by an accountable person at the right time. Temperature is part of that picture. So is receiving, cooling, sanitizing, allergen handling, and cleaning, and together they demonstrate compliance with the health codes a restaurant operates under.


**Temp is flexible by design, and lets any jurisdiction’s requirements be built in as a step in the workflow, including:**


- Receiving checks on incoming deliveries


- Cooling logs across the required cooling window


- Manual sanitizer concentration checks


- pH checks on food, captured with an approved probe


- Allergen and prep-station checks


- Handwashing and glove checks


- Cleaning verification tied to food safety


Every one of those steps flows into the same place. The finished records appear as digital HACCP reports in the BOHA! Ops app, and a manager can pull the day’s food safety standards at any time, from any location.


## Who sets the rules, and who enforces them


It helps to be precise about where food safety requirements actually come from, since the terms get used loosely. The[FDA publishes the Food Code](https://www.fda.gov/food/fda-food-code/food-code-2022) as a science-based model, but it is not federal law. It is guidance offered for states and localities to adopt.[FDA’s own adoption tracking confirms](https://www.fda.gov/food/fda-food-code/adoption-fda-food-code-state-and-territorial-agencies-responsible-oversight-restaurants-and-retail) that restaurant and retail food oversight is handled at the state and local level, not the federal level, which is why the specific code edition in force, and the exact rules an inspector checks against, vary by jurisdiction.


State and local health departments are the ones who adopt a version of the code, issue permits, run inspections, and hold the legal authority to cite a restaurant for a violation. A formal, written HACCP plan is not a universal requirement for every restaurant. Most quick-serve and full-service kitchens operate under active managerial control instead. A written HACCP plan is typically required when a kitchen runs a specialized, higher-risk process, and the list is more specific than most operators expect.


Specialized process


Example


Reduced oxygen packaging Sous vide, cook-chill, vacuum packaging


Curing or smoking as preservation Cured or smoked meats


Live product display Molluscan shellfish tanks


Additive-based acidification Sushi rice prepared with vinegar to hold outside temperature control


That last row is why BOHA! Temp’s pH capture is built specifically for sushi rice: acidifying rice to render it safe to hold outside refrigeration is exactly the kind of process that[triggers a variance and a written HACCP plan](https://www.health.mn.gov/communities/environment/food/docs/fs/specialprofs.pdf) in most jurisdictions, and the readings need to be documented as precisely as any temperature check.


This is also why Temp does not tell you what your jurisdiction requires. No software can, since the requirement depends on city, county or state laws, your process, and what the local enforcement agency, whether it be the health department or in some cases the agriculture department, has adopted. What it does is give you the flexibility to build any of it into the workflow, so whatever your local code demands becomes a step your team runs consistently rather than a rule buried in a binder.


## Accountability must be built into the workflow, not added on afterwards


Most food safety failures trace back to a gap in accountability rather than a gap in knowledge. A check gets skipped, a reading goes unrecorded, or a corrective action happens, but no one can say who did it or when. Paper cannot close that gap, because it captures a result without capturing responsibility. The fix is to make accountability a property of the workflow itself, so the record of who did what is created automatically as the work happens, rather than reconstructed afterwards.


In practice, that means the system has to serve two very different people at once. A manager needs to design the program and then verify it is running. Kitchen staff need to be told, in the moment, exactly what to do next. BOHA! Temp is built around that split.


### For managers and operators


Managers set up the workflow, the steps staff need to run consistently to keep food safe, and the corrective-action guidance that kicks in when a finding lands outside acceptable parameters. Then they review the output. Digital HACCP records are there on demand, so a manager can confirm the program is actually running, across a shift or across a region, without standing on the line to watch it happen.


### For location back-of-house staff


For the person in the back-of-house, Temp is a step-by-step guide through everything that has to be done to keep the back-of-house safe. It tracks time-dependent items and holds each action to a window, so an SOP or a corrective action gets done when it needs to be done.


## Why paper records fail the audit


There is nothing sinister about a clipboard, a binder, or a paper log. These formats simply cannot meet the regulatory expectations in place today. The same failure patterns show up in kitchen after kitchen:


- Checks get missed during a rush, and nobody notices until the binder comes out


- Handwriting is illegible, or the field is blank, and neither can be reconstructed after the fact


- There is no reliable way to establish when a reading was actually taken


- Corrective actions vary depending on who happened to be on the line that day


- Managers have no visibility into completion until it is too late to act


### The pencil-whipping problem


Pencil whipping is the industry term for what happens when staff skip the actual check and fill in a log with fabricated readings and approvals, to save time or hide noncompliance. It is usually less a character issue than a tool, workflow, or process one: the check is inconvenient, the shift is short-handed, the log is due, and nobody looks at that page until an inspector does.


This is worth grounding in real numbers rather than intuition. CDC tracks and reports on the causes of foodborne illness outbreaks nationally, and in its review of outbreak data reported from 2014 to 2022, the[CDC found that inadequate food temperatures remain a common cause of bacterial outbreaks](https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/volumes/74/ss/ss7401a1.htm) , with[inadequate hot and cold holding](https://www.cdc.gov/restaurant-food-safety/php/investigations/proliferation-contributing-factors.html) appearing again and again among contributing factors. Separately, CDC survey data on why staff skip steps points to[time pressure and short staffing](https://www.cdc.gov/restaurant-food-safety/php/practices/safe-food-preparation.html) as leading reasons. None of that data sets policy on its own, but it explains why the gap between what a log says and what actually happened is not a hypothetical risk.


A paper log cannot tell you whether a number came from a probe or from a pen. A digital check, timestamped and tied to the person who ran it, can.


## Automated corrective actions are what separate a record from a result


A log that only notes an out-of-range reading has documented a problem without solving it. The operational goal is to close the loop while the food is still recoverable, which means the response cannot depend on whichever staff member happens to be nearby or how well they remember the standard. When the corrective action is prompted automatically, timed, and tied to the reading that triggered it, the outcome stops varying by shift. This is where BOHA! Temp does its most concrete work, and the exact flow depends on what triggered the alert.


### When a piece of equipment reads out of range


Staff open the flagged unit and select Requires Action. Temp walks through Check Settings, then a wait period to let the temperature recover, then a re-check. If the reading is still out of range after that, the final step is Contact Manager or Call Service. Every step in that sequence requires a PIN sign-in, so the record shows exactly who checked the settings, who waited it out, and who made the call. Equipment monitored by BOHA! Sense feeds into this same workflow, so a fridge’s temperature drift can be caught overnight before the food spoils, becoming a Temp corrective action the moment readings fall outside of the safe range. Not fun to get this call at 1am, but much better than losing your entire inventory of ingredients.


### When food is cooling down


Staff log the final cook temperature, and a cooling timer starts, synced with the BOHA Timer App, matching the two-stage window the Food Code lays out for cooked[TCS food](https://www.transact-tech.com/resources/blog/what-are-tcs-foods) : down to 70°F within the first window, then down to 41°F within the second. At each gate, the system requires a fresh reading before it will let the process continue. Hit both windows and the record closes out as in range. Miss one, and the corrective action path opens instead.


Situation found


Typical response


Hot TCS food below 135°F, within the time window Reheat rapidly to 165°F for 15 seconds, then return to hot holding


Hot TCS food below 135°F, beyond the time window Discard and document the disposal


Cold TCS food above 41°F, within the time window Rapidly chill, or move the product to a working unit, then recheck


Cold TCS food above 41°F, beyond the time window Discard and document the disposal


Cooling stalls above 70°F at the first gate Reheat and restart cooling, or discard, per your plan


Equipment still out of range after the wait period Contact manager or call service, and log the outcome


*Every action in either flow, and every* *reading that triggered it, lands on the same daily HACCP log, with the employee, the timestamp, and a check or an “X” next to each step so a manager or an inspector can see exactly what happened and who was responsible.*

## From the walk-in to the corporate dashboard


At a single store, a digital HACCP record is a better binder. Across fifty stores, it becomes something else entirely.


There is a second payoff that shows up before the audit does. A real-time alert on an out-of-range cooler is not only a food safety signal, but it’s also an inventory signal. Catching a failing unit at the moment it drifts, rather than at the next manual check, is often the difference between moving product and discarding it. Prevention of loss and proof of compliance turn out to be the same discipline viewed from two angles.


Once food safety is digital, completion becomes a metric, and out-of-range findings become a trend line a brand can actually see: which locations are running their checks, which ones are logging corrective actions, and where a piece of equipment or a process is quietly failing across a region. That is visibility food safety and operations leaders can act on, without waiting for a store visit or a bad inspection score to find out something was wrong.


It is also how brand standards stop being a binder and start being something you can measure, so staff get to focus on service instead of a pre-inspection scramble, because the record is already built.

## **Frequently asked questions** about BOHA! Temp


### **What is BOHA! Temp?**


BOHA! Temp is a digital food safety and compliance tool that guides foodservice teams through required food safety checks, prompts corrective actions when a finding is out of range, and compiles the results into digital HACCP records. It captures temperature and pH readings through Bluetooth probes, and structures every other food safety step, from receiving to cleaning verification, into the same guided workflow.


### **What is a digital HACCP record?** ****


A digital HACCP record (or HACCP log) is the compiled, timestamped documentation of a kitchen’s food safety program: the critical control points monitored, the readings taken, the corrective actions logged, and the person accountable for each. BOHA! Temp builds these records daily and makes them available in the BOHA! Ops app, so they are ready the moment an inspector arrives.


### **Does the FDA require every restaurant to have a HACCP plan?** ****


No. Most restaurants operate under active managerial control rather than a formal written HACCP plan. A written plan is typically required only for specialized, higher-risk processes such as reduced oxygen packaging, sous vide, curing, smoking, or acidifying food like sushi rice to hold it outside refrigeration. State and local health departments determine exactly when a plan is required in their jurisdiction.


### **What is the difference between BOHA! Temp and BOHA! Checklist?** ****


They run on the same guided workflow engine, but they govern different workflow types. BOHA! Checklist runs operational tasks like maintenance, opening, and closing. BOHA! Temp runs food safety and health-code compliance tasks, and compiles the results into HACCP records. Many operations run both.


### **What corrective action does BOHA! Temp guide staff through?**


It depends on what triggered the alert. For equipment, Temp can be set up to walk staff through checking settings, waiting for the temperature to recover, and re-checking, escalating to a manager or service call if the unit is still out of range. For food, Temp times the required cooling windows and requires a fresh reading at each stage before the process can continue. Every step is signed with a PIN so the record shows who handled it.


### **Which temperature probes and pH testers does BOHA! Temp work with?** ****


BOHA! Temp works with approved Bluetooth temperature probes, including the Copeland Multi-Function Thermometer and the ThermoWorks Thermapen ONE Blue, RayTemp Blue, and TempTest Blue, plus the Hanna Instruments HALO2 wireless pH tester. It runs on our handheld or your own device, on iOS or Android.


### **How does BOHA! Temp prevent pencil-whipped records?** ****


Readings are captured through the workflow rather than written on a sheet at the end of a shift, each action requires a PIN sign-in from the person performing it, and records can be reviewed from any device. That combination makes fabricated records far harder to produce and far easier to spot.
