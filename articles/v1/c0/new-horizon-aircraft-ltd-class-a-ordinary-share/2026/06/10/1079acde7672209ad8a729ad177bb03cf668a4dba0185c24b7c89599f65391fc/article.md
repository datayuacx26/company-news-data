# Horizon targets Cavorite X7 prototype assembly in 2026

*Image: Horizon*


Ontario-based[Horizon Aircraft](https://www.horizonaircraft.com/) has set out a timeline for its flagship Cavorite X7 hybrid-electric vertical take-off and landing aircraft.


Assembly of the first full-scale prototype is planned for 2026 and initial ground and flight testing targeted for early next year.


The update was shared as part of the company’s Q2 results for the 2026 fiscal year which ended on 30 November, 2025. The results included details of Horizon’s financial position and development priorities as it advances towards full-scale aircraft testing.


Horizon Aircraft, which is developing hybrid-electric VTOL aircraft aimed at replacing helicopters in civil, emergency and military roles, said it has made “significant development progress” on the Cavorite X7. The aircraft is designed to use vertical lift only for take-off and landing, conducting most of its mission in conventional wing-borne flight.


Chief Executive Brandon Robinson said that the company had made “strong progress” in recent months on the X7 programme and is confident assembly of the first full-scale X7 prototype can be completed by the end of the year, provided major aerostructures arrive on schedule. Horizon is currently in active discussions with suppliers and large components such as the wing, fuselage, empennage and tail are expected to begin arriving over the summer and into the autumn.


In parallel, the company is continuing subsystem testing, including work on the aircraft’s vertical propulsion system, which comprises 14 ducted electric fans integrated into the wing and canards. Horizon is also preparing to begin testing its Pratt & Whitney Canada PT6 engine, configured with a pusher propeller to power forward flight and generate electricity for the wing-mounted fans.


If the programme proceeds as planned, Robinson said Horizon expects to have a full-scale prototype ready for ground testing at the beginning of 2027. Key technical challenges ahead include validation of the power electronics and battery systems, as well as development of the fly-by-wire control system.


Alongside technical progress, Horizon reported a strengthened liquidity position, ending the quarter with more than C$24 million in cash or cash equivalents. The company said this provides sufficient funding to support its development activities through 2026. During the period, Horizon was also awarded a non-dilutive grant under Canada’s Initiative for Sustainable Aviation Technology (INSAT) programme, supporting an all-weather eVTOL project valued at an estimated C$10.5 million.


The company has doubled its engineering team year on year and plans to double headcount again by the end of 2026 as work on the X7 accelerates. Last year, Horizon completed flight tests of its uncrewed, scaled Cavorite X5 demonstrator, with data from that programme feeding into the design of the full-scale aircraft.


The Cavorite X7 is designed to carry a pilot and six passengers, or a 680kg cargo payload, with a projected range of up to 430 nautical miles and a cruise speed of 250 knots. Horizon is targeting certification and entry into service before the end of the decade.
