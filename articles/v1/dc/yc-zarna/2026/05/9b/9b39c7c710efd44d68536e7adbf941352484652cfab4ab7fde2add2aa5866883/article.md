# Day in the Life of a PE Firm Running on Zarna

A $10M EBITDA multi-site specialty dermatology platform came in from Lincoln at 3:32 PM on Monday. A partner glanced at it on Tuesday and passed. At $10M it sat below the firm's $25M platform mandate, and dermatology wasn't a category the firm had a platform in or was looking to anchor one in. The read wasn't wrong. So the team moved on.


## The context the standalone screen missed


Zarna checked the same company against the firm's portfolio, prior deal work, and active add-on theses, and surfaced a fit the standalone screen missed. The firm doesn't own a dermatology platform. But it does own a multi-site medical aesthetics group, and that platform had recently decided to expand from injectables and laser into adjacent physician-led dermatology. Three weeks earlier, that portco's CFO had told the associate covering the account that he wanted Southeast density and that independent derm practices were coming to market cheaply, exactly the expansion vector the platform was planning. Zarna had logged that call in DealCloud at the time and stored the context in its own memory. It then corroborated the comment against the portco's buy-and-build M&A deck dated January '26 in SharePoint, which named Southeast dermatology as a priority adjacency, exactly this target's footprint.


## A note from months earlier


Zarna surfaced one more thing. Months before any of this, before the firm held a position in the space, a partner had hopped off a call with a banker at Houlihan and pinged Zarna on Teams with a passing thought: Southeast derm roll-ups were starting to look interesting, and this banker would be a good one to source from and learn the sector through if the firm ever moved on the space. That is the kind of context that comes out of day-to-day deal work and almost never gets captured, let alone re-surfaced when it matters. Zarna held onto it and pulled it back months later, alongside a separate deal.


## From pass to pipeline


The connection wasn't obvious. The target was a derm platform, the relevant portco was an aesthetics platform, and the link between them lived in a CFO's offhand remark, a board deck, and a months-old Teams message, none of it front-of-mind for the partner triaging a Tuesday inbound. What looked weak as a standalone platform looked valuable as an add-on for an asset the firm already owned and was actively trying to extend in exactly this direction. Zarna flagged it with the call note, the deck, and the Teams message attached, and it went back into the pipeline as an add-on candidate instead of a pass.


## Zarna didn't make the call


Zarna left the investment decision to the humans. What it changed was the quality of the information in front of them: context that either wouldn't have been documented at all, or would have taken too long to surface on a deal nobody had the bandwidth to dig into. It didn't make the call. It made sure the team was looking at the deal with the right firm context before they did.
