# 9 Shopify Alternatives for Building Your Online Store

Shopify makes it easy to launch an online store, but its true cost can grow quickly.


Transaction fees, paid apps, premium themes, and development work can add up, while template and platform limits may leave sellers wanting more control over their storefront, code, and data.


This guide compares nine genuine Shopify alternatives, from cheaper hosted platforms to open-source and build-your-own options.


We’ll look at their costs, limitations, technical requirements, ownership models, and best use cases to help you find the right fit for your store.


## **Why Sellers Look for a Shopify Alternative**


Shopify works well for sellers who want to launch quickly without managing hosting, security, or checkout infrastructure themselves. The reasons people start looking for cheaper alternatives to Shopify usually become clearer as sales, app requirements, and customization needs grow.


The most common cost concerns are:


- **Transaction fees:** On the US Basic plan, standard online card rates start at 2.9% plus 30¢. Sellers using an outside payment provider can also pay a 2% Shopify transaction fee on top of that provider’s processing charges.
- **Paid apps:** Reviews, subscriptions, upsells, advanced shipping, and other functions may require separate tools. For example, Recharge Starter costs $99 per month plus 1.49% and 19¢ per transaction, while Loox Convert costs $49.99 per month. Those two plans alone total nearly $150 per month before other apps are added.


Shopify’s current pricing confirms the additional third-party transaction fee, while its App Store shows how quickly individual subscriptions can stack up.


Merchants raise the same concern in public discussions. In a[Shopify Community thread](https://community.shopify.com/t/too-many-shortcomings-requiring-too-many-apps/181229) about app dependence, one seller described repeatedly paying “$5 to $15 a month” for small functions they believed should be built in.


An app developer offered the other side: apps can be more practical than hiring a developer, although managing too many can become a “maintenance nightmare.”


Control is the other major issue. Sellers may eventually want more freedom over:


- Storefront layouts and branding
- Checkout and custom product workflows
- Backend functionality and integrations
- Their code, hosting, and ability to move providers


Shopify themes can be customized, but more specialized functionality may require Liquid development, an app, or custom development. Shopify’s plan comparison currently lists checkout customization as limited on Basic, Grow, and Advanced, with full checkout customization offered on Plus.


The[r/ecommerce thread](https://www.reddit.com/r/ecommerce/comments/1jb8fi6/those_of_you_that_dont_use_shopify_what_do_you_use/) on Shopify alternatives reflects both sides of the decision.


Some merchants favor WooCommerce for deeper customization, control over hosting, and the ability to move their data between providers. Others argue that Shopify remains cost-effective because its simplicity lets new sellers focus on making sales rather than maintaining a website.


Migration can still create friction.


Shopify lets merchants export products and orders and download theme files, but a theme download does not include products, pages, blog posts, images, or other store content. Moving platforms may therefore require transferring several datasets, replacing apps, and rebuilding parts of the storefront.


None of this makes Shopify a poor choice, but alternatives become more appealing when lower recurring costs, custom functionality, or greater ownership matter more than the convenience of a managed platform.


## **How We Evaluated These Shopify Alternatives**


We compared each platform using the factors that matter most once a store moves beyond the launch stage:


- **Real cost:** Monthly plans, platform transaction fees, required apps, hosting, and other add-ons
- **Ownership:** How much control sellers have over their store, code, and data
- **Customization:** How far the storefront, checkout, and backend workflows can be changed
- **Ease of use:** The technical skill and setup time required
- **Scalability:** How well the platform handles more traffic, products, integrations, and operational complexity
- **Support:** The quality of documentation, customer service, and available developer help


There is no single best choice among Shopify competitors.


A hosted builder may be the right fit for a seller who values speed and simplicity, while an open-source or build-your-own platform may suit a business that needs greater flexibility and ownership.


The best alternative depends on the store’s current size, technical resources, budget, and long-term goals.


## **The 9 Best Shopify Alternatives**


The best alternatives to Shopify range from simple hosted builders to open-source platforms with much more flexibility.


Some are designed for sellers who want a faster or cheaper setup, while others are better suited to businesses that need custom functionality, stronger design control, or greater ownership.


The options below are reviewed using the same criteria: features, ease of use, pricing, transaction fees, ownership, scalability, and overall fit.


This makes it easier to compare not just what each platform offers, but which type of seller it is actually built for.


## **1. BigCommerce**


**Best for:** growing business-to-consumer (B2C) or business-to-business (B2B) stores that need more commerce features built into the platform.


BigCommerce is a hosted software-as-a-service (SaaS) platform that manages hosting, security, checkout, and software updates. It supports standard storefronts, headless builds, B2B selling, and multiple stores from one account, making it suitable for established retailers, wholesalers, and multi-brand businesses.


**Key features:**


- Customizable themes and a drag-and-drop page builder
- Product, order, and multi-location inventory management
- One-page checkout and support for multiple payment providers
- Marketplace and social-channel integrations
- Multi-Storefront controls for different brands or regions
- B2B tools such as company accounts, price lists, and quotes


**Ease of use:** A standard store can be launched without coding. Advanced theme changes, unusual product workflows, and headless storefronts generally require technical knowledge or developer support.


**What users like:** BigCommerce has a 4.2/5 rating from 579 G2 reviews, with users commonly praising its built-in features, integrations, and ability to manage complex catalogs. Its Trustpilot rating is lower at 1.2/5 from 455 reviews, where complaints often focus on billing, price increases, and customer support.


**Limitations:** The admin interface and page builder can feel less intuitive than simpler hosted platforms. Stores are also moved to higher-priced plans when they cross certain sales thresholds.


**Pricing and fees:** Core starts at $39 monthly or $29 per month annually. Growth starts at $105 or $79, while Scale starts at $399 or $299. BigCommerce does not add transaction fees when an embedded payment provider is used, but outside providers can trigger fees of 2%, 1%, or 0.6%, depending on the plan.


**Ownership and scalability:** Merchants can export their data and download theme code, but the commerce backend remains hosted by BigCommerce. The platform can support large catalogs, multiple channels, B2B operations, and regional storefronts.


**Best fit:** Established sellers that need stronger native B2B, catalog, and multi-store capabilities.


**Why it is a good Shopify alternative:** BigCommerce can reduce dependence on paid apps by including more advanced commerce functions natively, although its revenue-based pricing remains an important tradeoff.


## **2. WooCommerce**


**Best for:** sellers who want full control and are comfortable managing WordPress.


WooCommerce is a free, open-source ecommerce plugin for self-hosted WordPress sites. Merchants choose hosting, themes, payment providers, and extensions while retaining control of the store’s code and data.


**Key features:**


- Product, inventory, order, tax, and shipping management
- Customizable WordPress themes and checkout
- More than 140 payment gateway integrations
- Support for physical products, downloads, subscriptions, and bookings
- Extensions for marketing, analytics, and multichannel selling


**Ease of use:** Installing WooCommerce on WordPress is straightforward, but setup is more involved than Shopify. Merchants must manage hosting, backups, security, updates, and plugin compatibility or pay someone to do it.


**What users like:** WooCommerce has a 4.4/5 rating from 1,270 G2 reviews, with users praising its customization, WordPress integration, and extension ecosystem. Its Trustpilot rating is 2.3/5 from 141 reviews, where complaints often concern support, technical issues, and paid extensions.


**Limitations:** Plugin conflicts, updates, security, and performance require active management. Costs can rise when a store needs premium extensions, stronger hosting, or developer help. Reddit sellers also praise its flexibility while warning about the learning curve.


**Pricing and fees:** The core plugin is free, making it a free Shopify alternative. Hosting, domains, themes, and extensions cost extra. WooCommerce adds no platform transaction fee; US WooPayments card processing is 2.9% plus 30¢.


**Ownership and scalability:** Merchants own the code and data and can move between hosts. WooCommerce can support complex stores, but production readiness depends on hosting, optimization, and plugin quality.


**Best fit:** WordPress users and technical teams that value customization and ownership.


**Why it is a good Shopify alternative:** WooCommerce removes platform lock-in and per-sale platform fees, but shifts more technical responsibility to the seller.


## **3. Wix**


**Best for:** small stores that want a fast, flexible no-code launch.


Wix is a hosted SaaS website builder with built-in ecommerce, hosting, security, and business tools. It suits beginners and small businesses that want more design freedom than a rigid storefront template without managing infrastructure.


**Key features:**


- Drag-and-drop editor, AI builder, and 2,000+ templates
- Product, inventory, order, shipping, and tax management
- Abandoned-cart recovery and back-in-stock alerts
- Selling through Amazon, eBay, Etsy, TikTok, and social channels
- Wix Payments, PayPal, Stripe, and 80+ regional payment providers
- Online and in-person inventory syncing through Wix point-of-sale (POS)


**Ease of use:** A basic store can be launched without code, although the number of editor options may initially feel overwhelming. Advanced custom functions can be added through Wix’s developer tools.


**What users like:** Wix has a 4.2/5 rating from 1,871 G2 reviews, where users commonly praise its ease of use and broad feature set. Its Trustpilot score is 3.5/5 from 28,486 reviews, with positive feedback on design tools but recurring complaints about billing and support.


**Limitations:** Wix can support substantial catalogs, but complex B2B operations and highly customized checkout workflows may outgrow its standard tools. A Wix website also cannot be hosted outside Wix because it relies on proprietary platform technology.


**Pricing and fees:** Ecommerce begins on the Core plan at roughly $29 per month on annual billing. Business is about $39 and Business Elite about $159, although pricing varies by location. Wix charges no separate platform transaction fee; standard US card processing through Wix Payments is 2.9% plus 30¢.


**Ownership and scalability:** Merchants own their content and can export store data, but not the complete working website. Wix is production-ready for small and midsize stores, with enterprise options for larger organizations.


**Best fit:** New or growing sellers that want design flexibility without technical maintenance.


**Why it is a good Shopify alternative:** Wix offers a simpler design experience and no added platform transaction fee while still providing core ecommerce and multichannel tools.


## **4. Squarespace**


**Best for:** design-led brands with a focused product catalog.


Squarespace is a hosted SaaS website builder known for polished templates and strong visual presentation. Its ecommerce tools are built into the same system as its website, blogging, scheduling, invoicing, and marketing features.


**Key features:**


- Professionally designed, mobile-responsive templates
- Drag-and-drop storefront and product-page editing
- Unlimited physical products and services
- Inventory, order, discount, and customer-account tools
- Abandoned-checkout recovery and product subscriptions
- Instagram and Facebook promotion, POS, and Google Shopping support


**Ease of use:** Squarespace is one of the easier platforms for creating a professional-looking store without code. Its editor is approachable for beginners, although unusual layouts or custom commerce workflows may require Cascading Style Sheets (CSS), code injection, or extensions.


**What users like:** Squarespace has a 4.4/5 rating from 1,132 G2 reviews, with users praising its templates and ease of use. Its Trustpilot score is 3.3/5 from 4,369 reviews, where experiences with support are mixed and some users report billing or customization frustrations.


**Limitations:** Squarespace offers less backend flexibility and fewer deep commerce integrations than Shopify. Stores can accept only one currency at a time, and the platform is less suitable for large, operationally complex catalogs.


**Pricing and fees:** Current US annual pricing starts at $19 per month for Basic, followed by Core at $29, Plus at $49, and Advanced at $99. Basic adds a 2% online-store transaction fee, while Core, Plus, and Advanced charge 0%. Payment-processing fees still apply. Squarespace has no permanent free plan, but it offers a 14-day trial.


**Ownership and scalability:** Products and orders can be exported, but some product images, reviews, and other details do not transfer through the standard comma-separated values (CSV) file. The storefront and backend remain tied to Squarespace.


**Best fit:** Creators and lifestyle brands that prioritize presentation over complex commerce operations.


**Why it is a good Shopify alternative:** Squarespace combines attractive design and commerce tools with less app dependence, making it a strong choice for smaller, brand-focused stores.


## **5. Webflow**


**Best for:** design-focused teams that want precise control over the storefront experience.


Webflow is a hosted visual-development platform with ecommerce functionality. It lets designers build custom storefronts, carts, and checkout pages without being confined to a conventional theme, while still offering access to custom Hypertext Markup Language (HTML), CSS, and JavaScript.


**Key features:**


- Visual control over product pages, carts, and checkout
- Product, category, variant, inventory, and order management
- Physical, digital, service, and membership products
- Stripe, PayPal, Apple Pay, and browser-based payments
- Custom shipping rules and automatic tax calculations
- Content management system (CMS), animations, search engine optimization (SEO) controls, and custom-code support


**Ease of use:** Webflow provides more design freedom than most no-code builders, but it has a steeper learning curve. Sellers unfamiliar with web layout concepts such as containers, classes, breakpoints, and responsive design may need training or professional help.


**What users like:** Webflow has a 4.4/5 rating from 984 G2 reviews, with users frequently praising its design flexibility, CMS, and ability to translate design intent into a working site. Its Trustpilot score is 1.5/5 from 228 reviews, where recent complaints focus on pricing, complexity, bugs, and support.


**Limitations:** Webflow’s native commerce tools are less extensive than Shopify’s for multichannel sales, advanced inventory, and complex retail operations. Product limits also range from 500 to 15,000 items depending on the plan.


**Pricing and fees:** Standard costs $29 per month annually and adds a 2% Webflow transaction fee. Plus costs $74 and Advanced costs $212, with no platform transaction fee on either. Stripe or PayPal processing charges apply separately.


**Ownership and scalability:** Paid Workspace users can export HTML, CSS, JavaScript, and assets, but ecommerce databases, products, checkout, and other commerce functions are excluded. Products can be exported separately by CSV, so moving a complete functioning store still requires rebuilding the backend.


**Best fit:** Design-led brands with relatively straightforward commerce requirements.


**Why it is a good Shopify alternative:** Webflow offers much deeper visual control than a standard Shopify theme, but trades some operational commerce depth for that freedom.
