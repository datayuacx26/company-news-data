# Singapore National Day 2026: How Moving Forward and Going Beyond Can Begin at Home

Last Updated on August 5, 2026 byYolly Del Prado


On 9 August 2026, Singapore will celebrate its 61st National Day under the theme “ **Majulah Singapura, Go Beyond!** ” “ *Majulah Singapura* ” reflects our enduring aspiration to keep Singapore moving forward, while “ *Go Beyond!* ” calls on us to do better for Singaporeans and show greater care for one another and the wider community.


At home, these two ideas are closely connected. Moving forward means preparing for the ways our households, finances and needs may change. Going beyond means making those choices with consideration for the people who share our homes and neighbourhoods.


These choices may seem small, but together they influence whether[our homes and neighbourhoods](https://ohmyhome.com/en-sg/blog/13-types-of-neighbours-every-hdb-resident-has-had/?utm_source=blog&utm_medium=backlink&utm_campaign=SGND2026GoBeyond) remain welcoming, inclusive and ready for the years ahead.


So, what could moving forward and going beyond look like at home?


## 1. Choose a neighbourhood that works for the people you love


Thinking ahead about how your household’s needs may change is one way to care for the people closest to you. When choosing a home, that means looking at the neighbourhood through the eyes of those who will share it with you.


A flat might look close to an MRT station on a map, but try walking the actual route. Are there stairs, unsheltered paths or long stretches without anywhere to rest? A journey that feels manageable to you could be much harder for a young child, an older parent or a family member with limited mobility.


As you explore the estate, ask:


- Can your family reach public transport safely?
- Are shops and healthcare services within a comfortable distance?
- Are the main routes sheltered and step-free?
- Is there somewhere to rest along longer walks?
- Can children and older family members get around independently?
- Would the neighbourhood still suit your family if someone’s mobility changed?


The right neighbourhood should work for your whole household in everyday life. Seeing it from your loved ones’ perspective can help you choose a home where they feel safe, comfortable and able to get around with confidence.


## 2. Make room for care at home


In land-scarce Singapore, making good use of the space available is part of everyday life. A flexible layout can help your home adapt as your household changes.


Think about what each person in your household needs. Your child may need a quiet corner to study, while an older parent may find clear walkways easier to navigate. If you work from home, you may also need more privacy during the day.


If you are planning to buy a home, picture how your family would use each part of the property before deciding whether the layout is suitable. Consider:


- How much of the floor area is genuinely usable?
- Can the bedrooms accommodate the furniture you need?
- Is there enough privacy for work, rest and study?
- Could the layout adapt if a child or parent moves in?
- Will the home remain manageable as members of your household grow older?


If you already own a home, consider whether moving furniture, improving storage or repurposing an area could make the layout work better.

In either case, you may not need a larger property or a costly renovation. Using the space thoughtfully can help everyone under the same roof live more safely and comfortably.


## 3. Care for the spaces your community shares


Moving forward together also involves taking responsibility for the places we share. In a condo or HDB estate, this includes the corridors, lifts, playgrounds, parks and other common areas that residents use every day.


These spaces serve people with different routines and needs. To understand where greater consideration may be needed, look at:


- Whether common areas are clean and well maintained
- How much noise and foot traffic there is
- Whether shared facilities cater to different age groups
- How residents use the communal spaces
- Whether your household uses these areas considerately


You may not control how these spaces are designed or managed, but you can still play a part by keeping access routes clear, disposing of bulky items properly and reporting damaged facilities. Using common areas considerately helps keep them safe and comfortable for the whole community.


## 4. Choose a home that leaves room for life


Homeownership has long been closely associated with progress and stability in Singapore. For your household, however, that progress should not come at the cost of constant financial pressure.


Collecting the keys may be a proud milestone, but monthly repayments will shape what your family can afford for years to come. Before buying, ask:


- Would repayments remain manageable if your income changed?
- Will you retain enough savings for emergencies and retirement?
- Does the home suit everyone who will live there?
- Could its lease, Minimum Occupation Period (MOP) or resale conditions affect future plans?


Using CPF savings for housing can also affect the amount available for retirement. If you are considering a Plus or Prime flat, take time to understand the tighter conditions, including the 10-year MOP and the prohibition on renting out the whole flat.


A home should give your household a stronger foundation for the future, with enough financial breathing room to handle changing needs and care for the people who depend on you.

Here is a quick look at how you can put those ideas into practice in your home and neighbourhood:

Inside the home Use the available space and layout to support everyone’s needs today while allowing the home to adapt as your household changes.

Moving forward and going beyond do not always require grand gestures. Both can be reflected in how you choose a neighbourhood, share your home, treat communal spaces and make financial decisions for your family. When these everyday choices also make life better for the people around you, progress becomes something you share.

## Frequently Asked Questions


### Can I display the Singapore Flag outside my home in 2026?


Yes. Households may display the National Flag at residential premises from 1 July to 30 September 2026. During this period, it may be flown without a flagpole and displayed at night without illumination. The Flag must remain respectful, undamaged and unaltered.


### How can I take part in the “Go Beyond!” initiatives for NDP 2026?


You can participate through official NDP 2026 initiatives such as Give As One SG, Majulah Hunt, SG Flag Giveaways and Majulah Moment. These activities offer different ways to volunteer, celebrate and connect with others. Check the[official NDP website](https://www.ndp.gov.sg/going-beyond/) for participation details and updates.


### Where will the Singapore National Day Parade 2026 be held?


The Singapore National Day Parade 2026 will take place at the National Stadium. The official NDP website provides the show guide, venue map and latest advisories. Anyone attending or visiting the Kallang area should check for access and traffic updates before leaving home.


### Can I place National Day decorations along an HDB corridor?


National Day decorations must not obstruct escape routes or create fire hazards. SCDF requires a clear passage of at least 1.2m along common corridors, while staircases and landings must remain free of objects. Residents should also check their Town Council’s guidelines before decorating shared areas.
