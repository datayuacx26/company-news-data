# Most Surveyed Million-Dollar HDB Buyers Paid No COV: What Does It Mean?

A million-dollar HDB flat may sound like a home sold at an exceptional premium. Yet in a PropNex survey of its salespersons covering 110 such transactions completed in 2025, 69.1% of buyers reportedly paid no Cash-Over-Valuation (COV).


In other words, their agreed prices did not exceed HDB’s value of the flats. This challenges the assumption that most seven-figure HDB transactions depend on buyers paying large cash premiums above valuation.


It does not, however, settle the affordability question. A price may be supported by prevailing market evidence and still be beyond what many households can comfortably afford.


## The Survey in Context


The survey, published on 17 July 2026, covered flats sold for between $1 million and approximately $1.659 million.


Most of these flats were reportedly sold without COV:


**COV reported** **Share of surveyed transactions**


No COV 69.1%


Below $40,000 11.8%


$40,000 to below $80,000 6.4%


$80,000 to $100,000 3.6%


Above $100,000 5.5%


Unknown 3.6%


The transactions were not limited to executive or jumbo flats. Four-room flats accounted for 42.7% of the sample, followed by five-room flats at 35.5%, executive flats at 20% and jumbo flats at 1.8%.


Many were in established towns such as Toa Payoh, Bukit Merah, Kallang/Whampoa, Queenstown and Clementi. According to the salespersons surveyed, buyers were most attracted by the particular town or neighbourhood, proximity to an MRT station and a high-floor position.


Within this sample,[school proximity](https://ohmyhome.com/en-sg/blog/check-out-which-primary-schools-are-near-you/?utm_source=blog&utm_medium=backlink&utm_campaign=%241MHDBNoCOV) and the physical condition of the flat ranked below those factors. This should not be read as evidence that schools or renovation have little influence across the wider HDB resale market. The responses show what these particular buyers prioritised; they do not establish how much each attribute contributed to the eventual valuation.


## How Representative Is the 69.1% Figure?


The survey offers rare insight into who bought these flats and why, but it is not market-wide evidence of the COV paid across all million-dollar HDB transactions.


It covered 110 transactions handled by salespersons from one property agency. MND reported that[1,594 HDB flats sold for more than $1 million in 2025](https://www.mnd.gov.sg/newsroom/parliament-matters/q-as/view/oral-answer-by-ministry-of-national-development-on-hdb-resale-flat-prices) . The surveyed transactions therefore represented only a portion of the seven-figure resale segment.


Some responses were also incomplete. The COV amount was unknown in 3.6% of the surveyed deals, while household income was unknown or undisclosed in 25.5% of cases.


The finding should therefore be stated precisely: most buyers across the 110 transactions surveyed reportedly paid no COV. The survey cannot establish whether the same proportion applied to all million-dollar HDB deals completed in 2025.


## What Does No COV Actually Tell Us?


Cash-Over-Valuation is the difference between the agreed resale price and HDB’s value of the flat when the agreed price is higher. If the price does not exceed HDB’s value, the buyer pays no COV.


Consider two flats sold at the same price:


**Flat A** **Flat B**


**Agreed resale price** $1,050,000 $1,050,000


**HDB’s value of the flat** $1,050,000 $1,000,000


**COV** $0 $50,000


The headline price is identical, but the financing position is not.


Under HDB’s current financing rules, the maximum Loan-to-Value limit is generally 75% of the lower of the resale price or HDB’s value of the flat, subject to the buyer’s eligibility, credit assessment and the flat’s remaining lease.


Using that limit purely for illustration:


**Flat A** **Flat B**


**Amount used for 75% LTV calculation** $1,050,000 $1,000,000


**Illustrative maximum loan** $787,500 $750,000


**Balance not covered by that loan** $262,500 $300,000


**COV payable in cash** $0 $50,000


The balance not covered by the illustrative loan may be funded using eligible CPF savings and cash. The $50,000 COV for Flat B, however, must be paid entirely in cash.


These are simplified examples, not estimates of what an individual buyer can borrow. The actual loan may be lower after HDB or the financial institution considers the buyer’s eligibility, income, existing financial commitments, loan tenure and the flat’s remaining lease.


## Does No COV Mean the Buyer Did Not Overpay?


No COV only tells us that the agreed price did not exceed HDB’s value of the flat. It does not answer whether the home was affordable for that household or whether it will command the same price in future.


Before deciding whether a valuation-supported price is reasonable, a buyer still needs to answer three separate questions:


**1. What is the flat valued at?**


This affects how much of the purchase may be financed through a housing loan and eligible CPF savings.


**2. Can the household comfortably afford it?**


This depends on income, existing debt, available cash and CPF savings, future expenses and the amount left after completing the purchase.


**3. Is the flat worth that amount to the household?**


A buyer may place substantial value on being near family, shortening a daily commute or obtaining enough space to avoid another move.


These answers will differ between households. A family planning to remain in a well-connected five-room flat for decades may place considerable value on space, convenience and avoiding another move. Someone expecting to sell again within a few years has to give greater weight to transaction costs, the remaining lease and the likely future buyer pool.


A current valuation is evidence of prevailing market conditions. It is not a promise that another buyer will pay the same amount later.


## Who Is Paying Seven Figures for an HDB Flat?


The most useful reading of the survey is not that million-dollar HDB flats have become ordinary. It is that this segment serves several types of buyers whose circumstances are not interchangeable.


### First-Time Buyers in Their 30s


Buyers aged 30 to 39 and those aged 40 to 49 collectively accounted for approximately 71% of the transactions surveyed.


Among buyers aged 30 to 39, about four in 10 were reportedly purchasing their first home. Their purchases included four-room, five-room, executive and jumbo flats in locations such as Bukit Merah, Kallang/Whampoa, Queenstown and Toa Payoh.


A resale flat at this price may offer these buyers immediate availability, an established location or more space than some of the private properties available within the same budget. The trade-off is entering homeownership with a substantial financial commitment and potentially less flexibility for other goals.


### HDB Owners Moving to a Larger Flat


Among surveyed buyers aged 40 to 49, 46.3% reportedly moved from a smaller HDB flat. They mainly purchased five-room and executive flats priced from $1 million to $1.38 million.


For these households, moving to a larger HDB flat may provide the space they need while allowing them to remain in their preferred property type. Their usable budget, however, should be based on the net proceeds from their existing home rather than its headline selling price.


### Older Buyers Moving from Private Property


Among surveyed buyers aged 60 and above, 71.4% had reportedly moved from a private home. They bought four-room flats priced from $1 million to $1.328 million.


For these buyers, moving to an HDB flat may allow them to release equity while choosing a home that better suits their current needs and preferred location. A retiree may therefore have substantial property wealth even without a high monthly income.


The flat’s remaining lease is still important. Under HDB’s current financing rules, CPF usage and the maximum housing loan may be affected if the lease cannot cover the youngest buyer to age 95.


## Why Do Some HDB Flats Command Million-dollar Prices?


Million-dollar HDBs usually emerge when several hard-to-replicate attributes appear in the same flat. A buyer may be comparing not simply “a four-room HDB flat”, but a high-floor unit near an MRT station in a preferred town, with sufficient remaining lease and few comparable units available.


Scarcity changes how the evidence should be read. A standard four-room flat may have several recent comparable transactions, while a jumbo flat or executive maisonette may have very few. A town’s median resale price is therefore less useful when the unit itself is unusual.


The same caution applies within one development. A record sale for a high-floor unit with an open view should not automatically determine the price of a lower-floor flat facing another block.


This is why neither buyers nor sellers should rely on the highest transaction in a town or development without examining how closely the flats match.


## The Cash Requirement Buyers May Miss


Zero COV improves the buyer’s financing position, but it does not make a million-dollar purchase light on upfront funds. The buyer must still cover the option deposit, any portion not financed through the housing loan, Buyer’s Stamp Duty, legal fees and other purchase costs. Renovation and immediate repairs sit outside the purchase price entirely.


Buyer’s Stamp Duty is calculated using the purchase price or market value, whichever is higher. Based on current IRAS residential property rates, BSD is $24,600 on a $1 million purchase and $26,600 on a $1.05 million purchase.


Loan-servicing rules also apply. MAS caps the[Mortgage Servicing Ratio](https://ohmyhome.com/en-sg/blog/mortgage-101-what-mortgage-servicing-ratio-msr-and-total-debt-servicing-ratio-tdsr/?utm_source=blog&utm_medium=backlink&utm_campaign=%241MHDBNoCOV) at 30% of a borrower’s gross monthly income for applicable HDB housing loans, while the Total Debt Servicing Ratio is generally capped at 55%.


Meeting these regulatory limits only shows that the financing falls within the applicable thresholds; it does not account for every demand on the household’s income. Childcare, retirement savings, insurance and other ongoing commitments still need to be considered.


Buyers should also avoid using their entire cash buffer to absorb a valuation shortfall. A resale flat may require immediate repairs or renovation, while COV cannot be covered through a larger housing loan.


## Can Buyers Know the Value Before Making an Offer?


Buyers cannot submit a Request for Value before the seller grants them an Option to Purchase.


Under the[HDB resale process](https://ohmyhome.com/en-sg/blog/singaporeans-guide-buying-your-first-resale-hdb-flat/?utm_source=blog&utm_medium=backlink&utm_campaign=%241MHDBNoCOV) , the buyer and seller first negotiate and agree on a price. The seller then grants the OTP, which remains valid for 21 calendar days. Once the OTP has been granted, the buyer or their salesperson may submit the Request for Value.


This means buyers make their offer without certainty that HDB’s value will match it.


Buyers cannot eliminate the possibility of a valuation shortfall, but they can make a better-informed offer by studying completed transactions for flats with a similar:


- Development or immediate location
- Flat type and model
- Floor area
- Storey range
- Remaining lease
- Orientation and other distinctive attributes


HDB provides a resale flat price service for checking recent transactions. The official[data.gov.sg resale dataset](https://data.gov.sg/datasets/d_8b84c4ee58e3cfc0ece0d773c8ca6abc/view) cautions that recorded prices are indicative because the amount agreed between a buyer and seller depends on multiple factors.


Before negotiating, set two limits: the highest total price you are prepared to pay and the maximum valuation shortfall you can fund in cash. The second limit may require you to walk away from a flat even when the overall asking price initially appears affordable.


## Three Checks Before Making an Offer


### 1. Are the Comparisons Genuinely Similar?


Start with completed transactions, not current listings. Asking prices show what sellers hope to receive; they do not prove that buyers or HDB’s value will support those amounts.


Compare flats of the same model and floor-area range before accounting for storey, remaining lease, orientation and unusual features. A transaction from a substantially different layout or floor level may indicate demand without providing a reliable price benchmark.


### 2. What Will the Purchase Leave Behind?


Calculate what will remain after the downpayment, stamp duty, legal fees, renovation and any COV. A buyer who qualifies for the loan may still be stretching too far if completing the purchase consumes most of the household’s available savings.


The purchase should leave room for emergencies and future plans rather than using every available dollar to secure the unit.


### 3. How Long Will the Flat Continue to Meet Your Needs?


Paying more for location or space can be reasonable when those benefits will serve the household for many years. Buyers expecting to move again relatively soon should place greater weight on transaction costs, remaining lease and whether the future buyer pool is likely to support the same premium.


## What Do the Findings Mean for Sellers?


The survey shows that buyers will pay seven-figure prices for some HDB flats without necessarily paying above valuation. Sellers should not interpret this as support for any premium asking price.


Start with completed transactions for the same flat model and floor-area range, then account for differences in storey, remaining lease, orientation and unit-specific features. A record sale from a larger layout or substantially higher floor may demonstrate demand without providing a reliable benchmark for another unit.


Sellers should also remember that expecting a large COV narrows the buyer pool. Because COV must be paid in cash, even buyers who can support the monthly mortgage may be unable or unwilling to fund a significant valuation shortfall.


[Renovation can improve presentation and attract interest](https://ohmyhome.com/en-sg/blog/how-renovate-resale-hdb-flat-timeline-procedure/?utm_source=blog&utm_medium=backlink&utm_campaign=%241MHDBNoCOV) , but sellers should not assume that every dollar spent will be reflected in the selling price or HDB’s value of the flat.


## Million-Dollar Flats Are a Minority, but No Longer Anomalies


MND reported that 1,594 flats sold for more than $1 million in 2025, representing approximately 6% of HDB resale transactions that year.


Both figures matter. The 6% share confirms that seven-figure flats were not representative of the typical resale deal. Yet 1,594 transactions are too numerous to dismiss as isolated records. Million-dollar flats now form a recognisable segment within the HDB resale market.


The survey report also recorded 1,002 HDB flats sold for at least $1 million between 1 January and 16 July 2026.


This does not establish a new price baseline for every HDB town or flat type. It shows that particular combinations of location, connectivity, floor area, remaining lease and scarcity can support seven-figure prices, even as the affordability of such homes remains a separate question.


## What This Means for Buyers and Sellers


The survey challenges the idea that most million-dollar HDB deals are driven by buyers paying large sums above valuation. Across the 110 transactions studied, 69.1% reportedly involved no COV.


Buyers should treat that as market context, not reassurance that a seven-figure purchase is automatically sound. The stronger test is whether comparable transactions support the price, the household can complete the purchase without exhausting its financial buffer, and the flat will continue to meet its needs over the intended holding period.


For sellers, the growth of this segment creates an opportunity only where the unit’s attributes and completed comparable sales support the asking price.

## Frequently Asked Questions


### Can COV Be Paid Using CPF or a Housing Loan?


No. COV must be paid in cash. The maximum housing loan is calculated using the lower of the resale price or HDB’s value of the flat, subject to the buyer’s eligibility and other financing conditions.


### Why Can a Million-Dollar HDB Flat Have No COV?


A million-dollar flat has no COV when its agreed price does not exceed HDB’s value of the flat. In the survey, town or neighbourhood preference, MRT proximity and a high-floor position were the attributes buyers most frequently prioritised.


### How Can Buyers Reduce the Risk of COV?


Review completed transactions for closely comparable flats and set a maximum cash shortfall before negotiating. Do not base an offer solely on asking prices or an exceptional record sale. The risk cannot be removed entirely because a Request for Value is submitted only after the seller grants an OTP.
