# New CEA Property Agent Requirements From 2027: What You Need to Know

Last Updated on August 3, 2026 byYolly Del Prado


Whether you’re[buying a new home](https://ohmyhome.com/en-sg/buyer-form/?utm_source=blog&utm_medium=clickthru&utm_campaign=CEAAgentRequirements2027) ,[selling your property](https://ohmyhome.com/en-sg/property-agent-services/seller/book/address/?utm_source=blog&utm_medium=clickthru&utm_campaign=CEAAgentRequirements2027) , or[renting a space](https://ohmyhome.com/en-sg/tenant-form/?utm_source=blog&utm_medium=clickthru&utm_campaign=CEAAgentRequirements2027) , you want the agent guiding you to understand how the market works today, not rely on how it worked several years ago.


So to help property agents keep their practical knowledge current, the Council for Estate Agencies (CEA) is introducing a new requirement. From 1 January 2027, agents will need to complete at least three qualifying property transactions within their three-year registration period or pass a Refresher Examination before they can renew their registration.


For buyers and sellers, this adds a useful minimum standard. But does it mean you can simply look at an agent’s transaction count and know they’re right for you? Not quite.


Here’s what is changing, how the requirement will work and what you should still consider when choosing someone to guide you through your next property move.


## New Property Agent Requirements at a Glance


Here’s a quick overview of the key CEA property agent requirement changes from 2027:


**What is changing?** **Details**


Effective date 1 January 2027


Registration period Three years instead of one


Currency Requirement At least three qualifying transactions during the applicable period or passing the Refresher Examination


First cycle for existing agents 1 January 2027 to 31 December 2029


Annual CPD Continues separately at 16 hours a year


Existing agents do not need to meet the Currency Requirement before entering the first cycle. They will work towards it during the three-year period. Moving from annual to three-year registrations should also mean less renewal paperwork for agents and property agencies.


## **How Does the Currency Requirement Work?**


Agents can meet the requirement in one of two ways:


### **1. Complete at Least Three Qualifying Transactions**


An agent registered throughout the full three-year cycle must complete at least three qualifying transactions during that period.


This doesn’t mean they need to complete one transaction every year. They simply need to reach the required total by the end of their registration cycle.


Looking at activity over three years also gives agents some breathing room. Property transactions don’t all move at the same pace, and some can take much longer than others.


According to CEA, this timeframe gives agents flexibility without putting them under unnecessary pressure to transact.


### **2. Pass the Refresher Examination**


If an agent doesn’t complete the required number of transactions, there’s still another route available. They can meet the Currency Requirement by passing the Refresher Examination.


CEA has said that the examination will focus on the practice-related knowledge agents need for estate agency work. Agents who fall below the transaction threshold will also be guaranteed a place to sit for it.


Details such as the format, fee, syllabus, passing mark and retake arrangements haven’t been announced yet. CEA expects to share more information in the first half of 2029, so it’s best to wait for the official details rather than rely on speculation.


## **What Counts as a Qualifying Property Transaction?**


Qualifying transactions include:


- HDB resale and rental transactions
- Private residential sale, resale and rental transactions
- Commercial sale, resale and rental transactions
- Industrial sale, resale and rental transactions
- Foreign property sale transactions
- En bloc transactions


For most residential transactions, CEA will recognise one agent on each represented side of a completed deal.


Commercial, industrial and en bloc transactions may be handled differently because they can involve larger teams and much longer timelines. In these cases, CEA may recognise more than one agent on a represented side if the property agency submits supporting documents. Each case will be assessed individually.


## **What About New Property Agents?**


New agents will have some time to find their footing.


Anyone entering the industry from 1 January 2027 will receive a registration beginning on their date of entry and ending on 31 December of the third year.


For example, an agent who joins on 15 February 2028 will be registered until 31 December 2030.


They won’t need to meet the Currency Requirement during their first calendar year. From the second year onwards, it will apply on a pro-rated basis.


Using CEA’s current example, a new agent must complete at least two qualifying transactions during the remaining two years or pass the Refresher Examination.


## **Can Agents Ask for an Exception?**


CEA has said that agents facing extenuating circumstances can ask for their case to be reviewed. Serious illness and unusually complex transactions are among the examples given.


This is helpful because transaction frequency isn’t always entirely within an agent’s control. An en bloc, industrial or high-value commercial deal can involve months of work without being completed within the expected period.


However, exceptions won’t be granted automatically. Agents will need to provide supporting information, and CEA will assess each case individually.


## **What Happens If an Agent Does Not Meet Either Requirement?**


Completing fewer than three transactions doesn’t automatically mean an agent has to leave the industry. They can still remain eligible for renewal by passing the Refresher Examination.


If an agent completes neither the required transactions nor the examination, they won’t be able to renew their registration.


Should they decide to return later, they’ll need to complete the Real Estate Salesperson course and pass the Real Estate Salesperson Examination again.


## **Why is CEA Introducing This Requirement?**


Property rules don’t stand still. Financing considerations, eligibility conditions and administrative processes can all change, sometimes with a direct impact on a household’s plans.


If an agent has spent several years away from active property work, there may be questions about whether their knowledge still reflects the current market.


That matters when you’re making decisions such as whether you’re eligible to buy a particular home, how to finance it or how to coordinate your sale and purchase without leaving yourself in a difficult position.


Consumers appear to feel the same way. In CEA’s 2024 Public Perception Survey, 74% of consumers expected property agents to complete at least one transaction a year so that agents would remain familiar with the latest rules and guidelines.


However, as of 1 January 2026, about 40% of the 32,967 agents who had been registered since 2023 completed fewer than three recorded residential transactions between 2023 and 2025. The median number of residential transactions completed per agent annually during that period was two.


But these figures only cover recorded residential transactions, so they don’t tell us everything an agent may have been working on. Still, they help explain why CEA is introducing the Currency Requirement.


## **Does This Replace an Agent’s Annual Training?**


No. Agents must still complete at least 16 hours of Continuing Professional Development training every year:


- At least 12 hours of Structured Learning
- At least four hours of Self-directed Learning


CPD helps agents build and refresh their professional knowledge, while the Currency Requirement focuses on recent transaction activity or practice-related knowledge demonstrated through the examination. Agents must meet both requirements separately.


## **What Should Buyers and Sellers Take Away From This?**


The requirement provides a useful starting point, but it does not tell you whether an agent’s experience is relevant to your property. For example, an agent who regularly handles rentals may have limited recent experience selling a landed home, while commercial transactions may be fewer because each one takes longer.

4. What timeline should I prepare for?
5. Who will handle my viewings, negotiations and documents?

Those qualities won’t appear in a transaction number, but they can make a real difference to your experience.

## Frequently Asked Questions


### Does Meeting the Currency Requirement Guarantee an Agent’s Registration Renewal?


No. The Currency Requirement will be one condition for renewal. Agents must still satisfy other applicable requirements, including completing their annual Continuing Professional Development training. Consumers should continue checking the[CEA Public Register](https://www.cea.gov.sg/consumers/engaging-a-property-agent/check-if-your-property-agent-is-registered/?utm_source=chatgpt.com) to confirm that an agent’s registration is currently valid.


### Should I Be Concerned If an Agent Qualified by Passing the Examination Instead?


Not automatically. There may be circumstances that prevented an agent from completing three transactions within the registration period. For example, they may have faced a serious illness or spent considerable time working on a complex commercial, industrial or en bloc transaction that took longer to complete.


Passing the Refresher Examination provides another way for the agent to demonstrate that their practice-related knowledge remains current. However, you should still ask about their recent experience with your property type and situation before deciding whether they are right for you.


### What Happens to My Transaction If My Agent Cannot Renew Their Registration?


A property agent cannot continue conducting estate agency work without a valid registration. If your agent’s registration lapses while your transaction is ongoing, contact their property agency to confirm who will take over your case and how the handover will be managed.
