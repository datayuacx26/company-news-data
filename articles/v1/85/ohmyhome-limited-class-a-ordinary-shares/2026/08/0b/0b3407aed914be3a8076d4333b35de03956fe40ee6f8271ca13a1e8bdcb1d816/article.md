## Begin With the People You Want Around the Table


As your family gathers this Mid-Autumn, think about how your household may change by the time you celebrate together in the years ahead.


Children grow, parents get older and working arrangements can change. You do not need to predict every possibility, but it helps to consider who your next home may need to support.


Ask yourselves:


- Who will live in the home now?
- Could a parent or relative move in later?
- How much privacy does each person need?
- Will young children eventually need separate rooms?
- Does anyone regularly work or study from home?
- Would an older family member need step-free access?
- How long do you hope to stay in the property?


Your answers can give you a clearer idea of the space and layout your family may need, both for everyday life and future gatherings.


A larger home can certainly help, but size alone does not determine how well a home works. A practical layout may serve your family better than additional floor area that is difficult to use.

## Think About the Journey Home


Mid-Autumn may bring everyone home for one special evening, but the location of your home can shape how easily your family comes together throughout the rest of the year.


Travelling across Singapore for an annual reunion may feel manageable. Making the same journey several times a week for childcare, caregiving or family meals can feel very different.


When comparing neighbourhoods, consider:


- Travelling time to parents and close relatives
- Routes to workplaces and schools
- Access to MRT stations and bus services
- Nearby childcare, healthcare and daily necessities
- Whether older relatives can reach the home comfortably
- The safety and convenience of walking routes
- Future developments that may affect the area


You may find that[living closer to family](https://ohmyhome.com/en-sg/blog/proximity-housing-grant-phg-eligible-checklist/?utm_source=blog&utm_medium=backlink&utm_campaign=MidAutumn2026Buyer) is more useful than having an additional facility or a slightly larger home elsewhere.


Instead of comparing locations only by their distance on a map, think about how much time each one could give back to your family.


## Plan for Everyday Life As Well As Celebrations


It is natural to picture everyone gathering in the living or dining area during Mid-Autumn. However, your next home also needs to work on regular days, when everyone is following a different routine.


Imagine a typical weekday in the home:


- Is there enough storage for everyday belongings?
- Can someone work while another person watches television?
- Is the kitchen suitable for how often your family cooks?
- Do the bedrooms provide enough privacy?
- Is there space for children to play or study?
- Would the home be easy to maintain?
- Can the layout adapt as your family’s needs change?


Look at the floor plan before arranging a viewing and consider how everyone would move through the space.


A home can look spacious in photographs but offer little usable room once furniture and storage are considered. On the other hand, a smaller home with a practical layout may feel much more comfortable.


## Create Your Family Move Plan


Once you understand what your family needs, bring the practical and financial details together.


Your Family Move Plan should include:


- Your estimated purchasing budget
- Available cash and CPF funds
- Financing options and monthly commitments
- Preferred property type
- Suitable neighbourhoods
- Required floor area and layout
- Proximity to family, schools and workplaces
- Accessibility requirements
- Expected renovation or repair costs
- Your preferred purchasing timeline
- How long you intend to live in or own the home


Then sort your requirements into three groups:


- **Must-haves:** Things your family genuinely needs from the home
- **Strong preferences:** Important features with some room for compromise
- **Bonuses:** Features you would enjoy but can live without


This becomes especially helpful when two homes appeal to you for different reasons. Instead of choosing based on the stronger first impression, you can return to the priorities your family agreed on together.

## Shortlist Homes Without Losing Sight of Your Priorities


Searching for a family home in Singapore can become overwhelming. There will always be another listing, neighbourhood or property type to compare.


Instead of reviewing every available home, focus first on properties that meet your family’s most important requirements.


For each home, compare:


- Asking price and recent transactions
- Remaining lease or tenure
- Floor plan and usable space
- Property condition
- Likely renovation or repair costs
- Monthly maintenance charges, where applicable
- Transport and daily amenities
- Proximity to schools, workplaces and relatives
- Noise, privacy and surroundings
- Planned developments in the area


An attractive listing may still be unsuitable if it stretches your budget, adds too much travelling time or requires more renovation than expected.

## During the Viewing, Picture Your Family Living There


When viewing a home, start by imagining the next Mid-Autumn taking place there.


Where would everyone sit? Could older relatives move around comfortably? Would children have somewhere safe to play? These questions can help you understand whether the shared spaces would support the moments you hope to enjoy together.


Then consider how the home would work on an ordinary day. Where would school bags, groceries and laundry go? Would family members have enough room to work, rest and spend time together without constantly getting in one another’s way?


Once you have considered how the home might feel, inspect its practical condition:


- Natural light and ventilation
- Noise from neighbours, roads or nearby facilities
- Privacy from surrounding units
- Available storage
- The condition of walls, flooring and built-in fixtures
- Signs of leaks, moisture or other maintenance issues
- The condition of shared areas and facilities
- Mobile reception and the location of electrical points
- The walking route to the nearest transport stop
- The overall environment of the neighbourhood


Take photographs and notes, where permitted, immediately after each viewing. After several appointments, the details of different homes can easily begin to blur together.


One useful question to ask after every viewing is:


***“What would become easier for our family if we lived here, and what might become harder?”***


This helps you judge the home by how it would affect family life, not only by how attractive it appears during the viewing.

## Look Beyond the Asking Price Before Making An Offer


Once you find a suitable property, avoid deciding how much to offer based only on the asking price.


Consider:


- Recent transactions for comparable homes
- The unit’s floor level, facing and condition
- Renovation and immediate repair costs
- Competing listings in the development or area
- Current buyer interest
- Your financing and comfortable spending limit
- The seller’s preferred completion timeline
- Whether the home still meets your agreed must-haves


Remember to consider the total cost of the move. A lower-priced home may require substantial renovation, while a more expensive home in a convenient location may reduce transport or caregiving demands for several family members.


The aim is not simply to secure the home. It is to make a decision your family can remain comfortable with after the excitement settles.

## Prepare for the Move Before Getting the Keys


After your offer is accepted, there are still several steps to complete. Depending on the property type, these may include:


- Confirming your financing arrangements
- Preparing and reviewing the required documents
- Monitoring transaction deadlines
- Coordinating with the seller
- Planning renovation or repair work
- Arranging utilities and moving services
- Preparing for completion and handover


This is also a good time to prepare your family for the practical changes ahead.


Children may need to become familiar with a new route to school, while older relatives may need help learning how to reach the new home. Planning these details early can help everyone settle in more comfortably.

## Frequently Asked Questions


### What type of home suits a multigenerational family in Singapore?


An HDB 3Gen flat is the most purpose-built option, as its four bedrooms and three bathrooms give parents and married children more privacy under one roof. However, a larger resale HDB flat may offer more location choices, while a dual-key condo can provide greater separation between generations. Whichever you consider, compare the layout, accessibility and monthly costs, not just the floor area.


### Is Mid-Autumn a good time to buy a home in Singapore?


Mid-Autumn can be a meaningful time to begin your home search, especially if family gatherings have prompted conversations about space, location or living closer together. However, the festival itself should not determine when you buy. Your finances, housing needs and readiness to commit should still guide the timing of your move.


### Do I need a bigger home to host family gatherings comfortably?


No, a bigger home is not always necessary for comfortable family gatherings. A practical layout can matter more than total floor area. Look for connected living and dining spaces, enough room to move around and a layout that can accommodate additional seating without blocking walkways. Also consider whether older relatives can enter and use the home comfortably.
