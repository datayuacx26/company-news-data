# 15-Month HDB Wait-Out Period Removed: What Private Property Owners Need to Know

Last Updated on July 28, 2026 byYolly Del Prado


From 28 July 2026, current and former private property owners no longer need to wait 15 months to buy a non-subsidised resale HDB flat. To proceed immediately, the purchase must be made without CPF housing grants or an HDB housing loan.


Owners who have not sold their private properties may buy the resale HDB flat first, but they must dispose of all private residential properties in Singapore and overseas within 6 months of completing the HDB purchase.


The announcement gives affected households more flexibility, but it does not remove every HDB wait-out period. Here is what changed, who qualifies and which conditions remain.


## What Was Announced?


The Government removed the 15-month wait-out period with immediate effect on 28 July 2026.


Introduced in September 2022, the temporary measure generally required private residential property owners to:


**Sell their private property → wait 15 months → buy a non-subsidised resale HDB flat**


Eligible buyers may now proceed without serving that additional wait.

The change applies to existing private residential property owners and people who have already sold their properties.


It is specific to non-subsidised resale HDB purchases. In this context, “non-subsidised” describes how the flat is purchased, not whether its resale price is considered affordable. Buyers proceeding immediately cannot receive CPF housing grants or use an HDB housing loan.


## Who Does the 15-Month Wait-Out Period Removal Apply To?


The 15-month wait has been removed for current and former private residential property owners buying a non-subsidised resale HDB flat without CPF housing grants or an HDB housing loan.


Three groups are most directly affected:


### 1. Former owners serving the 15-month wait


If you have already sold your private property and are waiting to become eligible, you no longer need to complete the remainder of the 15-month period.


You may proceed once you have a valid HDB Flat Eligibility (HFE) letter and satisfy the other conditions for buying the resale flat.


### 2. Owners who have not sold their private property


You may buy an eligible resale HDB flat before selling your private property. You must then dispose of all private residential properties in Singapore and overseas within 6 months of completing the HDB purchase.


This may allow a more direct transition between homes, but it also places a firm deadline on the private-property sale.


### 3. Buyers with pending appeals


If you previously appealed against the 15-month rule, you no longer need to wait for HDB’s response. You may proceed to apply for an HFE letter, and HDB will contact affected appellants directly.


For HFE applications already being processed, HDB will update the eligibility status automatically. According to the Ministry of National Development (MND), no action is required from these applicants.


## Can You Buy a Resale HDB Flat Before Selling Your Private Property?


Yes, provided the HDB purchase qualifies under the revised rule.


If you buy the resale HDB flat first, all private residential properties must be sold within 6 months of completing that purchase. Overseas residential properties count towards this requirement.


Buying first can reduce the gap between homes. However, it changes your position as a seller. Once the HDB purchase is completed, the 6-month disposal period begins. If the private property takes longer than expected to attract a buyer, you may have less room to hold out for your preferred price.


You should also consider whether you can complete the HDB purchase before receiving the proceeds from the private-property sale. If those proceeds are needed to fund the next home, selling first may provide greater certainty.

Selling first is generally the lower-risk option when the HDB budget depends heavily on the sale proceeds or the private property may take time to sell.


Buying first may be workable for households with confirmed financing and sufficient available funds to complete the HDB purchase without relying on an immediate sale. The private property should also be realistically priced for the 6-month timeline.

## Which Rules Have Not Changed?


The announcement removes only the 15-month period for eligible non-subsidised resale HDB purchases.


The existing 30-month wait-out period remains for other purchases and financing arrangements. Private property owners must dispose of their property and wait 30 months before:


- Buying a BTO flat, with or without CPF housing grants
- Buying a resale HDB flat with CPF housing grants
- Buying a flat with an HDB housing loan
- Buying a new Executive Condominium from a developer


This distinction matters because the removal does not give every former private property owner immediate access to every type of HDB flat.


If your intended purchase depends on a housing grant or an HDB loan, the new immediate-purchase route does not apply. The same is true if you intend to buy a BTO flat or new EC.


Decide on the intended home and financing route before selling. Otherwise, you could dispose of the private property expecting to buy immediately, only to find that the option you want remains subject to the 30-month period.


## Do You Still Need an HFE Letter?


Yes. The removal of the wait-out period does not remove the HFE requirement.


According to HDB’s HFE guidance,[resale buyers must have a valid HFE letter](https://ohmyhome.com/en-sg/blog/the-ultimate-guide-to-hdb-flat-eligibility-hfe-letter-what-is-it-and-how-to-apply/?utm_source=blog&utm_medium=backlink&utm_campaign=15MonthWaitOutRemoved) before obtaining an Option to Purchase (OTP) from a flat seller and when submitting their resale application.


The HFE letter assesses the household’s eligibility to:


- Buy a new or resale HDB flat
- Receive CPF housing grants
- Obtain an HDB housing loan


If you use a housing loan from a financial institution, you may still proceed without serving the 15-month wait, subject to the lender’s approval. You must obtain a valid Letter of Offer before exercising the OTP.


Apply for the HFE letter and confirm your financing before committing to a property.


## What Should Private Property Owners Do Next?


The announcement creates more choice over the order of the two transactions. That makes early planning more important.


### 1. Confirm whether your purchase qualifies


Check that you intend to buy a non-subsidised resale HDB flat without CPF housing grants or an HDB housing loan. If you need grants, HDB financing, a BTO flat or a new EC, the 30-month rule remains relevant.


### 2. Apply for or review your HFE letter


Do this before obtaining an OTP. If an application is already being processed, HDB will update the eligibility status automatically.


### 3. Confirm how the HDB purchase will be funded


If you need a loan from a financial institution, obtain the required approval. Establish how much cash and CPF you can use and whether you can complete the HDB purchase before the private-property proceeds become available.


### 4. Decide whether to sell or buy first


Assess the private property’s likely selling period, not just its preferred asking price. Review recent comparable transactions, competing listings and the condition of the unit.


Buying first should not depend on an optimistic assumption that the private property will sell quickly at the highest price in the development.


### 5. Plan the completion dates together


The 6-month disposal requirement runs from the completion of the HDB resale purchase. The timing of the private-property sale and HDB completion should therefore be planned as one sequence rather than as two separate transactions.


## Why Was the 15-Month Rule Removed?


The 15-month wait was introduced as a temporary cooling measure in September 2022. Its purpose was to moderate demand for resale HDB flats and prioritise access for buyers with more urgent housing needs.


The Government assessed in July 2026 that the measure had served its purpose as the resale market showed signs of stabilising.


HDB’s Resale Price Index declined by[0.1% in Q1 2026](https://ohmyhome.com/en-sg/blog/hdb-resale-prices-dropped-for-the-first-time-in-nearly-7-years-should-you-buy-now-or-wait/?utm_source=blog&utm_medium=backlink&utm_campaign=15MonthWaitOutRemoved) and[a further 0.3% in Q2.](https://ohmyhome.com/en-sg/blog/hdb-resale-prices-dropped-for-the-second-consecutive-quarter-what-this-means-for-buyers-sellers/?utm_source=blog&utm_medium=backlink&utm_campaign=15MonthWaitOutRemoved) The final Q2 2026 index stood at 202.8.

This came after five consecutive quarters of slower or no price growth from Q4 2024 to Q4 2025. MND also pointed to the larger number of flats expected to complete their Minimum Occupation Period (MOP) and become eligible for resale over the coming years.


These conditions supported the removal of this particular measure. They do not mean that HDB resale prices will continue falling or that the separate 30-month rule has been relaxed.


## What Could the Change Mean for the HDB Resale Market?


It is too early to measure the effect.


However, the change could bring more private property owners into the resale market, particularly households that postponed right-sizing because they did not want to rent during the former 15-month wait. In our assessment, larger flats and homes in established estates may be worth watching because they can appeal to households moving from private housing.


This is not a forecast that resale prices will increase across Singapore. Additional flats reaching their MOP may expand supply, while buyers remain limited by their budgets and financing approvals. Any effect is likely to differ by town, flat type, remaining lease and condition.


Buyers should assess the individual flat rather than react to the announcement alone. Compare the asking price with recent completed transactions and consider whether the home will continue to suit the household over the intended holding period.

## Frequently Asked Questions


### Has the 15-month HDB wait-out period been completely removed?


No. It has been removed only for non-subsidised resale HDB purchases made without CPF housing grants or an HDB housing loan.


### Can I buy an HDB flat before selling my condo?


Yes, if the HDB purchase qualifies under the revised rule. You must sell all private residential properties in Singapore and overseas within 6 months of completing the HDB purchase.


### Can I use a bank loan?


Eligible buyers may use financing from a financial institution, subject to the lender’s approval. You must obtain a valid Letter of Offer for the confirmed loan before exercising the resale HDB OTP.


### Does the 30-month wait-out period still apply?


Yes. It remains relevant to BTO flats, resale HDB flats bought with housing grants, purchases requiring an HDB housing loan and new ECs bought from developers.


### What if my appeal against the 15-month wait is pending?


You no longer need to wait for HDB’s response. You may proceed to apply for an HFE letter, and HDB will contact affected appellants.
