Here is the opinion I will defend for the next 2,800 words: **qualified vs Warmly vs Storylane RepX is not a fight between three versions of the same product.** These are three different categories wearing similar marketing. Teams that treat them as interchangeable buy the wrong capability and pay for it every renewal.


I am Madhav Bhandari, CMO at Storylane. I have watched dozens of revenue teams shortlist all three, assume they compete head-to-head, then discover halfway through a trial that one identifies visitors, one chats inside Salesforce, and one actually shows the product. The winner is the buyer who knows which problem they are solving before they sign.


> **Definition:** An AI website conversion agent is software that engages an inbound visitor in real time, decides whether they fit your ideal customer profile, and moves the qualified ones toward a meeting or a next step without an SDR doing it manually.


Most teams are bleeding pipeline at exactly this layer. The question is which of these three tools does the part of the job that is actually broken for you.

## What each tool actually is (and how they differ)


Before you compare features, get the categories straight, because the SERP will not do it for you. One directory page I looked at even merges Warmly and RepX into a single entry. If you want the broader landscape, our rundown of the[best AI SDR tools](https://www.storylane.io/blog/top-ai-sdr-tools) frames where each of these sits.


### Qualified


Qualified is conversational marketing built around Salesforce. Its AI agent, Piper, sits on your site, chats with visitors, books meetings, and writes everything back into Salesforce natively. It is genuinely strong when your buying process is multi-threaded and your data model lives in Salesforce.


The catch is that the Salesforce dependency and enterprise pricing are load-bearing, so if you are not a Salesforce shop with the budget to match, most of what makes Qualified good is out of reach.


### Warmly


Warmly is a de-anonymization and intent-signal platform first, with an AI chat layer on top. It tells you which companies and, more selectively, which people are on your site, then blends in third-party intent data from sources like Bombora and job-change signals. Warmly states that it reveals about 65% of the companies and 15% of the individual contacts visiting your site (Warmly, 2026).


It is a different job from qualifying or demoing. Warmly is at its best when your problem is "we do not know who is here," not "we cannot show them the product."


### Storylane RepX


RepX is an AI sales agent that qualifies a visitor through questions and then plays an interactive product demo inside the same conversation. It is multi-modal across voice, video, and text, and it does not require a CRM to run. The differentiator is not the chat: it is that the chat can pull up the product and let the buyer walk through it.


One buyer said it best:


> "I think chat is hygiene. Everybody does that. This left hand side, the content, is it dynamically served?" - \[Global Marketing Head, HR tech software\]


## Qualified vs Warmly vs Storylane RepX: the category distinction most comparisons miss


No page in the top results frames these as three separate categories, and that failure is exactly why buyers get burned.


Here is the clean separation, and it is worth internalizing before you look at a single price:


- **Qualified answers "how do we run enterprise chat inside Salesforce?"** It is a conversation and routing engine tied to your CRM of record.
- **Warmly answers "who is on our site right now, and are they in-market?"** It is identity and intent, and the chat is a bolt-on to that core.
- **RepX answers "can the visitor understand and try the product before a rep is involved?"** It is qualification plus proof, in one session.


Buyers already think this way when you let them. They separate basic answering from dynamically served, interactive content, which is the same split the tools fall along. The moment a prospect can[qualify leads with interactive demos](https://www.storylane.io/blog/pql-interactive-demo-implementation-guide) instead of just reading a chat reply, you are in a different category of outcome.


Any comparison that flattens these three into "AI chatbots" is measuring the wrong axis. The directory page that merges Warmly and RepX is simply wrong on the facts.

## AI conversation quality & how each handles a live visitor


Feature grids flatten the thing that actually matters when a real buyer lands on your pricing page. Conversation quality is not "does it reply." It is whether the follow-ups deepen engagement and move someone toward a booked meeting, which is how buyers themselves judge it.


> "The more the engagement, the more you get a sense of what kind of things your prospects are asking for, the higher the probability that they'll further continue the chat and book a meeting." - \[Senior Marketing Operations Manager, construction / supply chain\]


Watch the same visitor hit each tool and the difference gets concrete. Say a RevOps lead lands from a paid ad, curious but skeptical:


1. **Qualified** greets them, asks a couple of qualifying questions, recognizes the account from Salesforce, and routes to the right rep or books a meeting. Strong on complex, multi-threaded enterprise motions.
2. **Warmly** identifies the company, enriches with intent data, and can trigger a templated chat or alert a rep to reach out. The intelligence is in the identification, not the conversation.
3. **RepX** asks discovery-style questions, profiles the visitor, and then pulls the relevant product flow up on screen so they try the exact use case they asked about, before any human joins.


The honest worry with any AI agent is that it will be shallow and generic, so the bar RepX has to clear is grounding: its answers and the demo it serves have to come from your real product and content, not spun filler.


When it is grounded, an interactive walkthrough beats a paragraph of chat every time, and it doubles as a lightweight[digital sales room software](https://www.storylane.io/blog/digital-sales-room-software) moment. It also matters that the agent asks good[discovery call questions](https://www.storylane.io/blog/discovery-call-agenda) , because a demo served against a bad read of the buyer is just a faster way to lose them.


## Setup, CRM dependency & time-to-value


Time-to-value is where the enterprise-versus-lean split becomes a calendar reality. The tool you can turn on this week is worth more than the one that is theoretically more powerful in a month, if your problem is bleeding pipeline right now.


- **Qualified** typically needs 2–4 weeks of onboarding and a working Salesforce setup to reach full functionality. That is fine for an enterprise with a RevOps team and wrong for a lean one.
- **Warmly** is lighter to stand up, but its payoff depends on traffic volume. Thin ICP traffic means thin results, no matter how clean the setup.
- **RepX** requires no CRM and stands up quickly, with the one dependency being a Storylane base plan underneath. Your demos live in an[interactive demo environment](https://www.storylane.io/blog/what-is-a-demo-environment) you likely already want.


The traffic-dependency point is not a footnote, and a buyer said it more bluntly than I would:


> "The idea is good, the thought process is great, but it completely depends on if I have my ICP coming into the website in the first place." - \[VP Business, DevOps / observability\]


None of these tools manufactures demand: they convert the demand you already have. If your ICP is not landing on the site, fix that first.

## Which one should you choose? (decision framework)


Skip feature scoring and answer one question: what is actually broken in your funnel? Match your situation to the tool built for that failure, not to the loudest brand.


- **If you are a Salesforce-centric enterprise** and want chat and routing native to your CRM, then choose **Qualified** , and budget for the onboarding.
- **If your core problem is not knowing who is on your site** , then choose **Warmly** and pair it with strong outbound to act on the signals.
- **If you want qualification and product proof in one session** , then choose **Storylane RepX** , especially if buyers routinely need to see the product before they will book.
- **If you have thin ICP traffic** , then fix demand generation first, because none of these three will save a site nobody qualified is visiting.
- **If you are early and lean** , then start with the lowest-commitment option that solves your specific break, and layer up as traffic and pipeline justify it.


For teams whose real goal is a smoother path from first touch to closed deal, the choice is downstream of your wider[buyer enablement](https://www.storylane.io/blog/complete-buyer-enablement-guide) motion, not just a tooling checkbox. Two use cases we see win with RepX: expediting "second," desired-state demos so reps only spend time on tailored ones, and turning a form fill into a self-serve interactive demo the prospect explores alone.


> "We are looking for a solution that can help our three sales team expediate second demos, desired state demos." - \[Chief Revenue Officer, industrial SaaS\]

## FAQ


**What's the difference between Qualified, Warmly, and Storylane RepX?**


They are three different categories. Qualified is Salesforce-native conversational chat for enterprises, Warmly is visitor de-anonymization plus intent signals, and Storylane RepX is an AI sales agent that qualifies a visitor and plays an interactive demo in the same conversation.


**Which is cheapest: Qualified, Warmly, or RepX?**


Warmly offers the lowest-friction way in with a free plan, but its paid pricing is modular and quote-based and climbs from roughly $10k/yr to about $30k/yr depending on tier and traffic. Qualified sits highest with a roughly $35k–$42k/yr enterprise floor, and RepX Chat has published tiers at $2,000/month (Growth) and $3,000/month (Premium), with a 30-day free trial. So the honest answer is that Warmly is cheapest to start but not always cheapest at the tier that includes AI chat.


**Does any of these tools require Salesforce?**


Qualified needs Salesforce for full functionality, which narrows it to Salesforce shops. Warmly and Storylane RepX do not require a CRM to run. If you are not on Salesforce, that single fact reshapes your shortlist.


**Can an AI agent show a product demo while qualifying a lead?**


Yes, and that is the specific thing RepX does. It asks qualifying questions, profiles the visitor, and then pulls up an interactive product demo inside the same conversation so the buyer can try the relevant use case before a rep joins. Neither Qualified nor Warmly serves an interactive demo in chat.


**Which tool is best for SMB versus enterprise?**


Enterprise Salesforce teams tend to fit Qualified. SMB to mid-market teams that need to know who is on their site lean toward Warmly, and teams of any size whose buyers need to see the product before booking tend to fit RepX. Match the tool to your bottleneck and your CRM reality, not to company size alone.
