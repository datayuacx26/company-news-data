# Intercom vs 1mind vs Storylane RepX: 2026 Buyer Guide

Full disclosure up front: I run marketing at Storylane, so I have a horse in this race. But here is the honest take I would give a friend evaluating **intercom vs 1mind vs Storylane RepX** , and it is not the take you will find on the ranking pages: these three are not competing in the same event.


Intercom Fin is a support-first AI, 1mind is an enterprise AI sales agent, and Storylane RepX is a demo-powered AI sales agent. Picking between them on headline price is the fastest way to buy the wrong tool.


I have watched buyers waste a quarter comparing a support chatbot to a sales agent because both say "AI" and both live on your website. They solve different jobs. This guide gives you the three-way answer the SERP does not: a real spec table, a total-cost model, and a scenario-based pick, so you shortlist on fit rather than on which vendor bought the most keywords.


> **Definition:** An AI agent for your website is software that autonomously greets, answers, and routes visitors in real time. What separates them is the job they are built to finish: resolving a support ticket, qualifying an enterprise deal, or running a live product demo that moves a buyer toward a decision.


## Quick verdict (TL;DR)


Here is the one-line version for each, so you can self-select before reading 3,000 more words. If you only read this section, read it as three different answers to three different questions, not as a ranking of one thing:


- **Choose Intercom Fin** if your primary problem is deflecting and resolving inbound support tickets. It is excellent in-app support and onboarding software, and it is the cheapest to start. It is not built to generate pipeline.
- **Choose 1mind** if you are an enterprise built around Salesforce and the Clari plus Salesloft ecosystem, with a security review that takes months anyway and budget for a six-figure annual contract. You get an "AI sales engineer" that can ride along on calls.
- **Choose Storylane RepX** if you are a growth-stage or mid-market team that sells through the product and wants the demo itself to qualify buyers. It is the only one of the three that turns a live interactive demo into the conversation.
- **The trap to avoid:** comparing a $29 support seat to a six-figure enterprise agent to a demo platform as if they were interchangeable. They finish different jobs, so cost-per-seat is a meaningless yardstick.

## Three tools, three categories (why this isn't apples-to-apples)


The single most common mistake I see is treating this as one race with three horses. It is three races. If you skip this framing, every downstream number misleads you, because you end up comparing a support deflection tool to a pipeline-generation tool on the price of a seat.


Think of it as three primary jobs. Intercom Fin exists to resolve support tickets and reduce human agent load.


1mind exists to act as an AI sales engineer for enterprise deals, including riding along on Zoom calls. Storylane RepX exists to run the product demo that qualifies and advances a buyer, which is a different motion from both.


The distinction is not academic, and buyers feel it on the ground. In our own sales calls, a customer described exactly why they bucket Fin separately: it is a routing layer, not a selling layer.


> "And then some of the differentiation with FIN is that it can route stuff to our support queue." - \[Head of Customer Experience, B2B SaaS\]


That is the tell. A tool whose differentiation is where it *routes* a conversation is a support tool, however good it is. A tool whose job is to *advance* a buyer through a demo is a sales tool.

## Intercom (Fin): overview, strengths, limits


Intercom is the incumbent most of your buyers already know, and Fin is its AI agent. I want to be fair here, because every ranking page shortchanges it: Fin is genuinely strong at what it is built for. If your pain is inbound support volume, onboarding questions, and ticket deflection, Fin is a mature, well-integrated answer that starts cheap.


Pricing is its most published dimension. Intercom lists roughly $29 per seat per month, with usage-based charges for each Fin resolution on top, as of 2026. Confirm the resolution rate with Intercom directly, because usage pricing is exactly where a cheap-looking seat cost quietly grows.


Where Fin wins is in-app support and onboarding. It answers documented questions well, it deflects repetitive tickets, and it keeps the customer inside the same chat while it hands off. A buyer of ours described that handoff mechanic precisely.


> "So the customer doesn't leave the chat. They're still in the same chat, but it's routed to a team member that is on support versus routing to, like our sales bucket." - \[Head of Customer Experience, B2B SaaS\]


Where Fin falls short is pipeline. It is not built to greet an anonymous inbound lead, qualify them, and run a product demo that advances a deal. The same buyer flagged a real risk when you try to force a support tool into a sales motion.


> "It disqualifies it as a lead and then still route support. But the customer doesn't know that they've been disqualified." - \[Head of Customer Experience, B2B SaaS\]


That is a hidden handoff. For support, silent routing is fine.


For sales, a quietly disqualified lead is lost pipeline nobody logged. Fin is the right answer to the wrong question if your goal is revenue.

## Which should you choose? (by scenario)


Enough framing. Here is who I would point at which tool, mapped to the situations buyers are actually in. Match yourself to the closest line rather than averaging across them, because the right pick is a function of your motion, not a universal ranking.


- **Support-led org, ticket volume is the pain:** Intercom Fin. You do not need a sales agent, and paying for one would be waste. Fin deflects tickets well and starts cheap.
- **Enterprise, Salesforce and Salesloft-centric, SE-heavy complex sales:** 1mind. If you have the budget, the security runway, and a call-heavy motion, an AI sales engineer that rides along fits, and the sales-gated process will not slow you down more than your own procurement already does.
- **Growth or mid-market, demo-led motion:** Storylane RepX. If buyers decide by seeing the product, put the demo at the front and let it qualify. This is the[digital sales room](https://www.storylane.io/blog/digital-sales-room-software) motion, run by an agent, and it is where RepX is purpose-built.
- **HubSpot shop:** confirm 1mind's CRM fit directly, since its integrations are Clari and Salesloft-centric, and otherwise choose between Fin and RepX on whether your goal is support or pipeline.
- **Need to prove ROI before you commit:** RepX or Fin, because both let you start and measure. 1mind's minimum one-year, no-POC model asks you to buy the outcome before you can see it.


If two lines fit you, you are probably running two jobs, and the honest answer is that a support tool and a sales agent can coexist. What you should not do is buy one and expect it to do the other.


One more note on sequencing, because budget timing trips buyers up. If you are early and cash-conscious, start with the tool that maps to your single biggest revenue leak this quarter, prove it, then add the second job later.


A team that lives on inbound demos should not delay a demo agent to stand up support automation it does not yet need, and a team drowning in tickets should not overspend on a sales agent it cannot staff. Sequence by the pain that is costing you the most right now, and let the POC numbers, not the vendor's roadmap, tell you when to add the next tool.


## How to run a fair evaluation


Do not take my table as gospel, or anyone's. The category framing holds, but the numbers move, and the only proof that counts is your own traffic. Run a short, disciplined evaluation scoped to exactly these three, and let the results overrule every marketing page including this one.


Work the checklist in order:


1. **Request written quotes from all three, dated.** Intercom's per-seat is public, but Fin usage, 1mind's contract, and the RepX structure all need a real number in writing before you can compare anything.
2. **Run a POC on real traffic.** RepX offers a 30-day POC and Fin a trial; 1mind does not, so weigh that asymmetry honestly rather than assuming parity.
3. **Measure cost-per-qualified-lead, not cost-per-seat.** Divide fully loaded annual cost by qualified opportunities produced in the POC. This is the number that exposes a cheap tool that finishes the wrong job.
4. **Test full-cycle capability against a live visitor.** Can the agent greet, qualify, demo, and book without a human? Watch where it hands off, and to whom.
5. **Score integrations against your actual CRM.** An agent that cannot connect natively to your CRM is a non-starter no matter how strong the demo, so confirm each vendor's connector for your exact stack.


Use your normal discovery discipline while you do it. The same rigor you use to[qualify buyers in discovery](https://www.storylane.io/blog/discovery-call-agenda) is what keeps a vendor demo honest, because a scripted showcase on the vendor's traffic tells you almost nothing about your own.
