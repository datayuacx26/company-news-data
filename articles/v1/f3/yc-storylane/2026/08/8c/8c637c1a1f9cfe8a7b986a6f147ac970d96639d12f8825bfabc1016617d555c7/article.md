# Intercom vs Spara vs Storylane RepX (2026)

Full disclosure before we start: I am the CMO at Storylane, and RepX is our product. So read this the way you would read any vendor's comparison, with a raised eyebrow. But I wrote it because nobody has actually compared Intercom vs Spara vs Storylane RepX honestly, and the buyer typing that exact query keeps landing on listicles that dodge the real question.


Here is my thesis, stated plainly so you can disagree with it: these three tools get lumped together as "AI sales agents," but they are three different categories of product, and only one of them converts an inbound visitor by showing them the product mid-conversation.


If your goal is support deflection, Intercom wins. If your goal is multi-channel lead routing, Spara has a case. If your goal is turning website traffic into pipeline by letting people see the product, that is the job RepX was built for.


> **Definition:** An AI sales agent is software that engages a website visitor in real time, answers their questions, qualifies them, and moves them toward a next step (a booked meeting, a trial, or a purchase). It differs from a support chatbot, which is optimized to resolve tickets, and from an AI SDR, which is optimized to route and enrich leads.


That distinction is the whole game here, so let's use it as the spine.


## Quick verdict: which one is right for you


If you want the answer before the analysis, here it is.


Intercom, with its Fin AI, is the right pick if you already run Intercom for support and want one AI layer across pre- and post-sale conversations. You are buying consolidation, not a demo engine.


Spara is the right pick if your bottleneck is qualifying and routing inbound across chat, voice, and email, and you have a rep team waiting to take well-sorted handoffs. You are buying an AI SDR layer, not a way to show the product.


Storylane RepX is the right pick if your problem is that visitors leave before they understand what you do. RepX answers in chat, voice, or video and surfaces an interactive demo inside the conversation, so a buyer sees the product instead of reading about it. You are buying conversion through show, not tell.


The mistake I see buyers make is treating these as three flavors of the same thing. They are not. Pick based on the job, and the choice gets obvious fast.


## The category confusion: these three aren't the same kind of tool


Here is the clarification no competitor page makes: Intercom, Spara, and RepX sit in three separate categories that happen to overlap on a chat widget.


Intercom is support-first. Fin extended into pre-sales conversations from 2025, but the center of gravity is still ticket resolution and a shared knowledge base. That heritage is a strength for post-sale and a limitation when the job is convincing a stranger to buy.


Spara is a multi-channel AI SDR. It qualifies, enriches against your CRM, and routes to reps across three separate products for chat, voice, and email.


If you are shopping the broader category, our own rundown of the[best AI SDR tools](https://www.storylane.io/blog/top-ai-sdr-tools) puts Spara in context. Its job is to sort and hand off, not to demonstrate.


RepX is a demo-powered agent. It is the only one of the three that can pull up a live, interactive product experience in the middle of a conversation. That is a different category entirely, and it is where the buyer situation for this query usually lands.

## What is Intercom (Fin AI)?


Intercom is the original customer-messaging platform, and Fin is its AI layer. For years the company owned the shared inbox and the support knowledge base, and that is still the product's spine.


From 2025, Fin extended into pre-sales conversations, so the same AI that answers a support ticket can now greet a prospect. The upside is real: one knowledge base, one AI, one system spanning the full customer lifecycle. For a team already standardized on Intercom, that consolidation is worth money.


The limitation is heritage. Reviewers note that response generation can feel slower than a purpose-built sales agent, and there is no native interactive demo, so Fin can describe your product but it cannot show it.


When the job is deflecting a "how do I reset my password" ticket, that is fine. When the job is converting a skeptical buyer who wants to see the workflow before they trust it, description hits a ceiling.


So my read: Intercom is a strong support platform that has grown a pre-sales arm, not a sales agent that happens to do support. Buy it for the former.


## What is Spara?


Spara is an AI go-to-market platform built around qualification and routing. It ships as three separate products: AI Chat, AI Voice, and AI Email, each engaging inbound in its own channel.


The strength is coverage. Spara qualifies visitors, enriches them against your CRM, and alerts reps so a human takes the handoff warm. If your funnel leaks because leads sit unrouted or unqualified, that is a genuine fix, and it maps to a pain buyers describe constantly.


> "The success state is you of course get better leads coming through. The qualified pipeline looks better than before and sales is not spending as much time on low quality." - \[role not captured, HR SaaS\]


The gaps are worth naming. The three channels operate independently rather than as one unified conversation, there is no native demo layer, pricing is not public, and there is no self-serve trial to kick the tires.


Spara sorts and hands off well. It does not show the product, which means the visitor who needs to see it before they believe it still walks.

## Intercom vs Spara vs Storylane RepX, feature by feature


Categories decide the shortlist. Features decide the winner. Here is where the three actually diverge.


### Engagement modality (chat, voice, video)


Modality is not a vanity spec, it is how much of a real conversation the agent can carry. Intercom is chat-centric. Spara spans chat, voice, and email, but as three products rather than one continuous thread.


RepX runs chat, voice, and video in a single agent, and can participate in a live video call. The practical difference: a buyer can start typing, switch to talking, and end up watching a demo without ever leaving the conversation.


A fair caveat on any voice-capable agent, ours included: evaluate turn-taking and response latency on your own traffic before you commit, because voice maturity varies by vendor and by use case. Ask for a live test, not a scripted one.


### Can it show the product? (native interactive demo)


This is the single biggest divide in the comparison. Neither Intercom nor Spara has a native interactive demo. They answer questions about the product with words.


RepX shows the product. When a buyer asks how something works, the agent surfaces a clickable demo of that exact workflow. Buyers consistently tell us the personalized, interactive version is what lands.


> "With HTML you can personalize everything on your product screens themselves. When I'm looking at your product, I can see the name of my church there and things like that." - \[role not captured, non-profit / ministry\]


If showing the product is not part of your conversion motion, this row does not matter to you. If it is, it decides the whole thing.


### Knowledge base depth and answer quality


All three train on some corpus. Intercom's roots are a mature support knowledge base, which is a genuine advantage for factual, post-sale answers. Spara runs leaner on knowledge and heavier on routing logic.


RepX trains on your full knowledge base and runs actual discovery, driving the conversation the way a good rep does rather than waiting to react.

### CRM and martech integrations

## Pricing and total cost of ownership


List price is the number vendors show you. Total cost is the number you actually pay, and for this trio they are structured very differently.


Intercom uses a seat plus metered-AI model, publicly starting from roughly $29 per seat per month, with AI resolutions billed on top. That scales with headcount and volume, so a growing team should model both axes, not just the seat count.


Spara does not publish pricing at all, so you are requesting a quote and cannot self-serve a trial to sanity-check value first. RepX Chat has published, visitor-based pricing (Growth $2,000/month, Premium $3,000/month, Enterprise custom) with a 30-day free trial, so you can size it against your own traffic directly.


Because I cannot honestly hand you three clean list prices, here is how to model total cost yourself. Use one illustrative frame:


Line item How to model it


Platform / seat fees Seats needed times monthly seat price, annualized


Metered AI usage Expected monthly conversations times per-resolution or usage rate


Add-on layers Any agent or demo layer priced on top of the base plan


Implementation Config, knowledge-base build, and integration time in loaded hours


Opportunity cost Value of leads lost while the tool ramps


Then divide fully loaded annual cost by the number of qualified opportunities the tool actually produces. As a purely illustrative example: a tool at $1,500 per month is $18,000 per year, and if it sources 120 qualified opportunities in that year, that is $150 per opportunity.


Run the same math for each vendor with your real numbers, compare cost per opportunity rather than sticker price, and the honest winner will surface. Whatever you do, do not compare Intercom's seat price to Spara's quote to a RepX estimate and call it a comparison, because that is comparing three different units.

## Switching considerations


Nobody writes about migration for these three, which is strange, because switching cost is where deals quietly die. If you are moving between Intercom, Spara, and RepX, plan for three things.


First, the knowledge base. Each tool trains on its own corpus, so budget time to rebuild and re-tune it rather than assuming a clean export. A support-tuned knowledge base does not automatically make a good sales agent, and vice versa.


Second, contract overlap. AI agent contracts are annual and rarely line up, so you will likely run two tools in parallel for a stretch. Plan the double-pay window instead of being surprised by it.


Third, the conversion motion itself. Moving from a description-based agent to a demo-based one, or the reverse, changes what your funnel expects.


Give the team a ramp, keep the old tool live until the new one clears your bar on real traffic, and measure cost per opportunity through the transition. A rushed cutover costs more than the license ever will.


## FAQ


**What's the difference between Intercom Fin and an AI SDR?**


Fin is a support-first AI extended into pre-sales, optimized to resolve questions from a knowledge base. An AI SDR like Spara is optimized to qualify, enrich, and route inbound leads to reps. Different jobs: one answers, the other sorts and hands off.


**Does Spara have interactive demo capability?**


No. Spara focuses on multi-channel qualification and routing across its AI Chat, AI Voice, and AI Email products, and does not include a native interactive demo layer. To show the product inside a conversation, you need a demo-powered agent like Storylane RepX.


**How much does Storylane RepX cost?**


RepX Chat has published, visitor-based pricing: Growth at $2,000/month (up to 10,000 monthly visitors), Premium at $3,000/month (up to 40,000), and Enterprise custom, with a 30-day free trial so you can validate value before committing.


**Which is best for converting inbound website visitors?**


If converting visitors depends on them seeing the product, Storylane RepX is built for exactly that, because it surfaces an interactive demo mid-conversation. If your visitors mainly need fast answers or clean routing, Intercom or Spara may fit better.


**Can these tools replace a human SDR?**


They augment rather than replace. They handle first-touch engagement, qualification, and after-hours coverage, which matters because Storylane's analysis of 8,211 RepX conversations found roughly two in three buyer questions arrive outside sales-team hours (Storylane, 2026). With reps spending only about 40% of their time actually selling (Salesforce, State of Sales, 2026), offloading first-touch is where the leverage is.
