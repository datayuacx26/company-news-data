Intercom (Fin) is a support-deflection engine. Warmly is a visitor de-anonymization and alerting layer. Storylane RepX is a demo-led inbound qualification rep.


Pick the job you actually need done, then pick the tool. Do that in the wrong order and you will buy the wrong category and blame the product.


I am the CMO at Storylane, so read the RepX sections knowing where my bias sits. I will still tell you plainly where Intercom and Warmly beat us, because pretending otherwise wastes your time and mine.

## They aren't the same tool: name the job first


No page in the top ten says this plainly for these exact three tools, so I will. You are not choosing between three flavors of the same thing: you are choosing which job to fund. Support deflection, de-anonymization, and demo-led qualification are different problems with different owners and different metrics.


Buyers feel this confusion in real time. One told us, "Curious if it helps with some support kind of things like support ticketing, does it bake into like HubSpot CRM?" - \[role not captured, construction tech\]. That is a fair question, but it targets a different job than "will this qualify my inbound and book meetings?"


So separate the three jobs before you shortlist:


- **Support deflection:** you want AI to resolve a heavy ticket load. That is Fin's home turf.
- **De-anonymization:** you want to know which accounts are on your site so reps can reach out. That is Warmly's core.
- **Demo-led qualification:** you want high-intent visitors to see the product and get qualified in the conversation. That is RepX.


Our roundup of[AI SDR tools](https://www.storylane.io/blog/top-ai-sdr-tools) shows how these jobs split across vendors. The point stands: name the job, then judge each tool against that job alone.


## Intercom (Fin): deep dive


Fin is the best support-deflection AI here, and it is not close. If your primary pain is ticket volume, Intercom's maturity, help-center grounding, and unified pre and post-sale platform are genuine strengths.


Where Fin struggles is pipeline. It was built for tickets, not for qualifying and converting a buyer who wants to evaluate your product. There is no interactive demo inside the conversation, and purpose-built AI agents tend to respond faster than a support-first stack retrofitted for pre-sales.


Here is the honest pros and cons:


- **Where Fin wins:** support deflection at scale, mature help-center grounding, one platform for pre and post-sale, strong ticketing and routing to a live support queue.
- **Where Fin falls short for pipeline:** no interactive product demo, qualification is thin because the tool is ticket-shaped, and it is not designed to move a high-intent visitor toward a booked demo.


On pricing, Fin runs a per-seat model (roughly $29 to $132 per seat per month billed annually) plus AI fees of around $0.99 per resolution. That structure is fine for support, where a resolution is the outcome you want. It gets awkward pointed at pipeline: a resolution and a qualified opportunity are not the same event, so you can pay per resolution without moving a deal forward.


## Warmly: deep dive


Warmly's real strength is knowing who is on your site. De-anonymization plus real-time rep alerts is a genuinely useful job: you see which accounts are browsing and trigger outreach while intent is hot. For account-based teams, that visibility is the whole point.


The chat and AI layer is newer and lighter than the de-anonymization core. Warmly is excellent at telling you an account is present and warm; it is less about running a deep, product-educating conversation that qualifies the visitor and shows them the product. Those are different muscles.


Warmly fits naturally alongside an[ABM funnel](https://www.storylane.io/blog/perfect-abm-funnel) , where account-level signal feeds targeted plays. Use it when your gap is "we don't know who's here," not "our visitors need to see the product before they'll book."


A word on Warmly's pricing, because the public numbers conflict and you deserve the honest version. Directory pages reference an entry point around $40 per month, while the Business tier is referenced near $700 per month and an annual Inbound Chat package runs into the tens of thousands per year.


Those are not the same product: the cheap entry number and the annual chat number describe very different scopes. Get a written quote for the exact tier and seat count you need before you compare it to anything.

### How RepX handles the "what stops it from making things up?" objection


Whenever an AI speaks to buyers, the fair question is what keeps it accurate. RepX is grounded in your own material: your docs, decks, recorded calls, and demos, not the open internet. It answers from what you have told it is true, which is what keeps it on-message about your product.


On sensitive topics like pricing and competitors, guardrails govern what the rep will and will not say, so it defers or routes rather than improvising a number it should not quote. The trust concern I hear more often is not accuracy at all: it is fit.


One buyer captured it well: "Our industry is interesting, right. Because it's all construction guys and so they have their own vibes about robots and AI. Right. So I would be a little hesitant." - \[role not captured, construction tech\].


That is a real consideration. The answer is to deploy the rep where digital self-education actually helps, not to force it on an audience that buys through relationships.

## Which one should you choose?


You do not need a scorecard, you need to match the tool to your gap. Here is the clean version:


- **If your gap is support ticket volume, choose Intercom (Fin).** You want deflection, help-center grounding, and handoff to a support queue. Fin owns that job.
- **If your gap is account visibility, choose Warmly.** You want to know which accounts are on-site and trigger timely outreach.
- **If your gap is converting high-intent visitors who need to see the product before they book, choose Storylane RepX.** Demo-led qualification captures the buyer while the value is live, not after they have taken the answer and left.

When generating digital opportunities is the objective, demo-led qualification is the job, and that points to RepX.

Nobody in the top ten addresses the setup most real teams run: these tools are not mutually exclusive. A sensible stack pairs a support-first tool for post-sale with a de-anonymization or demo-led rep for pre-sale. The jobs overlap only at the edges, so the combination is additive more often than redundant.


Buyers already think this way. One told us, "I don't think it like will replace like HubSpot chat with it because we do have some workflows that are bringing in inbound and we want that to function. ## Migrating from a legacy chatbot to an AI rep


If you are moving off a legacy chatbot like Drift or a support-first bot and pointing at demo-led qualification, do not rip and replace on day one. Run the new rep in parallel, prove it, then switch. A condensed version of the approach:


1. **Map your highest-intent pages first.** Pricing, product, and demo pages are where a demo-led rep earns its keep, so start there.
2. **Feed the rep your real material.** Load your docs, decks, recorded calls, and demos so it is grounded before it talks to anyone.
3. **Define your ICP in plain language.** Write the qualifying questions your best AE asks, so the rep qualifies to your standard.
4. **Run parallel, then cut over.** Keep the old chat live on low-intent pages while the AI rep proves out on high-intent traffic, then retire it.


This is also where[digital sales rooms](https://www.storylane.io/blog/digital-sales-room-software) come in, giving qualified buyers a persistent space to keep evaluating after the first conversation.


## Frequently asked questions


**Which of the three is cheapest?**


It depends on your volume, because they price on different axes. Fin can look cheap at low resolution counts but climbs with seats and resolutions, and Warmly's entry tier is inexpensive while its business and annual chat tiers are not. RepX pricing scales with your traffic, so model each option against your actual numbers rather than the headline.


**Which one shows product demos inside the conversation?**


Only Storylane RepX. It runs an interactive product demo inside the chat itself, while Fin and Warmly do not. If letting a visitor see the product before they book matters, that capability is the deciding factor.


**Does Warmly qualify visitors or just identify them?**


Warmly's core strength is de-anonymization: identifying which accounts are on your site and alerting reps. It has a chat and AI layer, but qualifying and product-educating each visitor is lighter than its identification core. Treat it as visibility and outreach first.


**Is Fin good for pipeline or only support?**


Fin is excellent at support deflection and solid when extended into pre-sales FAQs. For actively qualifying and converting high-intent buyers it is thinner, because it is built around tickets and resolutions rather than demo-led qualification. Buy it for support, not as your primary pipeline engine.


**Which integrates with HubSpot, and can I trial each?**


All three connect to HubSpot, and also cover Salesforce and Slack. On trials, Fin offers a trial, Warmly lists a paid entry tier from ~$40/mo, and RepX Chat has published pricing (Growth $2,000/month, Premium $3,000/month, Enterprise custom) with a 30-day free trial, so you can validate each against your own traffic first.
