## Capabilities compared: what each one actually does


Feature checklists lie by omission, so I will be specific about modality under each job to be done. The pattern that matters: 1mind is video-first and inbound-only, while RepX is chat-first with voice and video as opt-ins and interactive product demos built in.


### Lead qualification and discovery


All three can ask qualifying questions. The difference is what they do with the answer. RepX routes on it, which is the exact workflow buyers describe: "at the beginning of the chat you can prompt them with the question, are you coming from retail or manufacturing?" - \[role not captured, Salesforce/analytics consulting\], then it shows the matching demo path. If you want a starting point for what to ask, our[discovery questions](https://www.storylane.io/blog/discovery-call-agenda) guide maps cleanly onto these flows.


1mind qualifies through a scripted avatar conversation, which is polished but linear. Drift's qualification was form-and-playbook driven, and it was good for its era, but it never showed the product. Qualification that ends in "book a meeting" is weaker than qualification that ends in "here is the exact view for your vertical."


### Live demos and product experience


This is where the three diverge hardest. RepX surfaces a real interactive demo inside the conversation, so a prospect sees the product instead of hearing a description of it. This is the core of modern[buyer enablement](https://www.storylane.io/blog/complete-buyer-enablement-guide) : let the buyer experience value before a human is involved.


1mind's avatar talks about the product convincingly, but a talking head is still telling, not showing. Drift never had a native demo layer at all. For an analytics-heavy product, telling is not enough, as one buyer explained: "it's always been a challenging for me to sort of show them the right data, the right column headers... for a manufacturing customer or a prospect, their objects to forecast are different compared to a retail customer." - \[role not captured, Salesforce/analytics consulting\].


### Objection handling and meeting handoff


Handling an objection is easy to demo and hard to do well, because the honest move is often to hand a human a fully-briefed prospect rather than to keep talking. RepX passes conversation context and demo engagement into the follow-up so the seller picks up warm, which is how modern[digital sales rooms](https://www.storylane.io/blog/digital-sales-room-software) are supposed to work.


1mind keeps the avatar in the conversation longer, which suits teams that want the agent to carry more of the call. Drift's handoff was a calendar drop. Neither of those preserves what the buyer actually clicked through, and that context is the most valuable thing you can hand a rep.


### Channels and surfaces


1mind deploys on your website, in-product, and on live Zoom, Teams, and Meet calls, but it is inbound-only and video-first by design. RepX runs chat-first on the website and in-product, with voice and video as opt-ins, and it can gate itself by audience. Drift lived on the website chat widget.

## What to watch out for: real-world limitations


Comparison pages that only list strengths read as ads, so here are the trade-offs, including mine. Honest limitations are how you tell a decision guide from a brochure.


- **Live-agent hallucination risk.** Any autonomous agent, avatar or chat, can take a wrong turn or state something it should not. The more freely an agent constructs responses, the more room there is to deviate from fact. Insist on grounding, system-prompt controls, and tight knowledge-base scoping from whichever vendor you pick, and test the failure modes before you go live.
- **Published, visitor-based pricing.** RepX Chat has published tiers (Growth $2,000/month, Premium $3,000/month, Enterprise custom) with a 30-day free trial, so you can size it against your own traffic. 1mind bills persona and ingestion work as part of the engagement, so the sticker range understates the fully loaded cost. Model total cost of ownership, not the headline number.
- **The avatar is not free polish.** 1mind's production quality comes from workshops and avatar creation that take time, which is exactly the setup risk covered above. Polished output has a lead time.
- **Some buyers distrust AI agents entirely.** Certain segments prefer human contact and will bristle at any agent, and pretending otherwise is a mistake. The mitigation is a fast, clean handoff to a human rather than an agent that tries to replace the rep. Design for the handoff, not around it.


None of these are disqualifying. They are the questions a serious buyer asks in the room, and any vendor worth signing should answer them without flinching.
