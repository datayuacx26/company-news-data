# How Sales Actually Uses Interactive Demos: A Role-by-Role Playbook From Storylane's CEO

When a product marketing team builds one interactive demo and hands it to sales, adoption usually stalls. Storylane's own CEO, Nalin Senthamil, laid out why in a live AMA with customers: sales isn't one persona. It's at least four, and each one needs a different kind of demo at a different point in their day.


This piece breaks down exactly what a solutions engineer, an account executive, an SDR, and a sales leader each need from an interactive demo, plus the framework Storylane uses internally to map demo type to funnel stage.


## Why it matters


Product marketing and sales don't misalign because the content is bad. They misalign because most demo content gets built for a single, generic sales audience, then handed off without a plan for who actually touches it and when.


Nalin's answer to this, when asked directly why Storylane adoption stalls on some sales teams and not others, wasn't a feature. It was a role. He recommends finding the rep who's already using the tool well, having that rep walk their peers through how they use it, and letting the credibility of a top performer do what a dashboard or a training deck can't. A demo can be technically excellent and still go unused if nobody's mapped which role needs it and why.


## The solutions engineer: builder and enabler


Nalin describes[solutions engineers, or SEs](https://www.storylane.io/blog/storylane-for-presales-sales-engineers) , as the creators inside Storylane. They know the product best, they build the[demo library](https://www.storylane.io/blog/interactive-demo-library-playbook) , and their job is enabling reps to use it, not just producing content for their own calls.


Two things came up repeatedly as SE-specific needs. First, keeping demos current when the product changes constantly. Storylane's answer is tokenization: instead of manually editing a name, date, or logo every time a demo is reused, an SE sets that field as a token once and it updates everywhere the token appears. Search and replace works the same way for text changes across an entire demo in one pass. Second, controlling who can edit versus view. SEs set up team structures inside Storylane and grant view-only access to reps, so there's one source of truth instead of duplicated, drifting copies.


## The account executive: before, during, and after the call


Nalin points to three moments where AEs get the most value.


Before a call, sending a short demo or hub ahead of time raises show-up rates and gives the buyer enough context to ask sharper questions. Storylane's own funnel is the proof point Nalin shared: about half of prospects who received a pre-call demo actually opened and engaged with it before their scheduled call.


After a call, AEs use a browser extension inside HubSpot or Salesforce to pull up the right demo and drop a tracked link straight into a[follow-up email](https://www.storylane.io/blog/why-your-post-demo-leave-behind-is-losing-you-deals) , without leaving their CRM. Every future view of that link feeds back into the same CRM record, so the AE knows if a prospect re-engaged before their next touch.


During a live call, presenter mode hides the Storylane interface entirely and shows the AE a separate window of talking points the buyer never sees, while the buyer only sees a clean, guided walkthrough. Nalin's framing: this isn't meant to replace a full live product demo, it's a lightweight, guardrailed sandbox for show-and-tell moments in discovery calls, where reliability matters more than depth.


## The[SDR](https://www.storylane.io/blog/best-ai-sdr-tools) : creating awareness in cold outbound


SDRs live inside outbound tools like Lemlist or Smartlead, and Nalin's guidance for them is the most specific of any persona: keep it short, 10 to 12 steps at most, because the only goal at this stage is awareness, not evaluation.


The idea is simple. An SDR inserts a tracked demo link, or a GIF that opens into one, directly inside a cold email template. When a prospect clicks, that engagement flows back into the CRM as an intent signal, something an SDR can act on even when the prospect isn't ready to book a call. Nalin was direct about why this matters now more than ever: cold response rates are low across the board, and giving a prospect something to click and explore on their own terms works better than asking for thirty minutes up front.


## The sales leader: measuring impact and driving adoption


Sales leaders operate one level up, and Nalin walked through the reports built inside HubSpot and Salesforce that leaders actually watch: average time to close broken out by high, medium, and no demo engagement, which reps are sending the most demos, and which specific demos and hubs are converting best.


But the more interesting answer came when Nalin was asked what actually separates teams where adoption sticks from teams where it stalls. His answer wasn't about better dashboards. It was about modeling: find your best-performing rep, understand exactly how they're using the tool, and have them present that to the rest of the team. A rep hearing from a peer who's closing deals with it lands differently than the same message coming from a marketer or an admin.


## The stage-by-role demo grid


Nalin returns to one framework throughout the session: a simple grid mapping funnel stage against role against demo type. It's the closest thing to a master reference for what do I send, and when.


SEs sit above this grid rather than inside a single column. Their job is building and maintaining it for every role underneath, which is why Nalin describes their impact as running across the entire motion instead of one stage of it.


## The takeaway


Before sending a demo to sales, pick one role and one stage first. A guided demo built for SDR outbound is not the same asset an enterprise AE needs for a multi-stakeholder follow-up, even if the underlying product content is identical. The teams getting real adoption aren't the ones with the best-looking demo. They're the ones who matched the right format to the right role before anyone hit send.
