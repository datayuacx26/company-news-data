# Delta connects travelers to Europe’s best eclipse-viewing destinations

On Aug. 12, 2026, the skies over dramatic Iceland and sunny Spain will darken, turning day into night during a long-awaited total solar eclipse. This rare phenomenon will be best observed along a wide path of totality crossing both destinations, offering an unforgettable spectacle set against landscapes of extraordinary natural beauty and cultural appeal.


Delta is making it easier for customers to witness this once-in-a-generation event firsthand, including the addition of flights from New York-JFK to Reykjavik (KEF) on Aug. 8 and Aug. 9, creating additional capacity and more opportunities for customers to arrive in Iceland ahead of the eclipse. Combined with Delta’s existing network to Spain, travelers have convenient access to two of Europe’s most sought-after destinations for viewing one of the most spectacular astronomical events of the decade.


### **Iceland: Where Nature Meets Totality**


Iceland, renowned for its volcanic landscapes, glaciers, waterfalls, and expansive skies, offers one of the most dramatic backdrops on Earth to experience totality. With minimal light pollution and wide-open vistas, the country is ideally suited for eclipse viewing.


The maximum point of the eclipse will occur just off Iceland’s west coast, with the path of totality crossing the western part of the country, making the capital Reykjavik and the surrounding southwestern region easily accessible viewing points. Travelers can also venture beyond the capital to locations such as Westfjords, the Snæfellsnes peninsula, and the Reykjanes peninsula, or Iceland’s southern coast for unobstructed horizons and striking scenery.


Whether watching totality from a lava field, black sand beach, or geothermal spa, Iceland offers an otherworldly perspective on this celestial event.


Delta Air Lines’ service from New York (JFK) to Reykjavik (KEF), including added flights on Aug. 8 and 9, provides convenient access for travelers looking to position themselves for prime viewing.


### **Spain: A Summer Celebration Under Darkened Skies**


Meanwhile, the path of totality will cross the Iberian Peninsula from northwest to east, reaching key cities where Delta, committed to connecting people to unforgettable moments, and its SkyTeam alliance partners offer exceptional connectivity. From the coasts of Asturias and Galicia to the Mediterranean charm of Valencia, Tarragona, and the Balearic Islands, Delta passengers can choose to fly into Madrid, Barcelona, or the Portuguese cities of Porto or Lisbon, and then travel to the areas of mainland Spain where they can experience the "double sunset." Because the eclipse will occur late in the afternoon, it will be followed by the setting sun, a particularly magical moment during the Spanish summer.


Delta Air Lines operates daily nonstop flights from New York (JFK), Atlanta (ATL), and Boston (BOS) to both Madrid (MAD) and Barcelona (BCN), in addition to a route with three weekly frequencies between Barcelona and Seattle (SEA) on aircraft such as the Airbus A330-900neo and Boeing 767-300ER.


Additionally, Delta Air Lines operates daily nonstop flights from New York (JFK) to Lisbon (LIS) and to Porto (OPO). The route to Porto is the airline's latest addition to the Portuguese market, providing a seamless connection between the East Coast of the United States and the Atlantic coast of Portugal and northern Spain. The northern coast of Spain's Galicia region (the provinces of A Coruña and Lugo, 190 to 250 miles from Porto, respectively) will be one of the best locations in all of Spain to observe this eclipse.


Whether beneath Iceland’s vast northern skies or across Spain’s sunlit coasts and countryside, the 2026 total solar eclipse promises an unforgettable fusion of travel and astronomy—one that Delta is proud to help bring within reach.
