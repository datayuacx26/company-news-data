# Why semiconductor facilities need a clean continuum approach to cleanroom management

Across the U.S., Europe, and Asia, chipmakers are racing to expand capacity with multi-billion-dollar facilities that promise to shore up domestic supply chains and meet high-performance computing demand.1


But ambitious timelines are colliding with operational realities. Workforce shortages, regulatory complexity, and the challenge of replicating mature manufacturing ecosystems are stretching projects by years.


The delays underscore what the[semiconductor industry](https://www.abm.com/industries/semiconductor) is learning: even with massive capital and cutting-edge technology, it’s difficult to accelerate a megafab. Still, 18 new fab builders broke ground in 2025.2


Today, each must overcome:


- Multi-billion-dollar capital outlays with years before revenue begins
- Dense coordination across contractors, utilities, and cleanroom phases
- Audit-ready compliance with SEMI standards at every milestone


ABM’s Clean Continuum addresses these challenges by providing integrated cleanroom management across the fab lifecycle—from site preparation and construction clean, through pre-startup qualification and OEM tool installation, into steady-state operations with proactive maintenance.


At each transition where contamination risk spikes, ABM delivers integrated support for particle control, chemical purity, and environmental standards—partnering with EPCs, equipment vendors, and fab operators to maintain the conditions critical to project success.


By deploying a multidisciplinary, scalable workforce from groundbreaking through ramp, ABM eliminates the coordination gaps that emerge when cleanroom responsibility shifts between stakeholders.


This leads to continuous certification compliance, fewer interruptions to production (ITP), and seamless transitions that keep billion-dollar investments on schedule and producing to spec at first wafer.


## Why a clean continuum approach matters


Semiconductor manufacturing operates at the smallest tolerances in industrial history. As chips shrink into sub-5nm geometries, the impact of even a microscopic particle can be catastrophic, causing costly process interruptions, contamination risks, and lost productivity.


Beyond technology, maintaining the necessary level of control requires a workforce fluent in the[specialized procedures and environments](https://www.abm.com/solutions/service-line/cleanroom-services) that keep production uninterrupted.


By focusing on proactive contamination control and minimizing ITP across all facility functions, the Clean Continuum is how ABM ensures quality of performance and lifecycle reliability.


## The evolving challenges of semconductor cleanrooms


[Modern semiconductor facilities](https://www.abm.com/perspectives/semiconductor-support-services-solving-for-efficiency-demands) push the limits of both science and engineering. As chips shrink and complexity grows, so do the risks.


### Downtime, ITP, and Audit Pressure


Every second of downtime can translate into millions in lost output (to the tune of $100k an hour).3 Facility audits, revalidations, and unplanned tool shutdowns compound those costs.


ABM mitigates downtime and ITP through a combination of proactive maintenance, predictive analytics, and continuous cleanroom management, allowing fabs to meet stringent regulatory and environmental requirements while maintaining operational continuity.


### Vendor Management Complexity


Too many vendors can lead to miscommunication and compliance issues. As regulations become stricter (especially around technical systems and environmental standards), it’s essential to coordinate these services smoothly.


### Skilled Labor Shortages


The semiconductor industry is facing a critical talent crisis, with estimates suggesting that up to one million additional semiconductor workers will be needed by 2030 to keep pace with expansion. Skilled cleanroom engineers, technicians, and janitorial staff are in such high demand that many fabs are investing heavily in automation, training, and workforce development.4


### Obsolescence of Break-Fix Models


Traditional “break-fix” or ad hoc cleanroom service models can’t meet the constant uptime, predictive maintenance, and acute contamination control demands of today’s advanced fabs. The shift is toward integrated, data-driven, and proactive cleaning regimens supported by AI-enabled monitoring and automated maintenance.
