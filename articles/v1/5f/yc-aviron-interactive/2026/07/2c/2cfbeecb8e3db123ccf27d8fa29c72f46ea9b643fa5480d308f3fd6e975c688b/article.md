Two great machines. The real question is which one you will still be using in six months.

If you are a runner or want the highest possible burn and your joints can take the impact, the treadmill edges ahead. ## Which burns more calories, a bike or a treadmill?


The treadmill wins this one, but by less than you might think. Because running is weight-bearing and uses more muscle, it burns slightly more per minute.[Research on indoor machines](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25162652/) puts running at roughly 8 to 11 calories a minute and stationary cycling at about 8 to 10.5. In practice, a 150-pound person might burn around 530 calories in an hour of running versus about 480 cycling.


That gap is real but small, and it disappears the moment intensity enters the picture. A hard interval session on a bike will out-burn an easy jog every time. What actually moves the needle for weight loss is total effort over weeks, not which machine has a slightly higher number on paper. The machine with the bigger calorie number only wins if you keep showing up to use it.


## Which is easier on your joints?


This is the bike's biggest edge. Cycling is non weight-bearing, so your knees, hips, and ankles are not absorbing the repeated impact of running.[Harvard Health](https://www.health.harvard.edu/staying-healthy/the-top-5-benefits-of-cycling) notes cycling builds strength and aerobic fitness with far less joint stress than higher-impact cardio. If you are coming back from injury, managing arthritis, carrying extra weight, or just want cardio that does not leave you aching the next day, the bike is the gentler choice.


The treadmill's impact is not all bad news, though. Weight-bearing exercise is exactly what strengthens bones, which matters more as you age. Walking at an incline is a smart middle ground: it keeps impact modest while still delivering the bone and calorie benefits of being on your feet.

## Which should you choose?


Match the machine to your body, your goals, and your space.


**Choose a stationary bike if you:**


- Want low-impact cardio that is kind to your knees, hips, and back
- Are a beginner, a senior, or coming back from injury
- Have limited space or noise-sensitive neighbors
- Care most about consistent weight loss without soreness sidelining you


**Choose a treadmill if you:**


- Are training for a run, a race, or want to keep your running fitness
- Want the bone-strengthening benefit of weight-bearing exercise
- Prefer walking, jogging, and incline work over cycling
- Have the floor space and do not share a wall at 6 a.m.

## Frequently asked questions


### Is a stationary bike or treadmill better for weight loss?


Both work well. Treadmills burn slightly more calories per minute, but stationary bikes are low-impact, so many people can ride longer and more often without soreness. Since weight loss comes down to consistent total effort plus a calorie deficit, the better machine is the one you will use most days.


### Which is better for bad knees, a bike or a treadmill?


A stationary bike. Cycling is non weight-bearing, so it puts far less stress on your knees than running. If you prefer a treadmill, stick to walking, ideally at an incline, and keep the pace moderate to protect your joints.


### Do you burn more calories on a bike or a treadmill?


On a treadmill, slightly, because running is weight-bearing and uses more muscle. Estimates put running near 8 to 11 calories a minute and cycling around 8 to 10.5. The difference is small and easily erased by riding at higher intensity or with intervals.


### Is a bike or treadmill better for losing belly fat?


Neither targets belly fat directly, since you cannot spot-reduce. Both burn overall body fat, and belly fat comes off as you stay in a calorie deficit. Choose the machine you will use consistently and add some intervals for a bigger burn.


### Is a stationary bike or treadmill better for beginners and seniors?


The stationary bike is usually the easier, safer start. You are seated and stable, with no balance or fall risk, and you control the effort precisely. A treadmill is still a great option for beginners who prefer walking, especially with a gentle incline.
