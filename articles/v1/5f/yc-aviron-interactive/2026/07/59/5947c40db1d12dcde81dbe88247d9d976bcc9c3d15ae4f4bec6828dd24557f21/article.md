One Aviron membership covers unlimited family profiles, which means every person in your home can train on the same machine, under the same $29-a-month fee, with their own profile, stats, and streaks. There is no per-person charge and no second membership to buy. That makes a single Aviron one of the easiest ways to get a whole household moving without the cost stacking up the way it does with most connected fitness brands.


What that looks like in practice:


- **Unlimited profiles** on one membership, no extra fee per person.
- **Individual stats** for everyone, so each person tracks their own progress.
- **Shared competition** through leaderboards and team challenges at home.
- **One machine** that serves different goals and fitness levels at once.


## **How does the family membership actually work?**


It is refreshingly simple. You buy one membership, and you add as many profiles as you have people. Your partner gets a profile. Your teenager gets a profile. You get yours. Each one is separate, with its own workout history, its own achievements, its own streaks, and its own place on the leaderboard. Nobody's numbers get tangled with anybody else's.


This is a real difference from how most connected fitness brands price things. Many charge per account or quietly steer you toward a second membership for a second serious user. Aviron includes the household in one fee. For a home where more than one person actually rows, that is often the single biggest reason the math works. ## **Can different family members have different goals?**


Yes, and this is where one machine quietly does the work of several. Aviron World has 1,000+ workouts across eight categories, so the same rower can serve very different people in the same week. One person follows a structured program like the 12-week Row Your Fat Off. Another just wants a calm scenic row to unwind. A third is chasing a high score in one of the games. Same machine, same membership, three completely different sessions.


Because each profile tracks its own progress, everyone gets feedback that means something to them. A beginner sees their early wins without comparing against a more advanced rower in the house. An experienced member can push hard and watch their own numbers climb. The machine adapts to the person, not the other way around, which is what keeps a shared piece of equipment from becoming one person's hobby that everyone else ignores.


## **Does sharing a machine actually keep a family motivated?**


It can, and the competitive side is a big part of why. With everyone on their own profile, you get leaderboards within your own home, plus Aviron's monthly Team Challenges that span every machine type. A little friendly rivalry between family members turns out to be a strong reason to show up, and showing up is the whole game. Results come from the workouts people actually do.


The fun matters here more than anything. Aviron is built so the workout feels like play rather than a chore, which is exactly what gets a reluctant family member to try one more session. The platform reports a 92% one-year retention rate, and that kind of stickiness is contagious in a household: when one person is genuinely enjoying it, others tend to join in. A machine that sits unused helps no one, so the family that competes and cheers together usually keeps rowing together.


## **Is one rower enough for a whole household?**


For most families, yes. A rower is a turn-taking machine by nature, the same way a treadmill or a bike is, and unless several people want to work out at the exact same moment, one machine handles a household comfortably. People naturally train at different times: one before work, one after school, one in the evening. The shared schedule sorts itself out faster than you would expect.


If your home does grow into wanting a second machine, Aviron's cross-machine platform has you covered, with one membership and Team Challenges that span the rower, bike, treadmill, and Abs Hero.
