## 1. It is low-impact and easy on your joints


Cycling is non-weight-bearing, which means your knees, hips, and ankles are not absorbing the pounding they get from running. That is why stationary bikes are a go-to for people coming back from injury, managing arthritis, or rebuilding after surgery.[Harvard Health](https://www.health.harvard.edu/staying-healthy/the-top-5-benefits-of-cycling) notes cycling builds strength and aerobic fitness with far less joint stress than higher-impact cardio. You get the heart-rate benefit without the next-day aches that make people quit.


Low-impact does not mean low-effort. You control how hard the ride gets.


## 2. It is real cardio for your heart and lungs


Pedaling at a moderate to vigorous pace raises your heart rate and trains your cardiovascular system, the same engine that powers everything from climbing stairs to keeping up with your kids. The[CDC recommends](https://www.cdc.gov/physical-activity-basics/guidelines/adults.html) 150 minutes of moderate activity per week for adults. Break that into 20 to 30 minute rides and you hit the target on a schedule that actually fits a real week, no special gear or athletic background required.


## 3. It builds lower-body strength


Every pedal stroke works your quads, hamstrings, glutes, and calves. Turn up the resistance and the bike becomes strength training in disguise: your legs are pushing against real load, not just spinning freely. On a bike like the Fit Bike S, with 160 resistance levels, you can start light and add load as you get stronger, so the same machine grows with you instead of getting too easy.


## 4. It burns a lot of energy in little time


Cycling is one of the most efficient ways to burn energy indoors. A vigorous 20-minute ride can burn a few hundred calories depending on your weight, effort, and resistance, and intervals (short hard bursts with easy recovery in between) push that higher in the same window. The point is not a magic number. It is that you get a meaningful workout in the time it takes to watch a sitcom, without leaving the house.


Twenty focused minutes can do the work of a much longer, half-hearted session.

## Frequently asked questions


### How long should I ride a stationary bike to see benefits?


Most people see meaningful cardiovascular and strength benefits from 20 to 30 minutes a day, most days of the week. That adds up to the 150 minutes of weekly moderate activity health agencies recommend. Beginners can start with 10 to 15 minutes and build up as it gets easier.


### Is 20 minutes on a stationary bike enough?


Yes, especially if you add some intensity. A focused 20-minute ride with a few harder intervals gives your heart, lungs, and legs a real workout. Twenty minutes done consistently beats an hour you only manage occasionally.


### Does a stationary bike work your stomach and abs?


Cycling mainly targets your lower body, but your core works to stabilize you the whole time, especially during standing efforts and intervals. It is not a replacement for dedicated core training, but your abs are not sitting idle.


### Is a stationary bike good for bad knees?


Generally, yes. Cycling is low-impact and non-weight-bearing, so it puts far less stress on your knees than running. Keep the resistance moderate and your seat height correct, and check with your doctor if you are recovering from an injury or surgery.
