# Smart Treadmill Software: Why the Screen Matters

# Smart Treadmill Software: Why the Screen Matters as Much as the Motor


The motor gets you moving. The software decides whether you are still moving in March.


**Buy a treadmill on hardware specs alone and you will probably buy a good treadmill that you stop using. Roughly half of people who start an aerobic exercise program quit within six months, and almost none of them quit because the motor broke.** Modern hardware is good enough that a 3.5 CHP motor, a 12% incline and a cushioned deck are close to table stakes. Software is the part that still varies wildly, and it is the part that determines whether the machine becomes a habit or a very expensive drying rack. Here is what actually separates a smart treadmill from a treadmill with a screen bolted to it.

## Does treadmill hardware still matter?


Yes, and anyone telling you otherwise is selling you a tablet on a stick. Hardware sets the ceiling on what the software can ask you to do. There is a floor you should not go below.


- **Motor:** look for around 3.0 CHP or more if anyone in the house runs. Continuous horsepower, not peak.
- **Belt size:** 20 inches wide and 55 inches long is the practical minimum for running. Shorter decks force you to shorten your stride.
- **Incline:** 0 to 12% covers essentially every popular walking and running protocol.
- **Cushioning and capacity:** the difference between a joint-friendly deck and a sore shin, and the difference between a machine everyone in the house can use and one only some can.


For reference, the Aviron Victory runs a 3.5 CHP commercial-grade motor, tops out at 12.5 mph, inclines to 12%, uses a 20.5 by 57 inch belt over six shock absorbers, and carries up to 400 lbs. It sits about 4 inches off the ground, which matters more than people expect in a room with low ceilings.


But notice what all of that gets you: the ability to walk, jog and run at various gradients. Every decent treadmill in this class gets you that. Once hardware clears the floor, it stops being the thing that differentiates one purchase from another.


## Why does the software decide whether you stick with it?


Because the failure mode of a home treadmill is not mechanical. It is behavioral. Around 50% of people who begin an aerobic exercise program stop within the first six months, and most of the drop-off happens in the first three, according to research summarized in[Sports Medicine](https://link.springer.com/article/10.2165/00007256-199417010-00004) .


Streaks, levels and rewards are not decoration. They are the part of the machine that argues with your excuses.


Here is the reframe worth sitting with: that is not a willpower problem. Most cardio equipment was engineered to be mechanically excellent and psychologically empty, and it has to compete for your attention with a phone that hundreds of very smart people optimized to be impossible to put down. Asking discipline to win that fight every evening at 7pm is bad strategy.


Software is the only part of a treadmill that can change the odds, because it is the only part that can make the next session something you want rather than something you owe. That is the entire reason Aviron builds games, competitions and progression into the machine rather than shipping a workout list, and it is why **92% of Aviron members are still working out a year later.** We wrote more about the mechanism in[how to make workouts less boring](https://www.avironactive.com/blog/how-to-make-workouts-less-boring/) .

## What is the difference between a smart treadmill and a treadmill with a screen?


A screen displays. A smart treadmill responds.


The practical test is whether the content can change what the belt is doing. On a true smart treadmill, a coached interval session raises your speed at the right moment and a scenic route steepens the incline when the road climbs. You run the workout instead of fiddling with buttons mid-stride. On a treadmill that merely has a screen, the video plays and you are still the one operating the machine, which means you drift toward the same comfortable pace every time and your fitness plateaus.


A scenic route that adjusts the incline as the road climbs is software doing work. A video that does not is decoration.

## How do you know the software will still be good in five years?


This is the part almost nobody checks, and it is the one that will decide how you feel about the purchase in 2031.


A well-built treadmill frame and motor will comfortably outlive the software running on the console. So the real risk in a connected machine is not mechanical wear, it is a company that ships a screen and then quietly stops feeding it. Three signals worth checking before you buy:


- **Content cadence.** Does new content arrive on a stated schedule, or was the library finished at launch?
- **Update history.** Has the software meaningfully improved since the machine shipped, or only received bug fixes?
- **Warranty as a signal.** A long parts warranty tells you the company expects to still be servicing that machine years out. Aviron covers parts for 20 years with an active membership, and 10 years on the drive motor and frame regardless.

## Frequently asked questions


### What is a smart treadmill?


A smart treadmill is a treadmill whose software can control the machine and track you over time, not just display video. In practice that means connected workouts that automatically adjust speed and incline, a content library that updates, user profiles with saved metrics and progress, and an internet connection for streaming and updates. If the screen cannot change what the belt is doing, it is a treadmill with a display rather than a smart treadmill.


### Can you watch Netflix on a treadmill screen?


On some, yes, and it is worth confirming before you buy rather than assuming. Many connected treadmills deliberately restrict you to their own content. The Aviron Victory runs Netflix, Hulu, Disney+, Prime Video, Paramount+, HBO Max, YouTube and TikTok directly on the touchscreen, plus Spotify and Apple Music, so you are not forced into a class on the nights you would rather watch your own show.


### Do you need a subscription to use a treadmill with a screen?


To use the treadmill, usually no. To use most of what the screen offers, usually yes. The important thing is to find out exactly what survives if you cancel. On an Aviron, membership is optional and you retain profiles, metrics, badges and the companion app without it, while classes, games and scenic content require an active membership.


### Is a treadmill with a screen worth it?


It depends entirely on whether the software does anything. Paying extra for a display that plays video you could already watch on your phone is a poor trade. Paying for software that adjusts the workout, tracks progress across years, and gives you a reason to show up on a low-motivation night is a good one, because the most expensive treadmill is the one you stop using.


### What is the best app for treadmill running?


The best treadmill app is whichever one your machine can actually respond to. A third-party running app can log a session, but it cannot change your incline, so it will never give you a true guided workout. Built-in software that controls the machine will always beat a disconnected app for structured training, which is why the integration matters more than the app's feature list.


### Can more than one person use a treadmill's software?


Only if it supports separate profiles, and the limits vary a lot by brand. Shared accounts mix everyone's stats together, which makes progress tracking meaningless and quietly kills the motivation loop. Aviron includes unlimited profiles on every machine and covers the household on one family membership.
