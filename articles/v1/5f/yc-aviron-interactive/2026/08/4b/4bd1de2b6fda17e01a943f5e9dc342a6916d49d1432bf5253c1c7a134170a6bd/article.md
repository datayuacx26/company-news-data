## What is actually in the Aviron workout library?


If you have browsed an Aviron product page you have probably seen eight labels: Classes, Games, Scenic Destinations, Streaming, Competition, Pros vs. Joes, Guided Programs and Power Play. That list is accurate but it is a lot to hold in your head, and it hides the simpler structure underneath.


Those eight are really four modes with two flavors each:


- **Train** covers Classes and Guided Programs.
- **Play** covers Games and Power Play.
- **Stream** covers Scenic Destinations and Streaming.
- **Compete** covers Competition and Pros vs. Joes.


Once you see it that way the library stops being an intimidating grid of tiles and becomes a straightforward question: do I want to be taught, distracted, transported, or challenged today? Everything else is a detail.


## Train: classes and guided programs


This is the mode that looks most like a normal connected-fitness platform, and it is where you go when you want somebody else to make the decisions.


**Classes** are on-demand studio sessions led by a coach, with interval tracking that holds you to the target rather than letting you drift. The catalog is built to meet you at your level, so a first-week beginner and someone six months in are not funneled into the same session.


**Guided Programs** are the longer-horizon version: multi-session plans built with pro athletes and trainers, including work off the machine as well as on it. This is the mode to use if you have an actual goal with a date attached, because it sequences your sessions instead of leaving you to pick at random every evening.


Use Train when you want progression you do not have to design yourself.


## Play: games and Power Play


**Games** are the thing Aviron is known for, and the mechanic is simpler than it sounds: your effort is the controller. You zap bugs, blast bricks and outwit space bots, and the machine reads how hard you are working to drive what happens on screen. The point is not that games are fun in the abstract. It is that your attention sits on the game while the effort happens underneath, so you keep moving without negotiating with yourself about it.


The same library runs across rowers, treadmills and bikes, with content built for each machine.


**Power Play** is the hybrid, and it is the most underrated corner of the library. It combines the visual pull of a game with the structure of a guided program, so you earn points in a race toward a fitness goal rather than just chasing a high score. If you like games but worry they are not "real" training, this is the answer to that worry.


Use Play on the days you do not want to work out at all. That is not a weakness in the design, it is the entire design.


## Stream: scenic destinations and your own shows


**Scenic Destinations** put you somewhere worth looking at: Coron Island in the Philippines, Lofoten in Norway, Utah in the United States. These suit longer, easier sessions where the goal is time on the machine rather than intensity, and they are the natural pick for a recovery day.


**Streaming** is the honest acknowledgment that some evenings you simply want your own show. The screen runs Netflix, Hulu, Disney+, Prime Video, Paramount+, HBO Max, YouTube and TikTok, plus Spotify and Apple Music. It matters more than it sounds: a machine that only offers coached content quietly loses you on the nights when being coached is the last thing you want.


Use Stream when the win is simply showing up for 45 minutes.


## Compete: leaderboards, records and Pros vs. Joes


**Competition** covers live leaderboards, head-to-head racing against friends, and racing your own personal records. For a certain kind of person this is the only mode that matters, because a number to beat is more motivating than any amount of encouragement.


**Pros vs. Joes** is the standout. You go head to head against professional athletes and Olympians: MMA fighters and boxers, football, hockey and baseball players, world-class sprinters, Olympic snowboarders and skiers, and Olympic rowers. You will mostly lose, which is the fun of it, and it recalibrates what "going hard" actually means.


Streaks, levels and rewards run underneath every mode, so progress accumulates whichever one you pick.


Use Compete when you have 20 minutes and want them to count.


## Which mode should you pick today?


The library is only overwhelming if you treat it as a menu. Treat it as a response to how you feel and it resolves quickly.


If today feels like... Pick Because


"I genuinely do not want to do this" **Play** (a game) Attention goes on the game, effort happens anyway. Lowest activation cost in the library.


"I want to follow a plan" **Train** (Guided Program) Sequences your sessions so you are not choosing from scratch each time.


"I have 20 minutes, make them count" **Compete** (PR or leaderboard) A target forces intensity that a video cannot.


"I have 45 easy minutes" **Stream** (scenic or a show) Time on the machine is the goal, so make it pleasant.


"I am bored of my routine" **Play** (Power Play) Game pull plus real structure, so novelty does not cost you progress.


"Am I actually improving?" **Compete** (your records) Same effort, same test, tracked over months. The only honest answer.

## Why does using more than one mode matter?


Because the most common way to burn out on a machine you love is to find one favorite thing and do only that until it goes stale. Novelty is a finite resource, and spending it all in month one is how a good purchase turns into a clothes rack by month three.


Rotating modes solves that cheaply. It also happens to be better training: varying intensity and stimulus gives you harder hard days and genuinely easy easy days, instead of a permanent medium. We went into the evidence in[the science of workout variety](https://www.avironactive.com/blog/science-of-workout-variety/) , and into the motivation side in[how to make workouts less boring](https://www.avironactive.com/blog/how-to-make-workouts-less-boring/) .


A simple rotation that works: one Train session for progression, one Compete session for intensity, and one Play or Stream session for the day you would otherwise skip. Three sessions, three different reasons to show up. It is a large part of why **92% of Aviron members are still working out a year later.** If you want the mechanism rather than the menu,[fitness entertainment actually works](https://www.avironactive.com/blog/fitness-entertainment-actually-works/) covers it.

## Frequently asked questions


### How many workouts are in the Aviron library?


Over 1,000 on-demand workouts across games, classes, scenic destinations and programs, with fresh content added every month. The monthly cadence matters more than the headline number: a library that stops growing becomes a novelty with an expiry date, no matter how large it was at launch.


### What are Pros vs. Joes workouts?


Pros vs. Joes lets you race professional athletes and Olympians head to head, including MMA fighters and boxers, football, hockey and baseball players, world-class sprinters, Olympic snowboarders and skiers, and Olympic rowers. You are unlikely to win, and that is rather the point. It is the fastest way to recalibrate what a genuinely hard effort feels like.


### Is the workout library the same on the rower, treadmill and bike?


The same four modes run across all three, so you get games, classes, scenic destinations, streaming and competition whichever machine you own. The individual workouts are built for the machine, because a rowing interval and a treadmill incline session are not interchangeable.


### Do you need a membership to access the workout library?


Yes, the library content requires an active membership, because classes, games and scenic routes cost money to keep producing. What is worth knowing is what survives without one: your user profiles, workout metrics, badges and the companion app all keep working, so the machine stays fully usable. The specifics are in[do you need a subscription to use an Aviron](https://www.avironactive.com/blog/do-you-need-a-subscription-to-use-an-aviron/) and[what the membership includes](https://www.avironactive.com/blog/aviron-membership-whats-included/) .


### Can you watch Netflix instead of using Aviron content?


Yes. The screen runs Netflix, Hulu, Disney+, Prime Video, Paramount+, HBO Max, YouTube and TikTok, plus Spotify and Apple Music. Treat it as a feature rather than a fallback: the session you do while watching your own show still counts, and it is far better than the session you skip.


### Can each person in the house have their own progress?


Yes. Every Aviron machine includes unlimited user profiles, so each person keeps their own metrics, streaks, levels and records. Shared accounts mix everyone's numbers together, which makes progress tracking meaningless and quietly removes the reason to come back.
