# International Wire Transfer Fees: How to Cut Costs

International wire transfer fees cost more than most senders expect, and the headline fee is rarely the largest charge. The exchange rate markup your bank applies above the mid-market rate often costs more than the flat sender fee: on a $10,000 transfer, a 3% markup alone costs $300 before your recipient sees a cent. Cross-border payment costs remain stubbornly high globally, with[World Bank Remittance Prices Worldwide](https://remittanceprices.worldbank.org/) data showing the average cost of sending $200 internationally remained above 6% in early 2026, more than double the G20's 3% target for 2027. ## What Are International Wire Transfer Fees?


An international wire transfer fee is the charge your financial institution applies to move funds electronically between countries. Domestic payments move through a single local network. Cross-border transactions pass through a web of intermediary banks, global clearinghouses, and currency exchanges before reaching the recipient.


This structural difference explains the higher cost. While domestic transactions clear quickly for a predictable rate, international payouts involve multiple institutions taking a cut. You end up paying fees at the sending bank, again at any intermediary banks along the route, and once more when the receiving bank processes the funds. The exchange rate applied at conversion adds another layer of cost that the headline fee never captures.


## The Full Cost of an International Wire Transfer


The upfront price of a cross-border wire rarely reflects the final bill. A single transaction carries three separate layers of cost:


- Initiation and receiving charges imposed by the financial institutions on both ends. A[2026 NerdWallet survey](https://www.nerdwallet.com/banking/learn/wire-transfers-what-banks-charge) places the median outgoing international wire transfer fee at $45, with major US banks ranging from $35 to $50 per send. The receiving bank adds its own incoming fee, typically $15 to $16, deducted directly from the arriving funds.
- Intermediary bank deductions taken mid-transfer. Correspondent banks along the SWIFT (Society for Worldwide Interbank Financial Telecommunication) routing chain each deduct a handling charge directly from the transfer amount before it reaches the next institution, reducing what the recipient actually receives.
- Exchange rate markup above the mid-market rate. Banks apply a proprietary rate that typically runs 2 to 5% above the interbank rate. On a $10,000 transfer, a 3% markup costs $300, often more than the flat sender fee itself.

## Who Pays International Wire Transfer Fees?


Both parties share the cost of an international payment. The sender pays the initial international wire transfer fee to their financial institution. For SWIFT (Society for Worldwide Interbank Financial Telecommunication) transfers,[sender costs range from $25 to $65](https://www.nerdwallet.com/banking/learn/wire-transfers-what-banks-charge) , a hidden burden covered in detail in our guide on[paying international contractors](https://usedots.com/blog/what-are-the-hidden-financial-drains-when-paying-international-contractors/) .


The receiving bank charges a separate incoming fee, typically $15 to $16 at major US banks, deducting it from the arriving funds. Intermediary banks also take a cut mid-flight. They deduct handling charges directly from the transfer amount before it reaches its final destination.


You should increase the initial payment to cover this hidden gap. This prevents unexpected shortfalls and gets recipients the correct final amount.


## How to Avoid or Reduce International Wire Transfer Fees


Figuring out how to avoid international wire transfer fees requires specific routing choices. Exchange rate markups usually exceed the flat sender charge, but you can lower total costs through these steps:


- Initiate transfers online instead of inside a branch. This saves[$10 to $15](https://www.bankrate.com/banking/wire-transfer-fees/) per transaction.
- Send funds in the recipient's local currency where possible. Chase, for example, charges just $5 for foreign currency wires versus $40 for USD sends.
- Check whether your bank account tier qualifies for a fee waiver. Chase Sapphire Checking, Bank of America Preferred Rewards Platinum Honors, and Portfolio by Wells Fargo accounts each waive standard outgoing fees.
- Compare the exchange rate markup across providers before initiating. A 2% difference on a $10,000 transfer is $200 lost before the recipient sees a cent.
