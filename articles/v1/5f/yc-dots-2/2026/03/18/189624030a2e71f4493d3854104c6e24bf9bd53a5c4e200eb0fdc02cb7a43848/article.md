# What is Just-In-Time Funding and How Does it Work?

**Modern global enterprises are moving away from the inefficient practice of parking cash in dormant regional accounts. By adopting a dynamic approach to liquidity, businesses can maintain full control over their capital until the exact moment a payout is required.**


The traditional model of global mass payments is built on a foundation of lazy capital. For years, companies expanding internationally have been forced into a rigid cycle of pre-funding. To pay a contractor in Europe, a vendor in Southeast Asia, or a creative partner in South America, finance teams have had to manually calculate anticipated spend and move large sums of USD into various regional buckets days or even weeks in advance.


This friction does more than just create an administrative burden; it actively erodes a company’s financial health. When capital sits idle in a non-interest-bearing settlement account, it is effectively trapped. It cannot be used for R&D, it cannot be deployed for marketing, and it certainly isn't gaining value. For a mid-sized firm, these fragmented payment silos and inefficient liquidity management practices can be devastatingly expensive. Recent research indicates that these operational overheads and lost interest opportunities cost such firms roughly 2.5% of their total annual revenue, according to the[BCG Global Payments Report 2025](https://thepaymentsassociation.org/article/bcgs-global-payments-report-2025) .


### **What is just-in-time funding?**


In the context of a[modern payments API](https://usedots.com/platform/payouts-api/) , just-in-time funding is a specialized financial flow that allows a company to trigger payouts without maintaining a pre-funded balance on the[payment platform](https://usedots.com/platform/international-payments/) . Instead of a "push" model where you send money to a provider and wait for it to clear before you can distribute it, this is a pull model.


The API creates a real-time link between your primary corporate operating account and the global payout network. The moment a payment instruction is sent, whether for one person or ten thousand, the system calculates the exact total needed, pulls that amount from your central USD source, and routes it to the destination instantly. This ensures that every dollar remains in your control, potentially in an interest-bearing account, until the very millisecond the transaction occurs.


### **How does JIT funding work?**


To understand the mechanics, it is helpful to look at how traditional systems fail to meet the needs of a fast-scaled business. In a legacy environment, if you need to pay $100,000 to global contractors on Friday, you might need to initiate a wire on Tuesday to ensure the bucket is full. If only $80,000 is actually claimed, $20,000 stays stuck in that corridor until your next pay cycle.


A **JIT funding** workflow changes the sequence:


1. **Instruction:** Your system sends a payout request via API.
2. **Verification:** The platform checks the recipient's identity and compliance status.
3. **Liquidity Pull:** The platform pulls the precise amount from your linked bank account.
4. **Instant Settlement:** The funds are converted (if necessary) and delivered to the recipient’s preferred method (e.g., local bank transfer, digital wallet, or debit card).


This level of precision is becoming a necessity as interest rates fluctuate. According to data from the[Federal Reserve](https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/h15/) , maintaining liquidity in a central, interest-bearing environment is far more advantageous than spreading it across dozens of stagnant settlement accounts. By keeping your runway in your own accounts, you maximize the time your money works for you.


## Why is just-in-time funding becoming the CFO gold standard?


The shift toward this model is driven by a need for better capital velocity. CFOs are increasingly evaluated on their ability to manage working capital efficiently. When a company uses a payments API that supports just-in-time funding, they eliminate the need for buffer cash.


In a global economy where cross-border transactions are expected to reach trillions in volume, the hidden costs of traditional wires, including intermediary bank fees and unfavorable FX spreads, are magnified by the time-value of money. Organizations that utilize JIT funding can effectively increase their operational runway by ensuring that not a single cent is moved before it is absolutely necessary. This is particularly vital for platforms managing high-frequency, low-value payouts, such as gig economy marketplaces or affiliate networks, where pre-funding thousands of small transactions would be an accounting nightmare.

Instead, they are looking for infrastructure that acts as a transparent layer between their bank and their global workforce. This shift is highlighted in the[2025 AFP Liquidity Survey](https://www.financialprofessionals.org/training-resources/resources/survey-research-economic-data/Details/liquidity-survey) , which notes that real-time visibility and control over cash positions remain a top priority for corporate treasurers.
