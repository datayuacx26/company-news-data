# Beyond P2P: What is an Alternative to PayPal?

For startups and growing businesses that rely on a global workforce, using consumer-focused platforms like PayPal for international contractor payments can quickly shift from convenient to costly and complex.


While PayPal excels as a Person-to-Person (P2P) wallet, it was not designed for the high-volume, automated, and legally compliant demands of Business-to-Contractor (B2C) payouts. This structural mismatch is the root cause of many financial and compliance headaches for scaling companies. Let’s explore this in detail.


Woman processing contractor payments online via Paypal substitute. \[Image source: Pexels\]


## **Why P2P Platforms are Not for Mass Contractor Payouts**


As your business scales from paying a handful of freelancers to managing thousands of international contractors, the shortcomings of a P2P-style platform become obvious and critical:


- **Slow, Expensive Transfers:** Traditional cross-border payments often rely on legacy banking networks. A[2024 analysis](https://electroiq.com/stats/cross-border-payment-statistics/) highlights that, despite advancements, almost three in ten clients still identify cross-border imposition of efficiency, including speed and cost, as a major pain point. Sending a single international wire transfer can cost as much as $50, and these manual fees add fast (and become unsustainable) when paying hundreds of contractors.
- **Compliance and Tax Burden:** The volume of international payments requires automated collection of tax forms (like W-8BEN) and robust identity verification (KYC/AML). Regulatory fragmentation and various tax obligations in each market add layers of complexity, making manual management a significant operational risk and drain on accounts payable resources.
- **Opaque Fees and Poor Reconciliation:** P2P transactions lack the detailed data required for easy financial auditing. Since they lack transparency in currency conversion markups and non-standardized fees, businesses struggle with incomplete payment information, making it difficult to reconcile payments and adding uncertainty to cross-border banking operations.


The market's movement toward high-volume, high-value API-driven transactions is undeniable. Global B2B payments accounted for 80% of cross-border payment revenue in 2023, proving businesses are seeking infrastructure built for commercial scale, not consumer convenience.
