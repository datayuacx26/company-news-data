### Verification Targeting Scams


Identity verification is crucial for businesses sending payouts, and it's one of the many ways to ensure you're sending funds to the right person. While simple verification methods made a big difference in the past, fraudsters are catching on. Verification targeting is a scheme that aims to exploit verification processes.


This form of fraud is strategic. Criminals target vulnerable individuals, often looking for victims on marketplaces or social media. They may pose as buyers or friendly neighbors, requesting that targets verify their identification using platforms like Google Voice. They convince targets to share verification codes through social engineering tactics and deception.


Once that happens, scammers can use verification codes to create Google Voice numbers linked to the victim's real number. That's when the real fraud can occur. When a criminal creates a fake number tied to a real one, they can wreak havoc in many ways, including committing payout fraud to steal funds.


### Platform Manipulation by Fake Contractors


Here's one of the ugliest fraud trends you need to look out for in 2025. While you might go to great lengths to hire qualified contractors, you can inadvertently invite fraudsters in. Many criminals go to great lengths to get hired for jobs, even if they have no intention of delivering. The goal here is to gain access to your platform so that they can disrupt the system and steal funds.


Bad actors can manipulate a system in several ways. Once successfully onboarded, they will have the same capabilities as real contractors. As a result, they have the power to produce spam, provide inauthentic engagement, submit fake work, etc. Some tech-savvy fraudsters will use automation and AI to ruin your platform and steal money every step of the way.


### Mandate Fraud


Mandate fraud directly affects your contractors. After hiring talented workers, they go through the onboarding process and provide financial data for payments. Bad actors take advantage of the trust that targets place in your company. They pose as legitimate organizations to deceive contractors, convincing them to change bank or payment details.


The goal is to get contractors to send payouts to a fraudulent account. Because scammers impersonate employees of your business, they can easily fall prey to the scam.


### Inside Jobs and Contractor Collusion


Here's one of many trends focusing on what's possible when contractors develop internal plans to defraud your company. Contractor collusion occurs when two or more contractors you hire work together to manipulate the bidding system or change the payout process. This fraud aims to rig the game, inflate prices, and force you to pay more.


It's an inside job, and many scammers get into your system legitimately. After earning your trust, they begin to collude and harm your business.


### Global Payout Fraud Through Shell Entities


Hiring global contractors is a game-changer for businesses in many industries. It's an opportunity to get a fresh perspective, work with talented people worldwide, and control your hiring budget. With a platform like Dots, you can easily pay international contractors while staying compliant.


However, there is a risk of global payout fraud. Scammers can create shell companies. They pretend to be legitimate contractors, suppliers, or vendors. Once they have gained your trust, they may submit fake or inflated invoices to extract as much money from your organization as possible. Because they use an international shell company, they can easily direct funds and launder payouts to stay hidden. The shell company shields the true identity of scammers and masks fraudulent activity, allowing it to continue long before anyone raises a red flag.
