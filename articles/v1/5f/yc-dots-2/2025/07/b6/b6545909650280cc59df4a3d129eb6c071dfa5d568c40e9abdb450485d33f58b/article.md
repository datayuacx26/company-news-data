### The Different Forms


Before we get into the deadlines, let's look at the different types of forms you might need to file with the Internal Revenue Service (IRS). There are several types of 1099 forms. In 2024, there are a total of 22. Fortunately, you'll likely only focus on two.


The 1099 forms are to report payments sent to individuals who aren't employees. They're different from your garden-variety W-2s, which are for reporting employee compensation and withheld taxes. Because businesses typically aren't required to withhold taxes from non-employees, you must file 1099s to report payments to the IRS and send a copy to recipients for tax-filing purposes. Generally, organizations will collect Form W-9 from recipients to gather relevant taxpayer information, but it's on the business to complete and file the appropriate 1099 form.


The two main types of 1099s businesses typically file are Form 1099-NEC and Form 1099-MISC. The 1099-NEC form is the most common and used for reporting non-employee compensation. It applies to companies that provide payments subject to self-employment taxes. That means any payments sent to independent contractors, marketplace sellers, freelancers, influencers, affiliates, and more apply. The reporting threshold for 1099-NEC is $600 of non-employee compensation.


The 1099-MISC form is for reporting payments not subject to self-employment taxes. These payments might include rent, prize payouts, awards, payments to attorneys, etc.


It's important to note that, in the past, businesses would use 1099-MISC forms to report non-employee compensation. However, the IRS began using the 1099-MISC forms in the 2020 tax year, reserving the 1099-MISC for reporting miscellaneous income.


### Important Tax Deadlines To Be Aware Of


While 1099-NEC and 1099-MISC fall under the IRS Form 1099 umbrella. They're a type of information return that reports payments sent to non-employee individuals during the previous tax year, they serve different purposes and have unique filing deadlines.


As of 2020, the filing deadline for Form 1099-NEC is January 31st. If that specific date falls on a weekend or holiday, the deadline moves to the following business day. You must file the form to report payments of the given tax year by January 31st of the following year. That deadline applies regardless of whether you file on paper or electronically.


It also applies to all copies of the form. Copy A goes to the IRS. Meanwhile, Copy B and Copy 2 go to the person who received nonemployee compensation. The IRS doesn't automatically share nonemployee compensation information with states. Therefore, you'll have to send Form 1099-NEC Copy 1 to state tax departments where applicable.


Starting in 2020, the filing deadlines for Form 1099-MISC are March 1st or March 31st. The March 1st deadline applies if you file on paper. However, the IRS allows businesses to file electronically until March 31st.


**Penalty for Missing Deadlines**


So, what happens if you fail to file these forms with the required information by the deadline? The IRS imposes steep fines that could cost your business big bucks.


Late filing penalties vary based on how long past the deadline you file. However, they are generally between $60 and $310 per form in 2023! You're looking at a steep penalty if you miss the deadline for hundreds of payment recipients. The penalty structure is as follows:


• $60 per form when filed up to 30 days late


• $120 per form when filed by August 1st


• $310 per form when filed after August 1st


The maximum penalty per year, depending on when a business files the late form, can range from $220,500 to $1,260,000.


### Common Errors and How to Avoid Them


Penalties can apply even if you file the forms on time. Incorrect information and omissions may make the form unusable to the IRS and the taxpayer, rendering it useless and requiring action to avoid additional penalties. Providing correct information before the deadline can prevent fees. The IRS generally doesn't penalize companies for inconsequential errors that don't prevent the payee from using the 1099 form to complete their taxes. However, bigger issues will cause problems.


There are two types of Form 1099 errors, and the IRS classifies them into two distinct categories. Type 1 errors include incorrect codes, payee names, addresses, amounts, etc. Type 2 errors include incorrect payee or payer information and filing the wrong 1099 form.

### Consequences of Non-Compliance


Earlier, we covered some applicable late filing penalties. However, there are even bigger consequences for not complying with tax laws. Failing to file 1099 forms at all can lead to big penalties for your business.


Failing to file will incur a minimum penalty of $610 per form. That's just the minimum. The IRS can also impose a penalty of 10 percent of the income you should have reported on the form. Unlike late filing penalties, there are no maximum limits to failure-to-file penalties. Intentionally disregarding tax laws may also put your organization at risk for legal action.
