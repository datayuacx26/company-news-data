### Significance of Transparent Disbursements


There's a lot more money in esports than most realize. Events generate revenue from tickets, entry and registration fees, brand sponsors, and more. Several stakeholders are interested in the money that flows in and out of esports tournaments.


On top of all that, you have players who pour everything into esports tournaments. They have a vested interest in the outcome. Opaque distribution processes are only asking for trouble. When the parties involved can't see where prize money goes and how it's split among players, disputes are bound to happen. That's not all.


Esports is also a common target of fraud. Event organizers work hard to keep games fair, but fraud can also occur on the financial side of esports. Everything from friendly fraud involving credit card payments that cover entry fees to match-fixing, unfair prize money distribution, and manipulation of the game results can occur.


Transparency is paramount in esports in general. However, it's particularly important when sending payouts to players. Without it, there will always be suspicions of fraud and a higher risk of unscrupulous activity.


### Common Causes of Disputes


Disputes can arise for many reasons, but they typically occur due to a lack of transparency at some point in the prize money distribution process. Unsatisfied with the lack of transparency, players may accuse tournament organizers of favoring others or splitting prize pools unfairly. Issues can also occur among esports teams who collect prizes and share them among players who contributed to a win.


Esports participants may also dispute prize money distribution due to contract issues, accusing organizers, sponsors, or teams of taking too much of their share. Those are just a few challenges that esports companies often face. Players can raise many more disputes, but they always stem from hidden prize money payouts. By leaning into transparency with a payouts API, your organization can avoid many common concerns, ensuring fairness across the board.


Not only does that help mitigate issues, but it also provides peace of mind for all stakeholders.
