# Meal Delivery With No Refined Sugar and No Seed Oils

## Why refined sugar and seed oils dominate prepared foods


Commercial prepared meals rely heavily on cheap, highly stable ingredients to extend shelf life and cut manufacturing costs. Industrial seed oils, such as canola, corn, and soybean oil, undergo intensive chemical refining that makes them resistant to spoiling but leaves them highly oxidized. Ultra-processed foods now make up 57.5 percent of the average American adult's calorie intake[\[1\]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5307821/) , creating a constant metabolic challenge for individuals trying to manage their health.


- Economic margins: Highly processed seed oils cost a fraction of the price of premium fats like cold-pressed olive or avocado oil, allowing mass-market brands to maximize profit.
- Artificial shelf life: Refined sugars and deodorized fats resist rancidity, meaning meals can sit in supply chains for extended periods without noticeable flavor degradation.
- Formulated palatability: Industrial additives and sweeteners are chemically engineered to stimulate appetite, masking the taste of low-quality, nutrient-depleted base ingredients.


For individuals managing chronic conditions, this commercial reliance on additives creates a hostile dietary environment. Standard meal plans frequently fail strict medical criteria because they quietly incorporate these metabolic disruptors. Frequent exposure to refined sugars leads to sharp blood sugar spikes, while a high intake of industrial seed oils rich in omega-6 fatty acids can contribute to chronic, systemic inflammation. ## The inflammatory impact on insulin resistance and blood sugar


Refined sugars cause immediate, severe glucose spikes that force your pancreas to release heavy doses of insulin. Over time, a constant cycle of rapid spikes and steep crashes depletes your natural insulin response, leaving your body struggling to clear sugar from your blood. For anyone managing blood sugar, eliminating refined sweeteners is the critical first step to preventing this metabolic fatigue.


The second, often overlooked half of the equation involves industrial seed oils. These highly processed oils are rich in omega-6 linoleic acid, which easily oxidizes during high-heat manufacturing and cooking. While ancestral human diets maintained a balanced omega-6 to omega-3 ratio of roughly 1:1, the modern Western diet has driven this ratio to a highly imbalanced 16:1[\[2\]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5093368/) . Consuming these oxidized fats drives systemic chronic inflammation, which damages cellular membranes and physically disrupts the insulin receptors on your cells, making it much harder for glucose to enter. This is why we avoid seed oils completely in our kitchen.
