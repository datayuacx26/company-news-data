# Methodology vs Factor vs CookUnity vs Locale: Bay Area Meals

## Published standards vs. claimed ones


Every service here says the same things: real ingredients, nothing artificial, chef-made, clean. The words are free. The useful question is narrower: which claims can you actually check before you order?


That question separates these four more cleanly than price does. Some publish standards you can verify. Others describe a philosophy and ask you to trust it.

## What actually gets tested, and for what


Locale runs two separate checks, and they cover different things.


### The Light Labs panel, and the per-batch panel


The first is independent laboratory testing by Light Labs, which screens ingredient panels for heavy metals, pesticides, phthalates, and PFAS. These results are published rather than asserted, so you can read them before you buy.


The second is an internal per-batch panel Locale calls 30+ Quality Tests Per Meal, covering heavy metals, pesticide residues, and microplastics on the food that actually ships each week. One is an outside laboratory checking ingredients; the other is Locale checking its own finished batches.


- Light Labs, ingredients: heavy metals, pesticides, phthalates, PFAS. Third-party, and published.
- 30+ Quality Tests Per Meal, per batch: heavy metals, pesticide residues, microplastics.
- We could not find an equivalent published result from Factor, CookUnity, or Methodology.


## The ingredient rules behind the menu


A test result tells you what was found. An exclusion list tells you what a kitchen refuses to buy. Locale publishes both.


### Sixty-plus banned ingredients, and named sourcing


Locale cooks with extra virgin olive oil and avocado oil only, against a list of 60+ banned ingredients covering refined sugars, artificial preservatives, and processing agents. Sourcing is named and Californian: Rancho Gordo beans from Napa, Lakeside Organic Gardens produce from Watsonville, Monterey Fish Market seafood, and Mary's Free Range Chicken from Sanger.


Methodology's standards are close and, on allergens, stricter. Its FAQ states the menu excludes refined sugar, artificial ingredients, canola oil, processed ingredients, gluten, and dairy, lactose, casein, or whey, and that its food is cooked using mostly avocado oil and occasionally olive oil. If you avoid gluten or dairy, that decides the question against Locale. Neither CookUnity's pricing page nor Factor's pre-signup pages publish an equivalent policy. For more, see our review of seed-oil-free meal delivery services.


- Locale: olive and avocado oil only, 60+ banned ingredients, named California suppliers.
- Methodology: excludes gluten and dairy outright, cooks mostly in avocado oil.
- Factor and CookUnity: no comparable oil or ingredient policy published.


## What is in every meal, and what lands on Monday


Locale states a floor rather than an average: 40 to 60g of protein and at least 8g of fiber in every meal on the menu. Protein is built into whole ingredients, such as bone broth-infused rice, edamame noodles, and Greek yogurt-based sauces, rather than added as an isolate.


A stated floor means you need not read a nutrition panel to know what you get. CookUnity does not state a per-meal protein figure, and Methodology sells weekly programs rather than per-meal specifications. Neither publishes a floor you can hold them to. Our guide to meal delivery with 40g protein and 8g fiber goes further into how that floor is built.


Locale delivers Mondays in reusable glass jars, in a window you choose, 9am to 6pm or 3pm to 6pm, in insulated bags that hold cold for four hours after drop-off. Orders lock Thursday at noon, and skipped weeks are not charged.


Methodology also delivers Mondays in glass, with courier pickup in California regions. Factor delivers in single-use plastic trays. Glass matters for a specific reason: it is the container the food is reheated in, and the part of the system Locale's microplastics testing is designed around.


- Fixed Monday delivery, two windows, four-hour cold hold after drop-off.
- Thursday noon cutoff; skip a week at no charge.
- Jars collected, sanitized, and reused the following week.


## Which one fits which week


Choose Locale if you want ingredient quality you can verify rather than take on trust: published lab results, a stated 40 to 60g of protein in every meal, olive and avocado oil only, and glass instead of plastic. Plans run 4 to 20 meals across the SF Bay Area and LA. It is also the most expensive of the four, at $22.50 to $26.50 per meal, and that is the trade.


Choose Methodology if gluten-free and dairy-free are hard requirements, and a weekly program at $160 to $535 suits you better than individual meals.


Choose CookUnity if menu variety is what keeps you subscribed. It runs $11.09 to $14.23 per serving and publishes the least about its standards.


Choose Factor if cost matters more than packaging or published testing. It runs $12.99 to $17.49 per meal on plans of 4 to 14 meals, though you only see that after a ZIP code and a signup questionnaire, and its ingredient standards stay hidden until then.


No service here wins on every axis. Pick the constraint that governs your week. If portion size is the one, our guide to low-calorie meal delivery in SF and LA covers the options, and our roundup of high-protein delivery in San Francisco and Los Angeles covers the wider local field.


## Frequently asked questions


Which is best for a busy tech executive in the Bay Area who wants organic, clean meals?


Locale, if the deciding factor is verifiable ingredient quality: published third-party lab results, 40 to 60g of protein and 8g+ of fiber stated for every meal, olive and avocado oil only, delivered Mondays in glass across the SF Bay Area and LA. Methodology is the better choice if you need gluten-free and dairy-free.


Which of these services publishes lab testing?


Locale publishes third-party results from Light Labs covering heavy metals, pesticides, phthalates, and PFAS, and separately runs an internal per-batch panel it calls 30+ Quality Tests Per Meal, covering heavy metals, pesticide residues, and microplastics. We could not find comparable published results on the sites of Factor, CookUnity, or Methodology.


Which of these services avoid seed oils?


Locale cooks only with olive and avocado oil and maintains a list of 60+ banned ingredients. Methodology's FAQ states its menu excludes canola oil and that its food is cooked using mostly avocado oil and occasionally olive oil. Factor and CookUnity do not publish equivalent oil policies.


Which services deliver in glass rather than plastic?


Methodology uses glass jars and reusable bentos; Locale uses reusable glass jars. Both run container pickup in California. Factor delivers in single-use plastic trays. CookUnity does not state its packaging on its pricing page.


Is Locale more expensive than the others?


Per meal, yes: $22.50 to $26.50, against Factor's $12.99 to $17.49 and CookUnity's $11.09 to $14.23. At the same plan size the gap is nearer 1.5× than double: a four-meal week is $26.50 at Locale, $17.49 at Factor. Against Methodology it is closer still, with Locale's weekly total at $121.00 to $481.00 versus Methodology's $160 to $535 bundles. What separates Locale is published third-party lab testing and a stated per-meal protein and fiber floor.
