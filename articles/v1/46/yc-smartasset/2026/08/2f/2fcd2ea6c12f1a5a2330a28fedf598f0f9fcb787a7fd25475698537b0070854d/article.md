# Cold Calling Scripts and Tips for Financial Advisors

Cold calling scripts for financial advisors are often developed in response to a simple reality: reaching out to strangers by phone is difficult, even for experienced professionals. In fact, only 2.3% of cold calls in 2025 resulted in an actual meeting being booked, according to one study.1 But as David Wright, executive director of practice development at[M&O Marketing](https://www.mandomarketing.com/) , notes, “cold calling is one of the oldest, time-tested marketing methods in existence,” yet it still requires preparation and consistency to be effective. Thoughtful scripts provide a framework for these conversations, helping advisors approach calls with greater clarity, confidence and purpose.

## Why Cold Calling Works for Financial Advisors


Like any other[marketing strategy](https://smartasset.com/advisor-resources/financial-advisor-marketing-plans) , cold calling isn’t perfect for every situation. But when executed efficiently, it can help scale your advisory business.


“There’s something to be said for the communication, negotiation and networking skills that can be developed and honed through a cold calling experience,” Wright says.


That’s because cold calling can help you build rapport with prospective clients. Talking to potential clients about their financial goals and needs can help create personal connections, which can be challenging to achieve through email.


In early 2024, Kitces Research surveyed over 1,000 advisory firms on their marketing strategies and practices, finding that cold calling was one of the most underused yet surprisingly effective marketing strategies. While only 4% of advisors said they relied on cold calling or door knocking in 2023, those who did reported generating new business.


“In fact, for all its disrepute, cold calling or door-knocking is 2nd only to[client referrals](https://smartasset.com/advisor-resources/asking-for-referrals) in terms of success in generating at least some new business,” Kitces Research wrote in its “How Financial Planners Actually Market Their Services” report in 2024.2 “The business won, however, ranks last in terms of revenue per client at a median of just $3,750.”


The takeaway? Cold calling can help you start a conversation with a potential client about their money choices, with you as a potential resource.[Prospective clients](https://smartasset.com/advisor-resources/how-to-prospect-as-a-financial-advisor) may know that they need help with shaping their financial plan, and your cold call could be the trigger that prompts them to take action.

## Cold Calling Scripts for Financial Advisors


[Scripts](https://smartasset.com/advisor-resources/financial-advisor-first-meeting-script) and templates provide a blueprint for initiating cold conversations. When drafting a cold-calling script for marketing, certain elements should be included, while others can be personalized to fit a prospective client. If you’re looking for a basic cold calling script template to follow, consider the following.


### 1. Introduce Yourself


Any cold calling script starts with an introduction. This should identify who you are and mention any previous contact you might have had with a prospect if applicable.


**Example: “Hi, my name is John Smith. I’m an advisor with XYZ Company and we chatted briefly at a virtual webinar a couple of weeks ago.”**


This does two things. It establishes who you are and provides a lead-in for the call.


### 2. Warm Up the Call


Next, you can briefly mention more details about your company. Sales and marketing circles often refer to this part of a cold calling script as the[value proposition](https://smartasset.com/advisor-resources/financial-advisor-value-proposition) .


**Example:** **“Our firm works with people in their peak earning years who are looking for ways to maximize retirement savings while balancing other financial goals.”**


This part of your cold calling script should be specific to what you do and who you help as an advisor. Avoid generalities, but don’t overwhelm prospects with too many facts or figures.


### 3. Elaborate


After making a brief[warm-up](https://smartasset.com/advisor-resources/warm-leads-for-financial-advisors) statement, ask the person you’re calling if you can have five to 10 minutes of their time. If they give the green light, expand on your warm-up statement in more detail.


**Example: “One of the things I do is help clients pinpoint what their biggest challenges are with planning for retirement. I’d like to talk to you about some of the struggles you might be facing with creating your financial plan.”**


This part of your cold calling script is where you’ll do most of the talking. But remember, their time is valuable and you don’t want to tie it up. Instead, use this call as an opening to have a more in-depth conversation later.


### 4. Give the Floor to the Prospect


Once you’ve outlined why you’re calling, give the prospect a chance to talk. This is where it’s important to[ask questions](https://smartasset.com/advisor-resources/questions-great-financial-advisors-ask) that can spark conversation.


**Example:** **“Do you think that you’re on track to reach your retirement savings goal? If you’re not, what’s keeping you from getting there?”**


Steer clear of yes-or-no questions, which can quickly shut down the conversation. And use the prospect’s answers to focus your follow-up questions so that you’re finding their pain points.


### 5. Close With a Follow-Up Request


As you prepare to wrap up the call, ask the prospect if you can[follow up](https://smartasset.com/advisor-resources/how-to-follow-up-with-a-potential-client) with them at a later date.


**Example:** **“Thank you for talking over your retirement plans with me today. Is there a time next week when we could reconnect and discuss some ways I might be able to help you in more detail?”**


This part of the cold calling script requires you to make a direct request for more of a prospect’s time. And it’s possible they could say no.


But if you’ve been able to show some value and get the person thinking about what kind of help they may need by following the script, this could lead to a lengthier discussion down the line.

## Cold Calling Tips for Financial Advisors


Wright says there’s no magic script or formula that can guarantee that a cold call will turn into a warm lead. Here are five basic cold call elements to help you get and stay on a prospective client’s radar.


**Mindset** Before picking up the phone to make a cold call, it’s important to get in the right headspace. “Attitude is everything when you’re making cold calls,” Wright says. It’s easy to get discouraged, so don’t allow doubt or fear to dictate your day.


**Enthusiasm** The people you’re calling can feed off of your personality so set a positive tone early. Aim for professionalism but[talk to prospects](https://smartasset.com/advisor-resources/how-to-talk-to-clients) in a way that’s friendly and approachable.


**Transparency** The fastest way to get a prospect to hang up is to beat around the bush about why you’re calling. So when someone answers your cold call, briefly introduce yourself and the reason for your call.


**Cordiality** After you’ve given a brief introduction, ask your prospect if it’s a good time to chat. Wright says cold calling is a form of interruption marketing, so it’s important to be respectful of a potential client’s time.


**Context** Again, cold calling is not about listing out your greatest achievements; it’s about you talking through specific solutions you can provide as an advisor. So as you’re discussing a[prospective client’s challenges or needs](https://smartasset.com/advisor-resources/how-to-grow-your-client-base-as-a-financial-advisor) , tailor your answers to their specific situation and what value you can provide.

## Frequently Asked Questions


**Does Cold Calling Still Work for Financial Advisors?**


Cold calling is still an effective marketing tactic for financial advisors when it’s approached with a clear purpose and plan. Developing cold calling scripts and[templates](https://smartasset.com/advisor-resources/email-templates-for-financial-advisors) can help you initiate conversations that spark a prospect’s interest and encourage them to take the next step and meet in person.


**What Are the Three Cs of Cold Calling?**


In marketing, the three Cs of cold calling are confidence, clarity and connection. Advisors who use cold calling as a marketing strategy need confidence to initiate contact with prospects, clarity to understand the purpose and goal behind a cold conversation and connection to keep the discussion moving forward.


**What Is the 80/20 Rule in Cold Calling?**


The 80/20 rule for cold calling suggests that 80% of results will come from 20% of your efforts. For advisors, that means focusing your marketing approach on prospects who are most likely to be receptive to what you have to say. Another way to apply this principle is to spend 20% of the time you make contact with a cold call talking, while dedicating the other 80% to listening to what a prospect has to say.


## Bottom Line


Cold calling can seem intimidating, but with research and practice, it becomes more manageable. While scripts are useful, persistence is key to seeing results. As Wright notes, “A ‘no’ today doesn’t mean a ‘no’ forever. Timing is everything; if someone declines, let them know you’ll stay in touch in case anything changes.”
