# If You Invested $2K in Meta at Its 2012 IPO, How Much Could You Have Now?

Meta’s 2012 IPO did not deliver the strong early performance that some investors had anticipated, but that performance tells only part of the story. After the company’s sharp decline in 2022 and its subsequent recovery, the long-term picture looks different. Here’s how much a $2,000 investment at Meta’s IPO could be worth today.


## What $2,000 Bought at the 2012 IPO


Priced at $38 per share on May 17, 2012, Meta’s initial public offering started trading on May 18.1 An investor who committed $2,000 at the IPO price would have purchased approximately 52 shares (52.6 shares before rounding). While that may not have seemed like a large position at the time, its long-term value would have depended on how Meta’s business and share price changed over the following years. The table below shows how many shares a $2,000 investment could have purchased at the IPO.

This figure is rounded to the nearest whole share. Fractional shares may have been available, depending on the brokerage. Those shares were purchased before several major developments for the company, including its 2021 rebranding from Facebook to Meta and the significant decline in its share price during 2022.2


At the IPO, few investors could have predicted where Meta would be 14 years later. Over that time, the company expanded its business while the stock went through periods of both strong gains and steep declines. The value of those 52 shares changed along with the company’s performance and market sentiment.

## What That Meta Stake Is Worth in 2026


Meta’s share price fluctuates throughout each trading day. This example uses Meta’s closing price of $556.71 on July 31, 2026.3 At that closing price, 52 shares would have been worth approximately $28,949 before taxes and transaction costs.

This example assumes one purchase at Meta’s IPO with no additional contributions, no shares sold, no taxes and no transaction costs. Meta’s performance shows both the opportunity and the risk of investing in individual stocks. Companies can produce substantial long-term returns, but those gains may come with large price swings that not every investor is comfortable holding through.


## What Investors May Have Missed


Meta’s share price went through extended periods of both strong performance and sharp declines over the years. Investors who reacted to short-term price movements may have had different results than those who made decisions based on a longer-term investment plan.


One lesson from Meta’s history is that market prices and a company’s long-term prospects do not always move in the same direction over shorter periods. Rather than making decisions based on headlines or recent market performance alone, investors often benefit from evaluating whether the reasons they originally invested have materially changed.
