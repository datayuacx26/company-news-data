# Berkshire Hathaway vs. Nvidia: If You Invested $2,000 in Each 15 Years Ago, Which Is Worth More Today?

Berkshire Hathaway and Nvidia followed very different paths over the past 15 years. Berkshire generated value through a broad mix of businesses and investments, while Nvidia grew from a graphics chipmaker into a major supplier of technology for data centers and[artificial intelligence (AI)](https://smartasset.com/investing/ai-for-investing) . A $2,000 stake in either company would have produced significant gains, but one delivered a much larger return.


## What $2,000 Invested in Berkshire Hathaway 15 Years Ago Is Worth Today


[Berkshire Hathaway](https://smartasset.com/investing/how-to-buy-berkshire-hathaway-stock) Class B shares closed at $74.01 on August 3, 2011.1 A $2,000 investment at that price would have purchased approximately 27 shares, excluding fractional shares. Using the August 3, 2026 closing price of $513.14, your initial investment would have grown to $13,855 today.2


**Berkshire Investment** **Approximate Result**


Initial investment $2,000


August 3, 2011 adjusted close price $74.01


Shares purchased $2,000 ÷ $74.01 = 27 shares


August 3, 2026 adjusted close price $513.14


**Ending value** **27 × $513.14 = $13,855**


Based on these closing prices, your stake would have gained $11,855 in 15 years, excluding taxes and trading costs.


Over this period,[Berkshire generated returns](https://smartasset.com/investing/warren-buffet-retires) through a diversified collection of operating businesses and investments rather than relying on a single industry. Its holdings span insurance, railroads, energy, manufacturing and other sectors, while its investment portfolio includes stakes in large public companies.


This[diversification approach](https://smartasset.com/investing/what-is-diversification) produced steady, long-term gains, but fell short of Nvidia’s growth in computing and AI. ## What $2,000 Invested in Nvidia 15 Years Ago Is Worth Today


[Nvidia](https://smartasset.com/investing/if-you-invested-10000-in-nvidia-20-years-ago-how-much-could-you-have-now) closed at $0.34 per share on August 3, 2011.3 An initial investment of $2,000 would have bought you roughly 5,882 of shares. This estimate adjusts for splits, dividends or capital gains distributions, and excludes fractional shares. Using Nvidia’s August 3, 2026 closing price of $200.75, that position would be worth about $1.181 million.4


**Nvidia Investment** **Approximate Result**


Initial investment $2,000


August 3, 2011 split-adjusted price $0.34


Split-adjusted shares $2,000 ÷ $0.34 = 5,882


August 3, 2026 closing price $200.75


**Ending value** **5,882 × $200.75 = $1,180,812**


Based on these adjusted closing prices, your original $2,000 stake would have gained approximately $1.179 million in 15 years, excluding taxes and trading costs.


[Nvidia expanded beyond its graphics processing roots](https://smartasset.com/investing/if-you-invested-10000-in-nvidia-20-years-ago-how-much-could-you-have-now) as its chips became increasingly important for data centers, accelerated computing and artificial intelligence. Growing demand for this technology helped drive a sharp rise in the company’s stock price over the period.


This rapid expansion created a much larger return than Berkshire’s diversified strategy over the same period. However, Nvidia also experienced significant price swings along the way, which may have made the stock difficult to hold depending on your risk tolerance and portfolio needs.


## Why Nvidia Outperformed Berkshire Hathaway


Berkshire compounded wealth through a diversified mix of businesses and[long-term investments](https://smartasset.com/financial-advisor/financial-investments) . Nvidia followed a faster, more concentrated growth path as demand for its GPUs expanded from gaming into data centers and AI infrastructure.


That concentration came with different risks. Nvidia was more exposed to semiconductor demand, product cycles and AI spending, while Berkshire spread its exposure across a broader range of industries.


Nvidia delivered the higher return over this 15-year period, but that does not mean concentrated technology investing is a good fit for every portfolio.
