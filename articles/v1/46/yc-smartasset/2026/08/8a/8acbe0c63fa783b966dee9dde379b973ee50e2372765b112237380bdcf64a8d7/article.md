# I’m 50 and Felt Hopelessly Behind on Retirement. Then I Ran the Real Numbers.

Turning 50 could cause you to take another look at[your retirement savings](https://smartasset.com/retirement/average-retirement-savings-are-you-normal) . If your balance is lower than expected, you may wonder whether you’ve fallen too far behind to reach your goals. The good news is that your current balance is only part of the picture. Depending on when you expect to retire, you might still have options to grow your nest egg.


## What the Math at 50 Can Tell You


The next 10 to 20 years may have a larger effect on your retirement savings than you expect. Every contribution you make still has time to grow, and each year you continue working gives you another chance to add to your[retirement accounts](https://smartasset.com/retirement/retirement-accounts) .


Your 50s can also bring changes that create other opportunities to save. Higher earnings, a paid-off mortgage or fewer family expenses might all free up more of your income for[retirement contributions](https://smartasset.com/retirement/maximum-401k-contributions) than was possible earlier in your career.


Beginning at age 50, many workplace retirement plans and IRAs allow eligible savers to make[catch-up contributions](https://smartasset.com/retirement/all-about-catch-up-contributions) . For 2026, you may contribute an additional $8,000 to a 401(k), 403(b), governmental 457(b) plan or Thrift Savings Plan.1 You can also add an extra $1,100 to an IRA, and if you’re between ages 60 and 63, you may qualify for an even higher[super catch-up contribution](https://smartasset.com/retirement/super-catch-up-secure-2-0) up to $11,250.

## What 15 Years May Build for Your Nest Egg


Using[SmartAsset’s retirement calculator](https://smartasset.com/retirement/retirement-calculator) , let’s assume that you’re 50 years old, earn $100,000 per year and plan to retire at 65. You have $250,000 in a 401(k), $100,000 in cash and plan to save 20% of your income ($20,000 per year). Your goal is to live off $70,000 in annual[retirement income](https://smartasset.com/retirement/average-monthly-retirement-income-by-state) and[claim Social Security at 65](https://smartasset.com/retirement/average-social-security-check-at-age-65) .


Following those assumptions, the calculator projects that you could accumulate about $2.16 million in 15 years. That’s close to the estimated $2.17 million the calculator estimates you’ll need to support your retirement income goal through age 95.

## Which Retirement Levers Can You Pull Now?


After turning 50, you have several opportunities to increase your retirement savings. Catch-up contributions let you save more in workplace retirement plans and IRAs each year. Increasing your overall savings rate and working a few additional years can also improve your retirement outlook.


Another key decision is when to claim Social Security. Waiting longer generally increases your monthly benefit, while claiming earlier provides income sooner at a lower amount.
