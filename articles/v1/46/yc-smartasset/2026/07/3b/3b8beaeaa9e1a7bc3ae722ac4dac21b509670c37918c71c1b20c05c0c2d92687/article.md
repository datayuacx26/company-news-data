# At What Age Do Seniors Stop Paying Property Taxes? The Answer Depends on Your State.

Homeowners often believe they[stop paying property taxes after a certain age](https://smartasset.com/retirement/at-what-age-do-seniors-stop-paying-property-taxes) . In reality, there is no nationwide age at which property taxes automatically disappear. Whether you qualify for a property tax break depends on where you live, since states and local governments offer their own[property tax exemptions](https://smartasset.com/taxes/do-you-qualify-for-property-tax-exemptions) or credits. Not knowing what your state offers could mean missing on an opportunity to reduce your property tax bill.


## Common Types of Property Tax Relief


If your state offers[property tax relief](https://smartasset.com/taxes/what-is-a-homestead-tax-exemption) for older homeowners, it will generally fall into one of four categories. Some programs reduce the amount of tax you owe each year, while others limit future tax increases or allow you to postpone payment. The type of relief available depends on state and local law.


Type What It Does


**Exemption** Reduces the taxable value of your home, lowering your property tax bill.


**Credit** Reduces the amount of property tax you owe or provides a refund, depending on the program.


**Freeze** Limits future increases in your property’s assessed value or tax bill after you qualify.


**Deferral** Lets you postpone paying property taxes until a later date, often when the home is sold or ownership changes.


## Millions of Seniors Are Missing Property Tax Relief


AARP says millions of older homeowners who may qualify for lower property taxes never apply. According to its Property Tax-Aide research, only 8% (roughly 720,000) out of more than 9 million eligible seniors claim available property tax relief.1


One reason may be that most property tax relief programs are not automatic. Homeowners typically must apply, meet eligibility requirements and submit documentation before a deadline.


Eligibility requirements and application deadlines also vary by state and, in some cases, by local government. Depending on where you live, you may need to meet age, income, residency or other requirements before qualifying. Missing a deadline or failing to apply can mean[paying the full property tax bill](https://smartasset.com/taxes/property-taxes) even if relief is available.

## How to Find Property Tax Relief in Your Area


Start by checking your state or local tax authority’s website for the property tax relief programs available where you live. Because many programs are administered at the county or municipal level, eligibility requirements and benefits can vary from one community to the next.


As you review the available programs, pay close attention to the age, income and residency requirements. You’ll also want to note the application deadline and gather any documents you may need, such as proof of age, income or primary residence.


Before you apply, check whether your city or county offers additional exemptions, credits or other forms of[property tax relief](https://smartasset.com/taxes/smartasset-guide-to-lower-property-taxes) beyond what the state provides. A few minutes of research could uncover savings you might otherwise miss.
