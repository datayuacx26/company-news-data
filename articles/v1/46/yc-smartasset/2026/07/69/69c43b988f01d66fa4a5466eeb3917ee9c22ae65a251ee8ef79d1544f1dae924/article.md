This guide outlines practical lead generation tactics that many advisors use to grow their client base.

## Strategy #1: Build Your Client Referral Network


Client referrals are one of the most commonly used lead-generation strategies, and they’re also one of the most effective ways to grow your business. A 2024 Kitces survey of nearly 1,000 advisory practices found that client referrals scored highest among marketing tactics for both lead quantity and lead quality.2


Encouraging satisfied clients to refer their friends and family can be a straightforward and effective way to expand your advisory business, particularly when targeting[high-net-worth individuals](https://smartasset.com/advisor-resources/how-to-attract-high-net-worth-clients) . If you already work with wealthy clients, tapping their network may create opportunities for introductions. Referrals can bring the next generation of clients to your firm if your current clients recommend you to their heirs.


“Clients have busy lives and even though they like you, it may not occur to them that you’re looking for new prospects,” says Alvin Carlos, a[Certified Financial Planner™ (CFP®](https://smartasset.com/financial-advisor/what-is-a-certified-financial-planner-cfp) ) and managing partner at Washington, D.C.-based[District Capital Management](https://districtcapitalmanagement.com/) . “Tell them you’re looking to help 10 new clients this year, and you’d rather help those that are important to them.”


This approach enables you to strengthen relationships with your current clients by showing that you care about the people important to them. It also fosters an ongoing cycle of[lead generation](https://smartasset.com/advisor-resources/financial-lead-generation) , as new clients may continue to refer others to your business.


How do you get more referrals from clients? These strategies can help.


- **Ask:**[Asking for referrals](https://smartasset.com/advisor-resources/best-referral-sources-for-financial-advisors-2023) can be as simple as including a mention in your regular email newsletter. If you’re not yet using email to stay connected with current or potential clients, it’s a valuable tool worth exploring for lead generation and client engagement.
- **Develop a referral program:** A referral program can motivate clients to tell others about your business if there’s some incentive for doing so. Some advisors explore structured referral arrangements or client appreciation initiatives, though referral compensation may be subject to regulatory requirements and disclosure obligations.
- **Hosting client events:** If you’re hesitant to ask clients for referrals directly, you can encourage them organically through appreciation events. Taking time out of your busy schedule to meet with clients in an informal setting and treat them to a fun or interesting event can convey how much you value them, which could lead to more referrals.
- **Network:** Your clients aren’t the only source you may look to for referrals. Building[centers of influence](https://smartasset.com/advisor-resources/centers-of-influence) with other advisors, attorneys, and tax professionals can give you access to a broader pool of prospects.


Note that compliance rules apply when[paying for referrals](https://smartasset.com/advisor-resources/can-financial-advisors-pay-referral-fees) ; you must disclose any financial incentives you offer in exchange. Disclosures are also required when including third-party reviews or client testimonials in your marketing materials.

## Strategy #3: Take Advantage of Social Media


Social media can be an integral part of an advisor’s marketing and lead generation plan. After all, this may be the first contact a prospective client has with your advisory business. While this method is more complex than asking for referrals or using lead generation services, it can drive highly valuable organic growth.


Which channels or platforms are most suited to advisors? The Broadridge survey found that four in 10 advisors have added new clients through social media, with[LinkedIn](https://smartasset.com/advisor-resources/linkedin-marketing-for-financial-advisors) and Facebook being the channels where they’ve found the most success.


Establishing a presence on[social media](https://smartasset.com/advisor-resources/social-media-marketing-tips-for-financial-advisors) matters, but consider how that time is spent. Paul Sundin, certified public accountant (CPA), tax strategist and CEO of[Emparion](https://www.emparion.com/) in Chandler, Arizona, says it’s important to go beyond the basics of just creating profiles or making an offer.


“To have effective lead generation as a financial advisor, you need to do market research,” Sundin says. Specifically, it’s important to research the patterns that entice your target market, he noted. Social media can be an invaluable tool for that.


“Search for customer reviews, activities on different social media platforms and overall social media behavior,” he added. “Once you see the pattern for the keywords that entices them, use the keywords in your copy and content.”


This, Sundin says, can be a recipe for driving the right people to your[advisor website](https://smartasset.com/advisor-resources/financial-advisor-websites) .


If you don’t have hours to devote to social media each day, consider whether this is a task you might outsource. A virtual assistant, for instance, may be able to handle everything from content creation to posting to fielding comments so that your social media handles stay active and engaged.


## Strategy #4: Establish Trust by Providing Value


In[financial advisor marketing](https://smartasset.com/advisor-resources/digital-marketing-strategies-for-financial-advisors) , and marketing in general, one of the most common and effective lead-generation strategies is offering something for free, such as a[lead magnet](https://smartasset.com/advisor-resources/financial-advisor-lead-magnet) .


It’s actually a simple concept: You give[prospects](https://smartasset.com/advisor-resources/how-to-prospect-as-a-financial-advisor) something either useful or necessary, absolutely free. This can help initiate a relationship that may eventually lead to a client engagement. Lead magnet ideas for advisors might include:


- A printable retirement readiness checklist
- An ebook on estate planning and charitable giving
- A financial planning quiz
- A case study or white paper on a topic your clients care about
- An exclusive episode of a podcast you host


You can make your lead magnets available on your website. Prospects can access them by sharing their email address, which gives you another opportunity to market to them. This is a strategy that Shawn Plummer, CEO of[The Annuity Expert](https://www.annuityexpertadvice.com/) , has successfully used to capture an audience and generate clients.


“I leverage my expertise on annuities and insurance to educate visitors, provide thought leadership and position myself as an expert,” Plummer says. “This increases the trust I have with my visitors, which in turn increases the likelihood that they may reach out for a quote.”


The key to any lead magnet you create is personalization. In the Broadridge survey, just four in 10 advisors said they created content tailored to their ideal client base, despite nearly half of clients saying they desired more personalization. Creating an ideal client avatar or[buyer persona](https://smartasset.com/advisor-resources/buyer-personas) can help you home in on the type of content that’s most likely to resonate with your intended audience.


## Strategy #5. Share Your Expertise


[Seminar marketing](https://smartasset.com/advisor-resources/seminar-marketing-for-financial-advisors) is an opportunity to get up close and personal with prospective clients and demonstrate your unique value proposition. Hosting a seminar, workshop, or lunch-and-learn event on a topic relevant to your ideal clients and their pain points can put you in contact with leads who may be primed to take the next step.


If you’re unsure which topic to choose, put yourself in your ideal client’s shoes and ask yourself what they need help with most. Focusing on a single topic and including a strong call to action can make your seminar more impactful and effective for generating leads.


You’re not limited to in-person events either. Hosting online seminars or webinars could help you reach a broader audience, enabling you to grow your client base beyond your local area. You can set up the seminar details in your CRM to keep track of the number of invites sent and responses received.

## Tips for Converting Your Leads Into Clients


Generating leads is just the first step toward the ultimate goal of turning a prospect into a client. Successfully guiding leads through the sales process requires advisors to use a diverse set of skills and strategies.


### 1. Demonstrate Understanding


Understanding a person’s unique needs and demonstrating that you’re listening can go a long way toward building trust with prospective clients, says Carla T. Adams, founder of[Ametrine Wealth](https://www.ametrinewealth.com/) in Lake Orion, Michigan.


“We as advisors (and actually humans in general) are so keen to talk about ourselves and show off what we have to offer. When a prospect gives you an overview of their situation and you jump right in to tell them what you can do for them, it is actually often off-putting,” Adams said. “It takes people time to open up and talk about what’s really important to them, so give them the time and space to open up.”


### 2. Schedule a Timely Follow-Up


Not all prospects will sign on as clients after an initial meeting, and that’s okay. While persistence is vital for converting leads, it’s important to follow up strategically by using a combination of phone calls, emails, text messages and more. You don’t want to overdo it, though, so knowing[when to follow up](https://smartasset.com/advisor-resources/financial-advisor-follow-up-email) and when to give space is crucial.


This is something your customer relationship management (CRM) platform or an AI tool may be able to help with. More CRMs are integrating AI functionality that can draft follow-up emails you can send to prospects and clients, based on the inputs you provide. If your CRM doesn’t offer that feature, you may want to consider using an[AI note-taking tool](https://smartasset.com/advisor-resources/ai-note-taker-for-financial-advisors) that generates summaries and follow-up email templates.


### 3. Evaluate Your Client Conversion Rate


An advisor’s conversion rate will vary based on their target market, niche and sales process, according to Michael Collins, founder and CEO of[WinCap Financial](https://wincapfinancial.com/) in Winchester, Massachusetts. However, Collins recommends aiming to add between 10 to 15 new clients per year, achieving a conversion rate of 10% to 20%. At those rates, that means you would need to generate between 100 and 150 leads each year.


“However, it’s important to note that quality is more important than quantity. It’s better to have a smaller number of high-quality leads than a large number of unqualified ones,” Collins added. “As advisors, our focus should be on attracting the right clients who align with our niche and values.”


In fact, Adams thinks advisors shouldn’t be afraid to turn away leads that may not be the best fit for them, even if they have high revenue potential.


“Having clients that create too much stress and negative energy are a distraction from properly servicing your other clients and from seeking out new business that will better align with your values and keep your work enjoyable,” says Adams.


### 4. Build an Email Nurture Funnel


For leads not ready to convert immediately, build an email sequence that delivers consistent value over time. A well-designed nurture campaign should:


- Segment contacts based on interest or stage
- Include 5–7 emails over a few weeks
- Focus on education, not just selling
- Include a strong call to action (CTA) with each message
