# If You Invested $10,000 in Tesla 5 Years Ago, How Much Could You Have Now?

A $10,000 investment in Tesla five years ago would have experienced periods of significant gains and sharp declines. Investors who held their shares through those swings would have ended up with a different result than those who sold during the downturns. Here’s how much that investment could be worth today, and what the past five years can show about holding a volatile stock.


## **What $10,000 in Tesla Bought in 2021**


Tesla closed at a split-adjusted price of $220.17 on July 20, 2021.1 A $10,000 investment at that price would have purchased 45 shares, with $92.35 remaining in cash. The table below shows how that[investment](https://smartasset.com/investing/investment-calculator) breaks down.


Category Calculation


Initial investment **$10,000.00**


Split-adjusted Tesla share price **$220.17**


Shares purchased **$10,000 ÷ $220.17 = 45 shares**


Cost of 45 shares **45 × $220.17 = $9,907.65**


Cash remaining **$10,000.00 − $9,907.65 = $92.35**


Tesla completed a three-for-one[stock split](https://smartasset.com/investing/what-is-a-stock-split) in August 2022. The $220.17 share price above has already been adjusted for that split, making it directly comparable with Tesla’s current[share price](https://smartasset.com/investing/how-is-stock-price-determined-in-real-time) . Before the split, a $10,000 investment would have purchased 15 shares. After the split, those 15 shares became 45 shares.


A stock split increases the number of shares you own while reducing the price of each share by the same proportion. It does not change the total value of your investment at the time of the split.

## What That Investment Is Worth Today


Tesla closed at $380.84 on July 17, 2026. At that price, the 45 shares purchased with the original $10,000 investment would be worth $17,137.80. The table below shows how the investment performed over the five-year period.


Category Calculation


Current Tesla share price **$380.84**


Value of 45 shares **45 × $380.84 = $17,137.80**


Approximate gain **$17,137.80 − $10,000 = $7,137.80**


Total return **$7,137.80 ÷ $10,000 = 71.38%**


Based on these figures, the original $10,000 investment would have increased by approximately $7,137.80, representing a total return of 71.38%.


The table below shows how the value of those shares changed at different points over the five-year[holding period](https://smartasset.com/investing/what-is-the-average-stock-holding-period) .


Point in Investment Tesla Share Price Value of 45 Shares


July 20, 2021 purchase $220.17 (split adjusted) $9,907.65


December 27, 2022 low $109.10 $4,909.50


July 17, 2026 $380.84 $17,137.80


This example is based on historical share prices and is intended for illustration only. It excludes[taxes](https://smartasset.com/taxes/income-taxes) , transaction costs and the $92.35 in cash that remained after the original purchase.


## What the Past Five Years Can Show About Holding a Volatile Stock


Although the investment was worth $17,137.80 by July 2026, the return did not come from a steady increase in Tesla’s share price. During the five-year holding period, the stock experienced sharp gains and declines that would have tested an investor’s willingness to continue holding the shares.


One example came in 2022. Tesla reached a split-adjusted closing high of $409.97 on Nov. 4, 2021, before falling to $109.10 on Dec. 27, 2022, a decline of about 73% from its peak. Since this example assumes you purchased the shares before that high,[your investment would have declined by less](https://smartasset.com/investing) . Even so, it still would have lost about half of its value.


Category Calculation


Original investment value **$9,907.65**


Value at Dec. 27, 2022 low **45 × $109.10 = $4,909.50**


Paper loss **$9,907.65 − $4,909.50 = $4,998.15**


Decline from starting value **$4,998.15 ÷ $9,907.65 = 50.45%**


This example shows one of the challenges of holding a volatile stock. Large declines can make it difficult to stay invested, particularly when there is no way to know whether prices will continue falling or eventually recover. Some stocks recover after steep losses, while others do not. Past performance does not guarantee future results.
