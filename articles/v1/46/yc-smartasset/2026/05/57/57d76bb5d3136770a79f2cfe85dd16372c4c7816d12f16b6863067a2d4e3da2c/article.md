# How to Prospect as a Financial Advisor

[Financial advisor prospecting](https://smartasset.com/advisor-resources/financial-advisor-prospecting-tips) is about building relationships and starting conversations that naturally lead to new clients. Rather than trying to reach everyone, focusing on a well-defined client profile allows for more meaningful connections. Strong referral networks and strategic events can open doors to prospects who are already looking for guidance, while a robust digital presence keeps you visible and credible. Whether through social media, content marketing or direct outreach, successful prospecting happens when advisors consistently engage with the right audience.

## How to Prospect as a Financial Advisor


The average advisor needs to add 34 new clients to their rolls each year to meet their growth goals, according to the 2024 Natixis Global Survey of Financial Professionals.1 That’s a sizable target and one that requires a defined[client acquisition strategy](https://smartasset.com/advisor-resources/client-acquisition-strategies-for-financial-advisors) . Incorporating these tips into your plan can help you build a strong foundation to reach out to prospects. Here are the seven things you’ll need to know in order to create that strategy:


### 1. Know Your Clients


Prospecting works most effectively when you’re concentrating your efforts on your ideal or target clients. Fifty-two percent of advisors in the Natixis survey focus on client segmentation as part of their prospecting efforts.


Fleshing out an ideal client profile can offer insight into which marketing and client acquisition approaches may have the most impact. For example, do you want to work with clients who are in a specific[net worth](https://smartasset.com/advisor-resources/lessons-from-high-net-worth-clients) range? Or are you interested in offering advice to an underserved demographic?


You may need to develop multiple[buyer personas](https://smartasset.com/advisor-resources/buyer-personas) or client avatars, depending on whether your niche is broad or narrow. The better you understand whom you want to target and their specific needs, the more effectively you can tailor your messaging and outreach.


Beyond demographics, consider what your clients want from you. For example, a 2025 BNY Pershing Report2 examined what investors desire from their advisors and found that 40% of investors express a desire for more personalized advice. Thirty-two percent favored a transparent, flat fee pricing model, while 51% said they preferred to hear from their advisors quarterly or semi-annually. Taking these types of preferences into account can add another dimension to your prospecting efforts if it allows you to better understand your[ideal clients’](https://smartasset.com/advisor-resources/ideal-client-profile-for-financial-advisors) expectations.


### 2. Use Your Network


[Networking as an advisor](https://smartasset.com/advisor-resources/how-to-network-as-a-financial-advisor) can help you build valuable connections that may yield more opportunities for prospecting. The broader your network, the easier it may be to connect with another professional when you need specialized services. For example, if you need to consult with an estate planning attorney to create a financial plan, then someone in your network might be able to refer you.


That, in turn, can benefit you if you’re able to establish reciprocal relationships. For example, you might refer your clients to an estate planning attorney who may in turn refer their clients to you for financial planning services.


Collaborating in this way can allow both of you to[grow your business](https://smartasset.com/advisor-resources/how-to-grow-your-client-base-as-a-financial-advisor) , while ensuring your clients’ needs are met. If your network is still small, consider how you can expand your circle of professionals. That might include:


- LinkedIn outreach
- Attending financial advisor conferences
- Looking for opportunities to collaborate, such as guesting on another advisor’s podcast or blog
- Joining[professional organizations for advisors](https://smartasset.com/advisor-resources/5-professional-organizations-for-financial-advisors) or local small business organizations
- Getting active in your local community as a volunteer or pro bono advisor


Remember that to make your network bear prospecting fruit, you need to keep it warm. Sending occasional emails, texts, instant messages or direct mail to say hello can put you at the top of a colleague’s mind the next time they have a client who needs to meet with an advisor.

### 4. Tap Into Your Current Client Base


Referrals don’t have to come from other financial professionals; you can also get them from your existing clients. Getting referrals from satisfied clients can reduce your workload when prospecting, but you need to give clients a reason to refer you.


The simplest way to[generate leads](https://smartasset.com/advisor-resources/financial-lead-generation) through referrals is by delivering quality services that help your clients meet their goals. Happy clients are more likely to refer others to you, versus clients who feel that they’re just another number. For instance, 31% of investors polled by BNY Pershing say they want financial planning to be a collaborative experience between themselves and their advisor.


You can also utilize your current client base differently by seeking out prospects among their heirs or beneficiaries. Say you’re working with a married couple whose adult children are poised to receive a $10 million inheritance from them when they pass away. You might offer to meet with the family to discuss how the inheritance could be managed.


That can be an opportunity to subtly introduce your services to the inheritors so that they’re less inclined to move those assets to a different firm after their parents are gone.


You could also develop a[strong referral program](https://smartasset.com/advisor-resources/financial-advisor-referral-program) to incentivize your clients to recommend you to others. In either case, let clients know that they’re not obligated to offer referrals, but that you’d be deeply grateful and take it as a mark of trust if they were to tell friends and family about your business.


### 5. Go Where Prospects Are


Many prospecting activities can be done online, but they’re not a replacement for meeting with potential clients face-to-face. If you have an opportunity to[connect with prospects](https://smartasset.com/advisor-resources/best-way-to-get-new-clients-as-a-financial-advisor) in person in a neutral environment, it could be worth your time.


There are different ways you might do this. For example, say that your local chamber of commerce is sponsoring a seminar on financial planning for seniors. If that’s your target demographic, then you might volunteer to lead a discussion on estate planning or Medicare planning, if that aligns with your services.


By putting yourself in front of prospective clients, you have a chance to make a strong first impression. That can be important if your ideal clients place a high value on in-person interactions. Other opportunities to get active in your community and potentially land on the radar of prospective clients include participating in charity events and volunteering your services as a[pro bono advisor](https://smartasset.com/advisor-resources/pro-bono-financial-advisor) .


### 6. Increase Your Digital Footprint


[Digital marketing](https://smartasset.com/advisor-resources/digital-marketing-strategies-for-financial-advisors) and content creation can help you to expand your reach well beyond your local area. It can also be a more passive way to attract prospects, as you can create a single piece of content and spin it into multiple forms. For example, if you have a blog, you could repurpose part of it into an email that you send to your subscriber list or include some of the key takeaways in an[Instagram reel or YouTube short](https://smartasset.com/advisor-resources/social-media-for-financial-advisors-5-ways-to-drive-business) .


The type of content you create should be designed to appeal to the type of clients you’re hoping to attract. And if you don’t have a social media presence, now may be the time to start building one. In the Natixis survey, 51% of advisors said social media would likely be the most successful prospecting tool in the next five years.


Going back to the first tip, knowing your ideal clients is key when prospecting. Otherwise, you could be wasting time creating content that fails to reach your intended audience.


### 7. Host Free Events


Hosting events can also be an excellent way to showcase your expertise and connect with potential clients in a more personal setting. You could host[seminars](https://smartasset.com/advisor-resources/seminar-marketing-for-financial-advisors) , workshops or webinars focused on timely financial topics. These events can be tailored to address the specific interests and concerns of your target market.


For example, you might host a seminar on investment strategies for young professionals in a specific field.[Physicians](https://smartasset.com/advisor-resources/financial-advisor-marketing-to-doctors) often need specialized financial planning advice, which could be an opportunity to tap into a high-value niche. Providing valuable information and insights at these events can establish you as a trusted expert and encourage attendees to seek your services. Thirty-four percent of global advisors plan to lean into seminar marketing in the coming years, according to Natixis.


When planning events, focus on creating a welcoming and engaging atmosphere. Interactive elements such as Q&A sessions, case studies and group discussions can enhance the experience for attendees.


### 8. Follow Up Consistently


Regular follow-ups can increase the likelihood of[converting a prospect to a client](https://smartasset.com/advisor-resources/converting-prospects-to-clients) if your competitors are not doing the extra legwork. Sending follow-up emails or texts is a friendly way to reach out and remind a prospective client that you’re available to answer any questions they might have about your services.


Automation tools can help you follow up consistently, at a pace that feels comfortable but not overbearing. If you’re keeping track of prospect communications inside your[customer relationship management (CRM) platform](https://smartasset.com/advisor-resources/choose-the-right-crm-for-your-advisory-practice) , for example, you may have access to automated workflows that can send out messages on a set schedule. If a prospect has signed up for your email list, you can also keep them in the loop with regular newsletters or the occasional[drip campaign](https://smartasset.com/advisor-resources/financial-advisor-drip-marketing) to promote new offerings.

## Why Prospecting Is So Valuable for Financial Advisors


Financial advisors offer advice and make recommendations to their clients to help them reach their goals.[Successful advisors](https://smartasset.com/advisor-resources/how-to-succeed-as-a-financial-advisor) understand the importance of being able to sell their services to people who need them. Without clients, you don’t have a business. Here are the benefits you should be aware of when it comes to prospecting.


**Ensures business growth** Financial advisors rely on building strong client relationships, but even satisfied clients may not require ongoing services indefinitely. Prospecting helps maintain a consistent flow of new clients, ensuring business continuity and growth.


**Increases revenue** More clients mean more opportunities to provide financial planning, investment management and other services. By expanding their client base, advisors can increase their revenue potential and[assets under management](https://smartasset.com/advisor-resources/how-to-target-specific-client-asset-amounts-as-a-financial-advisor) .


**Enhances market reach** By actively seeking new prospects, advisors can expand their reach beyond their immediate circle, tapping into new demographics and markets that could benefit from their expertise.


**Builds a strong network** A well-planned prospecting strategy allows advisors to connect with potential clients, industry professionals and[referral sources](https://smartasset.com/advisor-resources/best-referral-sources-for-financial-advisors-2023) . This network can lead to valuable introductions and long-term partnerships.


While you might have a stable client list, you can never rule out the possibility they will decide to go somewhere else for advice. Regular prospecting can help you to continually acquire new clients so your business can thrive.

## Frequently Asked Questions


**What discovery questions should advisors ask prospects?**


Some of the most helpful[prospecting questions](https://smartasset.com/advisor-resources/financial-advisor-prospecting-questions) to ask center on goals and future needs. For example, you might ask a prospect what their ideal retirement vision looks like or what their biggest financial challenges are now. Asking open-ended questions can spark a conversation that can help you learn more about what a prospect needs from you.


**What are some prospecting best practices for financial advisors?**


As you prospect for new clients, focus on asking open-ended questions and spend more time listening than you do talking. Demonstrate emotional intelligence by empathizing with prospects’ concerns and repeating them back to them to show them you understand their perspective. Be polite and courteous, and if a prospect appears to be a firm “no,” don’t pressure them with heavy sales tactics or a flurry of unwelcome emails.


**How long does it take to convert a prospect to a client?**


The amount of time you have to invest in converting a prospect to client depends on where they are in the buying cycle and the strength of your approach. A client who’s on the fence about whether they even need financial advice may take longer to nurture than a client who knows what they want in an advisor and simply needs to have your[unique value proposition](https://smartasset.com/advisor-resources/financial-advisor-value-proposition) laid out for them.


## Bottom Line


A well-executed prospecting strategy is essential for sustainable business growth as a financial advisor. By understanding how to effectively identify and engage potential clients, you can create a steady pipeline of opportunities that align with your business goals. Your prospecting approach should be deliberate and aligned with the clients you want to serve. Whether you tailor your approach with referrals, networking, digital marketing or[direct outreach](https://smartasset.com/advisor-resources/cold-calling-scripts-for-financial-advisors) , you can establish meaningful connections with clients who are the best fit for your services.
