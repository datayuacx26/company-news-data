# DDR5 Memory Buying Guide for AM5: Why 6000 CL30 Is the Sweet Spot

Memory shopping produces more second-guessing than any other PC part. Speeds from 4800 to 8400, timings that read like license plates, EXPO versus XMP, and 2026’s firmer DRAM pricing all conspire to confuse. For AM5 Ryzen builders the good news is that the homework has been done for years and the answer has not changed: DDR5-6000 with CL30 timings and an EXPO profile is the sweet spot. Here is why, and what to buy.


## Why It Matters


On AM5 Ryzen chips, the memory controller runs happiest in a 1:1 ratio with the DRAM clock, and DDR5-6000 is the highest speed where that ratio holds reliably on virtually every CPU. Push the memory faster and the controller drops to a less efficient ratio, often costing as much latency as the speed gains back. That is why a well-timed 6000 kit routinely matches or beats pricier 7200+ kits on AM5 in games. Even on cache-rich X3D chips, where memory sensitivity is lower, 6000 CL30 remains the safe, optimal default.


## What to Look For


Four checkboxes. Speed: 6000 MT/s. Latency: CL30 is the quality marker; CL36 kits are cheaper but slower where it counts. Profile: EXPO is AMD’s one-click tuning standard, so prefer kits carrying it (many also include XMP for Intel flexibility). Capacity: 32GB (2x16GB) is the 2026 baseline for gaming; 64GB only if you edit video or run heavy multitasking. And a candid note on pricing: DRAM costs have climbed industry-wide this year, so expect sticker prices above what older guides quote, and treat dips as buying opportunities.

## Frequently Asked Questions


Common questions about DDR5 memory for AM5 builds.


Why is DDR5-6000 CL30 the sweet spot for AM5? At 6000 MT/s the Ryzen memory controller runs 1:1 with the DRAM clock, maximizing bandwidth and latency; faster kits force a less efficient ratio.


Is 32GB of RAM enough for gaming in 2026? Yes, 32GB (2x16GB) is the comfortable baseline; 64GB benefits video editing and heavy multitasking.


What is the difference between EXPO and XMP? EXPO is the AMD memory profile standard, XMP is the Intel one. Many kits carry both; on AM5, prefer EXPO-validated kits.


Does fast RAM matter on X3D CPUs? Less than on standard chips because the large cache absorbs many accesses, but DDR5-6000 CL30 remains the recommended default.
