# Best Home and Small-Office NAS in 2026: Pick by Scenario

A network attached storage device, or NAS, is one of the most useful and surprisingly affordable pieces of hardware you can add to a home or small office. Think of it as your own private cloud: a central, always-on place to back up every device automatically, stream your media library to any screen in the house, and share files securely, all without paying a monthly subscription that never ends. But the right NAS depends entirely on what you plan to do with it, because a unit sized for simple photo backups is very different from one built to stream 4K video to several rooms at once. Here are the best options on Newegg in 2026, matched to the real scenarios most buyers fall into.


## Why Own a NAS?


A NAS gives you three things a cloud subscription cannot match. First, ownership of your data: your files live in your home, on hardware you control, not on a distant server governed by someone else’s terms. Second, no recurring fees; you pay once for the device and drives rather than forever for storage tiers. Third, speed on your local network, where transferring and streaming large files is far faster than pulling them down over the internet. Most home units use a four-bay design, which lets you combine drives for both greater capacity and redundancy, so that if a single drive fails your data survives intact. One important budgeting note: these devices are almost always sold diskless, meaning you buy NAS-rated hard drives separately based on how much storage you need, so factor that into your plans.

## How to Choose


The simplest approach is to match the NAS to the workload you actually have. For simple backups, the Asustor or TerraMaster does the job affordably and without complication. For media streaming and heavier multitasking, the Ugreen brings the muscle to keep multiple streams smooth. For a small business, the QNAP delivers the features, reliability, and support you need to trust it with important data. In every case, plan your drive purchase alongside the unit itself, and seriously consider a redundant configuration, such as mirroring or a parity setup, so that a single drive failure never costs you your files. That redundancy is the whole point of a multi-bay NAS, so do not skip it. ## Frequently Asked Questions


Answers to the most common questions about choosing a NAS.


Do NAS devices come with hard drives? Usually no; most are sold diskless, so you buy NAS-rated hard drives separately based on the capacity you need.


How many bays do I need in a NAS? Four bays is the popular home choice, letting you balance capacity and redundancy so a single drive failure does not lose your data.


Is a NAS better than cloud storage? A NAS gives you data ownership, no monthly fees, and fast local access, while cloud storage offers off-site convenience; many people use both.


Can a NAS stream my movie collection? Yes, a NAS with enough processing power can stream and transcode media; step up to a more powerful model for smooth multi-stream playback.
