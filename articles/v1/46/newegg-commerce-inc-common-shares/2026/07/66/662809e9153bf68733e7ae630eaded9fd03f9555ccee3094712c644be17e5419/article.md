# Get Your AM5 Rig Ready: Motherboards to Buy Before AMD’s Next X3D CPU Lands

The AM5 platform is about to get more interesting. AMD’s 3D V-Cache processors already rule the gaming benchmarks, and strong industry signals point to the X3D family growing very soon, with a new option designed to bring stacked-cache gaming to more budgets. The good news for planners: Socket AM5 is a stable, well-supported platform, and every current X3D chip drops into boards you can buy today. Here is how to pick the right foundation now so you are ready on day one.


## Why It Matters


When a hot CPU launches, motherboards with mature BIOS support and good availability become the difference between building that weekend and waiting weeks. AM5 boards also carry forward: AMD has committed to the socket through 2027 and beyond, so a quality board bought today supports Ryzen 7000, 8000, and 9000 chips, including every X3D variant, usually after a simple BIOS update.


## What to Look For


Focus on four things. First, VRM quality: X3D chips are efficient at 120W TDP, so even mid-range boards handle them, but better VRMs run cooler. Second, BIOS flashback: a button that lets you update firmware without a CPU installed, essential if your board shipped before the newest chips. Third, storage lanes: at least one PCIe 5.0 M.2 slot future-proofs your fast storage. Fourth, networking: Wi-Fi 6E or Wi-Fi 7 and 2.5G LAN are now standard at surprisingly low prices.

## Frequently Asked Questions


Common questions about choosing an AM5 motherboard.


Will B650 motherboards support new X3D CPUs? Yes. The AM5 socket supports Ryzen 7000, 8000, and 9000 series chips including X3D variants; older B650 boards may simply need a BIOS update first.


What is the difference between B850 and X870? X870 adds stronger VRMs, USB4, and typically Wi-Fi 7 and faster LAN. B850 covers the essentials, including PCIe 5.0 storage, at a lower price.


Do I need PCIe 5.0 for an X3D gaming build? Not strictly. PCIe 4.0 performs excellently, but a Gen5 M.2 slot adds future-proofing at little extra cost.


What is BIOS Flashback? It lets you update motherboard firmware from a USB stick without a CPU installed, essential if your board shipped before your CPU was released.
