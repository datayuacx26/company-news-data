# Ulaanbaatar Runs on QR Codes. The Steppe Runs on Cash. Here's How to Handle Both.

Mongolia surprises people. You land expecting a cash economy end to end, and then the barista at a specialty coffee shop in Ulaanbaatar points you at a QR code taped to the counter. Locals barely touch banknotes in the capital anymore. They scan.


Then you drive four hours out of the city and the picture flips completely. No signal, no terminals, and a guesthouse owner who wants tugrik in hand.


So the honest answer to "how do I pay in Mongolia" is: you need two strategies, not one. Here's how to run both without opening a Mongolian bank account.


### Strategy one: the QR economy of Ulaanbaatar


In UB, the QR code that matters is QPay. It's the code you'll see at supermarkets, restaurants, pharmacies, taxis, the State Department Store, small kiosks, even some street vendors. Locals pay through their banking apps or wallets like SocialPay and MonPay, and the money moves instantly from their account to the merchant.


The catch for visitors: those local apps generally want a Mongolian bank account and a Mongolian phone number. You can see the QR code sitting right there on the counter, and you can't do anything with it. It's the same wall travelers hit all over Asia, and it's exactly the problem we wrote about in[The QR Codes You Can't Scan](https://moretapay.com/blog/the-qr-codes-you-can-t-scan) .


This is where Moreta Pay earns its spot on your phone. You top up the wallet from your home bank or card in your own currency, then scan the same merchant QR codes locals scan. You see the amount, the FX rate, and the merchant name before you confirm, the merchant gets paid instantly in tugrik, and you get 1% cash back. No Mongolian bank account, no local SIM required for signup, no mystery fees appearing on your statement three days later.


Practical flow at a UB restaurant: ask for the bill, the server points at the QR stand on the table or brings a printed code, you scan, check the amount matches, confirm, show the success screen. Done. It's faster than waiting for a card terminal, and plenty of smaller places don't have a card terminal anyway.


### Why your foreign card is a weak backup here


Cards do work in Mongolia, but only in a narrow band of places: bigger hotels, larger supermarkets, some restaurants aimed at tourists. Outside that band, terminals get rare fast. Small shops, market stalls, most taxis, and countryside businesses are QR or cash.


Even where cards are accepted, you're often dealing with foreign transaction fees from your home bank and no visibility on the conversion until the charge posts. Compare that with scanning a QR where the rate is on screen before you tap confirm, and the card starts to feel like the emergency option rather than the plan.


Tap-to-pay with a phone wallet is even less reliable. Don't build your Mongolia trip around it.


### Strategy two: tugrik cash for everything outside the city


Here's the part no payment app can fix. Mongolia is enormous, sparsely populated, and much of the classic travel experience (ger camps in the Gobi, horse treks in the Orkhon Valley, family homestays near Khövsgöl) happens far from mobile coverage. QR payments need a connection. The steppe frequently doesn't have one.


Rules of thumb that experienced Mongolia travelers follow:


-


**Withdraw or exchange in Ulaanbaatar before you leave the city.** ATMs and exchange offices exist in provincial towns like Mörön or Dalanzadgad, but they're sparse, sometimes empty, and you don't want your trip depending on one working machine.


-


**Carry small notes.** A countryside guesthouse or a roadside guanz (canteen) may struggle to break large bills. Ask for a mix when you exchange.


-


**Budget cash for the full countryside leg, plus a buffer.** Fuel stops, park entrance fees, snacks at soum-center shops, tips for drivers and guides. Assume all of it is cash until proven otherwise.


-


**Confirm with your tour operator what's prepaid.** Many multi-day tours include meals and accommodation, which shrinks your cash needs dramatically. Get this in writing before you decide how much tugrik to carry.


One nice surprise: some soum-center shops and even a few ger camps near popular routes do have QR codes now, wherever there's mobile signal. When you see one and you have bars, scan it and save your cash for the places that truly need it.


### A realistic week, mapped to payment methods


**Days 1 and 2, Ulaanbaatar:** Almost entirely QR. Coffee shops around Sukhbaatar Square, dinner at a khuushuur place, groceries for the road, all scannable with Moreta Pay. Keep a little cash for the odd vendor at Narantuul Market who prefers notes.


**Days 3 to 6, the countryside:** Cash territory. Your driver may accept a QR payment back in town, but everything en route (park fees, extra meals, a bottle of airag from a family by the road) is tugrik in hand.


**Day 7, back in UB:** QR again for your farewell dinner and last-minute cashmere shopping. If you're booking onward flights or hotels on platforms like Trip.com, QR-based online checkout works there too, so you're not forced back onto a foreign card for the digital stuff either.


If you're continuing on through Asia, the same app carries over. The QR habit you build in Ulaanbaatar transfers directly to places like Malaysia, where[DuitNow QR is everywhere](https://moretapay.com/blog/pay-in-malaysia-without-cash-duitnow-qr-2026-08-17) and cash is increasingly optional.


### Small things that save you hassle


**Screenshot nothing, verify everything.** Before confirming any QR payment, glance at the merchant name on screen and make sure it matches where you're standing. It takes one second and it's the habit locals have too.


**Top up before, not during.** Load your wallet from your home bank while you're on hotel wifi, not while a queue forms behind you at a till with one bar of signal.


**Don't convert too much to cash.** Tugrik is hard to exchange back outside Mongolia. Carry what the countryside leg needs, pay by QR in the city, and you'll leave with an empty-enough wallet.


**Taxis are a mixed bag.** Official taxis and ride-hailing in UB increasingly take QR. The informal "raise your hand and a random car stops" system is still cash, negotiated up front.


### The short version


Mongolia in 2026 is two payment worlds. In Ulaanbaatar, QR is the default and you can join it as a visitor. Beyond the city limits, tugrik cash is non-negotiable, so plan your withdrawals before the road trip starts. Get both right and money stops being a thing you think about, which is the whole point.
