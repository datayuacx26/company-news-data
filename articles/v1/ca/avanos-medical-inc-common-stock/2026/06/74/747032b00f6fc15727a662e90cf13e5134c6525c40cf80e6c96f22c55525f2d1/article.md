# Expanding International: What We’re Learning as We Grow

## ***By Dieter Krines, Vice President, International Sales – Avanos Medical***


Expanding an international business is never just about entering more countries or adding more revenue. It is about building a stronger, more focused, more connected organization that can deliver consistently across very different markets, customer needs, regulatory environments and healthcare systems.


Over the past several years, our International team at Avanos has been on that journey. We have been performing while transforming: reshaping our portfolio, strengthening our go-to-market models, improving distributor management, building stronger regional leadership teams, and focusing our energy on the areas where we believe we can win.


That work is creating momentum. But more importantly, it is giving us a clearer view of what it takes to expand International in a sustainable way.


**First, focus matters.**


International markets offer enormous opportunity, but not every opportunity deserves the same investment. Growth requires discipline. For us, that has meant sharpening our focus around key areas, while making thoughtful decisions about where to simplify, where to expand and where to build deeper expertise.


The temptation in international growth is to chase everything. The better path is to be clear about where you can create meaningful value for customers and patients, and then align the organization behind those priorities.


**Second, transformation and performance must happen at the same time.**


In a complex business, there is rarely a perfect moment to transform. You cannot pause the market while you restructure teams, integrate acquisitions, build direct models or improve commercial processes. You have to keep serving customers while changing how the business operates.


That has been one of the most important lessons for our team. Across International, our colleagues have continued to deliver while also building a stronger foundation for the future. That takes resilience, accountability and a willingness to keep improving even when the environment is challenging.


**Third, go-to-market strategy has to be local, not generic.**


One of the most important decisions in international expansion is how close you need to be to the customer. In some markets, a distributor model is the right answer. In others, building a more direct presence can strengthen relationships, improve execution and create better insight into customer needs.


We have seen the impact of going direct in markets such as the UK, Portugal and the Nordics. These moves bring us closer to clinicians and customers, but they also require careful planning, local leadership and strong execution. When done well, a direct model gives us better market insight, stronger customer relationships and a more scalable platform for growth.


**Fourth, distributors remain a critical part of the growth equation.**


At the same time, expanding direct does not mean distributors become less important. In many markets, strong distributor partnerships are essential. The opportunity is to manage those relationships with greater discipline, clearer expectations and better shared planning.


The best distributor relationships are not transactional. They are built on transparency, market knowledge, accountability and a shared commitment to customers. As we continue to expand, improving how we select, support and manage partners remains one of our most important priorities.


**Fifth, international growth is a team sport.**


No market grows through sales effort alone. Sustainable international expansion depends on close partnership across commercial, marketing, finance, HR, operations and back-office teams. When a region performs consistently in a complex environment, it reflects the discipline, collaboration and commitment of many people working together, often behind the scenes.


That same spirit of collaboration must extend across regions as well as functions. Some of the strongest ideas come from the people closest to the market. Our field teams see where customer needs are changing, where innovation is needed, where partnerships may create value and where we can make it easier to do business with Avanos.


Listening to those insights – and acting on them – is essential. The more connected we are globally, the better we can share what is working, solve challenges faster and build an International business that is stronger than the sum of its parts.


**Finally, expansion must stay connected to purpose.**


It is easy to talk about growth, margin, execution and market share. Those things matter. But they matter because of what they allow us to do for customers and patients.


Spending time with clinicians and seeing our products used in real care settings is always a powerful reminder. International growth is not only about building a bigger business. It is about expanding access, improving support for healthcare providers and helping more patients get back to the things that matter.


I am proud of the momentum our International team is building. We have strong regional leaders, committed teams and clear priorities. We are continuing to expand our direct presence, strengthen our portfolios, improve distributor management, invest in talent and identify new opportunities for growth.


The work is not finished. In many ways, we are just getting started.


But the direction is clear: a more focused, more accountable, more customer-connected International business, one that performs today while building for tomorrow.


That is how we expand sustainably, create value across markets, and continue building International for the future.
