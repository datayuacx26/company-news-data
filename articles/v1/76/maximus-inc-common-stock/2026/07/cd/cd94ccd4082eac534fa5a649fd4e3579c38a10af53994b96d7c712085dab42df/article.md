# AI digital modernization federal health outcomes

**Key Takeaways**


Federal health agencies have a significant opportunity to harness cloud, AI, and modern digital infrastructure to improve health outcomes for all. In this article, Maximus health leaders Cathy Muha and Barbara Keating share their insights for delivering patient-centered, AI-driven digital modernization: building seamless digital infrastructure for integrated care; prioritizing health literacy and CX to improve patient engagement; deploying integrated care models tailored to specific population needs; and adopting AI within clear compliance and data security frameworks. The path forward is co-innovation between government and industry that is patient-centered, compliant by design, and built for real, measurable impact.


The healthcare landscape is undergoing rapid transformation, driven by emerging technologies, changing regulations, and the need to improve outcomes. As federal agencies work to[modernize healthcare delivery](https://maximus.com/insights/agentic-ai-federal-health-program-delivery) , new innovations are poised to deliver quality, patient-centric services and experiences — but realizing that potential requires getting the fundamentals right.


## Understand the stakes — and the opportunity to improve patient-centered care


Federal health agency programs and services touch nearly everyone. These systems span from battlefield medicine to retirement benefits, and from rural clinics to huge data ecosystems.


Yet healthcare agencies are swimming in vast amounts of data while struggling with outdated legacy systems and a lack of integration, which are significant barriers to effectively using it. Integrating modern solutions to harness a more seamless exchange of health data can speed progress from research to policy, to care, and ultimately improve health at the population level.


### Cloud and AI priorities for government and industry partners


We see four ways industry and government can prepare to support faster patient-centered healthcare modernization and better customer experience (CX):


1. Invest in digital infrastructure for seamless integration of care across physical and virtual spaces.
2. Overlay customer experience and health literacy to improve patient engagement.
3. Focus on integrated care models for specific population conditions.
4. Develop frameworks to support AI adoption while ensuring privacy and data security.


When patients and caregivers have full information and access to continuity of care, that's holistic health, which leads to improved outcomes. Our goal is helping people live longer, fuller, healthier lives. Automation and AI simplify the collection, organization, and analysis of massive amounts of data, bringing the speed and clarity needed for accurate, real-time insights and better evidence-based decisions.


AI is not a replacement for human connections that are so integral to healthcare, but it can relieve administrative burden and provide insights to strengthen patient-provider relationships. It also helps protect sensitive data while detecting fraud and improper payments. From a security standpoint, the Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services (CMS) are currently using AI to flag suspicious billing patterns, with the next step being catching improper payments before bills are paid. These innovations have major financial implications for patients, providers, and government as a whole.


## Ensuring health program modernization is patient-centered


Technology-driven CX improvements across government are being built on a foundation of human-centered design. In the healthcare space, that narrows to patient-centered design: collecting information about patient experiences like satisfaction data and access to care, and using it to improve outcomes. Good health now goes way beyond that office visit or clinic visit and includes personalized online programs, health portals, on-demand video visits, online symptom management, and virtual wellness programs, all areas where we’re seeing huge growth.


Using the recommendations above as a framework, agencies can ensure positive patient experiences are at the center of modernization. Sophisticated CX solutions like[Maximus Total Experience Management](https://maximus.com/customer-experience/digital-government/federal-digital-government) (TXM) are powered by AI and leverage industry-leading cloud services as well as advanced analytics to deliver seamless, personalized experiences with omni-channel communication. FedRAMP-authorization provides assurance that sensitive patient information will be kept private and secure throughout the care lifecycle.


> Having a single source of truth with everything connected makes it so much easier to provide better care. That’s why there’s a strategic shift toward intelligence-driven customer experience that is driving a much faster deployment of adaptive and proactive insights.


Cathy Muha Customer Experience (CX), Market Lead for Federal Health, Maximus


## Built in compliance by design, not as a retrofit


Industry partnerships are essential to introducing private-sector quality experiences to the public sector landscape, but innovation in government comes with unique guardrails. Rather than fitting a new solution into the regulatory box after development, industry partners can help health agencies keep compliance at the forefront throughout the process.


> Working with the government is co-innovation by participating in regulatory sandboxes that are safe spaces for testing innovative products, services, and business models.


Barbara Keating Managing Director, Federal Health, Maximus


These environments work in agile sprints using FedRAMP-authorized tools alongside security teams throughout the development cycle. You have the R&D track for rapid experimentation and a regulatory track focused on documentation, traceability, and audit readiness. The entire process is done in tandem.


Innovative technologies can only support progress if they are built on a foundation of robust compliance and cybersecurity. With the right processes in place, industry and government can work together to navigate the hurdles that have slowed healthcare modernization — all in service of a very human-centric goal: improving health outcomes for all.
