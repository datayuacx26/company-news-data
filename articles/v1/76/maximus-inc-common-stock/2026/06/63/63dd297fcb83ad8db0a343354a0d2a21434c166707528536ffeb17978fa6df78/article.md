# How PASRR improves care planning and reduces risk

## Resident reviews strengthen Medicaid compliance, accountability, and person-centered care.


**Key takeaways**


Preadmission Screening and Resident Review (PASRR) serves two distinct functions. Pre-admission screening helps identify a person’s needs before admission to a nursing facility. Resident reviews are an ongoing process for reassessing people when changes in condition, diagnosis, or service needs occur. When completed on time and supported by clear documentation, resident reviews help states improve care planning, reduce compliance risk, and ensure Medicaid pays for care only when a state presents documentation that it complied with all required PASRR rules.


### Why resident reviews matter


As states look to strengthen Medicaid oversight and improve long-term services and supports (LTSS) outcomes, PASRR resident reviews are drawing renewed attention for their roles in both resident protection and Medicaid accountability.


The perspective that PASRR compliance works best when it is proactive, interdisciplinary, and clearly understood across roles was a prominent theme in recent conversations with state leaders and providers at the South Dakota Healthcare Association (SDHCA) Spring Institute, where Maximus clinical experts participated in a PASRR session with representatives from the South Dakota Department of Social Services, Division of Medical Services.


Resident reviews apply to individuals already residing in a Medicaid-certified nursing facility, regardless of payor source, diagnosis, or admission setting. These event-driven reviews occur when there is a change that could affect an individual’s PASRR determination, care needs, or appropriate placement. This may include changes in clinical or psychiatric condition, psychotropic medication changes, missing PASRR history, or the expiration of time-limited approvals.


That distinction is important because resident reviews are what keep PASRR current over time. A person’s condition may evolve after admission. Diagnoses may become clearer. Service needs may increase, decrease, or shift. Without a process for updating those determinations, care planning can fall out of sync with the individual’s needs. Resident reviews help prevent that gap by ensuring that PASRR continues to reflect the person’s current situation rather than just the information available at admission.


### The connection to Medicaid program integrity


Resident reviews help ensure that nursing facility placement remains appropriate, that PASRR-identified services are reflected in care planning, and that required documentation is current and complete. In practical terms, that means Medicaid payments are supported by evidence that federal PASRR requirements have been met.


When resident reviews are delayed, missed, or based on incomplete information, the risk extends beyond compliance paperwork. States may have difficulty demonstrating that an individual is in the right setting, receiving the right services, and supported by a current PASRR determination. That creates exposure to audit findings, survey issues, and improper payment risk. In this way, Resident Reviews are not separate from program integrity — they are one of the mechanisms that enable it.


### Support for better, person-centered care


Resident reviews also support[person-centered care](https://maximus.com/insights/person-centered-approach-to-assessments) by ensuring that PASRR recommendations are integrated into care planning as resident needs change. When conducted effectively, resident reviews help assess whether an individual continues to require nursing facility level of care, whether specialized services are needed, and whether another setting may now be more appropriate.


This is especially important for states seeking to prevent unnecessary or prolonged institutional stays. A strong resident review process can help surface opportunities for more appropriate services and settings while reinforcing the expectation that care plans should reflect the full picture of the resident’s needs. That alignment benefits both the individual and the Medicaid program.


### Shared accountability for improved compliance


Although PASRR involves multiple parties, nursing facilities play a central role in ensuring compliance with resident review. They are the ongoing point of contact for monitoring changes in a resident’s condition, maintaining current documentation, and recognizing when a review is warranted. At the same time, effective resident reviews depend on coordination across hospitals, referring providers, facility staff, and the clinical services vendors responsible for PASRR determinations.


One of the strongest themes to emerge at the SDHCA Spring Institute was the drive for greater alignment across stakeholders. PASRR works best when administrators, nursing staff, care coordinators, social services personnel, and compliance teams operate from a shared understanding of triggers, responsibilities, and timelines. Without that alignment, avoidable delays and rework can undermine both care planning and compliance.


> PASRR compliance does not rest with one role. It succeeds when administrators, nursing, social services, and compliance staff work from a shared understanding of triggers and timelines.


Ariel Ghanayem Program Manager, Clinical Services


South Dakota’s transition to a vendor-supported PASRR model is contributing to a broader shift in how PASRR is viewed across the state. Providers and state leaders are increasingly recognizing PASRR as a clinical, person-centered process. Greater engagement among providers, state staff, and oversight teams reflects a deeper understanding of how PASRR evaluations and service recommendations should inform individualized care plans.


One challenge repeatedly raised at the SDHCA event involves missed or incomplete diagnoses at hospital discharge. When key diagnostic information is missing or not clearly communicated, providers report downstream impacts, including resubmissions, rework, or delays in initiating or completing the resident review.


Beyond workflow frustrations, these challenges can disrupt timely care planning, create uncertainty about next steps, and increase compliance risk for facilities and states alike. They also illustrate a broader point: the timeliness of resident reviews depends in part on the quality of the information that enters the system in the first place. Improving communication across discharge and admission workflows can help reduce these delays and is essential to maintaining compliance and protecting Medicaid reimbursement.


### Modernization for stronger consistency and oversight


As states look to improve PASRR operations, many are also exploring modernization, including electronic PASRR systems. Providers expressed interest in moving away from paper-based workflows toward systems that capture all Level I screens, improve consistency, reduce confusion around submission triggers, and support more timely follow-up when reviews are needed.


While budget constraints remain a consideration, growing alignment among providers and state leaders suggests readiness for modernization is increasing. For states, these improvements are about more than operational efficiency. Modernized PASRR processes can help create clearer accountability, improve documentation quality, and make it easier to demonstrate compliance over time. That is especially important as PASRR oversight expands across compliance, survey, and LTSS stakeholders.


### What this means for Medicaid, LTSS, and HCBS state leaders


As PASRR oversight expands to include surveyors, compliance staff, and home and community-based services (HCBS) leadership, states face heightened expectations for consistency, timeliness, and defensible documentation.


For state Medicaid and LTSS leaders, strengthening resident review processes offers a clear opportunity to:


- Improve care quality and person-centered outcomes by ensuring services and care plans reflect current resident needs
- Reduce audit and survey risk through more timely, defensible reporting
- Reinforce program integrity by supporting appropriate placement and more accurate Medicaid payment oversight
- Build a foundation for broader LTSS modernization efforts


### Looking ahead


The takeaway from conversations at the SDHCA event was clear: PASRR resident reviews are more than a compliance obligation. They are a strategic lever for care quality and accountability, ensuring residents are served in the right setting, with the right supports, and with documentation that can withstand oversight. States that continue to invest in education, interdisciplinary alignment, and modernized PASRR systems will be best positioned to reduce risk while improving outcomes across HCBS and[LTSS](https://maximus.com/insights/dignity-of-risk) programs.
