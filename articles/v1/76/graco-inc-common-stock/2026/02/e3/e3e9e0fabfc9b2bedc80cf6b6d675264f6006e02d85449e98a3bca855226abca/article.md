## Innovations that made Graco: airless spray technology


In 1958, Graco introduced a breakthrough that literally moved paint forward, and moved the painting industry with it. Among Graco’s many innovations, perhaps one of our best known is airless spray technology. It stands out as the breakthrough that defined our reputation for expertly engineered products and continues to set the standard for industrial, commercial and even DIY painting projects.


The airless paint sprayer was[more than a product](https://www.graco.com/us/en/contractor/solutions/articles/intro-to-airless-paint-spraying.html) . It would become a benchmark in Graco’s legacy of innovation. It remains the foundation for countless solutions that help professionals move materials that matter and deliver results that exceed exceptional.


But Graco didn’t set out to change painting forever. We simply wanted to solve a problem.


**The need for a better way to spray paint**


Before 1958, painters typically relied on compressed air to atomize paint. Atomization refers to the process of breaking liquid into tiny, uniform droplets to create a smooth spray. Sprayers would force air and paint through a nozzle, essentially relying on air turbulence to create a spray pattern. Uneven atomization would result in streaks, “orange peel” finishes or thin spots.


It was inefficient. Compressed air sprayers at the time created a high-velocity mist, which often bounces off the surfaces to which it is applied. Aside from creating a big mess, this overspray or “bounce back” led to significant material waste. Paint ended up dispersed into the air or on surrounding surfaces. The lack of precision meant more cleanup and greater harm to the surrounding environment.


And it was a lot of extra work. Because of the inefficient dispersion, multiple passes were required to complete surface coverage. Skilled operators were required to maintain quality with the unruly equipment, and those operators required heavy ventilation and protective gear due to the larger volume of airborne particulates.


**The birth of the airless paint sprayer**


Enter Graco, which patented the first portable airless sprayer in 1958. Instead of relying on air turbulence inside the paint gun, it used hydraulic force of up to 3,000 psi (pounds per square inch) to atomize paint at the spray tip. The result was a more consistent droplet size, which meant a smoother spray, which meant a smoother finish.


No more orange peel.


The higher pressure allowed for more output and faster coverage, while the uniform spray pattern meant contractors required fewer passes to cover up weak spots and blemishes. In addition to decreased labor costs and reduced material waste, the Graco air sprayers were safer and contributed to more sustainable job site due to fewer airborne particulates.


Graco’s airless spray technology continues to define the industry.


Today, airless spray systems are the standard for professional painting and finishing for home, automotive, construction and finishing applications. It has formed the backbone of countless other Graco fluid management innovations. Contractors finish jobs faster. Manufacturers maintain consistent quality at scale.
