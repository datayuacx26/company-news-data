## Making What Matters: Diane Olson


“It feels like I’m wearing a purse!”


Diane Olson recalls the unexpected feedback to early prototypes of the Graco QuickShot. Instead of the usual comments on performance, comfort, or balance, the responses were a bit more subjective. It sparked a design shift that helped make the airless sprayer the most successful first-year product launch in Graco’s history, a testament to our commitment to customer-focused innovation.


Now Graco’s Vice President of Engineering for the Industrial Division, Olson has earned over 190 Graco patents during her three decades at Graco. Her name is emblazoned across the plaques on Graco’s patent display wall. She started as an intern in the Sioux Falls plant in 1995 before accepting a full-time role as an associate design engineer. The rest is a very productive history.


Olson beams when discussing her first patent, a reverse-flush paint sprayer that worked by circulating cleaning fluid backwards through the sprayer to remove paint, debris and buildup. “Design engineers love inventing and innovating, and a patent confirms you’ve achieved something meaningful,” she recalls. “Especially for young engineers, those first patents are a big deal.”


They’re a big deal at Graco, where plaques, coins, luncheons and even jewelry are the spoils of invention. But that excitement doesn’t trump the need to engineer solutions that meet genuine customer need. The messy, rewarding process of invention starts not with “What can we patent?” but with “What problem are we trying to solve?”


“As engineers, our first focus is finding the right solution for customers,” Olson says. “Patent opportunities follow naturally.”


Once a novel solution to a customer problem emerges, Graco’s robust IP protection program goes to work. In‑house intellectual property attorneys collaborate closely with each division to review new concepts, research existing patents, and identify features that warrant legal protections.


Those protections give inventors the confidence they need to innovate. “Graco’s commitment to IP protection sets us apart. It demonstrates our investment in innovation,” Olson adds. “That culture attracts and retains top engineering talent.”


Whether it’s a whole new product, a fresh mechanism or a creative twist on a familiar solution, Olson says teams can often sense early on when they’ve landed on something genuinely new. But novelty alone won’t earn a patent; a successful application must also be defensible and clearly differentiated. It has to solve a problem in a new way.


At 6,000 patents and counting, Graco has solved a lot of problems.


Innovation at Graco is rarely the work of just one person. Teams comprise mechanical and electrical engineers, designers, and technicians working together to innovate concepts, build, test and iterate designs until they have winning solutions. Project managers and leaders lend a fresh set of eyes to the process.


Of course, prototyping plays a central role. Early feasibility builds may be held together with 3D‑printed parts or adapted components to establish proof of concept. Later iterations evolve into semi‑ and fully functional units. And customer input guides every step of the process.


Which brings us to the QuickShot. Customers had long asked for a better high‑quality, fine‑finish, spray solution. In response, the engineering team conceived a portable, battery-powered spray system with an electronic gun, designed to deliver portability and performance. Early prototypes used a cross‑body strap to support the power pack.


The product worked. The aesthetics did not. “I thought, what could that kind of individual better relate to? What would they feel like fits?” remembers Olson. “So we put together a different concept that fit on a tool belt, which was much, much better received.”


That iterative rhythm isn’t just process, it defines Graco’s culture of collaborative innovation, shaped by real-world feedback.


As Olson puts it, “our goal is to provide effective solutions to customer problems.”


And she has been pretty effective. One unique incentive Graco provides to inventors is a garnet ring with room for 12 diamonds. Whenever an inventor receives a patent, another diamond is added to the ring.
