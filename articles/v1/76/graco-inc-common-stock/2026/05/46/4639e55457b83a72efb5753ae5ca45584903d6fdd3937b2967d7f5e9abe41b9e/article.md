# Graco’s Contributions to Support U.S. Military Efforts During World War II


In December 1941, America was thrust into war when Japan’s bombs crippled the Pacific fleet, killing more than 2,400 Americans. The bombing of Pearl Harbor marked a pivotal point in world—and Graco—history.


Like most U.S. companies, Graco retooled its manufacturing facilities and made a rallying cry to help our country’s war effort in Europe and the Pacific.[After only 15 years in existence](https://www.graco.com/us/en/about-graco/news/articles/2026/q1/the-invention-of-the-air-powered-grease-gun.html) , Graco shifted to wartime production, making parts for aircraft, tanks, as well as a mobile lubricating unit.


Wartime Production


### The Equipment Graco Contributed to WWII


In early 1942, the president established the War Production Board (WPB). The agency’s main purpose was converting peacetime factories into facilities to make weapons and military equipment. By the end of the year, Graco president and[CEO Leil Gray](https://www.graco.com/us/en/about-graco/news/articles/2026/q1/founding-brothers-of-graco-leil-russell.html) was appointed to the WPB’s lubrication equipment industrial advisory committee.


During World War II, most of Graco’s business was with the government supplying lubrication equipment to the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers and U.S. Marines Corps for off-road servicing of vehicles and other military items.


Here is a complete list of Graco’s creations during the war effort:


- **The Convoy Luber:** This mobile unit for the battlefield was designed to lubricate allied trucks, jeeps, tanks and aircraft in both Europe and the Pacific.
- **Landing gear struts for Beechcraft AT-11 aircraft:** Graco manufactured landing gear struts—critical shock absorbing components that connect an aircraft’s wheels to its fuselage. The U.S. Army Air Corps relied on the AT 11 to train bombardiers and navigators for combat missions in Europe, North Africa, and the Pacific. In 1944, Graco received a merit award from Beech Aircraft Corp. of Wichita, Kansas, recognizing the quality of its work.
- **Wing lock mechanism for the F4U Corsair fighter plane:** The F4U Corsair was a fighter-bomber used by the U.S. Navy and the U.S. Marine Corps. Its powerful engine allowed the plane to travel at speeds of more than 400 mph. A unique feature of the plane was its gull wings that folded up. The Graco-made folding wing lock mechanism relied on a hydraulic locking pin system to secure the wings.
- **Tank tread pins for the M4 Sherman tank and other treaded vehicles:** The pins were a vital part of tank tread, which are interlocking metal plates that form a continuous loop around the tank’s wheels. The Graco-made pins connected the metal plates together.
- **Tools for producing brass cartridge cases that hold bullets:** Graco provided dies and tooling to create bullet casings for machine guns, rifles, autocannons and anti-aircraft guns.


Making History


### Sacrifices Recognized


Graco received national recognition for its war efforts. In 1944 and 1945, Graco received the Army-Navy “E” award, which is presented to war plants in recognition of production excellence. Fewer than 3.5% of U.S. war plants received this award. The famous flag with the large “E” emblazoned on it was a badge of honor for these facilities.


In addition, Graco had at least six war bond drives, encouraging our employees to buy U.S. Treasury bonds to finance the war effort.


Graco employees also contributed in another way: 174 served in the military and four lost their lives. Many contributions and sacrifices were made during the nearly four years the U.S. was involved in the World War II. Those years strengthened the foundation that made Graco the company that would endure for over a century.
