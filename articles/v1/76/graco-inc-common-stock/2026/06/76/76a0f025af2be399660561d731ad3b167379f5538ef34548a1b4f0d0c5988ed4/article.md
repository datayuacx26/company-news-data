## Graco's Cleaner, Safer Manufacturing Centers


When people tour Graco’s manufacturing centers, they are taken aback by how organized and clean they are. Graco has a long-standing reputation for tidy, disciplined manufacturing environments, but it is not about showing off for factory tours. Clean and organized manufacturing spaces are essential to Graco’s commitment to continuously improving worker safety.


Simply put, a clean factory is a safe factory. Fewer obstacles, clearer pathways and well-delineated spaces for tools and materials reduce risk before work even begins, all while making work processes more efficient. For engineers and manufacturing professionals, that connection between organization and safety is practical and measurable.


It’s how we move materials that matter every day, whether or not a customer, investor or executive is walking the floor.


#### From 5S to G4


Many manufacturers are familiar with 5S. The methodology has been adopted and refined countless times. Here at Graco, we have been through many of those cycles, and while our safety leaders recognize the value of 5S, the challenge has always been sustainability and continuous improvement.


So Graco evolved the concept into something simpler. That evolution became G4.


G4 creates four clear levels that are easy to understand and audit:


- G1 focuses on having everything you need and nothing you do not.
- G2 ensures equipment surfaces and floors are free from dirt and debris.
- G3 emphasizes visual controls, documented processes and clear checklists.
- G4 requires ongoing auditing and follow-through to maintain standards.


Every manufacturing area is evaluated against these criteria, top to bottom. The goal is not perfection, but consistency and improvement.


#### Everyone plays a part


For engineers, one of the most important aspects of G4 is the emphasis on being proactive rather than reactive. We aren’t waiting for incidents or inspections to drive change, instead we review the manufacturing floor constantly for opportunities to improve.


Each manufacturing center has a safety committee that meets monthly. Committee members are assigned specific areas to audit. They look for issues, such as damaged racks, worn pallets, or potential trip hazards. These audits extend beyond the production floor to office and administrative spaces.


Graco safety leaders document every detail, with a system that turns observations into work tickets that are tracked to completion. The following month, they review progress and determine next steps. This rhythm creates a feedback loop that supports continuous improvement instead of one-time fixes.


Employees also play a direct role through a plantwide program called Start with Safety. QR codes placed throughout facilities allow employees to quickly submit safety ideas and highlight concerns. Those submissions feed directly into the same review process used by safety committees, translating employee observation into action.


In one recent example, an employee reported a single damaged rack beam. The beam was replaced the same day, reducing the risk of rack failure. The G4 and Start with Safety processes are designed to ensure we are not missing crucial details.


#### No financial justification needed


Once a potential safety improvement is identified, leaders ask one simple question: will it reduce risk?


If the answer is yes, that is justification enough. If an improvement helps prevent someone from getting hurt, there is no further discussion needed.


This principle guides our continuous investment in safety processes across manufacturing centers, from replacing damaged equipment to implementing new safeguards.


Recent initiatives include comprehensive lockout and tagout reviews for individual machines, ergonomic assessments to reduce strain and laceration prevention programs that replace traditional knives with safer alternatives. Machine interlocks are tested and audited to ensure equipment cannot operate unless guards and doors are properly in place.


Forklifts, a notorious hazard in any manufacturing facility, receive special attention. Blue warning lights have been added to improve visibility in blind spots, and badge-controlled access ensures only certified operators can use the equipment.


#### Safety inside the factory and beyond it


The same philosophy that drives safety in Graco manufacturing centers also drives how we design products for our customers. Safety considerations factor into the earliest stages of product development, with designs tested and evaluated against global standards and by independent third parties.


Ergonomic improvements, such as introducing electronic trigger systems, reflect lessons learned from real-world use. Lighter, less bulky equipment, improved controls, and intuitive interfaces help protect customers who rely on Graco products every day.


For Graco, safety is not a program with an end date. It isn’t just optics. It is the non-negotiable process that ensures our famously clean manufacturing environments translate into safe work environments and better products for our customers.


The result is factories that look as good the day after the tour as they do the day of. That means our workers, the ones who matter most, can do their jobs confidently, go home safely and come back the next day ready to move the world forward.
