## Graco at interpack 2026: Uptime is the Headline Metric for Food Processing and Packaging Operations


Packaging operations are facing pressure from multiple directions. Labor shortages, growing SKU complexity, faster line speeds and tighter margins are forcing manufacturers to rethink how they measure performance. At interpack 2026, one metric stood out across conversations on the show floor: uptime.


For 100 years, Graco has focused on helping manufacturers keep critical processes running. That experience was on display at interpack. Graco showcased a range of packaging and processing technologies at the event, all designed to help manufacturers keep lines running predictably, safely and efficiently. From adhesive dispensing to hygienic fluid handling, the message at Graco’s booth was consistent. When uptime improves, overall performance follows.


*The interpack team commemorates Graco’s centennial anniversary with a special cake at the show.*


#### Why uptime matters more than ever


Across food, beverage, personal care and consumer packaged goods operations, uptime has become a central performance metric. Instead of optimizing individual pieces of equipment in isolation, manufacturers are increasingly focused on a broader question: How consistently can the line run as planned without interruption?


Several industry realities are driving this shift. Fewer experienced operators mean there is less margin for error. Higher line speeds mean that there are now bigger impacts for even minor disruptions. Capital budgets remain tight, pushing plants to extend the life and performance of existing assets rather than invest in entirely new lines.


Nearly every performance indicator in a packaging and processing operation ties back to uptime, including throughput, scrap, labor efficiency and missed ship dates. When a line stops, profitability stops with it.


#### Common causes of production line downtime


While downtime can occur from many sources, packaging and processing teams often point to the same systemic challenges:


- Flow instability that disrupts downstream equipment
-


Component wear that leads to unpredictable failures


-


Poor restart performance after cleaning or changeovers


-


Delayed access to service or critical spare parts


-


Ineffective preventative maintenance schedules


Because production lines are interconnected systems, instability in one area can affect the entire line, even if no single component completely fails.

As pricing pressures continue to tighten margins, uptime remains one of the few metrics manufacturers can focus on to protect cost per unit. Stable, predictable fluid handling helps reduce variability across the line and gives operators confidence that systems will perform as expected.
