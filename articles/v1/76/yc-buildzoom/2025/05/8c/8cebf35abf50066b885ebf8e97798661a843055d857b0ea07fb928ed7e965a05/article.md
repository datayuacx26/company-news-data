# The Rise of Unpermitted ADUs: A Growing Risk for Insurers, Realtors, and Lenders Alike

*Why tracking accessory dwelling unit construction through verified permit data is critical to managing exposure, ensuring compliance, and protecting asset value*


The rapid rise of **Accessory Dwelling Units (ADUs)** has created new economic opportunities for homeowners and municipalities alike—but it’s also introduced **significant new risks** for insurance carriers, real estate professionals, and mortgage lenders. A substantial portion of ADU growth is occurring **without permits** , bypassing critical safety inspections, building codes, and valuation standards.


These unpermitted units are often invisible to traditional data sources, yet they directly affect **property valuations, insurability, risk exposure, and resale complexity** .


In this changing landscape, **[building permit data has become a frontline defense](https://www.buildzoom.com/data)** , offering the only reliable view into where ADUs are being built, who is building them, and whether construction complies with local codes. And **[BuildZoom](https://www.buildzoom.com/data)** is the most comprehensive and trusted authority on U.S. building permit activity—across all jurisdictions.


## **ADUs Are Booming — But Many Are Flying Under the Radar**


Driven by housing shortages, affordability challenges, and changing zoning laws, ADUs have exploded in popularity over the past five years.


According to[Freddie Mac](https://www.freddiemac.com/research/insight/20200716-identifying-accessory-dwelling-units-from-real-estate) , the number of ADUs in the U.S. increased by **more than 8.6% per year between 2016 and 2020** , with over **1.4 million properties** now estimated to include some form of accessory dwelling. John Burns Research & Consulting found that ADU construction rose significantly in California,[going from 3,000 units per year in 2018 to 23,000 per year in 2023](https://themortgagepoint.com/2025/03/03/the-rise-of-adus-what-lenders-and-borrowers-need-to-know/) .


Key factors driving growth:


- Zoning reforms enabling “gentle density” in single-family neighborhoods


- Rising rent and home prices


- Homeowners seeking multi-generational housing or rental income


- Investor demand for cash-flowing residential assets


However, **not all ADUs are permitted** , and the problem is widespread:


- A[2023 study by UC Berkeley](https://ternercenter.berkeley.edu/wp-content/uploads/pdfs/Jumpstarting_the_Market_--_ULI.pdf) found that **40–60% of ADUs in California** are built without permits


- **Portland, OR** officials estimate that[nearly half of ADUs](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/01944363.2024.2345730#d1e911) built prior to 2016 were unpermitted


- A **Los Angeles Times** investigation found[illegal ADUs presen](https://www.latimes.com/homeless-housing/story/2023-05-24/how-to-get-permits-for-an-adu-built-without-them) t on **tens of thousands of properties** , often violating fire, zoning, or electrical codes


## **Why This Matters for Insurers, Realtors, and Lenders**


Unpermitted ADUs can create **severe downstream consequences** for multiple stakeholders:


### **▸ For Insurers:**


- **Unreported square footage** increases exposure without proper underwriting


- **Higher risk of fire, electrical, or structural issues** without code compliance


- **Claims disputes likely** in the event of damage to unpermitted structures


- **Difficulty pricing replacement cost** when construction details are unknown


- Unpermitted structures represent a **growing blind spot in risk modeling**


*“Understanding the full scope of a property’s structures is critical for accurate risk management by insurers,” said Attila Toth, founder and CEO of ZestyAI. “Our findings underscore the importance of advanced analytics to help insurers ensure comprehensive coverage while managing risk effectively.*


*Detached buildings, like garages or ADUs, can go unnoticed during the underwriting process if not explicitly declared. An undisclosed and potentially dilapidated secondary structure exposes the insurer to unforeseen risk, leading to situations where claims for these structures are unexpectedly high, driving up losses.”* –[Insurance Journal, 2024](https://www.insurancejournal.com/news/national/2024/12/18/805317.htm)


### **▸ For Realtors:**


- **Misrepresented property listings** can trigger liability


- **Appraisal complications** when unpermitted square footage is included


- **Buyer risk** if future renovations, insurance claims, or resale are impacted


- **Disclosure violations** if the agent fails to flag non-compliance


### **▸ For Lenders:**


- **Over-inflated collateral values** based on illegal improvements


- **Increased risk of default** if ADU income is interrupted or disallowed


- **Title issues** or **city enforcement actions** post-close


- **Loan buyback risk** if underwriting was based on inaccurate property data
