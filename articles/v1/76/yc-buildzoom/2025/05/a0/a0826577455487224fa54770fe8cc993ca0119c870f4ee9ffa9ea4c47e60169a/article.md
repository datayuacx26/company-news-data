# The Missing Piece in Fraud Detection: How Building Permit Data Powers Accuracy, Insight, and Integrity

In the age of advanced analytics and AI-driven decision-making, businesses across sectors are leaning heavily on data to detect anomalies, flag risk, and prevent fraud. Yet many still overlook one of the most revealing and underutilized data sets available: **building permits** .


At BuildZoom, we specialize in turning raw permit data into structured intelligence—and we’ve seen firsthand how it fills critical gaps left by traditional data sources.


### **Why Fraud Detection Requires More Than Traditional Data**


Fraud costs businesses and institutions billions of dollars annually. Whether it’s inflated property valuations, phantom renovations, unpermitted construction, or false claims, these fraudulent activities are often masked in data that looks clean on the surface. That’s because most analytics models rely on **assessor records, MLS data, insurance claims, or tax records** —each of which can be incomplete, outdated, or misleading without proper context.


**Permit data tells a deeper story.** It provides timestamped, jurisdiction-verified evidence of actual work done on a property: when it was started, what type of work it was, who did it, and whether it passed inspection. This creates an immutable trail of documentation that can be cross-referenced to identify mismatches, detect suspicious behavior, and verify accuracy.


### **What Makes Permit Data a Key Differentiator**


Unlike other data sets that rely on self-reporting or subjective evaluation, **building permits are regulatory records** . That means they’re vetted by municipal agencies and tied directly to real-world activity. This makes permit data:


- **Highly Accurate** : Each record reflects an official transaction reviewed by a government body.


- **Chronologically Verifiable** : Time-stamped and event-based, permits provide a historical timeline of property changes.


- **Granular** : Data can be parsed down to project type, contractor, scope, and even inspection outcomes.


These traits make it uniquely valuable not just for verifying work, but for **detecting patterns that other data sets can’t reveal** .


### **Who Benefits From Permit-Based Fraud Detection?**


While insurance companies are obvious users—validating claims, pricing risk, and flagging suspicious construction activity—fraud detection through permit data is relevant across multiple industries:


- **Financial Services & Lending** : Detecting unpermitted property improvements that artificially inflate home values during refinance or home equity line applications.


- **Real Estate Investment & PropTech** : Verifying the legitimacy and value-add of renovations on investment properties before acquisition.


- **Government & Compliance Agencies** : Monitoring contractor behavior, identifying unlicensed work, or flagging potential labor law violations.


- **Home Services & Warranties** : Ensuring claims match up with actual authorized installations or improvements.


### **Why Other Data Alone Falls Short**


Assessor and tax records often lag behind reality, missing major renovations until years later—or never catching them at all. MLS data only reflects what a listing agent chooses to disclose. Claims data is reactive and subject to bias. Permit data, on the other hand, is proactive, consistent, and jurisdictionally anchored.


That’s why **permit data should not be optional—it should be foundational** to any fraud detection strategy.
