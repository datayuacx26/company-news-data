# Fighting Forever Chemicals in our Water Supply

Ensuring clean and safe drinking water is a growing concern in the United States. One particular issue is the presence of per- and poly-fluoroalkyl substances (PFAS) in drinking water, which has raised national alarm. A recent study conducted across the country compared the levels of PFAS in private wells and public water supplies. The findings revealed widespread PFAS contamination in drinking water, highlighting the need for alternatives to address this problem.


## The Scope of the Problem


A[newly published study](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160412023003069?via%3Dihub#s0040) collected tap water samples from various locations in the US, including private wells and public water supplies, between 2016 and 2021. Results showed that PFAS were detectable in about 45% of the samples. While PFAS levels were similar in private wells and public water supplies, certain regions, such as the Great Plains, Great Lakes, Eastern Seaboard, and Central/Southern California, had higher instances of PFAS contamination. The issue persisted over time with limited changes observed. It's important to note that private wells and small water supplies often lack testing, leaving a significant portion of the population unaware of PFAS exposure risks.


## The Need for Safer Alternatives


PFAS compounds have been widely used in coatings due to their unique properties, such as water and oil repellency. However, studies have raised concerns about the potential health risks associated with prolonged PFAS exposure, particularly with specific compounds like PFOA and PFOS. Consequently, finding safer alternatives to PFAS coatings has become crucial.

Expanding monitoring efforts is crucial to comprehensively understand PFAS exposure and associated health risks. spotLESS Materials can contribute by providing surface treatments that resist PFAS contamination, reducing the likelihood of PFAS leaching into drinking water from coated surfaces. Integrating geospatial data with PFAS information can help identify regions and populations vulnerable to contamination, enabling targeted mitigation strategies. Moreover, expanding monitoring programs to include unmonitored private wells and underserved communities will provide a more accurate assessment of PFAS contamination across different water sources.
