# How buyer intent tracking improves account priority

Every B2B SaaS revenue team eventually asks the same question: out of thousands of accounts in our total addressable market, which ones deserve attention today, this morning, before the next call block starts? Buyer intent tracking exists to answer that, but only when it's built to actually prioritize, not just report on what happened last week.


### **What "good" buyer intent tracking looks like**


Effective intent tracking does three things well, and most tools on the market only do one or two. First, it identifies which accounts are showing real in-market behavior right now, not a lagging quarterly snapshot pulled together for a board deck. Second, it scores those accounts against criteria that matter to your business specifically, not generic industry patterns borrowed from a category-wide model. Third, it puts that priority list in front of the people who need it, in the tools they already use every day, without requiring a habit change or a new login.


### **Why account-level scoring alone falls short**


Most intent platforms stop at telling you a company is "in-market." That's a start, but it doesn't tell a rep who to call, what to say, or why this account matters more than the fifty others also showing intent this week. Real account prioritization requires combining intent with fit data, firmographics, tech stack, past engagement history, and historical outcome data: what your actual closed-won deals looked like in the weeks and months before they became opportunities. Without that layer, "in-market" is just a label, not a priority.


### **The role of contact-level signal in account priority**


Account priority isn't only about which companies matter, it's about which specific people inside those companies are showing buying behavior. A account can technically be "in-market" while the specific contact your rep is calling has no involvement in the purchase decision. Layering contact-level intent (known and de-anonymized unknown website visitors, role changes, engagement history) on top of account-level signal is what turns a company name into an actionable target.


### **Turning intent into a prioritized list your team trusts**


UserGems' Data Agents continuously capture buyer intent, alongside job changes, funding events, and tech stack shifts, from both known and unknown visitors, combined with your existing CRM data, call transcripts, and calendar activity. Intelligence Agents then combine those signals with your CRM history into a custom AI scoring model built specifically from your own sales data, so weighting reflects how your buyers actually behave, not a category average built from companies unlike yours.


Because the model is transparent, sales and marketing can see exactly why an account is prioritized, which signals drove the score, how recently they occurred, and how much weight each one carried, and override the score when they know something the model doesn't, like a personal relationship with the champion or context from a recent call. That shared, explainable priority list is what finally gets sales and marketing chasing the same accounts instead of debating whose spreadsheet is right, surfaced directly inside Salesforce, HubSpot, or your SEP through the AI Chrome Extension so there's no separate tool for either team to check.


### **The payoff**


Teams that connect intent tracking to a custom scoring model, instead of treating intent as a standalone report living in a BI dashboard, see the difference immediately: reps start each day with a ranked list instead of a spreadsheet they have to build themselves, and marketing can finally point to accounts moving through the funnel rather than the same static account list quarter after quarter. That shift, more than any single new data source, is what turns buyer intent tracking from a nice-to-have report into the operating system for account priority.


### **What changes for reps day to day**


The clearest way to evaluate whether account priority has actually improved is to watch what a rep does at 9am on a Monday. In a broken process, that rep opens the CRM, sorts by last-activity date or deal size, and works down a list built mostly on habit. In a working process, the rep opens the same CRM and sees a ranked list already built for them, the accounts and contacts most likely to convert today, based on live signals scored against the company's own sales history, with the reasoning visible alongside each name. That difference sounds small, but multiplied across every rep, every morning, it's the difference between a team that's reactive to whatever's loudest and one that's systematically working the accounts most likely to close.


### **Frequently asked questions**


**Does account priority require contact-level data, or is account-level intent enough on its own?** Account-level intent is a useful starting filter, but it can't tell a rep who to call. Layering contact-level signal, known and de-anonymized unknown visitors, role changes, engagement history, on top of account-level intent is what turns a prioritized company into a prioritized person your team can actually reach out to.


**How often should an account priority list update?** Ideally continuously, as new signals arrive, rather than on a weekly or monthly refresh. A priority list that's a few weeks stale can send reps chasing accounts that have already cooled or missed ones that just started showing real intent.


**What makes a priority list "trusted" by both sales and marketing?** Transparency. When both teams can see exactly which signals drove a score and adjust it when they know something the model doesn't, they stop debating whose list is right and start working from the same one.


**Does account priority replace the need for rep judgment entirely?** No, the goal is to remove guesswork from where a rep starts their day, not to remove their judgment from the process. A transparent, editable model is designed so reps and managers can override a score when they have context the data doesn't capture, like a competitive threat or a relationship history that hasn't been logged anywhere.


**How should sales managers coach reps on using a new account priority list?** Frame it as a starting point for the day, not a rigid script, the goal is for reps to spend less time deciding where to start and more time on the conversations themselves, while still applying their own judgment about how to approach each specific account and contact.
