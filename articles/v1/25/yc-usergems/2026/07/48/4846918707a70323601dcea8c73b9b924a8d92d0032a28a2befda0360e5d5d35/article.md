# Signs your 6sense investment is failing (and what to do about it)

# Signs your 6sense investment is failing


**Quick answer:** Most 6sense frustration clusters around five things: intent signals that don't convert, reps who've stopped logging in, no way to attribute pipeline to the platform, decaying contact data, and a six-figure renewal nobody can justify. If three or more of these sound familiar, it's worth evaluating alternatives — starting with UserGems, Demandbase, ZoomInfo, Cognism, and Apollo.io, compared below.


You signed a six-figure contract expecting to identify in-market buyers before your competitors. Eighteen months later, your sales team ignores the platform, your intent signals feel disconnected from actual buying behavior, and nobody can answer the question: what pipeline came from 6sense?


This pattern plays out more often than vendors like to admit. Below: the five clearest warning signs your 6sense investment has stopped delivering, what to look for in an alternative, and how the top competitors compare.


## Top 5 signs your 6sense investment is failing


Most teams start questioning their 6sense investment when intent signals stop turning into actual conversations. You're paying for a platform that promises to identify in-market accounts, but your reps keep reaching out to contacts who have no idea what you sell.


The warning signs tend to cluster around five areas: signal quality, adoption, attribution, data accuracy, and cost. If you're experiencing three or more, it's probably time to look elsewhere.


### 1. Intent signals rarely convert to qualified pipeline


Intent signals, in theory, tell you which accounts are actively researching topics related to your product. When it works, it's powerful. When it doesn't, you're cold calling with extra steps.


The problem many teams run into is that the signal feels disconnected from reality: marketing sends over a list of "high intent" accounts, sales reaches out, and the response is crickets — or the contact has no idea what you're talking about. A few patterns tend to show up:


- **Signal opacity:** You can see an account is "surging" on a topic, but not which person engaged or what they actually did.
- **Timing gaps:** Accounts flagged as in-market show zero awareness of your category when reps call.
- **Weak conversion:** Leads from intent data convert at lower rates than referrals, inbound, or even cold outreach.


When sales consistently reports that "high intent" accounts feel cold, the signal has become a liability rather than an asset.


### 2. Sales reps ignore the platform or work around it


This shows up quietly. Reps start building their own lists in LinkedIn Sales Navigator. They skip 6sense recommendations entirely. They only log in when a manager asks for proof they're using it.


Unused software delivers zero ROI regardless of what it can theoretically do. If reps find the platform too complex to navigate daily, or the learning curve stretches into months, it's overbuilt for how the team actually works. Watch where reps spend their time — if it's not the tool you're paying six figures for, that tells you something.


### 3. You cannot attribute revenue to 6sense activities


A question that tends to surface at renewal time: what pipeline came from 6sense this quarter? If answering that requires a 30-minute explanation with caveats, there's an attribution problem.


Effective revenue tools connect directly to closed-won deals in the CRM — you can point to a signal, trace it to an opportunity, and see the outcome. When the only available metric is "influenced pipeline" that nobody fully trusts, or reports get manually stitched together across systems, the platform's value becomes nearly impossible to defend to leadership.


### 4. Contact and account data quality has declined


B2B data decays fast, mcontacts change jobs, emails bounce, phone numbers go stale. A platform that worked well two years ago can quietly become unreliable if data maintenance falls behind.


Watch for rising email bounce rates, reps wasting time on contacts who left the company months ago, and missing decision-makers at target accounts. When the team spends more time cleaning data than acting on it, the tool has become a bottleneck instead of an accelerator.


### 5. The cost no longer justifies the results


6sense contracts commonly run into six figures annually at the enterprise tier, frequently with multi-year terms — independent pricing trackers put median deals in the $50K–$60K range and full enterprise packages well above $100K. That's a reasonable investment when pipeline flows consistently. It's harder to justify when renewal conversations trigger internal debate about whether the platform is actually working.


If the platform sits underutilized while you pay enterprise pricing, or leadership keeps asking for proof of value, the economics have shifted. At some point the sunk-cost argument stops holding up.


## Why B2B revenue teams are replacing 6sense


The market has shifted since most teams first adopted 6sense:


- **Signal-based selling:** Teams want buyer signals tied to specific contacts with clear context, not anonymous account-level scores that require interpretation.
- **AI execution:** Modern platforms use AI agents to draft and queue outreach automatically, where legacy intent tools stop at the alert.
- **Integration depth:** Revenue teams expect native connections into Salesforce, HubSpot, Outreach, and Salesloft — not middleware that lags or breaks.
- **Accountability:** Leadership increasingly wants pipeline attribution and revenue guarantees, not "influenced" metrics.


Platforms built primarily around anonymous intent data are under growing pressure from alternatives that pair signals with execution.


## What to look for in a 6sense alternative


### Transparent, contact-level buyer signals


"Actionable" means signals tied to specific contacts with clear context on why they matter — job changes, website visits from known contacts, buying-committee identification. Opaque account-level scores that require interpretation don't qualify. The best platforms show exactly which person took which action, so reps can reference something specific instead of guessing at relevance.


### Native CRM and sales engagement integrations


The alternative should push tasks, contacts, and context directly into the tools reps already use. Ask whether integrations are native and real-time, or dependent on third-party connectors that can lag or break.


### Accurate, verified contact data


Verified emails and direct phone numbers, automatic job-change tracking, and freshness maintained without manual cleanup. Data quality directly drives deliverability and rep productivity.


### AI agents that execute outbound, not just flag it


The modern bar is AI that researches accounts, drafts personalized outreach, and queues it for send — turning a signal platform into a pipeline engine instead of a dashboard.


### Flexible pricing with revenue accountability


Usage-based pricing, revenue guarantees, or pipeline commitments shift risk from buyer to vendor and align incentives around outcomes instead of seat access.

## Questions to ask before switching from 6sense


1. **How do you measure and verify signal accuracy?** Ask vendors to explain how they validate that a signal indicates real buying intent, and request signal-to-pipeline conversion data.
2. **Which integrations are native to your platform?** Native integrations are built-in and update in real time; third-party connectors can lag, break, or need ongoing maintenance.
3. **How quickly will we see pipeline impact?** Some platforms need months of setup; others generate pipeline within weeks. Ask for timelines and references from similar customers.
4. **Do you offer a revenue guarantee?** A guarantee tied to pipeline or revenue outcomes shifts risk from buyer to vendor and signals confidence in the product.
5. **Can this run alongside 6sense during a transition?** Most signal-based platforms are modular — you can bring 6sense intent in as one signal among several while you evaluate, rather than ripping and replacing on day one.
