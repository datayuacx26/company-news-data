# Want to maximize network ROI? Start by minimizing technical debt.

## Why minimizing technical debt is hard


Knowing your organization has technical debt is one thing—but addressing it is another. Time, costs and resource availability often prevent organizations from replacing or upgrading infrastructure that isn’t technically failing but also doesn’t support future operational needs. It can be hard to make the case for a major improvement that will solve a problem down the road, especially if the new applications or technologies that will depend on it aren’t live.


But delaying action creates a cycle that’s hard to break free from. If you wait until new technology deployment forces the issue, then you face rushed upgrades to get the network ready in time. This is not only disruptive but can also lead to higher costs and mistakes; there’s less time to plan, assess options and coordinate implementation, so decisions may not support your overall network modernization strategy. This can create more complexity and make the next upgrade even more difficult and expensive.


That’s the thing about technical debt: **It behaves like financial debt that accrues interest** .[Technical debt compounds over time](https://www.belden.com/blog/your-hotel-network-infrastructure-is-about-business-not-tech) , and it gets worse the longer you ignore it. The further you push upgrades, the more likely you are to rely on short-term fixes that will make inevitable modernization more complex and expensive.
