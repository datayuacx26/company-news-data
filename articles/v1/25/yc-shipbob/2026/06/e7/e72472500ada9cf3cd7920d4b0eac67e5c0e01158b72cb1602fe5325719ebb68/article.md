## What is cross-border shipping?


Cross-border shipping is the process of transporting goods from one country to another to fulfill customer orders.


Unlike domestic shipping, it requires[clearing customs](https://www.shipbob.com/blog/customs-clearance/) , complying with each destination country’s import regulations, and managing the duties and taxes that apply to imported goods.


The process follows a predictable lifecycle:


1. An order is placed.
2. The brand prepares the shipment with customs documentation.
3. A carrier transports goods across borders.
4. Customs authorities clear the shipment.
5. A local carrier handles final delivery.


Today,[67% of brands already ship outside the US](https://www.shipbob.com/fulfillment-trends/) . For midmarket brands looking to scale, understanding cross-border shipping is essential to unlocking new revenue.


### Benefits of successful cross-border shipping for ecommerce brands


Expanding internationally opens significant opportunities for ecommerce brands. Here are the key benefits:


- **Revenue diversification:** Selling in multiple countries reduces dependence on a single market, which is especially valuable when tariffs or economic shifts affect your domestic business.
- **Access to new customer bases:** Cross-border shipping lets you reach customers in countries where demand already exists, without waiting to build local operations.
- **Competitive differentiation:** Many brands still avoid international shipping due to perceived complexity. Offering reliable cross-border delivery sets you apart.

- **Higher customer retention:** Customers who receive reliable international shipping are more likely to reorder from a brand they trust.


In the current tariff environment, brands with cross-border capabilities are better positioned to adapt when trade policies shift.

## Challenges and complexities of cross-border shipping


The 2025 tariff changes and de minimis suspension have made cross-border shipping significantly more complex. What was once a straightforward process now requires careful planning around duties, compliance, and cost management.


### 📚 Navigating customs regulations and documentation


Every country has its own import regulations, and getting documentation wrong can lead to delays, fines, or seized shipments.


At the core of customs compliance is the Harmonized System (HS) code, a standardized numerical code used globally to classify traded products. **HS code classification determines the duty rate on your goods.**


Misclassifying a product can result in overpaying duties, facing penalties, or having your shipment held at the border. You can find the correct code through your country’s customs authority website or by working with a licensed customs broker.


Brands should also be aware that certain product categories face additional import restrictions depending on the destination country. Shipping a restricted product without proper documentation can result in the shipment being returned or destroyed.


Essential[shipping documents](https://www.shipbob.com/blog/shipping-documents/) for cross-border shipping include:


- Commercial invoice with product descriptions, values, and HS codes
- Customs declaration form
- Bill of lading or air waybill
- Certificate of origin
- Packing list with weights and dimensions


Keep in mind that navigating import restrictions is ultimately the responsibility of the brand. A customs broker or trade compliance specialist can help you stay current on regulations.

The 2025 de minimis suspension made landed cost management even more critical. Previously, US-bound shipments under $800 entered duty-free. **With that exemption suspended, brands that relied on de minimis shipping now face duties on every shipment.**


Brands generally choose between two models for who pays these costs:


- **DDP (Delivered Duty Paid):** The seller pays all duties and taxes upfront. The customer sees one price at checkout with no surprise charges.
- **DDU/DAP (Delivered Duty Unpaid):** The customer pays duties upon delivery, which can lead to refused shipments and negative reviews.


For most ecommerce brands,[DDP shipping](https://www.shipbob.com/blog/ddp-shipping/) is the better option. We offer DDP capabilities for hundreds of destinations, so you can present accurate landed costs at checkout.


### ✈️ Long transit times and high shipping costs


For inventory replenishment, ocean freight is significantly cheaper than air but takes weeks instead of days. Most brands use ocean freight for planned transfers and reserve air for urgent restocks.


For direct-to-customer delivery, express shipping (2-5 days) costs more but provides a better experience. Economy options (7-21 days) are cheaper but can increase cart abandonment.


The most effective way to reduce both transit times and costs is to store inventory in the same country as your buyers. Orders then ship domestically, eliminating customs delays and reducing expenses.

### Choose the right shipping options for your customers


Brands should carefully select shipping models based on their target market and customer preferences. Here are the key decisions:


- **Express vs. economy:** Offer both where possible. Some customers will pay more for faster delivery, while others prioritize lower costs. Testing default options by country can help you find the right balance.
- **Carrier diversification:** Different carriers have strengths in different countries. Using multiple carriers ensures better coverage, competitive pricing, and backup options when service disruptions occur.
- **DDP vs. DDU by market:** In countries where customers expect all-inclusive pricing (most of Europe), DDP is essential. In other markets DDU may be acceptable, though DDP is still recommended for the best customer experience.


Understanding your target market’s preferences and price sensitivity will help determine the optimal approach for each country you serve.


### Prepare accurate customs documentation


Precise, comprehensive[customs documentation](https://support.shipbob.com/s/article/Customs-Information) is essential for smooth international deliveries. A single error on a commercial invoice can delay your shipment by weeks. Establish standardized procedures for completing:


- Commercial invoices with accurate product descriptions, values, and HS codes.
- Accurate product valuation that meets destination-country customs requirements.
- Proper country of origin declarations.
- Complete shipper and recipient information.


Building documentation checklists by country helps your team maintain consistency and avoid repeated mistakes.
