## What is an unboxing experience?


An unboxing experience is the complete sensory and emotional interaction a customer has when receiving, opening, and discovering an ecommerce order. It spans the outer box or mailer, the interior presentation, the inserts, and the product reveal.


It is a brand’s most tangible post-purchase touchpoint, and one of the few moments where a digital brand becomes physical.


The experience starts before the box arrives. Branded tracking like ShipBob’s[TrackBob](https://support.shipbob.com/s/article/Branded-Tracking-Page-TrackBob) keeps customers updated on a page that carries your logo, colors, and offers, so the reveal on the doorstep feels like the payoff to a story that is already underway.


## Why the unboxing experience matters for ecommerce brands


A branded unboxing experience increases perceived product value, drives repeat purchases, generates organic social content, and sets your brand apart from competitors shipping in plain brown boxes.


### It drives repeat purchases and customer loyalty


Acquiring a new customer can cost several times more than keeping an existing one, so the post-purchase moment is high-value. A thoughtful unboxing makes customers feel valued, and that feeling is what brings them back.


Shoppers reward the effort, too. When an order arrives in branded packaging,[55% of them](https://www.shipbob.com/product/customization-suite/) say they’re more likely to come back to the brand’s website and buy again.


Loyalty schemes and on-site personalization work, but they take time to build. A strong unboxing is simpler: it turns a routine delivery into a reason to reorder.


### It increases perceived value and tells your brand story


For most brands, the product itself might not be entirely unique. Packaging is where you show what makes the brand different. Branded packaging is the physical expression of your brand identity. It’s the one moment where a DTC brand competes on the same sensory level as in-store retail.


When a customer’s last several orders arrive in plain poly bags, the brand that shows up in a custom box with tissue paper and a personal note wins the memory. Tiffany & Co. built decades of brand equity on exactly this, and its[robin’s egg blue box](https://www.tiffany.co.uk/world-of-tiffany/heritage/blue-box-story.html) is now as recognizable as anything inside it.


### It generates social content and UGC


Short-form video on TikTok, Instagram Reels, and YouTube Shorts has made packaging more visible than ever. An unboxing designed to be filmed earns organic impressions you cannot buy.


You can also make that content easier to capture and measure. A QR code on an insert can link to a review prompt or a hashtag campaign, turning the unboxing moment into a content source you can track.


## How to build a branded unboxing experience


A memorable unboxing breaks down into five elements. Treat each as both a design choice and a fulfillment decision, and match the investment to your stage.


### Branded boxes and mailers


The outer packaging is the first thing a customer sees, so it should signal who the order is from before it is even opened.


You supply your own branded boxes or mailers, and a fulfillment partner like ShipBob stores each one as its own SKU and packs your orders in it. Use a box for structured, giftable products, and a mailer for soft or flat items that ship lighter and cheaper.


Match the spend to your stage:


- *Starter:* a plain box finished with a large branded sticker or a custom sender label.
- *Growth:* custom-printed mailers, which carry lower minimums and less dimensional weight.
- *Premium:* fully custom boxes with printed interiors.


Right-sizing matters as much as design. You set which products ship in your[custom packaging](http://shipbob.com/blog/custom-packaging/) , and ShipBob’s box selection algorithm packs each order in the most cost-effective box for its contents, which keeps dimensional weight and material use down without flattening your presentation.


### Custom tissue paper, stickers, and labels


The layer just inside the box is a low-cost, high-impact branding opportunity. Custom tissue paper, branded stickers, and custom sender labels all reinforce your identity the moment the lid comes off.


These touches are easy to add without redesigning your whole packing process, which makes them a smart first upgrade for brands just starting to invest in unboxing. Branded tissue with a sticker seal can make a plain box feel considered.


Tissue paper is also one of the most affordable custom elements, with per-unit costs well under a dollar at volume and minimums low enough to suit smaller runs.


### Product presentation and interior fill


How the product sits inside the box decides whether the unboxing feels curated or careless. Layering, a tissue wrap, and a tidy arrangement give customers something worth photographing.


Interior fill should look intentional, not like an afterthought. ShipBob’s standard void fill is paper-based, using Geami paper or kraft paper that keeps contents snug and reads as clean and considered.


Aim for the right amount of fill, not the most. A right-sized box with a neat product-to-fill ratio looks more premium and cuts material waste at the same time.


### Inserts: samples, QR codes, and cards


Inserts are where you extend the relationship past the sale. The toolkit includes gift notes, thank-you cards, discount codes for the next order, product samples, QR codes, and seasonal promotional inserts.


Choose samples that match what the customer bought, since a relevant sample is more likely to drive a follow-up purchase. A QR code can point to a how-to video, a loyalty sign-up, or a review request.


Match inserts to your stage:


- *Starter:* a printed card with a QR code.
- *Growth:* a card plus a product sample.
- *Premium:* kitted insert sets that change by product line, customer segment, or season.


Marketing inserts can be printed on demand and swapped by season or SKU without reworking your core packing steps, or stored as their own SKU when you want a fixed insert in every order.


### Personalization and gift messaging


Personalization is the difference between an order that feels mass-produced and one that feels meant for the recipient. A short note is the simplest version.


Handwritten notes work at 50 orders a month, not 5,000. The scalable version is a gift message a customer adds at checkout, which is then printed and included automatically during fulfillment.


Personalization can also mean segment-level rules. Using SKU-level packaging preferences, you can send a welcome card to first-time buyers or add a sample for repeat customers, without anyone on the floor having to remember to do it.


## How to execute branded unboxing at scale


Designing a good-looking package is the easy part. The hard part is delivering it identically on the thousandth order as on the first. That is a fulfillment problem, and it is where most brands either scale their unboxing or quietly abandon it.


### Embed your packaging rules into the pick-pack workflow


At volume, unboxing cannot depend on notes taped to a warehouse wall. The rules have to live in the system that picks and packs every order.


With ShipBob’s[Customization Suite](https://www.shipbob.com/product/customization-suite/) , you can set SKU-level packaging preferences and packaging rules once, for example, that a given product always ships in your black box with tissue and a welcome card, and they apply to every matching order.


The suite also covers kitting, where ShipBob picks and packs recurring product sets in advance so multi-item orders go out the same way each time. Gift notes and marketing inserts round it out, printed on demand or stored as SKUs.


### Keep it consistent across channels and warehouses


Brands rarely sell in one place. Your[DTC store](https://www.shipbob.com/blog/dtc-shipping/) , marketplaces like Amazon, Walmart, and TikTok Shop, and your wholesale orders should all feel like the same brand on arrival.


Because ShipBob fulfills those channels from one unified inventory pool, the same packaging rules apply no matter where the order came from. You are not maintaining a separate process per channel.


Packaging standards are also set at the network level, not facility by facility, so an order packed in one fulfillment center matches one packed in another.


### Avoid the common operational mistakes


A few predictable mistakes undo unboxing efforts at scale:


- Oversized boxes inflate dimensional weight and shipping cost, which is why right-sized box selection matters.
- Too many packaging SKUs create clutter on the floor and raise the odds of a pick error.
- Custom packaging that was not designed for the packing workflow slows down every order that uses it.
- Instructions that live in someone’s head instead of the system produce different results on different shifts.


Each one is avoidable when your packaging rules are built into how orders get picked and packed, not left to memory on the warehouse floor.


## What does custom packaging cost?


Packaging cost depends on the element, the supplier, and your order quantity, so set a budget before you design. The ranges below are typical starting points, not quotes.


### Typical costs for custom packaging


Element Typical cost Notes


Custom boxes ~$1.20-$6.50 per unit Standard corrugated at volume; premium rigid boxes run higher


Custom tissue paper ~$.017-$1.26 per unit Low minimums, often 50-100 units


Printed insert cards ~0.10-$0.50 per unit Samples vary by product


A simpler way to plan is by cost added per order:


- *Starter (about $0.25–$0.75 per order):* a branded sticker and a printed insert card with a QR code.
- *Growth (about $1–$3 per order):* custom tissue, an insert card, and a sample.
- *Premium (about $3–$8+ per order):* a fully custom box, tissue, kitted inserts, and gift messaging.


Custom kitting and printed gift notes carry additional fulfillment costs, and a partner like ShipBob packs these elements into standard picking and packing so your labor cost per order does not climb with every added touch.

## Unboxing experience FAQs


Here are the most commonly asked questions about the unboxing experience.


#### What is an unboxing experience?


An unboxing experience is everything a customer sees and feels when receiving and opening an ecommerce order, from the outer box to the interior presentation, inserts, and product reveal. It is a brand’s main physical touchpoint after the sale.


#### How much does custom packaging cost through a fulfillment partner?


Expect roughly $0.25 to $0.75 per order for a starter setup, $1 to $3 for a mid-tier setup, and $3 to $8 or more for a fully custom premium box. With a partner like ShipBob, branded materials are packed into standard picking and packing, so you pay for materials rather than extra labor on every order.


#### Can you use branded packaging for marketplace orders like Amazon or TikTok Shop?


Yes. For marketplace orders you fulfill through ShipBob rather than a marketplace’s own fulfillment service, the same branded packaging and insert rules apply as your DTC orders, because everything ships from one unified inventory pool.


#### What are the best sustainable packaging options for ecommerce?


Recyclable corrugated boxes, compostable mailers, paper-based void fill, and soy-based inks all reduce a package’s footprint while still looking premium. Right-sizing the box helps too, since less empty space means less material and less shipping weight. ShipBob’s standard void fill is already paper-based, using Geami paper or kraft paper.


#### How do you scale a branded unboxing experience without slowing down fulfillment?


Put the rules in the system instead of in people’s heads. SKU-level packaging preferences, kitting, and on-demand inserts, managed through a fulfillment platform like ShipBob’s Customization Suite, apply your packaging standards automatically to every order.
