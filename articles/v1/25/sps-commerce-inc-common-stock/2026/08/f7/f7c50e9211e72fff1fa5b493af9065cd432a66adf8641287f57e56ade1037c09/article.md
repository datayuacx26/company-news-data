Most tariff coverage tells suppliers what tariffs cost. This article is about something more challenging: finding out what they are costing you, specifically, right now. Tariff-era margin erosion hides. Companies bundle input costs into aggregate expense lines, watch gross sales instead of cash collected, and absorb cost increases gradually enough that no single month triggers an alarm.


McKinsey documented a consumer goods manufacturer that took more than a year to notice its core component costs had risen 12 percent, because it bundled those inputs with other manufacturing expenses. By the time the company discovered the erosion, stemming it required an average 8 percent price increase, a far bigger jump than its customers were used to absorbing. The money was leaving the whole time, and nobody had instrumented the profit and loss (P&L) statement to see it.


Below are 5 signals that tariff pressure is quietly eroding your margin, each paired with the specific place in your own data to check. A finance lead at a small or mid-market consumer packaged goods (CPG) supplier should be able to run all 5 checks within a week.


## Why Is Tariff Margin Erosion So Hard To See?


The visible tariff line is the smallest part of the story. The duty you pay at import shows up on an invoice. The second-order effects, absorbed input costs, deduction creep after repricing, slower payments from stressed retailers and distributors, spread across accounts that nobody reads as tariff exposure.


The scale of the quiet damage is measurable. S&P Global Market Intelligence found that companies paid at least[$1.2 trillion more in 2025 expenses](https://www.spglobal.com/market-intelligence/en/news-insights/research/2025/10/margin-math) than anticipated on January 1, producing a 64 basis point contraction in global corporate margins. At the same time, early 2026 earnings commentary showed companies publicly calling tariffs manageable while their filings revealed margin compression and consumer pushback against further price increases, a gap the advisory firm Wiss highlighted in its[review of the Reuters earnings analysis](https://wiss.com/tariffs-squeeze-corporate-margins/) .


Suppliers planning on stale margin assumptions are working from a picture that flatters them. The five signals below replace it with your own numbers.


## Signal 1: Is Your Gross-to-Net Gap Widening?


**What the symptom looks like:** Sales reports stay healthy while cash collected lags behind them. Gross revenue holds or grows, but the spread between what you invoiced and what actually arrived keeps widening.


**Why tariffs cause it:** Deductions and allowances already claim a large share of CPG gross sales. Retailers may deduct[5 to 15 percent of gross sales](https://www.spscommerce.com/community/articles/understanding-trade-spend-in-cpg-retail) for promotional or compliance-related reasons before a supplier ever sees payment. Tariff pressure widens that band: retailers under their own cost stress enforce compliance programs harder, and every pricing change you push through creates new opportunities for discrepancies.


**Where to check:** Trend gross-to-net monthly by retailer, not annually in aggregate. An annual aggregate view smooths the signal away; a monthly, per-retailer view shows which relationships are leaking and when the leak started, usually traceable to a specific repricing event or policy change.


**Related Reading:**[Understanding Net Sales Leakage](https://www.spscommerce.com/community/articles/understanding-net-sales-leakage)


## Signal 2: Why Is Calculating Tariff Impact on COGS So Hard?


**What the symptom looks like:** If someone asked what tariffs added to your[cost of goods sold (COGS)](https://www.spscommerce.com/community/articles/retail-accounting-terms-acronyms-and-formulas-cheat-sheet) last quarter, the honest answer would be an estimate, not a number. Input costs sit under broad expense headers, and no single line isolates tariff-exposed materials.


**Why tariffs cause it:** Aggregation masks volatility. The McKinsey consumer goods example above is the cautionary tale for this exact failure: an unnamed company[bundled raw material costs under broad manufacturing expense headers](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/growth-marketing-and-sales/our-insights/the-commercial-response-to-cost-volatility) , and a 12 percent component cost increase hid inside them for 14 months.


**Where to check:** Calculating tariff impact on COGS starts with instrumentation: break tariff-exposed inputs into their own tracked cost lines, each with an effective date. Once tariff-exposed materials carry their own lines, the month a duty change lands becomes visible in the statement your finance team already reads.


## Signal 3: Does Deduction Volume Move After Repricing Events?


**What the symptom looks like:** A few weeks after each tariff-driven price change, price discrepancy deductions tick upward. Each one looks small and routine, so accounts receivable clears them without escalation.


**Why tariffs cause it:** Every price change that is not synchronized across the cost agreement, the purchase order (PO), and the invoice generates a mismatch, and mismatches become deductions. Tariff volatility multiplies repricing events, and each event that reaches your retailers and distributors on different dates, or in different systems, creates a fresh wave of discrepancies.


**Where to check:** Overlay deduction volume against your repricing dates. If deduction spikes trail your price changes by two to six weeks, you are looking at a synchronization failure on your side rather than a change in retailer behavior. Synchronization failures are fixable, and the deductions they generate are frequently disputable.


## Signal 4: What Is the Real Financial Impact of Tariffs You Are Absorbing?


**What the symptom looks like:** Your pricing decisions assumed you would pass most tariff costs through. Your actuals say otherwise, and nobody made the decision to absorb the difference.


Why tariffs cause it: Absorption tends to happen by default rather than by choice. Goldman Sachs economists estimated that as of August 2025, US businesses were absorbing a net[51 percent of tariff costs](https://www.foxbusiness.com/economy/us-businesses-consumers-shoulder-bulk-tariff-cost-burden-goldman-sachs-finds) , in part because it takes time to raise prices on consumers and negotiate lower import prices with suppliers. The American Action Forum, citing surveys alongside the Goldman analysis, put US companies’ absorbed share at[more than 60 percent of tariff costs as of June](https://www.americanactionforum.org/research/the-impact-of-tariffs-on-small-businesses/) . Even a disciplined, deliberate response can trail the erosion: P&G raised US prices 2 to 2.5 percent to offset tariffs yet still reported a[fifth consecutive quarterly margin decline](https://wiss.com/tariffs-squeeze-corporate-margins/) , and its own Q1 FY2026 release shows 70 basis points of higher costs from tariffs and commodities pressuring gross margin.


**Where to check:** Compare the absorption rate you planned in your pricing decisions against what your actuals show. The planned number lives in your pricing memos; the actual number is your tariff-driven cost increase minus the revenue recovered through price changes. If the two have never been put side by side, the gap between them is your undecided absorption.


## Signal 5: Is Your Trading Network’s Stress Becoming Yours?


**What the symptom looks like:** Payments arrive slower, compliance enforcement tightens, and post audit activity gets more aggressive. None of it references tariffs, but all of it started in the same two quarters.


**Why tariffs cause it:** Margin stress spreads through supply chains. S&P Global found that firms whose supply chain connections were outperforming were[10 percent less likely to see margin compression](https://www.spglobal.com/market-intelligence/en/news-insights/research/2025/10/margin-math) than firms whose connections were under stress. And the pressure rolls downhill unevenly: Grant Thornton notes that retail operates on low margins, so[big-box retailers use their bargaining power](https://www.grantthornton.com/insights/articles/tax/2025/new-tariff-paradigm-how-businesses-can-respond) to negotiate with suppliers or shift sourcing, while smaller players with weaker negotiating positions face unit margin erosion or volume declines. This is the reader’s mirror at scale: a PYMNTS Intelligence survey of 60 chief financial officers (CFOs) at US middle-market companies found[more than 4 in 10 goods-firm CFOs reported declining operating margins](https://www.pymnts.com/business/2026/tariffs-test-margins-while-companies-invest-to-protect-profitability-study-finds) in 2025, while only 12 percent saw improvement.


**Where to check:** Watch retailer and distributor behavior shifts as leading indicators, before they show up in your financials. The signals worth tracking are:


-


- Days sales outstanding creeping upward by retailer


[Compliance chargebacks](https://www.spscommerce.com/community/articles/understanding-retailer-deductions-chargebacks-and-fines) rising without a corresponding change in your fulfillment performance


Post audit claims arriving for periods that previously closed clean


Payment terms renegotiation requests clustering in the same quarter

Deduction recovery is the fastest dollar because the money was already earned: invoiced, shipped, then clawed back, often on disputable synchronization failures. Cost instrumentation is the most durable fix because it prevents the next 14-month blind spot. Pricing renegotiation is the slowest and largest lever, and it goes better when you walk in with the instrumented numbers from Signals 2 and 4 instead of an estimate.


## Frequently Asked Questions


### What are tariff mitigation strategies for retailers and suppliers?


Common tariff mitigation strategies for retailers and suppliers include diversifying sourcing, reclassifying products where legitimately supported, negotiating cost sharing, and repricing. This article deliberately stays one step earlier: detection. Every mitigation strategy works better when you know precisely where the erosion is, and mitigation decisions belong with your own trade counsel and finance leadership.


### Do I need a landed cost calculator with tariffs?


A[landed cost](https://www.spscommerce.com/community/articles/how-leading-cpg-brands-are-using-ai-to-get-ahead-of-tariff-volatility) calculator with tariffs built in helps you price new purchase orders accurately, and it is worth having. It will not surface erosion that has already happened, though: A calculator models the cost of the next order, while the 5 signals in this article audit what past orders actually did to your margin.
