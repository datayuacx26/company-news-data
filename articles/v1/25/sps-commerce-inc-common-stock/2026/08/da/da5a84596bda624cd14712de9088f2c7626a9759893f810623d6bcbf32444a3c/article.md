# How to Prepare for Your First Modular: A Timeline for New Suppliers

In this article, learn about:


-


How to manage a modular reset timeline


-


How to transition from being a product-focused vendor to a category subject matter expert


-


The specific sales and inventory data needed to build a defensible, growth-oriented pitch


---


You’ve put in the hours, perfected your product, and finally heard that long-awaited yes from a buyer at a major retailer. However, seasoned suppliers will tell you that the yes is just the beginning. The real work of staying on the shelf, growing your footprint, and defending your space against the competition happens during the modular reset cycle.


A[modular reset](https://www.spscommerce.com/community/articles/modular-resets-explained-what-new-suppliers-need-to-know-about-the-mod-cycle) is a visual tool used by retailers and suppliers to strategically arrange products to maximize space and attract customers. For a retailer like Walmart, which holds a massive share of the retail and grocery markets, these reviews are high-stakes moments that determine if your products stay on the shelf, gain more space, or are replaced entirely.


If you treat the review as a single meeting to prepare for, you’ve already missed the window. Success is found in the months of preparation that happen long before you step into the buyer’s office.


## The 40-Week Reality


The biggest mistake a new supplier can make is underestimating the lead time required for a reset. In the world of grocery retail, the process of resetting a category can take up to 40 weeks. It involves a multitude of steps, from initial presentations to finalizing item setups and receiving physical samples to ensure the shelf flow is correct.


To stay on track, you must build your timeline in reverse. If your live date is in June, you need to know when your item setup data must be finalized, when samples must be in the buyer's hands, and when the actual review conversation happens relative to those deadlines. This sequential approach is a checklist that every first-time supplier needs in order to avoid missing their window entirely.

## Becoming a Subject Matter Expert


In a line review, you are an extension of the retailer’s merchant team. Retail buyers are incredibly busy, often managing thousands of[stock keeping units (SKUs)](https://www.spscommerce.com/community/articles/what-is-a-stock-keeping-unit-sku) across multiple categories. They rely on you to be the subject matter expert (SME) for your specific products and the category at large.


To be a successful SME, you have to balance your own company’s goals with the specific objectives of the buyer. You need to understand if the retailer views your product as a unit driver — a high-volume item that boosts category sales — or a profit driver, which might be a more niche item with a higher margin.


Coming to the table with a clear point of view is essential. Buyers aren't just looking for a list of your product's features. They want to know how your brand helps them win the category. This means staying at the forefront of consumer trends, knowing your competitors' strengths and weaknesses, and anticipating potential supply chain disruptions that could impact the retailer’s bottom line.


## The Data You Actually Need


While a great product matters, the line review is the time to validate your value with hard numbers. Buyers will typically expect you to share several key data points:


-


**Total category size and share** asks the question, **w** hat is the market share of your company compared to the total category?


-


**Performance metrics** ask how your product has performed since the last review in terms of sales and fulfillment.


-


**Pricing structure** ensures that **** you are ready to discuss tiered pricing that shows your cost and the retail price as you scale.


-


**Future innovation** means buyers want to see a pipeline of new ideas, such as flavor extensions or new pack sizes, to ensure your brand remains relevant as current product lifecycles end.


Don’t just present a basic list of stats. Use your data to tell a story. Instead of simply listing where your products are sold, show a map that highlights where you have a strong customer base and where there are opportunity gaps in your current distribution. This approach provides the buyer with the narrative they need to justify your growth to their own leadership.


## Case Study: Image One


The power of data-backed preparation is best illustrated by[Image One](https://www.spscommerce.com/resources/image-one/) , a family-owned apparel company based in Arkansas. Image One operates in sports-licensed and resort apparel, which is a highly seasonal and unpredictable market. Their success depended on quick turnarounds and being able to react to sleeper teams that suddenly break out and drive massive demand.


For years, Image One was a lower-priority vendor when it came to the allocation of open-to-buy dollars with major sporting goods retailers. To win more floor space, they realized they had to move beyond intuition and start speaking the retailer's language.


By layering different retailers' data to identify overall trends and asking the buyer exactly which metrics mattered most, Image One began providing weekly buying plan recommendations backed entirely by the retailer’s own data. Because the recommendations were verifiably accurate, the buyer developed a level of trust that transformed the relationship. Within the first year of this data-driven approach, Image One grew their business with that key account by 51%.


## Operational Readiness: The Explicit Checklist


Winning the shelf space doesn't mean much if you aren't operationally ready to ship the product. Your timeline must include an explicit checklist for item setup and packaging readiness.


This includes ensuring your[GS1 standards](https://www.spscommerce.com/community/articles/gs1-standards-for-new-suppliers-gtins-upcs-and-case-codes) are in order, specifically your Global Trade Item Numbers (GTINs) and Universal Product Codes (UPCs). You must also adhere to the retailer’s specific[packaging standards](https://www.spscommerce.com/community/articles/retail-ready-packaging-primary-secondary-and-tertiary-requirements-explained) , which cover everything from the primary container to the tertiary shipping[pallets](https://www.spscommerce.com/community/articles/pallet-building-101-ti-hi-stacking-and-why-retailers-care) . For instance, failure to comply with[labeling](https://www.spscommerce.com/community/articles/labeling-for-new-suppliers-gs1-128-sscc-and-where-labels-have-to-go) requirements can result in costly chargebacks and deductions that eat into your margins before your first order even ships.


## Common Mistakes to Avoid


Even the most well-intentioned suppliers can stumble if they lose focus during the review process. Avoid these common pitfalls:


-


**Over-focusing on the backstory:** Buyers are familiar with the category. Emphasize sales potential and competitive advantages, not the history of how the product was created.


-


**Not knowing your numbers:** You must be fluent in your costs, margins, and supply chain details. Any hesitation here can erode the buyer's confidence in your partnership.


-


**Asking for too much too soon:** It is often better to start with a realistic, phased rollout plan and expand based on proven performance rather than over-extending and failing to meet demand.


-


**Ignoring compliance costs:** When setting your pricing, you must factor in allowances, potential deductions, and the costs of remaining compliant with the retailer's routing guides.
