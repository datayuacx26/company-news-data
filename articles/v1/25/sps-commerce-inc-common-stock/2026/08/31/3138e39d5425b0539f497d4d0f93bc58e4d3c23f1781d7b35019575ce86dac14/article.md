# Reading a Planogram: What Buyers Send You and What They Expect Back

In this article, learn about:


-


How to reframe the planogram from a simple picture to a detailed specification document


-


The critical item data and imagery suppliers must provide to buyers to ensure a successful reset


-


The operational consequences of inaccurate dimensions and the role of modern compliance tools


---


For many emerging suppliers, there is a specific moment of tension that occurs right after a retailer says yes. The initial excitement of landing the account is often followed by the arrival of the complex, data-heavy planogram.


If you are seeing this document for the first time, it is easy to treat it as simply a nice visual representation of where your product sits on a shelf. However, treating a planogram as a one-way document is a mistake that can lead to misplaced products, broken category flow, and lost sales. To a retailer, a planogram is a blueprint for merchandising strategy and a formal specification document.


Understanding this document is important, but the relationship is a two-way street. Buyers expect you to send back accurate item dimensions, high-quality images, and specific data points to make the reset work. If you treat this exchange as optional you risk your product ending up with the wrong shelf space or facing count before it even hits the store.


## Decoding the Specification Document


A[planogram, or POG](https://www.spscommerce.com/community/articles/what-is-a-planogram-and-how-is-it-used) , is a schematic visual tool used to plan and control shelf layouts. It is built from sales data, shopper behavior research, and merchandising principles to ensure that products are arranged in a way to maximize space and profitability. When reviewing the planogram, it is important to look past the product images and focus on the technical instructions it contains.


A complete planogram typically includes several key views that tell store teams exactly what "done" looks like.


### Fixture View


The fixture view is the foundation of the planogram, detailing the specific equipment required, such as gondolas, pegboards, coolers, or racks. This view specifies shelf heights, depths, and the exact spacing between fixtures to ensure everything physically fits.


### Product View


The product view layers in the specific[stock keeping unit (SKU)](https://www.spscommerce.com/community/articles/what-is-a-stock-keeping-unit-sku) and universal product code (UPC) data for identification. This is where you find the following:


-


**Number of facings (NoF):** Tells you how many units of a product are visible from the front


-


**Number of units (NoU):** Indicates the total quantity allocated to the shelf


### SKU Listing


Finally, the document includes a SKU listing that serves as a checklist for merchandisers. This list includes[barcodes](https://www.spscommerce.com/community/articles/gs1-standards-for-new-suppliers-gtins-upcs-and-case-codes) , product descriptions, and units of measure to ensure the right variants are stocked.


Any gap in this specification creates a decision point for a field team, and improvisation can lead to compliance errors.


## How To Read a Planogram


Reading a planogram effectively means turning a digital schematic into a shelf that actually sells. Your goal is to ensure that the physical reality of the store matches the digital intent of the buyer. Some step-by-step best practices are:


1.


**Start by identifying the fixture and shelf layout** . You must confirm that the fixture type in the planogram matches the physical unit in the store. Take note of the number of shelves and their heights before any stock is placed.


1.


Once the fixture is verified, **move to SKU positioning** . The SKU and/or UPC from the planogram must match the actual product barcode on the carton. This prevents errors, such as stocking a 1-liter pack in a slot designated for a 500ml version.


1.


You should also **count the facings** on the shelf and compare them to the assigned NoF to ensure your brand blocking and visibility are maintained.


Good planograms also guide category flow, showing how brands and subcategories sit next to each other to help the shopper navigate the aisle. You should verify that your products are grouped according to these brand blocks and that any specified adjacencies, like chips next to dips, are respected. If the planogram includes promotional or seasonal callouts, these must be treated as time-sensitive priorities, with[end caps](https://www.spscommerce.com/community/articles/what-is-an-end-cap) or dump bins set up exactly as indicated.

## The Data Exchange Between You and Your Buyer


While the buyer provides the layout, the accuracy of that layout depends entirely on the data you provide during the item setup process. Retailers increasingly build resets from automated item master data feeds rather than manual entry.


The most critical element you owe to the buyer is accurate dimensions. This includes the height, width, and depth of the product in its final packaging. These measurements must match the actual production sample, not an estimate. If you submit inaccurate dimensions, it can throw off the automated space allocation for the entire reset. This might result in your product being given too much space, which wastes the retailer's money, or too little space, which leads to your product being left off the shelf entirely because it physically does not fit.


Beyond dimensions, buyers expect high-quality product images. These images are not just for aesthetics; they allow store teams to map the correct items to the correct slots visually. Best practices for these images include using a clear white background, cropping out all borders, and ensuring the product is upright and front-facing. When working with systems like DotActiv, these images typically need to be in PNG or JPG format and named by their barcode to ensure they link correctly within the software.


Major retailers like[Target](https://www.spscommerce.com/community/articles/targets-item-setup-a-step-by-step-guide) have a highly structured process for this data submission.


-


Once you are selected as a vendor at Target, you have a 48-hour window to begin item setup in LaunchPad.


-


You are responsible for completing an Item Spec Sheet, which determines which data fields you own and edit versus those the retailer manages.


-


This data is then validated by an Item Data Specialist (IDS) to ensure everything is ready for the shelf.

## The High Cost of Inaccuracy


Retail data is the lifeblood of a successful planogram. If your unit data is incorrect, it skews critical indicators like[days of supply (DOS)](https://www.spscommerce.com/community/articles/inventory-and-merchandising-metrics-at-major-retailers) and weekly movement.


When DOS is miscalculated, a retailer cannot accurately balance the number of facings your product requires. Your best-selling SKU might fall behind because it wasn't allocated enough space, leading to stockouts and lost revenue. Conversely, inaccurate[seasonal](https://www.spscommerce.com/community/articles/seasonality-and-gifting-cycles-in-health-beauty-and-wellness) data could result in a shortage of products like sunscreen during its prime summer season.


Inaccurate data also grinds the in-store execution process to a halt. If a field team arrives to find that the products do not fit the shelves as planned, they are forced to improvise. This often leads to reset drift, where daily restocking decisions accumulate until the shelf looks nothing like the original strategy. This unauthorized localization breaks the category logic, meaning no two stores share a consistent baseline for performance analysis.

## Closing the Compliance Loop


Planogram compliance means the actual store shelf matches the approved plan. Historically, many teams have overestimated their compliance because they only measured presence rather than accuracy.


Today, the industry is moving toward more sophisticated verification methods. While manual checks by store staff are still common, many retailers now use photo proofing or mobile-first digital checklists to verify placement. Some are even deploying AI and image recognition tools that compare a shelf photo against the approved planogram in real-time. These systems can detect missing facings or the wrong product in a slot quickly, allowing a rep to fix the issue before they even leave the aisle.


## Building a Stronger Partnership


Ultimately, the planogram is a tool for collaboration. While the retailer makes the final decisions, you have the opportunity to influence those decisions by providing clean data and demonstrating strong performance. In some cases, suppliers act as category leaders, providing insights on consumer behavior that help the buyer refine the category strategy.


If you find that your actual[packaging](https://www.spscommerce.com/community/articles/retail-ready-packaging-primary-secondary-and-tertiary-requirements-explained) does not match what is reflected in a planogram, this is a signal to flag the discrepancy back to your buyer or category manager. Asking for clarification on a specific instruction is a normal part of the process and shows that you are committed to the success of the[reset](https://www.spscommerce.com/community/articles/modular-resets-explained-what-new-suppliers-need-to-know-about-the-mod-cycle) .


By mastering the art of reading the planogram and being disciplined about the data you send back, you move from being a first-time supplier to a sophisticated retail partner. You ensure your products are positioned to win, your inventory is managed effectively, and your relationship with the buyer remains healthy.
