# The 2026 World Cup Effect on Norway's Retailers and Suppliers

It is no secret that Norway’s glorious return to the World Cup after 28 years left a lasting impression on both local and international audiences.


After the team went on to beat Brazil, reaching the quarterfinals for the first time in its history, the celebration went beyond just the football pitch.


Retailers and suppliers across Norway quite literally saw consumer demands shift overnight, in ways very few could have anticipated.


TV screens sold out. Grocery shelves turned red, white, and blue. A small club on the southwest coast found itself at the center of a global spotlight. And, for some retailers, the excitement came with a costly lesson in supply chain readiness.


Here's how Norway's World Cup run turned retail and supply in the country on its head, and what it revealed about the gap between consumer excitement and supply chain readiness.


## TV and Projector Sales Went up Before the Brazil Match


In the days leading up to Norway's match against Brazil, Norwegian shoppers began to look for larger displays and sharper picture quality, chasing that stadium-like experience for their own living rooms.


As a result, local electronics retailers saw[demand for home entertainment setups skyrocket](https://kommunikasjon.ntb.no/pressemelding/18983213/norge-feberen-slar-alle-rekorder-tv-salget-skjot-i-vaeret-for-brasil-kampen-projektorsalget-eksploderer-med-563-prosent?publisherId=17848377&lang=no) . According to Ardian Gashi, Elkjøp's sales manager for audio and video, TV sales roughly doubled compared to the same period the year before (with TVs over 75 inches being most favored).


Projectors saw an even sharper jump. As Gashi claimed “Sales have increased by as much as 563 percent.”


## World Cup-Themed Products Hit the Market


Electronics weren't the only category riding the wave. As Norway's World Cup run picked up steam, food brands across the country rushed to put the[national team imagery on their packaging](https://www.nrk.no/trondelag/na-skal-_alle_-selge-vm-produkter-for-fotball-vm-2026-1.17889392) .


Chocolate bars carried "Alt for Norge" branding. Bread came in football-shaped variants. Even an ice cream flavor from the 1990s made a comeback under its old World Cup-era name.


One of the companies that joined the football branding trend was Baxt, the maker of Vestlandslefse (a traditional Norwegian flatbread), which teamed up with the Norwegian Football Federation to release a football-themed version of the product.


Mølbach, managing director of Baxt, claimed this phenomenon wasn't only a marketing opportunity. It reflected something far deeper about what the World Cup meant to Norwegians. As he put it, "the sense of community and patriotism is strong" this year, a sentiment that helps explain why so many brands leaned into the moment, and why Norwegian consumers responded to it.


For a country that hadn't celebrated a World Cup appearance in almost three decades, this was a significant national moment, and one with real, measurable demand behind it.


## The "Haaland Effect"


No storyline captured the scale of the moment better than Erling Haaland's. As the famed striker scored his way through the tournament, most notably with two goals against Brazil,[many heads turned to his hometown](https://www.nrk.no/rogaland/fotball-vm-2026_-sa-mye-er-haaland-effekten-verdt-for-bryne-1.17948830) - Bryne (and especially to his football club - Bryne FK).


Joachim Baardsen, who runs the sports communications agency Baardsen Sport, even claimed “the World Cup is worth billions over time”, and that much of that value is likely to spill over onto both Bryne and Norway in general.


Baardsen called it the biggest commercial opportunity in Bryne FK’s history, one with the potential to build a brand that lasts long after this World Cup ends.


As Haaland's global profile continues to grow, so does the appeal of Bryne as a destination, and the commercial upside for the region and the businesses connected to it.


## The Norway Jersey Supply Crisis


Not every part of Norway's World Cup retail story was a success. As national pride translated into a sudden surge in demand for official Norway football shirts,[several retailers found themselves unable to keep up](https://www.nettavisen.no/sport/norges-landslagsdrakt-utsolgt-for-vm-frank-loke-og-xxl-slar-alarm/s/5-95-3090450) .


At XXL, one of Norway's largest sports retailers, demand for the Norway national soccer team jerseys became so high that the supply chain failed to handle it. Jan Christian Thommesen, XXL's communications director, pointed to the root cause: the supplier hadn't been able to produce enough shirts in time, leaving retailers with far fewer units than customers wanted.


Frank Løke, a former Norwegian national handball player who was among those unable to get a shirt, summed up the missed opportunity bluntly: "it's a crisis" that comes with real lost revenue potential for everyone involved, not just an inconvenience for fans.


His comment captured what many in the industry were thinking: a historic moment for Norwegian football had run headlong into a supply chain that couldn't respond fast enough to meet it.


## The Future of Norwegian Retailers and Suppliers


Norway's World Cup run showed how quickly customer expectations can shift when a nation rallies around a shared moment, and how unevenly retailers and suppliers can respond to it.


Elkjøp and Baxt moved fast because they had the flexibility to react to what customers wanted, when they wanted it. XXL's experience with jerseys tells a different story: demand existed, but the connection between retailer and supplier couldn't keep pace with it.


That gap rarely comes down to a lack of effort. More often, it's a[visibility problem](https://www.spscommerce.com/community/articles/costs-inefficient-vendor-onboarding-spsc/) . When a retailer and its supplier don't have shared, timely visibility into orders, inventory, raw materials, and production capacity, a sudden spike in demand becomes a missed opportunity rather than a sales record.


A unified system[connecting retailers and suppliers](https://www.spscommerce.com/community/articles/4-supplier-collaboration-strategies) helps close that gap. It gives both sides a shared, real-time picture of what's selling, what's in stock, and what's coming next, so a surge in demand can be met with a plan and not with panic.


Norway's next major moment, whether on the pitch or in another unexpected wave of consumer enthusiasm, no doubt, will test the same readiness.


And with increased global attention to Norway, thanks to the national team’s viral appearance on the pitch, the need for end to end supply chain visibility will likely only grow further.
