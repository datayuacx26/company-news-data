# Glossary of Common Health, Beauty, and Wellness Terms

Health, beauty, and wellness (HBW) products carry a vocabulary all their own. This glossary breaks down the most common HBW terms suppliers encounter, from FDA compliance to EDI to shelf placement.


**Term**


**Definition**


**1,4-Dioxane**


A trace contaminant that can form during manufacturing of ethoxylated ingredients like sulfates and is classified as a likely carcinogen. States such as New York[cap allowable parts-per-million](https://dec.ny.gov/environmental-protection/pollution-prevention/household-personal-cosmetic-dioxane-limits) , so brands often need lab results proving they fall under the threshold before a retailer will list the product.


**3-Way Match**


A retailer's automated check that lines up a supplier's invoice against the purchase order and the actual quantities received at the warehouse.


**Adjacencies**


The products placed immediately next to yours on the shelf. For a clean or prestige beauty brand, sitting next to a mass commodity item versus a clinical brand can change how shoppers perceive the product.


**Adverse Event Reporting**


The FDA-mandated process for notifying regulators about health complaints tied to a product, such as rashes or burns. Serious adverse events must be reported within[15 business days](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/investigational-new-drug-ind-application/ind-application-reporting-ind-safety-reports) , which means HBW suppliers need a reliable system for collecting and vetting customer complaints.


**Certificate of Analysis (CoA)**


A lab document verifying that a product batch meets its specifications, including potency, purity, and contaminant limits like 1,4-dioxane. Retailers and marketplaces increasingly request a CoA before onboarding a brand.


**Cosmetics Direct**


The[FDA portal](https://www.fda.gov/cosmetics/registration-listing-cosmetic-product-facilities-and-products/cosmetics-direct) where facilities register and marketed cosmetic products, including full ingredient lists, get listed under


Modernization of Cosmetics Regulation Act of 2022 (MoCRA). It's where the Responsible Person's registration and listing obligations get fulfilled.


**Cosmetic Facility Registration**


The MoCRA requirement that any facility manufacturing or processing cosmetics register with the FDA. Many indie HBW brands rely on co-packers, and registration gaps at the co-packer level can still create liability that follows the brand name on the label.


**Drug Facts Panel**


The standardized, non-discretionary label format required for over-the-counter drugs. Many beauty products, including sunscreens, acne treatments, and anti-dandruff shampoos, are legally drugs, and any therapeutic claim requires this rigid seven-section panel instead of a discretionary cosmetic layout.


**Direct-t-Consumer (DTC)**


A model where a brand sells straight to the end customer, usually online, without going through a retailer. Many HBW brands launch here, but scaling into retail requires an entirely different set of logistics and data capabilities.


**Fair Packaging and Labeling Act (FPLA)**


The federal law governing what a cosmetic label must include, covering identity, net contents, and manufacturer information. It's the legal backbone behind cosmetic labeling requirements, separate from the statute that governs OTC drug labels.


**First-Expired-First-Out (FEFO)**


An inventory rotation method that ships or sells the stock with the nearest expiration date first, rather than the stock received first.


**Gift Set (Value Set)**


A retail item built from multiple components and packaged as its own SKU, distinct from bundling a few existing products onto a shelf. A gift set requires its own item setup, packaging, and compliance work.


**Gifting Cycle (Gifting-Driven Demand)**


A short, date-locked demand window, such as Valentine's Day, Mother's Day, or the Q4 holidays, that pulls a large share of a brand's annual volume into two to four weeks.


**INCI Name**


The standardized international name used to identify a cosmetic ingredient on the label. Retailer "dirty lists" and state regulations are enforced against[INCI names](https://www.personalcarecouncil.org/resources/inci/) .


**Item Setup**


The process of entering a new SKU's data, packaging details, and compliance requirements into a retailer's system.


**Kitting**


Assembling multiple SKUs into a single sellable unit, such as a gift set or holiday bundle. When a kit combines items with different expiration dates, it can complicate both lot tracking and labeling.


**Line Review**


A retailer's periodic, often annual, process for deciding which brands and SKUs stay, grow, or get cut from a category. It's theevent that makes sell-through data matter in the first place.


**Lot / Batch Number**


A unique code identifying a specific production run. Accurate lot tracking lets a brand isolate and recall only the affected units during a contamination issue, such as the[2023 asbestos-in-talc concerns](https://www.fda.gov/cosmetics/cosmetics-news-events/fda-releases-data-agencys-2023-testing-talc-containing-cosmetic-products-asbestos) , rather than pulling an entire catalog.


**Minimum Advertised Price (MAP)**


The floor price a brand contractually requires sellers to advertise at. DTC-native brands used to controlling their own pricing often get their first MAP violation notice from a marketplace reseller rather than a big-box retailer.


**Minimum Remaining Shelf Life**


A retailer requirement that a shipment arrive with a set percentage of its shelf life still remaining, often cited around 75%. A shipment can be rejected at the dock for arriving too close to its expiration date even if nothing is technically wrong with it.


**Omnichannel Fulfillment**


A strategy that coordinates inventory across every sales channel, including DTC, marketplaces, and brick-and-mortar. HBW brands need a fulfillment partner that can deliver a branded unboxing experience for DTC orders while still meeting strict retail-ready standards for a Target or Ulta warehouse.


**OTC Monograph / Monograph Ingredient**


An active ingredient the FDA has pre-approved for a specific over-the-counter use, such as pyrithione zinc in anti-dandruff shampoo, without requiring individual drug approval.


**Period After Opening (PAO)**


The open-jar symbol on cosmetic packaging showing how many months a product stays safe and effective once opened. It's a voluntary label element rather than a federal mandate, but many retailers expect it, and the number behind it should be backed by preservative efficacy testing.


**Planogram**


A visual diagram showing exactly where a product sits on a shelf, including how many facings are visible to the shopper. The beauty aisle is one of the most SKU-dense sections in the store, so keeping a spot on the planogram through bi-annual resets depends on data-backed proof of velocity and sell-through.


**Poison Prevention Packaging Act (PPPA)**


The federal law requiring child-resistant packaging, or a specific warning label when a product isn't packaged that way, for certain OTC and cosmetic products.


**Precision Logistics**


A supply chain approach focused on tight accuracy in temperature, lot numbers, and expiration management.


**Preservative Efficacy Testing / Challenge Test**


Testing that confirms a formula resists microbial growth over its intended shelf life. It's the technical evidence behind both safety substantiation and period after opening (PAO) claims.


**Proposition 65 Warning**


California's required warning label for products containingchemicals that may contribute to an individual's overall lifetime risk of cancer, risk of birth defects, or other reproductive harm above a listed threshold. Once a brand sells into California, the Proposition 65 warning becomes a near-universal labelingrequirement.


**Responsible Person**


The manufacturer, packer, or distributor named on a cosmetic product's label. Under[MoCRA](https://www.fda.gov/cosmetics/cosmetics-laws-regulations/modernization-cosmetics-regulation-act-2022-mocra) , this party is legally liable for safety substantiation, adverse event reporting, and ingredient recordkeeping.


**Safety Substantiation**


The legal requirement to have data supporting a "reasonable certainty" that a cosmetic product is safe. This is now a mandate rather than a best practice, and brands that can'tproduce records risk having the FDA deem the product adulterated and force a recall.


**Seasonal Product Cycle**


A demand pattern tied to a season rather than a fixed date, such as sun care building through spring and summer or immunity products building through fall and winter.


**Sell-Through Rate**


The percentage of purchased inventory that has actually sold to consumers. It's the number retailers weigh most heavily during a line review, and a low rate puts a brand at risk of[SKU rationalization](https://www.spscommerce.com/community/articles/sku-rationalization) , meaning removal from the shelf to make room for a faster-moving product.


**Shelf-Ready Packaging (SRP)**


Packaging designed so store staff can place an entire case on the shelf in one move without unpacking individual units. Moving from decorative packaging to store-centric SRP is often a prerequisite for scaling an emerging brand into national chains.


**Shelf Reset**


The periodic event where a retailer physically rebuilds a category's shelf to match a new planogram, deciding which products keep their space, lose facings, or drop out of the assortment.


**Small Business Exemption (MoCRA)**


A carve-out that reduces MoCRA's registration and reporting burden for cosmetic manufacturers under a certain revenue threshold. Emerging HBW brands often assume MoCRA doesn'tapply to them and are caught off guard when it does.


**Stability Testing (Accelerated vs. Real-Time)**


Studies measuring how a product's identity, strength, and safety hold up over time under different conditions. The results establish expiration dates and, for OTC drugs, must be kept on file; for cosmetics, they support the mandatory safety substantiation record.


**Structure-Function Claim**


A claim that a product changes how the body works, such as "boosts collagen production" or "treats dandruff." In the U.S., these claims frequently push a product from cosmetic into drug territory, triggering stricter testing, facility registration, and labeling rules.


**Tamper-Evident Packaging**


Packaging that shows visible evidence if a product has been opened, such as shrink bands, seals, or breakable caps. It'smandatory for most OTC drugs and select cosmetics like liquid oral hygiene products, and failing to call out the tamper-evident feature on the label can get a product deemed misbranded.
