## What Tesco Is Doing


On[21 July 2026](https://www.tescoplc.com/tesco-expands-rapid-delivery-reach-through-new-uber-eats-and-deliveroo-partnerships/) , Tesco confirmed it will launch on Uber Eats in August, with Deliveroo following later in the summer. The foundation for this is Whoosh, Tesco's own rapid delivery service, which has been running from around 1,800 stores since 2021.

For Tesco, it means identifying who is buying what across channels that previously sat outside their data view. That is the part of this announcement that suppliers should pay close attention to.


## The Store-Pick Model: Why Suppliers Should Care


Tesco fulfils Whoosh orders from real store shelves, and the Uber Eats model will work exactly the same way. No dedicated picking facilities. Orders come in, pickers walk the aisles of a local Tesco, and the delivery goes out within 20 minutes.


The practical consequence is that rapid delivery and the standard weekly shop draw from the same stock. A gap in your on-shelf availability hits both channels at the same time. A product missing from the shelf on a Wednesday evening is missing whether the customer walked through the front door or ordered from their sofa. For UK grocery suppliers,[these pressures are already reshaping how Tesco and other major retailers manage their supplier relationships](https://www.spscommerce.com/community/articles/grocery-supply-chain-issues-3-pressures-reshaping-2026) .


Whoosh reached £400 million last year across 1,800 stores. Adding Uber Eats across more than 90% of the UK puts significantly more demand through those same shelves. Replenishment cadences that held up for planned grocery orders may need to move faster to keep pace with what is coming.


## How the Clubcard Tie-In Changes the Data Picture


Until now, Tesco customers who shopped through Uber Eats were largely anonymous to Tesco's loyalty system. Transactions happened on Uber's platform, and the data stayed there.


That changes in August. Customers who link their Clubcard to Uber Eats will generate loyalty data that feeds back into Tesco's own systems. Here is what that means at supplier level:


1.


**Substitution patterns become traceable.** When a picker cannot find your product and swaps it for a competitor's, Tesco has a record tied to a Clubcard member, a specific store, a time of day, and a category.


2.


**Out-of-stocks stop being invisible.** Gap data that previously sat on Uber's platform will now flow back into Tesco's view.


3.


**Range conversations get sharper.** Tesco's commercial teams already use Clubcard data to drive ranging decisions. Rapid delivery adds another layer of channel-specific performance to those discussions.


The data flows back to Tesco's systems whether a supplier is tracking it or not.


## Shelf Availability at 20 Minutes


A standard home delivery typically arrives an hour or two after the order, giving the customer time to notice a substitution when the bags come through the door. Uber Eats orders are different. A picker marks your product unavailable within minutes of the order being placed, and the customer sees the outcome before the delivery has even left the store.


Tesco's Whoosh operation already records this at order level. As Uber Eats volumes grow across more stores and more trading hours, that data becomes more consistent. Substitution patterns that were once too thin to raise in a trading review will gain enough weight to appear regularly.[OTIF and on-shelf availability](https://www.spscommerce.com/community/articles/improving-otif-performance) are already central to how Tesco evaluates its suppliers. Rapid delivery adds another dimension to both.


**The**[UK quick commerce grocery market is worth around £2.4 billion](https://www.globalbankingandfinance.com/uks-tesco-boosts-rapid-delivery-uber-eats-deliveroo-deals/) **and growing.** Suppliers who address availability gaps now will benefit from the channel's growth. Leaving those gaps open means they will surface with increasing regularity in commercial conversations with your buyer.

###### **Store-level stock visibility.**
