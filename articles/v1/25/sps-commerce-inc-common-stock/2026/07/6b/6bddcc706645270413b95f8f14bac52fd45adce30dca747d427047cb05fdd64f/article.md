# Retail-Ready Packaging: Primary, Secondary, and Tertiary Requirements Explained

In this article, learn about:


-


How primary, secondary, and tertiary packaging function as three distinct compliance surfaces


-


The "5 Easies" framework retailers, such as Walmart, use to evaluate retail-ready and shelf-ready packaging


-


Why physical packaging decisions can cause downstream EDI and ASN accuracy errors


---


When a brand builds a strong direct-to-consumer (DTC) business, expanding into major retail often feels like the natural next step. You’ve mastered the art of the unboxing experience, and your numbers are climbing, so you assume the jump to big-box shelves is just a matter of scale. But many emerging suppliers quickly hit a frustrating operational wall the moment their first major[purchase order (PO)](https://www.spscommerce.com/community/articles/what-is-a-purchase-order) arrives.


Suddenly, the conversation shifts from brand aesthetics to first-time quality (FTQ). The retailer is buying more than just your product. They are buying your ability to move that product through their highly automated distribution system without manual intervention. Decisions you made months ago about case pack sizes, barcode placement, and bottle shapes now become high-stakes compliance hurdles.


Most first-time suppliers think about packaging as a single branding decision. In reality,[retail-ready packaging (RRP)](https://www.spscommerce.com/community/articles/what-is-retail-ready-packaging) is a three-layer compliance system where each layer has its own function, its own point of failure, and its own scorecard consequence. If you want to survive your first shipment to Walmart or Target, you have to stop thinking about the box and start thinking about primary, secondary, and tertiary packaging as three distinct compliance surfaces.


## Primary Packaging: Protection Meets Regulation


Primary packaging is the layer that touches the product itself. For a shopper, this is the bottle of shampoo or the box of pasta they purchase. For a supplier, this is where brand flair meets strict federal and retailer-specific regulations.


The primary goal here is containment and information. However, suppliers in the[health, beauty, and wellness](https://www.spscommerce.com/community/articles/the-health-beauty-and-wellness-category-a-brands-guide-to-selling-and-scaling-in-retail) spaces often fall into the trap of treating primary packaging compliance as a single requirement. But compliance at this level is often triggered by the product’s ingredients and classification, running across two separate federal agencies. The[FDA governs tamper-evident packaging,](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/cpg-sec-450500-tamper-resistant-packaging-requirements-certain-over-counter-human-drug-products) while the[Consumer Product Safety Commission (CPSC) governs child-resistance](https://www.cpsc.gov/Regulations-Laws--Standards/Voluntary-Standards/Child-Resistant-CR-Packaging) .


If your primary packaging fails these safety standards, or if you fail to provide the protocol test documentation proving their success, your exposure is two-sided: safety-regulation penalties on one side and retailer chargebacks on the other.


Beyond safety, there is the functional side of design. While a perfume bottle shaped like a rose is beautiful, if it wastes carton space or requires excessive dunnage, it might drive up freight costs and will likely trigger[weak packaging](https://www.spscommerce.com/community/articles/packaging-compliance-weak-packaging-defect) defects in programs like Walmart's[Supplier Quality Excellence Program (SQEP)](https://www.spscommerce.com/community/articles/supplier-guided-checklist-walmart-sqep) .

## Secondary Packaging & The "5 Easies"


[Secondary packaging](https://www.spscommerce.com/community/articles/secondary-packaging-requirements-for-suppliers) is the invisible middleman of the supply chain. It groups multiple primary packages into cases, trays, or shrink-wrapped bundles that can be handled as a single unit. While a shopper may never notice it, this layer is central to how products move from the truck to the shelf.


This is where RRP and shelf-ready packaging (SRP) live. Retailers are increasingly demanding RRP because it shaves seconds off the labor required to restock shelves. Labor is typically the largest expenditure for retailers, accounting for a decent amount of total revenue.


To evaluate whether your secondary packaging is truly retail-ready, most retailers, led by Walmart, use the[5 Easies](https://www.spscommerce.com/community/articles/what-are-the-5-easies-of-retail-ready-packaging) framework:


1.


**Easy to identify** means that product details and barcodes must be visible from multiple angles.


1.


**Easy to open** means perforations should tear cleanly without knives, which improves safety and saves time.


1.


**Easy to stock** means associates should be able to move the entire tray onto the shelf in one motion.


1.


**Easy to shop** means consumers should be able to see and grab the product without digging through the case.


1.


**Easy to dispose of** means the packaging must be recyclable or easy to break down, reducing waste-handling costs.


Failure at this layer is rarely about the product itself. If your[advance ship notice (ASN)](https://www.spscommerce.com/community/articles/edi-856-ship-notice-manifest) indicates ten units in a box, but you ship five inner packs of two units each without an outer barcode that reflects the total count, you are creating a mislabeling issue that triggers automated receive failures.you are creating a mislabeling issue that triggers automated receive failures.

## Tertiary Packaging: The Bulk Handling Surface


Tertiary packaging refers to the outermost protective layer: the pallet, the corner boards, and the stretch wrap. This is the layer that is designed for transport durability. For many new suppliers, this feels like shipping, not compliance, but it is one of the most common places to get hit with an unexpected fine.


Retailers have rigid standards for pallet configurations, often referred to as[TI/HI](https://www.spscommerce.com/community/articles/pallet-building-101-ti-hi-stacking-and-why-retailers-care) (number of cases per layer x number of layers high). A pallet that is stacked too high or built with an unstable pattern can collapse in a distribution center (DC), leading to load stability defects and potential rejections.


Common tertiary compliance errors include:


-


**Overhang:** This is when cartons extend past the edge of the pallet, reducing stability and increasing the risk of damage.


-


**Poor securement:** This error occurs when using colored stretch wrap or having loose tails of plastic that can snag in automated sorting systems.


-


**Inadequate strength:** Cartons must meet minimum Mullen burst strength or edge crush test (ECT) requirements to survive the weight of the pallet stack.


At this level, physical packaging directly impacts your data accuracy. If your pallet build doesn't match the dimensions in your retailer system, the warehouse management system (WMS) will reject the load because the items won't fit the assigned storage slot.

## The True Cost of a Packaging Mismatch


Decisions made at any of these three layers cascade through the supply chain. Consider an industry-documented pattern where a supplier of beauty products accidentally places a secondary packaging label on the wrong SKU.


This single error can trigger a chain reaction. First, the shipment is flagged as a unit mismatch at the DC, causing it to be set aside for manual problem resolution. This creates a delay that can ruin a supplier’s[On Time In Full (OTIF)](https://www.spscommerce.com/community/articles/walmart-otif-fundamentals) score. Meanwhile, the electronic ASN is now inaccurate, triggering a chargeback that can start at $200 per order plus additional per-case fees.


By the time the product actually reaches the store, the brand has already lost its profit margin to administrative fines. Prevention is almost always cheaper than fixing a problem after the shipment leaves the dock. This is why successful suppliers moving from DTC to major retailers involve using packaging engineers early in the process and test their designs against retailer standards before mass production begins.

## Bridging the Gap Between Physical and Digital


It is important to remember that[packaging accuracy](https://www.spscommerce.com/community/articles/different-types-of-packaging-in-retail) and data accuracy are two sides of the same coin. Even if you have the perfect shelf-ready tray, it is useless if the data about that tray — the dimensions, weights, and[GTINs](https://www.spscommerce.com/community/articles/what-are-walmarts-gtin-standards) — isn't transmitted correctly via[electronic data interchange (EDI)](https://www.spscommerce.com/community/articles/edi-for-first-time-suppliers-a-plain-english-guide-to-the-documents-youll-send) .


Programs like Walmart’s SQEP now phase in PO accuracy, barcode labeling, and packaging compliance as separate scored categories. Getting your physical packaging right is the foundation, but your operational infrastructure must ensure that the digital “license plate” on that carton matches your EDI 856/ASN exactly.


The shift from DTC to retail is a discipline shift. Brands that design for all three layers of packaging from the start avoid the costly cycle of chargebacks and rejections that plague those who treat packaging as a late-stage branding decision.
