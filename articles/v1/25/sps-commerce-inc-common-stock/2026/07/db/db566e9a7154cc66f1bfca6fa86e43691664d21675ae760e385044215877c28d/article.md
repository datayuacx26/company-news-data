# UPC, GTIN, and Style Number Basics for Apparel and Footwear

In this article, learn about:


- How style numbers, SKUs, GTINs, and UPCs each support apparel operations.


- Why every size and color variation needs its own unique product identifier.


- How accurate product data helps prevent retail, marketplace, and fulfillment errors.


⁠As an apparel brand grows from a small operation into a multi-channel business, product data becomes a critical part of the supply chain. Designs, inventory, wholesale orders, marketplace listings, and fulfillment processes all depend on accurate product identification.


A clear product identification strategy helps prevent common operational issues, including rejected shipments, marketplace listing problems, and manual data corrections that slow teams down. For brands selling through wholesale partners and eCommerce channels, accurate identifiers create consistency across every step of the product lifecycle.


Understanding the difference between style numbers,[stock-keeping units (SKUs)](https://www.spscommerce.com/community/articles/what-is-a-stock-keeping-unit-sku) ,[Global Trade Item Numbers (GTINs)](https://www.spscommerce.com/community/articles/what-are-walmarts-gtin-standards) , and Universal Product Codes (UPCs) helps apparel, footwear, and accessory brands prepare for retail growth.


The key distinction is simple: Style numbers and SKUs help businesses manage products internally, while GTINs and UPCs provide globally recognized product identification across the retail ecosystem.

## Understanding the Role of Each Identifier


### Style Numbers


Style numbers identify the overall design of a product. For example, a brand may assign a style number to a classic button-down shirt design.


Style numbers help teams organize product lines, but typically does not distinguish between colors, sizes, or other product attributes.


**Style Number:** one per design, no matter how many colors/sizes exist


-


Style 2301 = "Essential Crewneck" (doesn't matter if it comes in 2 colors or 10, it's still style 2301)


-


Style 4410 = "Classic Oxford Button-Down"


Two different brands could both use "Style 100" for totally unrelated products. Style numbers aren't globally unique, they're just internal shorthand for a design.


### SKUs


SKUs are internal inventory identifiers that help brands manage stock, purchasing, and fulfillment. A brand may create separate SKUs for each color and size combination of a product. For example:

### Style Numbers Together: One Product, Three Identifiers:


### A customer buys a Navy, Large Essential Crewneck.


-

All three refer to the exact same shirt, but only the UPC means the same thing to every retailer, marketplace, and supply chain partner it passes through.


## Why Confusing Identifiers Create Problems


One of the most common product data mistakes is using an internal identifier, such as a SKU, where a globally recognized identifier is required.


For example, a wholesale retailer may require a GTIN to create a product listing or receive inventory. If a supplier provides an incorrect identifier, the retailer’s systems may reject the product data or flag the item for review.


Maintaining clear separation between style numbers, SKUs, and GTINs helps reduce errors and keeps product information consistent across every channel.

## Avoid Style-Level Barcode Errors


A common mistake is assigning the same barcode to every size and color of a product.


This creates problems because automated systems rely on GTINs to distinguish individual items. If a barcode does not identify the exact variation, inventory accuracy suffers and fulfillment teams may ship the wrong product.


A consistent product hierarchy helps both systems and people:


-


GTINs provide machine-readable product identification.


-


Color and size information helps employees and customers identify products quickly.


-


Standardized product data supports accurate ordering, receiving, and fulfillment.


## Understanding Dynamics Between GTINs and UPCs


### How UPCs Fit Into the GTIN Standard


The Global Trade Item Number (GTIN) is the global standard for identifying products throughout the retail supply chain. Different GTIN formats support different use cases, depending on the type of product or packaging being identified.


For apparel brands selling in North America, the most common format is the GTIN-12. GTIN is essentially the "umbrella term" for various types of product identifiers, including UPC and EAN (European Article Number).


### Common GTIN formats


-


**GTIN-12:** A 12-digit identifier commonly used for individual retail products in North America.


-


**GTIN-14:** A 14-digit identifier used for cases, cartons, and larger packaging configurations.


For example, a retailer may scan a UPC on an individual shirt to identify the exact product, while a warehouse may scan a GTIN-14 on a case containing multiple units.


The GTIN remains the product identifier regardless of the barcode format used. While packaging technology continues to evolve, the underlying product identity stays consistent.

## Product Identification Checklist for Apparel Brands


A strong product identification strategy helps brands scale into new sales channels while reducing operational challenges.


Use this checklist before launching products with retail partners:


-


Register GTINs directly through GS1 and ensure brand information matches your registration.


-


Assign a unique GTIN to every sellable product variation, including each color and size combination.


-


Use GTIN-14 identifiers for applicable case-level packaging.


-


Maintain accurate SKU structures for internal inventory management.


-


Review retailer labeling requirements before shipping products.


-


Keep documentation available to support GTIN ownership verification.


-


Use EDI transactions, including 832, 850, 856, and 810, to maintain accurate product and order information.


-


Prepare for future barcode requirements by evaluating 2D barcode adoption.
