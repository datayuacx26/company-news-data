# Product Roadmap Videos: Animate What Is Coming Next

## The short answer


A roadmap video should communicate direction and sequence while avoiding false precision.


The searcher wants a visual way to explain upcoming releases, themes, and priorities to customers, investors, or teammates.

## Who this workflow is for


This is for product leaders, founders, and customer-facing teams that need to communicate what is coming without turning the roadmap into a dense slide.


## What to prepare before generating


- A specific product launch videos goal tied to startup launch teams.
- One sentence that names the viewer, the problem, and the promised outcome.
- Any source assets: screenshots, charts, brand colors, logo files, fonts, or data points.
- The target channel and aspect ratio before you write the prompt.
- A final CTA that matches the viewer's intent.


## When to use this motion format


- You need to explain near-term product priorities.
- Customers need confidence that requested improvements are coming.
- Investors or teammates need a high-level view of momentum.


## A practical storyboard


Use this sequence as a starting point, then tighten the timing around the one action you want the viewer to take.


- 0-3s: Frame the product direction or customer theme.
- 3-10s: Show the current state and what has shipped.
- 10-22s: Animate upcoming themes as a timeline or stack.
- 22-30s: Highlight the next major release.
- 30-36s: End with feedback, waitlist, or update CTA.

## Channel cutdown plan


- Homepage: 16:9, 30 to 45 seconds, focused on the full product launch videos story.
- LinkedIn: 1:1 or 4:5, 20 to 30 seconds, silent-first with a strong first frame.
- Reels, Shorts, and TikTok: 9:16, 12 to 20 seconds, one hook and one proof point.
- Email or sales follow-up: 15 to 30 seconds, direct CTA and minimal animation noise.


## Production checklist


- Use themes when dates are uncertain.
- Separate shipped, next, and later items visually.
- Avoid promising exact timelines unless approved.
- Create customer-safe and internal versions.
- Include how viewers can follow updates or give feedback.


## Quality bar before publishing


- The first frame explains the topic without audio.
- Every text element is readable on a phone screenshot.
- Each motion beat has a job: reveal, compare, emphasize, transition, or close.
- The final frame tells the viewer what to do next.
- The video still makes sense if exported as a silent autoplay asset.


## What to measure


- Hook hold rate in the first 2 seconds.
- Click-through rate by offer and CTA variant.
- Thumb-stop rate by opening frame.
- Cost per qualified visit or trial.


## Common mistakes to avoid


- Overcommitting to dates in a public roadmap video.
- Showing too many roadmap items at once.
- Making the video sound like a feature dump instead of a strategy.

## FAQ


### Should a public roadmap video include dates?


Only include dates that the team is comfortable committing to. Otherwise use themes, now-next-later, or quarters.


### Who should see a roadmap video?


Customers, investors, internal teams, and partners may all benefit, but each audience may need a different version.


### What format works best?


Timelines, now-next-later boards, and theme cards work well for motion.
