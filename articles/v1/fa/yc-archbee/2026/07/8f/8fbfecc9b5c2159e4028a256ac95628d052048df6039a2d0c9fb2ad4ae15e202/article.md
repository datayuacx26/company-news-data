# Mastering Documentation During Cloud Migration: Stay Synced and Compliant

**Migrating to the cloud? There are many advantages, especially for businesses in the Banking, Financial Services, and Insurance sector (BFSI). First, it can help you lower costs.**


****According to a[McKinsey report](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/mckinsey-digital/our-insights/cloud-adoption-to-accelerate-it-modernization) , adopting cloud computing can reduce IT overhead costs by 30% to 40%.


However, during this challenging process, you shouldn’t forget about documentation. While documentation takes a back seat during cloud migration, it is essential for end-users. In this article, we are going to show you what happens during cloud migrations and how to avoid overlooking documentation from this process.


## **Why documentation gets overlooked in cloud migrations** #


When it comes to the BFSI sector, documentation isn’t always a top priority and that’s understandable. BFSI organizations prioritize stringent security measures, regulatory compliance, and maintaining uninterrupted services during cloud migrations. Security is most of the time the main reason for switching to the cloud. According to[Oracle’s Security in the Age of AI Report](https://www.oracle.com/a/ocom/docs/data-security-report.pdf) , 6 in 10 note security as the top benefit of cloud computing.


Source:[Oracle’s Security in the Age of AI Report](https://www.oracle.com/a/ocom/docs/data-security-report.pdf)


Unfortunately, the focus on security is not the only reason why documentation might be overlooked during cloud migration. Another issue is that existing documentation is often scattered across various platforms such as internal wikis, PDFs, and outdated Confluence pages. A survey revealed that[52% of organizations](https://info.aiim.org/enterprise-content-management-decisions) utilize three or more document management systems, leading to data silos and inefficiencies.


This makes it even more challenging to gather all of the data and verify if the documentation is up to date.[A report by Neontri](https://neontri.com/blog/cloud-migration-banking-challenges/) noted that nine in ten businesses encounter problems during cloud migration, with larger banks experiencing extended timelines, partly due to disorganized documentation.


BFSI firms operate highly complex IT ecosystems, often spanning decades-old legacy systems alongside modern digital services. Without[structured documentation](https://www.archbee.com/blog/how-documentation-powers-bank---fintech-partnerships) , teams struggle to identify dependencies between applications, databases, and services, causing bottlenecks during migration.


## **Poor documentation is a risk during BFSI cloud migrations** #


Forgetting about documentation is not an option during cloud migration. While financial institutions focus on infrastructure, security, and cost optimization, poor documentation often becomes an overlooked risk that can lead to severe consequences:​


### **Increased risk of compliance violations** #


BFSI firms must comply with strict financial regulations such as GDPR, PCI DSS, SOC 2, and FFIEC guidelines. Migration requires detailed documentation of data handling practices, security configurations, and access policies to ensure regulatory adherence. Poor documentation increases the risk of non-compliance, leading to hefty fines and legal consequences.


In 2020, Capital One was fined[$80 million](https://www.occ.gov/news-issuances/news-releases/2020/nr-occ-2020-101.html) by the U.S. Treasury’s Office of the Comptroller of the Currency due to poor cloud security controls and documentation failures following a major data breach that exposed 100 million customer records.


### **Operational disruptions** #


Cloud migrations in BFSI involve complex legacy systems with multiple dependencies. Poorly documented API structures, infrastructure blueprints, and data flow mappings can cause service outages, transaction failures, and broken integrations.


In 2018,[TSB Bank’s failed cloud migration](https://icedq.com/resources/case-studies/tsb-bank-data-migration-failure) resulted in **2 million customers losing access to their accounts for weeks** . The outage was caused in part by inadequate documentation of legacy systems and unstructured migration workflows, leading to integration failures and extended downtime. The bank was later fined[£49 million](https://www.reuters.com/world/uk/british-bank-tsb-fined-4865-million-pounds-over-it-platform-migration-failures-2022-12-20/) .


### **Increased costs** #


A lack of structured documentation often results in unexpected costs due to rework, troubleshooting, and extended project timelines. A[McKinsey & Company report](https://www.mckinsey.com/industries/technology-media-and-telecommunications/our-insights/cloud-migration-opportunity-business-value-grows-but-missteps-abound?) highlighted that organizations spend over $100 billion more on their cloud migrations than initially projected. Also, 75% of cloud migrations exceed budget and more than one-quarter experience cost overruns greater than 20%.


## **Example Case: a bank migrating from on-premise systems to the cloud** #


Let’s take a hypothetical example. ​A mid-sized regional bank started migrating its core banking system from on-premise servers to a cloud-based infrastructure to enhance scalability and security. However, outdated and fragmented documentation led to significant challenges during this transition.​


The bank's legacy environment depended on custom APIs, yet gaps in documentation made it hard for developers to map dependencies, leading to integration failures and service outages. Security teams found that existing policies were outdated and not aligned with cloud standards, creating compliance concerns and potential vulnerabilities. As a result, the migration timeline was extended by six months and operational costs increased by 30%, which also affected customer access to online banking services.


This scenario is not unique and is very similar to[TSB Bank's 2018 IT migration failure](https://icedq.com/resources/case-studies/tsb-bank-data-migration-failure) . But what can you do to avoid these issues during the challenging process of data migration? Maintaining documentation at every step of the process.


## **Best practices for maintaining documentation during migration** #


Effective documentation management is critical for successful cloud migration. It ensures a smooth transition, minimizes risks, and maintains regulatory compliance. A structured approach at each phase—pre-migration, during migration, and post-migration—helps avoid costly setbacks and operational disruptions.


### **Pre-migration: laying the foundation for success** #


The first step begins even before migration starts: Conducting a thorough audit of existing documentation. This includes reviewing infrastructure diagrams, API references, security policies, and compliance reports to identify outdated, missing, or incomplete information. Make sure that all of your documentation is in one place. Using a documentation platform like Archbee can help you gather all of your docs. You can then update them and keep track of the changes over time.


**Quantfolio** , which offers "Deep Alpha," a digital platform for investment advisory, had this issue. Their documentation was scattered across different platforms. They chose Archbee because it *“is markdown-based and supports both internal and external sharing”* . Now, Quantfolio has more[up-to-date documentation](https://www.archbee.com/customers/quantfolio) and they’ve noticed an increase in engagement from both readers and contributors.


Financial institutions often rely on a mix of legacy and modern systems, making documentation gaps inevitable. According to a Forrester report,[97% of organizations](https://www.adobe.com/content/dam/dx-dc/pdf/ue/forrester-digital-documents-business-necessity-ue.pdf) **had minimal or no digital document processes** . Addressing these gaps early helps avoid last-minute troubleshooting and ensures that teams have the necessary insights into system dependencies and workflows.


### **During migration: ensuring accuracy and collaboration** #


As the migration progresses, real-time updates to documentation are important to track infrastructure changes, security configurations, and data flow adjustments. Documentation platforms with[version control](https://www.archbee.com/blog/document-version-control-tactics) mechanisms like Archbee help teams maintain accurate documentation without losing historical data.


Collaboration among IT, security, and compliance teams is key to preventing inconsistencies. Cloud migration projects often involve **multiple stakeholders** , including third-party vendors, system integrators, and compliance auditors. **Implementing real-time documentation updates can ensure smooth communication across teams** .


### **Post-migration: aligning with new cloud workflows** #


Once migration is complete, documentation must be aligned with the new cloud infrastructure, security protocols, and compliance requirements. Financial institutions must update[IAM (Identity and Access Management)](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/IAM/latest/UserGuide/introduction.html) **policies, network configurations, backup and disaster recovery plans, and data encryption protocols** to reflect the cloud environment.


Regulatory audits in the BFSI sector require up-to-date documentation to demonstrate compliance with frameworks like **PCI DSS, GDPR, SOC 2, and FFIEC guidelines** . Failure to maintain proper documentation can result in penalties. In October 2020, the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency fined Citibank[$400 million](https://www.occ.gov/news-issuances/news-releases/2020/nr-occ-2020-132.html) for deficiencies in enterprise-wide risk management, compliance risk management, data governance, and internal controls.


Automating documentation updates can significantly reduce human errors and inefficiencies. Tools like[AWS Config](https://aws.amazon.com/config/) **,**[Azure Policy](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/governance/policy/) **, or**[Kubernetes Operators](https://kubernetes.io/docs/concepts/extend-kubernetes/operator/) can help automate infrastructure documentation and ensure compliance.

Treat documentation as a first‑class workstream with clear owners, tooling, and measurable outcomes.


-


**Pre‑migration: set the foundation**


- Inventory existing docs, find gaps, and centralize everything in one platform with versioning and access controls.
- Map systems, data flows, integrations, and controls; prioritize high‑risk areas like payments, customer data, and regulated workloads.
- Define taxonomy and templates; assign document owners with review cadences and SLAs.


-


**During migration: document in real time**


- Keep version‑controlled updates to architectures, configs, IAM policies, runbooks, and data flow diagrams.
- Use role‑based access so IT, Security, Compliance, and vendors collaborate safely.
- Link docs to change tickets, test results, and approvals; capture decisions, risks, and rollback steps.


-


**Post‑migration: align to the new cloud state**


- Update IAM, network, backup/DR, encryption, monitoring, and data retention to reflect the live environment.
- Maintain audit‑ready evidence packs and set a recurring review cadence with accountable owners.


-


**Automate wherever possible**


- Integrate with tools like **AWS Config** , **Azure Policy** , and **Kubernetes Operators** to auto‑capture changes, detect compliance drift, and generate infrastructure snapshots.
- Connect documentation to Jira for traceability; use CI/CD to publish docs on merges.


-


**Govern and measure**


- Establish standards for accuracy and freshness.
- Track KPIs: documentation coverage, time‑to‑find critical info, mean time to update after change, audit findings, and on‑call success rate. Review quarterly and improve.
