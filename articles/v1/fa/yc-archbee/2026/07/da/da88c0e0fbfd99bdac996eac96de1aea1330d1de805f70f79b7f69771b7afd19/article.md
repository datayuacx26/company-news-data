# From Internal Wikis to External Knowledge Bases: Expanding Your Documentation Strategy

**Knowledge bases have evolved from simple repositories of internal information to critical strategic assets for SaaS and tech companies.**


With the move toward digital-first interactions and self-service support, organizations are dedicating substantial resources to creating scalable, accessible documentation. Customers increasingly favor self-service over traditional support. According to a[Zendesk report](https://www.zendesk.com/blog/searching-for-self-service/) , 91% of customers said they would use a knowledge base if it met their needs.


However, making the shift from internal wikis to external knowledge bases is not an easy move. In this article, we’ll show you how you can expand your documentation strategy effectively and without breaking the bank.


## **Why teams make the shift to external knowledge bases** #


### **Reducing support load** #


A key argument for transitioning to external knowledge bases is the significant reduction in support ticket volume. When customers and developers can resolve issues independently through self-service documentation, it alleviates pressure on customer support teams.


[Digitail](https://digitail.com/) , an all-in-one software for veterinary clinics,[chose Archbee](https://www.archbee.com/customers/digitail) for its API documentation and successfully offered a better user experience. *“Our support time has reduced a lot, most simple questions can be answered by the assistant and what reaches our team in the end is more relevant and targeted problems.”*


### **Improving developer experience** #


For API-first companies or those providing complex SaaS products, high-quality developer documentation is critical. Developers expect clear, structured, and accurate information to build, integrate, and troubleshoot effectively. According to[Stack Overflow's annual survey](https://stackoverflow.co/teams/resources/knowledge-base-101/?) , 63% of developers spend more than 30 minutes daily searching for answers to their questions.


### **Increasing collaboration and easy scaling** #


For companies with third-party partners, resellers, or developers building integrations, external knowledge bases are invaluable tools. By making resources openly available, companies encourage broader ecosystem participation and innovation.


Also, a well-structured external knowledge base can adapt to the expanding needs of a growing user base. It makes it easier for new information to be integrated and users can easily find relevant content.


## **Key considerations before going public** #


There are many benefits to expanding your documentation strategy. However, there are also a few things you need to keep in mind before making the switch to an external knowledge base.


### **Privacy and security** #


Privacy and security are top priorities when shifting from an internal wiki to an external knowledge base, especially for BSFI and other SaaS businesses.[A report](https://www.oracle.com/a/ocom/docs/data-security-report.pdf) shows that 60% of C-suite executives note security as their main priority.


Internal documentation often includes sensitive or proprietary information that must be safeguarded.[Ensuring data anonymization](https://www.archbee.com/blog/api-documentation-before-its-too-late) is essential before making content public; proprietary algorithms, internal processes, or customer data should be removed or anonymized. Implementing robust[access controls](https://www.archbee.com/docs/team-access-control) is another critical step, with permission settings ensuring that only the right people can edit or view certain documents. Compliance requirements are also a major concern, especially for industries governed by stringent regulations such as GDPR or[PCI-DSS](https://www.pcisecuritystandards.org/) .


### **Content restructuring** #


Content restructuring is essential because internal wikis are often developed with a specific audience in mind: employees who understand company jargon, processes, and shorthand.


Making this content public requires restructuring to be more accessible and user-friendly. Before making the transition, companies should clearly define their audience. External knowledge bases may be intended for customers seeking product usage guides and troubleshooting steps, developers looking for[API documentation](https://www.archbee.com/blog/api-documentation) and integration guides, or partners requiring partnership policies and integration resources.


Simplifying technical language is necessary to ensure it’s understandable to a broader audience. Internal jargon should be replaced with user-friendly explanations and examples. The overall content hierarchy and navigation must be improved for intuitive categorization, tagging, and search functionality to enhance discoverability.


### **Branding and user experience** #


Branding and user experience are critical aspects to consider when building an external knowledge base. An external knowledge base serves as a public-facing resource and must reflect the company's brand identity and quality standards.


For example, Visualogyx created a knowledge base that follows its brand identity and is filled with visual examples which makes it easier for end-users to understand.


Source:[Visualogyx](https://help.visualogyx.com/)


[Visual consistency](https://www.archbee.com/blog/visuals-in-technical-documentation) is essential; the knowledge base’s appearance should align with the overall branding, including logos, colors, and typography. The tone of voice should be consistent with the company’s branding guidelines.[Accessibility](https://www.archbee.com/blog/documentation-the-european-accessibility-act-are-you-ready-for-the-shift) is another significant concern. Documentation should be compatible with screen readers, provide alternative text for images, and include accessible navigation structures.


## **Best practices for transitioning to an external knowledge base** #


To ensure a smooth and effective transition, here are some best practices that will help you clean up internal documentation, define a clear content structure, and prepare the content for an external audience.​


### **1. Audit and clean up internal documentation** #


Begin by conducting a thorough audit of your existing internal documentation. Identify and remove outdated, redundant, or irrelevant content. This process not only streamlines your documentation but also ensures that only accurate and useful information is made public.


### **2. Define a clear and intuitive content structure** #


Organize your[knowledge base](https://www.archbee.com/blog/build-product-knowledge-base) with a logical hierarchy that facilitates easy navigation. Categorize content into sections and subsections that align with user needs and common queries. For example, structuring content by user roles or specific tasks makes it easier for end-users to find what they are looking for.

A well-organized knowledge base enhances the user experience and can drive higher engagement. Companies that deliver effective self-service options see a[25% increase](https://www.renascence.io/journal/how-customer-experience-cx-drives-business-growth-in-2025-30-examples?) in customer satisfaction.


### **3. Adapt content for an external audience** #


Internal documentation often contains jargon or references that may not be familiar to external users. Rewrite content in clear, concise language, avoiding internal acronyms and technical terms without explanations. Providing step-by-step instructions with visual aids, such as images and videos, can significantly enhance comprehension.


### **4. Implement robust search functionality** #


Ensure that your knowledge base platform offers powerful search capabilities. Users should be able to find information quickly using keywords and phrases. Effective search functionality reduces frustration and enhances the self-service experience.


### **5. Regularly update and maintain content** #


Establish a routine for reviewing and updating knowledge base articles to keep them accurate and relevant. Encourage user feedback to identify areas where information may be lacking or unclear. Regular maintenance ensures that the knowledge base remains a reliable resource for users. Actually,[70% of customers](https://www.renascence.io/journal/how-customer-experience-cx-drives-business-growth-in-2025-30-examples?) prefer using a company's website to get answers rather than contacting customer service.

## Frequently Asked Questions


Going public turns your docs into a 24/7 support and adoption engine. Here’s what teams typically gain:


- **Lower support volume and costs:** Self‑service deflects repetitive questions so agents can focus on complex issues. According to a[Zendesk report](https://www.zendesk.com/blog/searching-for-self-service/) , 91% of customers will use a knowledge base if it meets their needs.
- **Faster developer success:** Clear, structured API and integration docs shorten time to “hello world” and speed up troubleshooting.[Stack Overflow data](https://stackoverflow.co/teams/resources/knowledge-base-101/?) shows many developers spend 30+ minutes daily searching for answers.
- **Scale without extra headcount:** A single, reliable source of truth serves customers, partners, and resellers—no more one-off explanations.
- **Better onboarding and adoption:** Task-based guides, examples, and visuals help users get value faster (self‑service options are linked to higher customer satisfaction).
- **Actionable content insights:** Search analytics reveal what users are looking for and where content gaps exist, so you can prioritize updates with confidence.
- **Stronger brand and discoverability:** Polished, consistent, and searchable docs build trust and can boost SEO with qualified traffic.
- **Healthier ecosystem:** Public docs make it easier for partners and integrators to build on your platform, encouraging community growth.


Before you hit “publish,” validate safety, clarity, and consistency.


- **Privacy and security:** Classify content, remove or anonymize sensitive data (PII, credentials, proprietary algorithms), and enforce role‑based access for editing and restricted areas. Confirm compliance needs such as GDPR, PCI‑DSS, or SOC 2.
- **Audience definition:** Decide who you’re writing for (customers, developers, partners) and tailor tone, depth, and examples. Consider localization and whether separate spaces are needed for different audiences.
- **Information architecture:** Build a clear structure with categories, tags, and task‑based navigation. Replace internal jargon with plain language and add visuals, examples, or code samples where helpful.
- **Branding and UX:** Align design with your brand, ensure mobile responsiveness and fast load times, and meet accessibility standards (e.g., WCAG 2.1 AA). Include alt text, keyboard navigation, and clear headings.
- **Search and discoverability:** Provide robust search with synonyms and filters. Add SEO basics (titles, meta descriptions, headings), redirects from old URLs, and an XML sitemap.
- **Governance and maintenance:** Assign content owners, define review cycles, use version control and approvals, publish changelogs, and enable feedback and analytics so content stays accurate and current.


Use this step‑by‑step process to make internal content public‑ready:


1. **Inventory and classify:** Gather all content, remove duplicates and outdated pages, and label what can be public vs. restricted. Strip credentials, customer data, and proprietary details.
2. **Decide what stays private:** Keep internal‑only runbooks, security procedures, and non‑public roadmaps behind access controls.
3. **Design the structure:** Define personas and top tasks, then create a clear hierarchy with categories, templates, and consistent naming.
4. **Rewrite for clarity:** Use plain language, explain acronyms, and add step‑by‑step instructions, prerequisites, examples, screenshots, and code samples where relevant.
5. **Make it findable:** Add descriptive titles, summaries, metadata, and internal links. Configure search with synonyms and filters, and set up 301 redirects from previous URLs.
6. **Ensure quality and accessibility:** Use proper headings, alt text, captions, and sufficient color contrast. Test across devices, browsers, and screen sizes.
7. **Set up governance:** Establish review and approval workflows, version control, a style and terminology guide, and a regular update cadence.
8. **Launch and iterate:** Soft‑launch to a small audience, collect feedback, monitor search analytics to fill gaps, and keep content updated as your product evolves.
