# How to Justify Documentation Investment to Your Leadership Team

Documentation is one of those things everyone agrees is important, but when budgets are tight or timelines are short, it’s often the first to be cut. You can think about documentation as insurance for your house. Everybody knows that it’s important, but it’s very low on the priority list for many. Yet, just like insurance, skipping it can lead to much greater costs down the road. For documentation managers, technical writers, and product teams, justifying investment in documentation is a challenge.


The key? Speak leadership’s language: **business impact** . Strong documentation isn’t just a nice-to-have. It reduces costs, improves user satisfaction, and helps your company scale faster.[IDC reports](https://computhink.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/10/IDC20on20The20High20Cost20Of20Not20Finding20Information.pdf) that document challenges account for a 21.3% loss in productivity, costing businesses approximately $19,732 per information worker annually.


This article breaks down how to build a compelling case for documentation investment by aligning it with measurable business outcomes.


## **The main benefits of documentation investment** #


While you might already know the benefits of investing in documentation, it’s crucial to showcase them to the leadership team.


### **Reduce onboarding friction** #


Poor onboarding ranks among the[biggest churn drivers](https://www.precursive.com/customer-onboarding-2021-report) in SaaS and API-first businesses. If users can't get up and running quickly, they'll leave. Effective documentation enables self-service, accelerates adoption, and eases the burden on support and success teams. Studies have shown that[40% of users](https://userguiding.com/blog/user-onboarding-statistics?) prefer self-service over human contact, underscoring the importance of accessible documentation during the onboarding process. By providing clear, detailed, and easily navigable resources, organizations can create a smoother onboarding experience, leading to higher user satisfaction and retention.


[Xenett](https://www.xenett.com/) , an accounting platform,[improved its user onboarding process](https://www.archbee.com/customers/xenett) by creating a public knowledge base with Archbee. “ *Now, if people have any questions, they look for the answers in our knowledge base. That also helps reduce our support tickets.”*


### **Lower support costs** #


Support teams frequently serve as the safety net when documentation is missing or unclear. Every minute they spend replying to a repeat question is money lost. A[Plivo study](https://www.plivo.com/cx/blog/how-much-does-customer-support-cost-and-how-to-reduce-it) reports that large companies (with annual recurring revenue over $100 million) typically allocate 5% or less of their ARR to customer service team costs. This suggests the support team is already underfunded and overworked. High-quality documentation dramatically reduces repetitive tickets by helping users find answers on their own.


[Phokus](https://www.phokus.io/) , an AI-driven ecosystem built for sport, also chose Archbee to support their customers in the onboarding process. Even from the early stages of the implementation, they’ve seen[great results](https://www.archbee.com/customers/phokus) . *“We’ve just launched and are already seeing a reduction in customer support queries. Feedback so far has been overwhelmingly positive.”*


### **Prevent churn** #


For API and platform companies, documentation is the user interface. If developers hit dead ends or can’t understand how to integrate, they’ll abandon your product. Clear, accurate, and[up-to-date docs](https://www.archbee.com/blog/keeping-product-documentation-up-to-date) are essential for long-term engagement. Actually,[8 in 10](https://www.wyzowl.com/customer-onboarding-statistics/) **users** say they’ve deleted an app because they didn’t know how to use it.

### **Reduced risk and errors** #


Clear documentation decreases the likelihood of costly errors, miscommunications, and compliance violations. Consistent documentation practices promote accuracy and reduce business risk. According to[a study by the IDC](https://computhink.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/10/IDC20on20The20High20Cost20Of20Not20Finding20Information.pdf) , employees spend approximately 2.5 hours per day searching for information, which amounts to nearly 30% of the workday. This inefficiency doesn’t only impact productivity but also increases the risk of errors due to reliance on outdated or incorrect information.

## **Common pushbacks: How to address them** #


When you advocate for investing in documentation, you may encounter various pushbacks from leadership—and that’s normal. Leadership objections are often based on misunderstanding. Here’s how to counter them:


**“We don’t have time to invest in docs.”**


You’re already spending time answering the same questions again and again. Documentation is a force multiplier, every hour you invest now saves many more down the line.


**“Customers don’t read documentation.”**


The data says otherwise. According to[Harvard Business Review](https://hbr.org/2017/01/kick-ass-customer-service) , 81% of customers try to take care of a problem themselves before reaching out to a representative. If they’re not reading your docs, the problem may be visibility or quality—not interest.


**“It’s too expensive.”**


Compared to the cost of support, churn, and lost sales, documentation is one of the most cost-effective investments you can make. Using a documentation platform can help you[cut these costs](https://www.archbee.com/blog/documentation-website-costs) even more, while also enhancing the end-user experience.


**“Documentation quickly becomes outdated.”**


Documentation should evolve alongside your product. Investing in tools, workflows, and dedicated personnel makes[maintaining up-to-date documentation](https://www.archbee.com/blog/developer-documentation-maintenance) manageable and sustainable. Establishing regular review cycles or integrating documentation directly into your product development process ensures it stays accurate and relevant.


**“Our product is intuitive enough without documentation.”**


Even intuitive products encounter edge cases, advanced features, or complex integrations that require documentation. Additionally, documentation supports diverse learning styles and user preferences—while some customers jump right in, others prefer structured guidance, detailed references, or[troubleshooting resources](https://www.archbee.com/blog/how-to-write-a-troubleshooting-guide) .


## **Building a business case for documentation** #


Winning leadership support for documentation often means translating your documentation needs into clear business outcomes. To persuade stakeholders effectively, your business case must emphasize measurable impacts and align directly with organizational goals.


Here’s a practical framework:


### **Identify the problem clearly** #


Clearly state the documentation gaps that your organization faces. Frame these issues not merely as documentation problems but as business challenges. For example:


- High volume of repetitive customer support tickets
- Long onboarding times for new users or developers
- Low adoption or increased churn due to unclear guidance


Explain how these issues negatively impact productivity, customer experience, or revenue.


### **Collect supporting data and metrics** #


Gather quantifiable evidence showing how documentation impacts business operations:


**Support metrics:**


Calculate the percentage of support tickets that could be resolved by improved documentation. For instance, “30% of support tickets are repetitive documentation-related queries, costing the company $X per year.”


**Productivity losses:**


Leverage statistics such as IDC’s finding that document-related issues can cause a **21.3% productivity loss, costing approximately $19,732 per information worker annually.


Customer onboarding metrics:**


Present data on average onboarding time, highlighting potential improvements. Example: “Currently, onboarding takes three weeks on average—better documentation can reduce this by at least one week.”


### **Outline financial and productivity benefits and competitive advantages** #


Clearly outline the anticipated ROI:


**Reduced costs:**


Show potential savings from decreased support volume, increased productivity, and reduced customer churn.


**Increased revenue:**


Cite how strong documentation can accelerate sales cycles and improve conversions. For example, studies show that clear product documentation positively influences purchasing decisions, especially in B2B and developer-driven markets.


**Productivity gains:**


Demonstrate how internal documentation enables faster knowledge sharing, reduces duplicated efforts, and improves engineering and operational efficiency.


**Competitive advantages:**


Explain how better documentation differentiates your product or service from competitors, influences customer decisions, and strengthens market positioning.


### **Define clear implementation steps** #


Provide a practical roadmap:


**Phase 1 (Short-term):**


Start with quick wins—prioritize documentation updates in the highest-impact areas (FAQs, onboarding guides, high-volume support issues).


**Phase 2 (Mid-term):**


Establish clear KPIs (support ticket volume, onboarding time, customer satisfaction scores) and document the improvements achieved.


**Phase 3 (Long-term):**


Scale documentation practices across teams, establish internal standards, and regularly report on documentation performance to leadership.


### **Show how it helps each team #


For engineering:** Emphasize dev productivity and reduced context-switching


**For product:** Highlight how good docs drive adoption and reduce friction


**For support:** Show how docs reduce tickets and improve customer satisfaction


**For sales/marketing:** Make the case for documentation as a conversion tool
