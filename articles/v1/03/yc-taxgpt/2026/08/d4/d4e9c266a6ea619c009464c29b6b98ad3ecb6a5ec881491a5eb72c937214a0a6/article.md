# TaxGPT vs Bizora AI: Beyond Tax Research

## What Each Platform Is Built to Do


Bizora and TaxGPT aren't competing to be the best version of the same tool. They're built around different definitions of scope: one is a research specialist, the other is a full operating system with research as one module inside it. Worth laying out plainly before going feature by feature.


### Bizora: built for the research


Bizora does one job and goes deep on it. Every answer traces back to primary sources, the IRC, Treasury Regulations, IRS rulings, and case law, and its Deep Research mode breaks complex questions into sub-queries, works each one against authoritative sources, and synthesizes a structured answer instead of a single-shot response. The View Steps feature shows that reasoning chain directly: which sources it pulled, how it connected them, and why it landed where it did. That's genuinely useful when you need to walk a client or a reviewing partner through how an answer was reached, not just hand them the conclusion.


Beyond the core research assistant, Bizora includes Vault for querying uploaded documents, Canvas for drafting memos and emails in the same thread as the research, and visual decision trees for mapping out multi-step tax positions. It's also unusually open on the integration side. An OpenAI-compatible API and a direct MCP connector mean firms can pull Bizora's research into Claude Desktop or their own tooling instead of being boxed into one interface. Pricing is public and low-friction, with plans starting at $29.99 a month and a 7-day free trial that requires no credit card, which matters for solo practitioners and small firms deciding whether to test it before committing.


### TaxGPT: research as one module in a larger system


TaxGPT's[AI research engine](https://www.taxgpt.com/ai-tax-research) covers similar ground: cited answers from the IRC, Treasury Regulations, IRS guidance, and case law, extended to Canadian federal and provincial tax law. But research sits alongside five other modules under one login. Writer handles memo and correspondence drafting, Matrix builds instant multi-jurisdiction comparison tables, Client Intelligence carries persistent client context across sessions, Agent Andrew automates return review, and Cowork runs end-to-end agentic workflows like tax prep, bookkeeping, and entity structure reviews inside the software firms already use.


The distinction that matters is this: in TaxGPT, a research answer doesn't dead-end in a chat window. It can feed a memo in Writer, inform a review in Agent Andrew, or sit inside a client's ongoing profile in Client Intelligence. That's the operating-system framing. Not that the research is better, but that it's one connected layer in a stack built to carry work from question to filed return.


## Research Depth and Jurisdictional Coverage


This is the dimension Bizora leans on hardest, so it deserves a fair look before getting into where TaxGPT pulls ahead. Both platforms cite real primary authority, the IRC, Treasury Regulations, IRS rulings, and case law, so neither is generating plausible-sounding guidance from general web content. Bizora's View Steps feature is a genuine strength worth crediting: it shows the full reasoning chain behind an answer, which sources were pulled and how they connect, and that kind of transparency is useful when a reviewing partner wants to see the work before signing off on a position.


Where the two diverge is depth rather than presence. Bizora covers all 50 U.S. states plus federal tax law, and its knowledge base lists select international codes, including Canada, India, the Netherlands, and Singapore, along with OECD Pillar Two, transfer pricing guidance, and EU VAT frameworks. TaxGPT covers the same U.S. jurisdictional range and states its Canadian coverage specifically at the federal and provincial level, including the Income Tax Act and CRA guidance. For firms doing substantive Canadian work, that provincial-level depth is the meaningful difference, not whether Canada is covered at all.


[TaxGPT's Matrix](https://www.taxgpt.com/taxgpt-matrix) also changes how multi-jurisdiction questions get answered: one query, multiple jurisdictions selected, and the result comes back as a fully cited, exportable comparison grid instead of separate answers run state by state. For SALT-heavy practices, that turns what would be several research sessions into one. So while Bizora's sourcing standard and reasoning transparency hold up well, the deeper question for any firm evaluating either tool is whether the output clears the bar for[defensible tax research](https://www.taxgpt.com/blog/there-is-a-difference-between-defensible-tax-research-and-just-tax-research-tools) , not just a citation-backed one, and that's where TaxGPT's added jurisdictional reach and comparison tooling pull ahead. For a broader look at how to approach tax research methodically, our[tax research guide](https://www.taxgpt.com/blog/tax-research) covers the full framework.


## Beyond Research: Return Review, Prep Automation, and Client Memory


This is where the category difference stops being a framing choice and becomes a measurable gap. Bizora's own comparison content concedes it directly: choose Bizora for research, choose TaxGPT if the bottleneck is return volume. A cited answer is genuinely useful, but it still leaves the return to prepare, review, and get through the door. That's the work TaxGPT is built to close out, and it's work Bizora doesn't attempt.


### Agent Andrew: automated return review


Agent Andrew reviews prepared Forms 1040, 1065, 1120, and 1120-S, reconciling the return against source documents like W-2s, 1099s, and K-1s. It sorts findings into three categories: red flags for transcription errors, omissions, and audit-risk mismatches, green flags for missed deductions and tax-saving opportunities, and cleared flags for items already resolved. TaxGPT reports this automates up to 90% of the review process, cutting what's typically a 30-to-90-minute manual senior review down to 5 to 10 minutes per return. Across a season with hundreds of returns, that's not a marginal time save; it's the difference between reviewing everything at the same standard and reviewing under fatigue by week six.


### Cowork: agentic workflows that run the work, not just answer questions


Cowork assigns dedicated AI agents to run entire workflows end to end inside the software a firm already uses, pulling documents from connected storage and handing off only when a judgment call is needed. Of its 19 workflows, a few are worth naming for evaluation purposes: 1040 preparation running inside ProConnect and similar tax software, K-1 production, R&D tax credit studies, and amended return processing. Each produces a client-ready deliverable rather than a draft that still needs assembly. One thing worth flagging for firms outside the U.S.: Cowork is currently available for U.S. firms only.


### Client Intelligence: context that persists


Client Intelligence keeps a running profile for each client, so research, review, and drafting all draw on the same context instead of starting from a blank chat every time. Bizora's Vault stores uploaded documents for reference, but it doesn't build a persistent profile the way Client Intelligence does. In practice, that's the difference between re-uploading a client's return and prior-year details every time a question comes up, versus asking the question and having the platform already know who the client is.


Put together, the workflow math is straightforward: a research answer is the start of the engagement, not the end of it. TaxGPT's additional layers are built to carry that answer through review, preparation, and delivery, which is the structural gap Bizora doesn't compete on.


## Security And Data Handling


For firms evaluating either platform, this isn't a nice-to-have; it's table stakes given the sensitivity of the data involved. TaxGPT is SOC 2 Type II certified, meaning an independent third-party auditor has verified its controls over time, not just at a single point in time. That's paired with automatic PII redaction, encryption at rest and in transit, and onshore hosting on AWS and Azure. Bizora, by contrast, states it's actively working toward SOC 2 audit readiness rather than holding the certification today. Its security page specifies AES-256 encryption at rest and TLS in transit, which are legitimate, real controls, but "bank-level encryption," the phrase used elsewhere on Bizora's site, is marketing language rather than an attestation. It's worth naming that distinction plainly rather than letting the two get conflated: encryption specs describe what a system does, while a SOC 2 report is independently verified evidence that it does it consistently.


That distinction matters more for firms than it might first appear. A CPA firm has its own compliance obligations to clients, and when a partner is choosing a vendor to hold client tax data, being able to point to a third-party audit report carries real weight in a due-diligence conversation, whether that's with a client, an insurer, or a state board. A published encryption standard is a reasonable baseline. An audited attestation is a different category of assurance, and for firms that need to document their own compliance posture, that's often the deciding factor.


## Which Should Your Firm Choose?


**Choose Bizora if** you're a solo practitioner or small firm whose need is primarily research, you want the lowest entry price with no long-term commitment, you value seeing the full reasoning trace behind an answer, or you want to build your own workflows through its API or MCP connector.


**Choose TaxGPT if** your firm needs more than research, meaning return review, prep automation, or persistent client context, you need provincial-level Canadian tax coverage or handle cross-border work, a SOC 2 Type II attestation is a requirement for your own compliance posture, or you'd rather run one platform than manage a research tool alongside separate systems for everything downstream of it.


If you're still deciding, run both on a real trial and check five things: Does it cite the specific code section or ruling you'd cite yourself, not just a plausible-sounding answer? Does it cover every jurisdiction your clients actually touch, including Canada if that applies? What happens to a research answer after you get it, does it feed into drafting and review, or dead-end in the chat? Can you see or reconstruct the reasoning behind a complex answer well enough to defend it to a client or reviewer? And what's the actual cost once you add up everything you'd still need to buy separately to cover what the platform doesn't do?

### FAQ


#### What is Bizora AI?


Bizora is an AI tax research assistant that delivers citation-backed answers drawn from the Internal Revenue Code, Treasury Regulations, IRS rulings, and case law. It also includes Vault for document analysis, Canvas for drafting, and Deep Research mode for breaking complex questions into sub-queries. It's built primarily for research, not for return preparation or review.


#### How much does Bizora AI cost?


Bizora publishes three tiers: Essential at $29.99 a month (with daily caps on deep research, web-search, and document questions), Pro at $69.99 a month with those limits removed, and Enterprise at $119.99 a month with added support and onboarding. A 7-day free trial is available with no credit card required.


#### What's the difference between TaxGPT and Bizora?


Bizora is a research-focused tool built around fast, citation-backed answers. TaxGPT is an AI Operating System where research is one module alongside return review, prep automation, client memory, and drafting, so the same platform can carry work from a research question through to a filed return.


#### Does Bizora prepare or review tax returns?


No. Bizora's own published comparison content states it as a research tool and positions return prep and review as work firms hand off to other systems. It does not offer return preparation or automated return review.


#### Does TaxGPT show its sources?


Yes. Every TaxGPT research answer includes citations to primary authority such as the IRC, Treasury Regulations, IRS and CRA guidance, and court decisions, so practitioners can verify the answer against the underlying source. Bizora's View Steps feature offers a similar kind of reasoning transparency on its own research answers.


#### Which is better for Canadian tax work?


Both platforms cover Canada, but at different depths. TaxGPT specifies federal and provincial coverage, including the Income Tax Act and CRA guidance. Bizora lists Canada as one of several supported international tax codes without specifying provincial-level detail, so firms with substantive Canadian provincial work should verify depth of coverage directly with Bizora before relying on it.
