# How To Use TaxGPT After Tax Season

### How TaxGPT Helps You Stay On Track


Now let’s talk about how to actually tackle each situation:


#### Start Tracking Deductions Now


One of the biggest regrets people have at tax time is realizing they didn’t track deductions all year. You remember spending money on things that should count, things like charitable donations, medical expenses, or work related costs. But you have no receipts and no systems, by April it’s too late.


Tax season is fresh, but you should start thinking about your situation. Some of these deductions might not be obvious and it helps to have a resource like TaxGPT to point you in the right direction. After you find out what's eligible you can start your simple system, this could be a folder for receipts, a spreadsheet or whatever works best for you.


The difference between tracking as you go and scrambling in April? Hundreds of dollars and a peace of mind.


#### Self-Employed? Plan for Quarterly Taxes


If you’re self-employed or have freelance income, quarterly taxes are a fact of life. If you expect to owe at least $1,000 after withholding, you probably need to make quarterly estimated tax payments. Miss a payment or underpay and you’re looking at penalties. Make a bad estimate and you either overpay all year or owe a big chunk in April. It’s stressful because the stakes feel real.


The key to managing this is adjusting your payments as your income changes throughout the year. If you earn $20,000 in the first half of the year, your Q3 payment should reflect that, not just be a fourth of your prior-year tax. The IRS has safe-harbor rules that protect you from penalties if you use the right method: either equal quarterly installments based on last year’s tax, or the annualized income method that adjusts as your income actually occurs.


The math can get complicated because it involves your filling status, withholding credits, and self-employment tax. That’s where TaxGPT is useful. Walk it through your actual income so far and it can help you figure out what you should pay next, and which method works best for your situation. You’re not guessing–you’re making a calculated decision.


Check in quarterly as your income changes, and you’ll avoid the April shock.


#### The SALT Deduction Window to Think About


If you’re a homeowner in a high-tax state, pay attention to this one. Right now you can deduct up to $40,000 in state and local taxes (property taxes, income taxes, sales taxes). But in 2030, Congress is letting the cap expire and it goes back down to just $10,000. That’s a pretty big drop if you’re paying a lot in taxes where you live.


If you’re already close to hitting that $40,000 cap, you might want to think about your tax strategy for the next few years. Some homeowners decide to prepay their state taxes or property taxes before 2030 to take advantage of the higher cap while it’s still there. But honestly, whether that makes sense depends entirely on your situation. Your income, where you live, what your mortgage looks like. It’s not something with a one-size-fits-all answer.


This is a good one to run through TaxGPT. Tell it your numbers and ask if prepaying taxes before 2030 would actually help you save money. You might be surprised at what the answer is.


Don’t wait until next year to think about this. You’ve got time to plan if you start now.
