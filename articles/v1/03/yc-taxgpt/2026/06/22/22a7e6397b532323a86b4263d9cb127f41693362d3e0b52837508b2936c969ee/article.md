# 35 Small Business Tax Deductions You’re Probably Missing (2025 Guide by Entity Type)

### 18. Startup and Organizational Costs


For new businesses, the IRS allows a deduction of:


- Up to $5,000 in startup costs
- Up to $5,000 in organizational costs (e.g., LLC formation, legal setup) - does not apply to sole proprietorships


If your total startup costs exceed $50,000, the deduction phases out and the rest must be amortized over 15 years.


Common deductible expenses:


- Market research
- Travel to suppliers or mentors
- Logo and branding
- Website development


**What to Track** :


- Receipts for pre-operational expenses
- Entity formation documents
- Legal and consulting invoices


### 19. Advertising and Marketing


You can deduct all expenses related to promoting your business, including:


- Paid social media ads
- Print and digital advertising
- Website development and hosting
- Graphic design and branding
- Promotional events and sponsorships


*IRS guidance allows deductions even for goodwill-based marketing (e.g., donating services for public exposure), as long as there’s a clear business intent.*


### 20. Cost of Goods Sold (COGS)


If you sell physical goods, COGS is a required deduction. It includes:


- Direct labor
- Raw materials
- Packaging and shipping
- Storage and factory costs


**What’s Required** :


Track beginning and ending inventory. Inventory valuation must follow consistent accounting methods (e.g., FIFO, LIFO, or average cost).


## Tax Deductions by Business Structure


Not all tax deductions are created equal. How you claim them (and which ones you're eligible for) depends heavily on how your business is structured.


### Sole Proprietors and Single-Member LLCs


These businesses report deductions on Schedule C and Schedule 1 of Form 1040. You’re subject to self-employment tax but also eligible for several personalized deductions.


### 21. Health Insurance Premiums (Section 162(l))


Deduct up to 100% of premiums paid for health, dental, and vision insurance for yourself, your spouse, and dependents, so long as you’re not eligible for an employer-sponsored plan.


- Deducted on Schedule 1
- Applies even if the plan is in your personal name
- Limited to business income generated


### 22. Long-Term Care Insurance


Premiums for qualified long-term care insurance are deductible, with annual IRS caps based on your age:


- Up to $480 if under age 40
- Up to $6,020 if 71 or older (2025 limits)


This deduction applies under the same rules as other self-employed health benefits.


### 23. Retirement Contributions


Sole proprietors can deduct generous contributions made to retirement plans set up for themselves:


- **SEP IRA** : Up to 25% of net earnings or $70,000 for 2025 (indexed annually)
- **SIMPLE IRA** : Up to $16,500 plus $3,500 catch-up
- **Solo 401(k)** : $70,000 (combined employer + employee) + $7,500 catch-up


These deductions reduce both income tax and self-employment tax, making them especially valuable.


### 24. Education Expenses (Skill-Based)


Deduct costs for classes, certifications, seminars, books, or professional publications, as long as they improve or maintain skills in your current business.


- Does not apply to training for a new profession
- Also includes costs for online courses and trade-specific software


***Pro tip*** *: Document the business purpose of each program in your records.*


### For S Corporations


S corps file Form 1120-S and issue Schedule K-1 to shareholders. Deductions must follow payroll and fringe benefit rules, but when structured correctly, the tax advantages are substantial.


### 25. Employee Reimbursement Plans (Health + Home Office)


S corporations can reimburse shareholders and employees for out-of-pocket expenses (including a home office) through an accountable plan.


- Reimbursements are deductible to the business and non-taxable to the employee
- Must follow IRS rules (written plan, substantiation, and timely reimbursement)
- Common for internet, utilities, home office square footage, and mileage


### 26. Payroll Tax Write-Offs


Unlike sole proprietors, S-Corp owners are considered employees. That means the business pays[payroll taxes](https://www.taxgpt.com/blog/common-payroll-tax-mistakes-and-how-to-avoid-them) , and those employer-paid amounts are fully deductible.


- Includes employer share of Social Security, Medicare, and FUTA taxes
- Must pay yourself a reasonable salary to stay compliant


This setup also reduces the owner's exposure to self-employment tax on profits.


### 27. Owner Fringe Benefits


Fringe benefits can be a gray area for S corps, especially for shareholders who own more than 2%. However, when structured properly:


- Health insurance premiums are deductible if included on W-2 (Box 1)
- Retirement contributions through a Solo 401(k) or SEP IRA are deductible as employer expenses only if they are based on the shareholders’s W-2 wages.
- Disability and life insurance may be deductible, but can create income to the shareholder


***Important*** *: Always distinguish between employee-shareholders and regular employees in your plan documents.*


### For C Corporations


C corporations file Form 1120 and pay taxes at the corporate level. While subject to double taxation (corporate tax + dividend tax), they offer greater flexibility in fringe benefits and tax planning.


### 28. Defined Benefit Pension Contributions (Cash Balance Plans)


C corps can sponsor cash balance pension plans and contribute well over $100,000 per year, depending on the owner’s age and income.


- Contributions are fully deductible to the corporation
- Not limited like traditional 401(k) plans
- Ideal for high-income owners seeking to defer more tax


These plans require actuarial oversight and are best set up with an experienced advisor.


### 29. Charitable Contributions


C corps can deduct up to 10% of taxable income for qualified charitable donations.


- Includes cash, goods, or inventory
- 25% limit applies for food inventory donations to qualified nonprofits
- Must receive written acknowledgment for gifts of $250+


Excess donations can be carried forward up to five years.


### 30. Tax Credits (e.g., WOTC, Health Insurance Credit)


C corporations have access to several powerful tax credits, including:


- Work Opportunity Tax Credit (WOTC) for hiring individuals from targeted groups
- Small Business Health Care Tax Credit for providing employee health insurance
- [Research & Development (R&D) Credit](https://www.taxgpt.com/blog/r-and-d-tax-credits) for qualifying innovation investments


These reduce your tax liability dollar-for-dollar, unlike deductions, which reduce taxable income.

### Structuring for Maximum Benefit


If your business has grown past the hobby or side-hustle stage, it may be time to evaluate whether your current[entity structure](https://www.taxgpt.com/blog/c-corp-s-corp-or-llc-choose-the-right-structure-for-tax-efficiency) still serves your goals. Deductions often hinge not on *what* you're spending, but on *how* your business is formed.


- Sole proprietors may want to consider forming an S corporation once net profits exceed their reasonable compensation threshold.
- Partnerships should carefully review their operating agreement to ensure deductions are distributed appropriately.
- C corporations should balance reinvestment strategies with dividend planning to avoid unnecessary double taxation.


## Hidden or Underused Deductions


### 31. App Expenses


Deductible items include:


- Paid apps used for marketing, scheduling, bookkeeping, communication, and collaboration (e.g., Slack, Zoom, Trello, Notion, ChatGPT) are generally deductible under Section 162 of the IRC.
- Cloud storage (e.g., iCloud, Google Drive, Dropbox)
- Device purchases (phones, tablets) must generally be capitalized but may qualify for depreciation or Section 179 expensing.


### 32. Health Savings Account (HSA) Contributions


If you're enrolled in a high-deductible health plan (HDHP), contributing to an HSA offers a triple win:


- Tax-deductible contributions
- Tax-free growth
- Tax-free withdrawals for medical expenses


This deduction applies whether you’re self-employed or your business offers HDHPs to employees.


#### 2025 HSA contribution limits:


- Individuals: $4,300
- Families: $8,550
- Additional catch-up (age 55+): $1,000


HSA funds can be used for a wide range of expenses from doctor visits to eyeglasses, dental work, and even some wellness services.


### 33. Tax Preparation Fees


If you pay for help preparing your business taxes, those fees are fully deductible.


Qualifying fees include:


- CPA or EA services for your business filings
- Online software
- Bookkeeping support or tax strategy consultations
- IRS representation fees related to your business return


*Note: Only business-related prep is deductible. For sole proprietors, this means the Schedule C portion of your tax prep, not the personal 1040.*


### 34. Business Loan Interest (Not Principal)


This is a common mistake: Many business owners think they can deduct their full loan payments, but you can only deduct the interest portion.


Deductible interest types:


- Loans for business equipment or vehicles
- Credit card interest on business purchases
- Interest on lines of credit or SBA loans
- Mortgage interest on commercial property


Not deductible:


- Loan principal
- Refinancing fees (unless amortized)


***Tip:*** *Use your amortization schedule or year-end statements from lenders to isolate interest paid.*


### 35. The “Create-a-Deduction” Strategy


This includes timing investments, using financial tools, and converting personal expenses into business benefits.


Some examples:


- **Donor-Advised Funds (DAFs):** Donate appreciated stock or cash to a DAF. Get an immediate deduction this year, and distribute funds to charities later.
- **Asset Timing:** Buy eligible equipment before year-end to trigger bonus depreciation or Section 179 expensing.
- **Reinvested Profits:** Put year-end profits into marketing, training, or hiring to reduce taxable income while fueling future growth.
- **Professional Education:** Prepay next year’s business workshops or certifications now for a current-year deduction.
