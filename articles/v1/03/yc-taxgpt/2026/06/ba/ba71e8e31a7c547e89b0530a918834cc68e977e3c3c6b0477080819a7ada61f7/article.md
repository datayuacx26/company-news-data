# Form 8889 Checklist for Tax Professionals

## HSA Reporting Shouldn’t Feel Like a Landmine, But It Often Is


Form 8889 is deceptively short, but every line hides a rule, safe harbor, or exception that can trigger underreported income, miscalculated deductions, or assessments years later.


Miss one month of eligibility? Your client’s deduction shrinks.


Misapply the last-month rule? You trigger unexpected income plus a 10% additional tax.


Overlook an employer contribution on the W-2? Now you’ve created an excess contribution problem, and potentially a 6% excise tax on Form 5329.


For clients juggling mid-year plan switches, late-year HSA openings, or nonqualified distributions, a sloppy Form 8889 can mean thousands in disallowed deductions or penalties. This checklist eliminates those risks by walking you through every eligibility rule, contribution limit, distribution category, and excess reconciliation, so your HSA filings are accurate.


## Before You Start: Pre-Filing Document Collection


Gather these before you touch Form 8889:


☐ Form 5498-SA, Verify total contributions and year-end FMV (often received in May)


☐ Form 1099-SA, Gross distributions, earnings, and distribution codes


☐ Form W-2, Box 12 Code W, Employer + cafeteria plan HSA contributions


☐ HDHP Plan Documentation, Coverage months, self-only/family, deductible & OOP max


☐ Taxpayer Contribution Records, Including amounts made by return due date


☐ Documentation for Rollovers/Transfers, HSA↔HSA, Archer MSA→HSA


☐ IRA→HSA Qualified HSA Funding Distribution Proof, Confirm one-time rule


☐ Receipts for Qualified Medical Expenses, Must be after HSA establishment


If eligibility or plan classification is unclear,[ask TaxGPT Research](https://www.taxgpt.com/tax-research-software) to confirm “eligible individual” status rules, HDHP requirements, and safe harbors with citations before you begin.


## Form 8889 Line-by-Line Preparation Checklist


### 1. Confirm HSA Eligibility and HDHP Status


- ☐ Confirm monthly “eligible individual” status


- HDHP coverage on the 1st day of each month
- No disqualifying other coverage
- Not Medicare-enrolled
- Not a dependent of another taxpayer


- ☐ Document monthly coverage type (self-only vs. family)
- ☐ Confirm plan meets HDHP thresholds


- For 2025 (Rev. Proc. 2024-25):


- Minimum deductible: $1,650 / $3,300 (self/family)
- OOP max: $8,300 / $16,600


- ☐ Apply safe harbors correctly


- Preventive care
- Chronic-care safe harbor
- Telehealth relief periods
- Use Pub. 969 to verify.


- ☐ Determine if the last-month rule applies


- If used, confirm the 12-month testing period was satisfied


Use[TaxGPT Research](https://www.taxgpt.com/tax-research-software) to validate monthly eligibility, last-month rule testing period rules, and HDHP safe harbor coverage with citations.


### 2. Gather All Supporting Source Documents


☐ Form 5498-SA


☐ Form 1099-SA


☐ W-2 (Code W)


☐ HDHP plan documentation


☐ Taxpayer and third-party contribution records


☐ Rollover and trustee-to-trustee transfer records


☐ IRA→HSA Qualified Funding Distribution proof


☐ Receipts for qualified medical expenses


### 3. Verify Annual Limits and Contribution Timing


☐ Use correct annual HSA limits for 2025:


- $4,300 self-only
- $8,550 family
- Catch-up (55+): $1,000


☐ Prorate contributions by eligible month unless last-month rule applies


☐ Include all contributions


- Taxpayer
- Someone else on their behalf
- Employer (Code W)


☐ Exclude from limit


- Rollovers
- Trustee-to-trustee transfers


☐ If IRA→HSA transfer occurred:


- Verify one-time rule
- Apply against limit
- Confirm testing period satisfaction


### 4. Review Distributions and Qualified Expenses


☐ Reconcile Form 1099-SA distributions with client expense records


☐ Verify only unreimbursed qualified medical expenses included


☐ Confirm expenses incurred after HSA establishment


☐ Identify nonqualified expenses → taxable + 20% additional tax unless exception


☐ Confirm exceptions: disability, death, or age 65+ (removes 20% tax but not income inclusion)


If a distribution category or expense qualification is unclear,[ask TaxGPT Research](https://www.taxgpt.com/tax-research-software) to confirm whether an expense is qualified and whether an exception applies.


### 5. Detect and Resolve Excess Contributions


☐ Compare total contributions to annual limits


☐ Identify excess contributions


☐ Determine if excess will be withdrawn by the return due date


☐ Compute 6% excise tax using Form 5329 Part VII if excess remains


☐ Adjust Form 8889 contributions and deductions accordingly


### 6. Special Situations to Screen


☐ Married taxpayers: Each spouse must have their own HSA; family limit split by agreement


☐ Mid-year coverage switches: Recap monthly contribution limits


☐ Last-month rule failures: Compute recapture in Part III of Form 8889


☐ Death of HSA owner:


- Spouse beneficiary = HSA continues
- Non-spouse = FMV becomes income to beneficiary
