# Year-End Business Tax Compliance Checklist for 2025

It’s late December. Your inbox is overflowing, your finance team is unreachable, and you’ve just discovered a missed payroll filing from August.


Sounds familiar?


For many business owners,[year-end](https://www.taxgpt.com/blog/year-end-tax-planning-guide) tax compliance becomes a last-minute fire drill. But starting early gives you time to organize your records, adapt to new 2025 tax changes, and avoid costly penalties that often go unnoticed until it’s too late.


This guide is your go-to 2025 year-end[business tax compliance](https://www.taxgpt.com/blog/business-tax-compliance-guide) checklist, built to help you stay ahead, IRS-compliant, and close the year with peace of mind.


## Year-End Financial Review and Tax Planning


Before you send that final invoice or log off for the holidays, it’s necessary to close out your accurate 2025 financials. Let me walk you through key tasks to review your books, lock in tax savings, and position your business for a smooth 2026 tax season.


### Close the books and organize records


#### 1. Review core financial statements


Pull your profit and loss statement, balance sheet, and cash flow report for 2025. Make sure all income and expense entries are accurate and complete. Cross-check against bank and credit card statements to reconcile balances and flag any discrepancies before tax filing.


#### 2. Reconcile accounts receivable, payables, and inventory


- Confirm that your accounts receivable are current and assess whether to write off uncollectible customer balances.
- Review accounts payable and verify that all liabilities (including bills due in early January) have been entered.
- If you sell goods, do a physical inventory count and adjust for losses, spoilage, or shrinkage. Obsolete inventory can often be written off.


#### 3. Organize documentation


Ensure you have neatly filed or digitized records of:


- Receipts for deductible expenses
- Loan agreements and repayments
- Vendor invoices and payments
- Payroll reports and bonus approvals
- Asset purchases and sales


### Time year-end transactions for maximum tax benefit


Once your books are in order, you can make strategic moves before December 31 to influence your 2025 tax liability. Timing is everything, and these adjustments can help reduce what you owe.


#### 1. Time income and expenses strategically


If your business uses cash accounting, consider whether to:


- Delay income by postponing late-December invoices to early January (pushing revenue into 2026).
- Accelerate expenses by paying early for supplies, software, or rent to claim deductions in 2025. This can be especially helpful if you expect a higher income next year or changes to your deduction limits in 2026.


*Want a deeper dive into how to time revenue and expenses to your advantage?*[Explore strategies for timing income and deductions effectively](https://www.taxgpt.com/blog/timing-income-and-expenses-strategies) *to minimize your tax liability and avoid surprises in 2026.*


#### 2. Make final-year asset purchases


Planning to upgrade your equipment or buy new software? Do it before December 31. You may qualify for:


- Section 179 expensing — Deduct up to $1,250,000 of qualifying equipment placed in service by year-end (2025 limit).
- Bonus depreciation — Deduct 40% of qualified new asset costs upfront in 2025 (dropping from 60% in 2024). These provisions let you deduct rather than depreciate over time, which can significantly reduce your taxable income.


#### 3. Adjust your Q4 estimated tax payment


If your profits were much higher (or lower) than expected in 2025, review your estimated tax obligations before January 15, 2026. Adjusting your Q4 payment can help:


- Avoid[underpayment penalties](https://www.taxgpt.com/blog/manage-tax-brackets-and-withholding-to-avoid-penalties)
- Catch up if your earlier estimates were too low
- Preserve cash flow if you overpaid earlier in the year


#### 4. Evaluate owner compensation and distributions


If you're an owner of an S-corp or partnership:


- Make sure your owner's salary is reasonable and compliant with IRS expectations.
- Double-check that you have enough basis for any distributions or losses you plan to deduct.
- Consider making capital contributions before year-end to support deductions or profit allocations.


### Maximize deductions and credits before December 31


Before the year wraps, make sure to lock in every eligible deduction and credit to reduce your 2025 tax bill. Even small moves can add up to big savings.


#### 1. Review all deductible business expenses


Go line by line through your general ledger or accounting software to confirm that all legitimate business expenses are captured:


- Office supplies, rent, utilities, and marketing costs
- Business insurance premiums
- Contractor and legal fees
- Subscriptions, licenses, and software
- [Home office expenses](https://www.taxgpt.com/blog/calculating-home-office-deductions-without-triggering-audits) (if applicable)
- Business use of vehicle: The IRS standard mileage rate for 2025 is 70¢ per mile (up from 67¢ in 2024). Be sure your logs are current, especially if you're a sole proprietor or single-member LLC.


#### 2. Make charitable contributions


If your business plans to donate to qualified 501(c)(3) organizations, do so by December 31 to deduct the amount on your 2025 return.


- C corporations can deduct charitable donations up to 10% of taxable income.
- Pass-through entities (S-corps, partnerships, LLCs) pass the deduction to individual owners. Keep documentation such as receipts or acknowledgment letters on file as the IRS requires proof for all donations.


#### 3. Claim available tax credits


Credits directly reduce your tax liability, sometimes dollar for dollar. Here are a few to check:


- **Small Business Health Care Tax Credit:** If you cover employee premiums via a SHOP plan, have fewer than 25 FTEs, and meet wage/payment thresholds (avg. wages under ~$56,000), you may qualify for up to 50% credit on premiums paid.
- **Work Opportunity Tax Credit (WOTC):** Available for hiring certain veterans, ex-felons, and long-term unemployed individuals.
- **R&D Tax Credit:** If your business developed new products, software, or processes this year,[R&D credit](https://www.taxgpt.com/blog/r-and-d-tax-credits) may apply.
- Look for industry-specific or energy efficiency credits, too, they vary by sector and location.


#### 4. Evaluate accounting methods and year-end elections


Consider small elections or changes that could streamline reporting or reduce taxable income:


- **De minimis safe harbor:** Allows immediate expensing of items up to $2,500 per invoice (or $5,000 with audited financials) rather than capitalizing. Must have a written policy in place.
- **Repair vs. improvement safe harbor:** If you made upgrades, make sure they’re properly classified for potential write-offs.
- **Qualified Improvement Property (QIP):** Improvements made to non-residential interior space may still qualify for accelerated depreciation.


#### 5. Take business owner-specific actions


Don’t leave owner-level deductions on the table:


- Maximize contributions to retirement accounts such as a[Solo 401(k), SEP IRA, or SIMPLE IRA](https://www.taxgpt.com/answer/is-there-a-limit-on-how-much-i-can-contribute-to-my-retirement-plan-as-a-self-employed-person) .
- If you’re an S-corp owner, double-check that your wages vs. distributions are reasonable and properly classified.
- Ensure you have enough basis to claim losses or take distributions. If not, consider contributing additional capital before year-end.


## Payroll and Employment Tax Compliance


As the calendar turns toward year-end, it’s super important to make sure every dollar paid and tax withheld in 2025 is properly reconciled and reported. Inaccurate payroll records can trigger costly IRS penalties, W-2 errors, or state compliance issues that spill over into 2026. *(Avoid these headaches by learning the most*[common payroll tax mistakes and how to avoid them](https://www.taxgpt.com/blog/common-payroll-tax-mistakes-and-how-to-avoid-them) *before year-end.)*


Use the checklist below to tie up payroll compliance:


### Reconcile Payroll Records and Year-End Totals


#### 1. Match payroll totals across systems


Cross-check total wages, withholdings, and employer taxes paid in 2025 across:


- Your payroll provider or software
- General ledger
- Quarterly IRS Forms 941
- State and local wage and tax reports


Correct any inconsistencies before W-2s are finalized. If a wage amount was underreported or a tax payment missed a deadline, file an adjustment or amended return immediately.


#### 2. Confirm all tax deposits are complete


Make sure you’ve made all required deposits for:


- Federal payroll taxes (FIT, Social Security, Medicare via EFTPS)
- State and local withholding (per jurisdiction rules)
- FUTA (unemployment tax) and SUTA (state UI)


The final federal deposit for 2025 wages (including year-end bonuses) may be due as early as January 2, 2026, depending on your deposit schedule. A missed or short deposit (even by a day) can result in penalties, so it’s worth double-checking your EFTPS and state deposit records now.


*Need a refresher on the rules for federal and state tax deposits? Read more about*[how to properly withhold and deposit payroll taxes](https://www.taxgpt.com/blog/guide-to-withholding-depositing-payroll-taxes) *.*


#### 3. Include all taxable benefits on 2025 W-2s


Ensure that the value of any non-cash benefits provided in 2025 is included in employees’ taxable income. These must be reported accurately to meet IRS requirements. Examples include:


- Personal use of company vehicles
- Employer-paid life insurance > $50,000
- Third-party sick pay (if not already reported)
- Group health premiums for 2%+ S corp shareholders
- Gift cards or year-end rewards that are not de minimis


#### 4. Process bonuses and final corrections


- If you issued year-end bonuses, verify they were taxed properly (withholding, FICA, FUTA).
- Adjust for any over- or under-withheld taxes. For example, if an employee exceeded the 2025 Social Security wage base of $176,100, stop withholding Social Security tax beyond that threshold.
- Review and void any paychecks issued in error, then confirm all wage totals and tax withholdings reflect only actual pay for the year.


This ensures the W-2s issued in January are accurate and prevent IRS matching issues or amended filings down the line.


### Prepare W-2s, 1099s, and Other Year-End Forms


Errors or delays in W-2 or 1099 filings can lead to IRS notices, penalties, and frustrated workers or contractors. Avoid that stress with a well-organized checklist!


#### Verify Worker and Contractor Information Early


Before generating any forms, ensure your records are accurate:


- Run a report listing employee names, Social Security Numbers (SSNs), and addresses. Double-check these against payroll files and W-4s.
- For independent contractors, validate their names, addresses, and Taxpayer Identification Numbers (TINs) using W-9s on file.
- Correct any mismatches or typos before processing forms, this helps avoid IRS rejections or mailing delays.


#### Prepare and Distribute W-2 Forms (Deadline: January 31, 2026)


Every employee who worked for you in 2025 must receive a W-2 summarizing their:


- Total wages, tips, and bonuses paid
- Federal and state income taxes withheld
- Social Security and Medicare tax contributions


#### File 1099-NEC and 1099-MISC for Nonemployees (Deadline: January 31, 2026)


Issue Form 1099-NEC to each contractor or freelancer you paid $600 or more for services in 2025. This applies to individuals, sole proprietors, and partnerships, not corporations, unless you're paying attorneys or medical professionals.


If you made other payments (e.g., rent, royalties, prizes, attorney fees), use Form 1099-MISC where applicable.


*Not sure if someone should receive a W-2 or a 1099?*[Use this quick guide to properly classify employees vs. independent contractors](https://www.taxgpt.com/blog/w-2-vs-1099-proper-classification-of-employees-contractors) *and avoid costly misclassification penalties.*


#### Complete Payroll Tax Returns by January 31, 2026


You’ll need to submit several final federal and state forms to close out the year:


- Form 941 (Q4): Reports payroll taxes withheld and paid. Due January 31.
- Form 940: Annual FUTA unemployment tax return. Also due January 31.
- State unemployment (SUI) annual filings: Deadlines and formats vary (check your state’s requirements now).


Even if you use a payroll service, it's ultimately your responsibility to ensure forms are filed correctly and on time.


#### ACA Reporting for Applicable Large Employers (ALEs)


If your business averaged 50+ full-time equivalent employees in 2025, you must:


- Provide Form 1095-C to employees by March 3, 2026 (if mailed)
- E-file forms with the IRS by March 31, 2026


Smaller businesses with self-insured health plans may also have 1095-B or 1095-C obligations. Start compiling coverage records now to avoid compliance gaps.


#### Report Any Backup Withholding on Form 945


If you were required to withhold 24% federal tax from any contractor payments (typically due to missing or invalid TINs), you must:


- Report those amounts on the contractor’s 1099-NEC
- File Form 945 by January 31, 2026, to report and remit withheld taxes


Late or missed 945 filings can result in penalties, so check your vendor list carefully, especially if you flagged anyone for backup withholding earlier in the year.


[Read more about backup withholding tax here.](https://www.taxgpt.com/answer/what-is-backup-withholding-tax)


### Update Payroll for the New Year


Once 2025 reporting is wrapped, it’s time to prepare your payroll system for 2026. New tax thresholds, wage rules, and employee elections kick in with the first payroll run of the year, and failing to update these can cause compliance headaches or payroll errors.


#### Implement New Tax Rates and Thresholds


- **Update federal tax tables:** The IRS releases new income tax withholding tables each year. Make sure your payroll software reflects the 2026 federal tables before running January payroll.
- **Adjust for Social Security wage base:** The 2026 figure will likely be higher than $176,100 (to be announced by the SSA in fall 2025). Update the cap in your system so excess wages don’t continue to be taxed for Social Security once an employee crosses the threshold.
- **Incorporate state/local updates:** Many states and municipalities change withholding formulas or flat rate percentages each year. Check for updates in your operating states and revise accordingly.


#### Adjust for Minimum Wage Changes


- Effective January 1, 2026, several states and cities are expected to increase minimum wage rates. Review your workforce (especially if you employ hourly staff or tipped workers) to ensure wages meet updated thresholds in each jurisdiction.
- Remember: Failing to apply the correct minimum wage not only violates labor laws but may also result in retroactive wage penalties or wage claim liability.


#### Apply New State Unemployment Insurance (SUI) Rates


- State unemployment tax rates (SUI) are often reassigned annually based on your claims history.
- Expect your 2026 rate notice to arrive by mail or online in December 2025 or early January. Input the correct rate into your system before processing the first payroll of 2026.
- Also update any state disability insurance (SDI) rates or local payroll tax rates if applicable.


#### Refresh Employee Benefits and Deductions


- Open enrollment elections (health, dental, FSA, etc.) usually take effect with the first January paycheck.
- Confirm your system reflects:


1. New health insurance contribution amounts
2. Updated retirement plan deferrals (e.g., 401(k), SIMPLE IRA)
3. Any voluntary benefit premiums or deductions


- The 401(k) elective deferral limit for 2025 increased to $23,500 (with a $7,500 catch-up for those age 50+). Watch for IRS announcements for 2026 limits and allow employees to adjust if needed.


#### Prompt Employees to Review Withholding and Address Info


- Encourage employees to submit a new Form W-4 if they experienced major life changes in 2025 (e.g., marriage, new dependents, large tax bill or refund).
- Collect updated mailing addresses from employees who moved—essential for timely and accurate W-2 delivery.


#### Reconcile and Reset Leave Balances


If your PTO or vacation policy tracks balances on a calendar-year basis, now’s the time to:


1. Review unused leave balances
2. Apply your company’s carryover or “use it or lose it” policy
3. Reset accruals for 2026


This is more of an HR compliance step than a tax one, but it’s crucial for avoiding disputes over leave payouts and ensuring payroll reflects correct leave availability for the new year.


## State and Local Compliance Essentials


Federal taxes get most of the attention, but ignoring state-level obligations is a costly mistake. Whether you operate in one state or many, the end of the year is your chance to reconcile state filings, finalize payments, and prepare for deadlines in early 2026. Here’s how to cover your bases:


### State Business Tax Obligations


#### 1. File State Income or Franchise Taxes


- Corporations and LLCs taxed as corporations should review their 2025 estimated state income tax payments. Most states align with the federal quarterly schedule, meaning the final payment is due January 15, 2026.
- Pass-through entities in SALT workaround states (like CA, NY, NJ, AZ, etc.) should finalize their 2025 pass-through entity tax (PTET) payments if elected. Missing the cutoff can disqualify your deduction.
- Start compiling state-specific return documents now, especially if your state’s filing deadline is in March or April 2026. If your tax year isn’t calendar-aligned, be sure you know your entity-specific due dates.


*Not sure exactly when your federal or state tax returns are due or how to file for more time?*[This corporate tax deadline guide breaks down due dates and extension options by entity type](https://www.taxgpt.com/blog/corporate-tax-filing-deadlines-extensions) *, helping you stay on track.*


#### 2. Comply with Franchise or Business Privilege Tax Requirements


- States like Delaware (March 1), Texas (May 15), and Alabama (March 15) require annual franchise or business privilege tax returns.
- These may be based on net worth, total assets, or gross receipts, and are separate from income tax filings.
- If your business is registered (aka foreign qualified) in multiple states, check if multiple franchise tax filings are due.
- Begin collecting the necessary capital account data or prior year return copies to streamline early 2026 filing.


#### 3. Reconcile Sales and Use Tax


- Double-check 2025 filings: Ensure all monthly/quarterly sales tax returns are filed and taxes remitted.
- Annual filers should note their return is likely due in January 2026 so start prepping it now.
- Run a report of non-taxed purchases in 2025. If your business bought equipment or supplies from out-of-state vendors without paying sales tax, you likely owe use tax. For example, you purchased a $10,000 printer from an out-of-state supplier that didn’t collect sales tax. You may owe $600–1,000 in state use tax, depending on your jurisdiction.


*If your business expanded into new states or started selling online in 2025, it’s also important to understand where you’ve triggered nexus. This guide explains*[how sales tax nexus works and where you're required to collect](https://www.taxgpt.com/blog/sales-tax-nexus-when-where-you-must-collect) *.*


#### 4. Complete State Payroll Tax Reconciliations


- Verify that all 2025 state income tax and unemployment insurance payments have been made.
- Most states require an annual wage reconciliation, matching the total 2025 payroll with what you’ve withheld and reported.
- Submit required forms (like state W-2 copies, state annual withholding summaries, or annual unemployment reports) by your state’s deadline, often January 31, 2026.
- In states with FUTA credit reductions (e.g., California or New York, depending on the 2025 UI trust fund status), calculate and pay any adjustment before the deadline.


#### 5. Account for Industry-Specific or Special Taxes


- Some states require additional filings depending on your business type:


1. Excise tax for fuel, alcohol, tobacco, or cannabis
2. Gross receipts tax (e.g., Nevada, Ohio, Washington)
3. Severance tax for resource extraction (e.g., in Texas or North Dakota)
4. Commercial vehicle highway use tax filings (e.g., NY HUT returns)


Review 2025 activity to determine if a true-up, special report, or year-end return is required. If your CPA isn’t managing this directly, build it into your compliance calendar.
