# Retail & E-Commerce Tax Guide 2025

## 5. International Expansion


Going global is a growth opportunity but it also invites a tangle of tax regimes, registration thresholds, and customs rules. Whether you're shipping physical goods abroad or offering digital products to overseas customers, international tax compliance is no longer optional.


From navigating VAT and GST systems to understanding how Section 321 de minimis limits affect your fulfillment strategy, this section helps you manage risk and stay profitable as you scale internationally.


*For foundational differences between VAT and GST systems, see our full*[VAT vs. GST guide](https://www.taxgpt.com/blog/vat-vs-gst-key-differences-for-international-e-commerce) *.*


### 5.1 Import Duties & Section 321 De Minimis


If you're shipping U.S. goods to international customers or importing products into the U.S. for resale, customs duties can eat into your margins quickly. Fortunately, Section 321 of the U.S. Tariff Act provides a major benefit for small shipments:


- **Section 321 de minimis threshold:** Allows duty-free import of shipments valued at $800 or less per day per recipient.
- **Applies to B2C cross-border sellers** who drop-ship from overseas warehouses (China, EU) to U.S. customers.
- **Limitations:** Must be a *qualifying entry* (one shipment per day per customer), and cannot be split to avoid duties. Certain restricted goods are excluded.


For retailers using third-party fulfillment (e.g., Cainiao, SF Express, DHL eCommerce), it’s critical that your carrier is properly classifying the shipment under Section 321. Otherwise, duties and delays can pile up at the border.


### 5.2 VAT / GST Registration Thresholds (EU, UK, CA)


Once you sell into foreign jurisdictions, you may need to register for VAT or GST and collect taxes at checkout. Unlike U.S. sales tax, these systems are destination-based and usually collected by the seller, not the buyer.


**Key registration thresholds:**


- European Union (EU OSS):


- €10,000 in total intra-EU distance sales triggers VAT registration under the One Stop Shop (OSS) regime.
- File one return covering all EU member states.


- United Kingdom (UK):


- No threshold for non-UK sellers.
- If you sell goods shipped into the UK (or use UK fulfillment centers), you must register from your first sale.


- Canada (GST/HST):


- C$30,000 (approx. USD $22,000) annual threshold for non-resident digital or physical sellers.
- Above that, registration is required, and you must charge GST or HST depending on the province.


### 5.3 Cross-border Returns & Delivered-Duty-Paid (DDP) Models


Returns and refunds become exponentially more complex in international commerce, especially when duties, taxes, and shipping fees are involved.


Two primary fulfillment models determine your exposure:


- **Delivered Duty Paid (DDP):**


- Seller takes responsibility for all duties, taxes, and customs clearance.
- Improves customer experience but increases cost and complexity.
- DDP returns often require tax refund claims in the destination country.


- **Delivered At Place (DAP):**


- Buyer pays duties on arrival.
- Reduces seller burden but can lead to cart abandonment or failed delivery due to surprise costs.


If you offer free returns, you must decide whether to absorb tax and duty losses or request refund documentation from customs authorities, a process that can take weeks and may require detailed paperwork.


### 5.4 Foreign Digital-Service Taxes & Marketplace Rules


If you sell digital goods, subscriptions, or SaaS to international customers, many countries now impose digital services taxes (DSTs) or VAT-equivalent obligations on non-resident sellers.


Key developments to track:


- **EU & UK:** Digital services (eBooks, online courses, SaaS) are subject to VAT at the buyer’s location — even if the seller has no presence in that country.
- **Australia & New Zealand:** Require immediate GST registration once threshold is hit and enforce compliance through platform reporting and penalties.
- **India, Turkey, France, and Italy:** Apply DSTs (2%–7.5%) on digital revenues from local users. While technically aimed at large tech platforms, enforcement may extend to mid-sized digital sellers.


In many cases, marketplaces are deemed tax collectors under foreign law:


- Amazon UK and EU, for instance, are required to collect VAT for non-resident sellers shipping into their marketplaces.
- Etsy, eBay, and Shopify Markets have built-in VAT tools but you remain responsible for registering, reporting, and reconciling if you operate multichannel.


## 6. Payroll & Marketplace Reporting


Retailers are now subject to a growing web of third-party reporting requirements and payroll-linked tax rules. From the shifting federal 1099-K thresholds to new state-level mandates and overlooked credits tied to in-store labor, these compliance layers affect your audit risk, staffing strategy, and tax refund potential.


Let’s unpack what changed in 2025 and how to stay ahead.


### 6.1 1099-K Threshold Changes:


The IRS’s planned enforcement of the $600 1099-K reporting threshold has been delayed again in 2025, but with a major adjustment: a $2,500 phased-in threshold now applies for the year.


Here’s what that means:


- **Who gets a 1099-K?** Anyone who earns $2,500 or more in gross payments via third-party processors (Stripe, PayPal, Venmo, Amazon, Etsy, Shopify Payments) will receive a Form 1099-K for 2025.
- **What’s reported?** *Gross* transaction volume, not net of returns, fees, or shipping.
- **Why it matters:** Many casual sellers (especially on marketplaces) may now receive 1099-Ks for the first time, leading to confusion at tax time or duplicate income reporting if not properly reconciled.


Action step for retailers:


- Match 1099-K totals to internal books, separating business vs. personal accounts.
- Educate small third-party vendors or dropship partners who may be unaware they’ll receive these forms.


### 6.2 State Marketplace Information Return Mandates


Individual states are creating their own marketplace reporting obligations, often targeting marketplaces and payment platforms, but also affecting sellers who operate across multiple jurisdictions.


Emerging trends:


- Colorado, Massachusetts, and California now require annual or quarterly information returns from platforms and, in some cases, sellers even if tax isn’t owed.
- Reporting includes seller identity, gross receipts by state, and product categories sold.
- Failure to file may trigger estimated assessments, sales tax notices, or withholding penalties even for compliant businesses.


If you sell through Shopify, Amazon, or multi-channel apps, ensure your platform:


- Supports state-level reporting exports
- Separates taxable vs. exempt sales by destination
- Flags non-taxable channels to avoid overreporting


***Pro tip:*** *Add a quarterly task to download your state-by-state gross receipts reports from each sales platform. Use these to preempt state notices and reconcile against your nexus exposure matrix.*


### 6.3 ERC & Tip Credit Opportunities for Brick-and-Mortar Staff


If you operate physical retail stores or hybrid models with in-person staff, don’t overlook payroll-linked tax credits still available in 2025:


#### Employee Retention Credit (ERC), Still Amendable


- While new ERC claims are no longer allowed for 2021 and earlier, you can still file amended returns (Form 941-X) until April 15, 2025 (for 2021 Q1).
- Retailers hit by supply chain disruptions, reduced operating hours, or occupancy mandates in 2020–21 may still qualify even if revenue didn’t drop by 50%.
- Average credit: $5,000–$21,000 per employee, depending on eligible periods.


**Caution:** Due to IRS backlog and fraud concerns, ERC audits have increased. Maintain payroll records, government mandate documentation, and revenue comparisons if claiming retroactively.


#### FICA Tip Credit, Often Missed by Retailers


- If you operate restaurants, cafes, or retail locations where tipping occurs, you may be eligible for the FICA Tip Credit under § 45B.
- This credit offsets the employer’s share of Social Security and Medicare taxes paid on tips reported by employees, over and above minimum wage.


**Eligibility note:**


- Employees must earn at least the federal minimum wage *excluding tips* for the credit to apply.
- Applies to both cash and credit-card tips.


**Action step:** Coordinate with your payroll provider or CPA to ensure:


- Tip wages are properly classified and reported
- Eligible tip credits are applied at year-end or on quarterly returns


## 7. Tax Credits & Deductions


While much of retail tax planning focuses on compliance and cost control, strategic use of tax credits and accelerated deductions can significantly reduce your federal and state tax liability. These incentives go beyond traditional manufacturing or tech sectors and many are tailor-made for retail, e-commerce, logistics, and hybrid businesses.


Here’s how to unlock meaningful cash flow savings by tapping into underused credits and deductions in 2025.


### 7.1 R&D Credit for Custom Site and App Development


Contrary to popular belief, the Research & Development (R&D) Tax Credit isn’t just for labs or biotech firms. If your retail or e-commerce business builds proprietary technology, including websites, shopping platforms, or custom integrations, you likely qualify for this powerful incentive.


Key qualifying activities:


- Designing or coding custom e-commerce platforms, product configurators, or checkout workflows
- Developing mobile shopping apps, APIs, or integrations with payment or inventory systems
- Running A/B tests or UX optimizations involving technical experimentation
- Building logistics or fulfillment automation in-house


See our full[R&D Credit Guide](https://www.taxgpt.com/blog/r-and-d-tax-credits) to understand eligible expenses, recordkeeping tips, and how payroll R&D wages can reduce both income and payroll tax liability via Form 6765.


### 7.2 WOTC for Warehouse Hiring


Retailers operating fulfillment centers or seasonal warehouses can benefit from the Work Opportunity Tax Credit (WOTC), a federal incentive that rewards hiring individuals from specific target groups.


Eligible hires include:


- Long-term unemployed individuals
- SNAP (food stamp) recipients
- Formerly incarcerated individuals
- Qualified veterans and youth in designated zones


**Credit value** :


- Up to $2,400–$9,600 per employee, depending on the category
- Claimed via Form 5884 after filing a pre-screening (IRS Form 8850) with your state workforce agency within 28 days of hiring


Warehouse operations often attract candidates from WOTC-eligible populations but most retailers leave this credit unclaimed due to lack of screening processes.


### 7.3 § 179 Expensing & Bonus Depreciation for Fixtures and POS Hardware


Upgrading your retail locations or fulfillment hubs? Tax law allows you to immediately deduct the cost of many capital improvements and assets under § 179 and bonus depreciation rules.


Eligible assets include:


- POS systems and terminals
- Barcode scanners, mobile checkout devices, and receipt printers
- Display fixtures, shelving, and lighting installations
- Security systems and HVAC improvements


**§ 179 (2025 limits):**


- Up to $1.22 million in qualified asset purchases can be fully expensed (subject to income limitations)
- Phased out dollar-for-dollar once purchases exceed $3.05 million


**Bonus depreciation (2025):**


- 60% of the remaining cost (after § 179) can be deducted immediately for qualifying new or used assets


***Tip*** *: Use § 179 for smaller, income-limited entities; use bonus depreciation for large-scale investments or pass-throughs with enough income to absorb it.*


### 7.4 Green Energy & EV Credits for Last-Mile Delivery


Retailers investing in sustainable logistics or last-mile delivery may qualify for green energy tax credits, especially if deploying electric vehicles (EVs), solar charging stations, or alternative fuel infrastructure.


Key opportunities:


#### **Commercial EV Tax Credit (45W)**


- Up to $7,500–$40,000 per electric van, truck, or delivery vehicle
- Applies to new vehicles placed in service in 2023–2032
- Credit amount depends on vehicle weight class and battery size


#### **Alternative Fuel Refueling Property Credit (30C)**


- Up to $100,000 per location for EV chargers or hydrogen stations
- Must be installed in qualified census tracts (low-income or non-urban areas)


#### **State incentives:**


- Many states offer stackable credits or rebates for EV fleets (e.g., California’s HVIP, New York’s NYTVIP)


Bonus deduction: Solar panels, battery storage, and rooftop energy systems installed at warehouses or retail sites may qualify for Section 179D deductions or energy investment tax credits (ITC).


## 8. Risk Management & Audit Triggers


Retail tax audits result from inconsistent filings, mismatched data, or exposure you didn’t even know you had. Managing audit risk in 2025 means identifying and addressing the red flags before a state revenue agency or the IRS does.


### 8.1 Common Nexus-Audit Flags


As nexus rules continue to evolve post- *Wayfair* , tax authorities are increasingly using data analytics to flag businesses that appear to have exposure but aren’t filing.


Here are common triggers for **nexus-based audits** :


- **Marketplace + website combo:** You sell through Amazon (where tax is remitted) *and* your own Shopify store (where it’s not), but only report Amazon sales.
- **Inventory-based nexus:** Using FBA or WFS creates physical presence in dozens of states. If those states don’t see registration, they may initiate a desk audit.
- **Affiliate or marketing presence:** Paying local influencers or running geo-targeted ads may create economic nexus (or even agency nexus), especially in California and New York.
- **Threshold breaches without registration:** Once a state’s sales threshold is crossed and no return is filed, your account may be flagged automatically for audit review.


### 8.2 False-Positive Fraud Filters (ZIP mis-mapping)


One of the most overlooked audit risks is not fraud but false-positive fraud flags caused by bad address data. Most tax engines use ZIP code–based mapping to apply local tax rates, but in edge cases, ZIP boundaries don’t match jurisdiction boundaries.


Here are a few examples:


- **Denver, CO:** Parts of ZIP 80210 fall inside and outside city limits — tax overcollection or undercollection is common.
- **Chicago, IL and LA, CA:** Nested districts with transit or local add-ons can cause incorrect local tax rates to apply if address data is too broad.


Why this matters:


- Overcollection can trigger customer complaints and refund liabilities.
- Undercollection leads to remittance gaps, especially when states cross-check seller data with shipping destinations.


**Mitigation strategy:**


- Use geolocation tax APIs (e.g., based on rooftop coordinates) instead of ZIP-only rules.
- Flag high-risk ZIPs where boundaries cross jurisdictions and test invoices regularly in those zones.

### 8.4 Voluntary Disclosure Agreements (VDAs) & Amnesty Windows


If you discover that you’ve had nexus or tax exposure in a state for years but never registered or filed, don’t panic, you may qualify for a Voluntary Disclosure Agreement (VDA).


**What is a VDA?**


- A legal agreement between a business and a state where the business agrees to register and pay back taxes for a limited lookback period (usually 3–4 years).
- In return, the state waives penalties and doesn’t audit prior periods.


When to consider a VDA:


- You used FBA or WFS and only now realized you created nexus in states like TX, PA, or IL.
- You exceeded a state’s economic threshold in 2022 but never registered or filed.
- You’re preparing for a merger, investment, or sale and want to clean up exposure.


**Amnesty programs** : Some states periodically offer short-term tax amnesty windows that go even further, waiving penalties and interest, but they’re time-sensitive and less common.


***How to initiate:*** *Work through a CPA, legal advisor, or third-party provider. States often allow anonymous inquiries so you can explore options before disclosing your identity.*


## 9. Year-End Controller Checklist


Whether you’re running a lean in-house team or managing multiple locations and channels, this checklist helps ensure your year-end process is both airtight and opportunity-aware.


### 9.1 Reconcile Sales-Tax Liability to General Ledger (GL)


Start by matching your sales tax payable accounts to your actual liability across states.


Key steps:


- Aggregate monthly tax collected per state from POS/gateway reports (Shopify, Stripe, Amazon, etc.).
- Reconcile to GL balances and filed returns, discrepancies often arise from:


- Manual journal entries for prior period adjustments
- Timing gaps between collection and remittance
- Returns or discounts improperly netted


***Tip:*** *Create a summary sheet that shows tax collected vs. tax remitted by jurisdiction to flag any over- or under-payments. This not only supports audit readiness but also helps refine your filing cadence.*


### 9.2 Close Inventory & Apply § 263a Capitalization (Including LIFO Review)


As part of year-end financial prep, inventory must be closed accurately.


Steps include:


- **Inventory count & valuation:** Conduct a physical count or confirm perpetual inventory reconciliation.
- **Apply § 263A capitalization rules (if applicable):**


Capitalize indirect costs such as purchasing, storage, and handling onto ending inventory if not exempt under the § 471(c)[safe harbor](https://www.taxgpt.com/answer/what-is-the-safe-harbor-for-estimated-taxes-and-how-can-it-prevent-underpayment-penalties) .


- **LIFO vs. FIFO review:**


If using LIFO, assess for LIFO liquidation risk, where reduced year-end inventory levels trigger recapture of older, lower-cost layers, increasing taxable income.


***Strategy tip:*** *If LIFO liquidation is unavoidable, consider offsetting gains with year-end bonus depreciation or accrued expenses.*


### 9.3 Issue 1099-NEC to Influencers & Contractors


If you’ve hired influencers, freelance creatives, or third-party marketing consultants in 2025, the IRS requires you to issue Form 1099-NEC if you paid them $600 or more and they’re not classified as employees.


Year-end tasks:


- Collect W-9s before issuing payments, you’ll need legal names and TINs.
- Issue Form 1099-NEC by Jan 31, 2026.
- File copies with the IRS and applicable states, especially those that participate in the Combined Federal/State Filing Program.


Common 1099-NEC recipients in retail/ecommerce:


- Influencers (paid for product promotion or content)
- Freelance developers, designers, and copywriters
- Independent warehouse workers or pick-pack contractors
- Affiliate marketers (if paid outside a platform)


**Risk alert:** Failing to issue 1099s can result in $60–$310 penalties per form, plus potential audits of your classification policies.


### 9.4 Multistate Estimated Income-Tax Payments


If your retail operations generate income across multiple states, due to inventory, payroll, or economic nexus, you may be required to make estimated state income-tax payments.


Key tasks:


- Review your apportionment factors to project in-state income accurately
- Calculate Q4 state estimates due by Jan 15, 2026, or earlier for certain fiscal-year entities
- Account for non-conforming states (like CA, NY, and NJ) that do not fully align with federal tax timing or calculation rules


### 9.5 Verify Str / Lodging-Sales-Tax Filings (Where Applicable)


If your business includes short-term rentals (STRs), pop-up shops, or lodging services, don’t overlook local lodging or occupancy tax requirements.


Checklist:


- Confirm monthly or quarterly STR filings in platforms like Airbnb, Vrbo, or Booking.com haven’t missed any jurisdictions.
- For direct-booking sites, ensure sales tax, lodging tax, and tourism levies are properly separated on invoices.
- Some cities (e.g., NYC, Chicago, Miami) require local registration and tax returns, even if you're already filing state sales tax.


**Pro tip:** Double-check venue bookings for Q4 activations (e.g., holiday pop-ups, event-based lodging) to ensure tax treatment is consistent with local STR rules.


### FAQs


#### 1. Is shipping taxable in all states, or only if the product is taxable?


Shipping taxability depends on the state and how the invoice is structured. In over 25 states, if the underlying product is taxable, the shipping charge is too. Some states also tax handling or combine it with shipping, making the entire amount taxable. To avoid overcharging customers, separate shipping and handling line items and ensure your tax engine or POS software accurately tags shipping based on each state’s rules.


#### 2. How do I stay sales-tax compliant if I sell on Shopify and other platforms?


Shopify sellers are responsible for collecting the correct tax across all states where they have nexus. While Shopify offers automatic tax collection tools, it doesn't handle registration or filing. Multi-channel sellers must consolidate marketplace vs. direct sales, ensure product tax codes are accurate, and file separate returns where needed. Shopify doesn’t file for you, it’s your responsibility to stay compliant with each state’s thresholds and filing requirements.


#### 3. When do I need to register for sales tax due to economic nexus?


Most states require registration once you cross their economic nexus threshold, typically $100,000 in sales or 200 transactions annually (though some states have removed the transaction count rule). Timing is key: you’re expected to register as soon as you exceed the threshold, not at year-end. Failing to register promptly can lead to penalties, back taxes, and interest. Keep a monthly nexus tracking log to catch exposure early.


#### 4. Do I need to collect sales tax if I only sell digital products?


Yes, in many states, digital products like eBooks, downloadable music, and SaaS subscriptions are taxable. Over 30 states impose tax on digital goods, and each has different rules. For example, a state may exempt eBooks but tax streaming subscriptions. If you sell digital products through Shopify or another platform, assign the right product tax code to avoid undercharging or overcollecting tax on those items.


#### 5. Does Shopify collect and remit sales tax automatically for every state?


No. Shopify collects tax based on your setup, but it doesn’t remit the tax to states or register your business. You must configure collection settings for each nexus state, assign accurate tax codes to your products, and file returns yourself (or through a provider). If you rely on marketplaces like Amazon that collect for you, keep those separate from Shopify direct sales to avoid double-counting or missing obligations.


#### 6. What happens if I use Amazon FBA or WFS but don’t register in all warehouse states?


If your inventory is stored in an FBA or Walmart Fulfillment Services warehouse, you likely have physical nexus in that state. Not registering can lead to tax notices or audits. Even if Amazon collects tax on your behalf, you may still need to register and file zero-dollar returns in warehouse states. Tools like the Amazon Inventory Event Detail Report can help map where you have physical nexus exposure.


#### 7. How should I handle sales tax for orders that include both taxable and exempt items with shipping?


In most states, when shipping is bundled with taxable and exempt products, tax must be prorated. If not, you risk either overcharging or undercharging tax. For example, a tax engine should calculate tax on 60% of the shipping charge if 60% of the order is taxable. Always separate items and use an invoicing system that applies shipping rules based on item taxability and the buyer’s location.


#### 8. What are common sales tax mistakes Shopify sellers make during Q4?


The busiest retail season often leads to compliance lapses. Common Q4 mistakes include:


- Missing new economic nexus thresholds due to year-end sales spikes
- Failing to update shipping tax rules for gift bundles or promotions
- Overlooking marketplace vs. direct-channel split when filing returns
- Not issuing or renewing exemption certificates for B2B buyers


Use Shopify’s reports + third-party tax software to reconcile collected vs. remitted tax before year-end.
