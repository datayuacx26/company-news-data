# Top Tax Deductions & Credits for Individuals

### Popular Tax Credits for Individuals


Tax credits provide direct reductions to your tax bill, often making them more valuable than deductions. Below are some of the most commonly claimed tax credits, their eligibility requirements, income phaseouts, and how they appear on **Form 1040** .

#### Earned Income Tax Credit (EITC)


The **Earned Income Tax Credit (EITC)** is a **refundable** tax credit aimed at assisting low- to moderate-income workers and families, particularly those with children. Because it is refundable, taxpayers can receive a refund if the credit exceeds their total tax liability.


**Eligibility Requirements**


To qualify for the EITC, taxpayers must:


- Have **earned income** from employment or self-employment.
- Meet **income limits** , which vary based on **filing status** and **number of qualifying children** .
- Have a **valid Social Security number** .
- Not file as **"Married Filing Separately"** .
- Be a **U.S. citizen** or **resident alien** for the entire tax year.
- Not be a **qualifying child** of another taxpayer.


**Phaseouts**


The amount of EITC gradually decreases as income increases and phases out completely at higher income levels. The specific phaseout ranges depend on the **number of qualifying children** and **filing status** . **** The EITC is claimed on **Form 1040** , with **Schedule EIC** attached if claiming qualifying children.


#### Child Tax Credit (CTC)


The **Child Tax Credit (CTC)** is designed to provide financial relief to families with dependent children under the age of **17** . It **reduces tax liability** and, in some cases, can be partially **refundable** through the **Additional Child Tax Credit (ACTC)** . The CTC is claimed on **Form 1040** , with **Schedule 8812** required to calculate additional credit amounts.


#### **Eligibility Requirements**


To qualify, the taxpayer must:


- Have a **qualifying child** who is **under age 17** at the end of the tax year.
- Ensure the child is a **U.S. citizen, U.S. national, or U.S. resident alien** .
- Ensure the child has **lived with the taxpayer for more than half the year** .
- Have provided **more than half** of the child’s financial support.


**Phaseouts**


The credit begins to **phase out** when **modified adjusted gross income (MAGI)** exceeds:


- **$200,000** for single filers.
- ‍ **$400,000** for married couples filing jointly.


#### American Opportunity Credit (AOTC)


The **American Opportunity Credit (AOTC)** helps taxpayers cover **qualified education expenses** for **higher education** . Unlike many education-related tax credits, **40% of the AOTC is refundable** , meaning eligible taxpayers may receive a portion of the credit even if they owe no taxes. The AOTC is claimed on **Form 1040** , using **Schedule 8863** to report qualified expenses and eligibility.


**Eligibility Requirements**


To claim the AOTC, the student must:


- Be pursuing a **degree** or other recognized **education credential** .
- Be enrolled at **least half-time** for at least **one academic period** during the tax year.
- Not have **finished the first four years** of higher education at the start of the tax year.
- Claim the credit for **a maximum of four years per eligible student** .


**Phaseouts**


The credit **phases out** for taxpayers with **modified adjusted gross income (MAGI)** between:


- **$80,000 to $90,000** for **single filers** .
- **$160,000 to $180,000** for **married filing jointly** .


#### Lifetime Learning Credit (LLC)


The **Lifetime Learning Credit (LLC)** supports taxpayers paying for **higher education** or **courses to improve job skills** . Unlike the AOTC, the LLC is **not refundable** , meaning it can reduce tax liability but will not generate a refund. However, it can be claimed for an **unlimited number of years** .


**Eligibility Requirements**


To qualify for the LLC:


- The taxpayer must have paid **qualified tuition and related expenses** for **themselves, a spouse, or a dependent** .
- The student does **not** need to be **enrolled full-time** or pursuing a **degree** .
- The credit applies to **all years of postsecondary education** , including **graduate programs and career training** .


**Phaseouts**


- **$80,000 to $90,000** for **single filers** .
- **$160,000 to $180,000** for **married filing jointly** .


The LLC is claimed on **Form 1040** , with **Schedule 8863** required to report eligible expenses.


### Strategy & Record-Keeping


The IRS typically requires taxpayers to keep records **for at least three years** from the date of filing. However, if you underreported income by more than **25%** , the statute of limitations extends to **six years** . In cases of fraud, there is no limit on how far back the IRS can audit. Here are the key documents you should retain to avoid audit risks:


- **Receipts & Invoices** – Keep records of deductible expenses, such as medical bills, business expenses, and education costs.
- **Charitable Donation Letters** – Donations over **$250** require a written acknowledgment from the receiving organization. Ensure receipts include the donation amount and confirmation that no goods or services were received in return.
- **Tuition Statements (Form 1098-T)** – If claiming the **American Opportunity Credit** or **Lifetime Learning Credit** , retain **Form 1098-T** , which is issued by educational institutions.
- **Mortgage & Property Tax Statements** – Homeowners who itemize deductions should keep mortgage interest statements ( **Form 1098** ) and records of property tax payments.
- **State & Local Tax Payments** – Save records of **state income tax payments** and **property tax bills** if claiming **SALT deductions** .
- **Medical Expense Documentation** – If medical expenses exceed **7.5% of AGI** , maintain receipts, insurance statements, and records of out-of-pocket payments.
- **Tax Forms (W-2, 1099, Schedule K-1, etc.)** – All income-related tax forms should be retained to ensure accurate tax reporting.


**Maximizing Your Benefits**


To reduce your tax liability effectively, it’s important to understand how deductions and credits work together:


- **Deductions Lower Taxable Income** – This can move you into a **lower tax bracket** , reducing the percentage of income subject to tax.
- **Credits Directly Reduce Taxes Owed** – Unlike deductions, **credits lower your tax bill dollar for dollar** , with refundable credits potentially leading to a refund.
- **Strategic Timing Matters** – If you expect a higher income next year, **deferring deductions** (e.g., medical expenses, charitable donations) may yield greater savings. Conversely, **prepaying deductible expenses** can help lower taxable income in the current year.
- **Optimize for Income Limits** – Some tax credits **phase out at higher income levels** . Lowering **adjusted gross income (AGI)** through deductions may help you qualify for income-based credits like the **EITC or American Opportunity Credit** .


### Final Thoughts


**Strategic planning** ensures you make the most of deductions and credits, helping you keep more of your hard-earned money. Maximizing your tax savings starts with choosing the one that offers the **greatest benefit** . To avoid issues, you must **keep proper documentation** for all claims and stay compliant with IRS rules.
