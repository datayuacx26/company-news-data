# Tax Season Research Prep: The Modern Approach

### How to Verify AI-Generated Research


The professional standards that govern tax practice, like Circular 230 and applicable codes of professional conduct, do not change because you used an AI tool. You are ultimately responsible for the accuracy of the positions you take. That means AI outputs need a verification step before they become the basis for client advice.


The core principle is: trust the citation, not the summary. AI tools compress nuance when they summarize, so when a response cites to the IRC, Treasury Regulations, or IRS guidance, pull the cited source and confirm the summary accurately reflects what the underlying source says. The speed AI brings to research makes it easy to skip this step—don't. Verification isn't a bottleneck. It's the professional standard.


**Verification Checklist**


1. **Confirm the citation says what the AI claims.** Pull the IRC sections or IRS document cited and read the relevant passage.
2. **Check for exceptions the AI may have omitted.** Many tax rules have phase-outs, elections, or anti-abuse provisions. Scan the source for provisions not mentioned in the AI’s response.
3. **Verify currency.** Check whether the provision has been amended by subsequent legislation or superseded by newer IRS guidance.
4. **For state issues, verify against state-published materials.** State conformity changes frequently. Confirm against the state revenue department’s own guidance or state statute.
5. **Flag any answer without citation.** If the AI provides analysis without sourcing it, treat it as a starting point only. Ask for citations or verify independently.

### Fitting AI Into Your Existing Workflow


The most common reason practitioners do not sustain the efficiency gains AI tools can deliver is a lack of workflow integration. The tool becomes something they try a few times and set aside rather than something that genuinely changes how work gets done.


**There are three natural points where AI adds the most value:**


- **Issue identification.** At the start of an engagement, AI is useful for quickly mapping relevant issues, identifying the applicable statutory framework, and surfacing adjacent considerations you should check.
- **First-pass research.** Once issues are defined, AI handles the initial research pass — getting a synthesized answer with citations quickly, in place of or alongside an initial database search.
- **Drafting research summaries.** After research is complete and verified, AI can accelerate drafting of research memos and client summaries, always subject to review before delivery.


**Two Things to Do Before Broad Adoption**


Establish verification standards in writing before your firm uses AI-assisted research in client work. Decide what verification steps are required, who is responsible, and how AI assistance will be documented. Firms that handle this well treat AI output the same way they treat work from a junior associate — useful starting material that requires review before it becomes client advice.


Build a shared prompt library for your most common research scenarios. A shared document with ten to fifteen templates covering your firm's frequent research situations gives every practitioner a consistent starting point and reduces the learning curve significantly. Individual practitioners developing their own prompting habits independently produces inconsistent results.


### The Bottom Line


AI tax research tools are most valuable when the practitioner using them has a clear process: precise prompts that give the AI the context it needs, a consistent verification habit that protects against over-reliance, and a workflow integration plan that makes the tool part of how research actually gets done rather than an occasional experiment.
