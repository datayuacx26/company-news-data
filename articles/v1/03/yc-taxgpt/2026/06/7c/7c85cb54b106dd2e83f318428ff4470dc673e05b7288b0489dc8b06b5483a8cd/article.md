# Schedule A Form 1040 Checklist for Tax Professionals

### Lines 11–14 – Gifts To Charity


**Confirm the organization is eligible**


- Contributions must be to qualified organizations (including U.S. charities and certain governmental entities).
- When unsure, check qualifying status using IRS tools or ask TaxGPT Research to analyze the entity description and rules in Pub. 526.


**Substantiation for cash contributions**


- Any cash contribution requires a bank record or written communication from the charity.
- Cash gifts of 250 dollars or more require a contemporaneous written acknowledgment that includes amount, date, and statement of whether any goods or services were provided.


**Noncash contributions and Form 8283**


- Noncash contributions over 500 dollars require Form 8283, Section A.
- Noncash contributions over 5,000 dollars generally require:


- A qualified appraisal, and
- Form 8283 Section B, signed by the appraiser and the donee organization.


- Special rules apply to vehicle donations.


**Valuation rules**


- Property is typically deducted at fair market value.
- Special rules apply to capital gain property, ordinary income property, and contributions of partial interests.


**Quid pro quo contributions**


- If the donor receives goods or services (for example, gala tickets, dinners, memberships), deduct only the amount exceeding the fair market value of what was received.
- Ensure the acknowledgment letter clearly states the value of benefits provided.


**State tax credit interactions**


- Reduce the charitable deduction for any state or local tax credit received, unless a small credit exception applies.
- In some cases, the “disallowed” portion may be treated as a state or local tax payment instead.


**Red flag:** Large charitable deductions without proper contemporaneous acknowledgment or Form 8283 are high-risk exam items.


### Line 15 – Casualty And Theft Losses From Federally Declared Disasters


**Confirm the loss is from a federally declared disaster**


For most taxpayers, personal casualty and theft losses are only deductible when attributable to a federally declared disaster.


**Attach Form 4684**


- Complete Form 4684 to compute the deductible loss.
- Transfer the correct amount from Form 4684 to Schedule A, line 15.


### Line 16 – Other Itemized Deductions


**Use this line only for specifically allowed items**


Examples include certain repayments under the claim of right doctrine and other specific deductions not subject to the 2 percent AGI floor.


**Do not resurrect suspended miscellaneous deductions**


Do not park items here that used to be miscellaneous itemized deductions pre-TCJA.

## Deadlines And Planning Timelines For Schedule A


Although Schedule A is filed with Form 1040, planning and documentation happen year-round.


**Annual filing deadline**


- File Form 1040 with Schedule A by the regular individual return due date for the year (or the extended deadline if Form 4868 is timely filed).
- Schedule A is not filed separately.


**Extension strategy**


- If documentation is incomplete by April, consider filing an extension so you can fully substantiate large itemized deductions.
- Use estimated amounts only when you can reasonably support them and reconcile before the extended deadline.


**Year-end planning**


- Bunch elective medical procedures and charitable contributions into a single year to clear the 7.5 percent AGI floor or to meaningfully exceed the standard deduction.
- Prepay state income estimates or property taxes when allowed (subject to SALT cap and any state limitations).


**Amended returns and lookback**


- If you discover missed deductions or disallowed items, file Form 1040-X within the usual statute of limitations period (generally three years from the original due date or two years from the date the tax was paid, whichever is later).


## Common IRS Adjustment And Rejection Triggers For Schedule A


- **Medical expenses below 7.5 percent of AGI**


Entering medical expenses without applying the AGI floor is a frequent exam adjustment.


- **SALT amounts over the 40,000 dollar cap**


Forgetting the cap or failing to aggregate all SALT categories correctly leads to disallowed amounts.


- **Including non-deductible taxes or HOA charges**


Federal income taxes, excise taxes, and homeowners association dues are not Schedule A taxes.


- **Deducting mortgage insurance premiums**


The itemized deduction for mortgage insurance premiums has expired for amounts paid after December 31, 2021.


- **Home equity interest not tied to buy, build, or improve**


Interest on home equity loans used for personal spending should not be deducted as mortgage interest.


- **Misallocated co-owner interest and property taxes**


Claiming 100 percent of interest or taxes when you are only a partial owner (or when Form 1098 is shared) draws scrutiny.


- **Charitable contributions without proper acknowledgment or Form 8283**


Large gifts with weak documentation are often reduced or disallowed on exam.


- **Trying to deduct suspended miscellaneous itemized deductions**


Unreimbursed employee expenses and similar items remain suspended through at least the end of the TCJA window.


### FAQs


#### When should a client itemize instead of taking the standard deduction?


Clients generally benefit from itemizing when the total of medical (above the AGI floor), SALT (within the cap), mortgage interest, charity, and other allowed items exceeds the standard deduction. Some clients may still choose to itemize for state tax or planning reasons even if Schedule A is slightly lower.


#### Can unreimbursed employee expenses still be deducted on Schedule A?


For most taxpayers, no. Miscellaneous itemized deductions subject to the 2 percent AGI floor, including unreimbursed employee expenses, are suspended for tax years beginning after 2017 and before 2026.


#### Are mortgage insurance premiums still deductible?


Not for amounts paid after December 31, 2021, under current rules. Do not treat post-2021 mortgage insurance premiums as mortgage interest on Schedule A.


#### Can I deduct homeowners association dues or special assessments?


HOA dues and assessments for common area maintenance are not deductible as taxes. Assessments for local improvements that increase property value, such as new sidewalks or sewer connections, are not deducted, but rather capitalized increasing the basis of the property..


#### How do I handle property taxes and mortgage interest when there are multiple owners?


Each owner deducts the portion of interest and taxes they are legally obligated to pay and actually pay. When Form 1098 lists only one owner, the other owner must report the Form 1098 recipient’s details and document the allocation and payments.


#### Do I need to send receipts or charitable acknowledgments with the return?


Typically, no. You do not mail substantiation with an e-filed return. However, you must retain it and be able to produce it if requested by the IRS.


#### What if a client lives in one state, works in another, and owns property in a third?


You still apply the overall 40,000 dollar SALT cap for Schedule A, but you must correctly allocate and track state and local income, sales, and property taxes across jurisdictions. This is where tools like TaxGPT Matrix are useful to visualize liabilities and credits and to avoid double counting.
