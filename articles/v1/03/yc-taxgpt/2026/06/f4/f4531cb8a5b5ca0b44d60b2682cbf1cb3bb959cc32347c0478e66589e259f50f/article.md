# Form 1120 Checklist for Tax Professionals

What should be a straightforward C corporation return often turns into a notice machine: one missed schedule, one wrong box on Schedule K, and suddenly you are dealing with balance due notices, international penalties, and “we adjusted your return” letters.


You are juggling:


- Entity classification (is this really a Form 1120, or should it be 1120-S, 1120-REIT, 1120-PC, or something else).
- Multiple supporting schedules and forms (1125-A, 1125-E, 4562, 4797, 5472, 8996, 8997, and more).
- Book-to-tax reconciliations, uncertain tax positions, digital asset questions, and foreign-owned disregarded entity rules.
- E-file mandates and assembly order that still trip up seasoned preparers.


This checklist turns Form 1120 into a predictable workflow, from eligibility and due dates through assembly and submission, so your corporate returns clear IRS processing with fewer notices, fewer penalties, and fewer late-night “did we attach that?” moments.


## Before You Start: Confirm Form 1120 Is the Right Return


Make sure you are on the right chassis before you build the engine.


☐ **Confirm the filer should use Form 1120**


**‍** Domestic corporations (including entities that elected corporate status) that are not required to file a specialized return.


Verify that no special return applies instead, for example:


- Form 1120-S (S corporations)
- Form 1120-REIT / 1120-RIC
- Form 1120-L (life insurance)
- Form 1120-PC (property and casualty)
- Form 1066 (REMIC), etc.


☐ **Check entity classification elections**


**‍** If the entity elected corporate status using Form 8832, ensure the election is properly documented and attached for the year of election.


☐ **Remember how broad the filing requirement is**


**‍** Most corporations (unless exempt under section 501) must file a return regardless of taxable income.

## Core Profile: EIN, Address, Tax Year, and Method of Accounting


Get the header details perfect; errors here ripple through e-file and IRS matching.


☐ **Verify entity data**


- EIN
- Exact legal name (matching charter and IRS records)
- Address (mailing vs physical where relevant)
- Tax year (calendar vs fiscal)


☐ **Confirm accounting method**


- Cash, accrual, or other
- Ensure consistency with books and Schedule K disclosures


## Timelines: Due Dates, Extensions, and E-File Rules


### **Standard Due Date**


☐ **Due date for most C corporations**


**‍** 15th day of the 4th month after year-end.


☐ **June 30 year-end**


**‍** Due on the 15th day of the 3rd month after year-end.


☐ **Weekend and holiday rule**


**‍** If the due date falls on a weekend or legal holiday, the due date shifts to the next business day.


### **Extension**


☐ **Form 7004**


**‍** File Form 7004 by the regular due date to request an automatic extension of time to file.


Remember: This extends time to file, not time to pay.


### **E-File Requirement**


☐ **E-file mandate**


**‍** Corporations must e-file Form 1120 if they file 10 or more returns of any type during the calendar year (income, employment, excise, etc.).


☐ **Hardship waivers**


**‍** If e-file is not feasible, follow the instructions for requesting a waiver and retain documentation.


☐ **Paper filing addresses (if not e-filed)**


**‍** Use the current IRS instructions to determine the correct mailing address based on:


- State
- Asset size
- Schedule M-3 filing status


For private delivery services (FedEx, UPS, DHL), use the submission processing center street addresses provided in the instructions.

## Payments and Estimated Tax: Avoid Preventable Penalties


☐ **Electronic deposits required** Corporations must use EFTPS for income, employment, and excise tax deposits.


☐ **Estimated tax** Follow corporate estimated tax thresholds and schedules to avoid underpayment penalties.


☐ **Tie-out to Schedule J** Confirm that all estimated payments, prior-year overpayments, and credits are captured and reconciled in Schedule J (Tax Computation and Payment).


## Page 1: Income and Deductions Checklist


Work top-down and tie everything back to supporting schedules and the trial balance.


### **Income Section**


☐ **Gross receipts or sales and returns and allowances**


☐ **Cost of goods sold**


- Attach Form 1125-A for COGS
- Verify that ending inventory ties to Schedule L


☐ **Other income items**


- Dividends and inclusions → Schedule C (Form 1120)
- Interest, rents, royalties
- Capital gains and losses → Schedule D (Form 1120)
- Gains or losses from business property → Form 4797
- “Other income” → attach a detailed statement


### **Key Deductions**


☐ **Compensation of officers**


- Attach Form 1125-E


☐ **Other major deductions**


- Salaries and wages
- Repairs and maintenance
- Bad debts
- Rents
- Taxes and licenses
- Interest
- Charitable contributions
- Depreciation and amortization (not claimed elsewhere) → **Form 4562** if required


## Tax Computation and Payments (Schedule J)


☐ Compute base corporate income tax and any other chapter 1 taxes.


☐ Apply credits where applicable:


- Foreign tax credit → **Form 1118**
- General business credit → **Form 3800**
- Other specific credits as applicable


☐ Compute total tax due or refund, incorporating:


- Estimated payments
- Overpayments applied from prior years
- Withholding and credits


## Mandatory Schedules and Common Attachments


### Schedule C – Dividends, Inclusions, and Special Deductions


☐ Report dividends and inclusions.
☐ Compute the dividends received deduction (DRD) using the updated worksheet in the instructions.
☐ Ensure Schedule C ties to page 1, line 4.


### Schedule J – Tax Computation and Payment


☐ Include all applicable taxes and credits.
☐ Address any CAMT-related relief or waiver per instructions.
☐ Tie the balance due or overpayment to page 1.


### Schedule K – Other Information (High-Risk Question Area)


☐ Accounting method and business activity code.
☐ Parent, subsidiary, and controlled group relationships.
☐ Ownership of other entities (corporations, partnerships, disregarded entities).
☐ Foreign ownership and reporting triggers (Forms 5471, 5472, and other international forms).
☐ Information return reporting (Form 1099):


- Did the corporation make payments requiring 1099s?
- Did it file or will it file the required 1099s?


☐ Uncertain tax positions (UTP)


- Determine if Schedule UTP is required.

## Schedules L, M-1, and M-2 – When Required


☐ **Schedule L** : Balance Sheet per books
☐ **Schedule M-1** : Book-to-tax reconciliation
☐ **Schedule M-2** : Analysis of unappropriated retained earnings


These can be omitted only if both:


- Total receipts < $250,000, and
- Total assets < $250,000


If both thresholds are met or exceeded, complete all schedules per instructions and tie them to the financial statements.


### Depreciation and Amortization – Form 4562


☐ File Form 4562 for any depreciation on the corporate tax return


☐ Complete Form 4562 for each business or activity on your return as required.☐ Retain detailed fixed asset schedules (basis, date placed in service, method, recovery period, convention).


## International and Foreign-Owned Disregarded Entity Requirements


### Form 5472 – 25% Foreign-Owned Corporations and Foreign-Owned DEs


☐ Form 5472 is required if:


- The U.S. corporation is 25% foreign-owned and has reportable transactions with foreign or domestic related parties, or
- You are filing for a foreign-owned U.S. disregarded entity (DE) that must file a pro forma Form 1120.


☐ For Form 5472, ensure you:


- Report monetary and non-monetary transactions with foreign related parties
- Complete base erosion disclosures (Part IX)
- Disclose certain disallowed or limited payments (for example, section 267A-type arrangements)


☐ Foreign-owned U.S. DEs


- File a pro forma Form 1120 with Form 5472 attached by the due date
- Use the dedicated filing address or fax (per current instructions)
- Extend using Form 7004 and indicate “Foreign-owned U.S. DE”

### Qualified Opportunity Fund (QOF) Items


☐ If the corporation is a QOF, file Form 1120 and attach Form 8996 (QOF certification).


☐ If the corporation holds QOF investments, attach Form 8997.


☐ Ensure related questions and disclosures in Schedule K align with Forms 8996 and 8997.


### E-File Readiness and Assembly Order


Make sure your electronic package mirrors a clean paper assembly:


- All required schedules and attachments in the order specified in the instructions
- Supporting statements and explanations last, labeled with the corporation’s name and EIN


## Return Content Validation: Schedule K “Sanity Check”


Before you transmit, use Schedule K as a control checklist.


☐ Ownership disclosures


- Identify 20% and 50% ownership thresholds
- Determine whether Schedule G (Form 1120) is required for certain shareholders


☐ Digital asset question


Confirm disclosures if the corporation received, sold, exchanged, or otherwise disposed of digital assets during the year


☐ 1099 reporting


- Confirm whether the corporation made payments that require Form 1099 reporting
- Confirm that required information returns were or will be filed


☐ Uncertain tax positions (UTP)


- Indicate whether Schedule UTP is required
- Confirm attachment if applicable
