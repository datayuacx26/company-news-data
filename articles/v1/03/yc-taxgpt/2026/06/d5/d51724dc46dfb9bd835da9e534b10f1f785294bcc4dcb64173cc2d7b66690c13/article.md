# Things to Consider Before Choosing an AI for Tax Work

There’s growing pressure across the accounting profession to “get on board with AI.” Firms are hearing it from peers, clients, and even vendors “move fast or risk falling behind”.


But the real danger isn’t moving too slowly. It’s choosing the wrong AI and hard-wiring mistakes into your workflow.


Adopting a poorly designed or generic tool can do more harm than waiting:


- Wrong answers mean potential compliance issues and you may not even know until it’s too late.
- No citations leave your staff without verifiable sources, exposing your work to scrutiny.
- Disjointed workflows force your team to jump between platforms, killing efficiency and inflating review time.


In tax, accuracy matters the most. One wrong reference, one unsupported claim, one missed update, and you’re risking penalties, client trust, and your firm’s reputation. Before you plug any AI into your process, you need a clear, profession-specific way to evaluate it.


The following checklist is built for tax professionals who care about accuracy, authority, and accountability. Use it to assess any AI tool so you can move forward with confidence.


## Authentication & Trust (Accuracy First)


Before you even think about interface or integrations, you need to ask:


*Can I rely on this AI to deliver precise, defendable, and jurisdictionally accurate answers every single time?*


Here’s what is really required:


- **Training data quality:** Was the AI trained on random internet content or on a professionally curated, tax-specific corpus? General-purpose models often hallucinate or confuse statutes. For instance, TaxGPT is built from authoritative sources like the Internal Revenue Code, Treasury Regulations, IRS Publications, court opinions, and notices.
- **Source verification:**
Every answer must come with direct citations to primary sources: Code sections, Regs, Notices, Pubs, and even case law. Without citations, you’re left guessing, and that’s a risk no compliance-driven professional can afford.
- **Update cadence:**
How quickly does the AI reflect new regulations, notices, revenue procedures, inflation adjustments, and court rulings? In fast-moving areas like ERC or SALT conformity, delays can lead to outdated advice. TaxGPT updates its corpus regularly to stay aligned with the latest guidance.
- **Jurisdictional depth:**
It’s not enough to cover the IRC. Your clients live at the intersection of federal, state, local, and sometimes international tax rules. Look for tools with granular support across all U.S. jurisdictions and a clear awareness of conformity (or lack thereof) across states. It's worth noting that TaxGPT is purpose-built with deep jurisdictional coverage, including nuanced handling of non-conforming states.
- **Auditability:**
Can you see why the AI gave a certain answer? You need passage-level context, citation transparency, and (ideally) version history to validate the accuracy of every output. TaxGPT provides fully traceable answers, so you never have to rely on a black box.


In short, if an AI tool can’t prove its work, it doesn’t belong in your workflow. Accuracy, auditability, and jurisdictional coverage aren’t some bonus features. They’re the baseline on which your tax work will heavily depend.


Next, let’s look at how these capabilities should translate into your daily workflow.


## Professional Requirements (Standards & Safeguards)


For tax professionals, adoption of AI means meeting the same standards your clients and regulators expect from you. If an AI tool can't meet your firm’s security, compliance, and documentation requirements, it shouldn’t be a part of your tech stack.


Here’s what to evaluate to ensure any AI meets the bar:


- **Security and compliance:** Your chosen platform should be SOC 2 Type II certified, with encryption at rest and in transit, granular access controls, SSO/MFA support, and least-privilege role management at the minimum. TaxGPT is built with these security standards front and center to support rigorous firm environments. Learn more →[security at TaxGPT](https://www.taxgpt.com/security) .
- **Data handling:** You must retain control over your client data. Look for platforms that offer data residency options, custom retention settings, and the ability to automatically redact PII. Equally critical is the AI should default to private index isolation, with no training on your firm’s data unless explicitly authorized.
- **Professional liability fit:** Can the tool support your documentation needs? You’ll want exportable workpapers, clear version history, and answer-level explainability that aligns with your compliance file. This is especially important when responses must be defensible to clients, partners, or auditors.
- **Audit trails:** Every interaction should leave a verifiable record. This includes thread-level history, immutable logs, timestamps, and user IDs, all exportable to PDF or DOCX for downstream documentation or internal reviews.
- **Contracts that cover you:** Especially for firms handling sensitive client data or operating in regulated environments, confirm whether the provider offers DPAs (Data Processing Agreements) or BAAs (Business Associate Agreements) when applicable.


AI tools must meet (or exceed) the same standards you already uphold. If a solution isn’t ready to pass your firm’s due diligence checklist, it isn’t ready to handle client-facing tax work.
