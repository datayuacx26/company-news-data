# Form 4868 Checklist for Tax Professionals

### Filing Extensions Should Be Simple. The Penalties Aren’t.


Form 4868 is only one page, but it sits on top of some of the most expensive compliance risks in individual tax prep. A weak estimate, the wrong payment designation, or a missed “out of the country” checkbox can make the extension invalid. That means your client is suddenly exposed to failure to file penalties and interest from the original due date.


The traps typically show up when you are working with:


- incomplete client data (missing W-2s or 1099s)
- late year tax events that shift liability
- ITIN situations
- international clients
- multi-state or dual-resident taxpayers
- last day e-file rejections


This checklist gives you a clear, defensible process for preparing Form 4868 so your extensions are timely, accurate, and protected from avoidable penalties.


**Need certainty on due dates or safe harbors?**[TaxGPT Research](https://www.taxgpt.com/tax-research-software) can confirm filing deadlines, residency-driven due dates, and penalty safe harbor thresholds with citations you can drop into your workpapers.


## 1. Before You Start: Confirm Dates and Eligibility


☐ Verify the client’s filing due date


- Most individual returns: April 15
- Form 1040-NR without wages subject to U.S. withholding may have a June 15 filing deadline, but Form 4868 must still be submitted by the regular due date to be timely unless automatic relief applies.


☐ Confirm the extension will be filed on time


- A late Form 4868 is not a valid extension.
- An invalid extension puts the client back under failure to file penalty rules.


☐ Reinforce the core rule with clients


- Form 4868 extends time to file, not time to pay.
- Any tax due should be paid by the original deadline to reduce penalties and interest.


If the client’s due date depends on residency, foreign income, or special status, ask[TaxGPT Research](https://www.taxgpt.com/tax-research-software) to confirm the correct deadline and authority.


## 2. Step by Step Form 4868 Preparation Checklist


### Step 1. Gather Required Taxpayer Information


☐ Full legal name(s) and current filing address
☐ SSN or ITIN


☐ **If no SSN or ITIN exists**


- Enter “ITIN TO BE REQUESTED”
- Do not attach Form W-7


- Apply for the ITIN later with the filed return, unless an ITIN exception applies


☐ Confirm name and TIN match IRS records to avoid e-file rejection


### Step 2. Estimate Tax Liability Reasonably


This is the most important part of a valid extension.


☐ **Prepare a reasonable estimate of total tax for the year**


- Use prior-year return data, year to date information, withholding records, and known credits.
- Document assumptions in the file.


☐ **Compute total payments already made**


- Withholding
- Estimated payments
- Credits applied from prior-year overpayments


☐ **Calculate remaining balance due**


- Pay as much as possible with the extension.


☐ **Apply safe harbor best practice**


- Aim to pay at least 90 percent of the expected total tax by April 15 to reduce penalty exposure.
- Use prior-year safe harbor rules when appropriate.


[TaxGPT Research](https://www.taxgpt.com/tax-research-software) can verify whether the client meets the 90 percent current-year safe harbor or should rely on prior-year 100 or 110 percent standards.


## Step 3. Choose and Execute the Payment Method


Any electronic payment made with “extension” selected automatically grants an extension.


☐ Direct Pay with the IRS, selecting extension as the reason
☐ EFTPS for recurring taxpayers and business owners
☐ Debit, credit, or digital wallet through IRS approved processors
☐ Electronic Funds Withdrawal when e-filing the extension


☐ Save the confirmation number and store it in the client file.


☐ **If paying by check or money order**


- Use the correct address from current instructions
- Include name, TIN, tax year, and Form 4868 notation on the check
- Do not staple payment to the form
- Use tracking for proof of timely filing


## Step 4. Decide Between E-file and Paper Filing


☐ E-filing is preferred


- Immediate acknowledgment
- Lower rejection risk
- Cleaner proof of timely filing


☐ If paper filing is necessary


- Ensure postmark is on or before the due date
- Use certified mail or equivalent tracking
- Keep the receipt in the file


## Step 5. Signature and PIN Procedures


☐ **Self-Select PIN**


- Taxpayer chooses a five-digit PIN
- Requires prior-year AGI or prior-year PIN
- IP PIN must also be entered if issued
- Joint extensions require a separate PIN for each spouse


☐ **Practitioner PIN (ERO method)**


- No prior-year AGI needed
- Taxpayer signs Form 8878
- Retain Form 8878 for three years
