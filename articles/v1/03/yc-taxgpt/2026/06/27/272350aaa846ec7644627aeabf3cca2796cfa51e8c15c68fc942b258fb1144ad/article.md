# Form 2555 Checklist for Tax Professionals

### Foreign Income Reporting Should Not Feel Like A Compliance Trap


Form 2555 lets qualified U.S. taxpayers working abroad exclude a significant portion of their foreign earned income and claim valuable housing exclusions or deductions.


But it is also one of the most technical and frequently misapplied individual tax provisions.


Small mistakes can invalidate the exclusion entirely:


- Miscounting foreign travel days
- Filing FEIE while claiming disallowed credits
- Selecting the wrong residency test
- Overstating foreign housing expenses
- Applying employer-provided housing incorrectly


And because Form 2555 interacts with Form 1040 Schedule 1, foreign tax credit rules, and international residency standards, even experienced practitioners can lose hours verifying eligibility.


Let’s simplify the complexity. This checklist gives you a clear, high-level process so your Form 2555 filings are complete, defensible, and compliant with 2025 rules.

## Eligibility Requirements ( FEIE Qualification )


**Confirm U.S. citizenship or resident alien status**


- Form 2555 cannot be used for U.S. Government employees working abroad.
- Private U.S. employer wages *can* qualify if services are performed outside the U.S.


**Verify foreign tax home**


- The taxpayer’s tax home must be in a foreign country.
- Evaluate whether the U.S. abode test is satisfied; a U.S. abode generally disqualifies the exclusion.


**Select the correct residency qualification method**


Taxpayer must satisfy **one** of these two tests:


**1. Bona Fide Residence Test**


- Must be a bona fide resident of a foreign country (or countries) for an uninterrupted period including a full tax year.
- Requires strong evidence of foreign ties (residency permits, foreign taxation, long-term intent).


**2. Physical Presence Test**


- Must be physically present in one or more foreign countries for **330 full days** during any 12-month period.
- Count *full* 24-hour days only; international transit time may not count.


**Waiver of Time Requirements for determining tax home (rare)**


- Applicable when war, civil unrest, or evacuation prevents meeting residency or physical presence rules.
- Must attach a statement to your return explaining that you expected to meet the applicable time requirement, but the conditions in the foreign country prevented you from the normal conduct of business.


**Note:** Attempting to use Form 2555 while filing as a nonresident in the foreign jurisdiction can undermine the bona fide residence position.


## Documentation Checklist


**Tax home documentation:**


- Work contracts, assignment letters, foreign address proofs.


**Bona fide residency support:**


- Residency permits, foreign tax filings, long-term lease agreements.


**Physical presence documentation:**


- Travel logs with exact entry and exit dates.
- Passport stamps and employer travel records.


**Income & housing substantiation:**


Foreign pay statements, employment contracts, housing receipts, valuation of in-kind benefits.

## Form 2555 Section-by-Section Checklist


### Part I: Basic Information


- Report foreign address, employer details, and nature of foreign work
- Disclose prior Form 2555 filings or revocations
- Apply the 5-year re-election restriction if FEIE was previously revoked


### Part II or III: Residency or Physical Presence


Choose only one test and complete the correct section:


**Part II: Bona Fide Residence**


- Provide start and end dates of residence


**Part III: Physical Presence**


- Enter each qualifying 12-month period
- List *every* foreign country visited and all qualifying dates
- Ensure travel days are complete 24-hour periods


### Part IV: Foreign Earned Income


Report foreign earned income received during the tax year


Include:


- Wages
- Self-employment income
- Employer-provided benefits
- In-kind housing or allowances
☐ Exclude:
- U.S. source income
- Pension income
- Interest, ordinary dividends, capital gains, and alimony


**Note:** Missing employer-provided benefits or incorrectly excluding U.S. source wages is a common exam issue.


### Parts VI–IX: FEIE + Housing Exclusion / Deduction


### **2025 Key Numbers**


- **FEIE limit:**


Up to **$130,000** per qualifying taxpayer


- **Base housing amount:**


$56.99 per day (16% of FEIE limit)


- **General housing cap:**


30% of FEIE limit (varies by foreign location—check high-cost city list)


### Checklist


☐ Compute qualified housing expenses
☐ Subtract base housing amount
☐ Apply appropriate housing cap
☐ Distinguish between housing exclusion (employees) and housing deduction (self-employed)
☐ Reconcile totals to Schedule 1:


- Line 8d: FEIE + housing exclusion
- Line 24j: Housing deduction


**Note:** The housing deduction is often misapplied for employees. Confirm employment status before calculating.


## Disallowances & Special Allocations


☐ Disallow foreign tax credits for any taxes paid on excluded income
☐ Allocate deductions and expenses between excluded and taxable income
☐ Confirm no Child Tax Credit or Earned Income Credit is claimed with FEIE
☐ Adjust SE tax calculations, excluded income cannot reduce SE tax


**Note:** Claiming FTC *and* FEIE on the same income is a high-risk compliance error.
