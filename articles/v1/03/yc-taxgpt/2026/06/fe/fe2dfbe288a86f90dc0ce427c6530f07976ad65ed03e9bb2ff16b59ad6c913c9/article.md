# 9 Tax Mistakes That Could Cost Small Business Owners Thousands (and How to Avoid Them)

## 5. Misclassifying Employees vs. Contractors


Hiring help is a major milestone for any small business, but getting worker classification wrong can quickly turn into a costly nightmare. Treating someone as an independent contractor when they meet the IRS definition of an employee can lead to serious financial consequences.


### Why It’s a Problem


- **Triggers audits and huge back taxes** : If the IRS finds misclassification, you could be liable for unpaid employment taxes, benefits, and penalties, sometimes for multiple years.
- **IRS reclassification = fines + liability** : Beyond financial penalties, misclassifications can also lead to lawsuits and damage your business’s reputation.


### How to Avoid It


- **Follow IRS guidelines** : The IRS uses tests like the “control test” (who directs work) and “behavior test” (who controls how tasks are done) to determine classification. Understand and apply these standards.
- **Draft clear contracts** : Every contractor agreement should spell out the relationship, scope of work, and independence of the contractor.
- **Reassess roles regularly** : As responsibilities shift over time, someone initially classified as a contractor might transition into employee status—review and adjust classifications accordingly.

## 6. Choosing the Wrong Business Entity


Your business structure is one of the biggest factors influencing how much tax you pay. Choosing the wrong entity can quietly drain profits through double taxation, missed deductions, or excessive self-employment taxes.


### Why It’s a Problem


- **Overpaying taxes** : For example, C-Corporations face double taxation, once at the corporate level and again when profits are distributed as dividends.
- **Ineligible for certain deductions or credits** : Some structures limit your ability to claim tax benefits like the Qualified Business Income (QBI) deduction.


### How to Avoid It


- **Understand the tax implications for each structure** : Sole proprietorships, Partnerships, S-Corps, and C-Corps all have different rules for[income reporting](https://www.taxgpt.com/blog/report-business-income-expenses-on-different-entity-types) , salary requirements, and tax rates.
- **Think beyond taxes** : Consider factors like liability protection, reinvestment goals, and whether you'll take a salary versus dividends.
- **Consult a CPA** : A professional can help you evaluate your current setup and suggest adjustments if your business evolves.

## 7. Overstating or Underreporting Income


The IRS cross-references what you report with information from clients, banks, and payment processors. If your numbers don’t match, it sends a clear signal that something is wrong.


### Why It’s a Problem


- **Penalties are steep** : Underreporting income can result in a 20% penalty for negligence, and if the IRS determines the misstatement was intentional, the penalty jumps to 75% of the unpaid tax.
- **Skyrocketing audit risk** : Income discrepancies are one of the fastest ways to trigger a full IRS audit, even if the error was accidental.


### How to Avoid It


- **Match your 1099s, deposits, and invoices** : Review every 1099 form you receive against your internal records. Even small differences can cause big problems.
- **Reconcile digital payment platforms** : Make sure payment processors like Stripe, PayPal, Square, and others match the income you report on your return.
- **Maintain consistent reporting across platforms** : Income must match not only your accounting software but also your bank deposits and third-party payment apps.

## 8. Claiming Deductions Incorrectly


Business deductions can save you thousands, but only if they’re claimed properly. Overstating deductions or claiming expenses that don’t truly qualify can raise red flags and lead to denied write-offs or worse, an IRS audit.


### Why It’s a Problem


- **Reduces AGI or self-employment tax improperly** : Taking deductions without proper justification can artificially lower your taxable income, exposing you to penalties if audited.
- **Too aggressive = audit bait** : High-risk deductions like home office expenses, business vehicle use, and meals are heavily scrutinized. If they seem inflated or poorly documented, the IRS takes notice.


### How to Avoid It


- **Apply the “ordinary and necessary” rule** : A deductible expense must be both ordinary (common in your industry) and necessary (helpful for running your business).
- **Use the right deduction category** : Know when an expense belongs under business deductions vs. personal itemized deductions. Misclassification can cost you tax benefits or your credibility during an audit.
- **Keep detailed logs and receipts** : Especially for mileage, meals, home office expenses, and travel. Good documentation is your best defense.

## 9. Ignoring Available Tax Credits


When it comes to reducing your tax bill, deductions are helpful, but credits are even better. Unlike deductions, which lower your taxable income, tax credits reduce your actual tax liability dollar-for-dollar. Yet every year, countless small business owners leave money on the table by overlooking credits they qualify for.


### Why It’s a Problem


- **Credits lower your tax bill directly** : Missing a $5,000 credit means you’re paying $5,000 more in taxes, not just losing a deduction.
- **Many valuable credits go unused** : Business owners often miss industry-specific opportunities like the[Research and Development (R&D) tax credit](https://www.taxgpt.com/blog/r-and-d-tax-credits) or hiring-related credits like the Work Opportunity Tax Credit (WOTC).


### How to Avoid It


- **Research industry-specific credits** : Some industries have targeted credits for innovation, sustainability, and workforce development. Know what’s available for your business type.
- **Ask your CPA during annual planning** : A good tax professional can flag opportunities you might otherwise miss and ensure you’re documenting eligibility properly.
