# Form 1040-ES Checklist for Tax Professionals

## Estimated Tax Doesn’t Have to Be a Penalty Minefield


For many clients, Form 1040-ES seems simple: four “quarterly” payments and a short worksheet. But as every tax professional knows, the real complexity hides underneath: safe harbor rules, uneven income, AMT, NIIT, self-employment tax, refundables, substantiation of withholding dates, and the ever-confusing 110% rule.


Underpay? Your client gets an IRC § 6654 penalty.
Overpay? You’re tying up their cash unnecessarily, and fielding frustrated calls about why their refund is smaller than expected.


When income fluctuates, seasonal spikes can make the penalty calculation even worse. And if you miss a safe harbor? Penalties accrue from installment due dates, not year-end, many clients discover this only after the CP2000 arrives.


This checklist eliminates the guesswork and walks you through the exact steps to calculate, document, and file Form 1040-ES accurately, minimizing penalty exposure and simplifying client communication.


## Before You Start: Confirm Estimated Tax Is Required


Gather these preliminaries before calculating any installments:


☐ Client expects to owe at least $1,000 after subtracting withholding and refundable credits.


☐ Withholding and refundable credits will be less than the smaller of:


90% of current-year tax, or


100% of prior-year tax (110% if prior-year AGI > $150,000 / $75,000 MFS).


☐ Exception: No estimated tax required if the client had zero prior-year tax AND was a U.S. citizen or resident for the full year.


If the filing requirement is unclear (refundables, prior-year zero tax, residency changes),[Ask TaxGPT Research](https://www.taxgpt.com/tax-research-software) to confirm whether estimated tax is required under § 6654.


## Form 1040-ES Line by Line Preparation Checklist


### A. Identify All Taxes and Credits Included in the Estimate


☐ Include income tax, self-employment tax, and AMT where applicable.


☐ Compute expected refundable credits (EIC, PTC, etc.) and subtract from estimated tax.


☐ Consider Additional Medicare Tax and Net Investment Income Tax, both of which affect required annual payment under § 6654.


### B. Compute the Estimated Tax Correctly


☐ Use prior-year return as baseline; adjust for current-year known changes.


☐ Complete the Form 1040-ES worksheet.


☐ If income is uneven, consider the annualized income installment method and attach Form 2210 Schedule AI.


☐ For W-2 clients, consider increasing withholding via Form W-4 to reduce or eliminate required installments.


### C. Apply Safe Harbor Rules (Critical for Penalty Avoidance)


☐ Prior-year safe harbor: Pay 100% of prior-year tax (110%if AGI > $150k / $75k MFS).


☐ Current-year safe harbor: Pay 90% of current-year tax.


☐ Apply the correct statutory rules under IRC § 6654(d)(1)(B) and (C).


**Note** : Using the wrong prior-year tax amount for fiscal year or short year clients results in immediate penalty exposure.


[Ask TaxGPT Research](https://www.taxgpt.com/tax-research-software) to confirm which safe harbor applies (100/110% vs 90%) and what “prior-year tax” means for short-year or fiscal-year clients.


### D. Determine Installment Due Dates & Amounts


#### Standard due dates (Calendar-year clients):


- 1st: April 15, 2025
- 2nd: June 16, 2025
- 3rd: September 15, 2025
- 4th: January 15, 2026


**Note** : If filing the return by February 2, 2026 and paying in full, the January payment may be skipped.


#### Fiscal-year clients:


Installments due on the 15th day of the 4th, 6th, and 9th months and the 1st month of the following fiscal year.


#### Short taxable years:


Apply Treas. Reg. § 1.6654-3 for proportional adjustments and annualization.


**Note** : Clients with changes to their annual accounting period must consider short-year rules.


### E. Special Rules for Farmers & Fishers


☐ Only one installment required: January 15 of the following year — equal to 66⅔%of current-year tax.


☐ Or file by March 2, 2026 and pay in full (avoids estimated payments).


☐ Eligibility: ≥ two-thirds of gross income from farming/fishing in current or prior year.


### F. Treat Withholding Correctly


☐ Wage withholding is deemed paid evenly across all installments unless actual dates are proven.


☐ Refundable credits reduce “tax” for § 6654 calculations; withholding counts separately.
