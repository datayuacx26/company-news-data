# Form 1099-INT Checklist for Tax Professionals

Form 1099-INT looks simple until you deal with mismatched TINs, forgotten backup withholding triggers, filing deadline issues, or a client who overlooked consolidated accounts.


A single missed solicitation, a misunderstood reporting rule, or a delayed backup withholding response can trigger penalties under sections 6721 and 6722.


Incorrect or missing 1099-INT filings can result in:


- Filing penalties per recipient


- Payee complaints


- IRS B-notice cycles and re-solicitations


- Mandatory 24 percent backup withholding


- Time-consuming corrections and administrative cleanup


This checklist keeps your filings compliant, audit-ready, and penalty-free.


## Before You Begin: Pre-Filing Documentation and Eligibility Checks


Gather these items before preparing any 1099-INT forms:


☐ Confirm you are the payer. Filing is required if you paid at least $10 of reportable interest to any single person or entity during the calendar year in your trade or business..


☐ Verify the payment is reportable interest, including deposit interest, registered obligations, savings certificates, interest retained by insurers, and other section 6049 categories.


☐ Review statutory exemptions. Some foreign recipients and payment types are exempt under section 6049(b).


☐ Collect Form W-9 for every payee to establish name, TIN, and backup withholding certification.


☐ Document TIN solicitations:


- Initial solicitation
- Annual solicitation
- B-notice procedures


- Confirm whether backup withholding is required for missing or incorrect TINs, underreporting notices, or non-responsive payees.


Need help determining whether a foreign payee is exempt under section 6049(b)? Use[TaxGPT Research](https://www.taxgpt.com/tax-research-software) to retrieve the exact statutory exception language.


## Form 1099-INT Reporting Checklist


(Line-Item Compliance)


### Step 1 – Confirm Reportable Interest


☐ Total interest paid per recipient is at least $10.


☐ Section 6049(b) exceptions reviewed and correctly applied.


☐ Consolidated accounts aggregated correctly.


### Step 2 – Validate Payee Name and TIN


☐ Obtain a certified TIN via Form W-9 or a valid substitute form.


☐ Apply initial and annual solicitations if the TIN is missing or incorrect.


☐ For IRS B-notices:


- Notify the payee within 15 business days


- Begin backup withholding 30 business days after the notice if no corrected W-9 is provided


### Step 3 – Apply Backup Withholding Rules


☐ Apply 24 percent backup withholding where required.


☐ Deposit withheld tax according to federal deposit rules under section 31.6302.


☐ Report backup withholding on:


- Form 1099-INT (copies provided to payees and IRS)


- Form 945 for annual withholding reporting


If you're unsure whether a specific payment triggers mandatory backup withholding,[Ask TaxGPT Research](https://www.taxgpt.com/tax-research-software) to review the IRS criteria and exceptions.


### Step 4 – Prepare the Recipient Statement (Copy B)


Must be furnished by February 2, 2026 (January 31, or the next business day when January 31 falls on a weekend). Include:


☐ Total interest income


☐ Federal income tax withheld, if any


☐ Payer name, address, and TIN


☐ Required IRS disclosure legend


Special rule: If backup withholding was applied, you must furnish a statement even if total interest is below $10.


### Step 5 – File Copy A With the IRS


☐ File by the IRS due date (March 2, 2026 for paper, March 31, 2026 for electronic).


☐ E-file if submitting 10 or more information returns in aggregate.


☐ Follow Publication 1220 e-file formatting rules.


☐ Note future system changes: FIRE system retires after CY 2026; IRIS becomes the sole platform.


### Step 6 – Confirm Interest-Specific Rules


☐ Include all reportable categories under section 6049.


☐ Follow special timing rules for items like U.S. savings bonds.


☐ Apply consolidated broker statement timing rules (due February 15 if used).
