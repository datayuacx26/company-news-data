# Form 8949 Checklist for Tax Professionals

Form 8949 is the IRS’s primary reconciliation tool for capital gains and losses. It ensures that every sale or exchange of capital assets aligns with Forms 1099-B and 1099-S before flowing to Schedule D. Whether you prepare returns for individuals, partnerships, trusts, or corporations, Form 8949 is the foundation for accurate capital gain reporting.

## Form 8949 Professional Checklist


### 1. Confirm filing requirement


☐ Verify that Form 8949 is required for the year.


☐ It is used to reconcile all sales and exchanges of capital assets with information reported on Forms 1099-B and 1099-S.


☐ Required for individuals, estates, trusts, partnerships, and corporations unless the limited summary-reporting exception applies.


If the summary-reporting exception is unclear or you need verification for a specific fact pattern, Ask[TaxGPT Research](https://www.taxgpt.com/tax-research-software) to confirm whether Form 8949 is mandatory for the year.


### 2. Separate transactions correctly


Prepare separate parts for short-term and long-term transactions.


Short-term:


☐ Part I using checkboxes A, B, or C


Long-term:


☐ Part II using checkboxes D, E, or F


Each checkbox corresponds to whether the broker reported basis to the IRS and whether a Form 1099-B was issued.


### 3. Apply the “Exception 1” rule


If all Form 1099-Bs show that:


☐ Basis was reported to the IRS, and


☐ No adjustments are required,


You may report summary totals directly on Schedule D without completing Form 8949.


Broker statements must still be retained in workpapers.


[Ask TaxGPT Research](https://www.taxgpt.com/tax-research-software) to double-check whether your client’s transactions qualify for Exception 1 or require individual reporting on Form 8949.


### 4. Data accuracy for each line item


For every transaction report:


☐ Proceeds in column (d)


☐ Cost or basis in column (e)


☐ Adjustments in columns (f) and (g) as needed


☐ A valid adjustment code whenever column (f) is used


Amounts must match the underlying 1099-B or 1099-S records unless adjustments are documented.


### 5. Maintain complete basis support


Maintain records for:


☐ Purchase price and dates


☐ Commissions and transactional fees


☐ Adjustments such as depreciation, distributions, wash sale basis increases, and carryover basis for gifts


These support entries in columns (e), (f), and (g).


### 6. Proper broker coordination


☐ Use the checkbox on Form 1099-B to determine whether the transaction belongs in boxes A, B, C, D, E, or F.


☐ Separate covered and noncovered securities for proper reporting.


☐ Reconcile all 1099-B totals with your Form 8949 entries before transferring to Schedule D.


### 7. Covered security rules


☐ Understand when brokers are required to report basis for covered securities.


☐ Covered vs noncovered classification affects which checkbox to select and whether basis is assumed to match IRS data.


### 8. Digital asset dispositions


Report sales, swaps, and other dispositions of digital assets, including:


- Cryptocurrency
- NFTs
- Tokenized assets


Retain basis and FMV records.


Ensure Form 1040’s digital asset question is answered consistently with reported activity.


### 9. 1099-K for personal property


For non-deductible personal-use property sold at a loss:


☐ Report the sale on Form 8949


☐ Use adjustment code L


☐ Enter a positive adjustment in column (g) to bring the loss to zero


### 10. Wash sale and related-party rules


☐ Apply wash sale loss disallowance under section 1091.


☐ Adjust basis on replacement shares and reflect disallowed loss in column (f) with appropriate coding.


☐ Apply related-party loss rules under section 267 when relevant.
