# R&D Tax Credit Explained: How to Qualify and Claim Your Business Incentives

## How to Claim the R&D Tax Credit


Claiming the **R&D Tax Credit** involves a structured process, including[completing IRS Form 6765](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-6765) , which is specifically designed for **Credit for Increasing Research Activities** . Proper documentation and accurate calculations are essential for ensuring compliance and maximizing the credit’s value.


### Step-by-Step Guide to Filling Out IRS Form 6765


1. **Gather Documentation** : Before beginning, collect all records of qualified expenses (e.g., wages, supplies, contract research) and any documentation supporting your R&D activities.
2. **Complete Section A or Section B** :some text


- **Section A** : Use this to claim the[Regular Credit](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i6765) , following the traditional method of calculating the credit based on a fixed base percentage and gross receipts.
- **Section B** : Choose this section to claim the **Alternative Simplified Credit (ASC)** , which simplifies the calculation by basing it on recent R&D expenditures without needing historical data.


3. **Identify and Complete Additional Sections as Required** :some text


- **Section C** : List any additional forms or schedules required based on your business’s structure, which may impact how the credit is calculated or reported.
- **Section D** : For qualified small businesses (QSBs) electing to use the credit against payroll tax, this section allows you to specify this election, especially helpful for startups with limited income tax liability.


4. **Calculate the Credit Amount** : Businesses are advised to calculate the credit under both the Regular and ASC methods, choosing the option that yields the greatest tax benefit.
5. **Submit with Tax Return** : Attach the completed Form 6765 to your company’s tax return. Be sure to keep copies of all supporting documents.


### Documentation and Record-Keeping Requirements


Proper documentation is required for substantiating the **R&D Tax Credit** claim. Required records may include financial statements, payroll records, and detailed project notes. Documentation should capture:


- **Technical Project Details** : Project descriptions, objectives, and technical uncertainties faced.
- **Employee Timesheets** : Detailed tracking of hours spent by employees on R&D activities.
- **Receipts and Invoices** : Proof of eligible expenses, such as materials used in R&D or fees for external research contractors.
- **Experimentation Logs** : Documentation of the processes, models, and alternatives tested.


Having this documentation in order helps verify qualifying expenses and activity compliance, reducing the risk of disallowance during IRS audits.


### Options for Calculating the Credit


There are two primary methods to calculate the R&D Tax Credit, allowing businesses to choose the one that provides the largest benefit.


1. **Regular Credit** : The Regular Credit calculation considers historical spending and involves a fixed-base percentage based on past R&D activities. It generally requires consistent R&D spending over multiple years, making it beneficial for companies with steady or increasing R&D investments over time.
2. **Alternative Simplified Credit (ASC)** : The ASC is a simpler option that calculates the credit based on current and recent R&D expenditures. Companies opting for ASC do not need historical data beyond the prior three years, making it ideal for newer businesses or those without extensive R&D records.


### Additional Forms Based on Business Structure


Certain **business structures** may require additional forms or schedules to claim the credit:


- **Partnerships and S Corporations** : Must complete additional schedules to pass the credit through to partners or shareholders.
- **Qualified Small Businesses (QSBs)** : Eligible startups with[gross receipts under $5 million](https://www.journalofaccountancy.com/issues/2023/jan/research-credit-payroll-tax-offset.html) can elect to apply the R&D Tax Credit against up to $250,000 in payroll taxes. These companies can utilize Section D of Form 6765 to specify this election, especially useful for startups with limited or no federal income tax liability.
- **Payroll Tax Offset for QSBs:** Qualified Small Businesses (QSBs) can elect to apply the **R&D Tax Credit** against their **payroll tax liability** . Eligible QSBs can offset up to[$250,000 annually for up to five years](https://assets.kpmg.com/content/dam/kpmg/us/pdf/2020/03/20127.pdf) . This election is made on[Section D of Form 6765](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/i6765.pdf) and allows startups to reduce payroll taxes, freeing up cash flow to reinvest in growth activities.


## Recent Legislative Changes Impacting the R&D Tax Credit


Legislative changes over recent years have significantly shaped the **R&D Tax Credit** , impacting how businesses can claim and benefit from it. Key laws include the **Tax Cuts and Jobs Act (TCJA)** and the **Protecting Americans from Tax Hikes (PATH) Act** , each introducing specific provisions and implications for R&D expenditures.


### Impact of the TCJA on R&D Expense Amortization


The **Tax Cuts and Jobs Act (TCJA) of 2017** introduced a significant change to R&D expense treatment starting in **2022** . Under the TCJA,[businesses are no longer allowed to deduct R&D expenses in the year they’re incurred](https://taxfoundation.org/research/all/federal/research-development-expensing-tcja/) . Instead, companies must **amortize these expenses over five years** for domestic research or **15 years for foreign research** , starting from the midpoint of the taxable year in which the expense was incurred. This shift in amortization requirements has impacted cash flow for businesses heavily invested in R&D, as they now face a delay in fully realizing the tax benefits of these expenses.


### Changes Brought by the PATH Act


The **PATH Act of 2015** made the R&D Tax Credit a **permanent feature of the U.S. tax code** under **Section 41** , which provided long-term certainty for businesses investing in R&D. The PATH Act also expanded the credit’s applicability by allowing **eligible small businesses** to[apply the R&D credit against alternative minimum tax (AMT)](https://www.journalofaccountancy.com/issues/2019/feb/r-d-tax-credit-against-payroll-tax-amt.html) and **payroll tax** liabilities. This extension opened the credit to more small-to-medium-sized companies, especially startups, helping them benefit from R&D tax incentives even if they lacked federal income tax liabilities.


### Future Considerations and Potential Legislative Updates


Future legislative changes may address **amortization requirements** established by the TCJA, as some lawmakers and industry advocates push to reinstate immediate deductibility of R&D expenses to ease cash flow constraints on innovative businesses. Additionally, there is ongoing discussion around **expanding or modifying the R&D Tax Credit** to align with evolving technology and industry needs. Companies should stay informed about potential updates, as any changes could further enhance the credit’s benefits or introduce new compliance requirements.


## Eligible Industries for R&D Tax Credit


The **R&D Tax Credit** is accessible across a broad range of industries, providing financial incentives for businesses engaging in innovative research and development activities. The **manufacturing industry** frequently qualifies for the R&D Tax Credit due to ongoing efforts to improve products, processes, and production efficiencies. **Software development** companies often engage in activities that qualify for the R&D Tax Credit, such as creating new applications, enhancing software performance, and developing complex algorithms. ### Biotechnology


The **biotechnology industry** benefits extensively from the R&D Tax Credit due to its focus on developing innovative products like new pharmaceuticals, medical devices, and diagnostic technologies. **Engineering firms** working on projects that require advanced technical solutions or novel design approaches are also eligible. ### Agriculture


In the **agriculture industry** , businesses can claim the R&D Tax Credit for research aimed at improving crop yields, developing new farming techniques, or creating more sustainable and resilient agricultural practices. Companies involved in developing bioengineered crops, improving soil health, or experimenting with agricultural technology often qualify for the credit.


### Food and Beverage


The **food and beverage industry** benefits from the R&D Tax Credit through activities focused on product development and improvement, such as creating new recipes, enhancing food preservation methods, or developing innovative packaging solutions. Companies in this sector also qualify when they conduct research to meet regulatory standards or to achieve better product consistency and quality.

### FAQs


#### What expenses qualify for the R&D Tax Credit?


Qualifying expenses include **wages** paid to employees engaged in R&D, **costs of supplies** used in experimentation, and **contract research expenses** for R&D performed by third parties in the U.S. Certain **cloud computing costs** related to R&D activities may also qualify.


#### Can startups claim the R&D Tax Credit?


Yes, startups can benefit from the R&D Tax Credit. **Qualified Small Businesses (QSBs)** with less than $5 million in gross receipts and within their first five years can apply the credit against payroll taxes, providing cash flow benefits even if they have little to no income tax liability.


#### How has the TCJA affected R&D deductions?


The **Tax Cuts and Jobs Act (TCJA)** introduced a requirement to **amortize R&D expenses** over five years for U.S.-based research (15 years for foreign research), beginning in 2022. This change limits the ability to immediately deduct R&D expenses, impacting cash flow for some companies.


#### What is the difference between the Regular Credit and the Alternative Simplified Credit?


The **Regular Credit** calculation uses a fixed base percentage and considers historical R&D spending, while the **Alternative Simplified Credit (ASC)** is calculated based on recent R&D expenditures without the need for extensive historical data. Businesses are advised to calculate both and choose the option offering the greater benefit.


#### How long can unused R&D credits be carried forward?


Unused R&D Tax Credits can generally be **carried forward for up to 20 years** , allowing companies to apply the credit in future years when they have tax liabilities. This flexibility makes the credit valuable over the long term, even if it can’t be fully used immediately.


#### How much is the R&D tax credit worth?


The R&D Tax Credit is typically worth **6% to 8%** of qualified annual R&D expenses. The exact amount depends on the method used for calculation (Regular Credit or Alternative Simplified Credit) and the company’s qualifying research expenses.


#### What activities qualify for the R&D credit?


Activities aimed at **developing or improving products, processes, software, techniques, or formulas** typically qualify, as long as they meet the IRS’s four-part test (purpose, technological nature, uncertainty elimination, and experimentation process).


#### Can small businesses claim the R&D credit?


Yes, small businesses can claim the R&D Tax Credit. Eligible small businesses (QSBs) can even apply the credit against payroll taxes, making it accessible for startups with limited or no income tax liability.


#### What is the 25/25 limitation?


The **25/25 limitation** refers to a cap on the R&D credit for C corporations with over $25,000 in regular tax liability. These corporations cannot offset more than 75% of their tax liability by utilizing the R&D credit.


#### How much R&D expenses can be claimed?


There is no specific cap on the total **qualified R&D expenses** a business can utilize when calculating the R&D credit amount. However, limitations apply based on tax liability and the chosen calculation method, so companies should carefully calculate to maximize their credit.
