# The 2025 Tax Bracket Adjustments Every Taxpayer Should Know

Each year, the IRS revises a range of tax provisions to adjust for inflation, aiming to prevent “ **bracket creep** .”


This term refers to the gradual shift of taxpayers into higher tax brackets due to inflation alone, rather than any real increase in their purchasing power, which can also reduce the value of tax credits and deductions.


Previously, inflation adjustments were based on the **Consumer Price Index** (CPI). However, the Tax Cuts and Jobs Act (TCJA) of 2017 changed the measure to the Chained Consumer Price Index (C-CPI), which typically reflects slower inflation growth than the traditional CPI.


For the 2025 tax year, the IRS has announced inflation adjustments that will appear on tax returns filed in 2026.


On average, these adjustments increase tax brackets and thresholds by approximately 2.8%.

## Impact on Income Brackets


- **For Lower-Income Taxpayers:** With expanded thresholds, more income may be subject to lower tax rates, potentially reducing the overall tax burden for low-income households.
- **For Middle-Income Earners:** These adjustments can influence withholding amounts, impacting take-home pay. Checking and updating your W-4 may help to keep tax withholding aligned with these new tax brackets.
- **For Higher-Income Individuals:** Although the top marginal tax rate of 37% remains the same, a higher threshold for this bracket means that some income previously taxed at 37% may now fall under a lower rate.


## Changes in the Standard Deduction


In addition to tax brackets, the IRS has also raised standard deduction amounts for 2025, offering tax-saving potential for many:


- **Single Filers:** Standard deduction is $15,750, up from $14,600 in 2024.
- **Married Couples Filing Jointly:** Standard deduction is $31,500, increased from $29,200 in 2024.
- **Heads of Household:** Standard deduction is $23,625, rising from $21,900 in 2024.


The standard personal exemption remains at $0, a change originally made by the TCJA and now made permanent. Note, however, that beginning in 2025 a new temporary deduction of $6,000 is available for individuals age 65 and older (subject to income phase-outs), enacted under the One Big Beautiful Bill Act.
