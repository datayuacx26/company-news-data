# Tax Filing Deadlines & Requirements You Need to Know

While filing taxes may feel overwhelming, staying ahead of important deadlines can save you time, money, and stress. Missing key dates can lead to costly penalties, but preparing early allows for smarter financial planning and peace of mind.


As tax season approaches, it's essential to familiarize yourself with the key deadlines and requirements. Whether you’re an individual taxpayer, self-employed professional, or business owner, being aware of these dates will help you avoid penalties, maximize your refund, and ensure a smoother, hassle-free filing experience.


## Key Tax Deadlines for Individuals


Let’s take a look at the important deadlines for individual taxpayers as we enter the 2026 tax season.


### **January 15, 2026:** Final 2025 Estimated Tax Payment


If you're self-employed, freelancing, or earning income without tax withholding (such as investments), you may be required to pay estimated taxes quarterly. The final estimated payment for the 2025 tax year is due on January 15, 2026. Missing this deadline could lead to penalties, so make sure to file **Form 1040-ES** or use online payment methods to pay your taxes on time.


### **January 31, 2026** : W-2 and 1099 Forms Sent Out


Employers must send out **W-2** forms to their employees by this date, and independent contractors should receive their **1099-NEC** forms. These documents summarize your income from the previous year and are necessary for filing your tax return. If you haven’t received them by early February, follow up with your employer or client to avoid delays.


### February 17, 2026: Renew Your Exemption from Withholding


If you claimed an exemption from federal tax withholding in 2025, this is the last day to renew that exemption for 2026. By filing a new **Form W-4** , you ensure your withholding is correctly adjusted to reflect your current tax situation, especially if you expect to owe little to no federal taxes.


### April 1, 2026: Required Minimum Distribution (RMD) Deadline


If you turned 73 in 2025, the IRS requires that you take your first **Required Minimum Distribution (RMD)** from retirement accounts like 401(k)s or IRAs by this date. Failure to withdraw the correct amount can result in substantial penalties, so consult your financial advisor or retirement plan provider for guidance.


### **April 15, 2026** : Federal Income Tax Day


April 15 is the final day to file your federal income tax return for 2025 and pay any remaining taxes. Filing electronically through tax software is the quickest way to submit your return and reduce errors. If you need more time, you can request an extension until October 15 by filing **Form 4868** , but remember that taxes owed still need to be paid by April 15 to avoid interest and penalties.


### **April 15, 2026** : First Quarter 2026 Estimated Tax Payment


For those required to pay estimated taxes, the first payment for the 2026 tax year is also due on April 15. If you expect to owe at least $1,000 in taxes and don’t have enough withheld from your paycheck, use **Form 1040-ES** to calculate and submit your quarterly payment.


### June 15, 2026: Second Quarter 2026 Estimated Tax Payment


June 15 is the deadline for the second quarterly estimated payment for 2026. Keeping up with estimated tax payments ensures you're not hit with a larger tax bill at the end of the year.


### September 15, 2026: Third Quarter 2026 Estimated Tax Payment


As the year progresses, September 15 is the next deadline for estimated tax payments. Staying current with these payments prevents you from being hit with underpayment penalties.


### October 15, 2026: Extended Tax Return Deadline


If you requested an extension in April, October 15 is your last chance to file your 2025 tax return. Even though the filing deadline has been extended, any taxes owed were still due by April 15, so filing by this date helps you avoid additional late fees.


### December 31, 2026: Year-End Financial Moves


December 31 is the last day to take many actions that could affect your 2026 taxes, like contributing to 401(k) accounts or making charitable donations. These steps could reduce your taxable income and set you up for success in the new year.


### January 15, 2027: Final 2026 Estimated Tax Payment


The last estimated tax payment for the 2026 tax year is due on January 15, 2027. This is your final payment before filing your 2026 tax return in the new year.


## Key Tax Deadlines for Businesses


Businesses also have important tax deadlines to remember throughout the year:


### January 15, 2026: Fourth Quarter 2025 Estimated Tax Payment


Just like individuals, businesses making estimated tax payments must settle their fourth-quarter obligations for 2025 by January 15. Use Form 1120-W worksheet to calculate your tax liability and avoid underpayment penalties.


### January 31, 2026: Issue W-2 and 1099 Forms


By this date, businesses must send out **W-2** forms to employees and **1099** forms to independent contractors. Failure to send these forms on time can result in penalties, so it’s crucial to get them out early.


### March 16, 2026: Partnership and S Corporation Tax Filing Deadline


Businesses structured as partnerships or S corporations must file their 2025 tax returns by March 16. Use **Form 1065** for partnerships and **Form 1120S** for S corporations, and make sure you include **Schedule K-1** to report the share of income for each partner or shareholder.


### April 15, 2026: C Corporation Tax Filing Deadline


C corporations that operate on a calendar year must file **Form 1120** by April 15. If your corporation follows a fiscal year, the deadline falls on the 15th day of the fourth month after your fiscal year ends. C corporations with a June 30 fiscal year-end have historically followed a special rule (return due the 15th day of the 3rd month after year-end); confirm the current-year due date, as this transitional rule has been phasing out.


### April 15, 2026: First Quarter 2026 Estimated Tax Payment


Corporations must also make their first estimated tax payment for 2026 by April 15. Staying on top of these payments ensures you’re in good standing with the IRS and prevents penalties for underpayment.


### June 15, 2026: Second Quarter 2026 Estimated Tax Payment


The second quarterly estimated tax payment for 2026 is due on June 15. Be sure to keep accurate financial records and adjust your payments as necessary to avoid surprises at year-end.


### September 15, 2026: Third Quarter 2026 Estimated Tax Payment


By September 15, businesses should make their third estimated tax payment for the year. Ensure you’re up-to-date with your payments to avoid penalties for underpayment.


### September 15, 2026: Extended Partnership and S Corporation Filing Deadline


If your business requested an extension, this is the final deadline to file your 2025 partnership or S corporation tax return. Use **Form 7004** to file for an extension and avoid penalties.


### October 15, 2026: Extended C Corporation Filing Deadline


For C corporations that filed for an extension, October 15 is the last day to submit your 2025 tax return. It’s essential to file on time to avoid penalties, but remember, any taxes owed were still due by April 15.


### December 31, 2026: Year-End Tax Moves


December 31 is the last day for businesses to make tax-saving moves for 2026, such as contributing to retirement plans or investing in new equipment. These actions can help reduce your taxable income and give your business a stronger financial position for the next year.
