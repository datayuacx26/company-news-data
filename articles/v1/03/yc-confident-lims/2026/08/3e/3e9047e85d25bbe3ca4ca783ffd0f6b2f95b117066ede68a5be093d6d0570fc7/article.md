### **Why Labs Are Diversifying Beyond Cannabis**


Market volatility, aggressive competition, pricing pressure, compliance burdens, and frequent regulatory changes push labs to look for more stable and scalable revenue streams. The data from our 2025 survey confirms it:


*Pie chart showing diversification plans for cannabis and hemp labs. About 39% of respondents say they already test non-cannabis or non-hemp samples. Another 26% expect to begin non-cannabis/hemp testing within the next year. Around 36% say they are not currently planning to diversify. This shows that a majority of labs are already diversifying or planning to do so soon.*


This signals a clear trend: labs are future-proofing their operations by expanding into adjacent, regulated industries.


‍


### **Which New Industries Labs Are Entering** ‍


###### Among labs already testing non-cannabis samples, the most common industries include:


*Pie chart summarizing which non-cannabis industries diversifying labs test today. About 73% of these labs test Food and Beverage samples and 67% test Nutraceuticals and Supplements. Around 40% work with Personal Care and Cosmetics, 33% with Agriculture, 20% with Environmental testing, 13% with Industrial Chemicals and 7% with Equipment or Hardware. Because labs often serve several industries at once, the percentages sum to more than 100%.*


These industries share something important with cannabis: **lighter compliance requirements, diverse sample matrices, and primarily chemical or microbiological assays** .


‍


### **New Matrices Mean New Assays**


Diversification doesn’t stop at industries—it extends to analytical scope. Labs reported performing a wide range of assays for non-cannabis/hemp matrices, including:


*Pie chart showing which assays labs perform on non-cannabis and non-hemp sample matrices. All responding labs, 100%, report running Microbial assays. About 79% run Moisture or Water Activity, and 71% run Pesticides, Solvents and Metals. Around 64% measure pH and 36% perform Nutrient testing. Roughly 29% run Alkaloid, Protein or Vitamin assays and 21% run DNA, Lipid or Mineral assays. Less than 15% report Flavonoids, Hydrocarbons or other specialized assays. Because labs can perform many of these assays at once, percentages exceed 100% in total.*


Many labs also indicated plans to add **new assays within the next year** , especially in pesticides, alkaloids, lipids, and microbials—areas where method complexity and traceability matter most.


‍


### **How Much Revenue Comes From Non-Cannabis/Hemp Testing?**


For most labs, diversification is still early—but growing:


- **66% of labs reported that non-cannabis/hemp work contributes less than 25% of their total revenue**
- Labs expect that share to increase, with more respondents projecting **25–90% of revenue** coming from non-cannabis samples by the end of this year


In other words, diversification is no longer experimental—it’s strategic.
