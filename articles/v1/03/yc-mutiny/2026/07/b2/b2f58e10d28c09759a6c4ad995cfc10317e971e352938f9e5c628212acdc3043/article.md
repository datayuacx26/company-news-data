# Pricing proposal template for B2B sales (with example)

A pricing proposal template is a reusable structure that orders the seven parts of a proposal: a considered header, a value-framing summary, packaging and pricing options, a marked recommendation, a rationale tied to the buyer’s outcomes, light proof, and a clear next step. Start from the template, then personalize each section with the buyer’s own words so the proposal reads as a recommendation built for them.


This guide covers why a template helps, the template itself, a filled-in example, and how to adapt it per deal. For the full process, see[how to write a pricing proposal that wins deals](https://www.mutinyhq.com/blog/how-to-write-a-pricing-proposal) , and for the anatomy of each section, see[what a pricing proposal should include](https://www.mutinyhq.com/blog/what-should-a-pricing-proposal-include) . You can also start from the[Mutiny pricing proposal blueprint](https://www.mutinyhq.com/blueprints/pricing-proposal) .


### Why use a pricing proposal template?


A template helps because it gets a strong first draft on screen in minutes and keeps every rep’s proposals consistent, which makes them easier to coach and faster to personalize. It also protects the deal, since the average B2B buying group runs to six to ten people and a consistent structure keeps a committee aligned.


According to[Gartner’s B2B buying research](https://www.gartner.com/en/sales/insights/b2b-buying-journey) , the typical buying group is six to ten stakeholders, so the proposal is read by people who never met the rep. A template ensures every one of those readers gets the value frame, the options, and the rationale in the same clear order. The template is a starting point, and personalization is what wins:[Proposify’s analysis](https://www.proposify.com/state-of-proposals-2024) of more than 1.28 million proposals found that customizing 30% of the executive summary for the client lifts close rates by 50%.


### The pricing proposal template


Copy this structure and fill each section with the specifics of your deal:


1.


Header. \[Your company\] proposal prepared for \[prospect company\]. Logos side by side, prepared-for and date.


2.


Value summary. The problem \[prospect company\] is solving, in their words, and what solving it unlocks over the next \[timeframe\]. One short paragraph written for the budget approver.


3.


Packaging and pricing options. Two or three plans, each with name, price, and three to five key inclusions:


-


\[Plan A\] at \[price\]: \[who it fits\]


-


\[Plan B\] at \[price\]: \[who it fits\]


-


\[Plan C\] at \[price\]: \[who it fits\]


1.


Recommendation. \[Recommended plan\], marked clearly, with one line on why it is the best fit for a team like \[prospect company\].


2.


Why this fits. Two or three bullets tying the recommended plan to the outcomes \[prospect company\] named in discovery.


3.


Proof. One relevant customer logo or a short quote from a company like \[prospect company\], plus one before-and-after result.


4.


Next step. The single action you want the approver to take, with a direct way to take it (sign, approve, or book the \[review type\]).


5.


Appendix (optional). Security, legal, and implementation detail, kept out of the main proposal so the core stays focused on the decision.


### A filled-in example


Here is the template completed for a hypothetical deal with Northstar, a 500-person SaaS company:


-


Header: “Acme proposal prepared for Northstar, July 2026,” logos side by side.


-


Value summary: Northstar’s RevOps team loses about 10 hours a week rebuilding pipeline reports by hand, which slows every forecast and delays decisions.


-


Options: Core at $12,000, Growth at $18,000, Scale at $28,000, each with key inclusions.


-


Recommendation: Growth, marked as the best fit for a team Northstar’s size.


-


Why this fits: Growth covers the two workflows Northstar named, automated reporting and live pipeline health, and scales to their planned headcount.


-


Proof: a SaaS RevOps customer quote and a before-and-after showing reporting time cut from 10 hours to under 1.


-


Next step: approve Growth at $18,000 annually, then book the security review.


### How do you adapt a pricing proposal template for each deal?


Adapt the template by rewriting the value summary and the why-this-fits section in the buyer’s own language, swapping in proof that matches their industry, and setting the recommendation to the plan that serves their stated goals. Keep the structure fixed and change the specifics, so the proposal stays consistent while reading as tailored to the account.


Heavy reliance on a generic template shows, and buyers can feel it. Treat the template as scaffolding for the parts that repeat, then invest your time in the value summary and rationale, which are the sections that move an approver.
