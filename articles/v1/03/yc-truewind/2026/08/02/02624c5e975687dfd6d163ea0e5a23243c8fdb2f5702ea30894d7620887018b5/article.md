## What Is FloQast and How Does It Work?


FloQast is a close management and accounting workflow tool built for mid-market and enterprise finance teams. It sits alongside the general ledger to help accounting teams coordinate month-end close tasks, track reconciliation sign-offs, and manage flux commentary across distributed teams.


The core workflow looks like this: controllers build a[close checklist](https://www.truewind.ai/blog/building-a-close-checklist-that-actually-works) inside FloQast, assign tasks to individual team members, and track completion status through a centralized dashboard. Reconciliations get tied to supporting documentation, and each sign-off creates an audit trail. Flux analysis gets captured as written commentary attached to account balances.


FloQast integrates with GL systems including NetSuite, Sage Intacct, QuickBooks Online, and Microsoft Dynamics to pull trial balance data for reconciliation matching. The matching process flags variances between the GL balance and the supporting document so preparers and reviewers know which accounts still need attention.


Here is what FloQast primarily handles:


- Close task orchestration, assigning checklist items to owners with due dates and status tracking across the full team
- Reconciliation management, pulling GL balances and matching them to supporting[workpapers for Sage Intacct](https://www.truewind.ai/blog/workpaper-automation-sage-intacct-guide) so reviewers see open items at a glance
- Flux and variance commentary, attaching written explanations to balance changes for review and audit purposes
- Reporting and dashboards, giving controllers visibility into close progress, bottlenecks, and completion rates by preparer


What FloQast does not do is automate the underlying accounting work. Transaction coding, journal entry preparation, and accrual calculations stay with your team. FloQast tracks whether the work got done; it does not do the work itself.


## Why Consider FloQast Alternatives?


FloQast works well for many finance teams, but it has real limitations that push certain organizations to look elsewhere. Understanding where those friction points show up helps clarify what to look for in an alternative.


Here are the most common reasons teams start looking at other options:


- FloQast is built around close checklist management and task tracking. Teams that need deeper workflow coverage, such as transaction coding, accrual preparation, or variance analysis, often find they still need additional tools to fill those gaps, which adds cost and coordination overhead.
- Pricing can be a barrier for smaller teams. FloQast targets mid-market and enterprise finance organizations, and its contract structure reflects that. Leaner teams or early-stage companies sometimes find the annual commitment hard to defend relative to what they actually use.
- Implementation takes time. Getting FloQast configured to reflect your actual close process requires upfront work, and teams without dedicated project resources often feel that burden acutely during their first few closes.
- AI capabilities vary in depth. FloQast has added AI features over time, but the core product remains a workflow orchestration and task management tool. Teams looking for AI that directly touches accounting execution, like transaction classification or automated journal entry preparation, may find that FloQast's AI layer stays closer to the surface of the close process.
- Sage Intacct users with complex dimensional structures sometimes report that FloQast's reconciliation and close tracking works at a level of abstraction above what their GL can actually execute, a gap that explains[why Sage Intacct close takes too long](https://www.truewind.ai/blog/sage-intacct-close-takes-too-long) for many teams, which means someone still has to do the dimensional posting work manually.
