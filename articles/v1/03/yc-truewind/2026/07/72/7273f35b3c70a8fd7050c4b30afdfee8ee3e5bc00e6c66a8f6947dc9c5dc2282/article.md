# Pilot vs. Fondo: Comparison, Pricing, and Alternatives in 2026

Choosing between Pilot and Fondo usually comes down to one question: do you want a software-led bookkeeping service that scales into full-service accounting and tax, or a startup-focused firm built specifically around Delaware C-corp bookkeeping, tax, and credits with a dedicated accountant? Pilot offers a software-led entry plan and adds US-based bookkeepers on higher tiers, alongside separate tax and CFO offerings. Fondo is purpose-built for startups and bundles multiple bookkeeping cadences with a startup tax package and a tax-credit service. Both are legitimate managed-accounting options; the right fit depends on your entity structure, close cadence, and how much accounting help you need beyond the books.


This comparison is current as of July 13, 2026. Pricing and plan details change frequently, so treat every figure here as directional and confirm the latest on each provider's official pricing page before you buy.


## Key takeaways


- **Pilot** offers tiered monthly bookkeeping plans that scale from a $99/month software-led cash-basis close to accrual accounting with a dedicated US bookkeeper, plus separate tax and CFO services. See[Pilot pricing](https://pilot.com/pricing-software) .
- **Fondo** is built for Delaware C-corps and offers monthly, quarterly, and annual bookkeeping with a real accountant, a dashboard, and Slack support, alongside its TaxPass filing package. See[Fondo pricing](https://fondo.com/pricing) .
- Pilot generally fits companies that want a productized service scaling toward full accrual accounting and CFO support; Fondo generally fits startups that want bookkeeping, startup tax, and tax-credit help from one startup-specialized firm.
- Both combine software with human accountants. The difference is emphasis, packaging, and how each maps to your stage and structure.
- **Truewind** is a distinct alternative: a digital accountant that automates close and reconciliation work inside your existing ledger, with reviewer approval, rather than replacing your books with a managed service.

## What does Pilot offer?


[Pilot](https://pilot.com/pricing-software) packages bookkeeping as a tiered software-led service with human bookkeepers, and it separates bookkeeping, tax, and CFO into distinct service lines.


The[Essentials plan](https://pilot.com/pricing-software) costs $99 per month and covers up to $100,000 in monthly expenses. It is a software-led, cash-basis monthly close with a standard chart of accounts, a year-end package for your tax preparer, AI answers about past transactions and current cash flow, and in-app support. It is designed to be lighter-touch and productized rather than heavily customized.


[Core](https://pilot.com/pricing-software) starts at $299 per month billed annually at the expense level displayed on Pilot's pricing page. It adds a dedicated US-based bookkeeper, cash- or accrual-basis bookkeeping, a custom chart of accounts, and data from bank accounts, cards, payroll, accounts payable, inventory, and payment platforms. Pilot says Core reports are delivered on the 10th business day.


[Custom](https://pilot.com/pricing-software) is for companies with complex structures or reporting needs. It targets reports on the sixth business day and can add accounts receivable, accounts payable, payroll administration, CFO advisory, financial planning, and fundraising support.


Pilot's[published tax packages](https://pilot.com/pricing-software) start at $750 for Starter, $2,450 for Essentials, and $5,400 for Standard. Each requires Pilot Bookkeeping, and the included returns and support differ by tier. The same official pricing page lists its R&D tax-credit service at 20% of the credit received and CFO services starting at $1,750 per month billed annually for the Basic plan.


Pilot should not be understood as a purely manual bookkeeping firm. Its entry plan is software-led, its product includes AI-assisted financial answers, and its higher tiers combine that software with human review and support.


## What does Fondo offer?


[Fondo](https://fondo.com/pricing) is built specifically for startups, particularly Delaware C-corps, and its packaging reflects that focus. It combines accountants with software and offers different close cadences as a company grows.


Fondo's[monthly bookkeeping plan](https://fondo.com/pricing) includes a dedicated accountant, a dedicated Slack channel, a finance dashboard, monthly close, and financial reporting. As of July 13, 2026, Fondo advertises a limited-time starting price of $399 per month, down from its normally displayed $499 starting price, and positions the plan for seed-stage and later startups.


Earlier-stage teams can choose[quarterly bookkeeping](https://fondo.com/pricing) from $1,198 per quarter or **annual** bookkeeping from $950 per year. Fondo positions those options for pre-seed startups. The same official page lists catch-up bookkeeping from $950 for companies that need historical books brought up to date.


Fondo's tax offering,[TaxPass](https://fondo.com/pricing) , currently advertises a limited-time price of $1,450 per year, down from a normally displayed starting price of $1,950. The published package covers a federal corporate return, one state corporate return, Delaware franchise tax, and one foreign-shareholder filing. Additional state, international, and tax-credit work may cost extra; Fondo's official page lists tax add-ons from $500.


Fondo also offers R&D tax-credit support. Its site references potential credits of up to $500,000, but eligibility and the amount depend on a startup's facts and qualified activity. Buyers should treat that figure as a statutory ceiling or marketing reference, not an expected or guaranteed result.


Fondo is narrower by design. That can be a strength for Delaware C-corps that want a provider accustomed to venture-backed startup bookkeeping and tax requirements.


## What should you compare before choosing?


When you strip away marketing, a few practical factors decide the better fit:


- **Entity structure.** If you are a Delaware C-corp raising venture capital, Fondo's packaging is tailored to you. If your structure is different or likely to become more complex, ask Pilot how Core or Custom would handle it.
- **Accounting basis and close timing.** Pilot explicitly offers accrual accounting on Core and Custom and publishes report-delivery targets. Fondo lets you adjust bookkeeping cadence from annual to quarterly to monthly as the company grows.
- **How you want tax packaged.** Fondo's TaxPass maps to a common Delaware C-corp filing footprint. Pilot's tax services are separate annual packages and require an active bookkeeping plan.
- **Advisory needs.** Pilot publishes a dedicated CFO service line. If strategic finance support is important, compare its scope and cost with an independent fractional CFO or another provider.
- **Support style.** Fondo includes a dedicated Slack channel on its monthly plan. Pilot uses in-app messaging, with email and phone access listed on Core.
- **Tax-credit economics.** Pilot publishes a percentage-based R&D fee. Fondo offers credit support but buyers should confirm its current fee, scope, eligibility process, and documentation standards directly.


During diligence, ask both providers who owns your account, what work is automated, what a human reviews, what happens when the books are late or wrong, which services cost extra, and how easily you can export your records if you leave.


## Who should choose Pilot or Fondo?


**Choose Pilot** if you want a productized bookkeeping service that can scale from a light cash-basis close into full accrual accounting, custom reporting, and optional CFO or back-office support. It can also fit companies whose structure or finance needs extend beyond the typical Delaware C-corp startup package.


**Choose Fondo** if you are a Delaware C-corp that wants a startup-focused provider, a dedicated accountant, and the option to keep bookkeeping, corporate tax, and tax-credit work with one firm. Its quarterly and annual cadences may also appeal to very early-stage teams that do not yet need a monthly close.
