## Types of Business Phone Systems Compared


Understanding your options is the first step in choosing how to choose the right solution. Let's look at the three main types of business phone systems available today: traditional landlines, VoIP systems, and cellular-based systems.


### Traditional Landlines


Traditional landlines are the copper wire-based office phone systems that have been around for decades, transmitting voice signals over a physical network of cables.


**Pros:**


- Traditional landlines represent a familiar technology for most people.
- Their main advantage has been reliability, especially during power outages, as long as the copper lines are intact and you have a non-powered phone.


**Cons:**


- Traditional landlines are being phased out by most major carriers, slowly becoming obsolete. Maintenance can be costly as the infrastructure ages.
- They are expensive to scale, while offering little to no advanced features compared to modern alternatives.


**Estimated Cost** : Typically $50-$100 per line per month, plus installation and maintenance fees.


### Business VoIP Phone System (Voice over Internet Protocol)


A VoIP business phone system gives you virtual phone numbers that lets you make calls over your internet connection, providing a feature rich solution at lower costs than traditional office phone systems.


**Pros:**


- VoIP phone systems often provide rich functionalities like auto attendants, call recording, and video conferencing — features that some businesses may appreciate and use every day. Auto attendants, in particular, enhance customer service by efficiently directing calls to the right departments or individuals.
- The upfront costs for your VoIP phone can be less expensive than traditional landlines, especially for long-distance calls or international calls.
- Mobile apps for VoIP phone system makes it easy to work-from-anywhere.


**Cons:**


- The biggest drawback of VoIP phone systems is its complete dependence on a stable, high-speed internet connection. If your internet goes down, your phones go down too.
- Call quality can suffer during periods of high bandwidth.
- Setup can be complex, sometimes requiring IT expertise.


**Estimated Cost:** Many VoIP phone systems cost between $10 to $75 per user, per month, depending on the provider and plan, however business VoIP pricing can vary widely based on features and number of users. Watch out for hidden fees for setup, hardware, and premium features.


### Cellular-Based Business Phone Systems


Cellular-based business phone systems utilize cellular networks to provide reliable communication without internet dependency.


You'll usually get a base unit, which automatically uses the strongest local cell tower to power the phones you connect to it.


> **Important Distinction:** Business phone coverage is NOT the same as your cell phone coverage. While your personal cell phone might struggle with poor reception in your area, cellular-based business phone systems such as Community Phone use specialized equipment with antennas that are up to 60 times stronger than regular cell phones. This means reliable, crystal-clear calls even in locations where your mobile device drops calls or has weak signal.


**Pros:**


- Unlike VoIP phone systems, these business phones work without an internet connection, making them a reliable business phone option even when your internet is down or during power outages (thanks to built-in batter backups).
- Get consistent call quality regardless of your internet connectivity.
- Setup is typically very simple—often using a plug-and-play base unit.
- They may also offer advanced features and better value-for-money plans, such as unlimited calling, call routing, collaboration tools, and unified communications for teams.
- Some providers offer mobile apps, so you can manage your calls even while you're away from your desk.


**Cons:**


- May have higher monthly costs compared to basic VoIP plans, though this is often offset by the reliability and advanced features included.


**Estimated Cost:** $30-$60 per line per month, often with simpler pricing structures.
