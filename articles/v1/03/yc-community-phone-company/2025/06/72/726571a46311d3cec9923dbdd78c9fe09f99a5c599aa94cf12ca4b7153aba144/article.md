## How to Set Up Call Forwarding


The way you set up call forwarding depends a bit on your phone provider, but most landlines give you two basic options: dial-in codes or an online account. Here’s how it usually works.


1. Dial the call forwarding activation code (often *72) on your phone.
2. Wait for the dial tone or prompt.
3. Enter the number you want your calls to go to, including the area code.
4. If required by your provider, press the '#' key (or the appropriate key) after entering the forwarding number to complete the setup.


### If You’re Using Dial Codes


This method’s been around forever, but it still works, especially if you’re using a standard landline.


1. Pick up the phone and wait for the dial tone.
2. Dial your provider’s activation code. This is often something like *72.
3. Enter the number you want your calls to go to, including the area code.
4. Listen for a confirmation tone or message. Then hang up.
5. Test it. Call your number from another phone to make sure the forwarding’s working.


To turn off call forwarding:


- Just pick up the handset again and dial the deactivation code (often *73).
- Wait for the confirmation tone and hang up.

If You’re Using an Online Account


Many carriers let you manage call forwarding online, which can be easier, especially if you’d rather not deal with dial codes.


Here’s the general process:


1. Log into your account on your provider’s website.
2. Go to call settings, look for something like “Call Forwarding” or “Call Management.”
3. Enter the forwarding number you want to use.
4. Save your settings and test it out.

## Types of Call Forwarding: How They Work


Not all call forwarding works the same way. Depending on your setup, you can choose how, and when, calls get redirected.


Call forwarding can be set up at the phone level (on your device) or managed by your carrier, depending on your provider and device.


### Unconditional Forwarding


Every call gets sent to another number right away. Your phone won’t ring at all, it's instant and automatic.


### Conditional Forwarding


Calls are only forwarded in specific situations, like when your line is busy, when there’s no answer, or when you’re out of range. It gives you a bit more control.


### Selective Forwarding


Some systems let you forward calls from specific numbers only, say, VIP clients or after-hours calls. You can also set up forwarding rules for a particular contact or group of contacts, allowing more precise control over who gets forwarded.

## Troubleshooting Call Forwarding: What to Do When It’s Not Working


Call forwarding usually just works, but when it doesn’t, it can be frustrating. Here’s what to look for if things go sideways.


### 1. You set it up, but calls still go to your original number


Start simple. Is the number you entered correct? Including the area code? It sounds obvious, but it’s easy to miss a digit.


Also, did you hear a confirmation tone when you set it up? If not, it might not have gone through. Try disabling and re-enabling it just to be sure.


### 2. Forwarding only works sometimes


This is usually because of how the rules are set. If you’re using conditional forwarding (like forwarding only when busy), those conditions might not be getting triggered. Try switching to full forwarding temporarily and see if that works better.


Also, make sure the phone you’re forwarding to is able to take calls, if it’s off, out of service, or in Do Not Disturb mode, the forwarding won’t complete.


### 3. You’re getting unexpected calls


This could mean you’ve got multiple forwarding rules running at once. If your system lets you view them, check for anything old or overlapping.


And if you’ve recently switched carriers or phone systems, sometimes there are default settings applied that you didn’t set yourself.


### 4. Everything was working fine, then it stopped


This one’s tricky. If nothing’s changed on your end, check with your provider, they may have made a backend update that reset your settings.


Also worth restarting your phone system or base unit if you’re using VoIP or something similar.


### 5. Nothing’s helping


Sometimes it’s just easier to call in backup. If you’re with Community Phone, their support folks can usually get to the bottom of things fast. Don’t waste an afternoon troubleshooting if a five-minute call can fix it.


## Tips & Best Practices for Smarter Call Forwarding


First, test it. Forward your calls, then pick up your other phone and call yourself. Try letting it ring, try not answering, just to see what actually happens. You don’t want surprises when a customer is on the line.


Second, keep your setup clean. It’s tempting to create a dozen rules, forward on Mondays here, weekends there, but the more complex it is, the more likely something breaks. Start with one or two simple rules. Add more if you really need them. Make sure call forwarding is turned on or off as needed, and check your device settings or call forwarding status to confirm if it’s currently turned on.


Third, use conditional forwarding if your schedule is predictable. For example, I set mine to only forward after three rings or when I’m in a meeting. That way, I still catch the calls I can take, but the rest don’t die in voicemail.


If you’re using an iPhone, you can set up or check call forwarding by going to Settings > Phone > Call Forwarding. Look for the phone icon in your status bar to see if call forwarding is turned on.


Also, if your system supports it, send a quick text when you miss a call. It doesn’t have to be fancy, “Sorry I missed you, I’ll call back soon” is enough. It tells people you care, and they’re less likely to move on to someone else.


Check your call logs once in a while. You might notice patterns, like missed calls during lunch hours, and adjust your setup to fix it.


And finally, if you’re forwarding to someone else, tell them. Sounds obvious, but I’ve seen situations where someone suddenly gets bombarded with business calls and has no idea why.

### 4. Why isn't my call forwarding working?


A: Common causes include:
