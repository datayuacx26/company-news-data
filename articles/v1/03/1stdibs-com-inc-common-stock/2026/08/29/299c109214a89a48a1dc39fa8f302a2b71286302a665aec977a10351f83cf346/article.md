# These Gorgeous Rooms Celebrate Indoor-Outdoor Living

Photo by Trevor Tondro


Ron Radziner, cofounder of Los Angeles firm Marmol Radziner, designed a[streamlined home in L.A.’s Mandeville Canyon](https://www.1stdibs.com/project/mandeville-canyon-los-angeles-ca/129612/) around a simple premise: Let Mother Earth do the loudest talking.


“We used a palette of dark materials to draw your attention beyond the interior space, allowing the changing light and surrounding trees to become the primary focus,” Radziner says. “Large windows frame the landscape, almost like massive paintings on a wall.”


The primary bedroom, and the terrace just off it, make the case most clearly.


An Extra Wall bed by[Piero Lissoni](https://www.1stdibs.com/creators/piero-lissoni/furniture/) for[Living Divani](https://www.1stdibs.com/creators/living-divani/furniture/) sits beside a custom nightstand designed by Marmol Radziner and produced by its in-house woodshop.[Friedrich Kunath](https://www.1stdibs.com/buy/friedrich-kunath/) ’s acrylic-on-canvas painting *Run Away* adds a jolt of color and humor against the coffee-colored wall.


Outside, an Extra Soft sofa, also by Lissoni for Living Divani, extends “the same horizontal lines and materials across the threshold,” Radziner says. This faces a mid-century-inspired outdoor fireplace built from Petersen Tegl’s Kolumba K55 bricks.


“My approach to designing the home was to help it sit within nature in the purest way possible,” says Radziner. “That goal was a thread I wanted to carry throughout the entire home. It’s all connected.”

[Marie-Christine McNally](https://www.1stdibs.com/design-firms/marie-christine-design/) ’s longtime Manhattan clients wanted their[second home, in Watermill, New York](https://www.1stdibs.com/introspective-magazine/marie-christine-design/) , to accommodate kids, cousins and a constant flow of guests without tipping into chaos.


The house offers multiple places to land, from interior seating areas to porches that feel more like living rooms, including a screened one just off the great room.


Here, a pair of hanging daybeds by Lowcountry Swing Beds flank the fireplace, facing each other across faceted tables that were sourced locally.


Because the porch sits between indoors and out, every piece pulls double duty. A neutral-toned outdoor rug grounds the arrangement, composed of durable furnishings that hold their own against the salt air and shifts in temperature and humidity.


“Every element was chosen to balance beauty with practicality,” McNally says, “and to set the stage for lots of great times and the making of many lasting memories.”

Over more than two decades, an[English-style 1920s brick house in Monroe](https://www.1stdibs.com/project/boxwood-court-residence-monroe-la/409702/) , Louisiana, has been expanded and renovated to keep pace with a growing family and their ambitious art collection. The latest modifications involved the kitchen and adjoining sitting room, which[Lee Ledbetter and Associates](https://www.1stdibs.com/design-firms/lee-ledbetter-and-associates/) remade as the home’s social core — and its most direct link to the garden.


“The kitchen-slash-sitting room opens into a formal courtyard, creating an easy dialogue between architecture and landscape,” Ledbetter says. “From the moment guests step inside, the courtyard is visible through a series of rooms and French doors, establishing a compelling sight line that draws the eye outdoors.”


The room itself combines old-world references with high-quality mid-century pieces. A custom banquette covered in Glant’s Liquid Leather hugs a 1957[pedestal table by Eero Saarinen](https://www.1stdibs.com/creators/eero-saarinen/furniture/tables/tables/) for[Knoll](https://www.1stdibs.com/creators/knoll/furniture/) , which, in turn, is met by a wrought-iron side chair designed by[Dorothy Schindele](https://www.1stdibs.com/creators/dorothy-schindele-1/furniture/) in the 1950s, reupholstered in[Donghia](https://www.1stdibs.com/creators/donghia/furniture/) ’s Crazy Love.


[Paul Tuttle](https://www.1stdibs.com/creators/paul-tuttle/furniture/) bar stools from the 1950s, sourced through[a La MOD](https://www.1stdibs.com/dealers/a-la-mod/) , wear the same red fabric. Near the fireplace, a 1965[Adrian Pearsall](https://www.1stdibs.com/creators/adrian-pearsall/furniture/) swivel lounge chair for[Craft Associates](https://www.1stdibs.com/creators/craft-associates/furniture/) , from[Wright Now](https://www.1stdibs.com/dealers/wright-now/) , sits atop an antique Samarkand Khotan rug from[Mansour](https://www.1stdibs.com/dealers/mansour/) , along with a polished-steel-and-glass[Nicos Zographos](https://www.1stdibs.com/creators/nicos-zographos/) TA.35 side table. Overlooking the vignette is David Bates’s 2010 oil painting *Lantern Fishing* .


[Phillip Jeffries](https://www.1stdibs.com/buy/phillip-jeffries/) ’s Manila Hemp grasscloth wraps the walls in a warm neutral that lets the art and ironwork pop.


“In the South,” Ledbetter says, “where terraces and gardens serve as true extensions of daily living for much of the year, the seamless relationship between inside and out is both timeless and essential.”

[Gil Walsh](https://www.1stdibs.com/design-firms/gil-walsh-interiors/) built her Palm Beach practice on the premise that Florida decorating doesn’t have to involve rattan and parrots. For a family[retreat in Key Largo’s Ocean Reef Club,](https://www.1stdibs.com/project/blue-skies-in-key-largo-key-largo-fl/147301/) she leaned instead into the homeowners’ collection of fine furniture and art, pairing[British colonial](https://www.1stdibs.com/furniture/style/british-colonial/) forms with contemporary accents.


The primary bedroom carries that mix the furthest. A[four-poster bed](https://www.1stdibs.com/buy/four-poster-bed/) is met by gray-upholstered lounge chairs arranged as an intimate sitting area. Overhead, the vaulted ceiling with exposed trusses draws the eye up and out toward a wall of windows.


Stone flooring runs unbroken through the space, with minimal carpeting delineating different zones, like a traditional study next to the patio doors.


“The design is anchored by timeless furnishings, layered textures and tailored detailing,” Walsh says. “Architectural elements and expansive windows invite abundant daylight and frame the lush tropical landscape.”

Architect[William Hefner](https://www.1stdibs.com/introspective-magazine/william-hefner-california-homes/) and designer[Jeremiah Brent](https://www.1stdibs.com/es/project/brentwood-brentwood-ca/327582/) teamed up on a[sprawling Brentwood house](https://www.1stdibs.com/es/project/brentwood-brentwood-ca/327582/) for an Iranian-born couple who wanted to be reminded of their roots while drifting away from the gilded,[Louis XVI](https://www.1stdibs.com/furniture/style/louis-xvi/) –inflected look of their childhood homes.


The result is a[Mediterranean](https://www.1stdibs.com/photos/style/mediterranean/) -inspired brick-and-stone structure built around a courtyard and pierced by a sleek glass-and-steel wing, making the spaces feel both timeworn and edgy. Arched openings inset with glass doors dissolve the boundary between room and garden right at the threshold.


In the entry, a minimalist pendant from[Obsolete](https://www.1stdibs.com/dealers/obsolete/) is suspended above a custom[travertine](https://www.1stdibs.com/furniture/material/travertine/) center table; a[Gio Ponti](https://www.1stdibs.com/creators/gio-ponti/furniture/) mirror, a grid-like 1970s French candelabra and a moellon-stone console, found on 1stDibs, form a layered tableau against the plaster wall and antiqued-marble floor; and an undulating[Edition Modern](https://www.1stdibs.com/creators/edition-modern/furniture/) sconce enlivens a nearby corner.


Between the travertine, the glass and the contemporary surprises, the foyer simultaneously introduces and sums up the aesthetics of the house as a whole.

For a top-to-bottom overhaul of a[19th-century townhouse in New York’s Greenwich Village](https://www.1stdibs.com/introspective-magazine/rees-roberts/) , interior designer[Lucien Rees Roberts](https://www.1stdibs.com/design-firms/rees-roberts-and-partners/) and his husband, architect[Steven Harris](https://www.1stdibs.com/introspective-magazine/steven-harris-and-lucien-rees-roberts/) , took cues from the clients’ love for the culinary arts.


The wife, an accomplished home cook, wanted multiple places to eat throughout the house — spots that would work as well for a casual weeknight meal as for a large dinner party.


One such nook is inside the kitchen itself: a custom table surrounded by reissued[Jean Prouvé](https://www.1stdibs.com/creators/jean-prouve/furniture/) chairs and topped by a snake-like glass candelabra by[Dorothy Thorpe](https://www.1stdibs.com/creators/dorothy-thorpe/furniture/) as a centerpiece.


A deep-seated sofa, a 1950s Italian lounge chair and a[Carole Gratale](https://www.1stdibs.com/creators/carole-gratale//) cocktail table converge on an[Oushak](https://www.1stdibs.com/furniture/rugs-carpets/style/oushak/) rug to create a comfortable seating area nearby.


“Not everything has to be important,” Rees Roberts says of furnishing a room. “More-neutral pieces need to be intermingled with those that are more figurative and sculptural.”

Perched on an incline above Wyoming’s Snake River Valley, with the Grand Tetons as a backdrop, this[innovative compound](https://www.1stdibs.com/project/mountain-house-wy/238052/) rewrites the rules of mountain living. Los Angeles design studio[Hammer and Spear](https://www.1stdibs.com/design-firms/hammer-and-spear/) teamed up with[Andersson/Wise](https://www.1stdibs.com/introspective-magazine/andersson-wise-architects/) and OSM Construction to devise a cluster of eco-conscious buildings and outdoor rooms that hug the terrain to establish epic views.


The dining room, furnished with a custom table designed by Hammer and Spear and handcrafted by[Petersen Antiques](https://www.1stdibs.com/dealers/petersen-antiques/?search=petersen%20antiques) , opens directly onto the hillside.[Afra & Tobia Scarpa](https://www.1stdibs.com/creators/afra-and-tobia-scarpa/?search=afra%20%26%20tobia%20scarpa) Monk chairs in black leather provide a graphic contrast to the rammed-earth walls and adobe floors with exposed hay fibers. An[antique rug](https://www.1stdibs.com/furniture/rugs-carpets/) softens the mood.


“Our vision was to create a space where the architecture and furnishings felt in quiet conversation with the land, never shouting over it,” explains Scott Jarrell, who[runs Hammer and Spear](https://www.1stdibs.com/introspective-magazine/hammer-and-spear/) with his wife, Kristan Cunningham,. “The view is cinematic, and we knew we had to meet that drama with restraint.”


Providing a touch of industrial ruggedness, a custom chandelier, made in collaboration with Randy Zieber and[Jason Koharik](https://www.1stdibs.com/buy/jason-koharik/) , features raw-crystal lighting elements hanging from blackened-brass chains, while the doors operate via a crank and leather-belt pulley system by Turner Exhibits.


Outside,[Carlos Motta](https://www.1stdibs.com/creators/carlos-motta/?search=carlos%20motta) ’s Asturias rocking armchairs and Bari Ziperstein’s custom ceramic-and-wood tables huddle around the[firepit](https://www.1stdibs.com/buy/fire-pits/) . “More than anything, we wanted this space to feel like a shelter that breathes with its environment,” Jarrell says. “A place to gather, to pause, to watch the light move across the mountains. A place with soul.”

Devin Hines and his[Hines Collective](https://www.1stdibs.com/design-firms/devin-hines/) team fashioned a[duplex penthouse](https://www.1stdibs.com/project/tribeca-penthouse-new-york-ny/296332/) in Manhattan’s Tribeca neighborhood into a stylish aerie. “The moment you enter this home you’re immersed in the impressive scale of the space,” he says. “You’re floating nearly 80 stories in the clouds, and we wanted you to really experience the elevated sense of serenity and openness showcased by the view.”


The living room has a custom two-sided sofa upholstered in[Kravet](https://www.1stdibs.com/dealers/kravet-inc/shop/) fabric, accompanied by an[Ayala Serfaty](https://www.1stdibs.com/creators/ayala-serfaty/) pouf from[Maison Gerard](https://www.1stdibs.com/dealers/maison-gerard/) . Near the windows,[André Sornay](https://www.1stdibs.com/creators/andre-sornay/) armchairs flank a shagreen[4-in-1 Saturn game table](https://www.1stdibs.com/furniture/tables/game-tables/shagreen-game-table-bronze-patina-brass-accents-ry-augousti/id-f_25126382/) by[R&Y Augousti](https://www.1stdibs.com/creators/r-and-y-augousti/furniture/) ; all were sourced through 1stDibs.


The[Contour coffee table](https://www.1stdibs.com/furniture/tables/tables/contour-coffee-table-demuro-das/id-f_34617482/) by[DeMuro Das](https://www.1stdibs.com/creators/demuro-das/furniture/) provides additional surface area, while a 21-foot silk rug by[Doris Leslie Blau](https://www.1stdibs.com/dealers/doris-leslie-blau/shop/) covers the floor. A gas-burning fireplace, whose chimney is clad in wave-like marble slabs extending 12 feet to the ceiling, anchors the composition with geological weight.


“The intention was for the interior to feel like a natural and cohesive extension of the outdoor loggia,” Hines says. “Even rooms without direct outdoor access offer uninterrupted views, making the entire home feel in step with the sky.”

“What better way to represent work-life balance than a home office situated right next to a bar leading out to the pool area?” asks Kara Smith, head of[KES Studio](https://www.1stdibs.com/design-firms/smith-firestone-associates/) .


In a calming contemporary[home for a family in Beverly Hills](https://www.1stdibs.com/project/beverly-hills-residence-beverly-hills-ca/306862/) , she created a study that doubles as an entertaining space, layered with subdued natural hues and textured finishes.


“The design of the home is California organic modern, which uses a neutral color palette with accents of taupe, cognac and blush in select areas and lots of beautiful plaster to provide warmth and richness,” Smith explains.


The bar/office embodies a high-style work-(and-play-)from-home ethos, furnished with whimsical[counter stools](https://www.1stdibs.com/buy/bar-stool/) , Table Art glassware plus a refurbished desk and chairs.


Smith used titanium travertine for the bar along with white-oak flooring. A[Tera lamp by Ceramicah](https://www.1stdibs.com/search/furniture/lighting/?oq=tera%20lamp%20by%20ceramicah&q=tera%20lamp%20by%20ceramicah&st=classified) provides ambient lighting, while a mirrored wall behind the bar reflects the pool area beyond.


“The indoor-outdoor flow was very important to the client,” Smith says. “We created this particular space to accommodate her love of entertaining as well as her professional commitments.”

Overlooking the Pacific Ocean in Southern California, a[sprawling Andalusian-inspired house](https://www.1stdibs.com/project/crystal-cove-ca/116272/) with interiors by[Ohara Davies-Gaetano](https://www.1stdibs.com/design-firms/odg-interiors/) strikes a lyrical balance between comfort and drama.


“You feel as though you’re suspended above the sea,” Davies-Gaetano says. “Despite that floating sensation, the space remains grounded — anchored by its sculptural presence.”


An[18th-century fireplace](https://www.1stdibs.com/furniture/building-garden/fireplaces-mantels/period/18th-century/) anchors this sitting area in the primary bedroom, while a plush[daybed](https://www.1stdibs.com/furniture/seating/day-beds/) near the wide-open portal, framed by aqua-colored[Kravet](https://www.1stdibs.com/dealers/kravet-inc/) drapes, offers the ideal spot from which to gaze out at the endless horizon.


On the other side of the divan are a[Lilian August](https://www.1stdibs.com/creators/lillian-august/) cocktail table, a[Niermann Weeks](https://www.1stdibs.com/creators/niermann-weeks/furniture/) swivel chair and a spindly[Oly](https://www.1stdibs.com/creators/oly-studio/) side table, all underneath a swooping Canopy Design chandelier. Repurposed antique doors, with their original patina intact, lead to the adjacent bath.


The hushed color scheme inside allows the Pacific blues and sunset hues to be the focus. “The result is a rhythm of living where nature and architecture are in quiet dialogue,” the designer says, “and the distinction between interior and exterior softly fades.”

In a[contemporary Austin house](https://www.1stdibs.com/project/zilker-mid-century-modern-austin-tx/393242/) bursting with maximalist pattern and color, designer[Fern Santini](https://www.1stdibs.com/design-firms/abode-fern-santini-design/) took a more restrained approach with the primary bedroom — a restorative retreat just steps from the pool and garden.


“The design direction from the client was that she wanted to feel like this was her sanctuary,” Santini says. “Less pattern, a bit more calm than the rest of the house. We discussed what ‘dreamy’ looks and feels like.”


[Porter Teleo](https://www.1stdibs.com/creators/porter-teleo/furniture/) ’s hand-painted wallpaper of biomorphic shapes in gentle hues artistically envelops the entire interior. Mounted on top of this are vintage charcoal nudes alongside an oil painting by the homeowner’s mother, personal touches that give the space warmth and intimacy.


A white shearling rug adds softness underfoot, while a club chair swathed in a fawn-like[Schumacher](https://www.1stdibs.com/dealers/schumacher/) gros point introduces a playful cuddliness. Custom wood nightstands and svelte brass lamps keep the palette refined, saving the pop of color for what Santini calls “the icing on the cake” — a bolster upholstered in[Hermès](https://www.1stdibs.com/creators/hermes/furniture/) scarves.


“The view outside is magical and really adds to the sense of tranquility,” Santini says. In a house with plenty of statement moments, this room stands out by scaling back. It’s still personal, still layered, but built for an exhale.

In an old[stone house in New Canaan](https://www.1stdibs.com/project/new-canaan-stone-house-new-canaan-ct/381822/) , Connecticut,[Studio McGrath](https://www.1stdibs.com/design-firms/mcgrath-ii/) designed a stylish living room that channels both tradition and ease. “We leaned into the home’s historic architectural details, and layered seating arrangements that work for everything from formal entertaining to quiet evenings by the fire,” says Lauren McGrath, who runs the firm with her mother, Suzanne.


The designers’ furniture choices traverse periods and styles. “We sourced some incredible antiques for this space,” McGrath says, “including the rare bronze mid-century coffee table and a pair of Scandinavian armchairs. Muted prints were incorporated to add dimension.”


[Lee Jofa](https://www.1stdibs.com/creators/lee-jofa/) Garden Roses fabric adorns the sofa by the fireplace, introducing botanical motifs that jibe with the leafy property, while a casual Susan Deliss stripe covers the slipper chairs. Soft silvery grays lead the gaze outside, through a pair of French doors. “It’s about bridging the past and the present,” McGrath says, “indoors and out, without missing a step.”

For a fashion-industry couple juggling three residences across two continents, Robert G. Stephan, principal of[Munich-based](https://www.1stdibs.com/introspective-magazine/robert-stephan/) firm[THE ROPE](https://www.1stdibs.com/design-firms/stephan-interiors/) , transformed a Bavarian villa in the city’s suburbs into an[expression of international modernism](https://www.1stdibs.com/project/bungalow-munich-de/262912/) .


Rather than defaulting to Alpine motifs or Teutonic restraint, Stephan orchestrated a cross-cultural dialogue that speaks to his clients’ nomadic lifestyle and cosmopolitan tastes.

For Stephan, this eclectic assemblage embodies a design philosophy that values provenance and craftsmanship. “It’s always worth having a closer look at the heritage and the context of design objects,” he says. “You can feel the beauty of timeless design.”

Atop a[townhouse](https://www.1stdibs.com/project/brooklyn-townhouse-brooklyn-ny/204172/) in Brooklyn’s Cobble Hill,[Steven Harris Architects](https://www.1stdibs.com/introspective-magazine/steven-harris-and-lucien-rees-roberts/) and[Rees Roberts + Partners](https://www.1stdibs.com/design-firms/rees-roberts-and-partners/) conjured a[pavilion](https://www.1stdibs.com/1stdibs-50/2022/design-firm/steven-harris-architects-rees-roberts-partners/) that defies urban expectations. RRP principal Lucien Rees Roberts crafted a space that prioritizes natural serenity over spectacle while honoring both modernist principles and classical proportions.


“It is a very spare, minimal interior, incorporating traditional elements,” he says, “but more of an[Aalto](https://www.1stdibs.com/creators/alvar-aalto/furniture/) take on traditional.”


A woolly white[Clam chair](https://www.1stdibs.com/buy/clam-chair-arnold-madsen/) by[Arnold Madsen](https://www.1stdibs.com/creators/arnold-madsen/furniture/) and low tripod stools by[Charlotte Perriand](https://www.1stdibs.com/creators/charlotte-perriand/furniture/) provide seating while visually tempering the pavilion’s linear design with organic roundness.


Perriand’s influence goes beyond individual pieces, informing the space’s functional beauty, including expansive windows that frame views of the neighborhood’s verdant streetscapes.


The terrace extends the interior volumes, blurring distinctions between sheltered and open-air living. For Rees Roberts, the project exemplifies his firm’s commitment to environmental sensitivity and human comfort.


“The presence of light and views is always a significant focus of ours,” he says. “Light should make you look good and feel good.”

In the sunroom of a[Hamptons home](https://www.1stdibs.com/project/hamptons-ii-long-island-ny/1703/) decorated by[Alexandra Loew](https://www.1stdibs.com/design-firms/alexandra-loew-inc/) , custom toile upholstery on the bergère and dining chairs pay tribute to the client’s Dutch and Belgian heritage.
