“[Buccellati](https://www.1stdibs.com/creators/buccellati/jewelry/) ” rolls off the tongue with the same lyrical richness as the names of Italy’s most delectable desserts: zabaglione, sfogliatella and amaretti. The tonal similarity is fitting. Not only does “Buccellati” denotes a festive Sicilian cookie as well[one of Italy’s great jewelry houses](https://www.1stdibs.com/blogs/the-study/buccellati-jewelry/) , but the latter has become synonymous with a sumptuousness that is instantly recognizable and unapologetically indulgent.


In the world of fine jewelry, the Milanese *maison* is associated with an aesthetic of extraordinary intricacy, transforming precious metal into objects of enduring beauty. Its signature hand engraving and lacelike openwork celebrate virtuosic craftsmanship, resulting in richly textured pieces that play with brightness and shadow, depth and dimension.


The[bracelet](https://www.1stdibs.com/jewelry/bracelets/bangles/vintage-mario-buccellati-18k-gold-filigree-wide-wrap-bracelet/id-j_29922852/) features intricately engraved filigree patterns.


[This striking cuff bracelet](https://www.1stdibs.com/jewelry/bracelets/bangles/vintage-mario-buccellati-18k-gold-filigree-wide-wrap-bracelet/id-j_29922852/) — dating from the late 1940s to the early 1950s and signed by the house’s founder and master goldsmith,[Mario Buccellati](https://www.1stdibs.com/creators/mario-buccellati/jewelry/) — perfectly encapsulates that aesthetic. Combining engraved filigree patterns with a distinctly Bauhaus silhouette, it is bold, sculptural and irresistibly glamorous.


Hamid Sadr, CEO of[Dover Jewelry](https://www.1stdibs.com/dealers/dover-jewelry/) , which is offering the jewel on 1stDibs, has an enviable collection of Buccellati creations from the mid-century period, considered among the most innovative in the house’s history.


The inside of the bracelet is signed “M. Buccellati.”


“Over the past two or three years, I have sold many incredible pieces back to the house of Buccellati for its growing archive, now enriched with statement pieces such as this,” he says. “The cuff is very different because of the fine filigree pattern. Many people know Buccellati for the *rigato* engraving \[in which multiple thin parallel lines give the gold a textile-like texture\], but this is an earlier, more traditional technique. The pattern is so precise, so beautiful. Each dot is individually engraved in the most delicate way to create a kind of softness.”


On the wrist, the bracelet’s articulated batons create an ever-changing play of light, revealing unexpected shimmering tones of gold, from radiant yellow to rich, warm amber. At once delicately feminine and daringly angular, it strikes a balance between quiet restraint and metallic drama.


The[bracelet](https://www.1stdibs.com/jewelry/bracelets/bangles/vintage-mario-buccellati-18k-gold-filigree-wide-wrap-bracelet/id-j_29922852/) ‘s clasp isn’t hidden, but instead it is a feature in its own right.


“What I love most about the bracelet is its clasp,” says Sadr. “It is very unusual in that it isn’t an insert or what is known as an invisible clasp for a seamless silhouette. It is a strong, standout feature in its own right and gives the piece an elegant, noble form. It is very different from some of the more iconic lacelike or woven-gold pieces, but its beauty reveals it to be unmistakably Buccellati on the wrist.”
