# How to choose a background check provider for hiring at scale

5


## Get ahead: Mitigate risk with built-in compliance tools


Complying with background screening laws and regulations is becoming more complex by the day. In addition to the Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA) and Equal Employment Opportunity Commission (EEOC) guidelines, employers must contend with 37 state and more than 190 local Ban the Box laws. There are also an increasing number of[Clean Slate](https://checkr.com/blog/what-do-automated-clean-slate-laws-mean-for-your-business) laws, salary history laws, constantly evolving[drug screening laws](https://checkr.com/resources/articles/drug-testing-laws) , and changing local regulations. A single violation of the FCRA could cost an employer up to $1,000 in statutory damages or even more in punitive damages.


## Complex web of laws and screening regulations makes compliance challenging


FCRA

FCRA is a federal law governing how companies order and consider information contained in a background check. It covers all 50 states, plus Washington DC and Puerto Rico.


47 states plus Washington DC have enacted laws that make medicinal and/or recreational marijuana use legal for adults under at least some circumstances.


37 states and 190 cities/counties have enacted Ban the Box laws that vary greatly based on scope, nature, location and and jurisdiction, but generally restrict some employers from asking about a candidate’s criminal history on initial job application forms.


19 states and 19 cities/counties, plus Washington DC and Puerto Rico, have enacted salary history laws that vary based on location and jurisdiction, but generally prohibit employers from asking for previous salary information.


10 states and 3 cities, plus Washington DC and Puerto Rico, have enacted laws that restrict the use of credit information in hiring decisions.


This web of laws can be further complicated when hiring at scale for locations in multiple cities or states, or when supporting a remote workforce. For employers that hire hundreds of employees per month, missteps when reviewing background check reports may exponentially increase the risk of costly litigation. Unfortunately, even large employers may struggle to allocate time or dedicated legal staff to stay abreast of constantly changing legislation across jurisdictions.


You can mitigate compliance risk by choosing a background screening provider that uses modern technology to efficiently sort and classify criminal charges from jurisdictions nationwide. Adjudication tools that allow you to set granular criteria for specific positions help ensure your team sees only relevant, reportable, and reviewable charges—even when you’re hiring at high volume.

## Must-have solutions for modern background checks


- **Compliance support for the FCRA, EEOC, Ban the Box, and local regulations** to help take the pressure off your HR team.
- **Improved efficiency and compliance support** with a platform that filters out information that is not legally reportable and dynamically generates the regional compliance forms you need.
- **A streamlined way to protect your business** if you operate in an area that has adopted Ban the Box laws that may include specific regulations about how to handle criminal records in the decision process. A built-in feature that helps you seamlessly gather candidate stories is a smart way to help ensure compliance for your company.
- **Easier adjudication** with tools that let you filter irrelevant records as part of your adjudication matrix. Minimize the need for manual review, reduce the risk of human error, and remove bias for a fair, compliant hiring process.
- **Adverse action** workflows that save time and improve compliance by generating candidate notification letters and managing mandatory waiting periods automatically.
- **Continuous monitoring** to notify you of reportable changes to your workers’ records in real-time so you can keep your company and customers safe.
- **Fair chance reporting** that combines resources, social proof, and analytics to help you understand, implement, and advance fair chance hiring practices and DEI initiatives.
