# What is the difference between an ITIN and an EIN for U.S. tax purposes?

**Key takeaways:**


1. The ITIN is a tax processing number issued by the IRS for individuals who are not eligible to obtain a Social Security Number (SSN).
2. The EIN is a unique nine-digit number assigned by the IRS to business entities operating in the U.S. for tax filing and reporting purposes.
3. While an ITIN is primarily used for tax filing purposes, an EIN is used in a variety of business scenarios, such as opening a business bank account, applying for business licenses, or setting up payroll for employees.


## Decoding ITIN and EIN: Your Guide to U.S. Tax Identification Numbers


Tax identification numbers can be tough to navigate, particularly when dealing with international contractors. In the U.S., two commonly encountered identifiers are the Individual Taxpayer Identification Number (ITIN) and the Employer Identification Number (EIN). If you're wondering what these are and how they differ, you're in the right place. Let's break down the ITIN and EIN, illuminating their purposes, uses, and key differences.


## The ITIN Explained


The Individual Taxpayer Identification Number (ITIN) is a tax processing number issued by the Internal Revenue Service (IRS). It's designed for individuals who are not eligible to obtain a Social Security Number (SSN), often because they are non-resident aliens or foreign nationals who have a U.S. tax filing requirement.


Take Maria, a graphic designer from Brazil who gets a contract job with a U.S. company. Maria isn't eligible for a SSN because she's a non-resident alien. However, she needs to file a U.S. tax return because she earns U.S.-source income. In this situation, Maria would apply for an ITIN to fulfill her tax responsibilities.


## The EIN Unraveled


On the other hand, the Employer Identification Number (EIN) is a unique nine-digit number assigned by the IRS to business entities operating in the U.S. It's used for tax filing and reporting purposes, similar to how individuals use a SSN or ITIN.


For instance, if John starts a new tech startup in the U.S., his business would need an EIN. This number would be used on all of the company's tax filings and would also be needed if John decided to hire employees.


## Distinguishing Between ITIN and EIN


The most significant difference between an ITIN and an EIN lies in who they're designed for and their intended purpose. An ITIN is for individual taxpayers who can't get a SSN, often because of their non-resident status. Meanwhile, an EIN is for business entities operating in the U.S., irrespective of the citizenship or residency status of the owner.


In terms of application, individuals apply for an ITIN using IRS Form W-7, while businesses request an EIN via IRS Form SS-4. Also, while an ITIN is primarily used for tax filing purposes, an EIN is used in a variety of business scenarios, such as opening a business bank account, applying for business licenses, or setting up payroll for employees.


## Bringing It Together


Both ITINs and EINs are crucial elements in the U.S. tax system, each serving distinct purposes. By understanding the difference between these two identifiers, you'll be better equipped to navigate the complexities of tax responsibilities for both individuals and businesses. Whether you're an international contractor like Maria or a budding entrepreneur like John, knowing your tax obligations is an essential part of your journey.
