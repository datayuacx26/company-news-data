# The Overlooked AI Disruption in Home Financing


Aug 7, 2026


The use of AI by borrowers is likely to fuel home refinancing activity, with impacts on the industry, markets and the broader economy.


## Key Takeaways


- AI adoption could boost activity in the $14 trillion U.S. residential mortgage market by an additional $2.1 trillion if mortgages rates were to rally to 5.5%.
- AI-powered financial tools could help homeowners capture savings and reduce the amount of time and effort to refinance mortgages, saving households a total of $4.3 billion in the rally.
- Increased mortgage activity could affect several sectors and markets, making AI-driven refinancing a multi-year, cross-asset investment theme.
- AI has the potential to automate manual origination processes, reduce costs and improve capacity, while making it easier for smaller firms to compete in the mortgage industry.


Refinancing a mortgage has traditionally been a time-consuming process, requiring homeowners to research lenders, compare rates, gather documentation and complete lengthy applications.


Artificial intelligence could dramatically simplify that experience—making refinancing faster, easier and more accessible for millions of borrowers. In addition to that, AI could help households find lower rates for their loans, which could lead to significant savings.


Morgan Stanley Research estimates that even a modest level of AI adoption by borrowers could generate an additional $2.1 trillion in refinancing activity within the $14 trillion U.S. residential mortgage market if the mortgage rate rallied to 5.5%.


"Greater volume in refinancing activities doesn’t just offer benefits to the companies enabling and supporting housing activity," says Jay Bacow, Co-Head of Securitized Products Research at Morgan Stanley., “We also expect increased efficiency in borrowing to have implications across fixed income and broader macroeconomic sectors in a multi-year, cross-asset theme.”


### **Making Refinancing Easier for Homeowners**


Over the past 25 years, only about 29% of U.S. homeowners have taken advantage of opportunities to prepay or refinance their mortgages, despite potential financial benefits. Many borrowers are unaware of refinancing opportunities, lack the time to evaluate their options or are discouraged by the complexity and paperwork involved.


Morgan Stanley Research estimates that AI could increase prepayment adoption from 29% annually to 60% in its base-case scenario by improving the borrower experience in several ways, including:


- Continuously monitoring mortgage rates and borrower eligibility
- Automatically preparing required documentation
- Personalizing and simplifying the application process
- Accelerating approvals and shortening closing timelines
- Identifying refinancing opportunities as soon as they become economically beneficial


"We are imagining a world where homeowners who use AI agents to manage personal finances can automatically shop lenders for the best rate and refinance when it makes economic sense," Bacow says. "We expect to see more AI disruption in the mortgage market from consumers adopting AI to manage their finances and refinance than from originators reducing closing costs and increasing efficiencies."


### **AI Could Also Reshape Mortgage Lending**


However, lenders are certainly positioned to benefit from greater AI usage as well. Today, the mortgage origination process—from receiving a loan application to the final disbursement of funds—remains highly manual and labor-intensive, with several tasks well suited to AI and automation.


Increased use of AI could reduce costs, increase operational capacity and improve efficiency across the lending process. At the same time, it may lower barriers to entry for smaller lenders and increase pricing transparency for consumers, potentially putting pressure on industry margins.


1.


Fixed Income


Mortgage-backed securities (MBS) investors are typically compensated for prepayment risk. If AI enables homeowners to refinance more efficiently, higher prepayment rates could reduce the relative attractiveness of MBS. More broadly, lower interest rates could increase demand for longer-duration fixed-income investments.


2.


Equities


Companies that enable or benefit from increased mortgage and housing activity could see stronger growth. These include credit bureaus, banks, homebuilders, home improvement retailers and mortgage servicing firms.


3.


Housing


By making mortgages easier to obtain and refinance, AI could improve access to homeownership for both existing homeowners and first-time buyers. Increased credit availability could also support higher levels of home construction and sales.


4.


Consumers


Helping borrowers identify better refinancing opportunities could increase household savings and accelerate debt reduction. In Morgan Stanley Research's base-case scenario, households would save an average of $3,583 per year, generating $4.3 billion in additional annual mortgage-related savings across 1.2 million U.S. households if primary rates fall to 5.5%. Those savings could free up income for spending in home-related categories such as furniture, pet care and housekeeping supplies, while also strengthening household balance sheets.
