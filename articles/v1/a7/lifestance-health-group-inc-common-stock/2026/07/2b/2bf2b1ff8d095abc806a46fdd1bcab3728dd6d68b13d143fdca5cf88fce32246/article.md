# Parenting a Child With ADHD: Tips and Strategies

## What Parents Can Do to Help Children With ADHD


- **Learn About ADHD:** When[parenting a child with ADHD](https://lifestance.com/blog/parenting-child-with-adhd-parenting-skills/) , learning about the condition is an important first step. Work with qualified behavioral health professionals to understand your child’s unique ADHD type and symptoms. Be selective about where that information comes from. There are plenty of myths about ADHD online, so lean on your child’s provider first and reputable sources second. For children under 6, the American Academy of Pediatrics and the[CDC recommend parent training in behavior management](https://www.cdc.gov/adhd/treatment/index.html) before medication is considered – an approach that teaches you concrete skills to support your child.
- **Establish Routines:** Structure and predictability are especially steadying for a child with ADHD. When parts of the day are predictable, your child has one less thing to hold in mind and more mental room to focus. Build simple, repeatable routines around the moments that tend to unravel, like mornings, mealtimes, homework, and bedtime. If overhauling the whole day feels overwhelming, start small. A short morning walk or a book at bedtime can anchor the day, and you can add more predictable rituals as they become habits. For a step-by-step approach, see this guide on[building routines for a child with ADHD](https://lifestance.com/blog/how-to-implement-routines-for-your-child-with-adhd/) .


- **Prioritize Sleep and Activity:** When you design those routines, protect two things: sleep and movement.


Regular exercise gives your child a healthy outlet for extra energy and may do more than that. A[2023 network meta-analysis](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpubh.2023.1133727) found that physical exercise may help improve executive function and ease core ADHD symptoms in children and adolescents. Activities with near-constant movement – soccer, swimming, basketball – are great options, while yoga and martial arts pair movement with focus and self-control. Learn more about how[exercise supports mental health](https://lifestance.com/blog/how-does-exercise-improve-mental-health/) .


Sleep matters just as much. Anyone struggles to concentrate when they are tired, and for a child with ADHD, rest is essential for attention and impulse control. A[2015 study](https://doi.org/10.1080/09297049.2015.1018153) of young children with ADHD found that greater sleep disturbance was associated with poorer attention and executive control. Sleep problems are especially common in children with ADHD, so understanding the[connection between ADHD and sleep disorders](https://lifestance.com/blog/adhd-medication-for-kids/#the-overlooked-connection-adhd-and-sleep-disorders) can guide what to try at home. An earlier, consistent bedtime and a calm wind-down routine can set your child up for a better day. If bedtime is a battle, this overview of[how sleep affects mental health](https://lifestance.com/blog/poor-sleep-hygene-mental-health/) may help.


- **Support Social and Emotional Growth:** Constant correction and redirection can wear on a child’s self-esteem, and the diagnosis itself can leave some children feeling different. That is why building confidence deserves intention. Encouragingly, the CDC reports that[parent-led behavior therapy](https://www.cdc.gov/adhd/treatment/behavior-therapy.html) can help improve a child’s behavior, self-control, and self-esteem.


You can strengthen your child’s self-esteem by:


• Setting aside one-on-one time that is just about connection


• Coaching them on the social skills that help friendships grow


• Building on[their strengths](https://lifestance.com/blog/the-superpowers-of-adhd/) instead of only managing struggles


• Noticing and praising positive actions, even small ones


Small, genuine encouragement adds up, reinforcing your child’s sense that they are capable. For more ideas, see this guide to[raising a child with healthy self-esteem](https://lifestance.com/blog/raising-a-child-with-healthy-self-esteem/) .
