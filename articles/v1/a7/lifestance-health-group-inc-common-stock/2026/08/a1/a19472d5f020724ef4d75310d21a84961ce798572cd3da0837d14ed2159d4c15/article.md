# How to Relax on Vacation When Your Trip Feels Anything But

## How to Relax on Vacation


Here are four habits that help me come home feeling rested.


1.


#### Keep your bedtime routine the same.


During a vacation, it can be tempting to throw your good habits and self-care regimen right out the window. But even if it’s late, I always strive to do every step of my bedtime routine, which gets my body ready for restorative[sleep](https://lifestance.com/services/sleep-disorder-treatment/) and keeps me grounded. That means not skipping my skincare products or forgoing the pre-bedtime lotion I use. In general, prioritizing sleep is a huge deal for me during vacation.


2.


#### Include moments in nature.


[Nature](https://lifestance.com/blog/benefits-of-nature-on-mental-health/) has this way of bringing you back to the present moment, and it’s still the case even when you’re on an activity-filled vacay. If you’re following an itinerary, schedule a hike in a nearby state park or lounge on the beach for an extra hour, just watching the waves rolling in and out. For me, my connection to nature during a beach vacation is collecting seashells, which is one of the most peaceful things I do.


3.


#### Work in quick breaks.


Oftentimes, you don’t need hours upon hours to accomplish a feeling of relaxation. “Micro-breaks” throughout a busy day can do this, too. During a vacation, this can mean sitting on an amusement park bench and watching people walk by for a few minutes or ducking into a store for some air conditioning and window shopping. If the location is particularly taxing (like Disney World, even though I love it!), I like to look at a map and come up with spots where I can tuck away and breathe a bit before rejoining the crowd.


4.


#### Practice mindfulness.


It’s always a good idea to try to live in the moment as much as you can during a vacation, especially when you’re making memories. If I’m feeling frazzled and stressed, I often use those times as opportunities to practice[mindfulness](https://lifestance.com/services/mindfulness-based-stress-reduction/) and pay more attention to the world around me: I pick out little details in the beautiful scenery, count cars, or just look up at the sky (preferably in a hammock!). And although it’s always nice to snap photos during a vacation, I try to hold back here and there so I can appreciate the moment without my phone in hand. It makes me feel a lot more laid-back—and isn’t that how we all hope to feel during a vacation?
