# Vagus Nerve Exercises That Worked for Me

## Vagus Nerve Exercises That Worked for Me


There are some low-powered ways to stimulate the vagus nerve naturally on your own at home. So as a curious researcher and experimenter, I decided to try out some of the vagus nerve exercises that seem like they have real evidence behind them:


- **Slow, deep breathing.** This is probably the most evidence-backed technique, as well as the easiest to do. A[2025 research review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12341363/) suggests that several forms of deep breathing boost vagal tone, nervous system relaxation, and emotional control while lowering levels of stress and cortisol. Based on that research, the best kind of vagus nerve breathing is deep belly breaths (also called diaphragmatic breathing) taken through the nose instead of the mouth, at a slow pace of six or fewer breaths per minute. That lined up pretty well with the breathwork practice I was already doing many evenings. So, I experimented with doing shorter, casual bouts of that style of breathing throughout the day, whenever I felt my body needed a shot of calm. It definitely did the trick.
- **Cold water on your face.** A 2022 systematic review and meta-analysis in[Psychophysiology](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/psyp.14183) suggests that immersing your body in cold water, or more conveniently just your face, activates the vagus nerve. I’m already a big fan of[cold plunging](https://lifestance.com/blog/how-cold-plunges-helped-me/) , so this one was easy for me. I’ve found that blasting cold shower water on my face, or dunking my face in ice water, is a great[somatic](https://lifestance.com/blog/somatic-exercises-at-home/) technique to interrupt an emotional stress spiral.
- **Humming.** Since the vagus nerve touches the larynx, it makes sense that vocal vibration might stimulate it. While humming has been researched less as a vagus nerve stimulator, one[2023 study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10182780/) found that humming results in lower physiological stress levels than exercise and sleep, and that routine humming may reduce the stress response and increase relaxation in the nervous system. I wasn’t very consistent with this one but I found myself humming once in a while when I remembered it and certainly noticed a mildly pleasant, relaxing feeling.


If you want a few more nervous system tools beyond these, a set of[grounding techniques](https://lifestance.com/blog/grounding-techniques-for-anxiety/) can pair nicely with vagus nerve exercises when stress hits.
