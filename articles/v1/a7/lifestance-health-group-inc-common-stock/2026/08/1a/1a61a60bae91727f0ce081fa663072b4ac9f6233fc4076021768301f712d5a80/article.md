# Lexapro for Anxiety: A Psychiatrist Explains How It Works and What to Expect

## Lexapro vs. Other Anxiety Medications


Lexapro is one of the medications most commonly chosen as a starting point for anxiety, but it is not the only first-line option, and it is not the right option for every individual. Clinical guidelines from the[American Academy of Family Physicians](https://www.aafp.org/pubs/afp/issues/2022/0800/generalized-anxiety-disorder-panic-disorder.html) identify SSRIs and serotonin-norepinephrine reuptake inhibitor (SNRIs) as first-line medications for generalized anxiety disorder; among these, escitalopram and sertraline ([Zoloft](https://lifestance.com/psychiatric-medication/zoloft/) ) are commonly preferred SSRIs, with venlafaxine and duloxetine as comparable SNRI options. Which medication is right for any individual depends on medical history, other prescriptions, prior response to treatment, side-effect sensitivities, and co-occurring conditions. This decision can only be made by a qualified provider after a thorough psychiatric evaluation.


A few practical reasons may lead psychiatrists to consider Lexapro first:


- It is less likely to interact with other medications an individual may already be taking
- People tend to experience milder withdrawal symptoms when stopping Lexapro under their doctor's guidance, compared with some other SSRIs
- It tends to cause fewer unexpected side effects than older antidepressants
- Lexapro is the most precisely targeted SSRI, which can mean fewer effects on other parts of brain function


Other anxiety medications a psychiatrist may consider include:


- **Other SSRIs:** sertraline (Zoloft), citalopram ([Celexa](https://lifestance.com/blog/celexa-for-anxiety/) ), paroxetine ([Paxil](https://lifestance.com/psychiatric-medication/paxil/) ), fluoxetine ([Prozac](https://lifestance.com/psychiatric-medication/prozac-fluoxetine/) )
- **SNRIs:**[venlafaxine](https://lifestance.com/psychiatric-medication/venlafaxine-effexor/) (Effexor XR), duloxetine ([Cymbalta](https://lifestance.com/blog/cymbalta-for-pain/) ), often used when comorbid pain is present
- **Benzodiazepines:** alprazolam ([Xanax](https://lifestance.com/blog/xanax-for-anxiety-psychiatrist-advice/) ), clonazepam (Klonopin),[lorazepam](https://lifestance.com/psychiatric-medication/lorazepam/) (Ativan), short-term and fast-acting but with risk of tolerance and dependence
- **Buspirone (BuSpar):** a serotonin-related non-SSRI anxiolytic with a moderately delayed onset of action
- **Beta-blockers:**[propranolol](https://lifestance.com/blog/propranolol-for-anxiety/) , used off-label for performance anxiety and the physical symptoms of panic
- **[Gabapentin](https://lifestance.com/blog/gabapetin-for-anxiety/) :** used off-label for anxiety, particularly when sleep is disrupted


A psychiatrist weighs the evidence base for each medication, the individual’s profile, and the treatment goal, whether the priority is rapid symptom relief, long-term remission, or a combination, when recommending where to start.
