# Coloring Apps for Mental Health: 10 Therapist-Informed Picks

## 10 Best Coloring Apps for Mental Health


There is no single best app. The right one depends on whether you want a guided, decision-free experience or a more open canvas. The picks below, listed in no particular order, span both ends. None of these apps is a medical product or a stand-in for care; think of them as tools that may support relaxation as part of a broader routine.


1.


#### Happy Color


A color-by-number app with one of the largest free libraries available, organized by theme. By matching a number to a shade, it removes the blank-page pressure entirely, which can make it a comfortable landing spot on days when decision fatigue is real.
**Where to get it:**[Happy Color](https://happycolorapp.com/)


2.


#### Pigment


Often called the closest thing to coloring on paper, Pigment offers realistic pencil, marker, and watercolor brushes with pressure sensitivity that shines on a tablet and stylus. It suits people who want to shade and blend rather than tap to fill.
**Where to get it:**[Pigment](https://pixiteapps.com/apps/pigment/)


3.


#### Colorfy


One of the originals in the category, with a deep library, offline coloring on downloaded images, and both color-by-number and freehand modes. A built-in community lets you browse and share finished pieces if that motivates you.
**Where to get it:**[Colorfy](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/colorfy-coloring-book-games/id1009442510)


4.


#### Lake


Lake leans into a calm, curated aesthetic, featuring hand-drawn collections from independent illustrators and soft, ASMR-style coloring sounds. The pared-down toolkit is designed not to overwhelm, which fits a wind-down ritual.
**Where to get it:**[Lake](https://www.lakecoloring.com/)


5.


#### Zen Color


Built explicitly around relaxation, Zen Color pairs intricate pages with ambient soundscapes and themed collections aimed at unwinding. The simpler patterns are a good match if busy designs feel like more pressure.
**Where to get it:**[Zen Color](https://zencolor.app/)


6.


#### Color Therapy


As the name suggests, this app foregrounds the wellness angle with calming background music, mandala-style pages, and a deliberately slower pace. It’s a fit for people who color specifically to settle rather than to chase the next image.
**Where to get it:**[Color Therapy](https://www.colortherapy.app/)


7.


#### Recolor


Recolor offers polished, professionally illustrated pages with strong shading and gradient tools, and fresh artwork added regularly. It tends to appeal to those who want their finished work to look refined.
**Where to get it:**[Recolor](https://www.recolor.com/)


8.


#### Colorgram


Colorgram emphasizes mindfulness and stress relief, with a clean interface, a range of tools, and optional reminders to pause and take a coloring break during the day.
**Where to get it:**[Colorgram](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/colorgram-adult-coloring-book/id1241675283)


9.


#### InColor


InColor blends coloring with light drawing tools and lets you turn your own photos, or AI text prompts, into pages, giving the practice a personal touch that can grow with your interest.
**Where to get it:**[InColor](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.inapp.incolor)


10.


#### Super Color


A lightweight, color-by-number option that runs smoothly even on older or lower-storage devices and works offline, making it handy for commutes or travel.
**Where to get it:**[Super Color](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=paint.number.pixel.art.coloring.drawing.puzzle.super.color)


Whichever you choose, the goal is the same as any[self-soothing technique](https://lifestance.com/blog/self-soothing-techniques-for-adults/) : a brief, repeatable way to downshift.
