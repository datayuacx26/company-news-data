# My Psychiatric Service Dog Changed My Life: What I Learned

## The Tasks Remi Performs for My Mental Health


In the service dog community, a “task” is a specific, trained behavior that directly mitigates a handler's disability. This is the legal and functional distinction between a service dog and any other kind of support animal. Remi's tasks were all built around the symptoms I experience from my specific diagnoses.


**Deep Pressure Therapy (DPT)**
Deep pressure therapy is one of the most recognized psychiatric service dog tasks. When my breathing and heart rate become elevated due to anxiety or C-PTSD symptoms, Remi applies his weight to my body, a technique[known](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11357748/) to regulate the nervous system and interrupt a stress response. For me, it functions as an early intervention before a full panic episode develops.


**Tactile Stimulation for Dissociation**
When I begin to dissociate, a symptom associated with C-PTSD, Remi is trained to make physical contact, typically licking my hand or face, to provide grounding sensory input. Dissociation can feel like drifting out of your own body or losing track of your surroundings, and Remi's response helps pull me back into the present moment.


**Panic Interruption**
If Remi observes the early physical signs that I am building toward a panic attack, he is trained to jump up onto my shoulders. The physical pressure and sudden sensory input interrupt the escalation and prompt me to sit down, which helps break the cycle before it becomes unmanageable.


**Medication Retrieval**
Remi is trained to respond to a specific daily alarm on my phone. When it sounds, he locates and retrieves the bag containing my medications and brings it to me. For anyone managing multiple[psychiatric medications](https://lifestance.com/psychiatric-medication/) , the consistency of this reminder can be especially valuable. Missed doses can directly affect mood stability and symptom management, and having Remi take ownership of that cue removed a point of friction that used to derail me regularly.
