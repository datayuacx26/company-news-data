# Skincare Devices on the Rise: Red Light Masks & High Frequency Wands

Once reserved for dermatology offices, skincare devices are now trending as at-home essentials. According to Spate’s Popularity Index, consumer interest is accelerating, with red light face masks and high frequency facial wands leading the charge. Both categories promise professional-grade results without the in-office price tag — and consumers are clearly ready to invest.


With Google and TikTok data showing double- and triple-digit growth,[skincare](https://www.spate.nyc/reports/body-care-trends-2024) devices are no longer niche gadgets. They’re shaping a new frontier in how consumers approach beauty: tech-powered, performance-driven, and highly shoppable.


## **Red Light Face Masks: A Growing Skincare Essential**


Red light face masks leverage red wavelength light to target aging and inflammation, stimulating collagen, boosting circulation, and energizing cells.


- Trend Growth: +88.6% YoY
- Google Search Lift: +90.1% YoY
- TikTok Lift: +24.4% YoY
- Popularity Share: 98.4% owned by Google


From luxury brands promising clinical-level results to budget-friendly Amazon alternatives, consumers are hungry for at-home treatments that save both time and money. Search insights reveal demand for comparisons and reviews of brands like Qure, HigherDose, and Omnilux — and a strong focus on discounts and retailer availability signals clear purchase intent.


## **High Frequency Facial Wands: Skincare Meets Technology**


High frequency facial wands use electrical currents to stimulate collagen, fight acne-causing bacteria, and calm inflammation.


- Trend Growth: +17.7% YoY
- Google Search Lift: +22.2% YoY
- TikTok Lift: +11.7% YoY
- Popularity Share: 59.1% on Google (11.2K monthly searches); TikTok dominates with 1.0M weekly views


Despite growing buzz, many consumers remain uncertain about functionality and safety. That hesitation creates an opening for brands to educate — clarifying proper usage, dispelling myths, and offering proof of results. On[TikTok](https://www.spate.nyc/reports/spate-x-niq-cracking-tiktok-shop) , hashtags like[#postpartumhairloss](https://www.spate.nyc/reports/2025-hair-report-care-and-styling-products) (693.6K weekly views), #collagenproduction (681.0K), and #finelines (677.4K) connect devices to broader beauty concerns, while 77.3% of TikTok views tie directly to TikTok Shop content. Leading the way: NuDerma, whose hashtag alone drives 292.4K weekly views.


## **Why Now?**


Devices represent more than just skincare upgrades. They tap into cultural currents of accessibility, convenience, and empowerment. Consumers want professional-quality treatments at home — saving both time and money while embracing rituals that feel futuristic.


As beauty embraces “skinimalism” and multitasking solutions, skincare devices fit neatly into a broader shift: technology-powered self-care that simplifies routines while amplifying results.


## **The Bigger Picture: A Signal for the Future of Beauty**


Skincare devices highlight several deeper shifts in consumer behavior:


- Education unlocks adoption: Clarity around usage and safety will accelerate mainstream trust.
- Shoppability drives conversion: TikTok continues to blend discovery and commerce seamlessly.
- Adjacent needs expand reach: Postpartum recovery, anti-aging, and skin health conversations create natural entry points.


## **The Opportunity for Brands and Retailers**


This isn’t just a passing fad. Skincare devices are carving out lasting relevance, and the[brands that win will](https://www.spate.nyc/reports/whitespace-opportunities-in-skincare-2022) :


- Educate to Differentiate: Build authority by answering the safety and efficacy questions consumers are already asking.
- Lean Into Shoppable Content: Short-form, engaging, shoppable videos will capture high-intent buyers.
- Target Adjacent Needs: Position devices within broader conversations like postpartum recovery, fine lines, and collagen health.


## **Looking Ahead**


With red light masks and high frequency wands surging, the next wave of skincare devices is already on the horizon. The question isn’t whether consumers will adopt — they already have. The question is: which brands will lead the charge in shaping the future of at-home beauty tech?
