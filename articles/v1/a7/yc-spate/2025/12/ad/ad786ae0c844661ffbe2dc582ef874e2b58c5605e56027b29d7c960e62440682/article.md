# How to Spot Whitespace Opportunities in a Crowded Market

## **Find Untapped Market Opportunities with Whitespace Analysis**


Today's consumer packaged goods (CPG) market is becoming increasingly saturated, leading to fierce competition among brands vying for the same customers. Roughly[30,000 products](https://professionalprograms.mit.edu/blog/design/why-95-of-new-products-miss-the-mark-and-how-yours-can-avoid-the-same-fate/) hit the market each year, but an estimated[85 percent fail](https://www.forbes.com/sites/steveking/2024/01/25/cpg-product-innovation-should-be-a-science-not-an-art/) within just two years. To achieve success, brands must do more than simply innovate. They must also be strategic, identifying where rivals are falling short and what types of products consumers can't currently find.


Whitespace opportunities represent unmet consumer needs and underserved segments that the competition is not yet capitalizing on. These opportunities aren't guesses or hunches. They're data-driven discoveries about what shoppers truly want and need.


Beauty, wellness, and[food](https://www.spate.nyc/reports/food-foresight-2024) brands that are standing out are leveraging trend data and search signals to unveil powerful insights, spotting gaps in the market that need filling. They're using data to uncover opportunities that are hiding in plain sight, all before they become obvious to the market at large.


## **What Are Whitespace Opportunities in Market Research?**


In market research, whitespace opportunities are gaps between what consumers want and what's currently available. Market whitespaces exist even in the most crowded markets. Consumer preferences are constantly shifting and evolving. New routines, new values, and new curiosities drive fresh demand every day, and those untapped market opportunities have great potential for brands.


Whitespace opportunities can take many forms.


• **Unmet Consumer Needs:** These market gaps exist when shoppers are searching for solutions that don't exist. Take, for example, the interest in products to repair and protect the skin barrier. Interest surged on social media platforms like[TikTok](https://www.glossy.co/beauty/tiktok-gen-z-skin-barrier-went-mainstream/) , leading to a rise in gentler, microbiome-safe products.


• **Emerging Cultural or Lifestyle Shifts:** Changing lifestyle habits can pave the way to great innovation. For instance, as the world began to recognize the importance of gut health, new[prebiotic drinks](https://www.fedupfoods.com/insights/the-rise-of-prebiotic-sodas) and[supplements](https://www.spate.nyc/reports/2025-supplement-trends-report) became big business.


• **New Ingredients and Product Categories:** Whitespace opportunities can also come in the form of new ingredients. One example is the widespread adoption of retinols in beauty and skincare. While they've been available for decades, the consumer trend analysis unveiled massive interest in over-the-counter retinol products in the early 2010s. Thus, a billion-dollar market was born.


## **Using Consumer Search Data to Identify Market Gaps**


To identify whitespace opportunities, brands must move past gut feelings and basic assumptions. For many decades, companies in the beauty and wellness space have set trends. However, thanks to the Internet, consumers now have all the power. With a world of information at their fingertips, the vast majority of shoppers[research products and brands](https://capitaloneshopping.com/research/consumer-product-research-statistics) before making a purchase. Brands can utilize those search signals to discover those elusive, untapped market opportunities.


Analyzing billions of search signals helps brands understand what consumers are actively looking for but can't find. Search behaviors can highlight unmet consumer needs. For instance, search behavior may indicate that people are repeatedly looking for something that only a few brands, if any, address. That's a whitespace opportunity.


It goes even deeper than that. Because fads come and go quickly, you can rely on search signals to differentiate those fast-fading trends from shifts with true staying power. Growth in search volume over time indicates that a trend is gaining momentum. Meanwhile, a sudden spike in interest may only reflect short-term interests.


This data-driven approach eliminates the guesswork from product development, enabling brands to base major decisions on the direction the market is truly heading. Rather than making those decisions on a hunch, brands can follow the market and identify whitespace opportunities worth capitalizing on.


### **Tracking Search Volume Trends for Emerging Opportunities**


In a saturated and competitive market, trend forecasting to capitalize on those whitespace opportunities before other brands do can mean the difference between standout success and falling by the wayside. Monitoring search volume changes can help you spot potentially game-changing opportunities early on.


For example, consider a sudden surge in a particular ingredient, concern or product type. That signals rising consumer interest and is something worth keeping an eye on.


As mentioned earlier, not all spikes in search volume reflect a genuine whitespace opportunity. Some are simple fads that can fade quickly. To differentiate those fads from real trends, year-over-year growth rates can be a helpful signal. You want slow, steady growth rather than a massive spike and sudden fade. A trend can show strong YoY growth due to a single spike and still fade quickly if interest is not sustained beyond that period. Examine historical search volume data alongside signals like sustained growth, awareness level, and predicted future performance to see how interest has changed over time.


A steady increase in search volume, paired with medium to very high awareness and positive predicted YoY growth, points to long-term consumer shifts rather than fleeting curiosity. Knowing how to separate the two is crucial. This type of early signal analysis allows brands to strike while the iron is still heating up. Brands can get ahead of the competition by introducing products as the trend is developing, rather than waiting until the market is already saturated.


### **Analyzing TikTok and Social Signals for Whitespace**


Search data can unveil what people are curious about. However, social media can reveal what people are actively discussing. Social media content, especially on platforms like TikTok, is a valuable resource for consumer trend analysis, helping brands discover what's capturing attention and building momentum.


Social media plays a major role in how beauty and wellness trends take shape, with the largest platforms often acting as early indicators. They can also accelerate trends and reveal whitespace opportunities in specific product categories or demographics. For example, the "glass skin" trend, limited initially to[Korean](https://www.spate.nyc/reports/new-wave-of-kbeauty) beauty markets, spread rapidly through social media.


TikTok alone reportedly drove nearly[$32 billion in beauty sales](https://www.cosmeticsdesign.com/Article/2024/08/20/tiktok-drives-31.7b-in-beauty-sales-how-viral-trends-are-shaping-the-future-of-cosmetics/) in 2023, making it a crucial resource for identifying trends. By combining social signals with search data, your brand can get a complete picture of emerging untapped market opportunities.


## **Conducting Competitive Analysis to Uncover Untapped Areas**


Successfully identifying market gaps to capitalize on requires a comprehensive understanding of not only consumer interests and trends but also your competition. Some whitespace opportunities exist because competitors overlook specific needs or consumer segments. Finding out what the competition isn't doing or where they're weak gives brands the opportunity to establish themselves as leaders or innovators before the crowd arrives.


When developing a crowded market strategy, you need to identify the spaces where your brand can thrive. Competitive analysis helps you do just that by mapping out what other brands are doing and highlighting what they're not. Successful competitive analysis involves reviewing product lines, messaging and positioning to identify untapped potential that is often hiding in plain sight.


For example, you might see skincare brands clustering around the same product types or claims while failing to address another area that is showing clear demand. That's a whitespace opportunity. Likewise, you might analyze product launches and claims across a category to highlight where innovation has slowed or where messaging fails to resonate. Those gaps often represent spaces where demand exists but no other rival is effectively meeting.


Know where the competition is weak. When you understand how other brands are navigating the market, you can strategically shape the narrative and position yours as a leader.


### **Finding Gaps Where Competitors Aren't Addressing Consumer Needs**


Even in highly competitive markets, mismatches often exist between what competitors offer and what consumers truly want. For example, brands may focus on one aspect of a category while completely ignoring other consumer priorities. Studying those disconnects is one of the most effective ways to identify whitespace opportunities.


Begin by analyzing consumer interest data, including trending product attributes and popular search queries. Then, use competitive analysis to see if other brands are offering products that align with those interests.


Once you start identifying gaps worth exploring, you can validate them by examining which consumer questions are currently unanswered. When thousands of people ask the same question, but no brand provides a clear answer or solution, you have a real opportunity to make an impact in the market.

## **Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)**


### **How do you identify whitespace opportunities in a saturated market?**


You can identify whitespace opportunities by analyzing what consumers want but can't currently find. Combining search volume data with social signals can help highlight emerging trends before they reach the mainstream. Meanwhile, competitive analysis can reveal untapped demands and areas where your rivals fall short, presenting golden opportunities to enter a competitive space while still making a significant impact.


### **What data sources help spot untapped market opportunities?**


Search data and social signals are the most valuable resources for consumer trend analysis. Analyzing search volume can reveal which trends have momentum and which are merely temporary fads. Meanwhile, social signals can reveal what's capturing consumers' attention.


### **How can brands tell the difference between a trend and a fad?**


The best way to differentiate short-lived fads from true consumer shifts is to examine year-over-year growth. Interest should rise steadily over time, rather than peak and then fade. Those long-lasting interests have staying power, indicating trends worth capitalizing on.


### **Why is competitive analysis important for finding market gaps?**


Even in saturated markets, there are still unmet consumer needs. Competitive analysis helps you understand what other brands are doing and what they are not doing. It's an opportunity to see where their products and strategies fall short, highlighting whitespaces ready to take advantage of.


### **How do consumer search patterns reveal unmet needs?**


Consumer search patterns can indicate that there are still unanswered questions and unmet needs, and the market is failing to provide solutions for them. Surges in questions about specific products, ingredients or attributes signal needs that no brand is currently meeting.
