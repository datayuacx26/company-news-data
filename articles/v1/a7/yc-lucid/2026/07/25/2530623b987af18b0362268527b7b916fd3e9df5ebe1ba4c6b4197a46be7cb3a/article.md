# What is enterprise agility? A guide to enhancing adaptability for your organization

## Key takeaways


- Enterprise agility is the ability to adapt at scale as organizations respond to market shifts with speed and intention while maintaining coordination.
- Achieving org-wide agility enables shorter feedback loops, faster time-to-market, enhanced cross-functional coordination, and improved ability to pivot.
- Successful agility transformation requires leadership to set a clear vision, practice flexible change management, increase visibility, and manage large-scale coordination.
- Using a visual collaboration solution provides a central place for large enterprises to track progress and enhance alignment while teams work together to increase responsiveness.


For large organizations that have been traditionally siloed, responding to change was like trying to turn a cargo ship: slow, painful, and filled with resistance. Recently, however, enterprise-level adaptability has become necessary for survival. Aligned responsiveness is more critical than ever as organizations face unprecedented pressure, tech disruption, and shifting customer expectations.


Enterprise agility transforms your organization from a slow-moving cargo ship into a fleet of speedboats that move in coordination. When you scale adaptability, your business meets customers’ needs sooner while your cross-functional teams are aligned and ready for anything.


Here’s what you need to know about enterprise agility, as well as a breakdown of the strategies you can take to implement a culture of adaptability and preparedness.


## What is enterprise agility?


Enterprise agility is the ability to adapt at scale, responding to change at the organizational level with speed and intention while maintaining coordination and momentum.


With enterprise agility, organizations can successfully pivot in response to market shifts thanks to an established culture of responsiveness, preparedness, and flexibility. Multiple factors can trigger the need to pivot, such as a poor product-market fit, diminishing returns from a product direction, or shifts in technology.


> “Enterprise agility is an approach to work that allows for quick, frequent adaptations in a business. It provides a safe place for creativity and risks to live, fail, and rise beyond what was previously thought possible. Enterprise agility keeps businesses from going down the wrong path for too long and ensures that their strategy is always reflecting the signals that may be coming to them either internally or externally.”
>
>
> —Alyssa Craig, Senior Manager of Program Management, Lucid


Unlike traditional[Agile](https://lucid.co/blog/agile-101-agile-fundamentals-for-beginners) approaches that improve adaptability within individual teams, enterprise agility aligns leadership and strategy to achieve organization-wide goals. To guide organizations on scaling resilience and adaptability, the PMI Agile Alliance created a[Manifesto for Enterprise Agility](https://lucid.co/blog/manifesto-for-enterprise-agility) . In the Manifesto, leaders can find the values and principles that help them create conditions for agility and better prepare their organization for change.


### Benefits of enterprise agility


Becoming a more agile enterprise offers a wide range of benefits for businesses, including:


- **Faster time to market** due to enhanced responsiveness and quicker time to completion
- **Enhanced cross-functional coordination** with better connection from strategy to execution across teams
- **Increased customer satisfaction** since teams are more in tune with customer needs and work with shorter feedback loops
- **Continuous improvement** , as enterprise agility uncovers regular opportunities to proactively identify and solve process inefficiencies
- **Greater ability to pivot** to market shifts or signals due to a[change-ready culture](https://lucid.co/blog/responding-to-change-with-flexible-planning)


## 7 strategies for enterprise agility transformation


Remember, enterprise agility is about strategic responsiveness. If leadership can’t pivot its strategy quickly in response to incoming data, team-level agility has little impact.


The following strategies can help you implement enterprise agility and ensure your strategy is effective at the organizational level.


### Strategy #1: Set the vision and objectives


Before leaders ask people to change how they work, they should clarify why the change is necessary. Since trying to implement agility for the simple sake of agility often falls apart, success should be tied directly to strategic business outcomes. Leaders should identify what they’re trying to improve and focus on being built for change while connecting enterprise agility transformation to the company's future.


Objectives should be made absolutely clear and available to everyone, so employees can establish a clear[roadmap](https://airfocus.com/product/roadmaps) that connects their roles to the overall business strategy. An effective roadmap outlines goals, deadlines, and other expectations for each team, helping people align their day-to-day actions with the broader vision. Ensure the roadmap is easily accessible to both employees and stakeholders.


Strong leader sponsorship upfront is necessary for scaling agility across your organization. Leaders need to let go of control and adopt a more sense-and-respond approach, crafting a North Star that aligns the organization while leaving room for teams to exercise some decision-making power. Select a few core strategic objectives, and work with teams on how you’ll accomplish them.


### Strategy #2: Practice effective change management


Successful change management during enterprise agility transformation is a unique challenge. You aren’t just changing a system or a process; you’re changing your organization’s culture and mindset, as well as the tools you're used to using to manage the organization or set direction. Traditional[change management frameworks](https://lucid.co/blog/fundamental-change-management-models) often fail to support enterprise agility because they view change as linear and fixed in duration, when enterprise agility is an ever-evolving endeavor.


For enterprise agility, leaders must adopt[an agile approach to change management](https://lucid.co/blog/agile-change-management-tips) itself, constantly learning, iterating, and pivoting based on feedback. Some practices that leaders should implement include:


- **Creating new dashboards** to measure progress and using them to validate results as they practice new methods of success
- **Modeling agile behaviors** such as embracing feedback loops and making it safe to fail
- **Establishing a culture of experimentation** to gain insight into what’s working before scaling that success to other teams
- **Training employees** on new practices or behaviors
- **Focusing on continuous improvement** while empowering employees to share their ideas and improve their work

### Strategy #4: Conduct large-scale strategic planning


Since enterprise agility involves scaling adaptability, it’s essential to connect work across teams and ensure visibility and alignment throughout the whole organization. If you’re practicing agility successfully, you’ll be able to pivot as an enterprise without chaos, ensuring your business doesn’t just react but is built to thrive sustainably on change.

Conducting large-scale planning, such as[PI planning](https://lucid.co/blog/what-is-pi-planning) , is also a great way to coordinate teams and account for change. Enterprise agility involves complex execution that needs to be continually updated and monitored.[Dependency mapping](https://lucid.co/blog/how-to-use-dependency-mapping-to-boost-team-agility) is a highly useful practice that visualizes the relationships between tasks, making it easier to manage cross-functional work and to adapt quickly to changes.


By mapping dependencies, teams can manage, mitigate, or eliminate potential blockers. You can see whether one team’s work is dependent on another, and enhance visibility and pattern recognition. Factoring dependencies upfront into your planning further connects strategy to execution and empowers teams to make decisions based on real-time data.

### Strategy #6: Use OKRs and metrics to determine success or progress


As you implement enterprise agility, it’s important to communicate the vision and have everyone aligned on objectives. But how do you know when those objectives are being met?


[OKRs](https://lucid.co/blog/setting-the-right-okrs) connect larger objectives to team tasks, provide clarity into progress, and improve employee engagement. By housing your OKRs in the same place where your team tracks tasks, you can enhance visibility into progress. When the relationship between strategy and business results is more explicit, it’s easier to identify gaps and determine priorities. For enterprise agility OKRs, track metrics such as:


- Customer satisfaction
- Time to value
- Employee engagement
- Pivoting frequency and speed


### Strategy #7: Continue to adapt and improve


Enterprise agility isn’t a one-time goal or achievement; it’s an ongoing process that will evolve as you learn through continuous change. Implementing agility throughout your organization is a daily practice and mindset that should be exemplified by leaders, especially during challenging times.


[Psychological safety](https://lucid.co/blog/how-to-create-psychological-safety-at-work) is important; there will be moments when your organization tries to pivot and fails. Even in failure, employees should be encouraged to try again. Focus on the learnings, remain open to feedback from both internal and external sources, and iterate on your approach to scaling agility.[Retrospectives](https://lucid.co/blog/retrospective-template-roundup) are a great way to reflect on what’s gone well and what could be improved.


What worked last quarter may not work again—but determining what to do next instead of simply repeating the same process is the whole point of enterprise agility.
