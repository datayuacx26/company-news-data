## How to compare fleet cards in Portugal


Look beyond the headline fuel discount:


- **Acceptance on Portuguese routes.** Lisbon, Porto, Algarve and Spain-bound routes have different station patterns.
- **Tolls and Via Verde.** Tolls are a major fleet cost in Portugal; separate toll workflows create admin.
- **IVA recovery.** A clean consolidated invoice is worth more than a box of pump receipts.
- **Plafond and limits.** A good fleet card lets you cap monthly spend, fuel type, merchant category and driver use.
- **EV charging.** Check MOBI.E interoperability, roaming fees and whether charging sits on the same invoice as fuel.
- **Fees and deposits.** Monthly card fees, minimum spend, service charges and guarantees can erase savings.

## 2. Galp Frota


Galp is the most recognisable local option for many Portuguese businesses. Galp Frota covers fuel and electric mobility services and is a natural starting point for companies whose drivers already use Galp stations.


**Best for:** Portugal-first fleets that want a familiar local brand.


**Watch out:** brand-led acceptance is weaker if drivers need the nearest station, regardless of logo. Check how plafond, EV charging and Via Verde-style toll workflows are handled before assuming everything sits on one invoice.


**Website:**[Galp Frota](https://www.galp.com/)


## 3. BP Frota


BP offers fleet-card products for small companies and larger fleets. It is useful when routes naturally fit BP stations and the company wants a traditional fuel-card relationship.


**Best for:** drivers whose routes already match BP coverage.


**Watch out:** compare EV charging, toll and invoice workflows separately.


**Website:**[BP Portugal](https://www.bp.com/)


## 4. Repsol Solred


Repsol Solred is strong for Iberian operations, especially companies that move between Portugal and Spain. It is a practical brand-led card for fuel, services and cross-border Spanish routes.


**Best for:** fleets with Portugal-Spain routes and regular Repsol use.


**Watch out:** it remains a provider network, not a universal expense card.


**Website:**[Repsol Solred](https://www.repsol.pt/)


## 5. PRIO and Intermarche


PRIO and Intermarche matter for local, price-sensitive routes. If a driver regularly uses those stations, a local card or loyalty setup can be cheaper than a broader network.


**Best for:** local fleets whose routes align with lower-cost stations.


**Watch out:** these are less useful for cross-border, toll-heavy or EV-mixed operations.


## 6. Andamur


Andamur is a serious option for transport companies operating across Portugal, Spain and France. It focuses on HGV corridors, driver services and Iberian route density.


**Best for:** HGV fleets on Iberian corridors.


**Watch out:** not built for every employee expense, parking session or light commercial use case.


**Website:**[Andamur](https://www.andamur.com/)


## 7. Radius, iCompario and Qonto


Radius, iCompario and Qonto help Portuguese buyers compare fuel-card and fleet-card options without starting from each provider site. They are useful when you want a quick market view before speaking with Galp, BP, Repsol, PRIO, Andamur or a modern all-in-one platform.


**Best for:** early research and shortlist building.


**Watch out:** compare each provider's fee model directly before signing.


## EV charging: do not ignore MOBI.E


Electric charging in Portugal runs through the national MOBI.E ecosystem, with operators such as Galp, PRIO, EDP, Iberdrola, MEO Energia, Chargemap and others present in the market. When comparing cards, ask:


1. whether the card works across MOBI.E-compatible charging points;
2. whether the price per kWh is visible before charging;
3. whether there are roaming or session fees;
4. whether charging appears on the same invoice as fuel;
5. whether drivers need another app.
