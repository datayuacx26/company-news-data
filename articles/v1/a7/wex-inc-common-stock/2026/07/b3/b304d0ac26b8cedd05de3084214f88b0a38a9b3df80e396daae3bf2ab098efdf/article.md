# What is ACH, DPO, PCI DSS? 13 payables terms explained

### Share


Are you new to accounts payable or the business payments sector? Getting started in this field can be exciting and rewarding. To help you transition smoothly and confidently, we’ve compiled a list of 13 important definitions you should know.


## **What is payment acceptance?**


Payment acceptance refers to the percentage of payment attempts that are successfully processed. A payment acceptance rate is the percentage success rate, which is also referred to as a payment authorization rate.


## **ACH, explained: what it stands for and how it works**


ACH stands for Automated Clearing House and is described by the U.S. Treasury Department as “the primary system that agencies use for[electronic funds transfer (EFT)](https://www.fiscal.treasury.gov/ach/) .” ACH allows businesses and consumers to pay bills automatically by providing an account number and bank routing number.


## **What does a card issuer actually do?**


A card issuer is an institution that issues cards and provides credit limits. Card issuers[manage card features](https://www.cnbc.com/select/credit-card-network-vs-card-issuer-difference/) such as terms of use, benefits, and more.


## **What is a chargeback** ?


According to Forbes, a[chargeback](https://www.forbes.com/advisor/credit-cards/what-is-a-credit-card-chargeback/) is “an action taken by a bank to reverse electronic payments.” Chargebacks can occur due to fraud, disputed purchases, or unauthorized transactions and result in funds being returned to the cardholder’s account.


## **DPO (days payable outstanding)**


DPO stands for days payable outstanding, and is a key KPI for any accounts payable team. This is the “average number of days it takes a company to pay back its accounts payable,” according to the[Corporate Finance Institute](https://corporatefinanceinstitute.com/resources/accounting/days-payable-outstanding/) . High and low DPOs can mean different things, so they shouldn’t be necessarily viewed as one being good and one being bad.


## **What is an ERP, and how does it connect to accounts payable?**


ERP stands for enterprise resource planning and typically refers to the software used to manage business processes, such as[inventory, sales, marketing, finance, and human resources](https://www.investopedia.com/terms/e/erp.asp) . ERPs often integrate accounts payable with other systems into a single system.


## **Merchant accounts**


Merchants are any person or company who is selling products or services. A merchant account is an account established by the merchant to accept payment for these products or services. These payments can often be accepted in a variety of forms, such as credit cards, debit cards, cash, and wire transfers.


## **PCI DSS: the security standard behind card payments**


PCI DSS stands for Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard, which is set by card networks to ensure the safety and security of a cardholder and suppliers’ sensitive information.


Watch the video below to learn more about PCI DSS.


## **Processor**


Payment processors are the companies or technology that facilitate the transfer of funds from a cardholder’s bank account to the merchant’s bank account.


## **Reconciliation**


Reconciliation is the process of verifying financial transactions to align expected payments with actual ones. Two examples of how accounts payable teams do this are ensuring:


- The balance in books/records equals the amount on bank statements.
- An accounts payable balance aligns with a balance schedule.


## **Settlement**


Settlement is the process with which processors facilitate the transfer of funds between the merchant’s bank and the cardholder’s bank to complete a transaction.


## [Tokenization](https://www.wexinc.com/resources/blog/credit-card-tokenization-basics/) : how it protects card data from fraud


Tokenization replaces original card data with a unique, generated placeholder, or “token.” It helps to reduce fraud because the tokens on their own have no value to someone trying to commit fraud.


Watch the video below to learn more about tokenization.


## [Virtual cards](https://www.wexinc.com/products/business-payments/virtual-cards/)


A virtual card is a single-use electronic card number that can be used at merchants instead of using a plastic card. They help to increase payment security, simplify supplier payments, and increase efficiency in accounts payable.
