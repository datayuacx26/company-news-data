# Realtor Virtual Assistant: Close More Deals, Do Less Admin

8 min read


There’s a version of Tuesday afternoon that every active realtor knows too well. You’ve got two listings live, a buyer wavering on a third offer, four showing requests sitting in your inbox, and somehow you’re the one updating MLS data and chasing a DocuSign envelope that should have cleared three days ago. The calls you should be making — the ones that actually move deals — are sitting on hold while you work through a queue that was never supposed to be yours.


Admin doesn’t feel dangerous when business is good, because you’re still closing deals despite it. But the workload compounds faster than your capacity, and at some point, the ceiling becomes concrete: you can only close as many deals as you have hours to give.


Meanwhile, the realtors who push past that limit but aren’t working longer days? They’ve offloaded the operational layer, so their time goes where it actually converts.


## What a Realtor VA Handles: A Full Task Breakdown


The scope of what a realtor virtual assistant handles is wider than most agents expect when they first start looking into realtor virtual assistant services. There’s more under the operational umbrella of a real estate business than is obvious until you start mapping it out by category:


- **Listing Admin:** A VA can handle the tedious process of entering data into MLS portals, pulling initial comps for your review, and coordinating with photography teams. They also manage the upload process for sites like Zillow Premier Agent and draft the initial listing descriptions, saving you from hours of data entry.


- **Client Communication:** Maintaining a high-touch client experience is difficult when you are constantly in showings. Your assistant can take over appointment confirmations, send follow-up emails after every showing, and provide regular status updates to buyers and sellers as escrow progresses.


- **Transaction Coordination:** A deal is only as good as its paperwork, and a


[virtual assistant agency](https://getmagic.com/) provides the oversight needed to keep files moving. They track critical document deadlines in platforms like Dotloop or DocuSign and loop in title or escrow officers when signatures are missing.


- **Lead Management:** Lead decay happens when you’re too busy to monitor your CRM. A VA can manage your daily updates in kvCORE or Follow Up Boss, ensuring every open house inquiry receives an immediate response.


- **Marketing Support:** A VA handles the recurring production work, such as scheduling social media posts, distributing monthly market reports, and executing email campaigns to your database. This consistency keeps your brand in front of your sphere of influence while you focus on the “belly-to-belly” work of selling houses.


## The Deal Cost of Doing Your Own Admin


Here’s where the math gets uncomfortable. At $8,000 to $15,000 per closing and two closings a month, your effective hourly commission rate runs somewhere between $115 and $175. That’s what your time is worth when it’s spent in front of a client.


Now map that against what admin is actually consuming:


- 15 hours per week spent on tasks that don’t require your license, your judgment, or your client relationships


- 60 hours per month (more than a full work week) applied to data entry, document chasing, and follow-up drafts


- $6,900 to $10,500 in monthly earning capacity, at your effective hourly rate, going to work a VA could handle instead


All of these figures represent revenue that you’re actively working for and not collecting.


Then there’s the lead follow-up cost, which compounds everything above. In a business where speed shapes conversion, the gap between responding within an hour and responding the next morning isn’t a minor inconvenience. It’s often a lost client.


If you’re managing your own admin, you’re also the one returning calls at 11 p.m. or the following morning. Leads cool. Prospects move on. You rarely know exactly what that silence costs you.


## Training a VA on Real Estate Workflows


Training a VA on real estate workflows takes more deliberate setup than a general admin handoff. While the compliance layer is real, the framework for managing it is cleaner than most agents expect.


### The Compliance Question


Real estate operates under strict requirements around MLS access and document handling, and those requirements don’t leave much room for improvisation. Working through a virtual assistant agency means the screening and baseline training protocols are already in place, so your job is to layer in the real estate specifics rather than build the whole system from the ground up.


### Who Handles What


The division of labor should be clear. The VA handles the operational layer while the licensed agent retains anything that requires professional judgment or carries legal weight. That means:


A VA… You…


Prepares and tracks DocuSign envelopes, flagging any unsigned items Advise a client on whether to accept a contingency


Enters and updates listings per your input Make pricing decisions


Logs contacts and runs follow-up sequences in kvCORE or Follow Up Boss Handle sensitive client conversations


Coordinates with title and escrow on logistics Negotiate timelines or contract terms


### Building SOPs That Protect You


Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs) are what make this arrangement work smoothly. When you document exactly how a recurring task gets done, down to the specific fields and templates involved, you’re building both a training guide and a compliance record.


For the tasks you run most often across Dotloop and kvCORE, one focused documentation session typically covers the bulk of the workflow. The VA follows the protocol; you review anything that touches a legal document or a client decision.


## How Realtors Who Use VAs Close More Deals


The shift doesn’t show up in one dramatic moment. It accumulates across dozens of small outputs that stack into a meaningfully different week:


- **Lead Response Time:** Inbound leads get a response within the hour because someone is monitoring them in real time, not waiting for you to clear your queue.


- **Showing Follow-Ups:** Every buyer who attended a showing receives a follow-up that afternoon, handled and sent before the end of the day.


- **Transaction Deadlines:** Dotloop files stay current, and contingency windows get flagged before they close, so nothing critical slips through a busy week.


- **Marketing Cadence:** MLS listings stay current, and market reports reach your sphere on time, rather than arriving late with an apologetic subject line.


- **Pipeline Activity:** Contacts in kvCORE or Follow Up Boss get logged and nurtured consistently, keeping leads warm between conversations.


When the administrative layer is consistently covered, the agent’s calendar fills with client-facing time rather than operational catch-up. Clients feel attended to throughout the process, which in real estate is the most reliable engine for referrals. Consistent follow-up by a VA is directly related to how frequently closings occur.


## Getting Started: Your First 30 Days


The first 30 days are a calibration period, not a full handoff. A VA who knows exactly what’s expected and how to execute it will deliver more in month two than one who was handed a vague brief on day one.


### Week 1


Identify your highest-priority recurring tasks and document how each one gets done. Most realtors start with MLS coordination and client communication follow-ups, as both recur every week and have clear enough parameters to document without a heavy lift. Getting the SOPs right at this stage saves significant back-and-forth later.


### Week 2


Hand the documented tasks off to your VA and walk through each SOP once in a live session to clear up any ambiguity. Your role this week is to answer questions and flag anything that needs adjusting before it becomes a habit.


### Week 3


Review the VA’s output before it goes out. This is where small gaps tend to surface: a template that needs refining or a step that was unclear in the SOP. Fix them now while the process is still being established, and you’ll rarely need to revisit them.


### Week 4


Step back from active review on the tasks running cleanly and start expanding the scope. By this point, you’ll have a clear read on where to add more, and you’ll also have real hours back, visible in your call volume and your ability to respond to inbound inquiries the same day rather than the next morning.
