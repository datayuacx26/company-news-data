# Microschool Grants and Funding Sources: 20+ Options to Finance Your School in 2026

Microschool funding comes from diverse sources including dedicated grants, state education savings accounts, corporate programs, and crowdfunding platforms. The best starting point for most founders is VELA, which offers grants up to $10,000 specifically for alternative education projects like microschools. Beyond VELA, microschool founders can access state ESA programs averaging $7,500 per student, federal STEM education funding, corporate giving initiatives, and specialized foundation grants.


At KaiPod, we’ve helped hundreds of microschool founders navigate the funding landscape through our[KaiPod Catalyst training program](https://www.kaipodlearning.com/start-a-microschool/) . Most successful founders combine multiple funding sources, using seed grants for startup costs while building toward tuition-based sustainability. Nonprofit microschools have access to the widest range of grant opportunities, though for-profit schools can still access state ESA programs, corporate giving, crowdfunding, and select federal grants.


The funding options below are organized by type, starting with the highest-value opportunities for microschool founders. Each listing includes eligibility requirements, typical award amounts, and application timing to help you prioritize your funding strategy.

## **Top Microschool Funding Source: VELA Education Fund**


VELA is a community of entrepreneurs dedicated to providing funding for alternative education projects. They’re especially focused on supporting small learning environments, making them the top source of microschool funding. Since 2019, VELA has distributed over $24.2 million through more than 2,000 grants across all 50 states.


VELA operates through a tiered funding system designed to support microschools at every stage:


**Micro Grants:** $2,500 or $10,000 for early-stage founders getting their schools off the ground.


**Next Step Grants:** Up to $50,000 for Micro Grant recipients ready to expand their reach.


**Bridge Grants:** Up to $250,000 for organizations prepared to scale their model widely.


### **How to Apply**


Founders must first join the VELA Founders Network (free membership) before accessing funding opportunities. Applications are accepted on a rolling basis throughout the year. Learn more about[VELA’s eligibility criteria](https://vela.org/eligibility-criteria/) .


### **Eligibility Requirements**


VELA funds organizations that serve K-12 learners outside traditional public, private, or charter schools. You must be operational (not in the planning phase), have proof of concept, and be U.S.-based. Traditional schools, daycare-only programs, and international organizations are ineligible.


The application process is competitive—for their 2022 Next Step grants, 550+ pre-applications resulted in just 32 recipients, or approximately 6% of initial applicants. However, the free network membership provides valuable connections and resources even before you receive funding.


## **Yass Prize: Education’s Million-Dollar Recognition**


The Yass Prize program supports organizations that provide sustainable, transformational educational experiences outside the traditional school framework. Known as the “Pulitzer of Education Innovation,” the prize has awarded over $46 million since its founding in 2021.


### **Award Amounts**


Award amounts vary by year. For the 2025 competition:


- **Grand Prize Winner:** $1,000,000
- **Finalists:** $250,000 each (4 finalists)
- **Semifinalists:** $100,000 each (18 semifinalists)


**Note:** Previous years had different tier structures. The amounts and number of awards at each level can change annually.

Organizations must demonstrate S.T.O.P. principles: Sustainable operations, Transformational impact, Outstanding results, and Permissionless innovation.


**Alumni Awards Program:** In 2025, the Yass Prize launched an Alumni Awards Program specifically for past awardees, providing additional funding opportunities for proven education innovators to expand their models.


Learn more at[yassprize.org/about](https://yassprize.org/about) .


## **State Education Savings Account Programs**


State ESA programs represent the most reliable source of per-student funding for microschools. As of 2025, 38% of microschools now receive state school choice funds, up from 32% in 2024. These programs allow families to use state education dollars for approved education expenses, including microschool tuition.


### **Arizona Empowerment Scholarship Accounts**


Arizona operates the nation’s most expansive universal ESA program with 98,244 students enrolled as of December 2025.


**Award Amount:** $7,000-$8,000 annually for standard students, with significantly higher amounts for students with disabilities


**Eligibility:** Universal—all Arizona students are eligible


**Application:** Ongoing at[esaonline.azed.gov](https://www.azed.gov/esa) with 30-day processing times


### **Florida Tax Credit Scholarships**


Florida’s FTC and FES-EO programs serve families statewide with universal eligibility.


**Award Amount:** $7,400-$11,950 depending on county and grade level (Monroe County pays highest)


**Eligibility:** Universal as of 2023


**Application:** Opens February 1 annually through Step Up For Students


### **Texas Education Freedom Account (Launching 2026)**


Texas launches its massive ESA program in Fall 2026—the largest initial school choice program any state has launched.


**Award Amount:** Approximately $10,000-$10,900 per student (students with disabilities may receive up to $30,000 additional)


**Eligibility:** Universal program with $1 billion appropriation potentially serving 80,000-100,000 students initially


**Application:** Opens February 4, 2026


### **West Virginia Hope Scholarship**


West Virginia’s Hope Scholarship explicitly includes microschools as eligible providers.


**Award Amount:** $5,267.38 per student for 2025-26


**Eligibility:** Universal eligibility beginning 2026-27 (currently has income restrictions)


**Participating Schools:** 233 participating nonpublic schools and microschools serving 10,530 students


**Application:** Opens annually in March


### **Virginia Education Improvement Scholarships Tax Credits**


Virginia’s program operates through an indirect tax credit model rather than direct payments.


**Award Amount:** Average ~$2,800 per student


**Eligibility:** Income limits of 300% federal poverty level ($93,600 for a family of four), with special needs students eligible at 400% FPL


**Annual Cap:** $25 million statewide


For families considering your microschool, knowing which states offer ESA programs can make the difference in affordability. Visit[EdChoice](https://www.edchoice.org/) to research current programs in your state.


## **Federal Education Funding for Microschools**


While most federal K-12 education grants do not directly fund private schools or microschools, important pathways exist for STEM-focused programs.


### **Project Lead the Way (PLTW)**


PLTW provides the most accessible federal-adjacent funding for STEM education. The program has distributed over $75 million to more than 6,600 schools over 10 years.


**Award Amount:** Covers participation fees, professional development, and equipment costs


**Eligibility:** Nonprofit private schools are explicitly eligible alongside public schools


**Application Deadlines:** November 7 and December 5, 2025 for 2026-27 school year


**Focus:** PreK-12 STEM and Career Technical Education programs


PLTW recommends applying during the fall semester to be considered for as many grant opportunities as possible, as they have partnerships with multiple corporate sponsors. Learn more at[pltw.org/investment/funding-grants](https://www.pltw.org/plan-for-pltw/investment/funding-grants) .


### **Innovative Technology Experiences for Students and Teachers (ITEST)**


Microschools with a strong focus on STEM education may qualify for[ITEST grants](https://new.nsf.gov/funding/opportunities/innovative-technology-experiences-students) supported by the National Science Foundation.


**Award Amount:** $500,000-$3.5 million for larger projects


**Important Note:** K-12 schools cannot apply directly. You must partner with eligible organizations like universities or nonprofits who serve as the lead applicant.


**Focus:** Programs that build STEM knowledge and interest, especially for diverse, underserved populations


**Application Period:** Through August each year for the following year’s awards


### **U.S. Department of Education**


The federal government has millions of dollars available for education through various grant programs. Register with the System for Award Management (SAM), then use[ed.gov/fund/grants-apply.html](https://www2.ed.gov/fund/grants-apply.html?src=pn) to find potential grant opportunities.


**Important Note:** Most Department of Education grants fund public school districts or state education agencies, not individual private schools. However, private school students may receive equitable services through Title programs (Title I, II, III, and IV) where your local public school district provides proportional services to eligible private school students.

## **Named Foundation Grants**


These foundations support education initiatives with verified current details as of 2025:


### **Mockingbird Foundation**


The Mockingbird Foundation funds music education programs for children and young adults.


**Award Amount:** $100-$10,000


**Eligibility:** Programs serving children 18 and under with 501(c)(3) status or government entity. Religious programs ineligible.


**Application Deadline:** January 15 annually


**Note:** Extremely competitive—funds fewer than 1% of inquiries. Submit an inquiry form at[mbird.org](https://mbird.org/grants/) to see if they’re interested in your full proposal.


### **Charles Lafitte Foundation**


This charitable organization funds K-12 education projects including special education, at-risk students, and innovative teaching.


**Award Amount:** Typically ~$5,000 (range: few thousand to $1 million for exceptional projects)


**Eligibility:** Prefers organizations with overhead under 15%


**Geographic Focus:** New Jersey, Florida, New York


**Application:** Accepted any time through[their website](https://charleslafitte.org/grants/) describing specific projects with established budgets and measurable impacts


### **Saxena Family Foundation**


This foundation supports STEM education programs, especially those targeted toward girls.


**Award Amount:** $5,000-$50,000


**Eligibility:** 501(c)(3) public charities only


**Geographic Focus:** California, Massachusetts, New York, Texas


**Application:** Rolling basis year-round atinfo@saxenafoundation.org or visit saxenafamilyfoundation.com


### **Change Happens Foundation**


This family foundation focuses on science, the environment, and innovative education.


**Award Amount:** Varies based on project


**Eligibility:** 3-year history of 990 forms required


**Geographic Priorities:** Iowa, San Francisco Bay Area, Santa Barbara County, Puget Sound, Portland


**Application:** Start by sending a letter of inquiry to[changehappens.us](https://www.changehappens.us/) . If interested, you’ll be invited to submit a full proposal.


### **Snapdragon Book Foundation**


If your microschool’s library will help support children in disadvantaged communities, Snapdragon donates books to fill it.


**Award Amount:** $500-$2,200+


**Eligibility:** Public, private, and experimental schools are explicitly eligible—one of the few programs that includes private microschools for library funding


**Application Period:** November to February each year at[snapdragonbookfoundation.org/application](https://snapdragonbookfoundation.org/application/)


**Note:** 100% of donations go directly to schools for books

## **Community Foundation Grants**


Community foundations offer localized funding opportunities in nearly every region. These foundations typically offer education grants ranging from $1,000-$15,000, with larger initiatives reaching $25,000 or more.


### **How to Find Your Local Community Foundation**


Use the Community Foundation Locator at[cof.org](https://www.cof.org/) to identify foundations in your area. Most community foundations support local education initiatives and may be more accessible than national foundations.


### **Example Programs**


**Community Foundations of Hudson Valley:** Fund for Excellence in Education provides up to $2,500 for K-12 teachers in public, private, and parochial schools in Dutchess, Orange, Putnam, and Ulster counties.


**Local United Ways:** Many United Way organizations support education initiatives in their communities through competitive grant programs.


Community foundations often have strong relationships with local donors interested in supporting education innovation in their own backyards. Building relationships with program officers can lead to multi-year funding partnerships.


## **Crowdfunding for Microschools**


While crowdfunding shouldn’t be your primary funding source, it can effectively raise money for specific projects or equipment needs.


### **Platform Comparison**


**DonorsChoose:** Achieves 75-77% success rates for education projects—nearly triple general crowdfunding platforms. However, it serves **only public schools and public charter schools** . Private microschools are ineligible. Visit[donorschoose.org](https://www.donorschoose.org/) .


**Adopt a Classroom:** Similar to DonorsChoose, this platform supports teachers but is limited to public schools. Visit[adoptaclassroom.org](https://www.adoptaclassroom.org/) .


**GoFundMe:** Accepts private school fundraising with overall success rates around 27%. Education campaigns average $2,500 raised. Fees total 2.9% + $0.30 per donation. Visit[gofundme.com](https://www.gofundme.com/) .


**Kickstarter:** Sees 42% overall success rates but education is not a top-performing category. The all-or-nothing model charges 5% platform fee + 3% + $0.20 per pledge only if successful. Visit[kickstarter.com](https://www.kickstarter.com/?country=US) .


**PledgeCents:** School-focused platform with 3% processing fee and features designed specifically for educational fundraising.


### **Crowdfunding Best Practices**


Research shows several factors dramatically increase success rates:


**Campaign Length:** 30-40 days is optimal—longer campaigns lose momentum


**Video:** Campaigns featuring videos raise 4x more than those without


**Updates:** Posting updates every 5 days correlates with 3x more raised


**Early Momentum:** Reaching 30% funding in the first week dramatically increases success probability


**Clear Goals:** Be specific about exactly what you’re funding (classroom library, STEM equipment, playground upgrades) and how donated money will be spent


**Personal Connection:** Share your story and vision authentically. People support people, not just projects.


For private microschools, GoFundMe, Fundly, or PledgeCents represent the best crowdfunding options since DonorsChoose excludes private schools. Set realistic goals of $2,500-$5,000 for first campaigns.


### **Accountability Matters**


Hold yourself accountable to donors, especially if you hope to solicit donations again in the future. Share updates on how funds were used and the impact they made. This transparency builds trust for future fundraising efforts.
