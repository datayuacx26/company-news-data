# Finding Joy at School: How Parents Can Support Their Kids' Mental Health

Mental health and well-being have become increasingly important for parents. In a parent survey conducted in 2022, four in ten parents ([40%](https://www.pewresearch.org/social-trends/2023/01/24/parenting-in-america-today/) ) expressed concerns about their child's mental health, including struggles with anxiety or depression.


Unfortunately, school often plays a significant role in these worries, with children sharing stories about negative incidents or social pressures they face. For parents considering a new school option—whether private school, homeschool, or an alternative like KaiPod—mental health[tops the list of reasons](https://www.edweek.org/leadership/parents-worried-about-their-kids-mental-health-see-the-fix-in-new-schooling-options/2024/02) to make a change.


#### At KaiPod, we often hear from parents whose children felt isolated or reluctant to attend school.


Parents are the most influential advocates for their kids. But when their kids feel lonely, excluded, bored, or pressured to stand out, parents often feel powerless to change their child is environment. While the majority may stick to their zoned school or what they think is “the norm,” more and more families are realizing that there isn't just one way to do school.


That's why we created a[Roadmap for Parents Rethinking School](https://www.kaipodlearning.com/roadmap-for-parents-switching-schools/?utm_campaign=Parent%20Roadmap&utm_source=Blog&utm_medium=mental-health-blog&utm_content=traditional-parents) , a guide for families who are ready to make a change.


### How Kate Found Confidence at KaiPod


Kate's path to KaiPod was marked by challenges typical of traditional schooling environments.


Between the ages of 10 and 12, Kate experienced three different schools, but no matter how great the people and campuses were, she would always end up preferring to spend time alone in the bathroom than with her teachers and peers. She was so excited each time she transitioned to a new school with high hopes of feeling accepted and happy, but over time, the bad days would far outweigh the good until every minute of every school day became a tremendous struggle.


#### Over time, the cost of going to school all day every day was taking a significant toll on her mental health.


After trying three different schools, Kate's family urgently needed to find an alternative. They realized that much of the problem was the requirement to be on campus seven hours per day, five days per week.


Her parents didn’t have the confidence or time to provide a successful homeschool experience, so they decided to give an online school a shot. Even though their online school provided live Zoom classes, office hours, and clubs, Kate had difficulty connecting in a way that allowed her to build meaningful relationships. She went from being in a physical classroom 35 hours per week to zero, and they felt frustrated that there was no option in the middle.


It wasn't until Kate’s mom found KaiPod that her educational journey took a positive turn.


#### At KaiPod, Kate found a supportive community.


Thanks to her mom’s research and willingness to make a change, Kate found herself immersed in a welcoming environment at KaiPod. The small group setting allowed her to connect with other kids and receive academic support with her online studies—a perfect middle ground between the overwhelming traditional classroom and isolation at home.


### Connections Form Naturally In Small Groups


Microschools like KaiPod have a unique opportunity to focus on well-being, inclusivity, and community through the bonds that form within small group communities.


Close-knit relationships cultivate empathy, compassion, and a sense of shared purpose. When kids’ primary needs to feel safe, understood, and respected are met, this opens the door to self-acceptance, spontaneity, and curiosity.


The mentorship and guidance Kate received from her KaiPod Learning Coaches was key to her progress. They provided invaluable support, helping Kate connect with other students and embrace her strengths. Most importantly, Kate found a sense of belonging at a place where she could be herself and rediscover the joy of learning.
