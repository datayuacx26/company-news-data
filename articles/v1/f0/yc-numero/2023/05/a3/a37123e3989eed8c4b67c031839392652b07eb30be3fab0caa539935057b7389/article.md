# Political Finance Compliance Best Practices

Finance compliance is a critical but complicated aspect of any successful campaign, and knowing where to get started can feel overwhelming. How do you learn the laws for your specific election? Who is best suited to help you stay in compliance? How much energy should you devote to compliance when so many other things need your attention?


We’ve detailed the basics of financial compliance that we’ve learned from the compliance teams we work with and included helpful tips that compliance expert[Jonathan Brill](https://www.scytl.com/about-us/management-team/jonathan-brill/) from Computare Partners was able to share with us during a recent interview.


## **The Basics of Financial Compliance**


### **What is campaign compliance?**


All political campaigns and political organizations are subject to specific political law and guidelines regardless of the level. There are many important things to be aware of regarding campaign finance laws.


In federal elections, for example,[political compliance laws](https://www.fec.gov/help-candidates-and-committees/guides/?tab=candidates-and-their-authorized-committees) include:


- political contribution limits
- candidate loans and debts
- how campaign funds can be used
- what records must be maintained
- finance reporting deadlines
- who campaign assets can be sold to
- what travel costs can be covered by campaign funds


…and so much more.


### **Why is political compliance important in politics?**


Finances are key in a campaign. Although raising the most money doesn’t guarantee victory, having a healthy budget is essential for the success of most campaigns. It enables you to hire campaign staff, organize events, and cover the costs of all necessary campaign materials. The way you raise *and* spend your campaign money must be in accordance with your specific election laws. Compliance becomes an issue when you don’t follow the legal requirements set for you.


It doesn’t take much for a loss of compliance to damage your entire campaign— even if it hasn’t actually happened yet. One compliance expert warns that a letter from the Division of Elections regarding a small error in your finances or reporting of a single political contribution can quickly become an article or tweet that’s reshared and retweeted as the validity of your campaign comes into question. The best answer for risk mitigation is to invest the time and resources it takes to keep your finances in line at *every* point of the campaign.


### **How do I find out about my political compliance expectations?**


Compliance expectations vary based on your level of race and political activities.


For local political campaigns, your city hall or local government website should be able to provide information about what it takes to remain financially compliant. For state races, a quick search of \[state\] + ‘political finance compliance’ typically provides the results you need. The[Federal Election Commission](https://www.fec.gov/) provides guidelines for national candidates regarding financial compliance.


## **Financial compliance best practices**


The[National Democratic Training Committee](https://traindemocrats.org/resources/campaign-financial-compliance/) suggests a number of policies and procedures to consider when building an effective campaign compliance program.


### **Maintain accurate records**


Keep all records that show your incoming and outgoing funds, and organize them into categories including:


- bank statements and deposit slips
- invoices
- receipts
- bills
- credit card statements
- copies of checks and money orders
- political contribution cards
- contracts


### **Authorize and track expenditures**


Whether for a $3 coffee or $3,000 in yard signs, record every expense from your campaign fund. It’s also wise to designate an amount that requires approval from the candidate and/or treasurer.


### **Track political contributions**


As part of your compliance plan, determine the timeframe in which political contributions should be processed, recorded, and reposited. One of the easiest ways to do so is to designate specific volunteers or[campaign staff to perform](https://www.numero.ai/blog/campaign-staff/) duties on certain days of the week. This table from the NDTC gives you an idea of what this might look like.

### **Reconcile your financials**


At least once a month, someone should review the campaign’s online bank transitions to ensure all deposits and withdrawals are recorded and the budget is correctly balanced. In addition, this person should look over the next month’s contracts and invoices so you can anticipate upcoming expenses.


### **Complete campaign finance reports**


This isn’t just best practice— it’s the law. The agency that your campaign reports to will have designated dates throughout the campaign period when you are required to submit a finance report detailing all of your incoming and outgoing money.


### **Stay organized**


Advancing technology and campaign practices means there’s no shortage of tools to keep your finances in line. Before you even kick off your campaign, compliance experts suggest choosing a tool to help you stay organized. For smaller local races, this might mean using a spreadsheet with a detailed schedule. Larger races will benefit from more involved, fully integrated strategies such as using compliance software or working with a compliance team.

## **Financial Compliance FAQs**


### **What is the most important element of a financial compliance program?**


To have a successful financial compliance program, accurate record-keeping is essential. This means keeping track of every transaction in and out of your campaign. By having the required documentation, you can manage compliance in an organized and efficient manner.


### **Who is typically on a compliance team?**


A financial compliance team is going to include a mixture of people who are involved with the finances of a campaign, such as the—


- Candidate
- Campaign treasurer
- Accountant (often a volunteer)
- Compliance firm
- Consulting attorney


The campaign treasurer and compliance firm will manage the department, with oversight from the[campaign manager](https://www.numero.ai/blog/how-to-become-a-political-campaign-manager/) as needed, particularly around reporting deadlines.


### **How much compliance is too much?**


As Jonathan Brill recently shared with us, “the biggest surprise for first time candidates is how much time goes into an election regardless of the size of the race.” Campaign compliance should be a top priority for every candidate, but it shouldn’t overshadow campaigning efforts. At the start of your campaign, it will take more time to bring on the right compliance team and establish an appropriate routine. However, once these basics are set, you can step back— at least until campaign finance reports are due!


### **How much do compliance firms charge?**


Compliance firms are relatively new to the world of campaigning but are becoming increasingly popular for candidates who are aware of the time and effort that go into maintaining regulatory compliance.


If you decide to work with a compliance firm, you should anticipate paying anywhere from a few hundred to thousands of dollars monthly. Typically, the cost is tied to the amount you are raising, the scope of your political activities, and the complexity of your state. Costs may also increase during the election year.


Services will vary from firm to firm, but these compliance experts will typically help campaigns to—


- establish and maintain payroll for campaign staff.
- maintain accurate records for fundraising and general finances.
- provide guidance for compliance laws at every level.
- create and file campaign finance reports to the appropriate regulatory agencies.
