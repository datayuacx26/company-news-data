# FAQs for First-Time Political Candidates

[Running for political office](https://www.numero.ai/blog/us-political-glossary/) is both challenging and rewarding. It also requires a lot of knowledge that’s difficult to obtain until you’ve been through a campaign.


How to[run a successful campaign](https://www.numero.ai/blog/how-to-run-for-office/) doesn’t have to be a mystery. This guide will walk you through everything you need to know, from the very first step you need to take to how to manage your money and campaign staff most effectively.


## **Running for Political Office**


### **What are the steps to run for political office?**


To officially begin your campaign, you must register to run for office. This notifies the appropriate commission of your candidacy and allows you to form a political campaign committee. For federal elections, you’ll file with the[Federal Election Committee](https://www.fec.gov/help-candidates-and-committees/registering-candidate/) ; for state and local elections, you’ll need to do a quick search to find out who you file with.


Once you’ve registered, it’s time to obtain your Federal Employee Identification Number. Your FEIN is typically necessary to create a campaign bank account.


You will likely need insurance if you hire any campaign staff, host fundraising events, or open a campaign office.


A finance director and campaign manager will typically be your first two hires. These staffers can help you establish your finance and campaign plan.


Once you have a strong campaign foundation, it’ll be time to announce your intent to run for office and consider growing your campaign staff. You’ll then hit the ground running with your fundraising and voter outreach efforts.


### **When should I fill out my candidacy application?**


If you’re running for federal office, you must[register](https://www.fec.gov/help-candidates-and-committees/registering-candidate/) and begin filing financial reports once you raise or spend more than $5,000.


Requirements for state and local elections vary. Every state provides online information about its registration requirements. You can find state guidance[here](https://www.usa.gov/election-office) .


### **How long does a political campaign last?**


Most candidates start campaigns up to 2 years before an election. In 2016, the[average](https://www.npr.org/sections/itsallpolitics/2015/10/21/450238156/canadas-11-week-campaign-reminds-us-that-american-elections-are-much-longer) national election campaign lasted 596 days. This period becomes shorter as you move to the state and local election levels, with local political campaigns typically lasting 3-6 months.


### **What political office can I run for?**


Local elections are a great place to start if you’ve never run or been elected before. Running a local political campaign helps you build a foundation for bigger campaigns down the road, both in terms of experience and a strong support base.


You may need to do some digging to kick off a local political campaign, as the types of offices available can vary between states and cities. To learn what local offices you can run for, look into when the next elections will be held and who your current elected officials are.


Information about state and national offices is more readily available.


### **Can anyone run for political office?**


To run for U.S. Senate, you must be a citizen for at least 9 years and be at least 30 years old. For U.S. Representatives, the minimums are 7 years of citizenship and 25 years of age. Requirements for[governor](https://ballotpedia.org/Qualifications_for_governor_in_each_state) and[state legislators](https://ballotpedia.org/State_legislature_candidate_requirements_by_state) vary from state to state, but there is typically an age minimum and residency requirement.


## **Creating a Campaign Strategy**


A successful campaign demands a comprehensive strategy, as you wouldn’t bake a cake without a recipe and the required ingredients.


### **How do you create a political campaign strategy?**


A[campaign strategy](https://www.numero.ai/blog/primary-election-strategies/) outlines the entire campaign through election day, including a timeline, vote goal, staff, and fundraising.


The[National Democratic Training Committee](https://traindemocrats.org/blog/making-political-campaign-plan/) recommends creating a four-pronged strategy that addresses each of these areas:


1. [Communications](https://traindemocrats.org/blog/candidate-political-campaign-communications-plan-template/) . How will your team deliver your campaign message to target voters?
2. [Digital](https://traindemocrats.org/course/political-campaign-digital-strategy/) . How will your digital advertising and online or social media presence help you reach potential voters?
3. [Budget and Fundraising](https://traindemocrats.org/blog/candidate-political-campaign-fundraising-plan-template-and-guide/) **.** What are your fundraising goals, and what strategies will you use to meet them? How much money do you need to achieve your political advertising plan?
4. **Field.** What is your field plan for[direct voter contact](https://www.numero.ai/blog/voter-engagement-gotv-field-events/) via canvassing, phone banking, and texting?


Addressing these departments helps the staff set goals and plan their next steps.


### **What makes a good political candidate?**


Before jumping into the characteristics that make a political candidate exceptional, it’s crucial to consider the challenges they may face throughout their campaign and once in office. An outstanding candidate will be adept at addressing every situation with ease.


A good political candidate is:


**Competent** . This may be obvious, but it’s worth mentioning. They must understand relevant issues, skillfully handle campaign details, and demonstrate strong leadership and decision-making abilities.
**Service-oriented** . A good candidate is running to help others, not elevate their status.
**Committed.** Campaigning is challenging work. A good candidate is willing to make personal sacrifices for long-term goals.
**Articulate.** Not everyone will agree with you, but a strong candidate should be able to communicate their stance on the various issues.


### **How many hours per week can you set aside for campaigning?**


Campaigning often becomes a full-time commitment for those running for state or federal office.
Call time should account for at least 2-4 hours daily, totaling 10-30 hours weekly.
Many weeks may require 50-60 hours of work in the month leading up to the election.


## **Fundraising**


### **How much money do you need for a political campaign?**


The cost of a political campaign can vary wildly depending on the office you’re running for, but the truth is that running for office at any level requires money.


In 2018, Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez, a grassroots candidate, raised over $2.1 million, which was modest compared to her competitors. Campaign funding has been reaching unprecedented levels. In 2020, the average Senate campaign raised[$43](https://www.fec.gov/data/raising-bythenumbers/?office=S) million, while the average Congressional campaign raised[$2.39](https://www.opensecrets.org/elections-overview/fundraising-totals?cycle=2020&type=HA&view=topraise) million. Joe Biden’s fundraising surpassed[$1 billion](https://www.nytimes.com/2020/10/28/us/politics/2020-race-money.html) . Additionally, eight of the ten[most expensive Senate races](https://www.nytimes.com/2020/10/28/us/politics/2020-race-money.html) occurred in 2020.


To learn more about the amount raised for federal campaigns, visit FEC.gov. For state-level campaigns, check your Secretary of State’s website; check with your City Clerk for local campaigns.


When drafting an initial campaign budget, consider creating a fundraising plan for three levels— best-case, worst-case, and a realistic budget somewhere in the middle. As you jump full-force into your fundraising efforts, you’ll better understand which budget is most appropriate or doable.


### **How can I raise money for my campaign?**


Four fundraising methods are typically used during political campaigns.


1.


**Calls:** Call time plays a crucial role in every campaign. You should expect to dedicate 2-4 hours daily to connect with potential donors and build relationships to generate contributions for your campaign. For more information and ideas on setting up call time, check out our[comprehensive fundraising guide](https://www.numero.ai/blog/political-fundraising) .


2.


**Fundraising events:** In-person events allow you to network with donors and ask for contributions face-to-face.


3.


**Grassroots or digital fundraising:** These campaigns rely on smaller, recurring individual contributions rather than large, one-time donations.


### **Are you willing to spend some of your personal finances on the political campaign?**


While fully funding a campaign on your own might not be feasible, contributing a manageable amount to your own campaign can be beneficial. By investing in your campaign, you not only demonstrate commitment to your cause but also serve as a constant reminder to believe in yourself. Be careful, you shouldn’t spend what you don’t have.


## **Building a Support Network**


### **How do you build a support network to help contribute to these campaign roles?**


People in your network can fall into one of five categories:


1. **Personal** . Family and friends in your close circle.
2. **Professional** . People you know from work, community, or organizations you’re involved with.
3. **Donors** . People who will contribute money to your campaign.
4. **Validators** . Those in positions of authority or leadership who can support your campaign.
5. **Supporters and volunteers** : People who may not have the means to contribute monetarily but will knock on doors and make phone calls to reach other voters about your campaign.


To keep track of everyone in your network, you’ll need a campaign Rolodex— a running record of everyone in your network and the contact information for each. You can organize this on a spreadsheet, but we recommend choosing software like[Numero](https://www.numero.ai/) , where you can maintain contact, contribution, and pledge records.


The[key](https://traindemocrats.org/blog/a-guide-to-political-networking/) to expanding your network is building and strengthening relationships. Focus on what you can do for others and weave that into conversations with them. Find ways to personalize your conversations, for instance, keeping a record of birthdays to check in with potential donors or congratulating someone on their child’s graduation. Showing potential donors and supporters that you care and are interested in them can go a long way in strengthening valuable relationships.


### **Have you discussed a potential campaign with your spouse or partner?**


Let’s face it: a political campaign can be draining. It’s both a marathon and a sprint that may require 12-18 months of heavy involvement. While you may be comfortable with the sacrifices that will entail, your partner and family must also be.


Are they okay with the time you’ll spend away from your family? Are they comfortable with the responsibilities you’ll take on if you win? Is your family okay with being in the spotlight at events? Before you register to run for office, these are all conversations you need to have.


## **Getting Your Message Out to Voters**


### **How many voters are there in your community?**


Typically, your Secretary of State’s office will regularly supply information on voter registration and the total number of voters in your district. The US Census Bureau also provides[information](https://www.census.gov/topics/public-sector/voting.html) about voter registration every two years after national elections.


Search registered voters + \[your state name\] to find more specific information regarding your state. For example, a quick search for[Kentucky](https://elect.ky.gov/Resources/Pages/Registration-Statistics.aspx) reveals the number, gender, and parties of registered voters for county, precinct, Congressional, Senate, House, and Supreme Court elections.


### **How many voters do you need to contact to reach your vote target?**


1. Determine your vote goal by researching a recent, similar election to identify the voter turnout percentage.
2. Find the current number of registered voters in your district.


Calculate the expected voter turnout using this formula:


Voter turnout percentage x total number of registered voters = expected turnout


To find your vote goal, multiply the expected turnout by the percentage of votes you need to win. If you’re running against one other candidate, you’ll need at least 51% of the votes. However, if there are more candidates, the required percentage will vary.


Remember that not every voter you contact will support you; some will vote for you even if you don’t directly engage with them.


### **What are ways to get your message out to voters?**


Engaging with voters often involves a combination of face-to-face interactions, paid and earned media advertisements, and communication via phone calls, door knocking, and social media. You’ll need to recruit volunteers to extend your reach and effectively convey your message.


Consider utilizing the following methods to connect with voters:


1. **Campaign events** : Organize events such as rallies, where you can simultaneously engage with a large audience and foster support for your campaign.
2. **Phone banks** : During these events, volunteers call persuadable or low-turnout voters to communicate your campaign’s key message.
3. **Door knocking/canvassing** : Similar to phone banks, volunteers visit specific neighborhoods to interact with targeted voters at their homes.
4. **Paid media** : Invest in various paid marketing channels, including TV commercials, online ads, direct mail, radio commercials, and Facebook or other online ads.


## **Campaign Roles and Responsibilities**


### **What are the key roles in a political campaign?**


There are a variety of roles in a political campaign, but filling all is unnecessary, especially if your budget doesn’t allow it. The larger your race, the more people you’ll need on your team.


### **CAMPAIGN MANAGER**


A **campaign** **manager** oversees almost everything that goes on within a campaign. They will supervise the rest of the staff, help you make strategic decisions, work with consultants, manage the budget, and help keep you on track.


You’ll typically hire a campaign manager if your fundraising target is more than $100,000.


### **FINANCE DIRECTOR**


A **finance** **director** manages everything money-related. Their responsibilities include creating and implementing the fundraising plans. In addition, they conduct donor history research, handle campaign donations, coordinate and manage fundraising events, and organize call time schedules.


It’s prudent to hire a finance director as one of your first staff members, as they play a crucial role in managing the funds vital for a successful campaign. A finance consultant can fulfill this role on a more limited scale for smaller campaigns, such as a run for state legislative or local office.


### **FIELD DIRECTOR**


A **field** **director** manages voter contact, volunteer recruitment, and your GOTV campaign. They are responsible for managing volunteers and field organizers, depending on the size of your campaign.


Hiring a field director is typically necessary for mid-level to larger congressional, statewide, and state legislative races.


### **TREASURER**


A[treasurer](https://www.thecampaignworkshop.com/blog/campaign-staff/campaign-job-descriptions) is mainly responsible for ensuring your campaign complies with campaign finance laws.[The Campaign Workshop](https://www.thecampaignworkshop.com/) recommends hiring a lawyer for this position to be sure they can effectively interpret election law.


### **POLITICAL DIRECTOR**


A **political** **director** works with various constituency groups to arrange their involvement with the campaign. For example, if you’re seeking endorsement from veterans or college students, a political director can be instrumental in engaging them.


Hiring a political director is wise for large city, statewide, or primary campaigns.


### **COMMUNICATIONS DIRECTOR**


A **communications** **director** is the bridge between your campaign team and the media. Their duties span a range of tasks, such as managing campaign messaging, arranging media appearances and interviews, and crafting press releases and candidate speeches.


A communications director is generally necessary for higher-level races, including those for the US Senate, governorships, or major mayorals.
