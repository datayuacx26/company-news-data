# 5 Enterprise AI Agent Use Cases That Work: StackAI's Bernard Aceituno for Forbes

Bernard Aceituno, cofounder of StackAI, was featured in[Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/councils/forbestechcouncil/2026/07/31/where-ai-agents-are-actually-working-five-use-cases-across-industries/) to answer a question circulating in the enterprise AI world: which agent use cases are actually working inside regulated organizations right now?


Drawing on three years embedded in enterprise AI deployments across financial services, legal, healthcare, and more, Aceituno observes that the highest-value agents tend to share three traits: they're event-triggered, they translate a real-world process directly into software, and they integrate tightly with the systems where work already lives. He organizes five proven use cases by industry.


In private capital, a triggered workflow handles the top of the funnel: an inbound deal email is classified by sector and stage, attachments are parsed, a financial summary is extracted and screened against the firm's criteria, and a preliminary memo is drafted into the CRM, so associates open their morning queue to find the initial work already done. For law firms and legal teams, a contract landing in a shared folder moves through agents that extract key clauses, compare them against the firm's playbook and precedent, and draft a redline summary routed to a reviewing attorney, ensuring nothing is sent without approval. In healthcare, a human-in-the-loop system for prior authorization assembles clinical evidence from the EHR, retrieves payer criteria, drafts the authorization letter, and routes it to a nurse case manager who approves, edits, or rejects in seconds.


In insurance, a triggered workflow integrated with the policy admin system classifies each broker submission, parses attachments, extracts and validates structured risk data, and drafts an acknowledgment, replacing the first hour of manual data entry. In banking, an agentic workflow built for internal audit reads a program, determines which fields to check, and runs specialist sub-agents that extract facts and place tick marks on source documents, turning hours of per-file review into a first cut in minutes with far broader coverage.


Aceituno closes with three takeaways: nearly all high-value agents are triggered rather than chat-based, each use case blends deterministic actions with LLM reasoning and human-in-the-loop steps for business-critical moments, and integrations matter because the most useful agents can reach every system needed to close the loop. His advice to CIOs is to ignore the noise and study the patterns that work in reality.
