# Lessons from Enterprise AI Deployments: StackAI's Hakan Gureren in VKTR

StackAI's Hakan Gureren was featured in[VKTR](https://www.vktr.com/) to distill what he learned from 34 enterprise AI engagements over the past year, laying out the six mistakes that most often stall AI programs before they reach production.


Drawing on anonymized composites from real deployments, Gureren argues that the CIOs who succeed with enterprise AI are the ones who know what to avoid. He walks through six recurring failure patterns.


The first is the builder-champion mismatch: the executive who signs a contract is rarely the person who does the work, so programs handed off to an already-busy director tend to starve. His rule is that if the sponsor can't name the three people who will build the first three workflows, don't sign. The second is the enterprise-wide kickoff. He contrasts a construction firm that rolled out an HR chatbot to 2,000 people on day one (adoption collapsed within a week) against a mid-market bank that gave access to just 14 people in one department, targeting tasks everyone already hated. The narrow rollout spread organically, driven by envy rather than mandate.


The third mistake is the 50-use-case master plan. Teams that try to scope dozens of ROI-ranked use cases at once end up with nothing shipped; the winners pick three that are narrow, structured, and ideally already exist as a manual process, then ship them in 90 days. Fourth is the duplicate agent problem, illustrated by an engineering firm that built the same invoice-processing agent four times. His fix is a central, searchable registry of agents, set up before an organization has ten of them. Fifth, the island problem, covers platforms that connect to nothing teams actually use; every custom integration adds weeks, so integration depth should be evaluated before committing. Sixth is the fear factor, where anxiety over data incidents keeps people working manually. Organizations that break through choose platforms with governance built in: role-based permissions, full audit trails, guardrails, and human approval steps that replace fear with trust.


Gureren closes with the consistent pattern among winners: a named executive sponsor who uses the platform, identified builders before signing, a single department as a beachhead chosen for pain and enthusiasm, three achievable use cases in 90 days, a central agent registry from day one, and a platform selected for deep integration and strong governance.
