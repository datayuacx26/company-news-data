# Patience, Passion, and Purpose: The Superpowers That Shaped My Online Teaching Journey


Discover how patience, passion, purpose, and communication shaped Teacher Vierre’s journey to teach English online and connect with global learners.


What does it take to make a difference when you teach English online?


For Teacher Vierre, the answer was not something he discovered in a single online lesson. It was something he learned over nearly a decade of meeting learners with different personalities, challenges, cultures, and dreams.


Some learners struggled to pronounce their first English sentences. Some needed confidence before they could express themselves. Others tested his ability to remain calm, understanding, and encouraging.


Through all of them, Teacher Vierre discovered three superpowers that would shape the teacher he became: **patience, passion, and purpose.**


And connecting all three was perhaps the greatest superpower of all: **communication.**


## Patience: Seeing Progress Before It Happens


Some lessons end with an obvious breakthrough. Others ask a teacher to believe in progress long before it can be seen.


Teacher Vierre once taught a young learner who was just beginning to find confidence in English. Words were difficult to pronounce. Building sentences was even harder. Sometimes, getting through a single word took several tries.


But Teacher Vierre kept trying with the learner.


When one explanation did not work, he found another way. A picture became a clue. A gesture through Total Physical Response (TPR) helped bring a word to life. Difficult words were broken into smaller syllables. Sentences were built piece by piece until the learner could begin expressing ideas more clearly.


There was no single lesson when everything suddenly changed.


Instead, the breakthroughs came quietly.


A word that once took several attempts became easier to say. A sentence needed less guidance to complete. An answer came with a little more confidence than the lesson before.


Each small win gave Teacher Vierre something to build on.


For nearly a year, they continued this rhythm—teach, try, encourage, and try again. What initially seemed like slow progress was becoming something much more meaningful: a young learner gradually gaining the confidence to speak up and use English independently.


For Teacher Vierre, patience became more than waiting for improvement.


It meant recognizing potential before the results were obvious—and continuing to guide a learner until that potential began to show.


Then, almost quietly, those small wins began to add up.


The words that once needed to be broken down became easier to say. Sentences that once required constant guidance began to flow more naturally. Questions no longer had to be repeated as often. Over time, the learner was not simply responding—he was expressing himself with clearer pronunciation, stronger grammar, and growing confidence.


For Teacher Vierre, this was the breakthrough he had been working toward all along.


It also changed the way he looked at teaching.


*Sometimes, a teacher has to believe in progress before it becomes visible.*


Patience was not simply giving a learner more time. It was continuing to find ways to connect, explain, encourage, and communicate until the learner was ready to take the next step.


That experience stayed with Teacher Vierre. Whenever he later encountered a learner who was struggling, he remembered what consistent guidance could eventually unlock.


Instead of focusing on what a learner could not do yet, he began asking a different question:


**What could this learner be capable of next?**


## Passion: Finding a Way to Connect


Not every learner enters an online lesson ready to participate.


Teacher Vierre once met a learner who already had a good foundation in English but showed little interest in the lesson. Getting him to participate was difficult. At times, even keeping his attention felt like a challenge.


Teacher Vierre could have simply worked through the material and ended the lesson.


Instead, he tried to understand what was behind the resistance.


He acknowledged something important: the learner *could* speak English. There was ability there. The challenge was finding a reason for him to engage.


So Teacher Vierre changed his approach.


Rather than pushing through every part of the lesson, he started a conversation. He encouraged the learner to talk about what interested him and used those interests as a way back into learning.


Nothing changed overnight.


But one lesson, the learner read a little more. In another, he participated without as much prompting. Slowly, the resistance began to fade. The learner who once seemed determined to disengage was beginning to meet Teacher Vierre halfway.


That experience gave Teacher Vierre a different way of looking at challenging moments in the classroom.


Behind a learner who seems uninterested may be someone who simply has not found a reason to connect yet.


And that is where passion matters most.


*Passion is not only the energy a teacher brings when a lesson is going well. Sometimes, it is the willingness to keep searching for a way in when connecting with a learner feels difficult.*


For Teacher Vierre, that meant looking beyond the behavior, seeing the learner behind it, and finding the right words to open a door that once seemed closed.


## Purpose: Realizing That Teaching Changes the Teacher, Too


Teacher Vierre had already been an educator for decades before joining 51Talk.


But teaching English online introduced him to something new.


The learners were not the only ones growing.


He was growing with them.


Every learner challenged him to become more patient, adaptable, professional, and intentional with his communication. Even the short intervals between online lessons taught him to manage his time more effectively and prepare himself for the next learner.


More importantly, he began to see teaching differently.


“Teaching the 51Talk way did not only give me a profession but a vocation,” Teacher Vierre shared.


That realization became his third superpower: **purpose.**


Teaching English online was no longer simply about helping someone complete a lesson.


It was about understanding what that lesson could eventually make possible for the learner.


## When Purpose Goes Beyond the Online Classroom


There comes a point in teaching when a lesson becomes more than something that happens on a screen.


Teacher Vierre experienced this through a learner who knew what she wanted to say but often struggled to express it in English. Finding the right words took time, sentences needed guidance, and speaking confidently was still something she was learning to do.


Teacher Vierre stayed with her through the process. He listened carefully, helped her organize her thoughts, and gave her the space to find the words for herself. As their lessons continued, their conversations began to change. The hesitation became less noticeable, sentences came more naturally, and the learner gradually became more confident expressing herself.


Then Teacher Vierre learned that while traveling, she had found herself in situations where she needed to communicate with the people around her—and she was able to use English.


For Teacher Vierre, that moment gave new meaning to all the lessons that had come before it. The sentences they had practiced were no longer exercises on a screen. They had become part of real conversations and real experiences.


Every repeated word, corrected sentence, encouraging reminder, and small breakthrough had been building toward something bigger. He was not simply helping someone improve in English; he was helping a learner develop the confidence to use it when it mattered.


## Imagine What One Lesson Can Start


Nearly a decade into his journey with 51Talk, Teacher Vierre has conducted thousands of online lessons. Yet the meaning of those years cannot be captured by a number.


It lives in the learner who finally pronounced a word that once seemed impossible, the quiet learner who eventually found the confidence to speak without being prompted, and the challenging lesson that changed because Teacher Vierre chose to understand the learner rather than simply move through the material.


Those experiences shaped Teacher Vierre just as much as he helped shape the learning experiences of others. Through them, he discovered that patience means continuing to believe in progress even when it is difficult to see. Passion means being willing to find another approach when the first one does not connect. Purpose comes from realizing that something practiced in an online lesson today may become something a learner confidently uses tomorrow.


Communication connects all of these lessons. It is not only about speaking clearly or correcting a sentence. It is about listening, understanding what a learner needs, and finding the right way to help that learner move forward.


That is also what makes teaching such a powerful journey. As learners develop their confidence, teachers continue developing their own skills, approaches, and understanding of what it means to make a difference.


Teacher Vierre could not have known where his first online lessons would eventually lead. Thousands of lessons later, it is the collection of small breakthroughs that tells the story—each one showing how an ordinary moment between a teacher and learner can grow into something much bigger.


## Your Teaching Story Could Start Here


Every teacher begins somewhere, and becoming a great teacher does not happen in a single lesson. It develops through experience: learning how to encourage someone who hesitates, finding creative ways to explain something difficult, discovering how different learners respond, and experiencing the satisfaction of watching confidence grow over time.


For teachers considering where their own journey could lead, Teacher Vierre’s story offers a glimpse of what is possible. Patience, passion, and the willingness to communicate can grow into something meaningful when they are used to help another person learn.


With every lesson comes an opportunity to help a learner find the confidence to **speak up, stand out, and succeed** . And somewhere along the way, teachers may discover just how much the experience helps them grow, too.
