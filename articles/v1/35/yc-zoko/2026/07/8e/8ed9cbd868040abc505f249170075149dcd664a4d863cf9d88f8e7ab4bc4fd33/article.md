## **What Is the WhatsApp Business API Policy?**


The WhatsApp Business API policy is Meta’s framework for how businesses can communicate with customers on WhatsApp. It defines when businesses can send messages, what types of messages are allowed, and how customer consent should be managed.


‍


### **It Defines Who Businesses Can Message**


Businesses cannot send WhatsApp messages to every phone number they collect. They need clear customer opt-in before sending business-initiated messages, especially for promotions, cart reminders, and campaign updates.


For Shopify merchants, this means consent should be collected through clear touchpoints such as checkout, sign-up forms, order pages, or WhatsApp subscription prompts.


‍


### **It Defines What Businesses Can Send**


The policy also outlines the types of messages businesses can send through WhatsApp. Messages should be relevant, accurate, and connected to a real customer action or need.

## **Key WhatsApp Business API Policy Areas Merchants Should Know**


‍


The WhatsApp Business API policy can feel complex at first, but most Shopify merchants should focus on a few core areas. These rules shape how businesses collect consent, send messages, and protect customer trust.


‍


### **Merchants Need Clear Customer Consent Before Messaging**


Shopify merchants should collect clear consent before sending business-initiated WhatsApp messages. This is especially important for promotional broadcasts, cart recovery messages, and campaign updates.


Common opt-in points include checkout pages, sign-up forms, order confirmation pages, and WhatsApp subscription prompts. Customers should also have a simple way to stop receiving non-essential messages.


‍


### **Merchants Need Approved Templates for Certain Messages**


Many business-initiated WhatsApp messages need approved templates before they can be sent. These templates should be clear, accurate, and tied to a real customer action or business purpose.


For example, an abandoned cart reminder should not read like an unrelated offer blast. Order updates, delivery alerts, and COD confirmations should clearly explain what the customer needs to know.


‍


### **Merchants Need to Respect the 24-Hour Service Window**


When a customer messages a business first, it opens a limited service window for replies. During this window, merchants can respond to the customer’s question or issue more naturally.


After that window closes, businesses usually need approved templates for business-initiated follow-ups. Shopify merchants should plan support, cart recovery, and delivery messages around this rule.


‍


### **Merchants Need to Match Each Message to the Right Category**


WhatsApp message categories help define the purpose of a business message. Marketing messages promote offers or campaigns, utility messages share order-related updates, and authentication messages support verification.


Shopify merchants should choose the category that matches the message purpose. Wrong categories can affect approval, customer experience, and message quality.


‍


### **Merchants Need to Handle Customer Data Responsibly**


WhatsApp conversations can include names, phone numbers, order IDs, delivery addresses, payment status, and support history. Shopify merchants should handle this information carefully across all workflows.


Responsible data handling supports trust and reduces risk. It also keeps WhatsApp useful for sales, support, COD updates, and post-purchase communication.

## **Common WhatsApp API Policy Mistakes to Avoid**


‍


Most WhatsApp policy mistakes happen when messages feel unclear, unwanted, or unrelated to the customer’s journey. Shopify merchants should avoid these issues before scaling campaigns or automation.


‍


### **Sending Promotions Without Clear Consent**


Promotional messages should only go to customers who have clearly agreed to receive them. This includes offer blasts, restock alerts, festival campaigns, and product launch updates.


Without clear consent, even a strong offer can feel intrusive. It can also weaken customer trust and increase reporting risk.


‍


### **Using the Wrong Template Category**


Order updates, promotions, and authentication messages serve different purposes. Shopify merchants should choose the template category that matches the message intent.


For example, a delivery update should not be written like a sales campaign. Clear categories help protect message quality and customer experience.


‍


### **Treating WhatsApp Like a Bulk SMS Channel**


WhatsApp works best when messages feel personal, timely, and connected to customer actions. Sending the same campaign to every customer can quickly create fatigue.


Instead, merchants should segment messages by product interest, order stage, or purchase history. This keeps WhatsApp useful for engagement and repeat sales.


‍


### **Ignoring Opt-Outs and Customer Preferences**


Customers should have a simple way to stop receiving promotional WhatsApp messages. Ignoring preferences can damage trust and make future communication harder.


A safer approach is to honor opt-outs quickly and keep essential order messages separate from marketing updates.
