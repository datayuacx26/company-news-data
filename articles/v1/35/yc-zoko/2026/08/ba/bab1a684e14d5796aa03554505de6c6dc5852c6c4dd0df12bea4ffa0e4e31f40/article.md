Unlike the standard WhatsApp Business app, the platform supports automation, software integrations, approved outbound messages, customer data triggers, and team-based communication.

## **1. Product Catalogs and Interactive Commerce Messages**


WhatsApp Catalog allows customers to explore products without moving immediately to another channel.


Merchants can send:


- Full catalog messages
- Single-product messages
- Multi-product messages
- Catalog links
- Interactive product carousels
- Product images, descriptions, and prices


Multi-product messages can display up to 30 products organized into separate sections. Interactive carousels present horizontally scrollable product cards within the conversation.


A beauty store could share a catalog section containing moisturizers after a customer asks for dry-skin recommendations.


‍


## **2. Shopify and E-commerce Platform Integration**


Store integration connects WhatsApp conversations with current customer, product, cart, and order information.


A strong integration can access:


- Product availability
- Customer profiles
- Shopping activity
- Abandoned checkouts
- Order details
- Payment status
- Fulfillment updates
- Previous purchases


This data helps businesses send messages based on real customer actions instead of generic campaign schedules.


For example, a Shopify event can trigger a WhatsApp message after a customer abandons checkout or places a COD order.


‍


## **3. Automated Customer Journeys**


Automated workflows turn repeated customer interactions into structured processes.


E-commerce merchants can automate:


- Product recommendation journeys
- Frequently asked questions
- Abandoned checkout reminders
- COD confirmations
- Payment follow-ups
- Shipping notifications
- Review requests
- Reorder reminders
- Customer feedback collection


WhatsApp Flows can also create structured in-chat experiences for forms, selections, and guided customer actions.


The customer receives a clear next step while the merchant reduces repeated manual work.


‍


## **4. Personalized Message Templates**


Businesses need approved templates when starting conversations outside the open customer service window.


Meta separates templates into three categories:


- **Marketing:** Offers, product launches, cart reminders, and re-engagement campaigns
- **Utility:** Order confirmations, shipping updates, invoices, and payment notifications
- **Authentication:** OTPs, verification codes, and account-access messages


Templates can include customer names, order numbers, delivery dates, images, documents, quick replies, and action buttons.


Personalization should use relevant store data rather than adding a customer’s name to every message.


‍


## **5. Targeted WhatsApp Broadcasts**


WhatsApp broadcasts help merchants communicate with opted-in customer groups at scale.


Brands can create separate audiences based on:


- Purchase history
- Product interest
- Customer location
- Average order value
- Previous campaign engagement
- Loyalty status
- Abandoned carts
- Time since the last purchase


A clothing store could send a restock alert only to shoppers who viewed or previously purchased that product category.


Meta may limit how many marketing messages individual users receive. Sending relevant campaigns protects message quality and reduces blocks or opt-outs.


‍


## **6. Abandoned Cart Recovery**


Cart recovery connects Shopify checkout activity with timely WhatsApp reminders.


A complete recovery workflow may:


1. Detect an abandoned checkout
2. Wait for a selected period
3. Send an approved reminder
4. Display the abandoned products
5. Answer purchase questions
6. Share the checkout link
7. Stop reminders after purchase


Cart reminders can also address common objections around delivery, sizing, returns, COD, or product suitability.


Since around 70% of online carts are abandoned, even a focused recovery workflow can address a major conversion gap.

## **7. Order, COD, Payment, and Shipping Automation**


Transactional messages keep customers informed after checkout and reduce repetitive support questions.


Merchants can automate:


- Order confirmations
- COD verification
- COD-to-prepaid requests
- Payment links

## **8. AI Chatbots With Human Handover**


AI chatbots can answer common product, order, delivery, payment, and return questions in real time.


A commerce-focused chatbot can:


- Recommend relevant products


- Answer product questions


- Check order information


- Explain delivery policies


- Handle common FAQs


- Identify buying intent


- Collect required details


- Transfer complex cases to an agent


The bot should receive current catalog, policy, and store information. It should never invent availability, delivery dates, discounts, or refund decisions.


Human handover remains essential for complaints, unusual payment issues, high-value purchases, and sensitive customer situations.

A shared inbox allows sales and support teams to manage one WhatsApp number together.


Useful inbox features include:


- Automatic chat assignment


- Team and agent routing


- Internal notes


- Conversation labels


- Customer history


- Priority queues


- Saved replies


- Agent performance reports


- Bot-to-agent handover


Customer and Shopify data inside the inbox helps agents understand the conversation without asking shoppers to repeat every detail.

## **10. Payments, Webhooks, and Performance Tracking**


WhatsApp payment availability depends on the merchant’s country, provider, and payment partner. Where supported, businesses can share payment requests or approved payment links within the buying conversation.


Webhooks connect WhatsApp with real-time customer events. They can record:


- Incoming replies


- Sent and delivered messages


- Read receipts


- Button selections


- Payment events


- Order updates


- Workflow completions


Merchants should track more than delivery and read rates. The most useful metrics include completed purchases, recovered carts, support resolution, opt-outs, payment completion, and revenue per campaign.

## **What Changed for WhatsApp E-commerce in 2026?**


‍


Several updates affect how merchants plan their WhatsApp strategy in 2026.


‍


### **WhatsApp App and API Coexistence**


Eligible businesses can use the WhatsApp Business app and the Business Platform with the same phone number. This allows smaller teams to retain familiar app features while adding automation and platform integrations.


‍


### **Per-Message Pricing**


Meta replaced its older conversation-based structure with per-message pricing for template messages in July 2025.


Starting[October 1, 2026](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/pricing/non-template-messages) , Meta plans to charge for service messages and utility templates sent within an open 24-hour customer service window. Merchants should review support volume and automation costs before this change takes effect.


‍


### **More Interactive Shopping Formats**


Catalog templates, multi-product messages, carousels, Flows, and payment connections are moving WhatsApp beyond basic notifications.


The strongest e-commerce setups now connect product discovery, customer questions, checkout, fulfillment, and retention inside one coordinated journey.

## **Common WhatsApp Business API E-commerce Mistakes**


‍


WhatsApp automation works best when messages reflect real customer actions. Poor setup can create irrelevant campaigns, repeated notifications, and frustrating support experiences.


Avoid these common mistakes:


- Messaging customers without clear opt-in
- Sending the same broadcast to every contact
- Mixing promotional content into utility messages
- Continuing cart reminders after checkout
- Using outdated inventory or order information
- Automating complaints without human support
- Measuring message opens without tracking sales
- Ignoring Meta’s pricing and policy changes
- Sending too many marketing templates
- Choosing a provider without strong store integration
