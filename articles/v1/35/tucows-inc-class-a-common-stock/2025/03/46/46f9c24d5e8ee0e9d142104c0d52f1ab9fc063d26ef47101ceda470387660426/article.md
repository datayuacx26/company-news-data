# Quick stress relief strategies for the workplace

# Quick stress relief strategies for the workplace


## 5–10 minute resets that work!


[Kaleigh Post](https://medium.com/@kpost_33504?source=post_page---byline--a90e3635be71---------------------------------------)


5 min read


·


Mar 31, 2025


--


Whether it’s deadlines piling up, back-to-back meetings, or unexpected challenges, stress can sneak up on us quickly. While a little bit of stress can be motivating, too much can leave us feeling overwhelmed, exhausted, and less productive. The good news? You don’t need a whole day at the spa or a long vacation to reset. Sometimes, just 5–10 minutes is enough to calm your nervous system and bring you back to a state of balance.


In honour of Stress Awareness Month, we’re sharing science-backed, quick stress relief strategies you can try right at your desk (or wherever work takes you).


*Disclaimer: If stress is significantly impacting your daily life, it may be helpful to seek support from a professional.*

## 1. Breathe


**Why it works:** Deep breathing helps activate the parasympathetic nervous system, which counteracts the fight-or-flight response. Research shows that slow, deep breathing can lower heart rate, blood pressure, and cortisol levels (the stress hormone).


**Try this:** The 4–7–8 breathing technique


- Inhale deeply through your nose for 4 seconds.
- Hold your breath for 7 seconds.
- Exhale slowly through your mouth for 8 seconds.
- Repeat 3–4 times.


This simple exercise sends a signal to your brain that it’s time to relax, helping you regain focus and composure.


## 2. Take a short walk


**Why it works:** Physical movement releases endorphins, the body’s natural mood boosters, and helps reduce cortisol levels. Walking also improves circulation, which can help you think more clearly.


**Try this:**


- Walk around your office, home, or outside for 5–10 minutes.
- If you can’t leave your desk, try standing up, stretching, and walking in place.


Even a brief walk can refresh your mind and body, making it easier to tackle the next task with renewed energy.


## 3. Try progressive muscle relaxation (PMR)


**Why it works:** PMR involves tensing and then relaxing different muscle groups, which can release physical tension and promote relaxation. Studies have shown that PMR helps reduce anxiety and improve overall well-being.


**Try this:**


- Start with your feet — tense your muscles for 5 seconds, then release.
- Move up to your calves, thighs, abdomen, arms, shoulders, and face.
- As you release tension, breathe deeply and notice the sensation of relaxation.


This technique helps you physically let go of stress, especially if you’ve been sitting at a desk all day.


## 4. Listen to music or white noise


**Why it works:** Music has been shown to lower stress levels and boost mood. Certain types of music, especially classical, lo-fi, or nature sounds, can have a calming effect on the brain.


**Try this:**


- Put on a playlist of soothing or uplifting music.
- Try white noise, rainfall, or ocean waves for a mental escape.
- Listen with headphones if you need a quick, private moment of relaxation.


Music can quickly shift your mindset, helping you feel calmer and more in control.


## 5. Engage your senses


**Why it works:** Engaging the senses can ground you in the present moment, reducing anxious thoughts. Aromatherapy, in particular, has been linked to reduced stress and increased relaxation.


**Try this:**


- Keep a small bottle of essential oil (like lavender, peppermint, or eucalyptus) at your desk. When you need a break, take a deep inhale.
- Run your hands under warm or cool water for a sensory reset.
- Chew gum or sip herbal tea to engage taste and smell.


Simple sensory experiences can bring you back to the present moment and help shift your focus away from stress.


## 6. Do a quick mindfulness exercise


**Why it works:** Mindfulness helps break the cycle of stress by encouraging present-moment awareness. Research shows that practicing mindfulness can lower stress, improve focus, and enhance overall emotional resilience.

**Try this:** The 5–4–3–2–1 Grounding Exercise


- Name 5 things you can see.
- Name 4 things you can touch.
- Name 3 things you can hear.
- Name 2 things you can smell.
- Name 1 thing you can taste.


This simple exercise helps redirect your thoughts and calm an overwhelmed mind.


## 7. Laugh it off


**Why it works:** Laughter reduces stress hormones and increases endorphins, improving mood almost instantly. It also helps shift perspective, making stressful situations seem more manageable.


**Try this:**


- Watch a short funny video.
- Read a few jokes or a comic strip.
- Chat with a co-worker who always makes you laugh.


A few moments of laughter can lighten your mood and help you reset for the rest of the day.


## 8. Stretch it out


**Why it works:** Stress often manifests physically as muscle tension, particularly in the shoulders, neck, and back. Stretching can relieve this tension and increase blood flow to the brain, improving focus.


**Try this:**


- Roll your shoulders backward and forward.
- Stretch your arms overhead and lean side to side.
- Tilt your head gently from side to side to release neck tension.


Taking just a few moments to stretch can help reduce physical discomfort and improve mental clarity.


## 9. Write it down


**Why it works:** Writing about stressors can help process emotions and clear mental clutter. Research suggests that journaling can reduce anxiety and improve problem-solving skills.


**Try this:**


- Take 5 minutes to jot down what’s stressing you out.
- List three things you’re grateful for to shift your perspective.
- Write down one small action you can take to feel more in control.


Writing can help externalize stress and make it feel more manageable.


## Final thoughts: Small actions, big impact


Stress is a part of life, but it doesn’t have to control your day. These quick, science-backed strategies can help you reset and regain control, even when work feels overwhelming. The next time you feel stress creeping in, try one (or a combination) of these techniques to bring yourself back to balance.


Remember, taking small breaks isn’t just about relaxation — it’s about improving your overall well-being and productivity. The more you integrate these quick resets into your routine, the more resilient you’ll become to stress over time.


And, of course, if stress becomes too much to handle alone, seeking support from a professional is always a smart step toward well-being.
