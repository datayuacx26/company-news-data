# How to report phishing and domain-related scams

# **How to report phishing and domain-related scams**


## Protect yourself and others

We’ve all seen them: suspicious emails, strange text messages, or pop-up ads promising deals that sound too good to be true. Sometimes, it’s obvious that something isn’t right. But what happens when you’re not sure? Maybe you’ve received an email or a text where someone tries to trick you with a fake website or message that looks just like the real thing — **that’s phishing** !


So, what can you do about it?


The good news is that there is more you can do than just ignore or delete these messages. You can take action by reporting phishing and domain-related scams to the appropriate domain registrar or other provider. This helps protect not only yourself but also others who might fall victim to these online threats.

## What is phishing and how does it work?


Phishing is a type of online scam where bad actors try to trick you into giving away personal information and login details, such as usernames, passwords, or financial details. They do this by pretending to be someone you should trust, like your bank or a popular website. Phishing attempts come in the form of emails, text messages, or fake websites that look nearly identical to the real ones.


You might get an email that says you’ve won a prize, need to reset your password, or have to update your account information urgently. If you click on the links or provide any personal information, the scammers can use that to steal from you or even commit fraud.


Phishing attempts often rely on convincing you that it’s an emergency. Watch out for requests to send money (or gift cards) right away or other messages with a strong sense of urgency, as this is a strong sign of a scam.

## What can you do about phishing and scams?


If you receive a suspicious message or see something that looks like a scam, don’t just delete it and move on. You can help prevent further attacks by reporting it to the appropriate authority — like the domain registrar.


Here’s how you can report phishing and other domain-related scams:


**Identify the domain registrar**


Every website is associated with a domain registrar. This is the company responsible for managing the domain name where the website lives. If you see a suspicious website, you can do a Whois lookup to find out which registrar manages the domain. Not sure how to perform the lookup?[Click here](https://tucowsdomains.com/whois-search/) **to get started!**


**Check the contact information**


Once you’ve found out which registrar provides the domain, find their abuse or support contact by visiting their website — or look through the **Whois data** to find the abuse contact email. Most registrars have a form for reporting phishing.


**Provide details of the scam**


Always include key details when submitting a report. This includes a link to the suspicious email or website (be careful not to visit it yourself!), the exact message you received, and any other information that might help the registrar investigate, like the phone number or email address it came from.


**Act quickly — but be careful**

The faster you report a phishing attempt, the quicker the registrar can take action. They can often suspend or take down the fraudulent domain to prevent others from falling victim to the same scam.


## Why is reporting important?


Reporting phishing and other scams is crucial for several reasons:


- **Protecting others** : The scam you encounter could trick someone else if it’s not taken down quickly. By reporting it, you help prevent further damage.
- **Helping investigate** : When registrars receive reports, they can investigate the situation, track down the perpetrators, and take the appropriate actions to stop them.
- **Building a safer internet** : The more people who report scams, the easier it becomes for companies and organizations to identify and remove malicious activity online.


## What happens after you report a scam?


Once you report a phishing attempt or domain-related scam to the registrar, they will typically begin an investigation. The registrar can take a few different actions depending on the severity of the scam:


- **Suspending the domain** : If a domain is confirmed to be used for phishing or fraud, the registrar can suspend it, preventing others from accessing the fake website.
- **Working with law enforcement** : The registrar may work with law enforcement or cybercrime agencies to help catch the people behind the scam.
- **Notifying the affected parties** : The registrar might notify other businesses or individuals who are at risk because of the fraudulent domain.


## Be proactive: Protect yourself from phishing


While reporting phishing and scams is important, it’s also essential to be proactive about protecting yourself. Here are a few tips to help you spot phishing attempts before they can harm you:


- **Look for suspicious links** : Hover over links in emails or text messages to see where they actually go. If the URL looks strange or doesn’t match the official website, **don’t click it** .
- **Verify before acting** : If you get an urgent message asking for personal information or money, verify it with the company directly. Call them or visit their official website to check if the request is legitimate.
- **Use Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA)** : This adds an extra layer of security to your online accounts. Even if someone gets your password, they’ll need a second form of identification (like an app notification) to access your account.


## Conclusion: Take action to stop scammers


Phishing and online scams can happen to anyone but by reporting suspicious activities to the appropriate registrar, you help keep the Internet safer for everyone. It only takes a few minutes to report a scam and it could save someone else from falling victim to fraud. So, next time you receive a phishing attempt, don’t just delete it — **take action and report it** !


It’s a simple step that can make a big difference and can help us on our mission of **Making the Internet better.**
