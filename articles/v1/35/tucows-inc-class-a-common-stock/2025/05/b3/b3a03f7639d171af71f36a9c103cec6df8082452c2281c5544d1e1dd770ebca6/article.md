Have you ever wished you had a personal assistant who never sleeps, always remembers your preferences (mostly), and can whip up a slide deck, summarize a white paper, or dig through data without breaking a sweat? Welcome to the world of AI agents powered by large language models (LLMs) — and yes, you can build one yourself.


This might sound like science fiction, but it’s not. In the first edition of *Graze and Grow,* Tucows’ internal knowledge sharing “show-and-tell”, Ashley La Bolle of Tucows Domains walked us through how anyone — even those without a tech background — can start creating their own LLM agents using tools like ChatGPT. The session was packed with practical advice, gentle warnings, and lots of encouragement.


### 🧱 The basics: What’s an LLM agent, anyway?


At its core, an LLM agent is a customized AI assistant. It runs on a large language model like ChatGPT, and you can train it to do very specific tasks by giving it instructions, examples, and even files to reference. Think of it like hiring a new employee — you onboard it, teach it the ropes, and then start assigning it jobs.


Unlike a one-off chat with a bot, an agent can remember what it’s supposed to do, meaning it gives you consistent results over time (no need to repeat yourself). That’s key if you’re integrating it into your workflow.


### 🔧 Getting stared: How to build one


Here’s Ashley’s five-step blueprint:


1. **Define a purpose** : Tell your agent who it is and what it’s for. Want a design assistant? A data analyst? A writing coach? Be specific.
2. **Set it up** : Use ChatGPT’s “Custom GPT” interface to configure the agent’s settings. Add detailed instructions, provide templates or examples, and upload supporting documents.
3. **Refine and test** : Play around. Tweak your instructions, run tests, and see how your agent responds. This is where the magic (and frustration) happens.
4. **Integrate into your work** : Once the agent consistently delivers the results you want, start using it in your day-to-day tasks.
5. **Iterate** : You’ll likely find things you want to improve. The best agents evolve over time.


**Bonus tip:** Keep a backup of your instructions and files somewhere else. ChatGPT doesn’t offer version control yet, so this will save you if something breaks.


### 🧠 What they’re good at (and not so good at)


LLMs are surprisingly skilled at generating text, summarizing information, brainstorming, analyzing patterns, and even designing basic visuals. But they’re not perfect. They can:


- **Hallucinate** (make stuff up confidently)
- **Struggle with complex logic or math**
- **Be biased based on their training data**
- **Fail with real-time or very recent info**


Ashley’s advice? Always be the human in the loop. Don’t blindly trust outputs. Review, fact-check, and refine.


### 🔐 One big consideration: Data privacy


If you’re thinking about building an agent for work, tread carefully. Make sure the tools you’re using are approved by your company. Don’t upload sensitive or confidential data unless you’re certain it’s secure and compliant with internal policies.


### 🤝 Sharing is caring


One of the best parts of our first Graze and Grow session was the emphasis on community. Tucows has an internal Slack channel where employees swap tips, get feedback, and share their agents. It’s a simple way to accelerate collective learning — and avoid reinventing the wheel.
