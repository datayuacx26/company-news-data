# What Is Campaign Analytics? Metrics, Tips and Tools

**Key Takeaways:**


- Campaign analytics captures performance data from paid and organic channels, giving you a structured view of what is driving results and what is not.
- Each channel, including email, social media, paid ads, and organic search, has its own core metrics. Knowing which ones to prioritize cuts through the noise.
- When campaigns run across multiple channels without a unified data layer, attribution gaps form and skew your results.
- The right campaign analytics setup helps your team move from manual reporting to faster, evidence-based decisions.


Most marketing teams aren't short on campaign data. They're drowning in it, and still can't say which campaign actually made money. Campaign analytics fixes that. It captures performance signals like clicks, conversions, spend, and reach from every channel, then structures them so you can see what worked, what flopped, and where the next dollar should go.


The catch is that the numbers only matter if you can connect them. This guide breaks down the metrics that count by campaign type, the tools that pull multi-channel data into one view, and how to read past the vanity numbers to the ones tied to revenue.

##


What is campaign analytics?


Campaign analytics is the system that captures campaign data from one or multiple channels and structures it for analysis and attribution. It measures performance signals — reach, engagement, conversions, spend — and ties them to outcomes so you can judge what worked.


Metrics are its building blocks, which is why analytics is often pictured as a dashboard of metrics and attributions. Campaign analysis is the next step: it digs into how your efforts measure up against your objectives and KPIs.[Competitive benchmarking](https://www.sprinklr.com/blog/competitive-benchmarking/) then adds outside perspective, comparing your results to the wider market so you can spot gaps and prioritize improvements.


### Where to find campaign analytics


Your marketing and advertising platforms come with native campaign analytics. For example, **Meta Ads Manager** is the native analytics for ad campaigns run on Meta-owned channels like Facebook, Instagram, WhatsApp, and Messenger. Other platforms like Google and LinkedIn have similar built-in capabilities.


However, campaign analytics native to a particular platform is always limited to insights for that specific channel where the data originates. You may have multiple campaigns running on different channels.


In such cases, you need a[campaign analytics platform](https://www.sprinklr.com/products/marketing-and-advertising/marketing-analytics/) to pull data from different campaigns and present them in dashboards for reporting, governance, and insights.

##


Difference between campaign analytics and campaign analysis


Given the homophonic nature of the two phrases, it’s easy to miss out on the important difference between campaign analytics and analysis. Here’s a quick overview:


**Aspects**


**Campaign analytics**


**Campaign analysis**


**Definition**


The measurement and collection of data related to a campaign's performance.


The interpretation and evaluation of that campaign data.


**Function**


Answers the " **what** " of the campaigns through raw numbers, metrics, and data points gathered from various platforms.


Inquires and answers the " **why** " and " **so what** " of the campaign by analyzing the trends, insights, successes and failures.


**Goal**


**Quantify** raw data into measurable units.


**Attribute** the data to tangible outcomes and inform future strategies.


**Examples**


Clicks, impressions, conversion rate,[cost per click](https://www.sprinklr.com/social-media-glossary/cost-per-click/) , and open rate.


Answers to specific questions behind the metrics, such as why clicks are more, but conversion rates are low.

Campaign analytics metrics by campaign type


Campaign analytics is an umbrella phrase for a collection of metrics captured and presented for your analysis.


It can differ depending on the campaign type and focus on aspects of the campaign’s performance, such as:


- **Research and awareness** (impressions, traffic,[brand mentions](https://www.sprinklr.com/social-media-glossary/brand-mention/) )
- **Engagement** (click-through rate, bounce rate)
- **Conversation** (conversion rate,[lead generation](https://www.sprinklr.com/blog/lead-generation/) , or ROAS)
- **Website/app** (user behavior flow, campaign landing page performance)
- **Cost and ROI** (campaign spend, cost per click)
- **Audience** (demographic, device types, interests)


In most cases you'll follow the metrics relevant to your campaign's focus and ignore the rest. Below are the most common campaign types and the metrics their analytics capture.


### 1. Paid ads or performance marketing (search, social, display)


Paid ads involve running campaigns on search engines like Google, social media channels like Instagram, video platforms like YouTube, and other online properties such as websites, apps, and online platforms.


Display ads and native ads are other subsets of paid ads.


**Paid campaign metrics**


**What they inform**


[Click-through rate (CTR)](https://www.sprinklr.com/social-media-glossary/click-through-rate/)


Percentage of people who clicked after seeing your ads


**Cost per click (CPC)**


Average price you pay for each potential customer who clicks on your ads


**Conversion rate**


Percentage of ad clicks that resulted in a desired business outcome


**Return on ad spend (ROAS)**


Revenue generated for every dollar you invest in your advertising campaigns


**Impression**


Number of times your ad was displayed to a potential customer


**Cost per acquisition**


Average cost to acquire a new customer through paid ads


**Lifetime value (LV)**


Total revenue a single customer is expected to generate throughout their relationship with your business


**Cost per impression (CPI) or**[Cost per thousand impression (CPM)](https://www.sprinklr.com/social-media-glossary/cpm/)


The cost you pay for every time or every thousand times your ad is displayed to potential customers


**Cost per lead**


How much it costs you to acquire a potential customer who has shown interest in your offering


**Cost per conversion**


The total cost incurred to achieve a specific business goal from your campaign


**Incremental ROAS (iROAS)**


Revenue the ad caused vs. sales that would have happened anyway — measured via holdout tests


**Marketing Efficiency Ratio (MER)**


Total revenue ÷ total spend — a blended check that catches when channel-level ROAS flatters but the business doesn't grow

### 2. Email marketing campaigns


Email is one of the most popular forms of marketing and sales campaigns used by all categories of businesses.


This involves sending emails with different calls to actions designed to drive specific business outcomes such as purchases, demo bookings, subscriptions, or simply brand awareness.


**Email campaign metrics**


**What they inform**


**Click-to-open rate (CTOR)**


Percentage of openers who clicked — a cleaner relevance signal than open rate


**Delivery rate**


Percentage of sent emails that successfully reached recipients' inboxes


**Bounce rate**


Percentage of sent emails that didn’t reach the intended recipient


**Open rate**


Percentage of delivered emails opened by recipients


**Reply rate**


Replies require intent, so mailbox providers treat them as a strong trust signal that lifts inbox placement


**Click-through rate**


Percentage of recipients who clicked on a link within your email


**Conversion rate**


Percentage of recipients who completed a desired action (e.g., purchase, sign-up) after clicking the email


**Unsubscribe rate**


Percentage of recipients who opted out of future emails


**List growth rate**


The rate at which your email list is expanding over time


**Inbox placement rate**


Whether your email reached a human inbox vs. spam/promotions


**Note:** Open rate is now a deliverability signal, not an engagement one. Apple Mail Privacy Protection now accounts for[~49%](https://www.geysera.com/blog/email-marketing/are-email-open-rates-dead-in-2026-what-apple-mpp-gmail-proxy-and-bot-opens-mean-for-your-data) of opens and inflates rates by 15-20 points, and only ~15% of marketers still treat open rate as a primary metric.

Goals could look like demand generation,[brand awareness](https://www.sprinklr.com/blog/brand-awareness/) , and even leads. You can distribute such content through organic or paid means.


**Content marketing metrics**


**What they inform**


**SERP ranking (by keyword)**


Where your website appears in search engine results for specific keywords


**Traffic**


Total number of visits your website receives from all sources


**SERP features**


Your visibility through rich results like featured snippets or local business listings in search results


**Unique visitors**


The number of distinct individuals who visited your website at a specific time


**Bounce rate**


Percentage of visitors who leave your website after viewing only one page


**Time on page**


Average duration visitors spend on a specific page of your website


**MQLs**


Leads from your organic marketing efforts that have a higher likelihood of becoming paying customers


**AI citations / mentions**


How often LLMs like ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Google AI Overviews cite or name your brand in answers

### 7. Post-campaign analytics


At this stage, your campaign is past the runtime. This is where you’ll measure the broader metrics of your campaign to gauge business impact and benchmark them against the pre-campaign metrics.


There are no blanket metrics to measure at this stage, and the ones you will measure will depend upon the campaign and business goals.


Here are some broad campaign metrics that you can analyze to gauge overall business impact:


**Post-campaign metrics**


**What they inform**


[Customer churn](https://www.sprinklr.com/blog/customer-churn/) **rate**


Percentage of customers lost


**Conversion rate**


Percentage of people who completed a desired action (sales, sign-up, free-trial, demo)


**Repeat purchase rate**


Percentage of existing customers who spent money on your business again


**Website traffic**


Number of people who landed on your digital properties during a campaign


**Cost Per Acquisition (CPA) or Cost Per Conversion**


Amount it costs to gain a desired outcome


**Return on Investment (ROI) or Return on Ad Spend (ROAS)**


Percentage of profit generated for every dollar invested in the entire campaign


**NPS**


A score that quantifies customer loyalty and likelihood of recommending your business


**Incremental lift (iROAS / incrementality testing)**


The revenue your campaign caused — measured by comparing an exposed group against a holdout control, versus sales that would have happened anyway

6 pro tips to consider before using campaign analytics


Here's what you should know before processing campaign analytics:


1. **Identify attribution before running campaigns** : Attribution is the least glamorous part of campaign analytics and the most valuable. Without it, spend disappears into a "black hole" of untracked, default-to-direct traffic, and you can't prove which campaigns drove revenue.
2. **Break data silos** : It’s easy to lose track of the tools you use for your campaigns and miss the fact that your teams are working in silos when it comes to analyzing the data. Try to bring campaign analytics under one dashboard using a[unified analytics platform](https://www.sprinklr.com/products/marketing-and-advertising/marketing-analytics/) so your teams can collaborate on holistic data.
3. **Learn your analytics jargon** : To be able to analyze the data from your campaigns, it’s important to familiarize yourself with the metrics your analytics will pull often. Identify the most used metrics and learn what they mean and how they impact your business.
4. **Report on a regular cadence:** Set up recurrent reporting cadence with your team or set up scheduled automated reports if your analytics platform supports. Keeping a regular tab on your campaigns can help you stay on top of trends and quickly pivot when there’s a requirement.
5. **Balance vanity and actionable metrics** : Metrics like impressions and clicks are easy to track, but they don't always translate to business value. Focus your analysis on metrics that demonstrate impact on the defined objectives.
6. **Benchmark against competitors:** Competitive benchmarking or comparing your key metrics (conversion rates, CPC, engagement) against industry norms or direct competitors reveals where you lead or lag. You can use this information to allocate resources and budgets to move the needle.


**💡 What's the difference between campaign analytics and marketing attribution?**


Campaign analytics measures what happened across a campaign; attribution assigns credit for the outcome to specific channels or touchpoints. Analytics tells you conversions rose; attribution tells you which touchpoints earned that lift so you can reallocate budget.
