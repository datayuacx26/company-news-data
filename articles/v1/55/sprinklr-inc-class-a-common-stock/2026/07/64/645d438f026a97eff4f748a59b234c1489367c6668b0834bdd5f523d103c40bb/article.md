##


So, how do you even score influence?


Good question. And the honest answer is: meticulously.


This year's ranking started with a universe of over 3,300 brands drawn from seven major global benchmarks and then narrowed to a competitive set of 419 CMOs and equivalent marketing leaders based on market presence, brand awareness, and confirmed leadership roles.


From there, the scoring looked at three dimensions of influence that together paint a pretty complete picture of how a CMO and their brand actually show up in the world:


1. The first – **Attention for Marketing Work,** captures how much attention a brand's marketing work earns in credible media and industry conversations.


2. The second – **CMO Attention, Sentiment & Salience** , looks at how visible and relevant the CMO themselves is as a leadership voice.


3. And the third – **Brand Awareness & Sentiment,** measures how the brand connects with its audience across engagement, sentiment, and reach.


None of these dimensions work in isolation. A great campaign means more when the CMO behind it has a strong public voice. A highly visible leader carries more weight when the brand's audience is actively engaged. The scoring reflects that interconnection, which is what makes the final list feel as credible as it does.


##


What 8.68 billion interactions tell you


We should talk about the scale for a second, because this is where the list starts to feel a little unreal. This year's rankings are powered by 8.68 billion social interactions tied to brand channels and over 6.19 million mentions of individual CMOs across news, blogs, forums, podcasts and social platforms.


That kind of volume matters because it lets you see things that smaller datasets simply miss. You can tell the difference between a campaign that performed well within its own channel and one that genuinely broke through into broader cultural and industry conversations. You can see which CMOs consistently show up in the discourse and which brands are quietly building momentum through sustained engagement rather than one-off spikes.


And when you layer this across industries, the patterns get really interesting.


i. Media and entertainment brands naturally generate massive conversation volumes, with median CMO mentions running into the thousands.


ii. Technology brands sustain elevated marketing attention through continuous product and platform innovation.


iii. Travel and hospitality brands tend to operate through fewer, more symbolic moments that punch above their weight across markets.


Same framework, completely different expressions of influence, which is exactly why the companion report matters. It gives you the deeper read on how Sprinklr's data helps compare influence fairly across industries, instead of flattening every brand into the same kind of score.
