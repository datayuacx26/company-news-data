# Why Some Brands Get Cited in AI Answers and Others Don't

In the SEO decade, the visibility game had ten slots. The buyer scrolled through a results page and chose. In the AI answer era, the visibility game has three or four slots. The AI engine chooses, and the buyer trusts the answer.


For brands inside those three or four citations, AI search is now the most valuable acquisition surface to emerge in a decade. For brands outside them, the surface does not exist, no matter how well their pages rank on Google.


*So, when an AI engine picks the handful of sources it cites, why would it pick yours?*


That is the central thesis of a new Sprinklr AI team paper accepted at SIGIR 2026 in Melbourne. **The team ran the largest controlled study of AI citation behavior published so far** , testing six leading AI engines on what makes one source more likely to be cited than another. The findings cut through a lot of the noise currently circulating in the GEO and AEO conversation, and several of them directly contradict the optimization advice marketing teams are currently being sold.

##


**The new visibility moment most marketing dashboards do not measure**


Buyers used to compare ten options on a search results page. Today they are asking ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, or Claude to compare three or four for them, and trusting the answer. By the time a prospect lands on your website, the AI has already framed your brand, summarized your strengths, and weighed you against competitors.


For a CMO, this changes three things at once:


- **The shortlist forms before the website visit.** If your brand is not cited in the AI answer, the visit does not happen. The funnel starts earlier and the top of it is narrower.
- **AI is a brand impression channel, not just a discovery one.** Every AI answer is also describing your brand, your sentiment, and your positioning, without your input.
- **The buyer's question is longer and more specific.** People do not type "CRM software" into ChatGPT. They ask "best CRM for a 200-person B2B sales team in healthcare." The questions driving AI visibility look nothing like the keywords driving SEO.


##


**Three things that actually decide which brand AI engines cite**


Sprinklr tested 18 different content qualities across six leading AI engines including the GPT-5 family, Gemini 2.5 Flash, Claude 3.5 Sonnet, and Kimi K2, to identify which ones consistently move the needle on citation.


##


**Three findings held across every model tested.**


**Specific beats comprehensive.** Tightly focused pages, the kind that answer one question well, were cited far more often than broad "ultimate guide" content. The long-form, cover-everything format that won the SEO decade is the wrong shape for the AI answer era. Your most-cited assets will look more like a sharp 600-word answer than a 4,000-word pillar page.


**Where your brand shows up matters as much as what you say.** When two strong sources are available, AI engines often cite the one they encounter first in retrieval. That means earned visibility on the third-party domains AI engines trust, including review sites, communities, and credible media, is no longer a PR side-project. It is a frontline brand investment.


**Substance moves citations. Layout does not.** Reordering headers, restructuring bullets, or adding "AI-friendly" formatting had almost no measurable effect. Completeness, evidence, and clear trust signals did. The growing cottage industry of "AI formatting hacks" is largely solving the wrong problem.


Seven of the eighteen factors tested turned out to have weak or inconsistent impact across models. The implication for marketing budget is not subtle. A meaningful share of current GEO advice is optimization theatre, and CMOs are paying for it.
