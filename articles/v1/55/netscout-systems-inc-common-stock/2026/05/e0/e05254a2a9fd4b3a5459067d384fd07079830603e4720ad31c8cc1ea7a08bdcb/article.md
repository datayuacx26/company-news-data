# The Digital Foundation of Public Trust Is More Than Skin Deep

Imagine a parent attempting to access Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program (SNAP) benefits to feed the family, only to meet a perpetual loading screen. Or a resident rushing to file an unemployment claim before a weekly deadline, only to find the portal timing out. For state and local governments, these are not just technical glitches. They are the high-stakes reality of service delivery failure, felt immediately at the kitchen table and on the front lines of public safety.


The problem is also felt behind the scenes, where daily administration depends on seamless network delivery. When a state worker cannot process a background check or a police officer is unable to pull up records, the mission is compromised. New artificial intelligence (AI)-driven tools also add layers of complexity to already-strained public systems, ironically increasing the demand on the very infrastructure they are meant to modernize.


As the year moves on, government IT leaders are navigating an era of hardened efficiency. With[23 states projecting general fund spending to decline or remain flat](https://www.nasbo.org/reports-data/fiscal-survey-of-states) and the[Department of Government Efficiency (DOGE)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Government_Efficiency) scrutinizing every line item, the fiscal reality is clear. At least 26 states[have launched their own “DOGE-style” task forces](https://www.epi.org/blog/at-least-26-states-have-launched-their-own-version-of-doge-these-states-are-simply-rebranding-longstanding-efforts-to-undermine-government-in-service-of-the-wealthy/) to audit state-level spending. State and local agencies must deliver flawless digital experiences with fewer resources and aging infrastructure. Now, every dollar spent must directly correlate to service uptime.


#### **The Network Observability Gap in Modern Public Services**


Too many agencies still rely on monitoring that checks for a basic heartbeat. In a modern service delivery chain, traffic traverses a complex path of owned infrastructure and unowned environments such as[internet service providers (ISPs) and cloud gateways](https://www.netscout.com/solutions/cloud-observability) . Knowing a service is technically “up” is not the same as knowing it is performing well. When a Department of Motor Vehicles (DMV) app stalls, the problem usually hides in the[“gray space” between these networks](https://www.netscout.com/solutions/network-performance-management) . This lack of visibility transforms a simple digital service into a bureaucratic roadblock.


True[network observability](https://www.netscout.com/solutions/network-observability) requires looking beyond surface-level metrics to the actual data moving across the wire. This allows IT teams to pinpoint if a software update at the application layer, aka Layer 7, is causing errors or if a gateway bottleneck is hindering field operations. Without this granular detail, IT departments may struggle to fix application-specific issues that leave staff and citizens frustrated. If the network performs poorly, it can create a domino effect that[halts critical services such as 911 dispatch](https://statescoop.com/911-faces-outages-cyberattacks-2025/) or mask underlying security vulnerabilities that leave the entire agency at risk and threaten public safety.


#### **Securing Critical Infrastructure Amid Fiscal Constraints**


Recently, government organizations have become primary targets for hacktivist-driven distributed denial-of-service (DDoS) activity. As budgets tighten, attackers exploit legacy infrastructure that is often too costly to replace. For example, a[July 2025 Government Accountability Office (GAO) report](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-25-107795) reveals that essential federal legacy systems maintain known cybersecurity vulnerabilities that cannot be remediated without modernization, jeopardizing citizens.


To maintain resilience, agencies must adopt a unified stance on security and performance. Unexpected spikes often signal a breach long before a traditional alert triggers. Because modern network traffic is largely obscured, deriving metadata from encrypted traffic without slowing down the network is essential. During volumetric DDoS attacks, ensuring 911 centers remain accessible requires the ability to prioritize high-value traffic flows in real time. By analyzing traffic data to understand these service dependencies, agencies can modernize in waves rather than via expensive, risky replacements.


#### **A Resilience Checklist for Communities**


To minimize disruptions, IT teams might look to several specific areas of oversight:


- Emphasize the health of application programming interfaces (APIs) for SNAP, DMV, and voter-registration platforms to ensure citizen-facing portals remain accessible.
- Strengthen connectivity for emergency services by identifying and eliminating single points of failure across hybrid cloud environments.
- Balance the increased network demand and architectural complexity introduced by new AI-driven initiatives to ensure they do not compromise core service delivery.
- Monitor performance across the entire service delivery chain, including unowned assets such as third-party Domain Name System (DNS) providers.
- Adopt unified observability and security solutions where the same data can be used so teams can coordinate responses.
