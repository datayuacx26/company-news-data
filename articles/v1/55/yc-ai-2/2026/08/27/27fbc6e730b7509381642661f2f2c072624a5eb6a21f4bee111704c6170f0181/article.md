## Start with the job and output


“Patent analytics software” is an umbrella term. Some products analyze patent families and owners across a market. Others retrieve prior art, monitor court activity, or turn evidence into a claim chart. Six questions expose those differences:


1. **What sources does the product cover?** Patents alone may be enough for a portfolio view but not for a litigation search that needs non-patent literature, archived web pages, standards, manuals, or product evidence.
2. **What is the unit of analysis?** A patent family, company, technology cluster, claim limitation, accused product, case, and judge answer different questions.
3. **What output does the team receive?** A dashboard, result list, alert, and editable invalidity chart are not substitutes.
4. **Can a reviewer trace every material conclusion to its source?** Citations should open to the exact passage, figure, image, or record.
5. **Does the workflow preserve attorney control?** The product should expose missing evidence and make review, revision, and approval visible.
6. **Does the system fit the buyer?** Patent litigators, search professionals, R&D leaders, and portfolio strategists need different controls and handoffs.

PatentSight+ presents itself as a strategic patent-analysis platform for high-value portfolio decisions.


**Best for:** IP strategy leaders comparing portfolio strength, competitive position, technology direction, or potential transaction targets.


PatentSight+ is built around normalized patent-family and ownership data plus portfolio metrics. Its Patent Asset Index combines Technology Relevance and Market Coverage to compare the strength and reach of patent families and portfolios.


The platform is useful when the question is strategic: Which companies lead a technology? Where is a portfolio gaining or losing strength? ## How to choose patent analytics software


A polished demo can hide the failures that matter most. Run a bounded pilot against work your team understands.


### 1. Define the decision and required output


Write down the decision, unit of analysis, source of record, final deliverable, owner, and reviewer. “Analyze this portfolio” is too broad. “Identify the strongest two Section 102 candidates, establish the support for each limitation, and produce an editable chart for independent attorney review” is a bounded assignment.


### 2. Match source coverage to the question


Ask which patent offices, court sources, non-patent-literature collections, standards, product repositories, and archived websites are covered. Record update frequency, through-dates, excluded content, normalization rules, and links to original records.


The forum can change the evidence boundary. An inter partes review challenge under[35 U.S.C. § 311(b)](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=2023&req=granuleid%3AUSC-2023-title35-chapter31) is limited to patents and printed publications under Sections 102 and 103. District-court analysis may also require product manuals, standards, source code, archived pages, videos, or other evidence.


### 3. Trace the entire evidence chain


Follow one proposition from retrieval through the final deliverable. The source, date, passage, claim construction, reviewer comments, edits, and approval should survive every handoff.


The[Northern District of California Patent Local Rules](https://cand.uscourts.gov/rules-forms-fees/local-rules/patent-local-rules) illustrate why topic-level relevance is insufficient. Rules 3-1 and 3-3 require parties to identify where and how claim limitations appear in accused products or prior art and to state the relevant invalidity theories. The controlling requirements vary by venue and posture, but the software should make exact support and missing support visible.


### 4. Evaluate confidentiality and control


Review which models and subprocessors receive matter data, whether inputs are retained or used for training, who can access a matter, where data is stored, how it is deleted, and what the vendor logs. Certifications help, but they do not answer every matter-specific question.


A deliberately unsupported proposition belongs in the pilot. The system should expose the gap or route it to review rather than manufacture a confident answer.


### 5. Score a real-work pilot


Build a benchmark with known references, hard negatives, ambiguous claims, recent records, missing sources, and your real output format. Score source coverage, retrieval, citation validity, proposition support, completeness, repeatability, reviewer time, correction time, and export usability separately.


Set scale, revise, and stop thresholds before the results arrive. A missed reference, unsupported proposition, confidentiality problem, and formatting defect should not carry the same weight.


## Frequently asked questions


### What is patent analytics software?


Patent analytics software helps teams search, organize, compare, and interpret patent-related data or evidence. Depending on the product, it may produce portfolio landscapes, prior art results, alerts, litigation intelligence, claim mappings, or legal work product.

### Is patent analytics software the same as patent search software?


No. Patent search software retrieves relevant patent records. Patent analytics software may also normalize families and owners, compare portfolios, visualize trends, analyze litigation activity, or map evidence into a legal deliverable. Search is one part of the broader category.


### How should a legal team evaluate a patent analytics tool?


A legal team should use a real, bounded workflow with known answers and a required output. The pilot should include hard negatives and missing evidence, then measure source coverage, citation support, accuracy, repeatability, reviewer time, security controls, and export quality before the rollout expands.
