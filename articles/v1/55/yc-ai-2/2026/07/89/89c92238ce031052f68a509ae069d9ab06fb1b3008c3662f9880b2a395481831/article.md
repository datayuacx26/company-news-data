July 7, 2026

Patent work is billed by the matter. Every search, every chart, every hour is attributable to a case and, often, passed through to a client. It's one of the most cleanly metered professions there is.


So it's strange that most of the software built for it is sold by the seat — a flat annual fee for access, priced privately, quoted only after a demo. That model fights the grain of how patent teams actually account for their work.


Today we're offering a new option in the space: &AI is now fully self-serve, with pricing you can see before you ever talk to us. Sign up, start a 7-day free trial, and put the platform on a real matter this afternoon.


## Three ways to buy patent tools — and what each one costs you


The search firm is transparent but priced like the work is still done by hand. A validity or invalidity search runs $1,500–$3,000 (Planet Patent), up to ~$6,999 for a full invalidity engagement (Sagacious IP). The cost is billable to the matter, which is right — it's just slow and expensive, so teams commission the searches they can justify and skip the rest.


The claim chart service prices per chart or per reference. Patprolytics lists evidence-of-use charts at up to $900 each; InvalidityCharts.com charges $175 for every prior-art reference you add, so a five-reference position is ~$875 in fees before any analysis.


Other AI platforms — like Patlytics and Solve Intelligence — sell per-seat subscriptions, priced behind a "Book a Demo" button as of this writing. There's no public per-search or per-chart number to compare. When a seat costs the same at two searches or two hundred, the per-unit price is hard to show plainly, and a flat seat is firm overhead that belongs to no single client.


At &AI, we chose a different pricing model: transparent, priced per unit of work, and mapped to the matter.


## What &AI charges


&AI runs on credits — you spend them on the work you do and nothing else. The average rates:

- **Claim chart:** ~$48–$96 on &AI, versus up to $900 per chart, or $175 per reference, elsewhere.

## Frequently asked questions


How much does &AI cost?


Credit-based and published: Pro is $625/user/month with 2,500 included credits and charged at $0.25/credit after; Pay As You Go has no subscription at a higher per-credit rate; Enterprise is custom. A prior art search runs ~$35–$70 and a claim chart ~$48–$96 on average, depending on the plan.


Do I pay for &AI even when I'm not using it?


No — and that's the point of pricing in credits rather than seats. A credit only becomes a cost when it becomes work: a search, a chart, an analysis. A per-seat subscription works the other way: the seat costs the same whether the attorney behind it runs two searches this month or two hundred. Unused credits roll over, so a quiet month funds a busy one instead of expiring.


Isn't a flat per-seat subscription simpler to budget?


It's simpler to invoice, but not to justify. A seat is a fixed access fee that's the same in a heavy month and an idle one, and it sits on the firm's books as overhead rather than mapping to a client matter. Credits tie spend to work performed and to the project it belongs to, so what you pay reflects the value you actually got — and it's attributable to the matter for billing.


How much does a prior art search cost at a search firm?


$1,500–$3,000, up to ~$6,999 for a full invalidity engagement. On &AI, ~$35–$70 in minutes. &AI data is based on real platform averages depending on the plan type, but actual costs will vary depending on the size and complexity of the search.


How much does a claim chart cost?


Up to $900 per chart at per-chart services, or $175 per reference at per-document tools. On &AI, ~$48–$96. &AI data is based on real platform averages depending on the plan type, but actual costs will vary depending on the number of claims and reference count on the chart.


Do other patent AI platforms publish pricing?


Most don't — Patlytics, Solve Intelligence, and DeepIP route pricing through a demo as of this writing. &AI publishes its rates because usage-based pricing is tied to discrete units of work with clear value.
