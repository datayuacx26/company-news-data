# AI Agents for Accounting: The Path Toward Zero-Day Close

Accounting teams have come a long way. Over the last decade, new tools have helped speed up close timelines, organize workflows, and improve visibility into financial operations. But even with modern close management software, the core process still relies on manual work—chasing down entries, reconciling accounts, resolving discrepancies, and formatting reports.


That’s why the **zero-day close** has become the next benchmark. It’s not just about closing faster, it’s about closing continuously. A zero-day close means your books are accurate, complete, and ready for review at any moment. It’s a future many finance leaders talk about, but few know how to achieve.


Traditional software can’t get you there. It streamlines workflows, but it doesn’t eliminate them. The only way to reach a truly continuous close is to change *who* does the work—and that’s where **AI agents for accounting** come in.


Unlike rules-based automation, AI agents don’t just move data—they understand the process. They connect directly to your ERP, automate reconciliations, detect anomalies, draft journal entries, and learn over time. By handling the routine tasks that slow down accounting teams, AI agents enable a faster, smarter, and more scalable close process.


The result isn’t just speed, it’s transformation. What once took days can now happen in real time. And for the first time, the zero-day close is within reach.


## **The Accounting Workforce Crisis**


Accounting teams are facing a crisis that software alone hasn’t solved.


Since 2022, more than[300,000 accountants and auditors have left the profession](https://www.forbes.com/councils/forbesfinancecouncil/2025/03/18/mind-the-gap-addressing-the-finance-and-accounting-talent-gap-in-2025/) — **17% of the entire workforce** . The pipeline isn’t rebounding:[CPA exam participation is at its lowest since 2006](https://www.smartvault.com/resources/the-great-accounting-exodus-part1/) , and the U.S. expects over[130,000 new accounting roles to open annually](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/business-and-financial/accountants-and-auditors.htm) over the next decade. That math doesn’t add up.


This shortage isn’t just about hiring. It’s about how work gets done. Today’s accounting professionals are stuck between the rigid structure of ERP systems and the manual flexibility of spreadsheets. ERPs remain the system of record—and they should. But they weren’t designed for dynamic workflows. That’s why accountants still spend hours toggling between NetSuite and Excel, manually tagging transactions, chasing inputs, and preparing reconciliations.


To bridge the gap, teams have adopted a long list of point solutions: close management tools like[FloQast](https://get.floqast.com/demo-request?utm_source=google&utm_medium=cpc&utm_campaign=us-search-brand-dg-bof-demo-transform&utm_content=e&utm_term=floqast&hsa_acc=6620168404&hsa_cam=22198625697&hsa_grp=176202115324&hsa_ad=732138933256&hsa_src=g&hsa_tgt=kwd-342497427256&hsa_kw=floqast&hsa_mt=e&hsa_net=adwords&hsa_ver=3&gad_source=1&gad_campaignid=22198625697&gbraid=0AAAAADqeRoAAfKmJPDIbeCsdZt0VLBtiJ&gclid=CjwKCAjwx8nCBhAwEiwA_z__03FYYi8joV_xk6qABGqMjY1fm2JZWoVLJ_mGY1gz7rPv_1LxCn9G7hoCYqEQAvD_BwE) , journal entry automation from[BlackLine](https://www.blackline.com/) , reconciliation modules, approval workflows. Each helps with a piece of the puzzle—but none solve the whole. These tools support task visibility, not task execution.


The result? Fragmentation. Every task becomes its own tool, each with different logic, data formats, and user experiences. Accountants spend their days stitching together systems that weren’t designed to work together—leaving little time for high-value analysis or leadership.


Even as software has expanded, the experience of accounting hasn’t fundamentally changed. Most close cycles still depend on human follow-up, spreadsheet validations, and manual reviews. The average time to close hasn’t improved in years. And without enough staff to shoulder the load, cracks are beginning to show.


The core issue isn’t that ERPs or accounting platforms are bad. It’s that they weren’t built to run workflows end-to-end. They document and store financial data—but they don’t chase missing inputs, flag anomalies, or answer “why did this number move?”


That’s where AI agents come in. Not as a replacement for ERPs—but as an execution layer that runs on top of them.


The pressure on accounting teams isn’t just anecdotal–it’s measurable. A staggering 87% of accountants report working overtime during the close process, while 75% say their workflows still depend heavily on manual effort. Time pressure is a constraint: more than half of professionals cite meeting deadlines as their biggest challenge, and 59% of businesses still take six days or more to close their books. Meanwhile, CFOs are feeling the downstream impact. Nearly 90% admit they’re making decisions based on incomplete or inaccurate data, and 62% say it’s increasingly difficult to hire skilled talent. These aren’t isolated issues–they’re systemic signals that the current model isn’t sustainable.

→ Agent compares historical posting patterns to detect accounts where expected transactions are absent
