# AI Agents for FP&A: Built for Modern Finance Teams

The role of FP&A is changing fast. Once focused primarily on reporting and budget management, finance teams are now expected to deliver real-time insights, guide strategy, and help the business navigate constant change.[According to Accenture’s 2024 CFO Forward Study](https://www.accenture.com/us-en/insights/consulting/cfo-forward-study-2024) , 72% of CFOs view FP&A as a primary source of strategic insight, leaning on these teams for forward-looking guidance rather than just historical reporting.


But while expectations have risen, the infrastructure hasn’t kept up. Most teams are still choosing between slow, manual Excel-based workflows or the implementation of rigid FP&A tools. The latter limits agility and flexibility, while the former lacks any real automation. FP&A teams are either stuck picking one or the other, and in many cases, doing both. Now, there’s a third option available to FP&A teams: **AI agents** .


AI agents sit on top of your existing systems—ERPs, spreadsheets, planning tools—and streamline the work that slows teams down. Whether you’re operating in Excel or a dedicated FP&A platform, agents enhance what you already use, enabling faster, smarter decision-making while prioritizing flexibility and automation. AI agents are the next evolution for FP&A teams to truly give them the edge in the strategic decision-making they are tasked with.

## **Why Traditional Workflows Break Down**


Modern FP&A teams manage a balancing act: tight timelines, shifting inputs, and rising expectations from across the business. Many still spend much of their time on manual work:


- Consolidating data from siloed systems
- Updating fragile Excel models
- Chasing business partners for inputs
- Rebuilding scenarios for every new assumption


For others, FP&A platforms offer some structure and automation, but often at the cost of flexibility. These tools still require predefined logic, frequent maintenance, and re-implementations when business questions evolve mid-cycle.


Whether your team relies on Excel or a dedicated planning platform, the core challenges are strikingly similar: too much time spent managing the process, not enough time driving insight.


A majority of teams[(57%) say budgeting takes 30 to 90 days, and 63% can't forecast beyond six months](https://fpa-trends.com/article/why-we-must-embrace-modern-fpa-systems) . These aren’t edge cases—they reflect a broader system slowdown. But they don’t have to be permanent. AI agents offer a new path forward by sitting on top of your existing tools, eliminating repetitive steps, and making forecasts, scenarios, and variance analysis available in real time—without rebuilding your entire workflow.


### **The Hidden Cost of Manual Forecasting**


Rolling forecasts are gaining traction—[49% of companies now use them](https://fpa-trends.com/article/2024-fpa-trends-survey-results-unveiled) —but most teams still lack the systems to support them effectively. Without automation, what should be a forward-looking, strategic process turns into a manual cycle of pulling data, updating models, and realigning with stakeholders, only to repeat it again next month.


Even for teams using FP&A platforms, rolling forecasts can be tough to scale. Traditional tools often require setup-heavy configurations, rigid logic, and frequent manual inputs. The challenge isn’t the model—it’s the infrastructure behind it.


AI agents change that by working alongside your existing tools. They automate data inputs, refresh forecasts in real time, and generate outputs the moment new information becomes available. Whether you’re operating in Excel or a planning platform, agents reduce friction and make rolling forecasts faster, smarter, and easier to manage.


That’s the difference between having a forecast on paper—and running with real-time financial intelligence.
