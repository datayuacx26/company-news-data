# Overview of U.S. Sustainability Regulations Relevant to the Fashion Industry

While most federal-level sustainability bills in the United States remain tied up in Congress or subject to legal uncertainty, near-term compliance pressure is increasingly shifting to state-level regulation.


California is currently leading the initiative. Under the[Climate Corporate Data Accountability Act](https://www.carbonfact.com/carbonfact-blogs/policy/californias-climate-laws) (SB 253), in-scope brands will be required to begin reporting Scope 1 and Scope 2 greenhouse gas emissions starting in 2026, with Scope 3 emissions reporting following from 2027.


[Extended Producer Responsibility](https://www.carbonfact.com/carbonfact-blogs/policy/textile-epr-overview) (EPR) requirements are also developing at the state level. California has already adopted a textile EPR law that is now moving into implementation, while New York and Washington have published legislative proposals that would make apparel and footwear producers responsible for the end-of-life management of their products.


In our previous article, we delved into[EU and EU membership state policies relevant to the fashion industry;](https://www.carbonfact.com/carbonfact-blogs/policy/eu-regulations-for-textile-brands) however, if your company is located in the United States or conducts business there, this article contains a list of the key regulations that should be on your radar.


## TL;DR


- [California's SB 253](https://www.carbonfact.com/blog/policy/californias-climate-laws) requires large fashion brands to report Scope 1 and 2 emissions from 2026, Scope 3 from 2027.
- California became the first U.S. state with mandatory[textile EPR](https://www.carbonfact.com/carbonfact-blogs/policy/textile-epr-overview) , requiring brands to fund product end-of-life management.
- New York and Washington have proposed textile EPR laws requiring producer-funded collection and recycling systems by 2027-2031.
