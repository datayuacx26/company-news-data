# For Fashion Brands: Navigating Sustainable Textiles Labeling Globally

In this era of heightened environmental awareness and climate legislation, apparel and footwear brands are increasingly under the microscope when it comes to their climate and sustainability practices. To address this, nations and regions are introducing a web of intricate textile labeling laws, each with its own set of standards and regulations.


In our previous blogs, we explored sustainability regulations relevant to the fashion industry in the[United States](https://www.carbonfact.com/carbonfact-blogs/policy/us-regulations-for-fashion-brands) and the[European Union](https://www.carbonfact.com/carbonfact-blogs/policy/eu-regulations-for-textile-brands) . In this overview, we’ll delve into the top apparel labeling regulations impacting fashion and textile brands around the world.


There’s a lot here, so if you have any questions about staying compliant with sustainable textiles labeling globally,[please reach out to our team](https://www.carbonfact.com/request-demo) .


## TL;DR


- EU's[Digital Product Passport](https://www.carbonfact.com/carbonfact-blogs/policy/digital-product-passport-fashion) for textiles launches 2027, requiring brands to disclose material composition and environmental data.
- France's[Environmental Cost](https://www.carbonfact.com/blog/regulations/french-eco-score) label becomes mandatory October 2026 if brands communicates any other any other environmental score (such as carbon footprint on the e-commerce store).
- EU's[updated greenwashing rules](https://www.carbonfact.com/carbonfact-blogs/policy/ucpd-fashion) ban unsubstantiated sustainability labels from September 2026, with enforcement penalties.
- US lacks federal environmental labeling standards; state laws like California's AB 1305 require carbon claim substantiation.
- Over 40 voluntary ecolabels exist in Asia; Australia targets textile circularity by 2030 through Seamless program.


## Textile Labeling in European Union


Currently, in the EU, there are four main pieces of legislation that will impact textile labeling: the EU Textile Labeling Regulation, the


[Ecodesign for Sustainable Products Regulation](https://www.carbonfact.com/blog/policy/espr-textile) (ESPR), the


[Directive on Green Claims](https://www.carbonfact.com/blog/policy/green-claims-directive-fashion) , and the


[French Climate and Resilience Law](https://www.carbonfact.com/blog/policy/french-eco-score) . The EU has also finalized a framework for helping companies track their environmental footprint, the


[Product Environmental Footprint (PEF).](https://www.carbonfact.com/blog/policy/pefcr-apparel-and-footwear)

### EU Eco-Label


[The EU Ecolabel](https://www.eu-ecolabel.nl/en/) , established in 1992, is a voluntary environmental label for products and services that meet specific criteria to reduce overall environmental impact. Products displaying the "EU flower" symbol comply with these criteria, which are based on a comprehensive life-cycle assessment.


Governed by Regulation (EC) NO 66/2010, the EU Ecolabel aligns with the ISO definition for a Type 1 Ecolabel. This means an independent third party awards it. The criteria are developed through collaboration between the European Commission, Member States, industry, consumer organizations, and environmental NGOs.


### EU Textile Labeling Regulation


The current Textile Labeling Regulation requires disclosure of fiber composition and non-textile parts of animal origin. However, it also does not keep pace with the development of new fabrics and recycling technologies. The EU Commission plans to revise the current Textile Labeling Regulation to:


- Include additional product information (e.g., origin, care instructions, sustainability-related parameters).
- Define what should be on physical labels and what on DPP.
- Reflect new fibers, recycled materials, and recycling technologies.


All EU and non-EU textile brands selling textile products in the EU must comply with the Textile Labeling Regulation. Footwear, however, is excluded, as it is regulated separately under EU footwear labeling rules.

### EU’s Product Environmental Footprint Methodology


The new[PEF Category Rules](https://www.carbonfact.com/carbonfact-blogs/policy/pefcr-apparel-and-footwear) (PEFCR) present a single, science-based method for product footprints, streamline compliance with the upcoming EU Green Claims Directive and Ecodesign Rules, and set a common language amongst brands, suppliers, and auditors.


After five years of work, the PEFCR for the fashion sector is now considered final, with “no further modifications” needed, has been approved by the Technical Secretariat and green-lighted by the European Commission.
