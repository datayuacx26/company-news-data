# [Guide] Life Cycle Assessments for Textile and Fashion Products

## TL;DR


-


Life Cycle Assessment (LCA) measures environmental impacts across a product's entire lifetime, from raw materials to disposal.


- EU regulations now mandate fashion brands conduct comprehensive LCAs and transparently communicate findings to consumers.


- LCAs evaluate 16 environmental indicators beyond carbon, including water use, toxicity, and resource depletion for holistic impact.


- LCAs identify environmental hotspots, validate green claims, ensure compliance, and inform low-carbon product development decisions.


## About LCA for Fashion


The textile, and fashion sector faces mounting pressure to reduce its impact, leading to a surge in interest in[tools like Life Cycle Assessment (LCA)](https://www.carbonfact.com/lca-for-fashion) .


The LCA methodology, which measures the environmental impacts of a product throughout its entire lifetime, has become a crucial instrument in understanding and addressing the ecological footprint of fashion and clothing brands.


This growing emphasis on LCA isn't just a trend. It's also a response to evolving regulatory landscapes. The European Union (EU), for instance, has taken decisive steps to promote sustainability, transparency, and circular economy practices in various sectors, including fashion.


These regulations mandate fashion businesses to undertake comprehensive LCAs of their products and communicate these findings transparently to their consumers. Read more about[textile regulations here](https://www.carbonfact.com/carbonfact-blogs/policy/eu-regulations-for-textile-brands) .


Additionally, the explosion of 'green claims' – apparel brands advertising their products as eco-friendly or sustainable – has driven a demand for authentic evidence to support these assertions.


Consumers, watchdog organizations, and policymakers are increasingly holding brands accountable for these claims, and a rigorous LCA serves as a credible tool to validate them. In fact, the[European Commission recommends the use of Environmental Footprint Methods](https://environment.ec.europa.eu/publications/recommendation-use-environmental-footprint-methods_en)


such as the Life Cycle Assessment.


In this article, we will guide you through the importance and the intricacies of calculating the environmental footprint of products in the fashion industry using Life Cycle Assessment.


## What Is a Life Cycle Assessment (LCA)?


LCA is a structured framework that evaluates the environmental impact of a product throughout its entire life span.


By looking at the entire life cycle of the product, including raw material extraction, processing, use phase, transportation modes and distances paved and waste management practices, this approach enables a comprehensive analysis of a product's overall environmental impact.


To have a comprehensive result of the product’s environmental footprint, textile brands need to answer these questions:


- What are the materials used for the product?
- Where are the materials produced?
- What chemicals are used in the supply chain?
- What processes does the product go through such as spinning, weaving/knitting, bleaching, dyeing, finishing, etc.
- How the product is transported and how long are the distances?
- What happens to the product when it becomes waste?


But here's the catch: the sheer number of questions can be overwhelming. How can we possibly unfold the complexities of sustainability in the fashion industry and make informed choices?


That’s where a life cycle assessment can help.
