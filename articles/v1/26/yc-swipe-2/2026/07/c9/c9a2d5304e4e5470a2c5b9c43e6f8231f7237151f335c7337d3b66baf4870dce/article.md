# Bill to–ship to GST invoice workflow for traders and distributors


> Where there is a mismatch between buyer and delivery location, managing GST transactions gets more complicated. The Bill To-Ship To GST Invoice Workflow assists traders and distributors in managing the transactions correctly and ensuring compliance with the GST guidelines. It guarantees the correct calculation of Input Tax Credit (ITC) claims, accurate invoice record-keeping and correct calculation of taxes even when goods are shipped directly to a third-party location.


It is essential that both wholesalers and distributors and trading businesses understand this process to minimise the risk of invoicing mistakes, to avoid disputes with the GST and to streamline the supply chain.


## What is the bill to-shop to GST invoice workflow?


The Bill To–Ship To GST Invoice Workflow is when the invoice is sent to one party (Bill To), and the goods are shipped to another party or location (Ship To).


Usually this is when a distributor buys the product on behalf of a customer and asks for the supplier to send them directly. This transaction falls under the provisions of the place of supply under Section 10(1)(b) of the IGST Act, as applicable in the context of GST.


This workflow eliminates unnecessary transportation, reduces logistics costs, and speeds up deliveries while maintaining GST compliance.


## How the bill to-ship to GST workflow works


There are three parties to the process:


- Supplier
- Buyer (Bill to)
- The name of the person to receive the delivery.


### Step-by-step workflow


Step Action GST Impact


1 Buyer places an order with the supplier Invoice prepared for buyers


2 Buyer instructs supplier to ship goods to another address Delivery location differs from billing address


3 Supplier issues invoice to the buyer GST charged based on place of supply rules


4 Goods are delivered directly to Ship To location Delivery proof maintained


5 Buyer claims Input Tax Credit ITC available if GST conditions are satisfied


## Example: Demonstrate bill to-ship to GST invoice workflow


**Suppose:**


- Supplier is in Maharashtra
- Distributor is registered at Delhi
- Goods are directly supplied to the customer of the distributor in Rajasthan.


**In this case:**


- Delhi is the place of issue of invoice to the distributor
- Goods are directly transported to Rajasthan
- As per GST law, the applicability of[GST](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goods_and_Services_Tax_(India)) will be determined by the provision of place of supply.


The goods are never physically delivered to Delhi and the distributor continues to be the buyer, so that he can claim input tax credit on all the compliance conditions provided.

## GST rules that apply to bill-to ship to transactions


To avoid incurring any issues, business should be aware of the GST provisions before issuing the invoice.


### Important GST rules


GST Requirement Explanation


Proper GSTIN Buyer GSTIN must be mentioned correctly


Invoice Details Bill to ship to addresses must appear separately

E-Way Bill Generated whenever applicable

Businesses can keep a complete record of their dealings, which can aid them in the event of an audit by the government, or a verification by the department.


## The bill to-ship to GST invoice workflow offers several advantages


The right Bill To–Ship To GST Invoice Workflow offers a number of operational and tax benefits.


1. Faster deliveries


Goods are shipped straight from the manufacturer to the end-user, bypassing and middleman locations.


1. Lower transportation costs


The costs of double transportation and unnecessary handling are avoided by the businesses.


1. Better inventory management


Distributors have the ability to serve customers without holding too much stock.


1. Accurate GST compliance


Correctly structured invoices help to meeting the requirements of the GST and reduces the risk of litigation.


1. Seamless input tax credit


Eligible businesses can avail ITC without any delays with a correct documentation.

## Common errors to avoid


The majority of businesses receive GST notices due to avoidable mistakes in their invoicing. The following are some common errors:


- Bill to/ship to addresses that are incorrect
- Wrong GSTIN mentioned on invoices
- Mistakes in place of supply selection
- Insufficient information or details in e-way bill.
- Incomplete transport documentation
- Unregistered tax invoices for claiming ITC
- Mismatch between GST returns and invoices


Frequent invoice verification greatly minimizes compliance risks.


## Conclusion


Traders and distributors involved in direct shipments often need the Bill To-Ship To GST Invoice Workflow to ensure they comply with the GST requirements. If done properly, it can faciliate logistics efficiency, lower transportation expenses and GST compliance.


Invoicing information, place of supply rules, GSTIN accuracy, and supporting documentation are critical areas that businesses must focus on. Adhering to the GST guidelines not only helps avoid penalties but also allows for smooth claiming of Input Tax Credit.


If you often make deliveries for third parties, having a proper Bill To–Ship To workflow will help you be more efficient and compliant with the GST regulations.


## FAQs


### What is bill to-ship to GST invoice workflow?


Bill To-Ship To GST Invoice Workflow is a GST transaction where the invoice is sent to a buyer and the goods are sent to another buyer. It is a method that is widely adopted by traders and distributors for simplifying their logistics and staying within GST rules.


### What is the GST law applicable to transactions where the shipment is done under bill to-ship to?


Bill to-ship to transactions will be governed by section 10 (1) (b) of the Integrated Goods and Services Tax (IGST) Act, 2017. The provision here sets out the rules for determining the place of supply where goods are billed to one person but sent to another on the buyer’s instructions.


### In a bill to-ship to transaction, is it possible to claim input tax credit (ITC)?


Yes, if all the conditions of the GST are met then the buyer will be eligible for Input Tax Credit. The buyer should have a valid tax invoice, the supplier should have paid the applicable GST and all such conditions of giving ITC that have been specified in the tax law should be met.


### Is e-way bill compulsory for bill to-shop to transactions?


Yes the e-way bill must be issued when the goods or services involved in the transportation exceed the prescribed e-way bill threshold value or as per applicable regulations of GST. The details specified in e-way bill should correspond with the details given in invoice and the transportation documents.
