# GST Penalty for Technical E-Way Bill Errors: When Is Section 129 Applicable?

# **GST Penalty for Technical E-Way Bill Errors: When Is Section 129 Applicable?**


## **Can a Technical E-Way Bill Error Attract GST Penalty?**


> The small discrepancy regarding the e-way bill does not necessarily entail that there will be a large penalty under Section 129 of the GST law. For **GST Penalty for Technical E-Way Bill Errors** , the authorities and the courts can also look into whether the relevant invoices were generated, whether there was a match between the goods and the relevant documents, and whether GST is already charged, as well as whether the discrepancy meant that there was a real possibility of tax evasion.


However, carrying goods without possessing the necessary e-way bill is indeed a much more serious issue than just a typing slip. Also, generating the e-way bill post-interception doesn’t simply mean the initial non-compliance is rectified.


In Falguni Steels v. State of U.P., the issues were thoroughly scrutinised by the Allahabad High Court and the penalty was set aside since the court concluded that the entire process was duly documented and that no intention of tax evasion was established.


## **What Is an E-Way Bill?**


An e-way bill is a type of online document that is utilised to track and trace the movement of goods as per the GST guidelines.


According to the CBIC guidelines, the person responsible for transporting goods must carry certain documents, namely, invoice, bill of supply or delivery challan, and e-way bill or e-way bill number in some acceptable manner. The e-way bill must be generated before moving the shipment in general.


A few aspects that the e-way bill helps in gauging are:


- Supplier details
- Recipient details
- Nature of goods being transported
- Taxable value of goods
- Invoice details
- Vehicle details
- Place of origin and destination of goods
- Period of validity of such transportation

## **What Is a Technical E-Way Bill Error?**


[Technical e-way bill errors](https://taxguru.in/goods-and-service-tax/section-129-proceeding-cannot-initiated-minor-mistakes-e-way-bill.html) are compliance errors that do not fundamentally hide or alter the actual transaction.


Examples include:


- A slight spelling error.
- A minor mistake in the address.
- Two or three digits being wrong in the document number.
- A minor mistake in the vehicle number.
- A short-term portal malfunction.
- Any delays that were caused by something that was beyond the control of the taxpayer.


It is important to note that technical errors are different from substantive violations, which involve fake documents, undeclared goods, serious undervaluation of goods, or the recipient not being disclosed.

## **What Does CBIC Circular No. 64/38/2018-GST Say?**


Circular No. 64/38/2018GST was introduced by CBIC to clarify penalties on small discrepancies in e-way bills.


According to the circular, Section 129 proceedings can be launched when goods are accompanied by correct documents along with an invoice but not with an e-way bill.


It also mentions that there are certain insignificant mistakes related to an e-way bill, for which Section 129 proceedings should not be initiated. The following situations are outlined in the circular:


- Typographical errors in the name of the supplier or recipient under which the GSTIN is correct.
- Errors in the PIN code when other details are correct, and the distance is not unjustifiably affected.
- Minor address errors whereby locality has been correctly mentioned along with other details.
- A few digits in the number being wrong.
- Mistakes regarding the HSN code where grade numbers are correctly given along with the tax type.
- Two or three digits being wrong in the vehicle number.


For specified minor errors, the circular explains that the penalty imposed is ₹500 as per the CGST Act, ₹500 under the relevant SGST Act, or ₹1000 under the IGST Act, as applicable.


## **Does the Circular Protect a Completely Missing E-Way Bill?**


Not necessarily.


The circular clearly differentiates between minor mistakes in an e-way bill and situations where there is no e-way bill at all.


In situations where there is an invoice but no e-way bill, Section 129 proceedings may still occur.


Therefore, businesses shouldn’t view the circular as a general exception to the e-way bill provisions.


## **What Happened in Falguni Steels v. State of U.P.?**


On January 25, 2024, the Allahabad High Court ruled in the case of Falguni Steels v. State of U.P. and Others.


Falguni Steels was a registered dealer of Steel Authority of India Limited. On February 17, 2019, Falguni Steels secured a shipment of TMT bars. The shipment was covered by two invoices provided by SAIL.


The invoices specified:


- Description of goods.
- Quantity of goods.
- Vehicle registration numbers.
- Applicable CGST and SGST.


The shipment was meant to be carried from SAIL Yard in Naini to the taxpayer's location at Lookerganj in Allahabad.


## **Why Was the E-Way Bill Generated Late?**


The taxpayer claimed that the e-way bill portal was facing technical problems.


It also stated that transportation could not begin on the invoice date because local restrictions had been imposed during Maghi Purnima and the 2019 Kumbh Mela.


The goods were eventually transported on February 20, 2019.


According to the case record:


- One e-way bill was generated before detention.
- The second e-way bill was generated after detention.
- Both were available before the final penalty order was passed.


The authorities rejected the documents and proceeded with the demand.

## **What Penalty Was Imposed?**


A notice was issued proposing:


- Tax of ₹1,29,862.
- An equivalent penalty of ₹1,29,862.


Falguni Steels deposited a total of ₹2,59,724 to secure the release of the goods.


The original order was later upheld by the appellate authority.


## **What Was the Taxpayer’s Defence?**


Falguni Steels argued that:


- Valid SAIL invoices existed.
- The invoices contained the correct goods and vehicle details.
- CGST and SGST had already been charged.
- The goods matched the invoices.
- The e-way bill delay arose from portal issues.
- Local transportation restrictions also affected dispatch.
- Both e-way bills were produced before the final order.
- There was no intention to evade GST.
- The authorities had passed non-speaking or inadequately reasoned orders.


## **What Did the Department Argue?**


The Department maintained that the e-way bill should have been generated before transportation commenced.


It treated the failure to produce the complete e-way bill documentation at the time of inspection as a violation of Rule 138 and proceeded under Section 129.


The Department also considered the documents generated after detention to be an afterthought.


## **What Did the Allahabad High Court Decide?**


The Allahabad High Court quashed the original penalty order and the appellate order.


The Court considered the following factors important:


- Valid tax invoices had been issued by SAIL.
- The invoices contained the vehicle number.
- The quantity and description of goods were properly recorded.
- GST had already been charged.
- No discrepancy was found between the goods and the invoice.
- The e-way bills were available before the final penalty order.
- The authorities had not established a definite intention to evade tax.


The Court observed that rejecting a post-detention e-way bill, without connecting the delay to an actual intention to evade tax, was insufficient to justify the penalty in the circumstances.

### **Why Did the High Court Quash the Penalty?**


### **1. The Transaction was Supported by Valid Invoices**


The invoices existed before the transportation and contained the key details required to identify the transaction.


Although an invoice does not replace an e-way bill, it supported the taxpayer’s claim that the supply was genuine and traceable.


### **2. The Goods Matched the Documents**


There was no material discrepancy in the description or quantity of the TMT bars.


The vehicle number was also mentioned in the invoices.


### **3. GST Had Already Been Charged**


The supplier had charged CGST and SGST.


This weakened the suggestion that the transaction was intended to remain outside the tax system.


### **4. The Authorities Did Not Properly Examine the Documents**


Instead of examining the documents and providing detailed reasons, the authorities mainly relied on the timing of e-way bill generation.


The High Court considered this approach insufficient.


### **5. No Concrete Evidence of Tax Evasion Was Identified**


The penalty orders did not identify evidence of:


- A fake invoice.
- An undisclosed recipient.
- Understated value.
- Incorrect quantity.
- Diversion of the goods.
- Reuse of documents.
- Suppression of the transaction.


## **Does Generating an E-Way Bill After Interception Cure the Violation?**


No, not automatically.


An e-way bill is ordinarily required before the movement of goods begins. Generating it after the vehicle has been intercepted does not erase the original non-compliance.


However, a later-generated e-way bill may still be relevant when combined with:


- An invoice created before movement.
- Correct tax details.
- Goods matching the invoice.
- Proof of portal failure.
- Transport records.
- A consistent explanation.
- Evidence that the transaction was accounted for.


The authority should assess the complete record rather than accepting or rejecting the document solely based on its generation time.


## **Does Absence of Revenue Loss Mean No Penalty?**


Not necessarily.


The absence of revenue loss can help show that a mistake was procedural rather than fraudulent. It does not automatically make every violation penalty-free.


Authorities may still examine:


- Whether the e-way bill was mandatory.
- Whether it was generated on time.
- Whether the error affected the traceability of the goods.
- Whether the explanation is credible.
- Whether similar violations occurred previously.
- Whether the transaction was fully recorded.

## **When Can Falguni Steels Help a Taxpayer?**


The judgment may support a taxpayer where:


- Valid invoices existed before movement.
- The goods matched the invoices.
- Vehicle details were available.
- GST was charged correctly.
- The supplier and recipient were genuine.
- Portal problems are supported by records.
- The document was generated before the final order.
- No financial implication or concealment was identified.
- The penalty order did not contain proper reasons.


## **When May Falguni Steels Not Apply?**


The judgment may provide limited assistance where:


- No invoice existed before transportation.
- The invoice appears backdated.
- The goods materially differ from the documents.
- The value or quantity is understated.
- The recipient is fictitious.
- The destination is inconsistent with the records.
- The same e-way bill has been reused.
- Portal failure is claimed without evidence.
- Violations are deliberate or repeated.
- Documents were created only after interception.


## **What Evidence Should Be Preserved During an E-Way Bill Portal Failure?**


Businesses should retain:


- Screenshots of the portal error.
- Date and time of every failed attempt.
- Error codes.
- Helpdesk ticket numbers.
- Emails sent to the GST support team.
- Invoice-generation timestamps.
- E-way bill-generation timestamps.
- Dispatch-register entries.
- Transporter communications.
- Vehicle and driver details.
- Internal approval records.
- Proof that the goods remained unchanged.


A simple statement that the GST portal was not working may not be sufficient without contemporaneous evidence.

## **What Businesses Should Not Assume**


Businesses should not assume that:


- A valid invoice replaces an e-way bill.
- Later generation automatically cures the violation.
- Every portal-error claim will be accepted.
- No revenue loss always means no penalty.
- Every e-way bill mistake qualifies as a minor discrepancy.
- Falguni Steels will apply identically in every jurisdiction.


## **Conclusion**


The Allahabad High Court’s decision in *Falguni Steels v. State of U.P.* provides meaningful protection against disproportionate penalties where delayed e-way bill generation arises in an otherwise genuine and documented transaction.


The Court considered the valid invoices, correct vehicle details, matching goods, GST already charged and absence of evidence showing an intention to evade tax.


However, the ruling does not dilute the requirement to generate a valid e-way bill before transportation begins. Businesses should treat it as a safeguard against arbitrary penalties, not as an alternative to compliance.


## **Frequently Asked Questions**


### **1. What is a technical e-way bill error?**


It is a limited compliance mistake that does not conceal the supplier, recipient, goods, value, tax liability or destination of the transaction.


### **2. Is moving goods without an e-way bill a minor error?**


Generally, complete absence of an e-way bill is more serious than a spelling, address or vehicle-number mistake. Section 129 proceedings may be initiated where an invoice exists but no valid e-way bill accompanies the goods.


### **3. Can an e-way bill be generated after interception?**


It may be generated later, but subsequent generation does not automatically cure the original violation. Its relevance depends on the complete factual record.


### **4. What did Falguni Steels decide?**


The Allahabad High Court quashed the penalty because valid invoices existed, GST had already been charged, the goods matched the documents and no intention to evade tax had been established.


### **5. Is proof of portal failure necessary?**


Yes. Screenshots, timestamps, helpdesk complaints and communication records can materially strengthen the taxpayer’s explanation.


### **6. Does CBIC Circular No. 64/38/2018-GST cover all e-way bill errors?**


No. It specifically identifies certain minor discrepancies. It does not provide a blanket exemption for moving goods without a valid e-way bill.
