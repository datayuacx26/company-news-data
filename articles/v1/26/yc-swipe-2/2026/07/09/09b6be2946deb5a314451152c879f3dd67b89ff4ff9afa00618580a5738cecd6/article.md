# Can GST Penalty Under Section 129 Apply Without Intent to Evade Tax?

# **Can GST Penalty Under Section 129 Apply Without Intent to Evade Tax?**


## **Can GST Authorities Impose a Section 129 Penalty Without Tax-Evasion Intent?**


> Section 129 of the GST Act is not meant to impose penalties in a mindless manner if there is some procedural flaw in the process of movement of goods. While considering a **GST Penalty Under Section 129** , the authorities must examine the complete facts and documents related to the transaction. In the case of Balaji Traders v. State of U.P., the Allahabad High Court refused to uphold the imposition of a penalty on the ground that the authorities had not established that the taxpayer committed GST evasion and had less regard for the examination of documents relating to the transaction.


The decision, however, does not mean that penalties cannot be otherwise imposed when it comes to missing or defective e-way bills. Rather, the eventual outcome of this issue would be determined by various factors, including the nature of the violation, existing documents, surrounding facts, and applicable legal precedents.


## **What does Section 129 of the GST Act Cover?**


Under the provisions of Section 129 of the Central Goods and Services Tax Act, 2017, detention of goods and vehicles while in transit is covered.


This will be applicable when goods are being transported or stored in contravention of the law and rules made under this Act. The proper officer can detain both the goods as well as the vehicle, and issue the required notice stating the penalty and then pass a final decision in accordance with law.


On a practical level, proceedings under Section 129 can arise when the following things happen:


- An e-way bill is either absent or not valid.
- The required invoice documents are missing.
- The goods being moved do not match the papers that are accompanying them.
- The vehicle, consignee or destination requires correction.
- There are concerns with regard to the proper declaration of the concerned transaction.


It should be noted that a question of procedural breach may not necessarily be the same as a question of viability of the penalty involved.

## **What is Mens Rea Under GST?**


Mens rea refers to the guilty mind or intention behind committing a violation of law.


In a GST situation, intent to evade tax may include actions such as:


- Falsely hiding the movement of goods.
- Issuing fake or backdated invoices.
- Undervaluing the price or quantity of goods.
- Delivering goods to a secret entity.
- Reusing documents for different shipments.
- Keeping a transaction off the books.


A mere clerical mistake, late preparation of documents, or lack of transport information cannot be equated with the intention.


## **Is Intention to Evade Tax Expressly Mentioned in Section 129?**


The wording that can be found in Section 129 refers to the transportation of goods unlawfully and in contrary with the GST Act or the provisions of the Act. However, it does not state in each clause that intention to evade tax must be proved.


Section 130 talks about confiscation separately, mentioning provisions related to serious violations involving intention to evade tax.


This has led to a debate in the law as to whether each court violation can entail a penalty under Section 129 or whether the officer must examine the intent of the taxpayer and its implications on tax.


In Balaji Traders, the Allahabad High Court reaffirmed its previous opinion about the need to prove intention to evade as an element in the case considered. Therefore, this decision should be understood in its factual and jurisdictional context, and not as an absolute national principle applicable to all disputes under Section 129.

## **What Happened in Balaji Traders v. State of U.P.?**


On October 6, 2023, the Allahabad High Court ruled on the case of M/s Balaji Traders and Another v. State of U.P. and Others.


The firm was registered as a dealer in cigarettes, pan masala and food-processing spices. The firm received an order from a dealer based in Nashik and raised a tax invoice dated November 18, 2022.


The transportation of goods was to be carried out by railways. The firm claimed that the Railway Receipt number was necessary for the proper completion of the e-way bill.


The owner went inside the Kanpur Railway Station to get the bookings done. The consignment, however, was kept in an e-rickshaw outside the railway station.


Goods were seized by GST. As per the taxpayer, the e-rickshaw driver told the officials that the owner was inside the railway station with the requisite documents. When the goods were seized, a penalty of ₹5,58,286 was levied.


## **What Was the Taxpayer’s Explanation?**


Balaji Traders maintained its position that the transaction was legitimate and was already supported by a tax invoice.


The main points put forward by the taxpayer were:


- Tax invoice had been generated before interception.
- Goods were in transit through legal means by rail.
- Railway Receipt number is necessary to finalise the e-way bill.
- Owner was present at the place to finalise the rail booking.
- The driver of the e-rickshaw has notified the authorities regarding the matter.
- Subsequent to this, documents in support and explanation were provided.
- There was no intent to hide the transaction or avoid GST.


The taxpayer further stated that there was no mention of any finding as regards the intention to evade tax either in any of the notices or in the order passed for levy of penalty.


## **What Did the GST Department Argue?**


The Department argued that the required documents were not presented at the time of the seizure of the goods.


It argued that the transport of goods without the necessary documents amounted to a violation of the Goods and Services Act. The Department also raised concerns over the transaction not being recorded in the case of the absence of interception of the goods.


Thus, the High Court had to determine whether this suspicion was sufficient to impose penalties or whether the authorities were required to carefully assess the documents and explanations provided by the taxpayer.


## **What did the Allahabad High Court Decide?**


The[Allahabad High Court](https://taxguru.in/goods-and-service-tax/penalty-tax-evasion-gst-intent-matters-error.html) has rejected both the penalty order as well as the order passed by the Appellate Authority.


The Court observed that the relevant authorities had failed to establish any finding against Balaji Traders. It also held that the explanation submitted by the taxpayer in relation to railway booking and the Railway Receipt number had also not been properly taken into consideration by the authorities.

### **Why was the Penalty Quashed?**


### **1. No Finding of Tax-Evasion Intent**


The notice and penalty order did not refer to the taxpayer's plans for evading the GST.


A serious penalty cannot be levied based on mere assumptions that certain transactions have not been recorded anywhere.


### **2. Relevant Evidence was not Properly Examined**


It was clarified that the owner of the business was at the railway station arranging the documents related to the booking.


It was expected that the authorities would check and provide reasons if they find this explanation unsatisfactory or untrue.


### **3. A Genuine Invoice Already Existed**


There was some documentation regarding the transaction. An invoice was created before the interception of the consignment.


While an invoice is not sufficient, it can still serve as evidence that the supply was not totally hidden.


### **4. Procedural Lapse is Treated as Tax Evasion**


The evidence included:


- A phoney recipient.
- Concealed goods.
- Underreported value.
- Erroneous quantity.
- Repeated documents.
- An unregistered destination.


Because the evidence was missing, the Court ultimately decided that the penalty order was invalid.


## **What Relief did the Court Grant?**


The Supreme Court:


- Cancelled the penalty order issued on November 25, 2022.
- Cancelled appellate order was issued on March 24, 2023.
- Instructed the concerned agencies to return the fine and penalty money within 15 days.
- Ordered the payment of interest at the rate of 9% on any delayed payment.
- Levied costs of ₹1,000.
- Allowed to take recovery of costs from the official responsible.


## **Does Balaji Traders Make Mens Rea Mandatory in Every Section 129 Case?**


Not necessarily.


This ruling goes to show that legal support is available whenever the transaction is genuine, documents existed before interception, and the order imposing a fine does not contain any evidence-based finding on tax evasion.


The fact remains that Section 129 provides a solution for cases when the goods are being transported against GST requirements. The legal implications in such cases might differ based on:


- The applicable High Court jurisdiction.
- The date of the transaction.
- Statutory amendments.
- The seriousness of the discrepancy.
- Whether the invoice existed before interception took place.
- Whether tax was charged.
- Whether documents were created retrospectively.
- The final judgments passed on the issue thereafter.


The Balaji Traders case thus has to be interpreted as a fact-specific judicial protection against mechanical imposition of penalties and not as a green signal to the transporter to carry out transactions even without prescribed documents.


## **When can the Balaji Traders Judgment help a Taxpayer?**


A taxpayer can get help from a ruling if any of the following is true:


- An actual invoice existed before the shipment took place.
- The supplier and receiver could be identified.
- The goods matched what was written on the invoice.
- Tax was charged or accounted for.
- It was a procedural error or could be explained.
- There were no signs of hiding anything.
- The taxpayer has a clear and immediately presented excuse.
- The authority has disregarded relevant proof.
- The penalty order does not reveal any intent to avoid tax.


## **When May the Judgment Not Help?**


Balaji Traders may offer limited assistance where:


- No invoice existed before interception.
- Documents were generated only after the goods were detained.
- Goods differed materially from the invoice.
- Quantity or value was understated.
- The destination was different from the documents.
- The supplier or recipient was fictitious.
- The same e-way bill had been reused.
- Similar violations had occurred repeatedly.
- The taxpayer could not explain the discrepancy.

## **What Evidence can Prove the Absence of Tax-Evasion Intent?**


Businesses should preserve:


1. Tax invoices generated before dispatch.
2. Purchase orders and delivery instructions.
3. E-way bill generation records.
4. Portal screenshots and error messages.
5. Railway receipts or transporter documents.
6. Vehicle and driver details.
7. GST return entries.
8. Proof that tax was charged and accounted for.
9. Communication with the recipient or transporter.
10. Evidence showing that the goods matched the invoice.


Contemporaneous records are generally more persuasive than explanations created after detention.


## **What should Businesses do during Section 129 Proceedings?**


### **Respond to Every Allegation**


The notice reply should directly explain:


- Why the document was missing or incomplete.
- When the invoice was generated.
- When transportation commenced.
- Whether tax had already been charged.
- Whether the goods matched the invoice.
- Whether any portal problem occurred.
- Why the transaction created no risk of revenue loss.


### **Request a Reasoned Order**


The authority should consider the reply and supporting documents and provide reasons for accepting or rejecting them.


### **Preserve All Interception Records**


Retain copies of:


- Forms GST MOV-01 to MOV-07, as applicable.
- Driver statements.
- Inspection reports.
- Detention orders.
- Notices.
- Payment records.
- Appeal documents.


### **Strengthen Dispatch Controls**


Before goods leave the premises, businesses should verify:


- Invoice number and date.
- E-way bill validity.
- Vehicle number.
- Recipient GSTIN.
- Delivery location.
- Product description.
- Quantity and taxable value.


## **What the Balaji Traders Judgment Does Not Mean**


The decision does not mean that:


- An invoice automatically replaces an e-way bill.
- Goods can be moved before generating mandatory documents.
- Every procedural mistake must be ignored.
- Later document generation always cures non-compliance.
- Intent must be decided identically by every High Court.
- Businesses are protected where records are faked or backdated.


The judgment primarily reinforces the need for fair, evidence-based and reasoned penalty proceedings.


## **Conclusion**


The Allahabad High Court’s ruling in *Balaji Traders v. State of U.P.* shows that a Section 129 penalty should not be imposed mechanically without examining the taxpayer’s explanation, available documents and surrounding circumstances.


Where a genuine invoice exists, and there is no evidence of concealment or deliberate tax evasion, the absence of a recorded finding on intent can be an important ground for challenging the penalty.


Nevertheless, prevention remains safer than litigation. Businesses should ensure that valid invoices, e-way bills and transport documents are generated and available before the movement of goods begins.


## **Frequently Asked Questions**


### **1. Can a GST penalty be imposed when a valid invoice exists?**


Yes, because a valid invoice does not automatically replace other mandatory documents such as an e-way bill. However, the invoice may help prove that the transaction was genuine and already documented.


### **2. Is mens rea expressly required under Section 129?**


Section 129 does not expressly use the words “intent to evade tax” in the same manner as certain confiscation provisions. Courts have nevertheless considered intent relevant in several fact-specific penalty disputes.


### **3. What did the Balaji Traders judgment decide?**


The Allahabad High Court quashed the penalty because the authorities had not recorded any intention to evade tax and had failed to properly consider the taxpayer’s railway-booking explanation and documents.


### **4. Can a taxpayer generate an e-way bill after interception?**


Subsequent generation does not automatically cure the original violation. The evidentiary value depends on the timing, transaction records, and the reason for the delay.


### **5. Can Balaji Traders be cited outside Uttar Pradesh?**


It may be cited as persuasive authority, but its binding effect depends on the court or authority hearing the dispute.


### **6. What is the best defence against a Section 129 penalty?**


The strongest defence usually includes contemporaneous invoices, transport documents, tax records, portal-error evidence and a consistent explanation showing that the transaction was genuine.
