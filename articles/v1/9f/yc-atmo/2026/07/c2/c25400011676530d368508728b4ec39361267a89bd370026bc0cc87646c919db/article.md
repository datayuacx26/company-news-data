### Weather Forecasts Matter: A High-Stakes Component in Launch Commit Criteria


The process of preparing for a space launch is multi-faceted, with engineers and meteorologists working hand in hand to establish launch parameters. These guidelines, known as the Launch Commit Criteria, define the exact weather conditions in which a spacecraft can safely launch. Factors such as temperature, wind speed, lightning, humidity, cloud thickness, and even atmospheric electric charge are measured with high precision.


‍


### Case Study: High-Altitude Winds at Cape Canaveral


Cape Canaveral’s coastal location introduces unique weather dynamics that demand constant monitoring, especially for high-altitude winds. During a launch, upper-level winds, which may differ vastly from surface winds, can place stress on a rocket’s structure and trajectory. A powerful wind shear event at an altitude where a rocket is traveling at high speeds could compromise the rocket’s stability or even cause it to deviate from its flight path. In the past, launches have been scrubbed due to unfavorable winds at upper altitudes, a decision that protects the rocket from potentially catastrophic stress or misalignment.


‍


In one example, a launch team at Cape Canaveral had to delay a rocket launch due to strong upper-level winds blowing across the Atlantic. These winds, undetectable at the surface, had the potential to throw the rocket off course, threatening its safe ascent. By relying on advanced wind profiling, including models and on-the-ground sensors, meteorologists were able to prevent a dangerous scenario, choosing safety and mission integrity over pushing a launch that wasn’t weather-ready.
