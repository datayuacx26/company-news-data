# Adalo Pricing: Current Plans, Real Costs, and Best Alternatives in 2026

Adalo pricing is one of the more straightforward models in the no code app builder space-but "straightforward" doesn't mean there aren't surprises. Whether you're a solo founder testing an app idea or an operations team evaluating tools for a customer-facing mobile app, the sticker price is only part of the story. This guide breaks down every Adalo plan as of July 2026, maps the total cost drivers most teams miss, and explains when an alternative like Jet Admin is the smarter long-term bet.


## Key Takeaways


- As of July 2026, adalo pricing uses flat, tiered billing with no usage based billing or workload units. Adalo starts at $0 for a free sandbox tier, $36/month for Starter, $52/month for Professional, $160/month for Team, and $200/month for a Business plan-all prices reflect annual billing, which provides approximately 20% savings across all tiers.
- The main cost drivers are number of published apps, app editors, and whether you need production features like push notifications, third party integrations, or governance controls. Adalo's pricing model eliminates hidden costs associated with usage limits on paid plans, but external fees (Apple Developer, Google Play, payment processing) add up.
- Adalo shines as a fast path to native ios and android apps for non-technical builders. Its predictable pricing works well for small to mid-size projects and mobile-first MVPs, and its built in database means you can build apps without writing code or managing infrastructure.
- Key limitations include vendor lock-in (Adalo does not allow code export), performance ceilings for complex apps with many records, and limited governance versus dedicated business-app platforms. These gaps make it less ideal for complex internal tools or multi-role customer portals.
- For teams that need secure business apps and customer or partner portals on top of existing data,[Jet Admin](https://www.jetadmin.io/customer-portal) offers stronger governance (role-based access down to rows, columns, and actions), SSO/SAML/SCIM, audit logs, and cloud or self-hosted deployment-connecting directly to databases, APIs, and SaaS tools rather than requiring data migration.


## Adalo Pricing at a Glance (Checked July 2026)


How much does Adalo cost? As of July 2026, Adalo offers flat-rate pricing for no-code app development across five tiers-checked directly on[Adalo's public pricing page](https://www.adalo.com/pricing) . Here's the quick summary:


- **Free (Sandbox):** $0/month. Build unlimited test apps with unlimited screens, but no publishing. Database capped at 500 records per app.
- **Starter:** $36/month (annual). One published app, custom domain, web publishing, and app store publishing for native ios and android apps.
- **Professional:** $52/month (annual). Two published apps, push notifications, geolocation, custom integrations, and up to 5 editors.
- **Team:** $160/month (annual). Five published apps, 10 editors, priority support, version history, white labeling, and Xano/google sheets integrations.
- **Business:** $200/month (annual). Extended features for larger organizations with custom needs.


All paid plans advertise unlimited app actions, unlimited users, and unlimited storage capacities. The billing model is flat-rate per workspace-no per-user, per-action, or per-record metering on premium plans. Monthly billing runs about 20% higher than annual across all tiers.


Adalo includes a built-in relational database (hosted Postgres) for app data, which eliminates the need for a separate backend for simpler projects. That said, Adalo's pricing is positioned slightly higher than some other platforms because it bundles native app publishing and an integrated database into the subscription.


One thing to note: the[Apple Developer Program](https://developer.apple.com/programs/) costs $99/year and a[Google Play developer account](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/6112435) costs $25 as a one-time fee. These are mandatory for any app store and google Play publishing-not unique to Adalo.

## Free Plan: What You Really Get With Adalo's Sandbox Tier


Adalo's free plan is a perpetual sandbox-not a time-limited trial. It's designed for testing and learning, not for shipping production apps.


Here's what you actually get on the free tier:


- Unlimited test apps with unlimited screens and unlimited app actions
- Hosted Postgres database with up to 500 records per app (Adalo's free plan limits users to 500 records per app)
- Multi-screen drag and drop canvas for building your app structure
- Ada ai assisted building features (prompt-based editing and building)
- Mobile preview for testing on android devices and iOS


And here's what you don't get:


- No published app capability (no custom domain, no app store or google play builds)
- Adalo branding remains on all web previews
- No push notifications, no external integrations, no custom fonts
- Record cap makes real user feedback testing at scale nearly impossible


Adalo's free plan allows building apps but not publishing them, which means it's suited for validating your app idea, modeling data flows, learning the adalo app builder, and building internal prototypes. It is not suited for any customer-facing or production use.


The practical workflow: start on Free to design and test your adalo app, then upgrade to Starter or Professional once you're ready to publish.


## Starter Plan: Lowest-Cost Path to a Live Adalo App


Adalo's Starter plan costs $36 per month when billed annually as of July 2026-making it the entry point to build production apps. It supports one published app with unlimited records and actions.


Core features on Starter:


- Publish one web app and native iOS/Android app from a single adalo app
- Custom domain with SSL
- Removal of Adalo branding
- Custom fonts and basic app analytics
- Ada ai powered builder included
- Adalo allows publishing of native apps to the Apple App Store and Google Play Store


Main limits versus higher tiers:


- Only one published app (solo founders or single-project teams)
- Single app editor-no collaboration features
- No push notifications, no external data integrations, no advanced team tools


The starter plan is ideal for solo founders, side projects, simple customer-facing apps, or mobile-first MVPs like a booking app or lightweight service directory where you need one native app in the app stores.


**Cost in context:** First-year total with Starter runs roughly $550-$432 for the Adalo subscription, $99 for an apple developer account, and $25 for a google play developer account. Compare that to hiring developers for even a basic native app through traditional development, where costs typically start in the thousands. For straightforward mobile development projects, Adalo delivers significant savings without writing code.


## Professional and Team Plans: When Your Adalo App Needs to Grow Up

These plans are appropriate when you need production-grade mobile apps with push notifications and integrations for dozens to thousands of users, but don't yet require enterprise governance or code ownership.


## Total Cost of Ownership With Adalo: What Drives Your Real Spend


Subscription price is only part of adalo pricing. Teams should think in terms of total cost of ownership (TCO) over one to three years.


**Confirmed direct cost drivers:**


- Adalo plan tier: Free vs Starter vs Professional vs Team
- Number of published apps (additional apps cost $25/month each beyond plan limits)
- Additional app editors beyond plan base ($15/month per editor)
- Annual vs monthly billing (annual saves roughly 20%)


**External mandatory costs:**


- Apple Developer Program costs $99/year for app publishing on the apple app store
- Google Play registration costs $25 as a one-time fee
- App Store commissions on in app purchases (typically 15–30%)
- Payment processing fees (Stripe, etc.) for any transactional apps


**Likely extras that add up:**


- Adalo's hidden costs include third-party integrations and backend services like Xano, Airtable, or Firebase for complex apps
- Automation tools like Zapier or Make for workflows beyond Adalo's built-in logic
- Separate analytics, email, or logging services
- Paid marketplace components for advanced UI or functionality


**Hard costs vs soft costs:** The line items above are "hard" costs. Soft costs-builder time, learning curve, redesigns for performance, and potential migration if you outgrow Adalo-can dominate the overall budget for production apps. Since Adalo does not allow code export, a future platform change means a full rebuild, not a simple migration.


Adalo offers flat-rate pricing for no-code app development, and its pricing model eliminates hidden costs associated with usage limits. But the total picture always extends beyond the subscription.


## Limits, Scalability, and Hidden Trade-Offs in Adalo's Pricing Model


Adalo's flat, no-usage pricing is attractive, but it hides non-obvious trade-offs in performance, scale, and lock-in that affect long-term cost.


**Performance and data constraints:**


- Adalo has a 10,000 record limit per collection, which is an architectural constraint rather than a plan-based one. Performance issues arise on large projects with many records, particularly on progressive web apps and android apps where list rendering and filtering can slow significantly.
- Adalo processes approximately 20 million data requests daily across its platform, but individual app performance depends on how efficiently you structure data and queries.
- The adalo database works well for simple to moderate data models. For complex apps with deep relationships and heavy filtering, you'll hit ceilings that no plan upgrade can fix.


**Vendor lock-in:**


Adalo does not allow code export, which may lead to vendor lock-in. Moving off Adalo means rebuilding your app's UI and logic from scratch on another tool or in react native / custom code. Your data and domain can be reused, but the app itself cannot. This is a strategic and financial risk that belongs in any long-term budget.


**Governance and access control:**


Seats and editors are limited by plan. While Adalo doesn't charge per end-user, governance controls for complex organizations-SSO, SCIM, granular role-based access-are limited compared to specialized business app builders focused on internal tools or external users portals.


If you're treating an adalo app as a long-term product, plan for: performance optimization time, periodic refactors, and a possible future migration budget.


## Adalo vs Other No-Code App Builders: How Pricing Really Compares


Adalo sits in a crowded field of visual code app builder platforms, and differences in pricing models matter more than headline monthly costs.


**Adalo vs Bubble:** Bubble charges based on monthly subscriptions plus "Workload Units"-a usage-based billing model where overage fees can spike unpredictably. Adalo's flat pricing with unlimited app actions avoids this, but Bubble offers deeper web app customization and is web-first rather than mobile-first.


**Adalo vs FlutterFlow:** FlutterFlow targets builders comfortable closer to code, charges per editor, and supports code export (a major differentiator). Pricing can run higher for equivalent features, but you own the output.


**Adalo vs Glide / Softr:** Both skew toward web applications and dashboards rather than native app development. They may lack app store publishing entirely and can become more expensive for equivalent record or data usage at scale.


**Adalo vs Thunkable:** Thunkable also targets native mobile apps and uses a drag and drop components approach, but its pricing and feature set differ-particularly around database hosting and integrations.


Adalo's value is strongest for mobile-first projects that need app store and google play publishing from a single visual app builder. If your priority is web portals, data dashboards, or other platforms that emphasize web publishing over native mobile, other tools may serve you better.


The bottom line: "cheapest per month" isn't the same as "lowest TCO." Usage-based billing, mandatory external databases, or consultant dependency can make some alternatives more expensive as apps grow. Compare not just plan prices but included core features, data limits, support, and long-term roadmap.

## FAQ: Adalo Pricing, Plans, and Alternatives


These FAQs address common questions not fully covered in the sections above, focused on pricing decisions and practical trade-offs.


### Is Adalo really free to use, and what are the limits of the free plan?


Adalo offers a true free plan with no time limit-it's a perpetual sandbox. You can build unlimited test apps with unlimited screens and unlimited app actions, but you cannot publish any app to a custom domain or the app stores. The free plan caps you at 500 records per app and keeps Adalo branding on all web previews.


All serious production apps will eventually require at least the starter plan ($36/month annual) to remove branding, connect a custom domain, or ship to ios and android apps stores.


### Does Adalo charge per end-user, seat, or app actions?


No. Adalo's paid plans as of July 2026 use flat monthly pricing per workspace rather than per end-user, per action, or per API call. App actions and database records are effectively unlimited on paid tiers. This distinguishes Adalo from platforms like Bubble that use workload units and usage-based billing.


Seats (app editors) are limited by plan-1 on Starter, 5 on Professional, 10 on Team. You pay more if you need additional editors ($15/month each) or additional published apps ($25/month each), but not for raw app users counts.


### What extra costs should I budget for beyond Adalo's subscription?


Concrete recurring costs include:


- Apple Developer Program: $99/year for apple app store publishing
- Google Play registration: $25 one-time
- Payment processing fees (Stripe or equivalent) if your app handles transactions
- Domain registration if using a custom domain
- Any paid third-party services (Xano, Airtable, Zapier, analytics tools)


Don't forget soft costs: builder time, performance tuning as your app grows, and a potential future migration budget if the app outgrows Adalo's capabilities. Factor these into your long-term ROI analysis.


### Can I start on Adalo and later migrate my app if I outgrow it?


Adalo does not offer code export, so "migration" means rebuilding the app's UI and logic on another platform or in custom code. You can typically reuse your data and domain, but the app itself must be reconstructed.


This is feasible but can be costly depending on app complexity. If you're building a long-lived or mission-critical product, factor a future rebuild budget into your planning. Keep your data model documented and portable from day one to minimize friction.


### When is a business app builder like Jet Admin more cost-effective than Adalo?


For apps deeply tied to existing databases or SaaS tools-requiring fine-grained permissions, audit logs, and possibly self-hosted deployment-Jet Admin often delivers lower total cost than trying to force-fit those requirements into Adalo through workarounds and custom integrations.


Operations, customer-success, IT, and product teams building customer portals or internal tools should compare the cost of Adalo plus bolted-on integrations against Jet Admin's native[data connections and governance features](https://www.jetadmin.io/integrations) before making a long-term commitment. The right choice depends on whether your project is mobile-app-first or data-and-access-first.
