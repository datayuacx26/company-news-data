# Softr Pricing & Review (July 2026): Is It Right for Your Client Portals and Business Apps?

Softr has become one of the most popular no code platforms for building client portals, internal tools, and lightweight business apps. But is it the right fit for your team - and what does it actually cost once you factor in real users, data growth, and governance needs? This review breaks down current pricing, capabilities, and limitations to help you decide.


## Key Takeaways


This is a July 2026 Softr pricing and product review focused on business operations, client portals, and internal tools. Softr is a no code web applications builder that lets teams create custom web apps without coding, sitting on top of existing data sources such as Airtable and Google Sheets.


- **Current tiers:** Free ($0), Basic (~$49/mo annual, $59/mo monthly), Professional (~$139/mo annual, $167/mo monthly), Business (~$269/mo annual, $323/mo monthly), and Enterprise (custom pricing). Each tier increases user caps, record limits, and feature access.
- **Total-cost drivers that matter most:** number of authenticated end users, data records per app, AI credit consumption, need for custom domain and white-labeling, and security or governance requirements like SSO and audit logs.
- **Softr excels at** fast, simple no code client portals and membership sites for small to medium audiences. It struggles when teams need granular control over sensitive data, complex workflows, or enterprise-grade compliance.
- **Jet Admin is typically the better fit** when teams need secure, governed customer portals and internal apps directly on top of databases, APIs, Airtable, or Google Sheets - with role-based access down to rows, columns, and actions.


## What Is Softr and How Do Teams Use It in 2026?


Softr is an AI-native no code platform designed for rapid application development without the need for hiring traditional developers. It lets business teams describe what they need in plain English, and Softr's AI co builder generates the application structure - pages, user management, and basic logic - automatically. From there, Softr's drag-and-drop interface enables non-technical users to build applications including CRMs, project trackers, knowledge bases, and custom portals.


Since its founding in 2018, Softr powers over 1 million builders worldwide. Recognizable brands like Netflix, Google, and Stripe are publicly referenced as users, though the exact scope of their usage isn't always disclosed. Teams use Softr to run internal and client-facing systems supporting business operations across industries.


Softr apps are hosted on Softr's cloud, reachable under a Softr subdomain or a custom domain on paid plans, and accessed by authenticated users with roles and permissions. Core building blocks include a visual builder with pre-built drag and drop blocks, basic logic and workflows, templates, and direct integrations with data sources like[Airtable and Google Sheets](https://docs.softr.io/how-to-guides/how-to-create-a-client-portal?utm_source=openai) . Softr is usually adopted first by operations, customer-success, and product teams that need to stand up self-service portals without waiting for engineering.


## Current Softr Pricing Overview (Checked July 2026)


All pricing details here are checked as of July 2026. SaaS pricing changes frequently, so verify on[Softr's official pricing page](https://docs.softr.io/workspace-and-billing/pricing-and-plans?utm_source=openai) before committing.


Softr offers a free plan for small projects alongside four paid tiers. Monthly billing prices are higher than annual; the annual discount works out to roughly two months free. Pricing increases with features and user base on Softr - here's the current monthly billing structure:


- **Free:** $0/month - 10 users, 1,000 records per app
- **Basic:** $59/month (starts at approximately $49/month on annual billing) - 20 users, 50,000 records per app
- **Professional:** $167/month (~$139/month annual) - 100 users, 100,000 records per app
- **Business:** $323/month (~$269/month annual) - 500 users, 200,000 records per app
- **Enterprise:** Custom pricing - tailored limits, SSO, audit logs, dedicated support


What changes across tiers: allowed number of users, records per app and per database base, custom domain support, removal of Softr branding, more advanced user permissions, improved performance, support response times, and AI credit allotments.


Conceptually, this structure differs from tools like Bubble or Webflow (which emphasize visitors or design environments) and from platforms like Jet Admin, which tie pricing more directly to governance features, environments, and data source connections rather than record caps.


## Detailed Softr Pricing Tiers and Who They're For


Here's a structured walkthrough of each plan, aimed at buyers comparing options for client portals and internal business tools.


**Free Plan ($0/month)**


- 10 total users (team + client), 1,000 records per app, 1,000 records per Softr Database base, 5 AI credits/month
- Community and documentation support only
- Softr branding displayed; no custom domain; no SSO or audit logging
- Ideal for: freelancers prototyping a single client portal, consultants testing ideas, or startups validating a concept with fewer than 10 real users


**Basic Plan ($59/month, ~$49 annual)**


- 20 users, 50,000 records per app, 10,000 records per base, 10 AI credits/month
- Standard email support
- Still limited branding control; no SSO; custom domain may be restricted
- Ideal for: small business teams running a single client portal or internal dashboard with moderate data


**Professional Plan ($167/month, ~$139 annual)**


- 100 users (scalable to 250 via add-ons at $10 per 10 users/month), 100,000 records per app, 50,000 records per base, 50 AI credits/month
- Custom domain and branding removal available; improved support
- SSO and advanced governance still reserved for Enterprise
- Ideal for: growing agencies deploying multiple portals, or teams where branding and a larger user base matter. This is the professional plan where most scaling companies land first.


**Business Plan ($323/month, ~$269 annual)**


- 500 users, 200,000 records per app and per base, 100 AI credits/month
- Elevated support, better performance
- Enterprise security features (workspace SSO, audit logs, IP allowlisting) still mostly require Enterprise
- Ideal for: mid-sized companies with several custom portals, larger internal tools, or substantial data volumes


**Enterprise (Custom Pricing)**


- SSO for apps and Softr Studio, audit logs, workspace permissions, MFA, IP allowlisting, dedicated support, onboarding, compliance agreements
- Ideal for: regulated industries, large enterprises, or agencies needing governed deployment across multiple workspaces


## What You Really Pay For: Softr Total-Cost Drivers


Headline Softr prices rarely reflect total operating cost. The real spend is driven by how many users access your apps, how much data flows through them, and how quickly your governance needs outgrow your current tier.


**Confirmed, public cost drivers:**


- **Authenticated end users.** Every plan caps total users. On the professional plan, add-on packs cost $10/month per 10 users, maxing at 250. Exceeding limits forces an upgrade.
- **Record and data limits.** Each tier caps records per app and per Softr Database base. If your Airtable or Google Sheets data grows steadily, you'll hit thresholds that push you into higher tiers.
- **AI credits.** Monthly allotments range from 5 (Free) to 100 (Business). AI features like Ask AI, database agents, and summaries all consume credits. Overages require purchasing add-on packs.
- **Security and governance.** SSO, audit logs, and workspace permissions are Enterprise-only. If compliance is a requirement, you'll jump straight to custom pricing.
- **Custom domain and white-labeling.** Removing Softr branding and mapping your own domain require Professional or higher.


**Interpretive cost drivers:**


- Running multiple workspaces for different clients or divisions multiplies your plan costs.
- "Shadow costs" are real: time spent working around limitations, redesigning apps that outgrow Softr's database or logic model, or eventually migrating to more flexible tools like[Jet Admin](https://www.jetadmin.io/customer-portal) or a custom stack.
- Unlike platforms that price mainly by internal seats (traditional SaaS) or by environments and governance features, Softr's model penalizes data and user growth - which is exactly what success looks like for a portal.


## Softr Feature Review: Strengths for Client Portals and Business Operations


For the most common use cases - client portals, vendor portals, membership areas, and basic internal dashboards - Softr delivers a genuinely useful set of capabilities.


Softr's visual, block-based no code builder and its AI co builder let users describe an app in plain language and get a working scaffold within hours. AI assistance speeds up app setup in Softr considerably: Softr's AI Co-Builder connects databases and applications automatically, making it possible for non-technical operations teams to build client portals and internal dashboards quickly. Users can launch new functionalities within hours using Softr, enabling rapid development of prototypes and MVPs in hours or days rather than weeks.


Building on existing data is a core strength. Softr integrates with Airtable and Google Sheets for data management, meaning you can use existing spreadsheets as the backend for your app. Softr allows seamless data connection without migrating data - data updates in Softr applications reflect automatically when connected to supported data sources.


Key strengths for business operations:


- Softr provides a library of templates for app building, including CRM-lite tools, project trackers, and client portals
- Softr allows defining user roles and access controls for applications, with built-in user authentication and role-based content visibility
- Applications built with Softr can function as Progressive Web Apps (PWAs), supporting mobile devices
- Softr automates workflows using integrated tools and can connect to third-party automation services
- Softr includes built-in security features such as user authentication and data compliance standards, including[SOC 2 Type II certification](https://www.softr.io/blog/soc2-certification?utm_source=openai) as of July 2026
- Reasonable branding and design customization for non-designers - the learning curve is minimal


Softr's own database option can reduce dependence on Airtable or Sheets, which is attractive for teams wanting to consolidate complex data. All of this makes Softr a great product for SMBs, startups, and lean teams that want to avoid full custom development and don't require sophisticated backend logic.


## Limitations and Risks: Where Softr Struggles for Serious Operations


For teams relying on client portals and internal tools as mission-critical systems, Softr has meaningful gaps.


**Reliability and support concerns:** Softr has experienced repeated service issues and outages. Customer support responses can be delayed or absent, particularly on lower-tier plans. Softr's notification system for usage limits is unreliable - users report inconsistent platform behavior with sudden functionality loss when thresholds are hit without warning. For business operations where portal downtime affects clients directly, this risk is significant.


**Architectural constraints:**


- Block-based layouts can feel restrictive for complex projects requiring sophisticated UX
- Limited ability to implement complex business logic, multi-step approvals, or deeply nested workflows - no code required doesn't mean every workflow is possible
- Performance issues arise when working with very large datasets, especially when syncing from Airtable or Google Sheets at scale; UI sluggishness increases with record volume and sync frequency


**Governance and security gaps:**


- No clear dev/test/prod environment separation on public plans
- Audit logging is mostly Enterprise-only
- SSO/SAML for both apps and the builder (Studio) requires Enterprise pricing
- Builder permissions for who can publish apps are less granular outside Enterprise


Softr is generally good enough for non-critical portals and internal dashboards, but may not meet the reliability, compliance, and support standards required in regulated or large-scale environments. Companies handling sensitive customer data or running complex operations should evaluate whether these limitations create unacceptable risk.


## Softr vs Alternatives for Client Portals and Internal Tools


Most buyers compare Softr with both other no code builders and more governed internal tool platforms. The right choice depends on what you're optimizing for.


**Softr vs pure no code app builders (Bubble, Webflow with memberships):** Softr often wins on speed for simple portals built on top of Airtable or Google Sheets. Tools like Bubble or Webflow may offer deeper design freedom or custom logic but require more technical skills and carry a steeper learning curve. Softr is more user friendly for non-developers who just need a functional portal.


**Softr vs database-centric internal tool builders (Jet Admin):** Softr optimizes for quick, no code site-like apps with a flexible visual interface. Jet Admin focuses on secure, data-connected business applications for internal and external users - connecting directly to databases, APIs, and SaaS tools with deeper governance out of the box.


**When off-the-shelf SaaS is better:** If your needs map neatly to standard CRM, helpdesk, or project management software and you don't need a custom portal, dedicated SaaS is often simpler. Softr and similar builders shine when standard software is too rigid or when per-seat SaaS pricing is too expensive for large numbers of external clients.


The decision comes down to complexity of business logic, data volume, governance requirements, and whether your primary pain is "we need a portal now" versus "we need a long-term, governed operations platform."

## FAQ: Softr Pricing, Limits, and Alternatives


These FAQs address common questions that don't fit neatly into the main narrative above.


### How does Softr's Free plan compare to paid tiers for real-world projects?


The Free plan supports up to 10 users and 1,000 records per app - sufficient for internal prototypes or a very small test client portal. Teams typically outgrow it when they need a custom domain, removal of Softr branding, more than 10 authenticated users, or higher record limits. For anything beyond a proof of concept, the Basic plan is the practical minimum.


### Can Softr handle large datasets from Airtable and Google Sheets?


Performance degrades as record counts climb, especially with frequent syncing. Notion data sources are capped at 100,000 records per app on public plans. For large datasets, Softr's native database or connecting to SQL/PostgreSQL sources performs better. If your data needs are substantial, a database-centric tool like Jet Admin - which connects natively to PostgreSQL, MySQL, BigQuery, and more - may be the more scalable solution.


### Is Softr secure enough for customer data and client portals?


Softr completed a SOC 2 Type II audit in July 2026 and supports HTTPS, user authentication, and basic role-based permissions on all plans. However, enterprise-grade needs like SSO/SAML, detailed audit logs, and workspace-level permissions require the Enterprise tier. The platform's security posture is credible, but securing an app still depends on the builder configuring permissions correctly.


### What hidden costs should I plan for when budgeting Softr?


Budget for upgrades triggered by user growth and record accumulation, AI credit overages, the cost of workarounds for missing features (multi-step approvals, advanced logic), and potential migration costs if you later move to Jet Admin or custom development. Support SLAs on lower plans are limited, which can create indirect costs when issues arise.


### When should I switch from Softr to Jet Admin or another tool?


Common breaking points include: needing complex workflows or multi-step approvals, strict compliance requirements (audit logs, identity federation), aggregating data from multiple databases in a single app, and requiring fine-grained permissions or extensive automations tied into existing systems. If your Softr app is becoming a patchwork of workarounds, it's time to evaluate a more governed platform.
