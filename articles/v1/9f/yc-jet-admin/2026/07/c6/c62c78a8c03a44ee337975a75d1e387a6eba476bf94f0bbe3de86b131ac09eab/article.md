Most costs are driven by active users, record limits, workflow runs, and extra seats rather than just the base subscription. ## How Noloco Pricing Works in July 2026


Pricing checked: July 2026. Noloco offers subscription-based pricing for internal tools and client portals, blending bundle tiers with usage-based elements like active users, records, workflow runs, and integrations.


Public information shows multiple pricing generations. Older per-user pricing charged $29–$49 per user per month. Newer app-based plans run approximately $99–$319 per month and include bundled team and client seats, with a free plan introduced after late 2022. Noloco includes internal team seats and external client seats in its pricing model, each counted separately.


An active app user is anyone with at least one session during the monthly billing cycle. This includes both team members and external client-facing users, which is critical for cost forecasting. Noloco charges only for active users, so dormant accounts do not generate fees.


Each paid tier includes a set number of team seats and client seats. Going beyond those bundles triggers per-seat overages, typically $4–$10 per extra team user and around $1 per extra client user depending on the plan. Exact prices and caps change over time, so the ranges and examples below are drawn from[Noloco's pricing page](https://noloco.io/pricing) and official documentation as of mid-2026.

## Key Limits That Drive Total Noloco Cost


Sticker price is only part of the total cost. Actual spend scales with active users, database records, workflows, and how many environments a team and clients require.


Noloco charges fees for additional users beyond the limits of each plan. Team seats vs client seats are counted separately: a person active across multiple apps under the same app builder workspace is typically counted once per billing cycle. Overages run +$4–$10 per extra team member and +$1 per extra client user on most pricing tiers. Different users accessing the same app all count toward the plan limits.


Noloco supports real-time data syncing from Airtable and Google Sheets, as well as from external data sources like SQL and HubSpot on higher tiers. You can sync Noloco with other databases, but synced row caps (25k–100k depending on plan) mean large data source volumes can force upgrades. Exceeding record limits may trigger add-on charges-roughly $75 per 25,000 extra rows on Business-like packages, according to[Noloco's pricing documentation](https://guides.noloco.io/account/pricing) .


Automation features are capped: the Business plan includes around 10,000 workflow runs per month, while the starter plan offers far fewer. Automation-heavy apps-think client notifications, status triggers, invoice reminders-can hit ceilings quickly.


Governance features like custom domains, removal of Noloco branding, role based access, field level permissions, and user authentication via SSO are gated to Pro, Business, or Enterprise. Even if your user and record counts fit a lower pricing tier, these requirements may force a higher plan. Noloco provides granular role-based permissions for user access, but accessing them means paying for advanced features on Pro or above. User permissions and user role configurations differentiate what each person can see and do within custom apps, and misconfiguring a user role can inadvertently classify a client as a team member-raising costs.


## Is Noloco Worth It? Evaluating Value for Different Teams


When is Noloco worth its price versus other tools? The answer depends on your team size, data volume, and how many external users touch your apps.


For small teams and solo builders, the free plan and starter plan ($39–$149/month) are attractive for lean internal tools and simple client portals. Noloco makes it straightforward for non developers to build apps, create web apps, and publish your app to your team without writing code. However, Noloco is designed for internal business tools only and does not support publishing to app stores, so teams needing mobile distribution should look elsewhere.


For growing businesses and agencies, Noloco's client portals, branding options, and bundled client seats are compelling. You can build internal tools, create powerful apps, and let clients access all their documents in the same app. But as your business grows, active users, records, and workflow volume increase-so buyers must model long-term usage carefully. Noloco offers granular role-based permissions to control access across different users and roles.


For larger organizations, Business and Enterprise plans add field level permissions, SSO, 2FA, white label branding, and dedicated support. These justify higher spend when governance matters. But per-active-user pricing can become expensive at thousands of users.


Switching costs matter too. Building on Noloco tables vs external databases affects lock-in. Use the 14-day free trial and the free tier to prototype one high-value workflow, measure actual active users and records for a month, and compare projected cost against time saved.

## How to Forecast Your Noloco Bill Before You Commit


Here is a step-by-step approach for estimating Noloco's total monthly cost using your own data.


1. Map user types and volumes. List your internal team members, external clients, and occasional guests. Estimate monthly active users based on who will have at least one session per billing cycle. Map them to Noloco's definitions of team seats and client seats.
2. Estimate record and sync volume. Check current row counts in Airtable, Google Sheets, SQL, or CRMs. Add 12–24 months of growth projections. Align totals with Noloco's stated record and synced-row bands on the billing page for each plan.
3. Count workflows and automations. List triggers like "new deal created" or "invoice overdue," estimate monthly runs, and compare to workflow-run allocations to avoid surprise overages.
4. Include governance and branding requirements. Features like custom domain, SSO, 2FA, field level permissions, or white label branding may force a higher tier even if you are under user and record caps.
5. Compare with alternatives. Build a simple spreadsheet where the same user, record, and workflow assumptions are priced out for Noloco and at least one alternative such as Jet Admin to see where long-term TCO diverges.


Use Noloco's 14-day free trial or free plan to validate these assumptions in practice before committing to annual billing.


## FAQs About Noloco Pricing and Plans


These FAQs address common pricing questions that often are not fully covered on plan comparison tables.


### Does Noloco have a free plan and a free trial?


Yes. Noloco offers both a permanent free plan and a 14-day free trial of the Business plan without a credit card required. The free plan has practical limits-2,000 records, 3 team seats, 7 client seats, and 100 workflow runs per month-so most production teams eventually move to paid plans. The free trial lets you test advanced features before deciding on a pricing tier.


### How does Noloco count active users across multiple apps?


A person who is active in several apps under the same workspace is typically counted once per billing cycle as a single active user. However, team training on exact counting rules matters-always confirm in the latest documentation because user role classification (team member vs client) affects per-seat charges differently.


### What happens if I exceed my record or workflow limits on Noloco?


Noloco can either charge overages automatically or require a plan upgrade. For example, additional records on Business-level plans cost roughly $75 per 25,000-row block, and extra workflow runs cost about $50 per 1,000 runs. Users typically receive usage notifications via email and in-app alerts before large charges appear on the billing page.


### Can I start on a lower Noloco plan and upgrade later without rebuilding?


Yes. Noloco is designed to let you move between plans without rewriting your apps. Your data, layouts, and configurations carry forward. However, certain governance or branding changes-like enabling a custom domain or configuring SSO-may require configuration updates when you move to a higher tier.


### When does it make sense to switch from Noloco to another tool like Jet Admin?


Consider switching when your data already lives in SQL, Postgres, or other databases and you want to build web apps on that existing stack without duplicating data into Noloco tables. Jet Admin connects directly to your data source and can be more cost-effective at very large user counts or when you need tighter integration with other tools and APIs. If you need to publish to app stores, Noloco does not support that-an app store-focused platform would be a better fit.
