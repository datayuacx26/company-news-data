# Bolt.new Pricing: Plans, Tokens, and Real Costs in 2026

If you're evaluating bolt.new pricing for your next project, the sticker price is only part of the story. Bolt.new is an AI web and app builder that uses a token based pricing model, meaning your actual bolt cost depends on how you use the platform, not just which plan you pick. Here's what you need to know before committing.


## Key Takeaways


- As of July 2026, Bolt.new lists four main tiers: Free, Pro, Teams, and Enterprise. Bolt offers a freemium model with a free plan and several paid tiers, all metered by tokens.
- Bolt offers a free plan with 1M tokens per month. The Pro plan costs $25 per month for 10M tokens. The Teams plan costs $30 per member per month with 10M tokens. Enterprise plans have custom pricing based on needs.
- Your real bolt cost is driven by both your subscription cost and your token usage - larger projects and heavier AI features burn through bolt tokens faster than simple prototypes.
- Free and lower tiers work well for building small personal experiments and light prototyping, but serious ai app builder workloads and collaboration push you toward paid plans.
- This article also compares total cost of ownership against alternatives and covers when a platform like Jet Admin may fit better for secure, long-lived business apps.

## Free Plan: What You Can Actually Build on Bolt.new


Bolt's free plan is designed for students, hobbyists, early-stage founders, and anyone evaluating ai powered app development platforms. It's a permanent free tier, not a time-limited trial.


What you get on the free plan:


- A browser-based ai app builder with access to AI code generation
- Ability to create both public and private projects
- Free plan includes 1 million tokens per month with a 300,000-token daily cap
- Website hosting with bolt cloud (limited to ~10 GB bandwidth and ~333,333 requests/month)
- File uploads up to ~10 MB


Key limitations include visible bolt branding on hosted sites, no custom domains, and no advanced collaboration or governance features. Free plan tokens do not roll over - unused tokens expire at the end of each calendar month.


Bolt tokens get consumed by every AI interaction: writing code, refactoring, syncing your project's file system. Even reading existing project files burns tokens, so as project size grows, each AI action costs more tokens. A simple landing page might stay within limits comfortably, but complex projects will hit ceilings fast.


Most users feel the push to upgrade when they're hitting token limits daily, need to remove bolt branding, or want custom domains for client-facing work.


## Pro and Teams Plans: Core Paid Options for Serious Builders


The pro plan is the go-to for solo developers and indie hackers who want more tokens and fewer restrictions. The teams plan targets agencies, startups, and engineering groups that need shared workspaces and admin controls.


**Pro plan highlights:**


- Bolt's Pro plan costs $25/month for 10M tokens - a substantial jump from the free tier's 1M tokens
- The pro plan offers 10 million tokens per month with no daily limit
- Custom domains, removal of bolt branding, and larger file uploads (~100 MB)
- Advanced ai features including image editing and faster model access
- Unused tokens on paid plans roll over for one additional month - tokens roll into the next billing cycle, valid for up to two months total as long as you maintain an active paid subscription


**Teams plan highlights:**


- Bolt's Teams plan costs $30/member/month with 10M tokens per seat
- Each member gets their own monthly token allotment - tokens are not pooled across team members
- Bolt teams includes centralized billing, role-based access controls, and admin permissions
- Support for private NPM registries and design system consistency


The practical difference matters: if your team has a mix of heavy and light users, some members will burn through their allotment while others barely touch theirs. You can't shift unused tokens between team members.


When deciding between Pro and Teams, consider this: sharing a single Pro account across multiple people violates terms of service, breaks audit logs, and creates security risks. If more than one person regularly needs to interact with the AI, go directly to Teams.


To model your monthly cost on Teams, multiply $30 by your number of team members, then add a buffer for occasional reload tokens if anyone expects heavy usage spikes.


## Enterprise and Bolt Teams at Scale


Companies move from Teams to bolt enterprise when they need SSO, granular permissions, audit logs, security reviews, data residency options, or custom SLAs. Procurement processes that require contracts and compliance documentation also force the Enterprise conversation.


Enterprise bolt plans typically include:


- Custom token pools significantly larger than standard tiers
- SAML SSO, SCIM provisioning, and audit trails
- A dedicated account manager and priority support
- Compliance support and documentation (SOC 2, GDPR readiness where advertised)
- Flexible billing options including annual contracts with minimum commitments


One[analyst report](https://sacra-pdfs.s3.us-east-2.amazonaws.com/bolt-new.pdf) from early 2026 estimates an annual base minimum of around $100,000 plus approximately $150 per seat plus roughly $0.01 per token overage for large enterprise contracts. These figures are illustrative - your actual quote will depend on negotiation.


At scale, bolt teams requires careful management: multiple workspaces, internal billing codes, cost centers, and monitoring token burn across many builders and projects. Technical evaluators should ask specific questions during enterprise negotiations: token overage pricing, data retention policies, backup and disaster recovery, sandbox vs production environments, and whether any on-prem or private-cloud deployment option exists.


## How Bolt Tokens Work (and Why They Drive Your Real Bolt Cost)


Bolt tokens are sub-word units used to pay for AI model input and output - similar to how other ai app builders meter usage. Tokens are the currency for generating AI code in Bolt. Every prompt you send, every code block generated, and every context sync of your project files consumes tokens.


The main token consumption drivers inside Bolt.new:


- Chat prompts to the coding agent
- AI code generations and refactors
- Context syncing - where your entire or partial codebase is sent to the model for understanding
- Additional ai features like plan mode discussions or image editing


Token consumption scales with project complexity and codebase size. A small, static site might use a few thousand tokens per iteration. A complex full-stack app with dozens of components can consume hundreds of thousands of tokens in a single request because the model needs to ingest substantial context. This is where most token usage accumulates - not from the prompts you type, but from the project files the system reads behind the scenes.


This differs from credit-based systems. In token systems, longer prompts and larger context windows cost more per interaction. Credit-based competitors typically charge one credit per AI call regardless of size, making costs scale more predictably but potentially limiting response depth.


**Token-saving strategies:**


- Keep prompts focused and specific rather than open-ended
- Archive or split very large projects to reduce context size
- Clean unused files from your project's file system - they still get pulled into context
- Use modes that don't rescan the full codebase when making small changes


## Total Cost of Ownership: Beyond the List Price


Total cost of ownership for an ai app builder includes more than subscription and token costs. You need to factor in developer time, third party services, and operational overhead across the lifecycle of your app.


Key TCO components for Bolt.new:


- **Subscription fees:** per seat (Teams/Enterprise) or flat monthly (Pro)
- **Token costs:** base allotment plus potential reload tokens or overage charges
- **Bundled infrastructure:** Bolt cloud includes hosting and databases, but external components like auth providers, email services, payment processors, and observability tools add their own monthly invoices
- **Environments:** staging vs production deployments, preview environments for reviews - each generates additional AI and infrastructure usage
- **Developer productivity:** if recurring AI errors force multiple retries, each retry burns tokens and time; production ready code on the first pass saves both
- **Governance overhead:** unlocking SSO, audit logs, or advanced access controls requires Enterprise pricing, plus internal compliance review time


A simple TCO framework: (seats × monthly plan price) + (estimated extra tokens × overage rate) + (external SaaS and infrastructure costs) + (internal labor hours for setup, monitoring, and governance). Run this calculation before committing to any annual plan.


## Bolt.new vs Other AI App Builders on Pricing


Most buyers evaluating bolt.new pricing are also looking at alternatives. Understanding pricing model differences matters more than comparing sticker prices.


**Solo builder scenarios:**


- Bolt's Pro plan costs $25/month for 10M tokens - your effective cost per interaction depends on project size and prompt complexity
- Lovable's Pro plan also starts at $25/month but uses credits, where each AI call costs one credit regardless of context size
- Base44's entry plan is cheaper at $20/month for 100 messages - simpler metering but with a hard cap on interactions
- On Bolt, complex apps consume more tokens per action, meaning heavy users may need more tokens despite similar subscription prices


**Small team scenarios (5 people):**


- Bolt Teams at $30/member/month = $150/month for the team, with each member getting their own 10M tokens
- Some competitors offer a single shared plan at $50–$100/month with pooled credits across unlimited users
- Per-seat billing on Bolt means your costs scale linearly with headcount - adding one person always adds $30/month
- Credit-pooling competitors can be cheaper for teams with uneven usage, but may throttle heavy users


For larger projects, token-based systems like Bolt can see costs scale unpredictably as codebases grow, while credit-based systems offer more predictable per-interaction pricing. Neither model is universally better - the right choice depends on whether your usage pattern involves many small interactions or fewer, very large context calls.

## FAQs About Bolt.new Pricing and Tokens


Answers reflect information available as of July 2026. Confirm details on Bolt.new's official documentation before making purchasing decisions.


### Do unused Bolt tokens expire or roll over?


On paid plans, unused tokens roll over for one additional month - meaning they're valid for up to two months total. However, this requires maintaining an active paid subscription; canceling or downgrading can forfeit rollover balances. On the free plan, tokens reset on the 1st of each calendar month with no rollover. If your usage is bursty rather than steady, rollover can meaningfully reduce your effective token costs on a paid subscription.


### Can my team share one Pro account instead of using Bolt Teams?


Sharing a single Pro account across multiple people typically violates Bolt's terms of service, breaks audit trails, and creates security risks. If multiple people regularly need AI access in the bolt dashboard, use the teams plan so each user has their own identity, permissions, and token tracking. The per-member billing exists specifically for this reason.


### What happens if I go over my monthly Bolt token allowance?


Depending on current policies, AI usage may pause until your next billing cycle reset, or you may be offered the option to purchase reload tokens at an additional cost. The exact overage mechanics - whether usage is blocked or billed automatically - should be confirmed in Bolt's billing FAQs. Test one billing cycle under realistic load before committing to an annual plan to understand how quickly you approach your limits.


### Is the Bolt.new free plan really free forever?


Bolt markets its free tier as an ongoing offering, but like most SaaS products, it reserves the right to change limits or eligibility at any time. There is no contractual guarantee the free plan stays unchanged forever. Teams running important apps should never rely solely on the free tier - budget for a paid plan or have a migration path ready if free limits shrink further.


### Can existing users keep older Bolt pricing after increases?


Forum discussions have referenced "grandfathered" users retaining older Pro prices after increases, but this is a policy decision Bolt can revise at any time. Grandfathering may depend on maintaining an uninterrupted paid subscription - canceling and resubscribing could reset you to current rates. Confirm grandfathering, renewal, and upgrade/downgrade rules directly with Bolt support before building a long-term budget around legacy pricing.
