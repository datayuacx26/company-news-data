Trying to pin down what you will actually pay for a Glide app? You are not alone. Glide pricing has changed multiple times, and the numbers you find across the web often contradict each other. This guide cuts through the noise with verified plan details, real cost drivers, and a practical comparison with alternatives-including Jet Admin-so you can make a confident decision for your next internal tool or customer portal.


## Key Takeaways


This guide is based on Glide's public pricing page, checked in July 2026. Glide's published pricing currently ranges from a free plan through several paid plans up to a custom-priced enterprise tier. Here is what matters most before you commit:


- The main cost drivers are not just the list price. How many signed-in users you have, which data sources you connect (especially external data sources like Google Sheets or SQL), and how many monthly updates your apps generate will determine your real bill.
- Glide's free plan is best treated as a sandbox for personal use and learning. The Explorer, Maker, Business, and Enterprise plans are designed for production business apps, each with different plan limits on users, updates, and data rows.

## Quick Answer: How Much Does Glide Cost in 2026?


Pricing checked on Glide's official site on July 16, 2026. Glide's current pricing plan lineup includes five tiers: Free, Explorer, Maker, Business, and Enterprise. Glide's setup encourages scalability from personal to enterprise applications, but the sticker price is only the starting point.


- **Free plan:** $0/month. Best for learning, prototyping, and personal projects-limited to one app with restricted updates and features.
- **Explorer plan:** Starts around $19/month with annual billing ($25/month if billed monthly). Aimed at individuals testing a published app with a small audience.
- **Maker plan:** Around $49/month billed annually. Glide's Maker Plan costs $60/month for individuals paying month-to-month, with limited app updates. Suits solo builders creating multiple published apps.
- **Business plan:** Approximately $199/month billed annually ($249/month on monthly billing). Built for teams needing unlimited apps, more users, and deeper integrations. Some sources reference a legacy pricing of $310/month for larger organizations; always verify against the current plan.
- **Enterprise plan:** Custom pricing (sales-quoted). The Enterprise Plan pricing is customized based on usage needs, with some historical references suggesting it starts at $750/month.


The base price on every paid tier covers a defined number of signed-in users and a monthly pool of updates. Going beyond these is where many teams see higher-than-expected bills. Monthly costs in Glide are influenced by the type of plan and user requirements, so a simple headline number rarely tells the full story.

## Inside Each Glide Plan: Limits, Pros, and Tradeoffs


This section breaks down each plan beyond the marketing bullets, focusing on practical limits that affect real deployments.


### Free Plan


Glide's free plan allows users to create one app with limited features. It is suitable for personal projects and learning purposes-think sandbox, not production. Limits include:


- One published app with Glide branding
- Up to 25,000 data rows via Glide Tables
- Community support only; no custom domain
- No workflow automations or external integrations


Glide requires a database before building apps, and on the free plan that database is limited to Glide Tables. This is rarely enough for customer-facing or internal production tools.


### Explorer Plan


At ~$19/month (annual billing), Explorer gives you one published app, 250 monthly updates, and support for Glide AI. You get two editors and up to 100 personal users. It is a step up for solo builders who want to ship a web app to a small audience but do not need advanced features.


### Maker Plan


The Maker Plan costs $60/month for individuals on monthly billing (~$49 with annual billing) and offers 5 GB of storage. You get three published apps, unlimited personal users, and 500 monthly updates. Custom domains and custom branding become available here, making it the entry point for polished apps.


Tradeoff: 500 updates can be consumed quickly if your app relies on external data sources or frequent automations.


### Team Plan


Some references cite a team plan at $125/month for companies, allowing real-time collaboration with up to 5 editors. This tier supports more powerful data sources (Google Sheets, Airtable, Excel) and makes sense for small teams building internal tools or lightweight customer portals. Availability of this tier should be confirmed on Glide's current pricing page.


### Business and Enterprise Plans


The Business Plan includes 500 GB of data and priority support. It ships with 30 included business email users; each additional user beyond that costs roughly $5/month with annual billing ($6/month on monthly billing). Update limits jump to 5,000/month, and you get unlimited apps plus support for advanced features like express support and more data source types.


Business plans include a specific number of users with options for additional users, making them suitable for a 40-seat internal field operations app or a growing customer portal.


The Enterprise Plan pricing is customized based on usage needs and unlocks SSO, dedicated account management, SQL and BigQuery data sources, and higher row limits. Enterprise plans are the only tier with custom terms for governance-heavy deployments.


## How Glide Measures Usage: Updates, Users, and Data Sources


Glide's pricing is not just about seats. Updates and how you use data sources can be just as important in your total cost.


An "update" is consumed when a workflow runs, when external data sources (like Google Sheets or Airtable) sync, when the Glide AI processes a request, or when any third-party API call fires. Updates are not the same as page views, sessions, or app opens. Glide supports multiple data sources including Google Sheets and Airtable, and every write or sync to those sources can consume updates.


**User types and how they count:**


- Internal users: employees with logins, counted as editors or team members
- External users: customers or partners signing in, typically counted as business email users on the Business plan
- Public users or anonymous visitors browsing a public app may not count against seat limits, but they also cannot trigger authenticated actions


**Data source impact:**


Glide Tables and big tables (Glide's internal storage) tend to be more generous with update consumption than external data sources. If your app relies heavily on Google Sheets or Airtable syncing, expect higher update usage. Data updates in Glide are charged if they exceed monthly limits at a rate of $0.02 per extra update.


**Numeric example:** If you run an inventory app with 5,000 product changes per day, that is roughly 110,000 updates per month. Even on the Business plan (5,000 included), you would face around 105,000 overage updates-costing approximately $2,100 in overage alone. Factor in user growth and integration complexity when evaluating whether Glide is worth it for long-term use.


## Total Cost of Ownership: What Really Makes Glide Expensive (or Cheap)


The list price on Glide's pricing page is only one part of the total cost. Actual spend depends on usage, governance needs, and how your teams work. Glide's pricing tiers can lead to unexpected additional charges if you are not careful.


**Core cost drivers beyond the subscription:**


- Number of signed-in users, especially external customers. Growing from 30 to 300 business email users on the Business plan adds roughly $1,350/month in seat costs alone.
- Monthly updates beyond the included quota. Apps that consume updates through heavy automations, external syncs, or Glide AI requests can push overage charges well past the base fee.
- Number of apps and environments. Needing separate dev, staging, and production setups may require higher tiers or multiple workspaces.
- Native mobile distribution. Glide apps cannot be published directly to app stores, so teams needing native iOS or Android apps must layer on third-party services-an added cost and complexity.
- Support and governance. Priority support, SSO, and audit capabilities are gated to Business or Enterprise, pushing you into higher tiers even with modest usage.


**Scaling example:** A simple internal dashboard for 10 users on the Maker plan may cost ~$49/month. Scale that same concept to a customer-facing portal with 500 sign-ins and frequent data writes, and you could easily land at $2,000+/month on the Business plan once you factor in extra users and update overages.


**Back-of-the-envelope checklist before committing:**


- How many users will sign in monthly (internal vs external)?
- How many data writes/syncs will your app trigger per day?
- Which data sources are required (Glide Tables, spreadsheets, SQL)?
- Do you need SSO, audit logs, or compliance controls?
- Will you need more than one environment or staging setup?


## Is Glide Worth It for Your Use Case?


The real question is: is Glide worth it for your specific workflow and risk tolerance? There is no universal answer.


**Where Glide tends to be a strong fit:**


- Small to mid-sized internal tools and data-driven dashboards
- CRUD apps where you control the user base and data sources are fairly simple
- Rapid prototyping when you need to build apps quickly with no coding required
- Structured data management for small teams using spreadsheets as a backend


**Where Glide is less compelling:**


- Public consumer mobile apps that need Apple App Store or Google Play distribution (Glide outputs a web app, not native apps)
- Apps needing native device capabilities or native code execution
- Highly customized UI layouts-Glide's customization options are limited to set templates, and teams wanting more customization may hit walls quickly
- Scenarios requiring an unlimited number of external users without per-seat costs


Glide can be cost-effective early on (free or Maker plan) but may become less attractive as the number of external users, integrations, and required update limits grows. Use this quick self-assessment: count your expected users, list your critical data sources, document compliance needs, and estimate your iteration speed. If the numbers stay modest, Glide makes sense. If they do not, keep reading.


## Glide vs Native and Low-Code App Builders (Pricing Perspective)


Glide sits firmly in the web app and internal tool camp. Some alternatives focus on native mobile apps or more traditional low-code approaches, and the pricing models differ significantly.


**No-code native app builders (e.g., Adalo):**


Adalo starts at $36/month, cheaper than Glide's $60/month Maker plan. Adalo creates true native apps for iOS and Android that can be published directly to app stores and Google Play-something Glide requires third-party services for. Adalo allows unlimited database records on paid plans and offers extensive design customization compared to Glide. However, Adalo may be less suited for data-heavy internal dashboards or apps backed by existing databases.


**Low-code business app platforms:**


Tools like Retool or other no code tools in this category typically charge per end-user or per environment, not per update. This can make costs more predictable for teams with high-volume data operations but modest user counts. Some platforms also offer self-hosted deployment, which Glide does not.


**Key cost differences to evaluate:**


- Does the platform output native apps or android apps, or only web apps?
- Is pricing per app, per seat, or per environment?
- How does it handle usage-API calls, workflow runs, or storage limits?


Teams needing direct app-store distribution should evaluate whether paying for Glide plus a third-party wrapper is cheaper or more complex than using a native-first platform. For internal tools, the calculus is different: Glide's speed of deployment often offsets its usage-based billing, at least at smaller scale.


## When Glide's Pricing and Limits Stop Making Sense


Some teams start happily on Glide but later run into scaling, governance, or pricing pain points. Here are common breaking points:


- **Rapid growth in external users.** Every customer or partner who signs in becomes a paid seat on the Business plan. A customer-support portal that grows from 100 to 5,000 users turns a $199/month subscription into a multi-thousand-dollar monthly expense in seat costs alone.
- **Heavy reliance on external data sources.** Apps syncing frequently with Google Sheets, Airtable, or SQL databases can spike update costs far beyond the included quota. A logistics app that writes thousands of app updates per hour could burn through the entire monthly allocation in days.
- **Complex permission models.** If your new app requires enterprise-grade governance-row-level access control, SSO, audit logs-you may be forced into Enterprise even though your usage is otherwise modest.


**Operational risks to consider:**


- Migration cost if you later outgrow Glide. Data locked in Glide Tables is less portable than data sitting in a standard PostgreSQL or MySQL database.
- Lock-in around proprietary tables versus portable databases should factor into early plan selection.


Revisit your assumptions about more users, data sources, and compliance requirements annually-not just at initial launch. What looks affordable today may not hold up under growth.


## Glide Alternatives for Internal Tools and Portals


Glide is only one of several options for building internal tools, admin panels, and customer portals without heavy custom development. Alternatives generally fall into a few buckets:


**Spreadsheet-driven no-code tools:** Platforms like Softr or AppSheet let you build apps on top of existing data in Google Sheets or Airtable. Pricing tends to be seat-based with fewer usage-based variables, which can make budgeting simpler for small teams.


**Visual app builders focused on native mobile:** Tools like Adalo or FlutterFlow output native iOS and Android apps. They charge per app or per user rather than per update, and they handle app building for app stores directly. Trade-off: they may offer less support for complex backend logic or multi-source data models.


**Internal-tool platforms (e.g., Jet Admin):** Platforms in this category connect directly to existing databases, SaaS APIs, and warehouses to build secure, branded portals and dashboards. Pricing models in this bucket tend to be more seat-based or workspace-based, avoiding the update-quota friction that defines Glide's approach.


When evaluating other no code tools, look for: support for your existing data (databases, SaaS APIs), pricing predictability, governance features (RBAC, SSO), and deployment options (cloud vs self-hosted). Each factor can tilt the decision.

## FAQ: Common Questions About Glide Pricing and Plans


This FAQ covers edge cases and clarifications not fully addressed in the main sections. All answers are consistent with Glide's official pricing and documentation as of July 2026; details may change over time.


### Does Glide charge per user, per app, or per workspace?


Glide's pricing blends several dimensions. Each pricing plan includes a base fee covering a set number of signed-in users, limits on the number of apps or pages per workspace, and usage limits like updates. On the Business plan, for example, you get 30 included business email users and unlimited apps, but each additional user beyond 30 costs extra.


Internal users (editors, team members) and external users (customers, partners) may be counted differently depending on the tier. A public user browsing without signing in typically does not count against seat limits. Teams with many external customers should pay close attention to user definitions and confirm with Glide's current plan documentation before launching large-scale portals.


### Can I Stay on an Older Glide Plan if My Pricing Changed?


Some long-time customers may still be on legacy pricing plans (like earlier Starter or Pro tiers). Availability of these grandfathered plans depends entirely on Glide's current policies. Do not assume you can sign up for historical plans mentioned in older blog posts-new workspaces are constrained to the current catalog.


If you are an existing customer seeking clarification about legacy vs new plan migration, reach out directly to Glide support. Yes free trial periods or promotional pricing from past campaigns may no longer apply to new accounts.


### What Happens if I Exceed Glide's Monthly Updates?


Most paid plans include a fixed pool of monthly updates. When that quota is exhausted,[Glide temporarily stops executing workflows](https://help.glideapps.com/en/articles/13417575-what-happens-when-your-team-runs-out-of-updates) , external data sources stop syncing, and Glide AI returns no response. Extra updates are billed at approximately $0.02 per update if you enable unlimited updates on your plan.


Teams with volatile or high-volume workloads-real-time IoT feeds, high-traffic CRMs, apps that consume updates heavily through automations-should monitor update consumption closely. Consider architectures that reduce write frequency or batch operations to stay within your current plan's quota.


### Is There a Truly Free Way to Run Production Apps on Glide?


While Glide offers a free plan, it is best for learning, prototyping, and small internal tools for personal use. Glide's free plan allows one app with limited updates, Glide branding, and restricted customization. Running a production-grade external app on the free plan usually runs into issues like inability to scale user counts, limited data rows, and no custom domain.


Teams needing a no-cost option for real production traffic should reevaluate their requirements or compare with other platforms' free tiers. Plan for a paid plan as soon as the app gains traction. App building for non profit or education use cases may also benefit from exploring whether Glide offers any special programs tied to an education email domain.


### How Should Glide Pricing Factor into My Business Plan?


Incorporate Glide subscription and expected overage costs directly into your business plan, alongside hosting, support, and maintenance budgets. Model at least three scenarios: conservative (few users, low updates), expected (moderate growth, regular integrations), and aggressive (rapid user acquisition, heavy automations). This reveals how Glide's pricing plans scale over time and where breakpoints occur.


Document a potential migration path-including options like Jet Admin for internal tools and portals-so that future plan changes or enterprise pricing shifts do not derail your long-term product and financial planning. Revisit this model quarterly, not just at launch.
