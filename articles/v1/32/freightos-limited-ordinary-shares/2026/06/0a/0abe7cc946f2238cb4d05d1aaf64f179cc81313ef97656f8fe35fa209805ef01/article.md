## **Analysis**


Approaching 100 days since the start of the Iran war, despite periodic reports that an agreement that would open the Strait of Hormuz is near, the sides continue to[exchange fire](https://splash247.com/hormuz-crisis-enters-dangerous-new-phase/) and[sanctions](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-05-30/us-says-deals-with-iran-for-safe-hormuz-transit-are-prohibited) , and the waterway remains closed.


For the container market, the closure has primarily meant upward pressure on freight rates via carriers passing on war-elevated fuel costs, which manifested in different ways on different lanes during the low demand months of March, April and most of May this year.

But peak season demand is kicking in early on east-west lanes, with[reports of contracted shippers already seeing allocations reduced](https://theloadstar.com/carriers-back-to-price-gouging-on-ocean-trades-they-cant-help-themselves/) and premiums applied. So spot rates that climbed moderately – about 15% – across the ex-Asia lanes through mid-May GRIs to levels around 20% higher than a year ago, are starting to spike this week.


Weekly averages for last week were about level to close out the month, with transpacific rates at about $3,200/FEU to the West Coast and $5,000/FEU to the East Coast, and Asia – Europe prices at about $3,000/FEU to N. Europe and $4,400/FEU to the Mediterranean. But[June 1st](https://www.hapag-lloyd.com/en/services-information/news/2026/05/shipping-between-far-east-and-europe--a-price-announcement-is-av.html)[GRIs](https://www.msc.com/en/newsroom/customer-advisories/2026/may/price-announcement-trade-from-far-east-to-nwc-med-and-black-sea)[and](https://theloadstar.com/freight-rate-recovery-more-about-demand-than-blanked-sailings/)[PSS](https://www.cma-cgm.com/news/5442/pss-from-asia-to-north-europe) introductions have daily rates spiking from $1,000/FEU to $1,800/FEU so far this week on these trades, with[additional](https://www.cma-cgm.com/news/5428/fak-rates-from-asia-to-the-mediterranean-amp-north-africa?cat=prices-surcharges)[significant](https://www.maersk.com/news/articles/2026/05/25/far-east-asia-europe-pss-june)[increases](https://www.maersk.com/news/articles/2026/05/18/far-east-asia-us-canada-pss-june)[announced](https://theloadstar.com/pay-to-play-is-back-as-shippers-face-a-brutal-shipping-environment/) for mid-month across these lanes as well.


Daily rates for Asia – Europe lanes have already surpassed peak season highs from last June/July, with transpacific still about $1,000/FEU short of last year’s brief, tariff frontloading-driven rate spike in July. Pre-existing war-related congestion in[some](https://theloadstar.com/transhipment-volume-rush-brings-congestion-jnpa-box-terminal/)[tranship](https://www.joc.com/article/choked-container-yards-strain-terminal-operations-at-sri-lankas-colombo-port-6227544) hubs, as well as[rail congestion](https://www.joc.com/article/disrupted-operations-leave-germanys-northern-rail-network-in-chaos-6228922) in Germany could also be a factor for rate pressure or delays for the relevant trades.


In trade war developments, IEEPA refunds – totalling about half of the total $166B paid – are[on the way](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-05-27/us-says-20-6-billion-of-tariff-refunds-on-the-way-to-importers) for importers whose customs entries had not already been liquidated, or finalized, by US Customs and Border Protection. But the Trump Administration indicated last week that it[may challenge refunds for liquidated entries](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-05-29/us-to-appeal-judge-s-order-for-broad-refund-of-trump-tariffs) , arguing that the CBP is unauthorized to reliquidate and refund closed out entries without importer-specific court orders instructing it to do so.


***Check out our full IEEPA tariff refund explainer and update page***[here](https://www.freightos.com/freight-resources/trade-war-update-white-house-to-challenge-half-of-all-ieepa-refunds/) ***.***


This challenge, if successful, could mean that these importers would need to sue the government in trade court in order to get these duties refunded, and even if unsuccessful could mean a longer wait for impacted importers while the legal issues get sorted out. In the meantime, some trade law experts are advising importers with liquidated entries to file protests if the window hasn’t closed yet.


The trade war has resulted in lower or flat import volumes to the US alongside trade diversions driving volume increases between other countries as global players[seek closer ties](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-05-29/canada-aims-to-boost-china-trade-as-wang-makes-first-visit-in-decade) and[trade growth](https://www.joc.com/article/double-digit-rate-increases-whip-up-intra-asia-trades-6228000) beyond the US. Asia – Europe trade for example grew significantly last year and continues on pace so far in 2026. Even so, trade tensions between China and the EU may be increasing, as the[EU considers legislation](https://www.joc.com/article/double-digit-rate-increases-whip-up-intra-asia-trades-6228000) to curb subsidized imports.


Part of this issue relates to e-commerce imports to EU countries, which continue to grow significantly even as they flatten to the US and are reflected in[diverging freighter capacity trends](https://theloadstar.com/dhl-fedex-ups-warn-eu-parcel-tax-risks-disruption-at-borders/) on these lanes. The EU will introduce a flat 3 EUR fee for low value imports starting in July, and a 2 EUR handling fee in November.


Though not as extensive as the US de minimis cancellation, these moves are likely to reduce EU e-commerce volumes arriving by air to some extent. Parcel carriers are[warning that the system is still not ready](https://theloadstar.com/dhl-fedex-ups-warn-eu-parcel-tax-risks-disruption-at-borders/) for the new reporting requirements that will accompany the fee introductions, and warn of delays at European borders if these take effect in July.


Air cargo rates were about level on most major lanes this week, though the Freightos Air Index global benchmark – which is about even with April levels – remains more than 30% higher than before the start of the Iran war and year on year as capacity reductions and elevated jet fuel prices continue to impact price levels.
