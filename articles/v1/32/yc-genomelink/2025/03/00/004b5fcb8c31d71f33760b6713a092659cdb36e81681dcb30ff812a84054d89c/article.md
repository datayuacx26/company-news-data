## **1. Introduction: Why Compare AncestryDNA vs MyHeritage?**


If you want to explore your family’s past, locate distant relatives, or confirm a rumored ancestral connection,[AncestryDNA](https://genomelink.io/blog/ancestrydna-review-by-experts) and[MyHeritage](http://blog.genomelink.io/posts/myheritage-review-by-experts) are two of the most popular DNA testing options. Both services provide user-friendly tools and a variety of resources to help you dig deeper into your family’s story.


These companies focus on **autosomal DNA** , which can reveal your ethnic mix and connect you with individuals who share parts of your genetic makeup. They also offer important extras:


- **AncestryDNA** : Ties into a vast record collection—especially helpful for people interested in United States history.
- **MyHeritage** : Has an especially strong international audience and competitive pricing.


By comparing these two leading tests, you’ll learn where each one shines and how they fit into your 2025 research goals.

## **2. Overview of Key Features**


Below is a quick look at the main offerings from both **AncestryDNA** and **MyHeritage** .


### **AncestryDNA**


- Tests your autosomal DNA across ~1,500 regions.
- Integrates with a large number of U.S. historical documents (e.g., Freedmen’s Bureau records, the 1950 U.S. Census).
- Strong network of existing users, providing many DNA match possibilities.
- Offers add-ons like “Traits,” which can give basic insights into physical features (such as hair texture).


### **MyHeritage**


- Uses a simple cheek swab for autosomal DNA testing.
- Analyzes your ancestry in 2,114 global regions.
- Provides advanced photo tools, including color enhancement and picture animation features.
- Often more cost-effective than other services—especially during sale periods.

## **4. Ethnicity Estimates and Reference Panels**


Both AncestryDNA and MyHeritage match your DNA to different population groups around the world:


- **AncestryDNA** : Covers about 1,500 regions. Known for frequent updates that refine your ethnic breakdown, especially for North America and Europe.
- **MyHeritage** : Boasts 2,114 regions, with a strong focus on European and Middle Eastern populations. Many users find its European breakdowns especially detailed.


As reference panels expand, you might see new details about your background or changes to your existing estimates. In both cases, these updates are free and happen automatically.

## **5. Family History Advantages**


### **AncestryDNA**


- **U.S. Historical Records** : Integrates with a wide range of American records, including Freedmen’s Bureau archives and census returns from 1790 to 1950.
- **Hint System** : Offers “shaky leaf” hints that link your DNA matches or listed ancestors to official documents and other member trees.
- **Collaborative Trees** : Let you work with family members to expand your shared lineage.


### **MyHeritage**


- **Worldwide Focus** : Especially helpful if you have ancestors from various parts of Europe or the Middle East.
- **Photo Tools** : Enhance and animate old photographs, adding a creative way to preserve and share your family’s story.
- **Free Data Uploads** : If you’ve already tested with another service, you can upload your raw DNA data at no charge. This feature can broaden your chance of finding new relatives.


‍


## **6. Recent Updates and Enhancements**


- **AncestryDNA** recently added more specialized breakdowns for certain regions in Africa, Europe, and North America. They also launched features like “DNA by Parent,” which divides your ethnicity results by maternal and paternal lines (if you’ve opted in).
- **MyHeritage** continues to refine its photo enhancement tools, adding advanced facial animation and an updated “DeepStory” feature. These extras allow you to create short narrative videos about ancestors, complete with still images and short scripts.


By offering these updates, both services keep your DNA results relevant and interesting over time.

## **10. Privacy and Security**


Both companies stress the importance of safeguarding your information and DNA sample.


### **AncestryDNA**


- **Data Sharing** : Only shares DNA data when required by a valid legal process.
- **Data Control** : Lets you download and delete your raw data if you wish.
- **Security Track Record** : No major data breaches have been reported so far.


### **MyHeritage**


- **Previous Incident (2018)** : Hackers accessed hashed passwords, but no DNA files were compromised.
- **Regulations** : Based in Israel, so it follows many European Union–style privacy guidelines.
- **User Control** : You can remove your data or request destruction of your physical sample at any time.


No matter which service you choose, you can decide how much personal info to share with potential DNA matches. You can also opt out of matching altogether if you want complete privacy.

## **12. Which One Should You Choose?**


It largely depends on your priorities:


- **Looking for U.S. History Resources** : AncestryDNA offers an extensive collection of American records and has a huge user database.
- **Budget-Friendly & International Focus** : MyHeritage often runs great sales and has a strong presence in Europe and the Middle East, which can boost your chances of finding relatives abroad.
- **Seeking the Largest User Network** : AncestryDNA generally has more total users, which could lead to more DNA matches.
- **Creative Photo Features** : MyHeritage stands out with its AI-based photo enhancements, animations, and “DeepStory” videos.


Some family researchers even test with one service and upload their raw data to the other. This approach offers the best of both worlds: a larger pool of potential matches and specialized features from each platform.
