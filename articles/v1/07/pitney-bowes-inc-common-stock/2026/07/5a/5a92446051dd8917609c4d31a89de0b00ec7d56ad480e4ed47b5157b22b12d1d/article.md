# New USPS® HazMat fees, stricter enforcement, and the $50 mistake you don't want to make

This is where many organizations get caught off guard. A beauty company shipping nail polish, a retailer sending electronics, or a corporate mailroom returning company-issued devices may all be shipping materials subject to HazMat requirements.


**Always verify*[USPS requirements](https://www.usps.com/ship/shipping-restrictions.htm?msockid=1248af45c81e6e5e2b5eb82ec9936f3e) *for specific products before shipping. Product composition and packaging requirements may affect classification.*


**What's the Difference Between Compliance and Non-Compliance?**


**Compliance: Declare It Correctly**


A compliant HazMat shipment typically includes:


- Proper hazardous material identification
- Required USPS declarations
- Appropriate labeling and markings
- Approved packaging
- Correct service selection based on shipment contents


When properly declared, USPS applies the applicable handling fee and processes the shipment according to HazMat regulations.


**Non-Compliance: The Costly Shortcut**


Non-compliance occurs when hazardous material is shipped without the proper declaration, labeling, packaging, or documentation.


Examples include:


- Shipping a lithium-battery-powered device without declaring it.
- Sending nail polish through Priority Mail without the required HazMat designation.
- Using incorrect packaging for regulated materials.
- Missing required labels or markings.


The result can be a **$50 non-compliance charge per shipment** , in addition to possible delays or corrective actions.


**The Most Unexpected Compliance Risk: Reusing Boxes**


Here's a scenario many businesses don't consider.


You reuse a shipping box to reduce waste. Great idea.


But what if that box still displays a lithium battery sticker or another HazMat marking from a previous shipment?


USPS may view that package as improperly labeled. Even if the contents are not hazardous, the presence of outdated HazMat markings can create compliance issues and potentially expose the shipment to non-compliance fees. Shippers should remove, cover, or black out old HazMat labels before reusing boxes.


In other words, sustainability is encouraged, but only when packaging is properly cleaned up before reuse.


**Why USPS Is Taking HazMat** **More Seriously**


Many Priority Mail and Priority Mail Express shipments travel through air transportation networks.


Lithium batteries, flammable liquids, aerosols, and other hazardous materials can create significant transportation risks if not properly identified and handled. USPS has emphasized that these changes are intended to improve safety and reduce the chances of regulated materials entering the network without proper controls.


For shippers, that means HazMat compliance is becoming less about paperwork and more about operational risk management.


**What This Means for E-commerce, Retail, and Mail Operations**


**E-commerce Sellers**


Many products sold online – especially electronics, beauty products, and wellness items – may require HazMat declarations. Missing those requirements can impact shipping costs and customer satisfaction.


**Corporate Mailrooms**


Organizations frequently return laptops, mobile devices, and technology equipment containing lithium batteries. HazMat compliance procedures should be part of every shipping workflow.


**Manufacturers and Distributors**


Product samples, replacement parts, and specialized supplies may require HazMat treatment depending on their contents.


**Healthcare and Life Sciences Organizations**


Medical devices, diagnostic equipment, and other regulated products often require additional review before entering the mailstream.


**Five Ways to Stay Compliant and Avoid Unnecessary Fees**


**1. Know What's in Your Package**


Review product contents, not just product names. Batteries, alcohol-based solutions, and aerosols often trigger HazMat requirements.


**2. Train Staff Regularly**


Shipping teams should understand which products require declarations and labels.


**3. Inspect Reused Packaging**


Remove or completely cover outdated HazMat markings before shipping.


**4. Verify Labels Before Drop-Off**


A missing declaration can be far more expensive than the handling fee itself.


**5. Use Shipping Technology That Guides Compliance**


Modern shipping platforms like PitneyShip can help identify HazMat requirements, surface USPS guidance, and generate compliant shipping labels helping reduce risk and ensuring teams stay current with changing regulations.
