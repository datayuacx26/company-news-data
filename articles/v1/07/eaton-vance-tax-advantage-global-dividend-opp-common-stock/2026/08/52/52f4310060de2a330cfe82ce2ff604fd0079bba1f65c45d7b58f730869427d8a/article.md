# Five Dividend-paying AI Tech Stocks to Buy

Five dividend-paying[AI tech stocks](https://www.stockinvestor.com/three-ai-technology-stocks-worth-owning/) to buy offer investors a chance to tap into a recent pullback.


The five dividend-paying AI tech stocks are among those that have dipped in price but still offer opportunities to profit. AI technology stocks are engaged in a variety of activities that include machine learning (ML), deep learning, natural language processing (NLP) and computer vision, according to Cambridge, Massachusetts-based[Akami Technologies](https://www.akamai.com/) (NASDAQ: AKAM), a cloud computing and cybersecurity company.


Akami Technologies is among the AI tech stocks whose share prices have pulled back in price. But like many AI tech stocks, Akami is not a dividend payer, so it did not make the cut for make this column’s list of five.


Key AI technology business activities include machine learning (ML), using computers that learn from data and improve in time without extra programming. Another capability is deep learning, a type of machine learning that uses layers of digital “neurons” modeled after the human brain.


A third AI tech business activity is natural language processing that helps computers understand, read and write human language. A fourth is computer vision that allows machines to recognize, analyze and understand pictures and videos.


**Five Dividend-paying AI Tech Stocks to Buy: Meta**


Meta Platforms, Inc. (NASDAQ: META), a dividend-paying multinational technology company headquartered in Menlo Park, California, owns and operates several prominent social media platforms and communication services that include Facebook, Instagram, WhatsApp, Messenger and Threads. With its share price down 25% from its 52-week high, Meta’s price-to-earnings (P/E) ratio of 18 and its dividend yield of 0.35%, it earned a spot on this list of five AI tech stocks.


BofA Global Research wrote that analysts seem to lack appreciation of Meta’s ability to monetize its growing capacity assets, which have demonstrated value based on recent capacity deals. Meta’s portfolio of AI assets includes frontier models, large-scale compute infrastructure, custom silicon, etc., BofA continued.


AI-driven benefits for the core advertising business, including deepening large language model (LLM) integration across the ad stack, should enhance content, ad targeting and advertiser return on ad spend (ROAS), according to BofA. Potential exists for upside rather than deceleration in analysts’ models for 2027 ad revenues, BofA added.


Meta also could monetize portions of its infrastructure, model capabilities or AI services through enterprise deals creating entirely new revenue streams, the investment firm wrote. Growing visibility that Meta can commercialize these assets externally could help alleviate AI capital expenditure return on investment (ROI) concerns and help support stock valuation.


“We see several catalysts ahead, anchored by Meta’s Connect Conference (Sept. 23),” BofA wrote in its research note. “Future announcements should include new LLMs, proprietary chip launches, personal AI assistant launch, business AI revenue platform launch (including subscriptions), enterprise licensing deals and enterprise capacity deals.”

**Five Dividend-paying AI Tech Stocks to Buy: Vertiv**


Westerville, Ohio-based Vertiv Holdings Co. (NYSE: VRT) is a designer, builder and provider of critical digital infrastructure services that offers a modest dividend yield of 0.085%. The company also provides core power management, thermal liquid cooling, racks and monitoring software for data centers, communication networks and industrial facilities.


Dr. Robert Castellano, PhD, a senior analyst with[Gilder’s Technology Report](https://www.gilderreport.com/offer/gilders-tech-report-launch/?source=GTRGURU) and several advisory services led by technology futurist George Gilder, told me that he thinks Vertiv has been “beaten down too much.” Vertiv supplies the power and cooling equipment needed inside AI data centers, and there is no AI buildout without either one, said Dr. Castellano, who also heads the[Information Network](https://www.theinformationnet.com/about-us) consultancy.


*George Gilder heads[Gilder’s Moonshots](https://www.gilderreport.com/offer/gilders-moonshots-launch/?source=GURUMS01) .*


Vertiv’s second-quarter sales increased 24%, adjusted earnings soared 60% and its management raised its guidance for 2026, Castellano said. That does not look like a company whose business is weakening, added Dr. Castellano, who also works on advisory services such as[Gilder’s Moonshots](https://www.gilderreport.com/offer/gilders-moonshots-launch/?source=GURUMS01) ,[Gilder’s Private Reserve](https://www.gilderreport.com/offer/private-default/?source=GURUGPR1) and[Gilder’s Tech Income Accelerator](https://www.gilderreport.com/offer/gilder-tech-report-pro-launch/?source=GTRPROGURU) .


“The stock is volatile, but AI racks keep using more power and generating more heat, which increases the amount of Vertiv equipment needed in each data center,” Dr. Castellano told me. “I see the pullback as a buying opportunity.”

**Five Dividend-paying AI Tech Stocks to Buy: NRG Energy**


Not only does Dr. Castellano own shares in Vertiv, but also NRG Energy (NYSE: NRG), headquartered in Houston, Texas, and operating as a major integrated energy and smart home services provider, generating power from diverse sources and supplying electricity and connected living products to millions of residential and commercial customers across North America. Despite a dividend yield of 1.51%, NRG has been “beaten down” after missing Wall Street’s second-quarter earnings estimate, Dr. Castellano told me.


The miss was primarily caused by higher interest and depreciation expenses, following the company’s LS Power acquisition, not by a decline in electricity demand, Dr. Castellano said. Vertiv’s adjusted earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization (EBITDA) still increased 34%, he added.


“AI data centers are running into a power problem, and NRG owns generating assets in markets where new data-center loads are being added,” Dr; Castellano advised. “Chips get most of the attention, but those chips are useless without electricity. I think the market reacted too strongly to one quarter and created a buying opportunity in NRG.”

**Five Dividend-paying AI Tech Stocks to Buy: Dell**


Dell Technologies Inc. (NYSE: DELL), of Round Rock, Texas, traditionally known for personal computers and laptops, has transformed into a big player in the artificial intelligence infrastructure boom by manufacturing high-performance, AI-optimized servers designed to house advanced graphics chips. The stock provides a current dividend yield of 0.51% and has soared 335.6% since it first was recommended in the Special Situations Portfolio of[Gilder’s Technology Report](https://www.gilderreport.com/offer/gilders-tech-report-launch/?source=GTRGURU) *.*


Dell is rated as a buy in BofA’s IT Hardware and Technology Supply Chain Coverage Cluster. Citi Research also rates Dell as a “buy,” citing enterprise IT infrastructure spending continuing to strengthen, supported by ongoing AI-related investments, server refresh cycles and broader demand for data center capacity.


Citi projects upside driven by a larger central processing unit (CPU) total addressable market (TAM), robust server demand and continued strength in AI infrastructure deployments, which support stronger growth.

**Five Dividend-paying AI Tech Stocks to Buy: Apple**


Jim Woods recently recommended technology giant Apple, Inc. (AAPL), of Cupertino, California, in the Growth Accelerators Portfolio of his[Forecasts & Strategies](https://jimwoodsinvesting.stockinvestor.com/offer/fs-web-default-2/?source=GURUFS) investment newsletter. Woods wrote that he expects to own the dividend-paying stock for years to come.


One reason to recommend it now is that Apple, with a 0.35% dividend yield, recently pulled back to what he considers “remarkably attractive levels.” Apple recently reported yet another record earnings quarter, but there are “some legitimate concerns” about its future growth and rising memory costs, causing some traders to sell shares post-earnings, Woods cautioned.


*Paul Dykewicz meets with Jim Woods, who heads[Forecasts & Strategies](https://jimwoodsinvesting.stockinvestor.com/offer/fs-web-default-2/?source=GURUFS) .*


But the sell-the-news reaction to Apple’s results was actually the development Woods said he was watching to develop, providing an opportunity to own AAPL at what he regards as a very attractive entry point. Wall Street firm Jefferies opted to downgrade Apple and cut its price target to $263.66, citing supply-chain indications that the planned release of a new all-glass iPhone had been canceled due to low yields.


“While this circumstance may be a short-term bump in the road for Apple, I do not think this is a major obstacle capable of compressing the company’s share price substantively,” wrote Woods, who also heads the[TNT Trader](https://jimwoodsinvesting.stockinvestor.com/offer/tnt-trader-web-default-2/?source=GURUTNT) ,[Tactical Trader](https://jimwoodsinvesting.stockinvestor.com/offer/tactical-trader-web-default-offer/?source=GURUTT01) *,*[Five Star Trader](https://jimwoodsinvesting.stockinvestor.com/offer/five-star-trader-web-default-2/?source=GURUFST) and[Bullseye Stock Trader](https://jimwoodsinvesting.stockinvestor.com/offer/bullseye-default/?source=GURUBEST1) advisory services that recommend both stocks and options.


Woods wrote that the news in no way alters his view that Apple is going to be a long-term winner in the AI race via leveraging its customer-facing characteristics. That is going to happen regardless of whether the company unveils a new all-glass iPhone, he added.
