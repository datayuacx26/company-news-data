# Five Dividend-paying Telecommunications Investments Rated Buy

Five dividend-paying[telecommunications investments](https://www.stockinvestor.com/three-wireless-stocks-rated-buy-amid-rising-revenues/) rated buy consist of two funds and three major U.S. wireless providers.


The five dividend-paying telecommunications investments rated buy offer an array of opportunities to simultaneously pursue share-price appreciation and income payouts from dividends.


With the big-five telecommunications and cable companies recently reporting second-quarter results, high-level corporate performance generally met analysts’ estimates for service revenues, with slightly better earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization (EBITDA), slightly lower unlevered free cash flow (FCF) on higher capital expenditures and generally positive aggregate levered FCF generation, wrote Michael Rollins, an analyst with Citi Research. Underlying that performance, wireless services showed improved financial performance from a stabilizing competitive environment, he added.


Revenue from mobile services performed reasonably well and relatively in-line for average revenue per user (ARPU), Rollins continued. In contrast, broadband revenue decelerated more quickly, although growth still occurred from mergers and acquisitions during the last 12 months by all three major telecommunications providers, he continued.


With broadband subscriptions nearly in-line, softer service revenue mainly stemmed from weakened broadband ARPU on a year-over-year basis due to “aggressive” telecommunications promotions to spur share-price gains through “converged bundle discounts,” according to Citi Research. Cable company competitors also engaged in pricing strategies and subscriber retention efforts of their own, Citi Research reported.


**Five Dividend-paying Telecommunications Investments Rated Buy: XLC**


The State Street® Communication Services Select Sector SPDR® ETF (NYSE Arca: XLC) seeks to provide investment results that correspond generally to the price and yield performance of the Communication Services Select Sector Index, before expenses. Dividend-paying XLC follows the S&P 500 Communications Services Index, which takes a broad view of communications services, said Bob Carlson, who heads the[Retirement Watch](http://www.retirementwatch.com/offer/rw-default/?source=GURURW1) investment newsletters.


The fund contains stocks such as the major cellular phone service providers that would be expected by most investors, said Carlson, a retired pension fund chairman who also recently updated his proprietary[IRA Calculator](https://www.retirementwatch.com/offer/rw-ira-calculator-v2?source=GURURWCALCV2) to help those assessing whether to convert to Roth IRAs.

**Five Dividend-paying Telecommunications Investments Rated Buy: VZ**


Verizon, the world’s second-largest telecommunications company by revenue and the largest wireless carrier in the United States, entered the artificial intelligence infrastructure market with an announcement during its Q2 2026 earnings call that it agreed to a $1 billion-plus dark fiber deal with Google, part of Mountain View, California-based Alphabet (NASDAQ: GOOG). The pact led to upgraded, full-year financial guidance for Verizon and an expanding strategy to retrofit central offices for artificial intelligence (AI) workloads.


Risk exists that cost cutting could have an unintended consequence on future operating performance, Citi Research commented. However, the investment firm maintained its Buy rating on Verizon with an unchanged target.


Citi Research plans to look further into the potential sources of upside in Verizon’s wireless wholesale revenue during the second quarter of 2026 relative to its estimates, Rollins wrote. His earnings per share (EPS) for 2026 is unchanged at $5.01, while incorporating some dilution in 2027 partly from a recently disclosed spectrum purchase.


“We believe the wireless competitive landscape is showing some positive signs of stabilizing over the near term, while Verizon is enhancing its go-to-market position with its refreshed rate plans, converged offers and small rebound in core prepaid,” Rollins wrote. “However, the larger question and risk that is still orbiting around the telco and cable stocks is the possibility that LEO \[low-Earth-orbit\] satellite constellations, including StarLink, become a direct and broader competitor to both wireless and broadband in the U.S. market.”

**Five Dividend-paying Telecommunications Investments Rated Buy: T**


Dallas-based AT&T Inc. (NYSE: T) delivered improved second-quarter 2026 results and reaffirmed its multi-year guidance, wrote Citi Research’s Rollins. AT&T’s results reflected an expanding fiber total addressable market (TAM), deeper converged service penetration and favorable operating leverage partly from efficiency initiatives, he added.


The telecommunications company’s results also reflect a more active employment of back book pricing actions, while expanding its promotional menu to improve share within the value segment, Rollins opined. The combination of favorable volume and better wireless service revenue growth should help AT&T meet its high-level advanced connectivity revenue growth of at least 5% and generate significantly better EBITDA growth, he added.


Once again, the entry of Starlink into the mobile category of telecommunications poses a risk to long-term industry structure and terminal value that may limit upside potential for at least the near-term, according to Citi Research. Rollins updated his firm’s valuation methods to reflect some of this uncertainty, contributing to a reduced price target of $28 for AT&T. However, he maintains a “Buy” on AT&T’s shares.

**Five Dividend-paying Telecommunications Investments Rated Buy: TMUS**


T-Mobile USA Inc. (NASDAQ: TMUS), of Bellevue, Washington, has shifted focus from volumes to average revenue per account (ARPA) growth, which grew 2.0% year over year and is trending toward the high end of fiscal year 2026 guidance. The company has a unique opportunity to increase existing customer pricing, and it leads the industry in digitalization and automation, which today is driving industry-leading margins of 41.8%, according to Timothy Horan, an equity research analyst with Oppenheimer.


TMUS received an “outperform rating from Oppenheimer’s Horan, after reporting results in-line for its just-ended quarter.” T-Mobile reiterated its guidance except for a modest increase to free cash flow (FCF) outlook, he added.


Also worthy of note, TMUS rolled out the beta of Live Translation, its first native AI network application and an early step toward embedding AI compute and models directly into the network core, Horan commented. Latency and edge compute will be critical for inferencing and physical AI applications where T-Mobile looks well positioned to support it, Horan added.


T-Mobile’s management sees the upcoming C-band auction and potential repurposing of 2.7GHz as opportunities to add to TMUS’ network leadership, Horan continued. The 2.7GHz would be a particularly synergistic fit as an adjacent band to the company’s 2.5GHz spectrum, which serves as the anchor of its 5G network, he added.


Satellite bidders are likely to drive up prices and spur competition, Horan concluded.
