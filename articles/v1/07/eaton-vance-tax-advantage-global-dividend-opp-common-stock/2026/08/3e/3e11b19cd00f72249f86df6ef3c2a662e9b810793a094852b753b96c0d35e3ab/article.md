Two dividend-paying space investments on the rise feature two funds and a stock that is scheduled to be acquired in 2027.


The two dividend-paying investments on the rise offer an opportunity to profit from an ongoing race for space. Bargain hunters may view now as a possible time to invest in stocks and funds of publicly traded space companies as they virtually dropped in price together and the market did not seem to distinguish one from the other, regardless of their respective businesses, wrote Dr. Robert Castellano, PhD, who is a senior analyst with[Gilder’s Technology Report](https://www.gilderreport.com/offer/gilders-tech-report-launch/?source=GTRGURU) and the leader of the[Information Network](https://www.theinformationnet.com/about-us) consultancy. For example, Rocket Lab (NASDAQ: RKLB), Planet Labs (NYSE: PL) and SpaceX (NASDAQ: SPCX) operate materially different businesses and face different capital requirements, yet their share prices have increasingly moved together, he added.


Rocket Lab combines launch services, spacecraft manufacturing and defense systems. Planet Labs generates recurring revenue from satellite imagery and geospatial intelligence. SpaceX combines launch services, Starlink communications, Starship development and increasingly capital-intensive artificial intelligence initiatives.


“The market has nevertheless reduced them to a single speculative category called space,” wrote Dr. Castellano, who works directly with technology futurist George Gilder, whose products also include[Gilder’s Moonshots](https://www.gilderreport.com/offer/gilders-moonshots-launch/?source=GURUMS01) ,[Gilder’s Private Reserve](https://www.gilderreport.com/offer/private-default/?source=GURUGPR1) and[Gilder’s Tech Income Accelerator](https://www.gilderreport.com/offer/gilder-tech-report-pro-launch/?source=GTRPROGURU) . That broad-brush treatment is characteristic of a market in which thematic classification and herd behavior increasingly overwhelm company-specific analysis, Dr. Castellano counseled.


George Gilder heads[Gilder’s Moonshots](https://www.gilderreport.com/offer/gilders-moonshots-launch/?source=GURUMS01) .

**Two Dividend-paying Space Investments on the Rise: Iridium**


McLean, Virgina-based Iridium Communications Inc. (NASDAQ: IRDM), a provider of global voice, data and other satellite services, recently reported its financial results for the second quarter of 2026. Iridium also announced on June 28, 2026, that it entered into a definitive agreement to be acquired by Rocket Lab Corporation. That transaction is expected to be completed in mid-2027, subject to approval by Iridium stockholders and the satisfaction of other customary closing conditions.


In light of the pending transaction, Iridium does not plan to hold conference calls to discuss its quarterly financial results or to update or provide financial guidance. However, it reported second-quarter total revenue of $225.2 million, a 4% increase from the same period of 2025.


Iridium’s service revenue, which primarily represents recurring revenue from its growing subscriber base, climbed 4% from the year-ago period and was 72% of total revenue for the second quarter of 2026.


“Iridium’s global network and expanding service portfolio continues to be the gold standard for mission critical applications,” said Matt Desch, CEO of Iridium.


I personally bought Iridium shares when its share price fell below $50, since the buyout price by Rocket Lab is $54. With Iridium’s dividend, I am hoping for a 10% return during the next year.


Chicago-based investment firm William Blair & Co. recently changed its rating on Iridium to market perform from outperform, so those seeking a big return on a space stock should understand the company’s growth potential has a limit. For me, I am content to pursue what I expect will be a 10% total return on this holding during the next year.
