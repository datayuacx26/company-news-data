# Validation & Qualification in Production Additive Manufacturing

### IQ, OQ, PQ — The Framework Behind Process Validation


Process validation in production environments typically follows the industry-recognized IQ/OQ/PQ structure.


**Installation Qualification (IQ)** confirms that equipment is properly installed, calibrated, and maintained according to manufacturer specifications. It establishes the baseline readiness of the manufacturing system.


**Operational Qualification (OQ)** evaluates the impact of key process variables. Parameters are intentionally tested at defined limits to determine how sensitive output is to variation. This stage identifies which variables truly influence part performance.


**Performance Qualification (PQ)** increases sampling under nominal operating conditions to statistically demonstrate repeatability and sustained capability.


Together, these stages provide documented, data-backed evidence that a process is stable and production-ready.


### Common Challenges in Applying Traditional Frameworks to Additive


Organizations transitioning additive parts into regulated production often encounter friction when traditional validation models are applied without adaptation.


Many validation templates were originally developed for injection molding or other conventional technologies. Terminology and assumptions do not always translate directly to additive builds. Without collaborative scoping, documentation expectations can quickly become misaligned.


Another common issue is overscoping validation efforts. Additive systems offer many adjustable parameters, but not all carry equal risk. A risk-based approach is essential to ensure that validation effort aligns with part criticality and economic reality.


Finally, imposing tolerances or inspection expectations designed for other manufacturing methods can unintentionally increase cost and complexity. Validation should support production efficiency — not undermine it.


A mature additive partner understands how to align regulatory expectations with the realities of additive process behavior.


### What Demonstrates Production Readiness?


In regulated industries, production readiness is proven through structured documentation and disciplined process control. While specific requirements vary by sector, robust additive manufacturing workflows typically include:


- Defined process flows and control plans


- Risk assessments such as PFMEA


- Inspection system validation


- Documented IQ/OQ/PQ execution


- Traceability systems linking parts to process conditions


- First article and production approval documentation


These elements form the backbone of audit readiness and appear in medical, aerospace, and automotive production frameworks alike.


Certification standards such as ISO 9001, ISO 13485, and AS9100 establish the baseline quality management system. However, certification alone does not guarantee production maturity. The differentiator lies in how thoroughly processes are defined, validated, and controlled.
