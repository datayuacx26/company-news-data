## **Why Do You Get Dandruff?**


Dandruff commonly results from an excess of a yeast fungus known as Malassezia that is found on all people's scalps. But in some individuals it overgrows and causes an excess of skin cell turnover and irritation. This speed up the flaking process and may cause itching and redness. Other common causes are oily scalps, dry skin, sensitivity to certain haircare products, infrequent hair washing and stress or changes in humidity level according to seasons.


****


Conditions like[seborrheic dermatitis](https://www.clinikally.com/blogs/news/how-to-get-rid-of-seborrheic-dermatitis-on-scalp) and mild[psoriasis](https://www.clinikally.com/blogs/news/treat-scalp-psoriasis) can also mimic or worsen dandruff symptoms. Diet, hormonal shifts, and a weakened skin barrier play a role too. Understanding the root cause matters because it helps you choose the right treatment. Someone dealing with an oily scalp needs a very different approach than someone whose flakes stem from dryness, which is exactly why picking the best shampoo for dandruff based on your actual scalp condition, rather than trends or packaging, makes such a big difference in results.


## **Common Signs You Need an Anti-Dandruff Shampoo**


Not sure if what you're dealing with is just a dry scalp or full-blown dandruff? Here are a some common signs that it's time to switch to a dedicated formula:


-


Visible white or yellowish flakes on your scalp or hair.


-


Persistent itchiness that doesn't go away after washing.


-


A scalp that feels oily yet still flakes.


-


Redness, irritation, or mild burning sensation on the scalp.


-


Flaking that gets worse with weather changes, stress, or certain products.


-


Hair that looks dull or feels greasy soon after washing.

## **How to Choose the Best Anti-Dandruff Shampoo for Your Hair Type**


Before choosing an anti dandruff shampoo, start by[identifying your scalp type](https://www.clinikally.com/blogs/news/how-to-know-your-scalp-type-a-comprehensive-guide) . Then, look for ingredients that are suitable for your hair type to help manage dandruff without compromising your hair's health. Here's what to look for based on your hair type:


### **For Oily Scalp**


Look for lightweight, oil-control formulas with ingredients like zinc pyrithione or salicylic acid. These help regulate excess sebum while clearing flakes, without weighing your hair down or leaving a heavy residue behind after each wash.


### **For Dry Scalp**


Select an anti-dandruff shampoo that contains anti-dandruff agents such as piroctone olamine, along with moisturizing agents such as glycerin or aloe vera. This blend helps to regulate flakes and avoid any dryness and tightness.


### **For Sensitive Scalp**


Choose a mild, non-irritating, fragrance-free with calming ingredients that contain less zinc pyrithione. Try to avoid the harsh sulfates, since they may aggravate redness, itching and irritation of a sensitive scalp.


### **For Color-Treated Hair**


Choose a sulfate-free shampoo specifically designed to help with dandruff without stripping colour. Ingredients, such as piroctone olamine are kinder to treated hair and support a healthy scalp and lustrous locks.


### **Anti Dandruff Shampoo for Women**


Many women love formulas that offer dandruff control along with extra attributes such as frizz control, shine or volume. The ideal anti dandruff shampoo for women should strike a balance between hair texture and style and scalp treatment.


### **Best Anti Dandruff Shampoo for Men**


Men's scalps are also known to produce more oil, and a good anti dandruff shampoo for men will have more potent ingredients and be lightweight with a non-greasy formula that is best for shorter hair and more frequent washings.

### **Ketoconazole**


This anti-fungal component works directly on Malassezia, the yeast that is the cause of most cases of dandruff. It is one of the best and most popular products for persistent or recurring flakes, and is recommended for moderate to severe dandruff issues.


### **Zinc Pyrithione**


Zinc pyrithione has been found to have antibacterial and antifungal effects, which helps to control oil production and yeast overgrowth on the scalp. It is a popular ingredient in daily anti-dandruff formulas because of its mild, yet effective action.


### **Selenium Sulfide**


This ingredient slows the rate of turnover in scalp skin cells, directly reducing flaking. It is particularly beneficial when treating more visible, persistent dandruff and is commonly used in medicated shampoos.


### **Salicylic Acid**


Acting as a gentle exfoliant, salicylic acid helps clear away dead skin cells and product buildup from the scalp. It works well for oily scalps prone to flaking and clogged follicles.


### **Coal Tar**


Over time,[Coal Tar](https://www.clinikally.com/blogs/news/coal-tar-for-haircare) helps to slow down the growth of skin cells, which can lead to reduced scaling and itchiness. An older ingredient that has been extensively studied and used in more stubborn and persistent dandruff problems.


### **Piroctone Olamine**


Piroctone olamine works to control flake-causing yeast, but it's milder than some of the stronger antifungals, and is also gentle enough not harm sensitive or color-treated hair, making it a popular ingredient in today's formulas.


## **How to Use Anti-Dandruff Shampoo Correctly?**


Using the right shampoo won't help much if it isn't applied correctly. Keep these tips in mind for the best results:


-


**Focus on the scalp:** Massage the shampoo into the scalp itself, not just the hair strands, since that's where the flakes and irritation actually originate.


-


**Let it sit:** Leave the shampoo on for 3-5 minutes so ingredients like ketoconazole or zinc pyrithione have enough time to act on the scalp.


-


**Rinse thoroughly:** Wash out all the product completely, as leftover residue can clog follicles and trigger further irritation or buildup.


-


**Stick to a routine:** **** Use it 2-3 times a week, or as directed on the label, since inconsistent use often delays visible results.


-


**Conditioning:** **** Apply a lightweight[conditioner](https://www.clinikally.com/collections/conditioners) afterward if needed, focusing on the lengths rather than the scalp.


## **Tips to Prevent Dandruff from Coming Back**


Getting rid of dandruff is one step, but maintaining it requires consistent habits. You can try some simple tips:


-


**Regular washing:** Wash your hair on a consistent schedule to prevent oil, sweat, and product buildup from accumulating on the scalp and feeding the yeast that causes flakes.


-


**Limit heat styling:** Excessive use of blow dryers, straighteners, or curling irons can dry out and irritate the scalp, making it more prone to flaking.


-


**Manage stress:** High stress levels can disrupt hormonal balance and trigger or worsen dandruff flare-ups, so relaxation habits genuinely help.


-


**Eat a balanced diet:** Include zinc,[omega-3 fatty acids](https://www.clinikally.com/blogs/news/your-tresses-influence-of-omega-3-fatty-acids-on-hair-health) , and B vitamins in your meals, since nutritional gaps can show up as a flaky, irritated scalp.


-


**Avoid sharing personal items:** Combs, towels, and hair accessories can transfer oils, fungus, and product buildup between people, so keep these to yourself.


-


**Rotate your shampoo:** **** Switch formulas periodically if flakes start returning, as scalps can build tolerance to the same active ingredients over time.
