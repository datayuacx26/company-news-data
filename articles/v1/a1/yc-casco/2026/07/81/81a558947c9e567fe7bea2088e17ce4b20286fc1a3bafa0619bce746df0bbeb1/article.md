8 minute read


Every packing guide for a Vegas conference says the same two things: it’s hot outside, it’s cold inside, bring layers. True-ish.


What those guides leave out is the third climate: a Business Hall full of 20,000 people under expo lights. You can be too hot and too cold in the same hour, repeatedly, for a week.

01 / The three-climate problem


## Dress for all three.


It can be 108°F outside, cold enough for a layer in the session rooms, and warm in the Business Hall by mid-afternoon. Wear something that can handle all three without taking over your day.


Outside


108°


### The heat is real


You may only be outside for a rideshare pickup, a pool event, or a walk that looked shorter on the map. Bring sunglasses and sunscreen anyway.


Sessions


62°


### Bring one easy layer


Session rooms can be genuinely cold. Pick something you can take off, tie to your bag, and put back on without thinking.


Business Hall


20K


### Dress for the crowd


By mid-afternoon, the show floor is packed and warm. Dark, breathable shirts hold up better than a heavy company hoodie.


### Wear the shoes you already trust


Mandalay Bay is enormous, and many “quick” indoor walks take 20 minutes.


15,000 steps / day


The Strip after dark, when the temperature finally becomes negotiable.

03 / The Black Hat-specific stuff


## Decide at home what you are bringing.


A clean travel laptop, just your phone, or your everyday laptop can all be reasonable choices. Pick the setup that matches your risk, secure it at home, and avoid second-guessing it all week.


Dark rooms, bright screens, full seats.


Photo by[Stem List](https://unsplash.com/photos/audience-in-a-conference-EVgsAbL51Rk)


OPTION 01


### Clean travel laptop


A fresh setup with only the files and accounts you need for the week.


OPTION 02


### Just your phone


Enough for your schedule, messages, and email if you do not need to work.


OPTION 03


### Everyday laptop


Fine if it is patched, encrypted, and cleared of anything you do not need.


### Bring your own connection


The official network is professionally run and closely monitored. The NOC regularly discusses what it sees. A hotspot or eSIM lets you make your own choice and move on.


### Bring enough battery for it


If your phone is also your hotspot, it will drain quickly. A reliable power bank is worth the extra weight.

Vendors bring a lot of giveaways, and some of them are genuinely useful. Leave room for what you want to take home. We will have a few things waiting at Booth 5300, too.

05 / Mandalay Bay, explained once


## Vegas distances are deceptive.


Mandalay Bay is at the far south end of the Strip, and the complex itself is enormous. Plan around where you are staying and give every meeting more travel time than you think it needs.


Stay connected


### Mandalay Bay · Delano · Four Seasons


All three connect directly to the convention center, so you can do the whole week without stepping outside. Staying elsewhere usually means rideshares in August traffic.


Free tram


### Mandalay Bay → Luxor → Excalibur


The tram is an easy, air-conditioned way to head north without walking outside or paying a surge price.


Internal distance


### Give yourself 20 minutes


A room-to-room walk inside the same complex can take 15–20 minutes. If the meeting matters, leave earlier than feels necessary.


DEF CON logistics


### About four miles north


DEF CON is at the Las Vegas Convention Center. Take the tram to Excalibur and the Monorail from MGM Grand, or allow 20–30 minutes for a rideshare.


Make time for it


### Mandalay Bay Beach


The pool is genuinely good in August, and plenty of evening events end up there during conference week.


Mandalay Bay Beach is worth a midweek break.
