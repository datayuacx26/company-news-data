### The New Drone Stack: Trusted, Modular and Built for the Mission


#### The U.S. drone market is entering a major transition.


Recent federal actions have placed new restrictions on foreign-manufactured drones and critical UAS components, including new models from DJI, Autel and other manufacturers that have historically played a large role in the U.S. market.


This does not mean every existing DJI drone disappears overnight. Many previously authorized drones remain in use today. But the direction of the market is changing. For new programs, new platforms and regulated deployments, customers are increasingly prioritizing trusted U.S. and Western suppliers that can provide secure components, transparent supply chains, long-term availability and support for mission-specific requirements.


The impact extends across every major drone vertical.


####
In agriculture, drones are used for crop monitoring, spraying, mapping and field intelligence.


As farmers and agriculture service providers evaluate future fleets, they need platforms that are available, supportable and trusted for long-term field operations.


In commercial and industrial markets, drones are becoming essential tools for inspection, mapping, construction, energy, utilities, security and emergency response. These customers need more than a capable airframe. They need a full technology stack that can be validated, supported and adapted to specialized workflows.


In aerospace and defense, FPV and small UAS platforms built around open source PX4, open architectures and off-the-shelf components are increasingly being adapted for mission-specific applications such as situational awareness, reconnaissance, perimeter monitoring and tactical support. As these systems move from commercial and hobbyist roots into A&D programs, trust in the component supply chain, software stack and manufacturing ecosystem becomes essential.


#### This is creating a new opportunity for U.S. and Western drone OEMs, component suppliers and technology partners.


The market is moving from dependence on closed, overseas drone ecosystems toward trusted, modular platforms built around secure compute, validated components, onboard AI and domestic capabilities.


As the U.S. and European drone market shifts toward trusted solutions, the winners will be the companies that can deliver more than drones. They will deliver the compute, software, sensors, supply chain and engineering foundation needed to build the next generation of intelligent, secure and mission-ready UAS platforms.

The Open-Q 6490CS SOM enables scalable, power efficient drone designs for commercial, agriculture, public safety and industrial applications.
