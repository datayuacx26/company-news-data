# PrimeLister Review 2026: What It Gets Right (and Wrong)

PrimeLister is a solid pick if you only want a standalone Poshmark or eBay bot, but it starts to fall when you look at it as a full crosslisting tool


. If you're a reseller trying to decide whether PrimeLister is worth your money in 2026, it really comes down to what you actually need it to do.


The tool is genuinely good at automating things like sharing closets and sending offers. But once you try using it across multiple platforms, it gets a little less impressive. Because it runs on a Chrome extension instead of the cloud, crosslisting can be slow and ties up your computer while it works.


On top of that, the pricing is split into pieces you build together instead of one plan, so you end up paying way more for a full toolkit than you would with a cloud-based option like Vendoo.


This PrimeLister review


breaks down exactly what it does well, where it falls short, and how it compares to more modern alternatives, so you can decide if it's actually the right fit for your business.


## Main Takeaways


- PrimeLister's Poshmark ($24.99/mo) and eBay ($15/mo) bots work well and can save you time on shares, offers, and relists.


- It's a Chrome extension, which means it runs on your browser, on your computer screen, and needs good internet access to keep it from crashing mid-task.


- If you want both bots along with crosslisting, you're looking at almost $90/month.


- Some sellers have reported inventory syncing issues that can lead to accidentally selling the same item on two platforms.


- There's no mobile app, so everything runs through your desktop.


## What PrimeLister Actually Does Well


There's a reason people use PrimeLister: the automation. If you're a single-platform seller who just wants to stop doing repetitive tasks by hand, the bots deliver on that at a good price. Here's what PrimeLister does best.


### **Poshmark Automation**


This automation bot shares your closet on a schedule, does community shares, and sends offers to anyone who's liked your stuff, all without you touching your phone.


This feature costs $24.99/month


and if you've got a big closet, it can be a huge time savings compared to manually tackling these tasks.


It keeps your closet looking active, which keeps your items bumped and keeps Poshmark's algorithm happy. If you're someone who used to spend an hour or two a day manually sharing your whole closet just to stay visible, this basically hands that job off completely


.


### **eBay Automation**


If you're an eBay seller, PrimeLister's eBay Automation will relist your stale items automatically and send offers to watchers who liked something but never committed to buying.


Both of these actions help to boost your sales as well as help you out in eBay's algorithm


, without you having to do anything. eBay tends to bury listings that sit too long without any activity, so having something relisting your slow movers in the background can make a real difference in what shows up in search.


### **Variable Task Intervals**


One thing that often gets overlooked: PrimeLister randomizes the gaps between shares and clicks so the activity looks more human


. That matters, because marketplaces can be quick to flag accounts that look bot-like, so this type of detail keeps your account safe. It's a small feature, but it's the kind of thing that shows the bots were actually built with real seller accounts in mind, not just speed.


## The Dealbreakers: Where the Tool Falls Short


Once you look past the automation features, some issues start to pop up, and they're the kind that matter more the bigger your business gets. A single-platform seller might not even notice most of these, but anyone trying to expand their audience with crosslisting will feel them fast. Here's where PrimeLister doesn't rank as well.


### **The "Form-Filling" Trap**


PrimeLister is built as a Chrome extension, which means it works by opening new browser tabs and typing your listing data into them right on your screen


, a method often called "form-filling." This is different from newer, cloud-based tools that process your listings on their own servers in the background.


Because PrimeLister runs through your browser, it ties up your screen while it's working and needs a solid internet connection to keep it working efficiently. In practice, that means you can't really do anything else on your computer while it's crosslisting


, and a spotty connection can stall the process.


### **Fragmented, Expensive Pricing**


PrimeLister's prices may look affordable at first, but it can get expensive quickly. The $29.99 "Crosslisting Basic" plan doesn't include true crosslisting across multiple platforms


, so to get the Poshmark bot, the eBay bot, and the "Crosslisting Pro" features, you end up stacking up three separate subscriptions.


Add those up and you're paying for the Poshmark bot, the eBay bot, and a crosslisting plan just to get the full toolkit, not one bundled price.


This e


nds up costing you nearly $90/month once everything's combined.


### **Ghost Inventory**


Many sellers have reported PrimeLister failing to sync inventory properly across platforms


. If you forget to manually delist an item, or the browser extension glitches, you risk selling the same item twice.


While mistakes happen, this can seriously damage your seller metrics and reputation if it happens too often


, and it also means refunding a buyer and possibly getting a bad review.


### **No Mobile App**


Since PrimeLister relies on a desktop Chrome extension, there's no way to crosslist or manage your inventory from your phone.
