# 10 Best Sites for SecondHand Items in 2026 (For Buyers & Sellers)

The best sites for second-hand items in 2026 are eBay, Poshmark, Mercari, Depop, and Vinted


, each offering a mix of vintage fashion, refurbished electronics, and general home goods. For buyers, shopping secondhand online has never been easier or safer, thanks to stricter authentication and buyer protection policies across most platforms.


For sellers, though, sticking to just one platform doesn't really cut it anymore. Every app attracts a different crowd, so the sellers doing well in 2026 are listing across multiple sites at once, usually with software handling the busywork of getting everything posted everywhere.


Whether you're hunting for good deals or trying to clear out your closet, picking the right platform (or platforms) makes a real difference


. Below, we'll break down the best sites for fashion, electronics, and general goods, how their fees compare, and how sellers are managing all of it without losing their minds.


## Main Takeaways


- eBay, Poshmark, Mercari and Depop are some of the best sites for second-hand items in 2026, but each one caters to a completely different crowd.


- Seller fees vary a lot by platform, from 0% on Vinted, to a flat 10% on Mercari, to as much as 20% on Poshmark.


- Buying secondhand isn't just about saving money, it also cuts down on textile waste and the demand for new manufacturing.


- Selling on just one platform limits your reach, and most sellers doing well in 2026 list across multiple sites at once.


## Best Sites for Second-Hand Clothing and Fashion


If you're buying or selling clothes, these are the platforms actually worth your time, and each one has a different type of audience.


### **Poshmark**


****


Still one of the best places for secondhand women's apparel and mall brands. Buyers get flat-rate shipping no matter the item, which makes it great for bundles, and the platform's social features (sharing, following, live shows) keep the community active. Sellers pay a 20% commission on items over $15


, which is steep in comparison to all other sites, but it can be a fair trade off for access to buyers already on there looking to shop.


### **Depop**


****


This is where Gen Z and streetwear shoppers usually shop and sell. Buyers can dig up unique pieces, Y2K fashion, vintage denim, one-of-a-kind reworked items, that you won't really find anywhere else. Sellers pay around a 10% fee, and trendy pieces can sell within hours of listing.


### **Vinted**


Vinted's growth has been hard to ignore, especially for buyers hunting casual, budget-friendly clothing. It charges sellers zero fees. Instead, buyers pay a small "buyer protection" fee at checkout. That makes it a solid spot for buyers looking for everyday basics at low prices, and for sellers offloading the kind of inventory where a 20% cut wouldn't make sense.


### **Grailed**


****


The go-to for high-end menswear. Buyers come here specifically for designer archives, verified sneakers, and pieces that have been authenticated, which matters a lot when you're spending a lot of money on secondhand luxury.


### **ThredUp**


****


Buyers get a huge selection of pre-owned clothing, which makes browsing easier than digging through individual sellers' closets. For sellers, you mail in a bag of clothes and ThredUp handles the listing, photos, and shipping. It's a lower payout per item, but about as hands-off as reselling gets.


## Best Sites for Electronics, Vintage, and General Goods


Fashion isn't the only category worth shopping secondhand. Refurbished laptops, vintage furniture, old collectibles, and general household goods all have their own dedicated marketplaces too. Here are the best sites for secondhand items beyond clothing.


### eBay


With over 130 million active buyers worldwide, eBay is still the biggest marketplace out there


. If you're hunting for something obscure, a rare vintage find, a specific electronics part, a discontinued collectible, this is usually where you'll find it. Sellers benefit from that same reach, since a niche item that won’t move anywhere else will often find its buyer here.


### Mercari


The most user-friendly app for general goods: toys, video games, small home items, everyday stuff. Buyers get a clean, simple shopping experience. As of 2026, Mercari charges sellers a flat 10% selling fee with no separate payment processing charge


, which is simpler and often cheaper than stacked fee structures like eBay's or Poshmark's.


### **Facebook Marketplace**


****


The go-to for bulky items that can't be shipped, think couches, dressers, large appliances, but smaller items sell well here too. Buyers can browse locally on FBMP and pick items up in person, and sellers avoid shipping costs and fees entirely with cash meetups. Shipping is also an option for smaller items if you'd rather not meet up in person.


### **OfferUp**


****


Similar to Facebook Marketplace in that it's built around local buying and selling, but with the option to ship smaller items too. OfferUp is a solid spot for electronics, furniture, and general household goods, especially for buyers who'd rather deal with someone local than wait on shipping.


### **Etsy**


****


Has a huge vintage marketplace built in, and it's worth knowing about even if you don't think of Etsy as a resale site. Buyers come here specifically for genuine vintage furniture, art, and decor, pieces with actual history, not mass-produced reproductions. For sellers, it's a great site to list vintage finds.
