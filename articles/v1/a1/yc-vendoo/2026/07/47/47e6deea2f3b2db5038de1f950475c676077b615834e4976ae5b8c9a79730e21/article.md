## How to Identify True Vintage Clothing


Finding the great vintage brands to resell is only part of the equation


. Successful vintage resellers also learn how to recognize authentic vintage garments, especially when the label is missing, unfamiliar, or no longer in business.


Fortunately, vintage clothing often reveals its age through construction techniques, materials, labels, and manufacturing details. The more of these characteristics you recognize, the easier it becomes to identify profitable pieces while sourcing.


Look for these common indicators:


- Single stitch construction on t-shirts (rather than the double-stitch used today)
- Selvedge denim and redline selvedge
- Paper garment tags
- Sheer care labels commonly found in dresses intended to be worn with slips
- Hand-finished hems and seams
- Metal zippers instead of plastic
- Union labels, including ILGWU tags
- Made in USA, Made in UK, or Made in France labels
- Natural fiber content such as wool, linen, silk, and cotton


None of these features alone guarantees that a garment is vintage, but several appearing together can provide strong evidence of its age.


### Learn to Recognize Union Labels


Union labels are one of the most useful dating tools available to vintage resellers. Organizations such as the International Ladies' Garment Workers' Union (ILGWU) periodically updated their labels over the decades, allowing collectors to estimate when many garments were manufactured.

### Don't Ignore Lesser-Known Brands


Many new vintage resellers focus exclusively on recognizable names like Levi's or Ralph Lauren. While those brands are excellent finds, countless lesser-known labels can also sell exceptionally well.


Brands such as Jonathan Logan, Carole Little, Albert Nipon, Leslie Fay, Lilli Ann, Koret of California, Pendleton 49er, Lady Manhattan, and White Stag regularly attract vintage buyers despite being less familiar to the average shopper.


Instead of judging an item solely by the brand name, evaluate the garment as a whole


. Era, quality, style, rarity, and condition often matter just as much as the label itself.

## Vintage Sizing and Measurement Best Practices


Many vintage pieces do not have size tags. Even when they do, vintage sizing differs significantly from modern sizing standards. For this reason, you want to include both the " tagged vintage size


" (for accuracy) and the " best modern fit size


" (for practicality).


For SEO purposes, many vintage sellers select a modern size equivalent


as the size when listing. If you have a blouse tagged as a size large, but it fits more like a modern small, listing it as large *wouldn't make much sense,* as the listing would only be shown to buyers who are size large.


Be sure to always include


detailed measurements


, including chest/bust, shoulders, sleeves, waist, hips, inseam, leg opening, and length.


Clear measurements reduce returns and build buyer confidence, especially for higher-value vintage items.

## Best Vintage Brands FAQ


### What is considered vintage clothing?


Clothing is generally considered vintage once it is at least 20 years old. Items that are 100 years old or older are typically classified as antiques. Authentic vintage should not be confused with modern "vintage-inspired" clothing


### Which vintage brands sell for the most money?


Brands such as Levi's, Chanel, Louis Vuitton, Harley-Davidson, Gunne Sax, Ralph Lauren, and designer labels often command the highest resale prices. Value also depends on rarity, condition, style, and era. Scroll up for a full list!


### Where is the best place to find vintage clothing?


Many resellers source vintage clothing at thrift stores, estate sales, church rummage sales, flea markets, yard sales, storage auctions, and Facebook Marketplace.


### What are the best platforms to sell vintage clothing?


Vintage performs especially well on Etsy, Depop, eBay, Poshmark, Vinted, Instagram, and Vestiaire Collective. Each platform attracts slightly different buyers, so crosslisting often leads to faster sales.


### How can you tell if an item is vintage?


Look for details such as single-stitch construction, metal zippers, union labels, paper tags, natural fibers, and Made in USA or similar country-of-origin labels. Multiple indicators together provide stronger evidence of age.


### How should I handle vintage sizing in listings?


Always include detailed measurements and explain the best modern fit. You can reference the tagged vintage size for accuracy, but sizing should be guided by measurements to reduce returns and confusion.
