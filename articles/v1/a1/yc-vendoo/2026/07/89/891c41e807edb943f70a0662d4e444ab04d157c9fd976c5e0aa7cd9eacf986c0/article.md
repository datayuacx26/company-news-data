# What Does CIB Mean on eBay? Complete-In-Box Guide

## What Does CIB Mean on eBay?


CIB stands for Complete In Box


, and on eBay that means a listing includes the original box, the manual or instruction booklet, and any inserts that shipped with the item, not just the bare disc, cartridge, or unit by itself.


That's the direct answer to what does CIB mean on eBay: it's a middle condition tier, priced above a loose, item-only listing but below a factory-sealed or new copy. You'll see the term most often on video game listings, but it shows up just as often on vintage toys, cameras, and other boxed collectibles where buyers care whether the original packaging survived.


## Main Takeaways


- **CIB** means Complete In Box: the original box, manual or instructions, inserts, and the item itself, with no factory seal.
- **Middle-tier pricing** puts CIB between loose and new/sealed on guides like PriceCharting and GoCollect.
- **No enforced standard** exists, so sellers disagree on whether registration cards, hang tabs, or foam inserts must be present to call something CIB.
- **Price gaps** are real: CIB copies commonly sell for 2-4x the loose price, but a fraction of what a sealed or graded copy brings.
- **Beyond gaming** , vintage toys, cameras, and electronics listings use CIB the same way: box, manual, unit, and inserts.
- **Photos over labels** matter most, since box wear, a torn manual, or a missing insert can drag down value even on a technically complete listing.

Before comparing a CIB listing's asking price to anything else, it helps to see where CIB actually sits on eBay's condition ladder. eBay and third-party price guides split video game and collectible listings into four rough tiers, each commanding a different price for the same title.


- **Loose** : the item alone, no box, no manual, no inserts, the cheapest tier.
- **CIB (Complete In Box)** : the box, manual, and inserts included, but opened and used.
- **New/Sealed** : never opened since it left the manufacturer, commanding a large premium over CIB.
- **Graded** : authenticated and encapsulated by a service like WATA or VGA, sitting above even sealed copies for high-demand titles. Grading-service reputations vary and have shifted over time, so confirm a service's current standing before paying a grading premium.


That authentication is exactly why graded copies of rare titles can outprice sealed ones: a grading service verifies both the seal and the condition of what's inside it

### What Exactly Counts as "Complete"


Source: CGC Games


For complete in box video games, most sellers agree on a baseline definition of "complete": the original box, the manual or instruction booklet, the disc or cartridge itself, and whatever inserts shipped inside the box, such as:


- Foam padding or plastic trays
- Registration cards
- Protective flaps or hang tabs
- Any other manufacturer-included insert


A Nintendo 64 game missing its cardboard insert isn't CIB by the strict definition


, even if the box and cartridge are both present. That's where the grey areas start. Some sellers call a game CIB without its registration card or a Sega Saturn's spine flap, on the logic that those pieces didn't affect gameplay and get lost easily over time.


Collector forums debate exactly where the line sits between complete and complete enough, and eBay has no rule settling it either way.


Console CIB is a different animal than single-game CIB


. A complete console listing needs the console itself, all cables, at least one controller, and the box. Check listing photos component by component either way, since a missing AC adapter or a single controller instead of two changes the deal even when the seller's title says CIB.


## CIB vs Other eBay Video Game Condition Terms

Loose means the item only: no box, no manual, nothing else


. It's the cheapest tier and, for video games, often the easiest to verify, since a cartridge or disc is harder to fake than a full retail box. Loose buyers take on less authentication risk, but they give up the collectible upside that comes with the box and manual.


New/Sealed sits at the top of the range, but the label hides an important split. A factory-sealed copy has never been opened since it left the manufacturer, while a resealed copy has been opened and shrink-wrapped again, sometimes to hide a missing manual or a swapped disc. eBay's item condition guidance tells sellers to describe condition accurately, but it can't stop a reseal from looking sealed in photos


. Catching that is squarely a matter of knowing how to avoid eBay scams before you pay.


Outside gaming, CIB shows up on vintage toy listings with original packaging and inserts, and on cameras with a body, lens cap, strap, manual, and case. The logic carries over unchanged.


## How CIB Status Affects Price and Buying Decisions


CIB commands a premium over loose because it's more display-ready and more liquid at resale. A buyer choosing between a bare cartridge and a boxed copy with its manual will usually pay more for the version that looks complete on a shelf.


That premium narrows fast once you compare it to sealed or graded copies of the same title: a CIB copy of a mid-tier SNES game might run three times its loose price


, while a sealed copy of the same game runs ten times or more.


For high-value titles, third-party grading from WATA, VGA, or CGC adds another tier above sealed. A graded slab authenticates both the seal and the game's condition inside it, something no seller's CIB label can do on its own.


Grading isn't a guarantee of resale value by itself, since a slab still needs a buyer willing to pay for that specific title and grade combination, so weigh the premium against actual demand before paying for it. That authentication is exactly why grading fees run into the hundreds of dollars for rare titles, and why it isn't worth it for a common release.

## Tips for Sellers Listing CIB Items on eBay


A few habits cut down on item-not-as-described disputes and buyer confusion when you're listing CIB items.


### **Photograph every component separately**


Box exterior, box interior, the manual, any inserts, and the item itself out of the box. A single photo showing everything piled together invites an item-not-as-described claim, and eBay case outcomes generally favor whichever side documented condition better.


### **Separate completeness from physical condition**


"CIB, box has shelf wear and a corner tear" builds more trust than "CIB, excellent condition," because it tells the buyer what they're actually getting instead of asking them to take your word for pristine.


### **Use eBay's category-specific item specifics**


Like the dedicated Completeness dropdown in the video game category, instead of burying condition details in the title or description alone. That field gets indexed and searched independently of your listing text. ## Frequently asked questions


### Does CIB mean the item is new?


No. CIB means the box, manual, and inserts are included with the item, but the item's been opened and used, unlike a factory-sealed copy. It sits in the middle of eBay's condition scale: worth more than a loose, item-only listing, but well below a new or sealed one.


### What's the difference between CIB and New/Sealed?


CIB has been opened, so the box, manual, and item are all present but not factory-sealed. New/Sealed has never been opened, though a resealed copy can look sealed while hiding a missing manual or swapped disc, so it's worth checking photos closely. Buyers pay the largest premium for verified factory-sealed or graded copies.


### Why do CIB prices vary so much for the same game or item?


Because CIB isn't a standardized grade. One seller's CIB listing might skip a registration card that another insists on including, and box condition, insert completeness, and packaging differences all affect what a buyer will pay. Checking a price guide like PriceCharting or GoCollect against the listing photos is the fastest way to sanity-check an asking price.


### Does CIB apply to things other than video games?


Yes. Vintage toys, cameras, and small electronics use CIB the same way: the original box, manual, and inserts included alongside the item itself. A camera listed as CIB, for example, should come with its body, lens cap, strap, manual, and case, not just the camera body.


### How do I verify a seller's CIB claim is accurate before buying?


Check photos for the box, manual, and inserts individually, not just a stack shot showing everything piled together. Compare the asking price to a guide like PriceCharting, which tracks loose, CIB, and new prices separately, since a big gap from the CIB average can signal a reseal or a missing piece. Ask the seller directly about anything the photos don't clearly show before you pay.
