# What Is a Rainscreen? Understanding the Layer Behind Advanced Moisture Management

When most people think about a home's exterior, they only focus on the material they see on the outside. But behind every high-performing exterior wall system is a series of components working together to manage moisture and help protect the structure. One term that's becoming increasingly common in residential and commercial construction is **rainscreen** . While it's often associated with modern, high-performance building design, many homeowners and even some builders may not fully understand what a rainscreen is, how it works, or when it might be beneficial.


**What Is a Rainscreen?** A rainscreen is a wall design approach that creates a drainage and ventilation space between the siding and the wall assembly behind it. Rather than relying on the siding alone to keep water out, a rainscreen system recognizes that wind-driven rain and moisture can occasionally get behind exterior cladding. The goal is to provide a pathway for that moisture to drain and dry before it impacts the wall assembly.


A typical rainscreen assembly includes:


-


Exterior cladding (such as siding)


-


An air gap or drainage cavity


-


A water-resistive barrier (WRB)


-


Structural wall sheathing and framing


Together, these components help manage water, improve drying potential, and support long-term wall performance.


**Why Moisture Management Matters**


Moisture is one of the biggest threats to a building's durability.


When water becomes trapped within a wall assembly, it can contribute to:


-


Fungal decay


-


Mold and mildew growth


-


Reduced insulation performance


-


Premature material deterioration


That's why modern building science focuses on managing moisture rather than assuming it can be completely prevented.


A properly designed wall assembly gives water a way to escape and allows the wall to dry if moisture does get inside.


**How Does a Rainscreen Work?**


Think of a rainscreen like a drainage plane behind your siding.


When rain hits the exterior of a home:


1.


The siding acts as the first line of defense.


2.


Any moisture that gets behind the cladding can drain downward through the cavity.


3.


Air movement within the cavity helps promote drying.


4.


The water-resistive barrier provides an additional layer of protection for the structure.


This layered approach helps create a more resilient building envelope.


**Do All Homes Need a Rainscreen?** Building requirements vary by climate, wall design, local codes, and project goals. In areas that experience frequent rainfall, high humidity, or significant wind-driven rain, builders may choose rainscreen assemblies to improve moisture management and drying potential. Many architects and building professionals incorporate rainscreen principles into high-performance wall systems because they can help support long-term durability. Since every project is different, builders should always follow local code requirements, manufacturer installation instructions, and project specifications.

**Benefits of a Well-Designed Wall Assembly** Whether or not a project includes a dedicated rainscreen system, builders increasingly focus on creating walls that effectively manage moisture and support long-term performance. Potential benefits of a well-designed assembly include:


-

-


Enhanced drying potential


-


Long-term durability


-


Advanced protection of structural components


-


Increased confidence in varying weather conditions


**The Bottom Line** A rainscreen is not a product—it's a building design strategy that helps manage moisture behind exterior cladding.
