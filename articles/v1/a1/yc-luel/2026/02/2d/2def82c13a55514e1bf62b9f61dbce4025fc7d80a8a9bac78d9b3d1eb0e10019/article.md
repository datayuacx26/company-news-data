# Launching Luel: a rights-cleared marketplace and collection engine for multimodal data

Frontier labs have hit a wall. Public web data is tapped out, synthetic-only pipelines risk degeneration, and the next generation of models needs rights-cleared multimodal data that doesn't exist at scale on the open web. Luel is the marketplace — and the global contributor network behind it — built to close that gap. We're announcing it today as part of the \[Y Combinator W26 batch\](https://www.ycombinator.com/launches/PKl-luel-the-marketplace-for-multimodal-data).


## The problem


The internet's "easy data" is largely exhausted. What's left is low-signal, repetitive, or messy. Companies are spending huge budgets on generic, low-signal datasets from legacy vendors, and most of those datasets fail production requirements: unclear rights, weak provenance, missing consent, inconsistent metadata.


Frontier labs are running into the same constraint at the same time. The text on the open web is no longer enough. The new generation of models — embodied agents, robotics policies, speech systems, multimodal assistants — needs first-person video, paired sensor data, regional speech, in-the-wild interactions. None of this exists at scale on the open web, and the supply that does exist rarely clears procurement.


## What we're building


Luel is two things stitched together.


The first is a marketplace of completed datasets, ready to license. The catalog ranges from patient–doctor conversations in South Asia to gemstone-manufacturing footage for robotics. Each entry ships with consent evidence, chain-of-title and audit logs.


The second is a collection engine: a global, vetted contributor network that produces datasets to a buyer's spec, scoped end-to-end with QA, recruitment, payouts and delivery. The same pipeline that licenses off-the-shelf datasets also runs custom collections — nothing is one-off.


The output is the same in both directions: rights-cleared data with a provenance trail that holds up under enterprise review.


## How it works


An AI team submits a dataset spec — modality, scenario, instructions, devices, QA rules. We post the listing and instantly match vetted contributors against it. Submissions run through multi-stage QA. The dataset is delivered, typically within days, with rights documentation attached.


What ships isn't raw footage. It's a structured dataset — manifest, sensor channels, annotations, provenance, consent — auditable end-to-end against the spec it was collected for.


## Why now


Three things converged into the same window. Public-web data is no longer enough to keep training-set sizes growing. Synthetic-only data has documented failure modes for the model classes labs are trying to ship. And procurement teams at the largest buyers will no longer accept datasets whose rights story they cannot verify.


That is the constraint Luel was built to solve. The catalog and the collection engine sit on the same rails, so that whatever shape the next training run takes — speech, video, sensor, multimodal — the data layer underneath is paid at the source, licensed at the surface, and instrumented for audit.
