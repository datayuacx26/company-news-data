# Getting Around in Canada Without a Car

Whether it’s battling the traffic in busy cities like Toronto and Vancouver or trying to navigate small towns and suburbs, getting around Canada can sometimes feel like a chore, especially if you don’t own a car or drive anymore. But reliable transportation for seniors plays a crucial role in maintaining independence, confidence, and quality of life.


In this article, we’ll review the types of senior transportation options in Canada that make it easy to stay active and connected in your life without needing to drive.


## **Why More Seniors in Canada Are Choosing Not to Drive**


Driving in Canada can be challenging at any age, but icy roads, snowstorms, heavy traffic, and long distances can make it especially stressful for older adults. It’s also common for seniors to feel uncomfortable driving due to changing health status, like arthritis, vision loss, or medication side effects like sleepiness or dizziness. Add in the cost of insurance, gas, maintenance, and parking – *and* the stress that comes with car ownership – it’s no surprise that many people are hanging up the keys and opting for alternative senior transportation options instead.


Keep reading to learn more about the senior transportation options available to you (and why we think you’ll prefer personalized ride services like GoGo that are tailored to the needs of older adults).


### **Option 1: Public Transportation**


Canada’s major cities are known for robust public transit systems like buses, subways, streetcars, and commuter trains. Many provinces offer discounted fares for older adults, making public transit a budget-friendly form of senior transportation.


That said, public transit isn’t always ideal. Long walks to stops, crowded vehicles, steep stairs, whacky weather, and fixed schedules are limiting, stressful, and exhausting. While it works well for some, many seniors prefer more personalized ride services that better accommodate comfort, mobility, schedule, and lifestyle.


### **Option 2: Community and Municipal Transportation for Seniors**


Across Canada, many municipalities and non-profits offer community-based transportation for seniors. These programs often include shuttles, vans, or volunteer drivers who provide scheduled rides for seniors to medical appointments, grocery stores, or senior centers.


While these senior transportation programs can be invaluable to many, they usually come with restrictions. Trips must be booked far in advance, routes are limited, and flexibility is often minimal. For seniors who want more independence, on-demand ride services are becoming the preferred solution.
