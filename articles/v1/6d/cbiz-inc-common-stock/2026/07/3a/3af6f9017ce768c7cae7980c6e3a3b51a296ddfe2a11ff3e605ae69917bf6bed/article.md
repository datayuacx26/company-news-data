# Responding to an IRS Exempt Status Examination: A Guide for Not-for-Profit Organizations

An IRS examination of a[not-for-profit’s](https://www.cbiz.com/industries/not-for-profit-education) exempt status, or the risk of revocation, can feel high stakes and disruptive. While the process is serious, the most effective response is not reactive. A calm, organized, and disciplined approach can help protect your mission and position your organization for a more favorable outcome. The practical steps below reflect what often proves effective when the IRS scrutinizes exempt status.


## Respond Quickly and Get Organized


Treat IRS correspondence as time sensitive from the moment it arrives. Open the letter immediately, calendar all deadlines, and assign clear ownership. Missing a response date can limit your options and escalate issues unnecessarily.


Designate a single point of contact to manage communications with the IRS. Acknowledge receipt of the notice promptly, and if additional time is needed, request a reasonable extension in writing. Maintain a detailed log of all correspondence and documents shared throughout the examination.


Before engaging with the IRS, prepare deliberately. Review the standard audit questionnaire with your CPA and/or attorney before any conversations take place. Gather and reconcile key materials, including governing documents,[Forms 990](https://www.cbiz.com/insights/article/form-990-five-common-mistakes-and-how-to-avoid-them) , financial statements, board minutes, policies, program descriptions, and fundraising records. Your goal is consistency: ensuring your documentation and explanations clearly align with your original exempt purpose and demonstrate how current activities further that purpose.


## Prepare for IRS Meetings and Interviews


Use early interactions with the IRS to listen carefully and clarify scope. Initial meetings often provide insight into the agent’s focus areas, perceived risks, and framing of the examination. Pay close attention to the questions asked and comments made because they can signal where the IRS is concentrating its review.


Take contemporaneous notes during all meetings. Ask the agent to explain what information they need and why, and confirm your understanding of issues, expectations, and next steps in writing after each interaction.


During interviews, maintain strict factual discipline. A few ground rules can help keep responses accurate and controlled:


- Answer only the question asked; avoid volunteering additional information.
- Stick to verifiable facts. Do not speculate or offer opinions.
- If you do not know an answer, say so and offer to follow up.
- Assume every response becomes part of the IRS administrative record and may be reviewed by managers, Appeals, or Chief Counsel.


## Handle Information Document Requests Strategically


Information Document Requests (IDRs) deserve careful attention. How, and how quickly, you respond can influence the pace, clarity, and direction of the examination.


Consider preparing a concise written narrative that outlines your organization’s structure, mission, programs, funding sources, and the facts supporting your exempt status. This context can help the IRS better understand the documents you provide and reduce misinterpretation.


**When responding to IDRs:**


- Never ignore or leave a request incomplete.
- If the IRS focuses on a known risk area, assess the facts early, remediate where possible, and be prepared to explain the issue clearly.
- Produce only what is responsive to the request.
- Keep copies of everything you submit and consider indexing materials to improve clarity and organization.


## Work With Experienced Not-for-Profit Tax Advisors


Do not navigate an exempt status examination alone. Engage a CPA and/or attorney with experience in not-for-profit tax controversy. The right advisors can help frame issues effectively, preserve privilege where applicable, coordinate responses, and guide you through Appeals if necessary.


Handled thoughtfully, an IRS examination does not have to derail your operations or distract from your mission. Process discipline, factual clarity, and experienced guidance remain your strongest tools for managing and resolving an exempt status examination.

#### Frequently Asked Questions


An IRS exempt status examination is a review of whether a not-for-profit organization continues to meet the requirements for tax-exempt status under the Internal Revenue Code. The IRS evaluates governing documents, Forms 990, financial records, programs, and operations to determine whether activities align with the organization’s stated exempt purpose. These examinations may arise from routine compliance reviews, targeted enforcement initiatives, or identified risk indicators.


A not-for-profit should respond to an IRS audit or examination notice promptly and in an organized manner. Best practices include calendaring deadlines, designating a single point of contact, and consulting with experienced not-for-profit tax advisors before engaging with the IRS. Responses should be factual, complete, and limited to what is requested. Thoughtful handling of Information Document Requests (IDRs) and careful preparation for interviews can help manage risk and keep the examination focused.


IRS exempt organization examinations involve technical rules and potentially significant consequences, including penalties or revocation of tax-exempt status. CPAs and attorneys with not-for-profit tax controversy experience can help frame issues appropriately, coordinate responses, preserve privilege where applicable, and guide organizations through the Appeals process if needed. Experienced advisors can help protect compliance while allowing leadership to remain focused on the organization’s mission.
