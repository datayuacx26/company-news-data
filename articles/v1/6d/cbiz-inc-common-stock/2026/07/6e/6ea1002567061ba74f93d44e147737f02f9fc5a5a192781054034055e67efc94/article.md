# 1031 Exchange Deadlines: 45-Day and 180-Day Rules Explained

A successful 1031 exchange requires more than selecting the right replacement property. Missing an IRS deadline may result in losing your tax deferral. Understanding 1031 exchange deadlines helps preserve capital, maintain your transaction timeline, and avoid costly mistakes before closing.


## 1031 Exchange Deadlines: 45-Day and 180-Day Rules


Two deadlines define every exchange:


- Identifying the replacement property within 45 days.
- Completing the acquisition within 180 days.


Both periods run simultaneously. Investors have 45 days to identify replacement property and must complete the exchange within 180 calendar days of the transfer or closing date of the relinquished property, or by the tax return due date, including extensions, if earlier.


The 45-day window is often the most challenging, as it starts immediately after the transfer or closing date of the relinquished property. Market competition, financing delays, due diligence findings, and negotiations can limit options, making early planning critical.


Weekends and holidays count toward both deadlines, so investors should avoid waiting until the last days to identify or close on replacement property. These deadlines generally can’t be extended, except in limited circumstances such as IRS relief for federally declared disasters. A compressed timeline can hinder evaluation of long-term investment suitability.


## How Identification Works Under 1031 Exchange Deadlines


Investors must submit a written, signed notice within 45 days that clearly describes each replacement property by address, legal description, or another specific identifier.


Verbal discussions or vague descriptions are insufficient. Written identification should follow the qualified intermediary’s required process, clearly describe the property, and be properly documented and delivered before the deadline.


You can identify properties using:


- **Three-property rule** : Identify up to three potential replacement properties, regardless of value.
- **200% rule** : Identify more than three properties if their combined fair market value doesn’t exceed 200% of the relinquished property’s fair market value.
- **95% rule** : Identify any number of properties if you ultimately acquire identified replacement property with a fair market value of at least 95% of the aggregate fair market value of all identified replacement properties.


Identifying backup properties helps reduce deal risk, especially when a preferred replacement property faces competitive bidding, financing issues, or due diligence concerns.


## How the 180-Day Rule Affects Closing Strategy


Investors must complete the replacement property acquisition within 180 days of transferring the relinquished property, or by the taxpayer’s return due date, including extensions, if earlier. Because this period includes the 45-day identification window, they have limited time to finalize financing, complete due diligence, negotiate terms, and close.


Closing delays pose significant risks. Title issues, lender requirements, environmental reviews, appraisals, or negotiations can push the transaction close to the deadline. If the acquisition is not completed on time, the exchange may fail even if the property was properly identified.


Investors should consider how the 180-day period aligns with tax filing deadlines. Depending on the sales date, a tax return extension may be necessary to preserve the full exchange period. Early involvement of tax advisors is recommended.


## Why the Qualified Intermediary Matters


Successful exchanges depend on an independent party that holds sale proceeds, provides required documentation, and helps ensure funds aren’t treated as received by the investor. Specific criteria apply when selecting a qualified intermediary. Investors should discuss these requirements with a trusted advisor to understand how they may affect the exchange structure.


If an investor receives or has actual or constructive control of sale proceeds at any time, the transaction may become taxable. Engaging a qualified intermediary before closing is critical, as the exchange structure must be established before funds are disbursed.

## Common Deadline Mistakes to Avoid


Execution problems often arise before investors recognize a risk. Common mistakes include waiting until after closing to search for replacement property, assuming the identification period can be extended, or relying solely on a preferred property without backup.


Other risks include incomplete property descriptions, overlooked financing constraints, lack of coordination with the qualified intermediary, or underestimating the time needed for due diligence and closing.


These issues are difficult to correct once deadlines begin. A strong strategy should start before the relinquished property closes, with clear plans for property identification, financing and advisor coordination.


## What Clients Should Consider


Meeting 1031 exchange deadlines requires coordination across several moving parts:


- Financing terms and lender timing
- Property sourcing and backup options
- Due diligence, inspections and title review
- Exchange documentation and intermediary coordination


You should also evaluate:


- Gain exposure
- Cash needs
- Replacement property availability
- Entity structure


- Tax return filing deadlines and extension needs
- Whether identified properties align with the investor’s long-term strategy


A practical approach includes engaging a qualified intermediary before closing, identifying replacement properties early, confirming financing, preparing backup options, and coordinating tax, legal, and real estate advisors before the 45-day window begins.
