# Why Your Best Applicants Never Finish Applying

The teacher shortage is well documented. According to the National Center for Education Statistics,[74% of public schools](https://nces.ed.gov/whatsnew/press_releases/10_17_2024.asp) reported difficulty filling vacant teaching positions before the start of the 2024–25 school year — with "too few qualified candidates applying" cited as the top challenge nationwide.


But there's a parallel problem that gets far less attention: the candidates who found your opening, clicked Apply, and quietly walked away before they ever finished.


It's not visible in your data, and there's no way to know it happened. Those candidates simply move on — often to a district whose process got out of their way.


## **Applications Lose Candidates at a Staggering Rate**


Across industries, application abandonment is extraordinarily high.[According to Appcast](https://www.shrm.org/topics-tools/news/talent-acquisition/people-92-never-finish-online-job-applications) , one of the most widely cited sources in recruitment research, 92% of candidates who click "Apply" never complete the application.


The biggest culprits are length and friction. Appcast's[Recruitment Marketing Benchmark Report](https://hirevire.com/articles/mobile-recruitment-statistics) found that applications taking under five minutes to complete convert at 12.47%. Applications taking over 15 minutes? Just 3.61%. That gap means roughly 2 in 3 candidates who started a long application would have applied if the application was easier to complete.


For K-12 districts, this dynamic has added stakes. You're competing for a smaller pool of credentialed candidates, many of whom have multiple options. Every unnecessary step in your application process is a missed connection — a candidate who was ready to say yes, lost to friction before they ever finished.


Nimble has spent years studying how teachers experience the K-12 hiring process, interviewing and surveying candidates across the country. The frustration around applications comes up constantly:


> "There are so many applications I started but didn't go back to finish because they were taking too long."


> "Filling out the same paperwork over and over is tedious after a bit — are they really reading through all this?"


These weren't disinterested candidates. They were interested enough to start. The process is what lost them.


## **Mobile Is No Longer a Nice-to-Have**


How candidates apply has also fundamentally shifted. Mobile applications now account for[67% of all job applications](https://hirevire.com/articles/mobile-recruitment-statistics) — a number that continues to climb each year. If your application wasn't designed for a phone — or if it requires downloading, filling out, and emailing back a PDF — you're not just creating friction. You're functionally invisible to a substantial portion of your candidate pool before they ever get started.


Mobile-optimized application flows show significantly higher completion rates than desktop-focused ones. This isn't an emerging trend. It's the current reality of where candidates are.


## **What "Streamlined" Actually Looks Like**


A better candidate experience isn't just about shortening a form. It's about removing every step that doesn't need to be there — and making the steps that remain as easy as possible.


In practice, that means:


- **Mobile-first design** — not a desktop form that technically loads on a phone, but an experience built for one
- **No redundant data entry** — candidates shouldn't be retyping information they've already submitted elsewhere
- **Clear, informative job postings** — teachers consistently cite lack of school and role information as a reason they don't apply in the first place
- **Visibility on the channels where teachers are searching** — not just your district website


Each of these is a lever. Districts that get them right don't just have better candidate experiences, they have more applicants. And in a market where qualified candidates are in short supply, volume matters.
