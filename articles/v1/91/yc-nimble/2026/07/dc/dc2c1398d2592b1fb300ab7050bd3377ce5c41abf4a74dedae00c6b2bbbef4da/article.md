# Stand Out with a Stand-Up Portfolio

‍


Some states require teaching portfolios as part of the certification process. Some principals ask to see them when interviewing candidates for a teaching role. Some schools review them when considering teachers for position changes or promotions. No matter how it's used, a teaching portfolio is an excellent way to document your efforts as a teacher and their impact on your students.


As you create a teaching portfolio, keep a few things in mind:


**Are there specific requirements?** This is crucial. If your portfolio is used for certification, evaluation, or renewal, ensure you understand the requirements. While creativity is valuable, your portfolio must align with prescribed expectations.


**What story are you telling?** Think of your teaching portfolio like a piece of creative writing—it has a setting, a theme, and a story arc.


**Setting:** Your scope could be limited to a single cohort, school year, or subject area, or it could be broader, reflecting your growth over time.


**Theme:** Highlight what makes you unique as a teacher. Your focus might be student-centered (e.g., supporting neurodivergent learners, promoting student voices, or fostering a specific classroom culture) or instructional (e.g., project-based learning, arts integration, multiculturalism, STEM).


**Arc:** Structure your portfolio with a clear progression. For example:


**Beginning:** Lesson plans, student diagnostics, brainstorming sessions.


**Middle:** Student growth through drafts, interim assessments, and research.


**End:** Culminating projects, presentations, or video clips showcasing student achievements.


For each entry, include a concise explanation of the intention and outcome.


**What value do you bring to teaching?** Your portfolio is your professional showcase. In addition to student work, include key career-related artifacts like your resume and teaching certification(s). If you have honors, awards, or recognitions, include those also. And a statement reflecting your teaching philosophy helps introduce you by conveying your values and goals.


**Authenticity Matters** Your portfolio should be accurate and reflective of real classroom experiences. It shouldn't just feature polished successes—it should illustrate the learning process, including challenges and growth. At the same time, ensure it is well-organized and visually appealing.


**Presenting Your Portfolio** For an easy-to-read format, consider using online platforms like Colibri or Crevado, which help create digital teaching portfolios.


A well-crafted teaching portfolio is more than just a collection of documents—it’s a powerful tool that tells your story as an educator. A strong portfolio highlights your unique strengths and impact. By thoughtfully curating your materials, structuring your narrative, and presenting your work in an accessible format, you create a resource that not only showcases your expertise but also reinforces your commitment to effective teaching and student success.
