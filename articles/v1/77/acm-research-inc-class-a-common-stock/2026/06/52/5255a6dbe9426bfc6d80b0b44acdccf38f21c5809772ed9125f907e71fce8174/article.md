# SPM Wafer Cleaning for Advanced Semiconductor Manufacturing

Wafer cleaning is one of the most critical steps in the semiconductor process flow, especially as dimensions continue to shrink to below the 1-nanometer (nm) node. Photoresist residue and particulates can reduce yields in large-die AI chips, impacting the performance of the final product or causing the chips to be scrapped.


The sulfuric acid hydrogen peroxide step, or sulfuric peroxide mixture (SPM), is essential for removing organic contaminants from post-etch substrates and ion-implanted films, as well as for platinum silicide residue removal. **** SPM processes traditionally have been performed in tanks or baths, which allows sulfuric acid to be reused multiple times by refreshing the chemistry with hydrogen peroxide.


However, in a bath or tank, undissolved photoresist can leave behind particulates, leading to defects in the process. Single-wafer systems can reduce particulates, but splatter and sulfur particles reforming from the mist in the chamber can leave chemical residue on the wafer, which can also impact yields for large-die chips. In addition, single-chamber systems can have lower process throughput due to the preventive maintenance required every two to three days to remove particles from the chamber.

## Single-Wafer SPM Cleaning FAQs


What is SPM cleaning and how does it work in a single-wafer system?


SPM (sulfuric peroxide mixture) cleaning uses a combination of sulfuric acid and hydrogen peroxide to remove organic contaminants, such as photoresist residues, from wafer surfaces. In a single-wafer SPM system, the chemistry is precisely delivered and controlled on a wafer-by-wafer basis within a process chamber, with the ability to tightly control SPM temperature depending on process requirements. This helps reduce defect risk by minimizing undissolved residues and particle carryover common in bath-based processes.


Why is high-temperature SPM wafer cleaning important for advanced nodes?


As device dimensions shrink and defect budgets tighten, the ability to remove organic residues effectively becomes increasingly critical for yield. High-temperature SPM is commonly used for demanding cleaning and stripping steps (including high-temperature sulfuric acid photoresist stripping), helping to support stringent cleanliness requirements in advanced semiconductor manufacturing.


What advantages do single-wafer SPM tools offer compared with traditional batch/tank SPM?


Single-wafer SPM tools provide better particle control, improved process consistency and reduced defect risk compared with batch systems. By limiting contamination inside the chamber, they can also extend maintenance cycles and increase system uptime.


How does ACM’s nozzle design help reduce sulfur deposits, particles and maintenance downtime?


ACM’s single-wafer SPM tool incorporates a patented nozzle cover designed to prevent acid mist splatter and reduce acid gas emissions by up to ~90%. The nozzle cover both delivers SPM and exhausts vapors above the wafer, and the nozzle assembly/nozzle cover is rinsed between wafers to help limit residue buildup. This approach supports stable particle performance over an extended operating period and enables longer preventive maintenance cycles.
