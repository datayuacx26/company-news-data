# Selecting the Best Equipment and Process for Your Wet Cleaning Application

As the feature sizes and critical dimensions of logic, memory and analog semiconductor devices shrink with advanced technology nodes, the sensitivity to submicron contaminants has increased significantly. Consequently, the use of wet cleaning technology to remove particulates and contaminants is critical to the success of semiconductor manufacturers.


Single-wafer and batch cleaning technologies are both used extensively across the manufacturing spectrum. Each offers different benefits from a cost and particulate-removal perspective, depending on the technology node and specified process sequence. In this blog, we explain the differences between batch and single-wafer cleaning technology and what customers should consider when choosing between the two for their cleaning requirements.


## Market Context and Technology Convergence: Memory Devices and Advanced Packaging


The shrinking of technology nodes and the introduction of new 3D transistors and high-aspect-ratio features are creating significant challenges for semiconductor manufacturers. New transistor technologies, such as gate-all-around (GAA) and vertically stacked complementary field effect transistors (CFET), require advanced cleaning technologies to penetrate spaces that are mere angstroms wide.


At the same time, 3D-NAND is rapidly adding layers and is expected to have 1,000 layers by 2027, with aspect ratios approaching 100 to 1. Soon, DRAM aspect ratios will also approach 100:1. These features require the removal of not only particulate contamination but also hidden contaminants, such as residual metals and chemicals that can impact the performance of the semiconductor chip.


The rapid growth of 3D packaging also adds further challenges for wet cleaning. These chip dimensions require sophisticated cleaning techniques to remove residues and create clean surfaces at the bottom of these deep structures. Scarce water sources and a focus on sustainability require creative solutions to reduce the use of clean water in the rinsing process.

Batch processing is the optimal choice for bulk strip and cleaning processes, such as photoresist removal or a wet etch, where all the material is removed. Batch processing is also considered the preferred technology for mature process nodes, where a lower cost of ownership is most important. Batch systems typically process 50 to 100 wafers at a time, making them more efficient for legacy devices.


The advantages of batch processing include high throughput, lower cost and reduced chemical consumption per wafer. However, some of the drawbacks include the risk of cross-contamination from backside particulates, reduced wafer-to-wafer control, and the potential for the entire batch of wafers to be scrapped if damage occurs during the process.


Single-wafer tools are ideal for advanced technology nodes where higher process control and precision are required (<45nm). Fragile or expensive substrates, such as silicon carbide (SiC), can benefit from single-wafer handling techniques as well.


Single-wafer tools provide superior process control, higher yield due to reduced defect density, less cross-contamination risk and faster processing. As a result, these tools also typically cost more than a batch system due to the added complexity and higher chemical usage. However, the process performance at advanced nodes typically outweighs these concerns.


## Key Considerations


When choosing between a batch tool and a single-wafer cleaning system, there are several areas to consider.


One key factor is the uniformity of the clean or material strip as well as that of the contamination removal. On a wafer with flat or large geometries, a batch system may be sufficient. However, if there are small features that require optimized contamination removal, a single-wafer tool may be the better choice.


At advanced technology nodes, a single-wafer system gives you the ability to perform a backside clean and bevel removal needed to ensure optimal process performance for the ensuing steps.


Most importantly, the overall cost of ownership needs to be considered. It is important to take into account both the economics of chemical usage in a batch or single-wafer system and the wafer throughput of the overall process.

## Batch and Single-Wafer Wet Cleaning FAQs


What are the main differences between batch and single-wafer wet cleaning?


Batch wet cleaning processes multiple wafers at once, offering high throughput and lower cost per wafer, while single-wafer cleaning processes wafers individually for greater process control, improved uniformity and reduced contamination risk. The right choice depends on device complexity, technology, process requirements and manufacturing economics.


When should semiconductor manufacturers switch from batch to single-wafer wet cleaning?


Semiconductor manufacturers typically transition from[batch to single-wafer wet cleaning](https://www.acmr.com/tools-and-processes/wet-processing/) as device geometries shrink, process complexity increases and contamination tolerances become tighter. Advanced technology nodes below 45nm, high-aspect-ratio structures and advanced packaging applications often require the higher precision, uniformity and contamination control that single-wafer systems provide.


What applications are best suited for batch wet cleaning systems?


Batch wet cleaning systems are commonly used for mature semiconductor nodes, bulk strip processes such as removal of silicon nitride with hot phosphoric acid, photoresist removal and wet etch applications where high throughput and lower cost of ownership are priorities. They are ideal for processes where complete material removal is required across multiple wafers simultaneously.


What applications benefit most from single-wafer wet cleaning?


Single-wafer wet cleaning is best suited for advanced logic, advanced DRAM, 3D-NAND,[wafer-level packaging (WLP)](https://www.acmr.com/industries/wafer-level-packaging/?utm_source=website&utm_medium=webpage&utm_campaign=wetprocessingpage) ,[panel-level packaging (PLP)](https://www.acmr.com/industries/panel-level-packaging/) and other applications requiring precise contamination removal, improved defect control and optimized cleaning uniformity. Fragile substrates such as silicon carbide (SiC) also benefit from single-wafer handling techniques.


What factors should be considered when choosing between batch and single-wafer cleaning?


Key considerations include feature size, contamination sensitivity, wafer geometry, throughput requirements, chemical consumption, process uniformity and total cost of ownership. Advanced semiconductor nodes and complex device architectures generally require the tighter process control available with single-wafer systems.
