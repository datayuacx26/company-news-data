# Why KYC is required to use global accounts (and how to do it easily)

Global accounts simplify cross-border payments by allowing users to easily access foreign bank accounts. Unlike traditional banks, Know Your Customer (KYC) verification acts like a network of vigilant security cameras and checkpoints, ensuring that only genuine users gain access to the payment platform. Without these checks, criminals could infiltrate the system, using it to launder money, fund illicit activities, or commit fraud, thereby endangering the entire financial system. KYC prevents such financial crimes by verifying the identity of every user, keeping global accounts safe and compliant.


Whether you’re a freelancer receiving international payments, an entrepreneur expanding into new markets, or an individual managing multi-currency finances, KYC is the gatekeeper that protects the system.


## **What is KYC?**


[Know Your Customer (KYC)](https://www.investopedia.com/terms/k/knowyourclient.asp) is a mandatory process used by financial institutions, fintech platforms, and other regulated entities to verify the identity of their customers. The process typically involves collecting and validating personal information, such as government-issued identification, proof of address, and, in some cases, additional documents like bank statements or tax identification numbers.


KYC is an essential part of global financial regulations, designed to ensure transparency, security, and compliance with[anti-money laundering (AML)](https://www.investopedia.com/terms/a/aml.asp) and[counter-terrorism financing (CTF)](https://www.occ.treas.gov/topics/supervision-and-examination/bsa/counter-terrorist-financing/index-counter-terrorist-financing.html) laws. For global accounts, KYC is particularly critical because they facilitate cross-border transactions.

## **Why KYC is required for global accounts**


KYC validation helps fintechs maintain a secure, transparent, and compliant financial system. Below are the key reasons why KYC is relevant:


### **1. Prevents financial crime**


Global accounts facilitate transactions across countries, which can attract bad guys trying to exploit the system for illicit activities like money laundering, fraud, or terrorism financing. KYC ensures that financial institutions and fintech platforms know exactly who is using their services, reducing the risk of accounts being used for illegal purposes. By verifying identities, institutions can track money flow and flag suspicious activities, aligning with global AML and CTF regulations.


### **2. Regulatory compliance**


Financial institutions and fintech platforms operating globally must adhere to strict regulations set by bodies such as the Financial Action Task Force (FATF), the European Union’s Anti-Money Laundering Directives, or country-specific regulators like the Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) in the UK. These regulations mandate KYC to ensure that businesses operate within legal frameworks, avoid penalties, and maintain their licences to operate.


### **3. Builds trust and security**


KYC fosters trust between users and service providers. When customers know that a platform verifies the identities of all its users, they feel more confident that their funds are secure (especially if their hands are clean). It also protects users from identity theft and fraud by ensuring that only authorised individuals can access and operate accounts.


### **4. Enables cross-border transactions**


Global accounts often involve multiple jurisdictions, each with its own regulatory requirements. KYC ensures that users meet the compliance standards of all relevant countries, enabling seamless cross-border transactions. Without KYC, platforms would struggle to meet the varying legal requirements, potentially limiting their ability to offer services in certain regions.


### **5. Protects the financial ecosystem**


Unverified accounts pose a risk not only to the platform but also to the broader financial ecosystem. By implementing KYC, global account providers contribute to a safer financial environment, reducing the likelihood of systemic risks caused by fraud or illicit activities. This protection benefits all stakeholders, from individual users to banks and governments.


## **The KYC process: What to expect**


While KYC requirements vary slightly depending on the platform and jurisdiction, the process generally involves the following steps:


1. **Personal information submission** : Users provide basic details, including their full name, date of birth, nationality, and contact information.
2. **Identity verification** : This typically requires uploading a government-issued ID, such as a passport, driver’s licence, or national ID card.
3. **Proof of address** : Users may need to submit a utility bill, bank statement, or other official document that confirms their residential address.
4. **Additional checks** : Depending on the platform, users may need to provide further details, such as their source of funds, tax identification number, or a selfie holding their ID for facial recognition.
5. **Verification review** : The platform reviews the submitted documents, often using automated systems or manual checks, to confirm the user’s identity.
6. **Approval or follow-up** : Once verified, the user gains full access to the account. If there are discrepancies, the platform may request additional documents or clarification.


The process can take anywhere from a few minutes to a few days, depending on the platform’s efficiency and the complexity of the user’s case.


## **Challenges of KYC for global account users**


While KYC is essential, it can present challenges for users, particularly those new to global accounts or operating in regions with limited access to formal documentation. Common hurdles include:


- **Document availability** : In some countries, obtaining government-issued IDs or proof of address can be difficult, especially for individuals in rural areas or those without fixed addresses.
- **Language barriers** : Since global accounts are used by global users, it is common to encounter language struggles when uploading supporting documents, as they may not be in the platform's primary language(s).
- **Verification delays:** Users may experience verification delays when using certain financial services, which can be frustrating. Many platforms with high volumes of applications or manual review processes can experience verification delays, frustrating users who need immediate access to their global accounts for time-sensitive transactions.
- **Privacy concerns** : Some users hesitate to share personal information due to fears of data breaches or misuse, particularly in regions with a history of data security issues.

## Tips to speed up KYC approval


Here are some things to note to avoid unnecessary delays.


- Use the same name format as it appears on your ID.
- Avoid using nicknames or abbreviations.
- Provide a well-lit, uncluttered background for selfies.
- Submit documents in the recommended format (e.g., JPEG or PDF).
- Check your email regularly in case the provider requests additional information.
- Ensure all uploads are clear, no glares or blurs.
