# Germany digital nomad visa: Requirements and how to apply

Germany remains one of Europe’s strongest economies with one of the best standards of living globally. It then makes sense that the country is a favourite destination among digital nomads and remote workers outside the EU.


The challenge is that Germany does not have a visa specifically labelled "digital nomad visa." However, remote workers can legally live and work there on a Freiberufler (freelancer) visa or a Selbständiger (self-employment) visa. Both allow non-EU citizens to reside in Germany while earning income from clients outside the country. Requirements include proof of income, a registered address, and health insurance.


These pathways existed long before digital nomad visas became mainstream. And so, Germany became one of the earliest options in Europe for remote workers who want to live and work abroad. This article explains the two main visa options available for digital nomads and remote workers, what each requires, how to apply step by step, and what to expect during the process.

## Does Germany have a digital nomad visa?


Germany doesn't currently offer an officially labelled digital nomad visa, but there are two main pathways for freelancers and self-employed professionals from outside the EU.


### The Freiberufler Visa (Freelance Visa)


The Freiberufler Visa enables non-EU nationals, including digital nomads, to live and work independently in Germany as freelancers. So if you are a “liberal professional”, whether you are an artist, writer, IT professional, engineer, or consultant, you can opt for this visa. It offers the flexibility to work with multiple clients and is a suitable alternative, as Germany has no dedicated digital nomad visa yet.


[Section 21 of the German Residence Act (Aufenthaltsgesetz)](https://migrando.de/en/blog/residence-permit/paragraph-21-aufenthg-all-about-your-residence-permit/) makes provision for the Freiberufler visa. The difference between a Freiberufler and a Selbständiger (which we will discuss soon) matters significantly for tax purposes in Germany. Freiberufler, or “liberal professionals”, are exempt from trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) and do not need to register a business (Gewerbe). Selbständige, or entrepreneurs running a business, pay trade tax and must register with the local trade office. The Freiberufler category is the more advantageous classification for most digital professionals.


### The Selbständiger Visa (Self-Employment Visa)


The Selbständiger visa is better suited to entrepreneurs who run a business than to those who practise a liberal profession. If you are launching a tech startup in Germany, operating an e-commerce business serving German customers, or running a consultancy that goes beyond the definition of a liberal profession, the Selbständiger is the appropriate visa to process. It requires a more detailed business plan and is subject to additional scrutiny, including evaluation by the Chamber of Commerce and Industry (IHK) in some cases.


For most digital nomads working as developers, designers, writers, consultants, or other knowledge workers, the Freiberufler visa is more applicable. So, let’s just focus on that.

## Who qualifies for a German remote work visa?


Anyone who meets the eligibility requirements and is either a freelancer or self-employed can apply for a German freelancer visa, provided there is an economic interest or regional need for their professional services. Germany accepts all those in liberal professions.


The liberal professions recognised under German law include:


- Software developers and IT professionals
- Graphic designers and UI/UX designers
- Content writers, copywriters, and journalists
- Translators and interpreters
- Consultants in management, strategy, and professional services
- Engineers and architects
- Healthcare professionals, including doctors, dentists, and therapists
- Tax advisors and auditors
- Artists, musicians, and performers


The common thread is that these are knowledge-based or creative professions requiring specialised expertise. They are distinct from trade or commercial activities.


Eligibility criteria include


- Being a non-EU/EEA national
- Working in a recognised “liberal profession” under German tax law
- Proving demand for your freelance services through client letters or contracts
- Financial stability
- A valid German health insurance
- Evidence of professional qualifications, if required.


## What are the requirements for a German digital nomad visa?


### Proof of freelance work and client demand


This is the most important part of the application. German authorities want to see that you have real clients paying for your services throughout your stay. You can use client contracts or letters of intent that should show a decent salary for your role. Check the standard rates for your services using German salary surveys such as the[Honorartabelle](https://ms-kfzgutachter.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/honorartabelle-2025-2.pdf) .


You are also required to have at least two clients from Germany to sign letters of intent or contracts. While you can work with non-German clients on the Freiberufler visa, having German clients strengthens your application and demonstrates a strong connection to the German economy.


### Income requirements


There is no fixed minimum income requirement, but applicants should demonstrate an annual income of €9,000 to €12,000. This amounts to around €750-€1,000 per month, which is below the cost of living comfortably in German cities but may be enough to support yourself without relying on German social welfare.


While it is a plus that Germany only requires you to earn a modest income, you must have sufficient funds to cover housing and basic needs. Bank statements showing a reasonable balance, tax documents from previous years to demonstrate steady freelance income, and client contracts specifying payment terms all support the income aspect of your application.

### Valid health insurance


German health insurance is mandatory for all residents and is required as part of the visa application. Temporary travel insurance is sufficient when you are submitting your documents. But you will need a valid comprehensive German insurance subsequently. Once you arrive in Germany and apply for the residence permit, you will need to switch to a recognised German health insurance provider. Public insurance (gesetzliche Krankenversicherung) through providers like Techniker Krankenkasse, AOK, or Barmer is the most common route for freelancers, with premiums based on declared income.


Techniker Krankenkasse (TK) costs[approximately 17.29% per month. This includes the 14.6% that every public insurance user pays and 2.69% that goes to TK](https://www.germany-visa.org/insurances-germany/health-insurance/companies/tk/) . So, if you earn €3,000 monthly, you pay €40.35 monthly on health insurance.


### Proof of accommodation and Anmeldung


[A signed lease and a completed Anmeldung are required](https://service.berlin.de/dienstleistung/324288/standort/121885/en/) , not optional. The Anmeldung must happen within 14 days of moving in, under Germany's Bundesmeldegesetz, with fines of up to 1,000 euros for missing the deadline. It also unlocks your Tax ID, bank account, health insurance, and residence permit application, since the Ausländerbehörde requires your registration certificate before processing the permit. A landlord confirmation (Wohnungsgeberbestätigung) is needed too; a lease alone does not satisfy registration.

### Professional qualifications


For regulated professions such as medicine, law, engineering, and financial advisory, which have specific licensing requirements, you need to provide proof of professional qualifications. This is not required for most digital roles, such as software development, design, and writing. Your client contracts and portfolio are good enough to demonstrate competence.


### Passport validity


A valid passport with at least one year of validity beyond the intended period of stay. The passport must have sufficient blank pages for entry and residence permit stamps.


### Required documents checklist


The specific documents required vary depending on the German consulate handling your application and your individual circumstances. It is better to contact the consulate in your jurisdiction for the requirements that apply to you. The standard document set includes:


- Valid passport with at least one year of validity
- Completed visa application form (Visum-Antrag), available from the[German consulate website](https://digital.diplo.de/)
- Two recent biometric passport photographs
- Signed rental agreement or confirmed accommodation in Germany
- Proof of health insurance valid in Germany
- Client contracts, letters of intent, or signed agreements from clients (ideally including German clients)
- Business plan or description of planned freelance activity
- Evidence of income: tax returns, bank statements, or contracts specifying payment
- Professional qualifications or portfolio, where applicable
- Curriculum vitae in German or English
- Visa fee payment (see fees section below)


For the Selbständiger pathway, a detailed business plan and potentially an assessment from the local Chamber of Commerce or a relevant authority may be required in addition to the above.


### Visa fees


[The consular fee varies across jurisdictions, but it is around €75 for most applicants.](https://www.auswaertiges-amt.de/en/visa-service/215870-215870) An additional fee of €23.50 to €47 might apply for visa application centre services, including biometrics processing. Self-employed applicants submitting a business plan may need to pay €100 to have the document examined by the Chamber of Commerce and Industry.


Visa fees are non-refundable regardless of the outcome of the application.


## How to apply: step-by-step


**Step 1: Determine your visa pathway:** Confirm whether your work qualifies as a liberal profession (Freiberufler) or a commercial enterprise (Selbständiger). If you are unsure, consulting an immigration lawyer familiar with German law before applying is worth the investment. Applying under the wrong category causes delays and potential rejection.


**Step 2: Gather your documents:** Compile the full document set listed above. Every document should be accurate, complete, and consistent with the other documents. Inconsistencies between your business plan, client contracts, and income evidence create questions that delay processing.


**Step 3: Schedule an appointment at your local German consulate or embassy:** You will need to attend an in-person interview at the German embassy in your home country. Wait times for appointments can be significant, particularly at busier consulates in major cities. Book as early as possible once your documents are ready.


**Step 4: Attend the appointment:** Bring all original documents plus copies. The visa officer will review your application and may ask questions about your freelance work, client relationships, and plans in Germany. Answer clearly and consistently with what your documents show.


**Step 5: Wait for processing.** Visa applications typically take 4 to 12 weeks to be processed. The processing time for an application does not exceed 45 working days. Applications requiring prior agreement from German authorities take longer. Do not book non-refundable travel until the visa is confirmed.


**Step 6: Enter Germany on the entry visa:** Those who arrive with an entry visa will need to inform immigration authorities that they are entering the country to work as freelancers and intend to apply for the Freiberufler (freelance residence permit) to continue living and working. Register your address at the local Bürgeramt (citizens' registration office) within 14 days of moving in, as Anmeldung (registration) is legally required for all residents.


**Step 7: Apply for the residence permit:** Once in Germany, apply for the freelance residence permit (Aufenthaltserlaubnis zur freiberuflichen Tätigkeit) at the local immigration authority (Ausländerbehörde). This is the multi-year permit that allows continued residence. High-earning digital nomads can receive a freelancer residence permit for a period of three months with the possibility of extension up to three years.

## Costs, duration and renewal of the German freelancer (Freiberufler) visa


**Visa application fee**


The German freelancer visa (Freiberufler visa) generally costs €75 when applying for a national (D) visa at a German embassy or consulate abroad. Once in Germany, the residence permit for freelance work typically costs around €100, though fees may vary slightly by city and immigration office.


**Processing time and validity duration**


Processing times vary depending on where you apply and the workload of the German authorities. Most applicants can expect a decision within 4 to 12 weeks, although some cases take longer if additional documents are required or if local authorities need to review the application.


A German freelancer's residence permit is usually granted for up to three years initially. The exact duration depends on factors such as your profession, financial situation, client contracts, and the strength of your business plan.


### **German freelancer visa renewal process**


Before your permit expires, you can apply for an extension through your local immigration office ( *Ausländerbehörde* ). To renew, you will generally need to show that:


- Your freelance activity is still active and viable.
- You have generated sufficient income to support yourself.
- You maintain valid health insurance.
- You have complied with German tax and registration requirements.


The renewal application should ideally be submitted several months before the permit expires, as appointment wait times can be lengthy in some cities.


### Path to long-term residency


One of the main advantages of the German freelancer visa is that it can lead to long-term residency. If your freelance business is successful and you can support yourself financially, you may become eligible for a settlement permit (permanent residence) after some years of legal residence in Germany.

### Frequently asked questions


**Does Germany have an official digital nomad visa?**


Germany has recently recognised the growing trend of digital nomadism and has introduced a freelancer visa to accommodate this new breed of professionals. The visa allows you to legally reside and work in Germany while contributing to the country's diverse and innovative landscape. The visa is technically the Freiberufler (freelance) residence permit rather than a specifically branded digital nomad visa, but it serves the same function for remote knowledge workers.


**How long does the German freelance visa application take to process?**


Processing typically takes four to twelve weeks from the date of the application appointment. Applications requiring prior agreement from German immigration authorities can take longer. Peak periods at busy consulates further extend timelines. Applying four to six months before your intended move date provides sufficient margin.


**Do I need German clients to qualify for the Freiberufler Visa?**


Having German clients significantly strengthens an application. A foreigner with freelancer status must have at least two clients from Germany. Having only non-German clients does not automatically disqualify an application, but the case for economic benefit to Germany is weaker without German clients. Letters of intent from German businesses, even before work has formally begun, can serve as the client evidence required.


**Do I need a German bank account to apply?**


You do not need a German bank account in order to get your freelance entry visa approved. Bank statements from your home country account demonstrating sufficient income and savings are acceptable for the initial application. Opening a German bank account is advisable once you are in Germany, as it is required for many practical aspects of life, including rental agreements and some client payment arrangements.


**Can my family join me in Germany on the freelance visa?**


Spouses and dependent children of Freiberufler visa holders can apply for family reunification residence permits to join the primary applicant in Germany. The family members' permits are tied to the primary applicant's status. German language requirements and proof of adequate housing and income to support the whole family apply to the family reunification application.
