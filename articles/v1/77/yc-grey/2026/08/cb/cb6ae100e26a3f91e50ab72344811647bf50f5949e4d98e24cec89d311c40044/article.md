# What is the currency of Switzerland? Franc symbol and code

If you have always thought the currency used in Switzerland is the euro, it is unlikely you are the only wrong person in the room. Switzerland's currency is the Swiss franc (currency code CHF), issued by the Swiss National Bank and divided into 100 centimes. Switzerland and Liechtenstein both use it. Despite being surrounded by the eurozone, Switzerland has kept the franc, which is widely seen as a stable, safe-haven currency.


So, if you are planning a trip or business dealings involving the country, it is good that you didn't assume Switzerland uses the euro because of its central European location. This guide explains what the Swiss franc actually is, why Switzerland has kept its own currency, and what to know before spending or sending money there.


## What is the currency of Switzerland?


Switzerland's official currency is the Swiss franc, issued and managed by the[Swiss National Bank (SNB)](https://www.snb.ch/) , Switzerland's independent central bank


Switzerland is not a member of the European Union and has deliberately chosen to remain outside the eurozone. The country has maintained its independent currency and monetary policy despite being geographically surrounded by eurozone countries (France, Germany, Italy, and Austria).


This is closely tied to Switzerland’s political and economic independence. It has maintained long-standing neutrality and is not a member of the EU. The people and policymakers made this choice.


## The Swiss franc symbol and code


The ISO 4217 currency code for the Swiss franc is CHF. The code combines “CH”, from the Latin name for Switzerland, *Confoederatio Helvetica* , with “F” for franc. It is Latin for the Swiss Confederation. This is why the code uses "CH" rather than an abbreviation of "Switzerland" in English.


You may also see the franc written as Fr. or SFr. in Switzerland, depending on the region and language. Switzerland has four official languages: German, French, Italian, and Romansh.


- **German regions (Standarddeutsch)** : Use Fr. or CHF. The currency is called Franken, and the smaller unit (cents) is called Rappen (Rp.).
- **French regions (Romandie)** : Use Fr. or CHF. The currency is called franc, and the cents are called centime (ct.).
- **Italian regions (Svizzera italiana)** : Use Fr. or CHF. The currency is called franco, and the cents are called centesimo (ct.).
- **Romansh regions (Rumantschia)** : Use Fr. or CHF. The currency is called franc, and the cents are called rap (rp.).


The Swiss franc is subdivided into 100 centimes (in French-speaking areas) or Rappen (in German-speaking areas), similar to how a dollar is divided into cents.


Depending on the local language, the placement of the code changes on official documents:


- **German and English** : The code or symbol usually goes before the amount (e.g., CHF 100 or Fr. 100).
- **French and Italian** : The code or symbol usually goes after the amount, separated by a space (e.g., 100 CHF or 100 fr.

## Which countries use the Swiss franc?


Switzerland is not the only country using the Swiss franc. Liechtenstein, a small country between Switzerland and Austria, also officially uses the Swiss franc as its currency. These countries have a formal currency union agreement and have close economic and monetary ties.


A smaller and less widely known place where CHF is also used is Campione d'Italia. It is a small Italian municipality near Lugano, entirely surrounded by Swiss territory. Because of its isolation from the rest of Italy, it has been more practical to work with the Swiss economy. As a result, the Swiss franc circulates widely there alongside the euro.


You may also find Swiss francs accepted informally in some border areas near Switzerland, but the euro or local currency is normally used there.

### Franc to US dollar and euro


The Swiss franc is known as a safe-haven currency. This means investors worldwide often buy it during economic or political uncertainty because Switzerland is seen as relatively stable with low inflation rates and a remarkable banking system.


Its value against currencies such as the US dollar and euro changes regularly based on interest rates, economic conditions and investor confidence. ## Getting and spending francs as a traveller


Switzerland is one of the more expensive countries to visit, so it is worth planning your spending before travelling.


You can generally pay with your card across Switzerland, including at restaurants, shops, hotels, and even many smaller establishments. Contactless payment is very common and widely expected.


You can use cash for smaller transactions at local markets and in some rural areas. Although CHF 1,000 exists, it's best to stick to smaller denominations. However, Switzerland remains one of Europe's more card-friendly countries.

## Frequently asked questions about the currency of Switzerland


### **Does Switzerland use the euro?**


No. Switzerland uses the Swiss franc and is not part of the eurozone. Some businesses in tourist areas may accept euros, but paying in Swiss francs is usually more straightforward.


### **What is the symbol for the Swiss franc?**


There is no single symbol used everywhere. You will commonly see CHF, Fr., or SFr. when prices are shown in Swiss francs.


### **Which countries use the franc?**


Switzerland and Liechtenstein officially use the Swiss franc. It is also widely used in Campione d'Italia, an Italian town surrounded by Switzerland.


### **How much is 1 dollar in francs?**


The dollar-to-franc exchange rate changes continuously with market conditions. So, check a live currency converter or financial data source before travelling or transferring money.


### **Is Switzerland expensive?**


Yes, Switzerland is one of the most expensive countries in the world. It is generally more expensive than many other European countries, particularly for accommodation, food and transport. Set a higher daily budget when planning a trip.


### **Can I use my card in Switzerland?**


Yes. Visa and Mastercard are widely accepted, including at most hotels, restaurants and shops. Contactless payments are also common. Keeping some cash is still useful for smaller purchases.
