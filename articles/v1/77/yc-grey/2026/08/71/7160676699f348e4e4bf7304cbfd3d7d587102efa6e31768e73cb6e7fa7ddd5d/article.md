## **How work visa sponsorship works in Spain**


There are two main ways most non-EU professionals move to Spain for work. The first is the standard work permit, where a Spanish employer offers you a job and agrees to sponsor your application. The employer applies for authorisation before you relocate, proving the role cannot easily be filled locally where required. Once approved, you apply for your visa and can travel to Spain to begin work. This route is common across sectors such as hospitality, construction, logistics and many skilled occupations.


The second option is the Highly Qualified Professional (HQP) route, designed for experienced professionals taking specialist or senior roles. It's typically used by multinational companies, technology firms, and engineering businesses that hire highly skilled international talent. In both cases, the employer leads the process by preparing the paperwork, submitting the application to the relevant authorities and ensuring immigration requirements are met, making your job offer the foundation of your work visa application.

## **How to apply for a sponsored role in Spain**


Following the right application process improves your chances of securing sponsorship and moving through Spain's work visa process smoothly.


- **Find recognised opportunities** : Search company career pages, LinkedIn, InfoJobs and international job boards for employers actively hiring foreign professionals or advertising roles that mention visa sponsorship.
- **Tailor your CV** : Adapt it to Spanish expectations by keeping it concise and highlighting measurable achievements, relevant technical skills, and language abilities that match the specific position.
- **Secure a job offer** : Once selected, your employer prepares the employment offer and begins the work permit or Highly Qualified Professional application before you apply for your visa.
- **Prepare your documents** : Have your passport, educational certificates, employment contract, professional licences and any required translations ready to avoid delays during the sponsorship and visa application process.

## **Frequently asked questions**


### **Which companies sponsor work visas in Spain?**


Many Spanish employers sponsor work visas, particularly in technology, engineering, finance and consulting. Companies such as Glovo, Cabify, TravelPerk, Revolut, Siemens Gamesa, Airbus, Banco Santander, Accenture Spain and Deloitte Spain regularly recruit international professionals when qualified local talent is unavailable for specialist positions.


### **Do I need a job offer first?**


Yes. In most cases, you must have a confirmed job offer before applying for a Spanish work visa. Your employer starts the sponsorship process by submitting the required paperwork to the immigration authorities. Without an employment contract from a sponsoring company, you generally cannot begin the application process.


### **What is the HQP route?**


The Highly Qualified Professional (HQP) route is a fast-track immigration pathway for experienced professionals, managers and specialists hired into skilled positions. It's commonly used by multinational companies and technology firms, offering a quicker application process than the standard work permit, provided salary and eligibility requirements are met.


### **How long does the permit take?**


Processing times depend on the application type, but the Highly Qualified Professional route is usually much faster than a standard work permit. Once a complete application has been submitted, the Spanish authorities often issue a decision within 20 working days, although additional checks can occasionally extend the timeline.


### **Can I bring my family?**


Yes. Most sponsored workers can include their spouse or registered partner and dependent children in their immigration plans. Depending on your circumstances, certain dependent relatives may also qualify. Family members receive residence permits linked to your status and can usually live with you throughout your employment in Spain.
