# 50 basic Moroccan Arabic (Darija) phrases to learn before you travel

If you’re heading to Morocco as a traveller or digital nomad, packing the usual essentials for the trip is only half the preparation. Learning a handful of basic Moroccan Arabic (Darija) phrases is the other half, and might significantly impact your experience as a visitor.


Moroccan Arabic, known as Darija, is the everyday spoken language of Morocco and differs from Modern Standard Arabic. Learning a few phrases for greetings, shopping, and directions makes travel smoother and locals warmer. Start with hello (salam), thank you (shukran), and how much (bshhal).


Even if it isn’t as smooth as the natives’, sincere attempts at Darija are usually well-appreciated. This guide gives you 50 practical Darija phrases you’ll actually use: greetings, shopping, ordering food, asking directions, handling transport, and everyday exchanges.

## What is Moroccan Arabic (Darija)?


Darija is the Arabic dialect spoken in Morocco. It is[different from Modern Standard Arabic (MSA)](https://talkinarabic.com/moroccan/msa-vs-moroccan-darija/) , the formal form of Arabic used in official documents, news, literature, and across the Arab world.


Over centuries, the dialect has been influenced by Berber (Amazigh) languages, French, and Spanish. This explains why the vocabulary and pronunciation can sound very different from MSA and other Arabic dialects spoken in countries such as Egypt, Saudi Arabia, and Lebanon.


An Arabic speaker from Egypt or Saudi Arabia may find it difficult to follow a conversation in Darija. Most Moroccans, however, learn MSA at school and can understand it and speak more formally when needed. This means that phrases from a general Arabic phrasebook may not always be the phrases you hear in everyday conversations in Morocco.


French is also widely spoken in Morocco, a legacy of the French protectorate era, and many urban Moroccans mix French and Darija in daily conversation. Spanish has some presence in the north, near Tangier and the Spanish enclaves.

## Money and travel tips for Morocco


It is not enough to learn ‘salam’ and ‘afak’. You also need to learn about Morocco’s financial system and how to manage your finances in Morocco.


The Moroccan dirham (MAD) is a closed currency. This means you cannot access, exchange, or hold it outside Morocco.


So, you have to be in Morocco physically before you can exchange your currency for dirhams. You can access exchange services at the airport, banks, or authorised exchange bureaux. ## Frequently asked questions about Darija phrases


### **Is Darija hard to learn?**


Darija is considered challenging for beginners, particularly because it is a spoken dialect with heavy vowel dropping and consonant clusters, and it is not commonly taught in formal Arabic language programmes, which usually focus on Modern Standard Arabic. However, learning basic conversational phrases for travel purposes is very achievable. Native French, Spanish, or Arabic speakers often find it faster to pick up conversational Darija given the language's mixed vocabulary.


### **Is Darija the same as Arabic?**


Darija is a dialect of Arabic, but it differs substantially from Modern Standard Arabic (the formal written and broadcast Arabic used across the Arab world) and from other regional dialects such as Egyptian or Levantine Arabic. A speaker of MSA or another Arabic dialect will typically find Darija difficult to understand due to its distinct vocabulary, pronunciation, and heavy influence from Berber and French.


### **What language is spoken in Morocco?**


Morocco has two official languages: Modern Standard Arabic and Tamazight (Berber). In everyday life, most Moroccans speak Darija (Moroccan Arabic) as their primary spoken language. French is widely used in business, education, and government, a legacy of the French protectorate period. Some communities in the Rif and Atlas mountain regions speak Berber languages as their first language.


### **Can you get by with French or English in Morocco?**


French is very useful in Morocco, particularly in cities, business settings, and among educated Moroccans. Many signs, menus, and official documents are in French alongside Arabic. English is increasingly spoken in tourist-facing businesses, hotels, and by younger Moroccans, but it is less universal than French. Combining basic Darija phrases with French, where you can, gives the most practical coverage.


### **How do you say good morning in Darija?**


Good morning in Darija is *sbah lkhir* (صباح الخير), pronounced "sbah lkheer." This is a warm and commonly used greeting in the morning across Morocco, whether you are greeting a shopkeeper, taxi driver, or hotel staff.


### **What is the currency used in Morocco?**


Morocco uses the Moroccan dirham (MAD). It is a closed currency, meaning it cannot be legally exchanged or obtained outside Morocco. Travellers exchange currency for dirham once they arrive, either at the airport, banks, or authorised exchange bureaux.
