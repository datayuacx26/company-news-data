## **How work visa sponsorship works in the Netherlands**


Getting a work visa in the Netherlands starts with finding the right employer. Not every company can sponsor international workers.


To hire non-EU nationals under the Highly Skilled Migrant (HSM) scheme, an employer must first be approved by the Dutch Immigration and Naturalisation Service (IND) and appear on the official Recognised Sponsor Registry.


Once you're hired, the company becomes your official sponsor and applies for your work and residence permit on your behalf, making the process much faster than many other immigration routes.


You'll also need to meet the minimum salary requirement for the Highly Skilled Migrant scheme. In 2026, the minimum gross monthly salary is €5,942 for applicants aged 30 and over and €4,357 for those under 30. Your employer prepares and submits the application, pays the government fees and handles the legal compliance required throughout the sponsorship process.

## **How to apply for a sponsored job in the Netherlands**


### **Find recognised sponsors**


Search for vacancies with employers listed on the IND Recognised Sponsor Registry. Many companies also advertise sponsored roles on LinkedIn, Indeed, company career pages and specialist recruitment websites for international professionals.


### **Tailor your CV**


Adapt your CV and cover letter for every application. Highlight relevant technical skills, measurable achievements and international experience, making it clear that you meet the requirements for a Highly Skilled Migrant position.


### **Secure a job offer**


Once you receive an offer, your employer begins the sponsorship process. They submit your work and residence permit application to the IND and act as your official sponsor throughout the procedure.


### **Prepare your documents**


Gather the required documents early, including your passport, educational certificates, employment contract and any additional paperwork requested by your employer or the Dutch immigration authorities to avoid unnecessary delays.


### **Wait for approval**


Processing times vary, but recognised sponsors benefit from the faster Highly Skilled Migrant procedure. Once approved, you'll receive your residence permit, allowing you to relocate and begin working in the Netherlands.

## Frequently asked questions


### **What is a recognised sponsor?**


A recognised sponsor is a company approved by the[Dutch Immigration and Naturalisation Service (IND)](https://ind.nl/nl) to hire skilled workers from outside the European Union. These employers can apply for work and residence permits on behalf of eligible employees, making the visa process faster and more straightforward than standard immigration routes for qualified international professionals.


### **Do I need a job offer first?**


Yes. In most cases, you must secure a job offer from an IND-recognised sponsor before applying for a Highly Skilled Migrant permit. Your employer starts the application process after offering you the position, so you cannot normally apply for this work permit independently without employer sponsorship first.


### **What is the salary threshold?**


To qualify under the Highly Skilled Migrant scheme, your salary must meet the annual minimum set by the Dutch government. In 2026, this is €5,942 gross monthly for applicants aged 30 and above, and €4,357 gross monthly for those under 30, excluding holiday allowance in most cases.


### **How long does the permit take?**


Processing times vary, but applications submitted by recognised sponsors are usually handled much faster than standard work permits. Once the employer submits a complete application, the IND often reaches a decision within two to four weeks, although complex cases or missing documents can take longer to process.


### **Can I bring my family?**


Yes. If you qualify for a Dutch Highly Skilled Migrant permit, your spouse, registered partner and dependent children can usually apply to join you. Eligible family members receive residence permits, and partners are generally allowed to work in the Netherlands without needing a separate work permit after approval.
