# Best business bank accounts for an LLC in 2026

Choosing a business bank account is one of the earliest decisions you have to make when setting up an LLC. Your choice will ultimately determine how easily you manage your finances and how much you spend on fees. Having to switch banks in the middle of an operation can be an administrative mess for you and your clients. So, aim to get it right from the inception.


The best business bank account for an LLC depends on your priorities: low fees, cash deposits, integrations or international payments. Online providers often waive monthly fees, while large banks offer branches and credit. An LLC should always keep a separate account to protect its liability shield and simplify taxes.


Choosing a business bank account depends on what your business needs. Some businesses handle large cash volumes and need quick deposits at physical branches. Others work with international clients and want seamless multi-currency support or low-cost overseas transfers. Many simply want the lowest possible fees and a clean digital experience. This guide compares the leading options and explains what to weigh before committing.


## Why an LLC needs its own bank account


The most important reason to separate business and personal bank accounts is that you are legally required to do so.


As the name implies, a limited liability company (LLC) provides its owners with a liability shield. So, your personal assets are generally protected from business debts and lawsuits. But this protection depends on maintaining a clear separation between the business and the business owner.


Mixing personal and business funds in one account is called commingling. A court can "pierce the corporate veil" by disregarding the business's separate legal entity status. As such, your personal assets can be used for your business liabilities, which defeats the point of starting an LLC in the first place.


The[US Small Business Administration](https://legacy.sba.gov/blog/5-ways-separate-your-personal-business-finances) recommends keeping business and personal finances separate. Doing so also makes bookkeeping, tax filing, and tracking business expenses much easier


A dedicated business account also makes your business look more professional when paying vendors or receiving client payments. You may also need one to access services such as merchant accounts for accepting card payments.

## The best business bank accounts for an LLC


There are plenty of business bank accounts available to LLCs, from fully online options to traditional banks with physical branches. The right choice depends on how you run your business and what you need from your account.


### **1. Mercury**


Mercury is popular with startups and online businesses. It offers free business chequing and savings accounts with no monthly fees or minimum balance requirements. Everything can be managed online, and it integrates with several accounting and payment tools. Users can create physical and virtual corporate debit or credit cards with custom spending limits.


- **Fees:** No monthly fee or minimum balance, free ACH transfers.
- **Best for:** Startups, online businesses, and tech-focused LLCs that prefer digital banking


### **2. Chase Business Complete Banking**


Chase is one of the largest traditional banks in the US, with branches across the country. Its Business Complete Banking account offers access to in-person banking, though a monthly fee applies unless you meet certain requirements that allow it to be waived. Such criteria include having a $2,000 minimum daily ending balance and spending $2,000 on eligible purchases using a Chase business credit card. The bank also offers invoicing features, fraud protection, and mobile check deposits.


- **Fees:** $15/month, waivable with a qualifying balance or transactions
- **Best for:** LLCs that deposit cash regularly or want access to physical branches


### **3. Bluevine Business Checking**


Bluevine offers a free business chequing account with tiered interest rates on your account balances, unlike most free business accounts. It is entirely online with no physical branches. It has up to $3 million in FDIC insurance through its partner, Coastal Community Bank/


- **Fees:** No monthly fee on the standard. Plus and Premier accounts have monthly fees that can be waived under certain criteria. They also offer a higher interest rate than standard accounts and are better for larger LLCs.
- **Best for:** LLCs that want to earn interest on operating funds without paying account fees


### **4. Novo**


Novo is a popular fintech platform offering business accounts designed for small businesses and freelancers. It integrates with popular invoicing and accounting tools and offers a straightforward, low-frills experience. Its banking infrastructure and FDIC insurance ($250,000 protection) are provided through its partner bank, Middlesex Federal Savings. Novo reserves ensure you can split funds into up to 20 automated sub-buckets, such that you can automatically move a set percentage of your income to each reserve, whether it is for taxes, salaries, or saving towards a business expense.


- **Fees:** No monthly fee, no minimum balance
- **Best for:** Freelancers and small LLCs that want a simple account with good software integrations


### **5. Bank of America Business Advantage**


Bank of America offers a range of business accounts with extensive branch and ATM access across the US. It is a solid option for LLCs that value an established relationship with a bank and access to business credit products. The Business Advantage Fundamentals Banking is best for smaller businesses and startups, while the Business Advantage Relationship Banking is tailored to older and larger LLCs


- **Fees:** $16 to $29.95/month, depending on tier, waivable with qualifying balances
- **Best for:** Established LLCs seeking a full-service bank with credit and lending options


### **6. Wells Fargo Initiate Business Checking**


This is an update and rebranding of the Business Choice Checking account. Wells Fargo is a well-established business banking product with a wide branch network and straightforward account tiers. It requires a $25 opening deposit to activate. It includes 100 fee-free transactions per month with free cash deposits for amounts under $5,000 (then $0.30 per $100). There are higher tiers like Navigate and Optimise Business Checking for larger businesses.


- **Fees:** $15/month, waivable with a minimum daily balance of $2,000.
- **Best for:** LLCs wanting a large traditional bank presence


### **7. Grey Business Account**


Grey Business is designed for businesses that receive and send money internationally. It gives eligible businesses access to multi-currency accounts, allowing you to receive payments in currencies such as USD, GBP, and EUR using local banking details.


For an LLC working with overseas clients, this can simplify international payments and reduce the costs associated with traditional wire transfers. You can also manage different currencies from one platform rather than maintaining separate accounts for each market.

## Best free and low-cost options


If avoiding monthly fees is your top priority, several of the accounts above stand out specifically for this. Grey, Mercury, Bluevine, Novo, Relay, and Axos all offer accounts with no monthly maintenance fee and no minimum balance requirement.


The downside is that these options are fully free online accounts and don’t have physical branches. So, if your account deals with cash, making cash deposits might be somewhat difficult. Many digital banks use third-party networks (like Green Dot) for cash deposits, which can be slower, cost extra, and be less convenient than depositing cash directly at a branch.


For businesses that handle little or no cash, a free online account can be a cost-effective choice. If you regularly deposit cash, a traditional bank with local branches may be a better fit. If you deal with multiple currencies and international clients, a multi-currency account like Grey makes it much easier and cheaper to receive international payments.


## What to look for in an LLC account


Before choosing, weigh the following factors against how your specific LLC works.


- **Monthly fees and waiver conditions:** Some accounts advertised as "free" charge a fee unless you maintain a certain balance or meet specific transaction requirements. Check the conditions before opening an account.
- **Cash handling:** If your business regularly receives cash, choose a bank with local branches or a reliable cash deposit network such as Green Dot. An online account without a convenient way to deposit cash may not be suitable for a cash-heavy business.
- **Integrations:** Look for connections with tools you already use, such as QuickBooks, Xero, payment processors, and payroll platforms. Good integrations can save time and reduce manual work.
- **International payments:** If you work with clients outside the US, check the fees for sending and receiving international payments. Also, check whether the account provides local receiving details in currencies your clients use.
- **Opening deposit and balance requirements:** Some accounts require an initial deposit or minimum ongoing balance. Make sure these requirements fit your business’s cash flow.

## Frequently asked questions about business bank accounts for LLCs


### **What is the best bank account for an LLC?**


There is no single best option: it depends on your business's specific needs. Grey, Mercury and Bluevine are strong choices for online businesses wanting no fees. Chase and Bank of America suit LLCs that handle cash regularly or want branch access. Relay suits businesses that want built-in sub-account budgeting. Evaluate against your actual transaction patterns rather than a generic ranking.


### **Is there a free business bank account for an LLC?**


Yes. Mercury, Bluevine, Novo, and Grey all offer business accounts with no monthly maintenance fee and no minimum balance requirement. These are strong options for LLCs that operate primarily through digital payments rather than cash.


### **Does an LLC need a separate bank account?**


Yes, in practice, even if not strictly required by every state's law. Keeping business and personal funds separate is essential to preserving the LLC's liability shield. Commingling funds is one of the most common reasons courts allow the "corporate veil" to be pierced, exposing the owner's personal assets to business liabilities.


### **What do I need to open an LLC bank account?**


It depends on the bank. Typically, you will need your LLC's Articles of Organisation (or Certificate of Formation), your EIN (Employer Identification Number) from the IRS, your LLC operating agreement (some banks require this), and a valid government-issued ID for the account signatories. Requirements vary slightly by bank.


### **Can I get an LLC bank account entirely online?**


Yes. Mercury, Bluevine, Novo, Relay, and Axos all offer fully online account opening with no branch visit required. Traditional banks like Chase, Bank of America, and Wells Fargo also offer online applications for many of their business account tiers, though a branch visit may still be required in some cases, depending on the specifics.


### **How do I receive international payments as an LLC?**


Opening a multi-currency account alongside your primary US business account is the most cost-effective approach for LLCs regularly invoicing international clients. Grey provides GBP and EUR accounts with real local banking details, allowing UK and European clients to pay as they would a domestic supplier, with the full amount arriving without SWIFT wire deductions.
