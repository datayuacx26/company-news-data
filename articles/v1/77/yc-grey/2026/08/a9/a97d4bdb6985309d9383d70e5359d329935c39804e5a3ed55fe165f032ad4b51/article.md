# 41 companies in Germany that sponsor work visas in 2026

Germany is one of the best countries to move to as a professional. Despite the country’s economic prowess, they still face shortages in technology, engineering, healthcare, logistics, and manufacturing. This creates a unique opportunity for eligible Nigerians and other African professionals to fill in the gaps.


Germany sponsors foreign professionals through the skilled worker visa and the EU Blue Card, both of which require a qualifying job offer. Employers in engineering, IT, healthcare and manufacturing regularly hire from abroad. The Blue Card requires a recognised degree and a salary above a set threshold.


This guide explains how the sponsorship process works, which companies are known to hire from abroad, and what to expect once you arrive.


## How work visa sponsorship works in Germany


Unlike countries such as the[UK](http://grey.co/blog/uk-visa-sponsorship-jobs-nigerians) , Germany doesn’t have a formal register of licensed sponsors. There is one official list of companies that can sponsor foreign workers. Instead, any company in Germany can hire someone outside the EU if the role meets the visa requirements, and the employer is willing to go through the process. Usually, large companies and multinationals already have international recruitment teams familiar with the process.


There are two relevant visa routes for skilled workers. For a full explanation of visa options and living in Germany as a foreigner, see our[Germany digital nomad and expat work visa guide](https://grey.co/blog/living-in-germany-expat) .


### **The skilled worker visa**


This option is available to non-EU citizens who have a recognised qualification (degree or vocational qualification) and a valid job offer from a German employer. Your qualifications must generally be recognised in Germany, and the job should match your training or experience. Salary requirements depend on the occupation and current immigration rules rather than a single fixed threshold. The employer does not need to demonstrate that no EU candidate was available (as is required in some countries, including[Luxembourg](http://grey.co/blog/luxembourg-work-permit) ), but the role must align with the applicant's qualifications. Full details on requirements are available at[Make It In Germany](http://make-it-in-germany.com/) .


### **The EU Blue Card**


An EU Blue Card is the juicy route, but more difficult. It offers a faster path to permanent residence, easier family reunification, and the ability to move and work within the EU. This option is
designed for highly qualified professionals with a recognised university degree and a job offer that meets the minimum salary requirement. In 2026, the minimum annual gross salary is approximately €48,300 for most professions and around €43,760 for recognised shortage occupations, including IT, engineering, mathematics, natural sciences, and healthcare (subject to annual updates).


With the EU Blue Card, you can move to other EU countries after 18 months, and you can become a permanent resident after 21 months, especially if you have good German language skills.

## Industries most likely to sponsor


Germany is experiencing labour shortages across several sectors, making it easier for qualified international professionals to secure jobs with visa sponsorship. If you have experience in one of these fields, your chances of finding an employer willing to hire from abroad are significantly higher.


- **IT and software:** Software engineers, data scientists, cloud architects, and cybersecurity professionals.
- **Engineering:** Mechanical, electrical, civil, and aerospace engineers with recognised qualifications and German or English communication skills are in high demand.
- **Healthcare:** Doctors, nurses, dentists, physiotherapists, and other healthcare workers. They might need to write the appropriate qualifying exam. Clinical roles generally require German language proficiency, often at the B2 level or above.
- **Manufacturing and production:** Skilled trades, process engineers, and quality management specialists are in demand in the chemicals, automotive, and food production sectors.


## How to apply for a sponsored role in Germany


Finding an employer willing to sponsor your work visa requires more than simply submitting applications. Following the right process improves your chances of success.


### **Step 1: Get your qualifications recognised**


Germany requires many foreign qualifications to be recognised before a work visa can be issued. Use the official[ANABIN](https://nigeria.diplo.de/ng-en/anabin-2743860) database or the[Recognition in Germany](https://www.anerkennung-in-deutschland.de/html/en/index.php) portal to verify whether your degree or vocational qualification is recognised.


Regulated professions such as medicine, nursing, pharmacy, and teaching usually require additional recognition from the relevant German authority before you can begin work.


### **Step 2: Find roles on the right platforms**


Focus on employers that regularly recruit international talent. Good places to search include:


- LinkedIn Jobs
- StepStone Germany
- Indeed Germany
- Make it in Germany Job Exchange
- Company careers pages


Where it is possible, filter for roles that explicitly mention "sponsorship" or "relocation support”. Most large companies accept applications in English even for roles that eventually need you to learn some German.


### **Step 3: Tailor your CV to German standards**


German employers expect a detailed CV that clearly outlines your qualifications and work experience.


Your application should usually include:


- A professional CV
- A tailored cover letter ( *Anschreiben* )
- Copies of relevant qualifications
- Professional certifications where applicable


Unlike UK CVs, German CVs commonly include a professional photograph and personal details such as your date of birth, although this is becoming less common at international companies.


### **Step 4: Receive a job offer**


Once you successfully complete the interview process, your employer issues a formal employment contract.


If you're applying for an EU Blue Card, your salary must meet the applicable threshold. For the Skilled Worker visa, your qualification must generally be recognised and relevant to the position offered.


### **Step 5: Apply for your visa and wait**


Submit your visa application through the German embassy or consulate in your country. The documents they commonly ask for include:


- Valid passport
- Signed employment contract
- Qualification recognition documents
- Completed visa application form
- Biometric photographs
- Proof of health insurance (where required)
- Any additional documents requested by the embassy


Processing usually takes between six and twelve weeks, although timelines vary depending on your country and profession.


### **Step 6:** Register your address and settle in


After arriving, you must register your address ( *Anmeldung* ) with the local registration office, usually within 14 days.


You'll also need to:


- Obtain your tax identification number,
- Open a German bank account,
- Register for health insurance (if applicable), and
- Resume your job.

## Frequently asked questions


### **Which companies in Germany sponsor work visas most actively?**


Many big German companies regularly hire international talent and support work visa applications. These include SAP, Siemens, Deutsche Telekom, Bosch, Deutsche Bank, Allianz, Zalando, Delivery Hero, and the Big Four firms (Deloitte, PwC, KPMG, and EY). Healthcare providers such as Fresenius and hospital groups like Charité also sponsor qualified medical professionals. Always confirm that sponsorship is available for the specific role before applying.


### **What is the EU Blue Card?**


The EU Blue Card is a residence and work permit for highly qualified non-EU professionals. In Germany, you generally need a recognised university degree and a qualifying job offer that meets the minimum salary threshold (with lower thresholds for shortage occupations). It offers benefits such as faster access to permanent residence and easier family reunification..


### **Do I need to speak German to work in Germany?**


It depends on the industry. Many IT, engineering, finance, and multinational companies use English as their working language, so German may not be required initially. However, healthcare and customer-facing roles usually require German, often at B2 level or higher. Learning German can also improve your job prospects and everyday life in Germany.


### **How long does the German work visa process take?**


After submitting a complete application, most work visa applications are processed within six to twelve weeks. Qualification recognition for regulated professions can take an additional three to six months, so it is worth starting this process as early as possible..


### **Can I bring my family to Germany on a work visa?**


Yes. Spouses and dependent children can join you in Germany on a family reunification visa. This process is usually easier and faster for people with an EU Blue Card. However, your salary must be enough to support the family, and you will need appropriate accommodation for your family size.
