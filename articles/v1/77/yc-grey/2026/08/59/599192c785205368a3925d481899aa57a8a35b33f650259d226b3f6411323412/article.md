# Sal, Cape Verde: Complete travel guide

Golden beaches, year-round sunshine and crystal-clear Atlantic waters have made Sal the gateway to Cape Verde for travellers from around the world. Whether you are looking for a relaxing beach escape, thrilling water sports or a laid-back island atmosphere, Sal offers the perfect introduction to this volcanic archipelago.


Most visitors arrive through Amílcar Cabral International Airport before heading to Santa Maria, the island's lively resort town on the southern coast. Here, colourful streets, beachfront restaurants, local markets and a long stretch of white sand create an easy-going destination that suits couples, families, solo travellers and digital nomads alike.


Beyond the beaches, Sal offers plenty to discover, from floating in the salt crater at Pedra de Lume to watching lemon sharks along Shark Bay and enjoying unforgettable sunsets over the Atlantic. This guide covers everything you need to know before visiting, including the best places to see, costs, transport, safety tips and practical travel advice.


## **Everything you need to know about Sal Island, Cape Verde**


Sal Island is one of the most popular destinations in Cape Verde, located in the central Atlantic Ocean around 350 miles (560 kilometres) off the coast of West Africa. Covering just 216 square kilometres, it is relatively small and mostly flat, with a warm, dry climate and more than 350 days of sunshine each year.


The island attracts visitors for its long stretches of golden sand, turquoise waters and relaxed atmosphere. It is also recognised as one of the world's leading destinations for kitesurfing and windsurfing thanks to its reliable trade winds. Popular attractions include the colourful town of Santa Maria and the famous Pedra de Lume salt crater, where visitors can float effortlessly in mineral-rich water.


The best time to visit Sal is between November and June, when rainfall is low and temperatures typically range from 24°C to 30°C. These months offer pleasant weather for beach holidays, water sports and exploring the island comfortably.


## **How to get to Sal Island, Cape Verde**


Travelling to Sal Island is straightforward, with most international visitors arriving through Amílcar Cabral International Airport (SID), the island's main gateway. The airport is located near Espargos, around 20 minutes from Santa Maria, where many of Sal's hotels, resorts and beaches are found. Direct flights operate from several European cities, making the island one of the easiest destinations in Cape Verde to reach.


Airlines such as TUI and easyJet offer seasonal and year-round flights from cities including London Gatwick, Manchester, Lisbon, Paris and Frankfurt. Travellers from other parts of the world can usually connect through Portugal or another European hub before flying to Sal.


After landing, reaching your accommodation is simple. You can book a private airport transfer in advance, take a shared minibus if available or use one of the licensed taxis waiting outside the arrivals terminal. Most journeys to Santa Maria take about 20 minutes, depending on traffic.

## **What to see and do in Santa Maria**


Santa Maria is the beating heart of Sal Island and the place where most visitors choose to stay. Located on the southern coast, this lively beach town combines beautiful white-sand beaches with colourful streets, fresh seafood, local culture and a relaxed island atmosphere. Whether you are visiting for a few days or a week, Santa Maria offers everything you need within easy walking distance.


At the centre of town is the **S** anta Maria Pier, where local fishermen return each day with freshly caught tuna, lobster and other seafood. Around the pier, you'll find cafés, restaurants, souvenir shops and beach bars overlooking the Atlantic. As the sun sets, the town transforms into one of Cape Verde's liveliest nightlife destinations, with traditional Morna music, Kizomba dancing and live bands filling bars such as Ocean Café and Buddy's Bar until late into the night.


**Popular places to stay:**


- **Hilton Cabo Verde Sal Resort:** Luxury beachfront resort with pools, spa facilities and direct beach access.
- **Meliá Tortuga Beach:** All-inclusive resort popular with families, couples and longer holiday stays.
- **Boutique hotels:** Smaller guesthouses and boutique hotels close to the town centre offer easy access to restaurants, beaches and nightlife.


**Typical costs:**


- **Local taxi within Santa Maria:** €2–€4 (about 200–400 CVE)
- **Airport taxi to Santa Maria:** €15–€20 (about 1,500–2,200 CVE)
- **Casual meal at a local restaurant:** €7–€12 per person

## **Best things to do in Sal, Cape Verde**


Discover the top attractions in Sal, from golden beaches and natural wonders to watersports and unforgettable island experiences.


### **Santa Maria beach**


Sal’s most famous beach stretches along the coast of Santa Maria, offering soft white sand, clear Atlantic waters and a relaxed atmosphere. It is perfect for swimming, sunbathing, beach walks and watching spectacular sunsets.


### **Pedra de Lume salt crater**


Located inside an extinct volcano, Pedra de Lume is one of Sal’s most unique attractions. Visitors can float effortlessly in the salt-rich waters, explore the historic salt pans and enjoy panoramic views of the surrounding landscape.


### **Shark Bay**


Shark Bay offers a chance to see lemon sharks swimming close to the shore in their natural environment. Visitors can walk into the shallow waters and observe these gentle creatures with the help of local guides.


### **Buracona Blue Eye**


The Buracona lagoon is famous for its natural “Blue Eye” effect, where sunlight creates a glowing blue colour inside a volcanic cave. It is one of Sal’s most photographed natural landmarks.


### **Watersports**


With strong Atlantic winds and warm waters, Sal is a favourite destination for kitesurfing, windsurfing, diving and snorkelling. The island’s conditions attract both beginners and experienced water sports enthusiasts.

## **Where are the best areas to stay in Sal, Cape Verde?**


Choosing where to stay in Sal depends on the type of experience you want. Most travellers base themselves around Santa Maria, the island’s main tourist hub, while quieter areas like Pontinha offer a more peaceful escape.


### Santa Maria: Best for first-time visitors


Santa Maria is the most popular place to stay in Sal, offering easy access to beaches, restaurants, bars, shops and local experiences. It is ideal for travellers who want everything within reach, from morning beach walks to evening entertainment and seafood restaurants.


### Pontinha: Best for a quieter luxury stay


Located close to Santa Maria, Pontinha offers a more relaxed atmosphere with upscale resorts and beachfront accommodation. It suits couples and travellers looking for privacy while still staying near the island’s main attractions.


### Espargos: Best for local experiences


Espargos is Sal’s main town and offers a glimpse into everyday island life. It is a practical choice for budget travellers, those on longer stays, or visitors who want to experience Cape Verde beyond the resort areas.


### Accommodation costs in Sal


- **Budget stays (€40–€80 per night):** Comfortable hotels and guesthouses offering simple rooms, often without luxury facilities or all-inclusive options.
- **Premium resorts (€180–€350+ per night):** Luxury properties such as Hilton Cabo Verde and Meliá Dunas Resort offer pools, beachfront access and resort experiences, although some are further from local communities.


Choosing where to stay depends on whether you prefer convenience, local culture or a full resort experience.


## **Managing payments while in Sal, Cape Verde**


Landing in Sal means stepping into a relaxed island rhythm, but knowing how to pay for things makes exploring much easier. The local currency is the Cape Verdean Escudo (CVE), which you will use for everyday purchases such as local meals, taxis, markets and small shops. Some tourist areas also accept euros, especially hotels and restaurants.


As you move around the island, you will find ATMs in popular areas like Santa Maria, while larger hotels, restaurants and shops commonly accept debit and credit cards. However, smaller businesses and local vendors may still prefer cash, so keeping some escudos with you is always useful.

## **Frequently asked questions**


### **How do I get to Sal?**


Most visitors reach Sal by flying into Amílcar Cabral International Airport (SID), the island’s main airport. Direct flights operate from several European cities, including destinations in the UK, Portugal and Germany, making Sal one of the easiest Cape Verde islands to access for international travellers.


### **Is Sal worth visiting?**


Yes, Sal is worth visiting for travellers seeking beautiful beaches, warm weather and a relaxed island escape. The island is especially popular with sun-seekers, watersports enthusiasts and visitors who want to experience attractions such as Pedra de Lume salt flats and the famous Blue Eye lagoon.


### **What is the best time to visit Sal?**


The best time to visit Sal is between November and June, when the island experiences warm temperatures, minimal rainfall and comfortable trade winds. These months offer excellent conditions for swimming, sightseeing, beach activities and watersports, making it the ideal period for exploring the island.


### **Do I need a visa for Sal?**


Visa requirements depend on your nationality. Many European and UK passport holders do not need a visa for short stays but must complete Cape Verde’s online EASE pre-registration before arrival. Always check the latest entry requirements before travelling, as rules can change.


### **What currency does Sal use?**


Sal uses the Cape Verdean Escudo (CVE) as its official currency. Euros are also commonly accepted in tourist areas, with a fixed exchange rate of approximately €1 to 110 CVE. However, using local currency is often more convenient when paying for smaller purchases and everyday expenses.


### **Can I pay by card in Sal?**


Cards are accepted at many hotels, resorts and larger restaurants in Sal, especially around Santa Maria. However, smaller shops, local restaurants and taxis often require cash payments. Carrying some Cape Verdean Escudos or euros is recommended when exploring outside the main tourist areas.
