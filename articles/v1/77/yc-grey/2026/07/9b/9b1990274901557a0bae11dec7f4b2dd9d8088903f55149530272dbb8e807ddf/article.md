# Expat Living in Spain: Banking and Finance Guide | Grey

Spain is one of Europe's most popular destinations for expats, offering a high quality of life, excellent public services and a relatively affordable cost of living. Whether you're moving for work, retirement or a better lifestyle, understanding how to manage your finances is an essential part of settling in.


Expats living in Spain need a NIE (Foreigner Identification Number), a registered address, and a Spanish bank account to access local services. The cost of living is moderate by European standards, with a single expat needing €1,400 to €2,200 per month. Madrid and Barcelona are the most expensive cities.


From opening a bank account and understanding Spain's financial system to managing international payments and avoiding unnecessary transfer fees, this guide covers everything you need to know. Whether you're receiving a salary, pension or freelance income from abroad, you'll also learn how to manage your money efficiently while living in Spain.


## **Why do expats choose to live in Spain?**


With more than 300 days of sunshine in many regions, fresh local cuisine and one of Europe's best public healthcare systems, Spain offers an appealing balance of affordability and quality of life.


Beyond the lifestyle, Spain has well-established international communities that make settling in much easier. Popular destinations such as the Costa del Sol, Valencia and Madrid are home to thriving expat populations, international schools, English-speaking services and active social networks, helping newcomers integrate quickly.


Whether you're relocating for work, retirement or remote living, Spain combines modern infrastructure with a welcoming culture and excellent connectivity to the rest of Europe. From vibrant cities to relaxed coastal towns, it offers a lifestyle that appeals to professionals, families, retirees and digital nomads alike.

## How to move to Spain as an expat: A step-by-step guide


Relocating to Spain involves a few essential administrative steps, regardless of whether it's for work, retirement or a new lifestyle. While the process differs for EU and non-EU citizens, planning ahead will make your transition much smoother.


**Step 1: Secure the right visa (Non-EU citizens only)**


Apply for a visa that matches your purpose of stay, such as a Digital Nomad Visa, Non-Lucrative Visa, Work Visa or Student Visa. EU citizens do not need a visa to live and work in Spain.


**Step 2: Obtain your NIE**


Apply for a Número de Identidad de Extranjero (NIE), your foreigner identification number required for employment, banking, taxes and most official transactions.


**Step 3: Register your address (Padrón)**


Register your residential address with your local town hall (Empadronamiento). This is often required when accessing public services and completing residency formalities.


**Step 4: Open a Spanish bank account**


A local bank account is essential for receiving your salary, paying rent, setting up direct debits and managing everyday expenses.


**Step 5: Register for healthcare**


If you're employed or legally resident, register for Spain's public healthcare system. Alternatively, many visa holders, including digital nomads and retirees, are required to maintain private health insurance that meets Spanish immigration requirements.

## How to open a bank account in Spain as an expat


Opening a Spanish bank account is one of the first financial tasks you'll need to complete after moving. A local account makes it easier to receive your salary, pay rent, set up direct debits and manage everyday expenses.


**Step 1: Choose the right account**


- **Resident account:** For individuals living and paying taxes in Spain.
- **Non-resident account:** Suitable for those buying property, spending part of the year in Spain or preparing to relocate.


**Step 2: Gather the required documents**


- Valid passport or national ID (EU citizens)
- NIE (Foreigner Identification Number)
- Proof of address
- Proof of employment, income or student status (if requested)


**Step 3: Visit a bank or apply online**


Submit your documents, complete the application and verify your identity. Some banks allow non-residents to begin the process online before visiting a branch.


**Step 4: Activate your account**


Once approved, you'll receive your IBAN, debit card and access to online banking, allowing you to make transfers, pay bills and receive payments in Spain.

## Tax rules every expat in Spain should know


Understanding Spain's tax system is essential before relocating, as your tax obligations depend on your residency status and the type of income you earn.


**Tax residency**


- **Tax residents:** If you spend more than 183 days in Spain during a calendar year, or your main economic interests or immediate family are based there, you're generally considered a tax resident. Tax residents must declare and pay tax on their worldwide income.
- **Non-residents:** If you spend fewer than 183 days in Spain and have no significant economic or family ties there, you're generally taxed only on Spanish-sourced income, such as rental income from property in Spain.


**Income tax (IRPF)**


Spain operates a progressive income tax system, with combined state and regional rates typically ranging from:


- Up to **€12,450:** 19%
- **€12,451–€20,200:** 24%
- **€20,201–€35,200:** 30%
- **€35,201–€60,000:** 37%
- Above **€60,000:** 45–47% (higher in some regions)


**The Beckham Law**


Qualifying foreign professionals, entrepreneurs, and digital nomads may apply for Spain's Special Tax Regime, commonly known as the Beckham Law. If approved, you'll pay a flat 24% tax on Spanish employment income up to €600,000 for up to six tax years, while many types of foreign income remain exempt from Spanish taxation.


**Wealth tax**


Spain also levies a Wealth Tax on certain high-value assets. Tax residents are assessed on their worldwide net assets, while non-residents are taxed only on qualifying assets located in Spain.


## Frequently asked questions


### **What is the NIE, and how do I get one?**


The **Número de Identidad de Extranjero (NIE)** is a foreigner identification number required for most legal and financial activities in Spain. You'll need it to open a bank account, sign a rental agreement, pay taxes and register for utilities. You can apply through a Spanish consulate before moving or at a police station after arriving in Spain.


### **Can I keep my UK or US bank account while living in Spain?**


Yes. There's no requirement to close your existing UK or US bank account when you move to Spain. Many expats keep their home-country account for pensions, savings or existing financial commitments while opening a Spanish account for everyday expenses. Using both accounts together often provides greater flexibility when managing international finances.


### **Do I need private health insurance to move to Spain?**


It depends on your visa and residency status. Many non-EU visa holders, including those applying for the Non-Lucrative or Digital Nomad Visa, must have private health insurance that meets Spanish immigration requirements. If you're employed in Spain, you may qualify for the country's public healthcare system through Social Security contributions.


### **Can foreigners buy property in Spain?**


Yes. Foreigners can buy residential or investment property in Spain without being residents. You'll need an NIE, a Spanish bank account and a local solicitor to complete the purchase. Before buying, it's important to carry out legal checks, understand local taxes and budget for additional costs such as notary and registration fees.


### **Do I need to pay tax in Spain on foreign income?**


If you're a Spanish tax resident, you generally pay tax on your worldwide income, although tax treaties and special regimes such as the Beckham Law may reduce your liability. Non-residents are typically taxed only on income earned in Spain. A qualified tax adviser can help determine your obligations.
