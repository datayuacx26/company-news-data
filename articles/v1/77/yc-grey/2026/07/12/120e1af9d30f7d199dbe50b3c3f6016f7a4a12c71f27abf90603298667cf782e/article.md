# ACH vs Wire Transfer: Key Differences Explained | Grey

ACH and wire transfer are the two most common ways money moves between bank accounts in the US. However, they don’t work the same way, and understanding the difference helps you choose the right option for your payments. The right choice depends on how quickly you need the payment completed, how much you want to pay in fees, whether the transfer is domestic or international, and whether the transaction needs to be reversible.


ACH transfers are processed in batches through the Automated Clearing House network and typically take one to three business days. Wire transfers are sent directly between banks and usually arrive the same day or within 24 hours. ACH is cheaper and better for domestic payments. Wire is faster and used for international or large-value transfers.


This article is a breakdown of ACH and wire transfers, comparing fees, transaction speeds, transaction limits, and best use cases.


## What is an ACH transfer?


ACH stands for Automated Clearing House. It is the main electronic payment network used to move money between bank accounts in the United States. The network is operated by Nacha (formerly the National Automated Clearing House Association).


Instead of processing each payment one at a time, ACH collects payment instructions from banks throughout the day, groups them into batches, and processes them at scheduled intervals. This batching system keeps ACH transfers inexpensive, although they are generally slower than real-time payment methods.


There are two main types of ACH transactions:


- **ACH credits:** Money is pushed from the sender's bank account to the recipient's account. Common examples include payroll direct deposits, business invoice payments, tax refunds, and person-to-person transfers.
- **ACH debits:** Money is pulled from a payer's account with their authorisation. Examples include utility bills, mortgage payments, insurance premiums, gym memberships, and subscription services.


ACH payments follow Nacha's Operating Rules, which set the standards for how payments are processed, how long they take, when transactions can be returned, and the responsibilities of everyone involved in the payment process.

## What is a wire transfer?


A wire transfer is a direct electronic transfer of money from one bank account to another. Unlike ACH payments, which are processed in batches, wire transfers are handled individually. This makes them faster and more suitable for sending large sums or time-sensitive transactions.


There are two main types of wire transfers:


- **Domestic wire transfers:** In the United States, domestic wires are processed through either the Fedwire Funds Service (controlled by the Federal Reserve) or the Clearing House Interbank Payments System (CHIPS). Fedwire completes each transaction individually in real time. This means that if you initiate a transaction before the bank's daily cut-off time (usually between 4:00 p.m. and 6:00 p.m. Eastern Time), the payment is completed the same business day.
- **International wire transfers:** These are typically sent through the SWIFT (Society for Worldwide Interbank Financial Telecommunication) network. SWIFT isn’t exactly involved in moving the money from the sender to the receiver. Instead, it transmits the payment instructions that specify how the money will move from one bank to another. An international wire transfer may go through one or more correspondent banks before reaching the recipient, depending on the countries and banks involved. For a more detailed explanation, see[h](https://grey.co/blog/telegraphic-transfer-explained)[ow telegraphic transfers work.](https://grey.co/blog/telegraphic-transfer)


Wire transfers are commonly used when speed, security, and certainty of payment are more important than keeping costs low. Typical examples include:


- Purchasing property or other high-value assets
- Paying international suppliers
- Settling large business transactions
- Moving investment funds between financial institutions

## ACH vs wire transfer fees: which costs less?


ACH transfers are most often cheaper than wire transfers. And this fee difference might be significant enough if you are making frequent transfers.


### **ACH transfer fees**


Most US banks charge nothing for standard incoming ACH transfers. Outgoing ACH from a business account may cost $0.25 to $1.00 per transaction, depending on the bank. If you want the transaction completed on the same day, Nacha will charge an additional fee per transaction. Most banks pass this fee on to their customers, charging $5 to $15 per transaction.


### **Wire transfer fees**


Wire transfers are generally more expensive than ACH payments because they are processed individually and, in the case of international transfers, often involve multiple banks.


- **Domestic wire transfers:** Most major US banks charge **$15 to $30** for online wire transfers and **$25 to $35** for wires initiated at a branch. Receiving a domestic wire may cost **$15 to $25** , though some banks waive the fee.
- **International wire transfers:** Outgoing international wires typically cost **$35 to $50** . In addition, the recipient's bank may charge an incoming wire fee, and intermediary (correspondent) banks may deduct their own fees before the money reaches the recipient.


**Hidden costs in international wire transfers**


The transfer fee is only part of the total cost of an international wire. Two additional charges often reduce the amount the recipient receives:


- **Correspondent bank fees:** If the payment passes through intermediary banks, each may deduct $10 to $25 from the transfer before it reaches the recipient.
- **Exchange rate markup:** If the receiving bank converts the money into another currency, it typically applies an exchange rate that is 1% to 3% less favourable than the mid-market rate.


By contrast, ACH transfers have a simpler and more transparent pricing structure, with fixed fees and no correspondent bank deductions because the payment stays within the US banking system.

## Which is faster, ACH or wire transfer?


Wire transfers are generally faster than ACH transfers, especially for urgent payments. However, Same Day ACH makes the difference less obvious.


- **Standard ACH:** Usually takes one to three business days. The exact timing depends on when the payment is submitted, as ACH transactions are processed in batches several times throughout the business day.
- **Same Day ACH:** Eligible payments submitted before the bank's cut-off time (typically around 2:45 pm ET) can settle on the same business day. Availability varies by bank and transaction type.
- **Domestic wire transfers:** Processed individually through Fedwire or CHIPS and usually arrive the same business day if sent before the bank's cut-off time (often between 4 pm and 6 pm ET). Once completed, a wire transfer is generally final and cannot be reversed.
- **International wire transfers:** Typically take **one to five business days** , depending on the countries involved, the currencies being transferred, and whether intermediary banks are part of the payment route. Transfers between major currencies such as USD and GBP or USD and EUR often arrive within one to two business days.


So, if speed matters, choose domestic wire transfer or Same Day ACH for urgent payments within the US and international wire transfer for cross-border payments.


If time is on your side, then a standard ACH is usually the better option. It works fine for payrolls, recurring bills, supplier payments, and routine invoices where waiting an extra day or two is not a problem.

## Frequently asked questions


### **Can an ACH transfer be reversed?**


Yes, within defined windows. ACH credits (money sent to a recipient) can be reversed within five banking days if they were sent in error. ACH debits (money pulled from a payer's account) can be returned by the receiving bank within two business days for administrative reasons, and consumers have up to 60 days to dispute an unauthorised ACH debit under EFTA protections. Business accounts have shorter windows governed by contract rather than EFTA.


### **Is a wire transfer safer than ACH?**


Both are highly secure, but they have different risk profiles. Wire transfers are final once settled, which protects the recipient against returns but offers the sender no recourse if they make an error or are defrauded. ACH transfers are returnable within defined windows, which gives senders some recourse but also means recipients should wait for transfers to fully clear before releasing goods or services of significant value. For verified, expected transactions, both are safe.


### **What is the ACH transfer limit per day?**


Limits vary by bank and account type. Most US retail bank accounts limit outgoing ACH transfers to $5,000 to $25,000 per day and $25,000 to $100,000 per month for standard accounts. Business accounts typically have higher limits. Premium or verified accounts may have limits up to $500,000 or higher. Contact your bank to confirm your specific account limits.


### **How do I send a wire transfer internationally?**


Contact your bank via online banking, mobile app, or branch visit. You will need: the recipient's full name, their bank account number or IBAN, the bank's SWIFT code or BIC, the bank's name and address, the amount and currency, and your reason for the transfer. Some banks require identity verification or additional documentation for international wires above certain amounts.

### **What happens if a wire transfer is sent to the wrong account?**


Contact your sending bank immediately. The bank will initiate a recall process via the SWIFT gpi tracking system or domestic wire network, requesting that the receiving bank return the funds. Recovery success depends on whether the recipient bank is cooperative and whether the funds have been further moved. This process can take weeks and is not guaranteed to succeed. Always verify account details with the recipient before initiating any wire transfer.
