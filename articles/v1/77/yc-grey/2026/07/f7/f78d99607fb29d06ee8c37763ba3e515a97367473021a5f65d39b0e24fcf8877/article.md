# How to register as self-employed in the UK and US

Starting your own business is an exciting step, but before you start earning, it's important to understand the legal and tax requirements that come with being self-employed. Proper registration ensures your business, whether freelance, consulting, product-based, or professional services, remains compliant, avoids penalties, and is built on a solid foundation. While the process is relatively straightforward, the rules differ significantly between the UK and the US.


To register as self-employed in the UK, sign up for Self Assessment on the HMRC website. You must register by 5 October following the tax year you started trading. In the US, no formal "self-employed registration" exists. You file taxes as a sole proprietor using Schedule C with your annual tax return. Most states require a business licence or sales tax permit.


This guide explains how self-employment registration works in both countries, the documents you'll need and the key deadlines to keep in mind before you start trading.


## **What does it mean to be self-employed?**


Being self-employed means you work for yourself rather than an employer. Instead of earning a salary through payroll, you generate income by providing services, selling products or running your own business. The terms below are often used interchangeably, but they have different meanings:


- **Self-employed:** A broad term for anyone who works for themselves instead of an employer.
- **Freelancer:** A self-employed person who usually works with multiple clients on short or long-term contracts.
- **Sole proprietor (US):** The default business structure for self-employed individuals, where the owner and business are legally the same entity.
- **Limited company (UK):** A separate legal entity from its owner, offering greater liability protection but with additional reporting and compliance requirements.

## **How to register as self-employed in the UK**


If you're starting a business, freelancing or earning income outside traditional employment, you'll need to register with HM Revenue & Customs (HMRC) as self-employed. The process is straightforward, but it's important to register on time to avoid penalties and ensure you're meeting your tax obligations.


1. Create a Government Gateway account on the HMRC website if you don't already have one.
2. Register for Self Assessment using your Government Gateway credentials. This tells HMRC that you'll be responsible for reporting and paying your own taxes.
3. Receive your Unique Taxpayer Reference (UTR) number. HMRC will send this to you after your registration is processed. You'll need it whenever you file tax returns or communicate with HMRC.
4. Choose your business structure. Most people start as sole traders, while others may prefer a limited company for liability protection and potential tax advantages.

## **How to register as self-employed in the US**


Starting as a self-employed professional in the US is straightforward once you understand the process.[The IRS receives approximately 27 to 30 million nonfarm sole proprietorship returns each year](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-prior/p1136--052025.pdf) , highlighting just how common self-employment has become. Here's how to get started:


1. **Choose a business structure:** Most freelancers begin as a sole proprietorship, while others may prefer an LLC for liability protection or an S Corporation for potential tax advantages.
2. **Apply for an Employer Identification Number (EIN):** Sole proprietors can often use their Social Security Number, but an EIN is recommended if you hire employees, open a business bank account or operate as an LLC.
3. **Register with your state if required:** Depending on your location and business activity, you may need a business licence, sales tax permit or local operating permit.
4. **File your taxes correctly:** Self-employed sole proprietors report business income and expenses using Schedule C, submitted alongside Form 1040 during the annual tax filing process. Keep accurate financial records throughout the year to simplify reporting and maximise eligible deductions.


## **What you'll need before registering as self-employed**


Preparing the right information before you register will make the process quicker and help you avoid delays. While the exact requirements differ between the UK and the US, most self-employed individuals should have the following details ready:


- **National Insurance Number (UK)** or **Social Security Number (US)** for tax identification.
- **Business name** , if you've chosen to trade under one instead of your personal name.
- **Business address** , which may be your home address or your registered business location.
- **Business start date** , showing when you began or plan to begin trading.
- **Business activity** , including a clear description of the products you sell or the services you provide.
- **Estimated annual income** , which may be required for tax registration, licensing or planning your tax obligations.
- **Contact details** , including your phone number and email address for official correspondence.
- **Business bank account details** , if you've already opened one to keep your personal and business finances separate.


Having this information ready before you begin can save time and make your registration process much smoother, whether you're registering in the UK or the US.


## **What to do after registering as self-employed**


Registering your business is only the first step. Once you're officially self-employed, it's important to build good financial habits from the beginning. Open a separate business bank account to keep your personal and business finances apart, making it easier to track cash flow, manage expenses and prepare accurate financial records throughout the year.


Next, create a system for recording every payment you receive and every business expense you incur. If you're based in the US, set aside money for quarterly estimated tax payments to avoid penalties. In the UK, regularly save a portion of your income so you're prepared for your annual Self Assessment tax bill and National Insurance contributions.

## **Common mistakes to avoid when registering as self-employed**


- **Missing registration deadlines:** Delaying your registration can lead to penalties, unnecessary stress and compliance issues. Make sure you understand the deadlines that apply in your country and complete your registration as soon as you start trading.
- **Mixing personal and business finances:** Using one bank account for everything makes it harder to track income, claim legitimate business expenses and prepare accurate tax returns. A separate business account keeps your finances organised from day one.
- **Failing to keep receipts and financial records:** Keep receipts, invoices and bank statements for every business transaction. Good record-keeping simplifies tax filing, supports expense claims and protects you if your tax authority requests additional information.
- **Choosing the wrong business structure:** Selecting a sole proprietorship, limited company or LLC without understanding the tax and legal implications could cost you money. Review your options carefully as your income and business grow.
- **Ignoring your tax obligations:** Don't wait until tax season to think about your finances. Set money aside regularly for taxes and understand whether you need to make estimated payments or file annual returns.
- **Not planning for international payments:** If you work with overseas clients, relying only on traditional bank transfers can increase costs. Consider payment solutions that make receiving foreign currency faster, more affordable and easier to manage.


## Frequently asked questions


### **When do I need to register as self-employed?**


The timing depends on where you live. In the UK, you must register for Self Assessment by 5 October following the end of the tax year in which you started trading. In the US, there is no formal self-employment registration, but you must meet federal, state and local tax and licensing requirements before or shortly after starting your business.


### **Can I be employed and self-employed at the same time?**


Yes. Many people have a full-time or part-time job while running a freelance business on the side. You'll continue paying tax through your employer, but you'll also need to declare your self-employed income separately and meet any additional tax obligations that apply to your freelance earnings.


### **Do I need a business name to register as self-employed?**


No. You can trade using your own legal name if you prefer. However, many freelancers choose a business name to create a stronger brand and appear more professional. If you do, make sure the name complies with your country's business registration rules and doesn't infringe existing trademarks.


### **What is a UTR number?**


A Unique Taxpayer Reference (UTR) is a 10-digit number issued by HMRC to individuals and businesses in the UK. It's used to identify you for tax purposes and is required when filing your Self Assessment tax return, communicating with HMRC or managing your online tax account.


### **What's the difference between a UTR and an EIN?**


A UTR is a UK tax reference issued by HMRC to identify taxpayers for Self Assessment. An Employer Identification Number (EIN) is issued by the IRS in the US and identifies businesses for federal tax purposes. They serve similar administrative functions but belong to different tax systems.
