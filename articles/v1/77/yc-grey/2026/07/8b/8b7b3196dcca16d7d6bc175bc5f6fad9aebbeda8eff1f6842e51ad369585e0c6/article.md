# Chase Virtual Card Number: How to Get One Today | Grey

If you shop online regularly, you have probably wondered what happens to your card number after entering it at a payment checkout and how safe it is. Even recently, Chick-fil-A and Origin Energy reported breaches that exposed customers’ contact details and partial card numbers. A virtual card number helps reduce that risk by replacing your real card details with a temporary one.


A Chase virtual card number is a temporary card number generated in the Chase mobile app for eligible Chase cards. It lets you shop online without exposing your real card number. To get one, open the Chase app, select your eligible card, tap "Account services" and then "Virtual card number." The virtual number works at most online merchants.


Whether you're shopping with a retailer for the first time, signing up for a free trial, or simply want an extra layer of security, this guide explains how Chase virtual card numbers work, which cards support them, how to generate one, and the best alternatives if your card isn't eligible.


## What is a Chase virtual card number?


A Chase virtual card number is a temporary card number linked to your eligible Chase card account. You use this temporary number when shopping online instead of your actual card number. The merchant processes the payment normally, but your real card number is never shared.


Since the merchants never receive your real card details, virtual card numbers add an extra layer of protection against data breaches and card fraud. If the merchant has a breach and the virtual number gets compromised, your physical card number remains hidden. So, your money and account are safe.


According to[Chase's official website](https://www.chase.com/) , the virtual card numbers feature is only available for eligible Chase cards through the Chase mobile app. The temporary number is linked to your real account for just that transaction, so it appears like a normal transaction on your bank statement


Eligible cards include:


- Chase Sapphire Preferred and Sapphire Reserve
- Chase Freedom Flex and Freedom Unlimited
- Chase Ink Business cards (selected products)
- Other selected Chase Visa and Mastercard cards


Open the Chase app to check your card type and confirm if the feature is available for your account and card.


### How to get a Chase virtual card


If your card is eligible, generating a Chase virtual card barely takes two minutes once you are in the app. Here is how to get a Chase virtual card from your Chase app:


1. Open the Chase mobile app and log in to your account.
2. Select the card you want to generate a virtual number for. Make sure it is an eligible card. You will see this option if it is supported.
3. Tap "Account services." This is usually accessible from the card's main screen or the menu.
4. Tap "Virtual card number." If your card is eligible, this option appears. If it isn't there, your card doesn't currently support the feature.
5. Review the virtual card details. Chase displays the temporary card number, expiry date, and CVV. These details replace your real card information at checkout.
6. Copy the details or use them directly. When checking out online, enter the virtual number, expiry, and CVV in place of your real card information.


The virtual number expires after some time or once you have used it. If you need a new one, simply generate another virtual card number in the app.


## Using a Chase virtual card for online shopping


Virtual card numbers are particularly useful in three situations.


### **Subscriptions on new platforms**


If you have ever forgotten to cancel a subscription and got charged, you should understand why a virtual card number is a good idea. When signing up for a subscription on a service you have not used before, using a virtual number means your real card is not stored by that service. So, if you decide to cancel, you do not have to update your card details everywhere.


### **One-off purchases from unfamiliar stores**


If you are buying from a smaller retailer or a site you have not used before, a virtual number limits your exposure if the site ever gets compromised.


### **Signing up for free trials**


Using a virtual card number is a hack when trying to sign up for free trials, and you don’t want to be charged automatically when the trial ends. A virtual card number expires either when you use it or after some time. This means the charge does not go through if you forget to cancel.


Once you have generated the number and you are at the checkout page, here’s what to enter:


- The virtual card number in the card number field
- The virtual expiry date in the expiry field
- The virtual CVV in the security code field.
- The billing address is the same as the one on your real Chase account.


Do not enter the actual card details printed on your physical Chase card!


### How to pay with a Chase virtual card


There isn't much difference between paying with your physical card and paying with a Chase virtual card. The steps are similar; you are just entering a virtual number instead of your real card number.


**Step 1:** At the checkout page, select "Credit/debit card" or "Pay by card" as the payment method.


**Step 2:** Enter the virtual card number, expiry date, and CVV from the Chase app. Again, do not enter the details on your physical card.


**Step 3:** Enter your billing address as registered with Chase.


**Step 4:** Complete the purchase.


### If your payment is declined


If your virtual card number doesn’t work on a platform, if might be because some merchants don’t accept tokenised or virtual card numbers. This is more common with:


- Merchants that verify the card number against a specific format
- Hotels or rental car companies that need to hold a card for incidentals
- Some subscription platforms that require a card to be stored long-term


If a virtual number is rejected, you can try again with your real card number for that specific merchant.


### Chase virtual card limitations


While Chase virtual card numbers can be very useful, they come with some limitations.


- **Only available for eligible cards:** Not all Chase cards support virtual card numbers. If the option does not appear in your app, your card does not have this feature yet.
- **They are designed for online purchases:** Virtual card numbers are for online purchases only. They cannot be used if you are buying from a physical store or using an ATM.
- **Some merchants do not accept them:** Hotels, car rental companies, and certain subscription services may require your physical card instead.
- **Foreign transaction fees still apply:** A virtual card doesn't remove the foreign transaction fees charged by your Chase card when shopping with overseas merchants.

These options work best if you already bank with one of these institutions and primarily shop in US dollars. They keep everything inside your existing credit relationship and usually require no extra account setup. The main limitation is that they are tied to a single currency (almost always USD) and are primarily designed for customers with US residency and a traditional credit card.

## Frequently asked questions


### **Which Chase cards have virtual card numbers?**


Chase Sapphire Preferred, Chase Sapphire Reserve, Chase Freedom Flex, Chase Freedom Unlimited, and selected Chase Ink Business cards currently support virtual card numbers. The easiest way to check is to open your card in the Chase mobile app and look for the "Virtual card number" option under Account services. If the option isn't there, your card isn't currently eligible.


### **Does the Chase virtual card number change?**


Yes. Chase generates a new virtual card number each time you request one. The number is temporary and typically expires after a defined period or number of uses. This is a security feature: an expiring number limits how useful it is to anyone who obtains it without your consent.


### **Can I use a Chase virtual card abroad?**


You can use a Chase virtual card number for purchases on foreign websites where you pay online. However, if the purchase is in a foreign currency, the same foreign transaction fees that apply to your physical Chase card also apply to the virtual number. Virtual card numbers do not remove foreign transaction fees.


### **Does a Chase virtual card work for subscriptions?**


It depends on how the subscription platform handles card validation. Some subscription services accept tokenised numbers and can charge them repeatedly. Others require a long-term card on file and may have issues with numbers that expire. For subscriptions, check whether the virtual number is locked to a single transaction or can be used for recurring charges.
