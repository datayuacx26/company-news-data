# Google Pay Virtual Card: How It Works and Where to Use It

Contactless payments have become part of everyday life in countries that support them. Whether you're buying groceries, booking flights, or shopping online, chances are you've used a digital wallet like Google Pay. But every time you enter your card details on a website, you expose your card number to another merchant. That's where Google Pay's virtual card feature comes in.


A Google Pay virtual card is a tokenised version of your physical card that Google generates for online purchases in Chrome, masking your real card number. It is currently available with select US bank-issued Visa and Mastercard cards. To use one, add your card to Google Pay, enable virtual card numbers in your card settings, and use the autofill option at checkout.


Since Google introduced this feature in 2022, it is now available with Visa, Mastercard, and American Express cards. In this guide, you'll learn how Google Pay virtual cards work, which banks support them, how to set one up, and when they're a better option than using your physical card online.


## What is a Google Pay virtual card?


Google Pay (via Google Wallet) handles virtual cards in two ways: you can add a pre-existing third-party virtual debit/credit card to your wallet, or Google Wallet automatically creates a secure virtual account number (token) when you link a physical card.


A Google Pay virtual card is a digital version of your debit or credit card generated by Google during online checkout. Instead of sharing your actual card number with a website, Google creates a unique virtual card number. The payment still comes from your real card, but the merchant never sees your actual card details.


According to[Google's official support pages](https://support.google.com/googlepay/answer/11234179?) , the feature works through Google Chrome's autofill functionality. When you reach a payment form on a supported website, Chrome detects that a virtual card is available and offers to automatically enter the tokenised number.


This works through a process called tokenisation. Your real card number is replaced by a different number that is mathematically linked to your account. Let’s say there is a data breach on the platform where you used your card, and the virtual number is stolen. The number cannot be used to access your bank account or to make transactions elsewhere.


## Which banks support Google Pay virtual cards?


While Google Wallet is available in most parts of the world, Google Pay virtual cards are mostly only available with some US card issuers. However, the list has been growing since the 2022 launch.


**Currently supported in the US:**


- Capital One (Visa and Mastercard cards)
- American Express (selected cards)
- Citi (selected Visa and Mastercard cards)
- Several other US bank-issued Visa and Mastercard products


Some international markets have access to elements of Google Wallet's tokenisation features, but the full virtual card number feature for Chrome autofill is not universally available outside the US.


The feature usually works with both Visa and Mastercard from supported issuers. Some American Express cards might also be supported, depending on the bank. Check your specific card in Google Wallet to confirm whether virtual card numbers are available for it.


To check if your bank supports Google Pay virtual cards:


1. Open the Google Wallet app.
2. Add your debit or credit card (if it hasn't already been added).
3. Look for an option for virtual cards when paying online, or in the card details.
4. Alternatively, contact your bank and ask: “Does my card support Google Pay virtual card numbers for online payments?”


If your card or bank is not yet supported, we will provide alternatives you can choose. Also, not all payment gateways accept Google Pay virtual card numbers. If this happens, you'll need to use your physical card details instead.


## How to use Google Pay virtual card


[Using the feature](https://support.google.com/wallet/answer/12058983?) requires Google Chrome on a desktop or Android device, and a supported card added to Google Wallet. Here is how to use a Google Pay virtual card:


### **Step 1: Add your card to Google Wallet**


Open Google Wallet at[wallet.google.com](http://wallet.google.com/) or through the Google Wallet app. Add your eligible card by entering the card details or scanning it. Google may send a verification code to your bank.


### **Step 2: Enable virtual card numbers**


Once your card is added, go to the card details in Google Wallet. Look for the option to enable virtual card numbers. Not all cards show this option. If you do not see it, your card may not be eligible yet.


### **Step 3: Open Chrome and go to a shopping website**


The feature works in Chrome on desktop and Android. It might not work in other browsers, such as Safari, Firefox, or Edge.


### **Step 4: Proceed to checkout.**


When you reach the payment form, Chrome will detect your saved Google Pay card and offer autofill. If a virtual card is available, Chrome will show you the option to use a virtual card number instead of your real number.


### **Step 5: Select the virtual card option**


Click or tap to use the virtual card. Chrome fills in the tokenised card number, expiry date, and security code automatically.


### **Step 6: Complete your purchase**


The merchant processes the payment using the virtual number. The charge appears on your real card statement as a normal transaction.


## How to find your Google Pay card number


Google doesn’t issue a permanent virtual card number, so you cannot just copy the numbers and use them for other transactions. Instead, the virtual card number is generated every time you need to use your card, which is part of what makes it safe.


To find your Google Pay card number, open your Google Wallet, go to the card, and look for the virtual card or device account number section. This shows you the tokenised card number associated with your account on that device or browser. This is not the same as your real card number printed on the physical card.


Google's system can generate merchant-specific or session-specific numbers. This means the number used at one retailer may differ from the number used at another, even on the same card. This limits the damage if one merchant's systems are compromised.


For many online purchases, the virtual number is passed directly through Chrome's autofill without being displayed to you. This is by design. The system handles the masking automatically, so you do not need to manually copy a virtual number for each purchase.


## Google Pay virtual card limitations


Google Pay virtual cards offer extra security for online shopping, but they do have a few limitations.


- **Works only in Google Chrome:** You can't use the virtual card feature in browsers like Safari, Firefox, or Microsoft Edge.
- **Limited device support:** It works in Chrome on desktop and Android devices. However, this feature is not available on iPhones or iPads due to iOS restrictions.
- **Not every bank is supported.** You can only use a Google Pay virtual card if your bank and card issuer support it.
- **Available mainly in the US.** Most users outside the United States won't have access to Google Pay virtual cards yet.
- **May not work on every website.** While rare, some online stores don't accept virtual card numbers. If this happens, you'll need to use your physical card details instead.

## Frequently asked questions


### **Does Google Pay generate a virtual card for every purchase?**


Google Pay uses tokenised card numbers to protect your real card details. Depending on your bank, the virtual number may be unique to a merchant or change over time, helping reduce the risk of fraud.


### **Which countries support Google Pay virtual cards?**


The feature is currently available mainly for eligible cards issued by participating US banks. Availability in other countries is still limited.


### **Can I use Google Pay virtual card on Safari or Firefox?**


No. The Google Pay virtual card autofill feature only works in Google Chrome. On iOS devices, Chrome does not support the feature even after installation, because Apple restricts browser-level payment APIs on the iPhone and iPad.


### **Is Google Pay virtual card safer than a regular card?**


Yes, in the specific sense that your real card number is not exposed to the merchant. If a merchant's payment system is breached, the tokenised number that was stolen cannot be used at other merchants. Your real card number remains safe. Standard card security still applies for all other types of fraud.
