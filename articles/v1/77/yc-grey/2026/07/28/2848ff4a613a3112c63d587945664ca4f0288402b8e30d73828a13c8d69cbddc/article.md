# How to find freelance clients on LinkedIn: Proven strategies

LinkedIn is one of the best places to find freelance clients because it's where many hiring decisions start. Instead of competing with hundreds of proposals on freelance marketplaces, you can connect directly with founders, marketing managers, product leaders, and business owners. When used well, it becomes a long-term client acquisition channel rather than just another job board.


To find freelance clients on LinkedIn, start by optimising your profile to read as a service provider rather than a job seeker. Post regular content showing your expertise. Search for decision-makers in your niche and send personalised connection requests with a specific reason. Convert connections into conversations with a soft call to action, not a pitch.


This article provides a guide to finding freelance clients on LinkedIn using proven tactics.


## Why LinkedIn is the best platform for freelance clients


LinkedIn can sometimes look like the place people come to brag about their achievements, which can be intimidating. However, there is more to the platform. You are unlikely oin to run into a recruiter for a Fortune 500 at a local store to pitch your services. LinkedIn provides freelancers the unique opportunity to connect directly with people making hiring decisions.


Here are some reasons LinkedIn remains one of the best places for freelancers to find new clients.


- **Decision-makers are on LinkedIn:** The platform has over a billion members, including founders, marketing directors, product managers, creative directors, and operations leaders who actively use it for professional activities. These are the people who hire freelancers.
- **The people there are already in work mode:** Unlike X (formerly known as Twitter), Instagram, or any other social media platform mostly designed for entertainment, LinkedIn users are in default work mode. They are thinking about business problems, competitor activity, and team capacity. So it is easier to catch their attention with well-timed, well-crafted content or a cold message.
- **It is less competitive than freelancing platforms:** On platforms like Upwork or Fiverr, every job attracts dozens or even hundreds of proposals. On LinkedIn, you're contacting potential clients directly instead of competing in crowded job listings.
- **No platform fee:** LinkedIn does not take a commission on projects that come through it. Every penny from the client goes directly to you.


## LinkedIn for freelancers: how to set up your profile


LinkedIn is very much about the optics. How you are perceived matters a lot in determining whether you will achieve any success. Your LinkedIn profile needs to look more like that of a service provider, not a desperate job applicant. Here is how to set up a LinkedIn profile as a freelancer.


### **Headline**


Your headline appears everywhere on LinkedIn, including in connection requests, search results, and comment sections. It is the first thing a potential client reads. So, it has to be top-notch in a way that tells your potential clients what you do and the results you deliver.


So, instead of: "Freelance UX Designer"


Try: "UX Designer for B2B SaaS Companies | Helping Reduce User Churn Through Better Onboarding"


### **About section**


Focus on your client's problem before talking about yourself. A simple structure that works is to lead with the problem your client may have, what you do to solve it, and evidence that it works. End with a clear call to action.


Here is a brief draft


“I help B2B SaaS companies improve user onboarding and increase product adoption through user research and UX design. If you're looking to improve how customers use your product, let's connect.”


### **Featured section**


Use this to display portfolio links, case studies, and testimonials. It is the next place a client looks after reading your headline and about section.


### **Services section**


Many users don’t know they can create a service page on their LinkedIn.[LinkedIn's Services feature](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a569534) allows you to list specific services that appear in search. Enable this and list the specific services you offer with brief descriptions.


### **Banner image**


Replace the default blue background with something that aligns with you and your work. This helps you look more professional. You can use a logo, a visual representation of your work, or a simple graphic that states what you do.


## A LinkedIn freelance strategy that works


Finding clients on LinkedIn comes down to four consistent activities.


**1. Optimise your profile:** Your profile is more likely to come up in searches and attract potential clients when it is optimised. Profile optimisation is pretty much everything we mentioned in the last section.


**2. Share useful content:** It is possible to get intimidated by some of the posts you find on LinkedIn. In the end, everyone is positioning for their next big thing, so you might as well start talking about your work, too. Sharing insightful content builds visibility among people who do not yet follow you and establishes credibility among those who do. And you have to do so consistently.


**3. Reach out to the right people:** Connect with decision-makers who fit your ideal client profile instead of waiting for clients to find you. Draft cold messages that briefly explain the problem they might have, how your services solve that problem, and the result to expect.


**4. Follow-up.** Most clients won't hire after one interaction. Staying in touch naturally over time often leads to opportunities. Don’t be too pushy. Spread follow-up over weeks and months to convert warm connections into clients.


## How to use LinkedIn to get clients


There is no shortcut to getting clients on LinkedIn. Any strategy must include consistency if it is going to work, and not just breezing in and out.


Here is a 30-minute routine you can apply five days per week to build your presence and generate conversations over three to six months.


1. **Engage with three posts from people in your target niche.** Comment with something specific and substantive (not "great post"). This makes you visible to the post author and their audience.
2. **Send two targeted connection requests** to decision-makers in companies that fit your ideal client profile. Personalise each request with a single specific reason for connecting.
3. **Follow up on one existing conversation.** If you have had an exchange with someone, send a short, natural follow-up.
4. **Post one piece of content per week:** Prioritise quality over frequency. One well-constructed post per week will make you more credible than five mediocre posts.
5. **Monthly profile audit.** Review your headline, featured section, and recent activity through the eyes of a potential client. Is everything still accurate? Does it still communicate clearly? Does it still align with my work goals? Tweak your profile whenever it is necessary.

## How to write LinkedIn outreach messages that get replies


Most LinkedIn outreach fails because it asks for too much too soon. The goal of a first message is not to sell your services, but to start a conversation. See these examples, you can adapt as fit:


### **Connection request (150 character limit)**


"Hi \[Name\], I enjoyed your recent post on \[topic\]. I work with \[type of business\] on \[service\]. I'd love to connect.”


### **First message after connection is accepted**


"Hi \[Name\], thanks for connecting. I noticed \[company\] has been \[specific relevant thing, e.g. expanding into a new market / shipping a new product feature / hiring for X role\]. Happy to share some thoughts on \[relevant area where you have expertise\] if it would be useful."


### **Follow up if no reply after one to two weeks**


"Hi \[Name\], I wanted to follow up on my previous message in case it got buried. I also found something that might be relevant: \[piece of content, article, or resource relevant to their situation\]."


What makes these templates work is the personalisation without necessarily making a pitch. Each one demonstrates that you paid attention to the recipient's specific situation rather than sending a mass message, and none of them asks for a meeting or a proposal in the first contact.

## What content to post on LinkedIn as a freelancer


The content that generates client interest is not inspirational or promotional. It is useful and specific.


### **Content types that work**


- **Case studies:** "A client's onboarding completion rate was 34%. Here is what we changed and what happened to it." Anonymise the client if needed.
- **Opinion posts:** Your take on a specific practice, tool, or trend in your niche. Be willing to have a point of view.
- **Client wins:** "Shipped a project last week. A few things I noticed that might be useful for others building similar products." Contextual, specific, and useful.
- **Process or methodology explainers:** "How I structure a UX audit for a product I have never seen before." Decision-makers want to understand how you think.
- **Observations and questions:** "Three companies I have spoken to recently are all struggling with the same onboarding problem. Is this something you see in your category?" Invites conversation.


### **Types of content to avoid**


- Motivational quotes not connected to your area of expertise
- Life updates not related to professional context
- Reposted articles without your own commentary
- Generic "looking for clients" announcements

## Frequently asked questions


### **Do I need LinkedIn Premium to find freelance clients?**


No. A free LinkedIn account is enough for most freelancers. Premium can provide extra features like InMail, but personalised outreach and useful content work well without it.


### **How many connection requests can I send per day on LinkedIn?**


A good target is 10–15 personalised connection requests daily. The quality of your connection requests is more important than sending large numbers of generic requests. While you can do more than 15, note that LinkedIn might restrict your account if you send too many connection requests.


### **What is the best LinkedIn headline for a freelancer?**


The best LinkedIn headline should include what you do, who you help, and the result you deliver. For example: "Content Strategist for B2B SaaS | Helping Teams Build Content That Generates Pipeline." Avoid vague titles like "Freelance Writer" or a headline that makes you look like a job seeker ("Open to opportunities").


### **How long does it take to get clients from LinkedIn?**


With consistent activity, most freelancers start seeing conversations with potential clients within four to eight weeks. The first client typically comes three to six months after starting, though this varies significantly by niche, outreach quality, and the target audience's activity on LinkedIn.


### **Should I post my freelance services on LinkedIn?**


You can, but "I am available for freelance projects" posts rarely generate clients directly. What generates clients is demonstrating your expertise through case studies, opinions, and useful content, and then letting potential clients come to you. If you want to signal availability, do it subtly by including it in your profile's Services section and mentioning it naturally in conversations rather than broadcasting it.


### **How do I receive payments from LinkedIn clients in another country?**


Provide the client with payment details in their own currency. A US client gets a US routing number and account number. A UK client gets a UK sort code and account number. An EU client gets an IBAN.
