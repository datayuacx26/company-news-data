# UK visa sponsorship jobs: 17 places to target

The UK immigration scene has changed a lot over the last couple of years, with stricter immigration requirements and a higher salary threshold for the Skilled Worker visa. However, the UK still offers the most realistic potential for Nigerians trying to move out of the country.


UK visa sponsorship jobs are roles offered by employers on the UK Home Office register of licensed sponsors. For Nigerians, the main route is the Skilled Worker visa, which requires a sponsoring employer, a job at or above the salary threshold (typically £41,700 since July 22, 2025), and English language proficiency. The top sources of sponsored roles are NHS Trusts, tech firms, universities, and care providers.


While it is now a bit more difficult, it is still very possible to get a job in the UK with visa sponsorship, especially if you know where to look. It is important to know which employers actually sponsor, which sectors have the most openings, and how to apply in a way that stands out. This guide covers all of that, starting with 17 specific places to target.


### UK job visa for Nigerians: how to qualify


Most Nigerians moving to the UK for work apply through the[Skilled Worker visa](https://www.gov.uk/skilled-worker-visa/your-job) route. To qualify, you must meet several eligibility requirements set by the UK Home Office before you can submit your visa application.


1. **Secure a job offer from a licensed sponsor:** You must have a confirmed job offer from a UK employer that is authorised to sponsor overseas workers. Only employers on the UK government's register of licensed sponsors can issue a Certificate of Sponsorship (CoS), which is required for your visa application. Before accepting an offer, verify that the employer appears on the official sponsor list.
2. **Your job must be eligible:** The position must be on the list of occupations that qualify for the Skilled Worker visa and be assigned an eligible Standard Occupational Classification (SOC) code. Many professional, technical, healthcare, engineering, education, and skilled trade roles qualify, while most low-skilled or temporary jobs do not.
3. **The job must meet the salary threshold:** The current minimum salary threshold for a Skilled Worker visa is £41,700 per year. Some occupations have a higher[‘going rate’](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/skilled-worker-visa-going-rates-for-eligible-occupations) . To qualify, you must be paid whichever is higher: the minimum threshold or the going rate for the job. Jobs listed on the[immigration salary list](http://www.gov.uk/skilled-worker-visa/when-you-can-be-paid-less) usually have a lower salary threshold of £33,400 per year (or lower for specific health and care roles). This might also be applicable for new entrants, Under-26 applicants, and some PhD holders or postdoctoral positions. Verify the current threshold before applying, as this figure has changed multiple times in recent years.
4. **English language proficiency.** You must demonstrate English ability through a recognised test (such as IELTS or PTE), a degree taught in English, or by being a national of a majority English-speaking country. Nigeria is not on the exemption list, so you will need a test result unless you have an English-language degree from an approved institution
5. **Maintenance funds.** You must show you have enough money to support yourself on arrival, typically at least £1,270 in your bank account for 28 consecutive days before you apply. Some employers cover this requirement in their sponsorship offer.

## UK work permit visa: what you need to know


The Skilled Worker visa is the main route for most people, but it is not the only one.


- **Skilled Worker visa:** Requires employer sponsorship, a qualifying role, and a salary at or above the threshold. Valid for up to five years, renewable. After five years of continuous legal residence, you can apply for indefinite leave to remain (ILR), which gives you permanent residence.
- **Health and Care Worker visa:** A subset of the Skilled Worker visa for doctors, nurses, allied health professionals, and adult social care workers. It has a lower salary threshold in some cases and a reduced visa application fee.
- **Global Talent visa:** For researchers, artists, and digital technology specialists who are recognised as leaders or emerging leaders in their field. It doesn’t require employer sponsorship. Requires an endorsement from a recognised endorsing body. Very selective.
- **Scale-up visa:** For workers joining fast-growing UK companies that meet specific criteria. Requires an initial sponsoring employer but gives more flexibility to change jobs after six months.

### How to apply for a UK-sponsored job from Nigeria


Once you secure a job offer from a licensed sponsor, the application process is relatively straightforward. Here's what to expect.


**Step 1: Check that the employer is licensed to sponsor visas.**


Before applying for any role, confirm that the employer appears on the[UK government's register of licensed sponsors](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/register-of-licensed-sponsors-workers) . Only organisations on this list can issue a Certificate of Sponsorship (CoS), which you need for your visa application.


**Step 2: Tailor your CV to UK standards.**


A UK CV is typically no more than two pages, written in reverse chronological order, and does not include a photograph, date of birth, marital status, or religious affiliation. Focus on measurable achievements rather than listing responsibilities.


**Step 3: Apply through the employer's careers portal.**


Most UK employers accept applications through their own recruitment websites. Create job alerts for your preferred roles and apply early, as sponsored positions often attract a large number of applicants.


**Step 4: Prepare for interviews.**


Many UK employers conduct two or more interview stages, often beginning with a virtual interview. Expect competency-based questions and prepare your responses using the STAR method (Situation, Task, Action, Result).


**Step 5: Receive your job offer and Certificate of Sponsorship.**


If you are successful, your employer will issue a formal offer and assign you a Certificate of Sponsorship (CoS). This electronic document contains a unique reference number required for your visa application.


**Step 6: Submit your Skilled Worker visa application.**


Apply online through UK Visas and Immigration (UKVI) using your CoS reference number. You'll also pay the visa application fee and the Immigration Health Surcharge (IHS), which gives you access to the UK's National Health Service during your stay. Visa fees vary depending on your occupation and the length of your sponsorship, so always confirm the latest amounts on[GOV.UK](http://gov.uk/) before applying.


**Step 7: Attend your biometric appointment.**


Book an appointment at a UK Visa Application Centre in Lagos or Abuja to provide your fingerprints, photograph, and supporting documents.


**Step 8: Wait for a decision.**


Most Skilled Worker visa applications made outside the UK are processed within about three weeks after your biometric appointment, although processing times can vary depending on your circumstances.

## Applying for a UK eVisa from Nigeria


The UK has moved to a digital immigration system known as the eVisa, which replaces physical immigration documents for most visa holders.


### **What is an eVisa?**


An eVisa is a digital record of your immigration permission held by the UK Home Office. Instead of relying on a physical Biometric Residence Permit (BRP), you access your immigration status online through your[UK Visas and Immigration (UKVI) account](https://www.gov.uk/government/organisations/uk-visas-and-immigration) .


### **How does it work?**


After your visa is approved, you'll create or sign in to your UKVI account to view your immigration status. You can generate a share code whenever an employer, landlord, or another organisation needs to verify your right to work or live in the UK.


If you're travelling to the UK for the first time, you may still receive a short-validity entry vignette in your passport (where applicable) to allow you to enter the country before relying on your digital immigration status.


**Common issues Nigerian applicants should avoid:**


- **Name mismatches:** Ensure your passport, Certificate of Sponsorship, educational certificates, and visa application all use exactly the same name.
- **Incomplete supporting documents:** Missing employment letters, qualification documents, or financial evidence can delay your application.
- **Incorrect financial evidence:** Where maintenance funds are required, provide official bank statements from a recognised financial institution covering the required period.
- **Late biometric appointments:** Visa application centres in Lagos and Abuja can become fully booked, particularly during peak travel seasons, so schedule your appointment as soon as possible.

## Frequently asked questions


### **What is the minimum salary for a UK Skilled Worker visa?**


The general salary threshold for a Skilled Worker visa is £41,700 per year as of 2026. Jobs listed on the[immigration salary list](http://www.gov.uk/skilled-worker-visa/when-you-can-be-paid-less) usually have a lower salary threshold of £33,400 per year (or lower for specific health and care roles). This might also be applicable for new entrants, Under-26 applicants, and some PhD holders or postdoctoral positions. Verify the current threshold before applying, as this figure has changed multiple times in recent years.


### **Which UK employers sponsor Nigerians most often?**


The NHS is the UK's largest sponsor of overseas workers, recruiting thousands of doctors, nurses, pharmacists, physiotherapists, and other healthcare professionals each year. Large care providers such as HC-One, Barchester Healthcare, and Care UK also regularly sponsor international recruits. Outside healthcare, employers including Amazon, Google, Microsoft, TCS, Infosys, Deloitte, PwC, EY, KPMG, Barclays, and HSBC frequently sponsor skilled professionals in technology, finance, consulting, and engineering.


### **Can I bring my family on a UK Skilled Worker visa?**


In most cases, yes. Your partner and dependent children under 18 can apply to join you in the UK as dependants. Each dependent pays a separate visa application fee and the Immigration Health Surcharge. Your salary must be above a minimum threshold to sponsor dependents. The dependent rules and salary thresholds have been tightened in recent years, so confirm the current requirements at[gov.uk](http://gov.uk/) .


### **How long does a UK sponsorship visa take to process?**


Most Skilled Worker visa applications submitted from outside the UK are processed within **three weeks** after you attend your biometric appointment. Priority and Super Priority services may be available for an additional fee, depending on your location and the type of application. Delays can occur if further checks or additional documents are required.


### **Do I need IELTS for a UK Skilled Worker visa?**


Most Nigerian applicants need to demonstrate English language proficiency. IELTS for UKVI is the most widely used test. You typically need a minimum score of B1 (CEFR level) or above, though the exact requirement depends on the role. Graduates with degrees taught in English from UK universities or other approved institutions may be exempt. A full list of exemptions is on the UK Visas and Immigration website.
