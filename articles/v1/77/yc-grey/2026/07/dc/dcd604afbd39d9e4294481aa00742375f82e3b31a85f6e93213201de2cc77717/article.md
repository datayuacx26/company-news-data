# EMT payment in Canada: What it is and how to send one

If you’ve ever needed to send money to a friend, pay your rent, or split a bill in Canada, you’ve probably come across something called an EMT. An EMT (email money transfer) payment in Canada is the older name for what is now branded as Interac e-Transfer. It lets you send money from your bank account to another person's email address or mobile number within minutes. Most Canadian banks charge $1 to $1.50 per EMT, though many premium accounts include free transfers.


It’s fast, simple, and doesn’t require sharing your bank details. This guide walks you through email money transfer (EMT), what it means, how to send one, what it costs, and what to do when you need to send money beyond Canada's borders.


## What is an EMT payment?


EMT stands for email money transfer. It is an electronic money transfer option for sending money from one Canadian bank account to another using just an email address or phone number. No account numbers are shared between sender and recipient.


When you send an EMT, your bank withdraws the funds and notifies the recipient. The recipient logs in to their banking app, answers a security question, and accepts the transfer. The money moves through the[Interac network](https://www.interac.ca/) .


The name "EMT" comes from the service's early days. Interac eventually rebranded it as Interac e-Transfer to create consistent naming across all Canadian banks. But many Canadians still use the older term, and it shows up on older rental agreements, freelance invoices, and some bank statements. Both names refer to exactly the same service.


## MT vs Interac e-Transfer


There is no difference between an EMT and an Interac e-Transfer. They are the same product.


The rebrand happened because different banks were using slightly different names for the same service. Some called it EMT, others called it e-Transfer. Interac standardised the name to Interac e-Transfer across all institutions.


You might still see "EMT" on:


- Older rental agreements listing payment instructions
- Invoices from freelancers or small businesses using old templates
- Bank statements from institutions that have yet to adopt the new terminology


Whenever you see EMT on a Canadian document, treat it as an Interac e-Transfer and follow the same sending process.


## How to send an EMT in Canada


The process is almost identical across all Canadian banks.


1. **Access your account:** Log in to your online banking or mobile banking app.
2. **Find the transfer section:** Look for "Interac e-Transfer," "Send Money," or, in older interfaces, "EMT."
3. **Add your recipient:** Enter their email address or mobile number. If you have paid them before, they may already appear in your saved contacts.
4. Enter the amount you want to send.
5. **Create a security question and answer:** This is how the receiver verifies the transfer. Choose something they already know, or message them the answer separately. Do not use anything easily guessable.
6. **Add a description:** This is usually optional, but it is good to include something like "April rent" or "Invoice 042" to help the recipient understand what the payment is for and for your records.
7. **Review and confirm:** Check all details before confirming. Once you have initiated the transaction, it is difficult to cancel or reverse it.
8. **The recipient accepts the transfer:** They get an email or text with a link, log in to their bank, and answer the security question. The funds then appear in their account, usually within minutes.


Many Canadians enable “autodeposit” on their account. This skips the security question and deposits the transfer automatically. If you send money to someone with an autodeposit, it reaches them almost immediately, without them needing to take any further action to receive it.

## EMT payment limits


Interac does not set a transfer limit. Every bank sets its limits on how much you can send. This limit depends on your account type and how long you have been a customer.


**Typical limits for personal accounts across major banks:**


- Per transaction: $3,000
- Daily: $3,000 to $10,000
- Weekly: $10,000 to $40,000
- Monthly: $20,000 to $40,000


Business accounts generally have significantly higher limits (sometimes $10,000+ per transfer and much higher daily/weekly caps).


These limits are rolling. This means they are calculated over the previous 24 hours, 7 days, or 30 days. They do not reset at midnight. If you sent a transaction that triggers your limit around 2 pm. The limit is not reset by 12 midnight the next day. It has to be after 2 pm the next day. If you initiate a transaction that triggers your weekly transfer limit on Thursday, it does not reset by Sunday midnight. You have to wait till the next Thursday


To increase your transfer limit, contact your bank directly. Most banks can raise limits for verified customers with a good account history and a valid reason. You may need to show ID and explain the purpose of larger transfers.


Some banks offer Interac e-Transfer Large for transfers between $10,000 and $25,000 per transaction, which requires separate registration.

## Frequently asked questions


### **Is an EMT the same as an Interac e-Transfer?**


Yes, completely. EMT is the older name for the service now called Interac e-Transfer. The process, fees, and network are identical. If someone asks you to pay by EMT, send an Interac e-Transfer to their email address or phone number through your bank's app.


### **How long does an EMT take to arrive?**


For recipients with Autodeposit, usually within minutes. For recipients without Autodeposit, it is pending until they log in and accept it, which can take anywhere from minutes to several days.


### **Can I cancel an EMT after sending it?**


If the recipient has not accepted it yet, you can cancel through your banking app by finding it in pending transfers. If they have already accepted it, or if Autodeposit has triggered, cancellation is not possible. You would need to ask the recipient to return the funds.


### **Can I send an EMT to the US?**


No. Interac e-Transfer is a Canadian domestic service only. For transfers to the US or any other country, you need an international transfer method such as a wire transfer or a service like Grey that shows the full cost upfront.


### **What happens if the recipient does not accept an EMT?**


If the recipient does not accept within 30 days (some banks have a shorter window), the transfer is automatically returned to your account. The original transfer fee is not refunded.
