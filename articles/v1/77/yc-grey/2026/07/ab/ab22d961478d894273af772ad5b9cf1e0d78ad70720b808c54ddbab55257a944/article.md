# Interac e-transfer limit: How much Canadians can send per day

Need to send a large payment through Interac e-Transfer? Before you hit "Send", it's worth checking your bank's transfer limits. While Interac is one of the fastest and easiest ways to move money between Canadian bank accounts, every financial institution sets its own daily, weekly, and monthly caps.


If you're sending a large amount, you'll quickly run into one question: what's the Interac e-Transfer limit?


Interac e-Transfer limits in Canada vary by bank but typically allow $3,000 per transfer, $10,000 per day, and $30,000 per week for personal accounts. RBC, TD, Scotiabank, and BMO each set their own limits within these ranges. Business accounts have higher limits and may use Interac Bulk Disbursement for larger transfers.


In this guide, we'll explain how Interac e-Transfer limits work, compare the limits at Canada's biggest banks, and look at your options when you need to send more than Interac allows.


## What is an Interac e-Transfer?


An Interac e-Transfer is one of Canada's most popular ways to send money between bank accounts. Instead of using account numbers, you can send money using the recipient's email address or Canadian mobile phone number.


Transfers are processed through the Interac network and supported by most major Canadian banks and credit unions. Once the recipient accepts the transfer or has AutoDeposit enabled, the funds are deposited directly into their account.


Most transfers arrive within minutes, although processing times can vary depending on the sender's bank, the recipient's financial institution, and security checks.


Interac e-Transfer is designed for domestic Canadian transfers, making it ideal for paying rent, splitting bills, sending money to family, or paying freelancers within the country.


You can also find more information about how the service works on the official Interac website.


## Interac e-Transfer maximum amount


If you're wondering about the Interac e-Transfer limit, there isn't a universal limit across Canada.


Interac provides the payment network, but your bank decides how much you can send. That means two people using Interac can have different limits depending on where they bank and the type of account they hold.


For most personal banking customers, you'll typically see:


Limit Typical maximum


Per transfer CAD 3,000


Per day Up to CAD 10,000


Per week Up to CAD 30,000


Per month Up to CAD 125,000


These limits exist for several reasons.


### Fraud prevention


If someone gains unauthorised access to your account, transfer limits help reduce the amount of money that can be moved before suspicious activity is detected.


### Customer protection


Banks monitor Interac transfers for unusual behaviour. Daily and monthly caps provide an extra layer of protection against scams and account compromise.


### Regulatory compliance


Canadian financial institutions must comply with anti-money laundering (AML) and fraud prevention regulations. Transfer limits are one of several controls banks use to manage financial risk.


### Different accounts, different limits


Not every customer has the same limits.


Business accounts often have higher sending limits than personal accounts, and some banks offer premium customers larger transfer allowances. If you regularly send high-value payments, it's worth checking whether your account qualifies for increased limits.

Although these numbers are useful as a general guide, your personal limit may be different.


For example, your bank might approve higher limits if you've been a customer for several years, maintain larger account balances, or use a premium banking package. On the other hand, newer customers or those with basic accounts may have lower limits while their banking profile is established.


It's also important to remember that these are sending limits, not account balance limits. Having enough money in your account doesn't automatically mean you'll be able to transfer the full amount if you've already reached your daily or weekly cap.


Finally, while most banks share similar per-transfer limits, the weekly and monthly limits can make a noticeable difference if you're sending several payments over a short period. If you regularly pay contractors, suppliers, or family members, checking all three limits, not just the daily cap, can help you avoid unexpected delays.

## How to increase your Interac transfer limit


If you've reached your Interac e-Transfer limit, you may be wondering whether you can increase it. The answer is: sometimes.


Because each financial institution sets its own limits, the process varies from bank to bank. Some banks allow eligible customers to request higher limits, while others review increases on a case-by-case basis.


Here's how to find out if you can send more.


### 1. Check your current limits


Before requesting an increase, log in to your online or mobile banking and check your current Interac e-Transfer limits. Most banks display your per-transfer, daily, weekly, and monthly sending limits, so you can quickly see which limit you've reached and decide on the best next step.


### 2. Contact your bank


If your limits aren't high enough, get in touch with your bank via online chat, customer support, or a local branch.


Some financial institutions allow you to request a temporary increase for a one-off payment, while others may offer a permanent increase if you qualify.


### 3. Verify your identity


For security reasons, your bank may ask you to verify your identity before increasing your limits.


Depending on the institution, they may also review:


- Your account type
- How long you've been a customer
- Your account balance and activity
- Your transaction history
- The reason for the larger transfer


### 4. Consider upgrading your account


Some banks offer higher Interac e-Transfer limits on premium or business accounts.


If you regularly send large payments, whether for work or personal reasons, upgrading your account could give you access to higher limits without needing to request increases each time.


***Tip:*** *If you frequently hit your Interac limit, it may be worth asking your bank whether there are alternative payment methods that better suit larger transfers.*

If you're making a large purchase, paying international suppliers, sending money abroad, or moving a significant amount between accounts, you'll probably need another option.

### Bank wire transfers


Wire transfers are among the most common options for sending large amounts.


Unlike Interac, they're available for both domestic and international transfers, and they typically support much higher payment limits. They're often the preferred choice for large purchases, business payments, paying overseas suppliers, or other high-value transfers.


The trade-off is that wire transfers can be more expensive than other payment methods, and traditional banks often add exchange rate markups to international transfers.

### Split the payment across multiple days


If your payment isn't urgent, you may be able to send it in several smaller transfers.


For example, if your daily limit is CAD 10,000 and you need to send CAD 25,000, you could spread the payment across three days.


Just remember that your **weekly limit** may also affect how quickly you can complete the transfer.


### Business payment solutions


Businesses that regularly send payments to employees, contractors, or customers often use specialised payment services instead of standard Interac e-Transfers.


Depending on your bank, these may include:


- Interac Bulk Disbursement
- commercial wire transfers
- payroll platforms
- business banking payment services


These options are designed for higher payment volumes and larger transaction amounts than standard personal banking.

## Frequently asked questions


### Can I send CAD 10,000 in one Interac e-Transfer?


Usually not. Most Canadian banks limit individual Interac e-Transfers to CAD 3,000 per transaction. Even if your daily sending limit is CAD 10,000, you'll typically need to split the payment into multiple transfers.


### Are there Interac e-Transfer fees?


It depends on your bank and account. Many Canadian chequing accounts include Interac e-Transfers at no extra cost, while others charge a small fee for each transfer.


### How long does an Interac e-Transfer take?


Most Interac e-Transfers are completed within a few minutes. However, delays can happen if additional security checks are required or if the recipient needs to manually accept the transfer.


### Can I cancel an Interac e-Transfer?


Yes, but only if the recipient hasn't accepted the transfer or received it through Autodeposit. Once the money has been deposited into the recipient's account, the transfer generally can't be cancelled.


### Can I send an Interac e-Transfer internationally?


No. Interac e-Transfer only works between participating Canadian financial institutions. If you need to send money outside Canada, you'll need an international transfer service.
