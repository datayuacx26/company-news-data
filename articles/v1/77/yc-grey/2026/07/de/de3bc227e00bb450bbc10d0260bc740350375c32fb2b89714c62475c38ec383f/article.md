# 11 reasons your KYC verification is failing (and how to fix them)

Seeing "KYC verification failed" doesn't necessarily mean your account has been rejected. More often, it means the verification system couldn't confirm your identity using the information or documents you submitted.


The challenge is that providers don't always explain exactly what went wrong. As a result, many people keep uploading the same documents without fixing the underlying issue.


Most KYC verification failures come from a small set of fixable mistakes, such as a blurry or expired ID, a name mismatch between your document and profile, poor lighting, an unsupported document type, or incorrect personal information. Once you've corrected the issue, you can usually pass verification on your next attempt.


In this guide, we'll explain why your KYC verification is failing, how to resolve the most common issues, and what to do if your verification still doesn't go through.

## What does it mean when KYC verification fails?


Know Your Customer (KYC) is the process financial companies use to confirm that you are who you say you are before giving you access to their services.


To complete KYC, you'll usually be asked to provide information such as your full name, date of birth and address, along with a government-issued identity document. Many providers also require a selfie or short video to confirm that the person opening the account matches the identification provided.


If your KYC verification failed, it doesn't usually mean you've been banned or that there's something wrong with your account. More often, it means the verification system couldn't confidently confirm your identity using the information or documents you submitted.


Financial companies are required to carry out these checks to comply with anti-money laundering (AML) and financial crime regulations, helping protect both customers and the wider financial system.

## 11 reasons your KYC verification keeps failing


Most KYC mistakes fall into a relatively small number of categories. Identifying the specific issue can save you time and help you avoid multiple failed attempts.


### 1. Your ID photo is blurry


One of the most common causes of KYC failure is submitting an image that's difficult to read.


If important details such as your name, date of birth or document number aren't clearly visible, the verification system may reject your submission automatically.


**How to fix it:** Retake the photo in a well-lit area, keep your camera steady, and make sure the entire document is visible and in focus before uploading it.


### 2. Your identity document has expired


Many providers only accept identity documents that are currently valid.


Even if your passport or driving licence expired recently, it may no longer meet the verification requirements.


**How to fix it:** Check the expiry date before uploading your document and use a current, valid form of identification.


### 3. Your name doesn't match your document


Your profile information needs to match your identity document exactly.


Even small differences, such as using a nickname, leaving out a middle name that's included on your ID, or making a spelling mistake, can cause your verification to fail.


This is especially common if you've recently changed your name after marriage or another legal change but haven't updated all your documents.


**How to fix it:** Double-check that the name on your account matches your identity document character for character before submitting your verification.


### 4. The lighting is too poor


Even if your photo looks clear to you, shadows and low lighting can make it difficult for automated verification systems to read your document accurately.


Dark images can also hide important security features that help confirm the document is genuine.


**How to fix it:** Take the photo in bright, natural light whenever possible. Avoid dim rooms and make sure your entire document is evenly illuminated.


### 5. Glare is covering part of the document


This is another issue that catches many people out.


Bright reflections from overhead lights, camera flashes or sunlight can obscure text, photographs or security features, preventing the verification system from reading the document correctly.


**How to fix it:** Turn off your camera flash, adjust the angle slightly and retake the photo until there's no glare covering any part of the document.


### 6. You're using an unsupported document


Not every identity document is accepted by every financial provider.


For example, one platform may accept national identity cards, while another only accepts passports or driving licences. Uploading the wrong document can result in an immediate rejection, even if it's perfectly valid.


**How to fix it:** Check which identity documents your provider accepts before uploading your verification. If several options are available, choose the one that's easiest to photograph clearly.


### 7. Your proof of address doesn't match


Some financial providers ask for proof of address in addition to your identity document. If the address on your utility bill, bank statement or other supporting document doesn't match the one you entered when creating your account, your verification may be rejected.


This can also happen if you've recently moved house but haven't updated your records.


**How to fix it:** Make sure the address on your application matches the address shown on your supporting document. If you've moved recently, use an accepted document that reflects your current address.


### 8. You don't meet the minimum age requirement


Most regulated financial services require customers to be at least 18 years old, although the minimum age varies depending on the country and product.


If the date of birth on your identity document shows that you don't meet the eligibility requirements, your KYC check won't be approved.


**How to fix it:** Check the age requirements before applying. If you aren't eligible yet, you'll need to wait until you meet the minimum age.


### 9. You've created more than one account


Many financial providers only allow one account per customer.


Creating multiple accounts with the same personal information, email address, phone number or identity document can trigger security checks and prevent your verification from being completed.


Sometimes this happens unintentionally because people forget they signed up previously.


**How to fix it:** Try signing in to your existing account instead of creating a new one. If you can't access it, contact customer support before submitting another application.


### 10. Your internet connection interrupted the verification


Identity verification relies on images and personal information being uploaded correctly.


If your internet connection drops while documents or selfies are uploading, the files may be incomplete or corrupted, even if the upload appears to finish successfully.


**How to fix it:** Use a stable Wi-Fi or mobile data connection and wait until every document has uploaded completely before closing the app or browser.


### 11. Your face doesn't match your identity document


Many providers use facial recognition technology to confirm that the person opening the account is the same person shown on the identity document.


Poor lighting, low image quality, wearing sunglasses or hats, or significant differences between your appearance and your ID photo can make it difficult for the system to verify your identity.


**How to fix it:** Take your selfie in a well-lit area, remove anything covering your face, look directly at the camera and follow the instructions provided during the verification process.


## How to fix a failed KYC check step by step


If your verification has been rejected, it can be tempting to upload the same documents again and hope for a different result. In most cases, you'll have a better chance of success by identifying the cause of the rejection first and correcting it before resubmitting your application.


### 1. Read the error message carefully


Many providers explain why your verification failed, even if the message is brief. It might mention that your document couldn't be read, your face verification didn't match, or that some of your information was incorrect. Understanding the reason for the rejection will help you avoid making the same mistake again.


### 2. Review the information you submitted


Compare the details on your application with your identity document. Check that your full name, date of birth and address match exactly, and confirm that you're using a valid, unexpired document. Even a small typo or a missing middle name can prevent your verification from being approved.


### 3. Retake your photos if needed


If the issue relates to image quality, take new photos rather than uploading the same ones again. Use good lighting, place your ID on a flat surface, avoid reflections and make sure all four corners of the document are visible. If your provider requires a selfie, remove anything covering your face and look directly at the camera.


### 4. Submit your verification again


Once you've corrected the problem, upload your documents again and wait for the verification process to complete. Avoid submitting multiple applications in quick succession unless you're instructed to do so, as repeated attempts can sometimes trigger additional security checks.


### 5. Contact support if the problem continues


If you've reviewed your information, uploaded clear documents and your KYC verification still fails, contact customer support. Include any error messages you've received and explain the steps you've already taken. In some cases, the verification team can manually review your application or request additional documents to complete the process.


## Why legitimate customers sometimes fail KYC


Receiving a failed KYC result doesn't mean you've done anything wrong.


Modern verification systems rely heavily on automated document analysis and facial recognition. While these technologies are highly effective, they're not perfect.


A legitimate customer can still fail verification because a document couldn't be read clearly, a selfie didn't meet the required quality, or the information provided couldn't be matched with enough confidence.


Temporary technical issues can also affect the verification process. High demand, interrupted uploads or image processing errors may result in a failed check, even when all the information is correct.


That's why it's important not to panic if your first attempt doesn't succeed. In most cases, reviewing the reason for the rejection, correcting the issue and submitting a new verification is enough to complete the process successfully.


## How long does KYC verification take?


The time it takes to complete KYC verification depends on the financial provider, the documents you've submitted, and whether your application requires additional review.


In many cases, automated verification is completed within a few minutes. If your documents are clear and all your information matches, you may be verified almost immediately.


Some applications, however, take longer. A manual review may be required if the verification system can't confidently read your documents, your information needs to be checked more closely, or additional documents are requested.


Delays are also more common during periods of high application volumes or if there's an issue with the images you've uploaded.


If your verification has been pending for longer than the timeframe communicated by your provider, it's worth contacting customer support to check whether any further action is needed from you.

## Frequently asked questions


### What happens if I don't complete KYC verification?


This depends on the provider, but you'll usually have limited access to your account until verification is complete. Some services won't allow you to send or receive money, while others may restrict certain features until your identity has been confirmed.


### Why do legitimate customers fail KYC?


Most failed KYC checks are caused by document quality issues, incorrect information or automated verification systems being unable to confirm a person's identity with enough confidence. It doesn't necessarily mean there's anything wrong with your application.


### Can I use my account without completing KYC?


Many regulated financial services require KYC before customers can access key features. Depending on the provider, you may be able to create an account, but you'll often need to complete verification before you can use services such as transfers, withdrawals or foreign currency accounts.


### Is my KYC information deleted after verification?


Financial providers are generally required to retain customer verification records for a period defined by the regulations in the countries where they operate. The exact retention period varies depending on local legal requirements and the provider's policies.


### How many times can I retry KYC verification?


The number of attempts varies between providers. Some allow several verification attempts before asking you to contact support, while others may temporarily pause further submissions after repeated failures. If you're unsure, it's best to review the provider's guidance or contact their support team before trying again.


### Can I complete KYC using a digital copy of my ID?


Most providers require you to upload clear photographs or scans of an original, government-issued identity document. Whether digital identity documents are accepted depends on the provider and the country where you're verifying your account.
