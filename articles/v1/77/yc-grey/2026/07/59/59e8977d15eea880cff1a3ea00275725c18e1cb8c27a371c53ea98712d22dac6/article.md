## What to look for in a USD account as a UK resident


Before we delve into the best options for a USD account as a UK resident, it is important to know what each account offers and if those provisions meet your expectations.


First, check whether it provides a real US account number and routing number. This allows clients to send payments via the domestic route (ACH), which is cheaper and faster than international wires. Opening a dollar account without ACH details means you have to receive payments via international wire instead. This comes with a sending fee of around $25-$45 per transfer on their end. The money moves through one or more banks and is converted at marked-up rates before reaching your account. This means you might be losing $30 to $45 on a $1,000 payment.


Other things to check include:


- **USD debit card:** Can you spend directly in dollars without triggering a conversion?
- **Conversion fees:** What does it cost to move between USD and GBP?
- **Monthly fees:** Does the account require a monthly fee to maintain?
- **Transfer limits:** Are there caps on how much you can receive or send?
- **Setup speed:** Can you open it today, or does it take weeks?


Understanding what a[foreign currency account](https://grey.co/blog/getting-your-foreign-currency-account) actually provides will help you pick the right option.

## Frequently asked questions


### **Can I get a real US bank account as a UK resident?**


Not through a traditional US bank without a US address, Social Security Number, or physical presence in the US. However, fintech platforms like Grey and Wise issue real US routing numbers and account numbers to UK residents. These function identically to a US bank account for receiving ACH payments and are accepted by US employers, clients, and platforms.


### **Which is better, Wise or Grey, for a USD account?**


Both are strong options. Wise applies the mid-market rate with a disclosed conversion fee of 0.4% to 0.8%, making it slightly cheaper on smaller amounts. Grey charges a 0.8% deposit fee (capped at $10) and 1% conversion (capped at $6), making it more predictable and cheaper on larger conversions. If you also need EUR and GBP accounts alongside USD, Grey manages all three in one place.


### **Does Revolut give UK residents a real USD account number?**


Revolut does provide USD balance holding and a USD IBAN on some plans, but it does not always provide a full US ACH routing number and account number in the same way Grey and Wise do. This means Revolut is less suited for receiving ACH payments directly from US clients or payroll platforms.


### **Can I receive ACH payments into a UK account?**


Yes, if your account includes a real US ABA routing number and a US account number. Grey and Wise both provide these details for UK users. Once you have them, US clients can pay via ACH as a standard domestic US transfer with no international wire fees involved.


### **What is the cheapest USD account for UK residents?**


For receiving USD income, Grey and Wise are the cheapest options. Both avoid the high monthly fees and poor conversion rates that traditional bank foreign currency accounts typically charge. The cheapest for your specific situation depends on your volume: Wise tends to be cheaper for smaller amounts, and Grey's conversion cap makes it more cost-effective for larger regular transfers.
