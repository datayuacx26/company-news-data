# Expat Guide to Costa Rica: Cost, Banking, Visas | Grey

Costa Rica has become one of the world's most sought-after destinations for expats, attracting retirees, remote workers, entrepreneurs, and families looking for a better quality of life. From its lush rainforests and Pacific beaches to its relaxed lifestyle and strong focus on wellbeing, the country offers far more than scenic beauty. It also combines affordability with modern infrastructure, making it an appealing place to live, work, or retire abroad.


One of the biggest reasons people choose Costa Rica is its relatively low cost of living, established residency programmes, and stable banking system. Depending on your lifestyle and location, a single expat can live comfortably on $1,500 to $2,500 per month. This way, you can enjoy a high standard of living without the costs associated with many other popular expat destinations.


Before making the move, it's important to understand your visa options, expected living expenses, and how to manage your finances. The country's two main residency routes are the Pensionado (retiree) Visa and the Rentista (income-based) Visa, each designed for different applicants. This guide covers everything you need to know to plan your move to Costa Rica with confidence.

## **Why is Costa Rica a top destination for expats?**


Costa Rica continues to attract expats from around the world thanks to its warm tropical climate, political stability, and consistently high quality of life. Unlike many countries in the region, it has enjoyed decades of democratic governance without a standing military, contributing to its reputation as one of Latin America's safest and most stable places to live. Expats also benefit from an excellent healthcare system, with access to both public and private medical services at costs that are often significantly lower than those in North America and Europe. When you combin this with a slower pace of life and stunning natural scenery, Costa Rica offers an appealing balance of comfort and affordability.


Another major draw is its well-established expat communities, which make settling in much easier. Popular areas such as Tamarindo, Atenas, and Escazú offer a mix of modern amenities, international schools, English-speaking services, and active social networks. Whether you're retiring, working remotely, or starting a new chapter abroad, these communities provide the support and familiarity many newcomers value while still allowing them to experience Costa Rica's unique culture and lifestyle.

## **What does it cost to live in Costa Rica?**


Costa Rica offers a lower cost of living than many countries in North America and Western Europe, although your monthly expenses will vary depending on where you choose to live and your lifestyle. A single expat can typically live comfortably on $1,500 to $2,500 per month, while couples often spend between $2,000 and $3,500. The biggest expenses are usually rent, followed by food, healthcare, transport, and utilities. Living outside the main tourist hotspots can significantly reduce your monthly costs without compromising your quality of life.


Major cities such as San José generally have higher rental prices, while coastal destinations command premium rates due to their popularity with tourists and retirees. The Central Valley, including towns like Atenas, is known for offering one of the best balances between affordability, climate, and access to amenities. Meanwhile, Liberia in the Guanacaste Province has become an increasingly popular option for expats seeking a lower cost of living with easy access to beaches and an international airport.

## **How to move to Costa Rica as an expat**


Relocating to Costa Rica is much easier when you have a clear plan. While the process varies depending on your nationality and chosen residency route, most expats can complete their move within **three to 12 months** , depending on visa processing times and document preparation. Following these steps will help you settle into your new life with fewer surprises.


1. **Research your visa options:** Determine whether the Pensionado, Rentista, or another residency programme best matches your circumstances.
2. **Set your budget and choose where to live:** Compare locations such as San José, Escazú, the Central Valley, or coastal towns based on your lifestyle and monthly budget.
3. **Secure accommodation:** Rent a property before committing to a home purchase, giving yourself time to explore different communities.
4. **Open a local bank account:** This makes it easier to pay bills, receive income, and manage everyday expenses once you're settled.
5. **Apply for residency and register locally:** Gather your required documents, submit your residency application, enrol in Costa Rica's public healthcare system if applicable, and complete any remaining registration requirements before beginning your new life.


## **Which Costa Rica residency visa should you apply for?**


Costa Rica offers several residency pathways for expats, each tailored to a different lifestyle and financial situation. Whether you're retiring, living on passive income, investing in the country, or working remotely, understanding the requirements, income thresholds, and application process will help you choose the visa that's right for you.


### **Pensionado Visa (Retiree)**


**Requirements**


- Receive a guaranteed lifetime pension or government retirement benefit.
- Provide an apostilled pension verification letter.
- Submit a police clearance certificate and civil documents (such as birth or marriage certificates where applicable).
- Complete a medical examination.


**Income threshold**


- Minimum guaranteed pension income**:** USD $1,000 per month**.**


**Application process**


- Gather all supporting documents.
- Submit your application through the[Costa Rica General Directorate of Migration](https://migracion.go.cr/) , either independently or through an immigration lawyer.
- Attend any required appointments and await approval.


### **Rentista Visa (Passive Income)**


**Requirements**


- Have a stable source of passive income from outside Costa Rica.
- Provide financial records proving the source of income.
- Submit apostilled civil documents and a police clearance certificate.
- Complete local health and background checks.


**Income threshold**


- USD $2,500 per month in guaranteed passive income, or
- A USD $60,000 deposit in a Costa Rican state-owned bank.


**Application process**


- Compile proof of passive income or bank deposit.
- Submit the required documentation to the immigration authorities.
- Complete the necessary health and security checks before residency is granted.


### **Inversionista Visa (Investor)**


**Requirements**


- Make a qualifying investment in Costa Rica.
- Provide proof of investment and the source of funds.
- Submit a valid passport and police clearance certificate.
- Provide supporting business or property documentation where applicable.


**Income threshold**


- **Minimum investment:** USD $150,000 in eligible investments such as real estate, businesses, forestry projects, or approved ventures.


**Application process**


- Complete the qualifying investment.
- Gather the required financial and legal documentation.
- Submit your residency application through the General Directorate of Migration or an immigration lawyer.


### **Digital Nomad Visa**


**Requirements**


- Work remotely for an employer or clients based outside Costa Rica.
- Hold comprehensive international health insurance.
- Provide proof of employment, freelance contracts, or business ownership.
- Have a valid passport.


**Income threshold**


- USD $3,000 per month for an individual.
- USD $4,000 per month when applying with dependants.


**Application process**


- Complete the online application through Costa Rica's Digital Nomad platform.
- Upload all supporting documents.
- Pay the required government application fee.
- Await approval before relocating to Costa Rica.


## How banking works in Costa Rica for expats


Setting up a local bank account is one of the first financial steps many expats take after moving to Costa Rica. Banks such as Banco de Costa Rica (BCR) and BAC Credomatic accept foreign customers, although eligibility and onboarding requirements may differ depending on your residency status.


**Key requirements to open a local bank account:**


- Valid passport or Costa Rican residency ID
- Proof of address
- Proof of income or source of funds
- Tax identification number (where applicable)
- Initial deposit (required by some banks)


**Common limitations of local banking:**


- Lengthy account opening and verification processes
- Strict documentation requirements

## Choosing the right healthcare in Costa Rica as an expat


Costa Rica is widely recognised for its high-quality healthcare system, making it one of the most attractive destinations for expats seeking affordable and reliable medical care. Whether you rely on the public system, choose private healthcare, or invest in international insurance, understanding your options will help you make the right decision before relocating.


**Public healthcare (Caja – CCSS)**


- Mandatory for legal residents
- Monthly contributions are based on your declared income, typically 13–18%
- Covers around 85% of the population through 29 hospitals and hundreds of EBAIS community clinics
- Free healthcare for children under 18 and pregnant women
- Excellent for routine care, chronic conditions, major surgeries and long-term treatment
- **Limitation:** Non-emergency specialist appointments and elective procedures can involve lengthy waiting times.


**Private healthcare**


- Faster access to doctors, specialists and diagnostic tests
- GP consultations typically cost US$50–100, while specialist appointments cost more
- Modern hospitals with English-speaking staff, including Hospital CIMA (Escazú), Clínica Bíblica (San José) and Hospital La Católica (San José)
- Hospitals may require an upfront deposit before admission.


Many expats use a hybrid approach, enrolling in Caja to meet residency requirements while maintaining private or international health insurance for quicker access to treatment. International providers such as Cigna Global, GeoBlue and Bupa Global are popular choices for those who travel frequently or want comprehensive medical cover both in Costa Rica and abroad.


## Frequently asked questions


### **Is Costa Rica a safe place for expats?**


Yes. Costa Rica is widely regarded as one of the safest countries in Latin America, thanks to its political stability and high quality of life. However, petty crimes such as pickpocketing and vehicle break-ins can occur in tourist areas. Taking sensible precautions and choosing a safe neighbourhood will help you enjoy a secure and comfortable lifestyle.


### **How much money do I need to retire comfortably in Costa Rica?**


Your budget depends on where you live and your lifestyle. Many retirees live comfortably on US$2,000–3,500 per month, covering housing, groceries, healthcare and leisure. Coastal towns and luxury communities generally cost more than smaller inland cities, so it's worth researching living costs before making the move.


### **Can foreigners buy and own property in Costa Rica?**


Yes. Foreigners enjoy the same property ownership rights as Costa Rican citizens, making it relatively straightforward to purchase a home or investment property. The main exception is certain Maritime Zone properties near the coastline, which are subject to concession rules. Always work with a qualified lawyer before completing a purchase.


### **Do I need to speak Spanish to live in Costa Rica?**


No, but learning Spanish will make everyday life much easier. English is commonly spoken in tourist destinations, international businesses and many expat communities. However, you'll find Spanish useful when dealing with government offices, local markets, healthcare providers and building stronger relationships within your local community.
