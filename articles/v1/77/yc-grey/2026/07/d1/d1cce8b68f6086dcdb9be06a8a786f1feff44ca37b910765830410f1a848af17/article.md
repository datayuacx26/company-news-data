# Freelance web designer: How to get started and find your first client

The first place most people discover a business is no longer a shopfront, but a website. Within seconds, visitors decide whether to stay, explore or leave. That's why companies invest heavily in websites that not only look professional but also build trust, showcase their brand and convert visitors into customers. For freelance web designers, that demand has created opportunities across startups, agencies, personal brands and established businesses.


As a freelance web designer, you'll create websites using platforms such as Webflow, Framer, WordPress or Squarespace, helping clients establish a strong online presence without building everything from scratch. It's also one of the most rewarding creative services to offer, with beginner freelancers typically charging between $1,000 and $3,000 per website, while experienced designers often earn $5,000 to $20,000 or more for larger, more complex projects.


If you're learning web design for the first time or even turning an existing skill into a business, this guide will show you how to build a portfolio, attract your first clients, price your work confidently and grow a freelance career that can scale well beyond a single project.

## **What does a freelance web designer do?**


Freelance web designers help businesses create websites that are visually appealing, easy to use and designed to achieve specific business goals.


Every project usually begins with a conversation on discovery , where the designer learns about the client's business, target audience and objectives. From there, they create wireframes to map the site's structure, design the interface in tools like Figma, then build the website using platforms such as Webflow, Framer or WordPress. Before launch, they test the site across devices, fix any issues and ensure everything performs smoothly. Many designers also provide ongoing maintenance, content updates and technical support after the website goes live.


Although the roles often overlap, a web designer and a web developer are not the same. A web designer focuses on layout, user experience, branding and visual design, while a web developer writes code and builds custom functionality behind the scenes. Many freelance designers work with developers on larger projects, while no-code platforms now allow designers to build complete websites independently.


## **Building your freelance web design career**


The fastest way to become a freelance web designer is to master one platform, create a portfolio that showcases real projects and consistently market your services.


- **Learn web design fundamentals:** Start with layout, typography, colour theory, responsive design, accessibility and user experience. Strong design principles matter far more than simply knowing how to use design software.
- **Master one website platform:** Choose a primary platform such as **Webflow** before exploring alternatives like Framer or WordPress. Becoming highly proficient in one tool makes it easier to deliver professional projects efficiently.
- **Build 3 -5 portfolio websites:** Create sample websites for fictional brands or redesign existing sites to demonstrate your design process, creativity and problem-solving skills to potential clients.
- **Launch your own portfolio website:** Treat your website as your strongest marketing tool. Showcase your work, explain your services, include testimonials where possible and make it easy for prospects to contact you.
- **Choose a niche and begin outreach:** Focus on industries such as SaaS, agencies, consultants or e-commerce, then pitch businesses with personalised messages. With consistent learning and outreach, many designers secure their first paying client within **three to six months** .

## **Freelance web design services clients will pay for**


Offering a mix of project-based and recurring services helps you increase your earning potential while creating more predictable monthly income.


- **Website design** : Design professional websites from the ground up that reflect a client's brand, improve user experience and support business goals.
- **Website redesigns** : Refresh outdated websites with modern layouts, improved navigation, stronger branding and better performance without rebuilding everything from scratch.
- **Landing pages** : Create high-converting landing pages for product launches, lead generation campaigns, webinars and paid advertising.
- **E-commerce websites** : Build online stores that showcase products, simplify the buying journey and improve conversion rates.
- **Conversion optimisation** : Improve existing websites by refining layouts, calls to action, page structure and user journeys to generate more enquiries or sales.
- **Website maintenance retainers** : Provide ongoing updates, security checks, content changes and technical support through monthly recurring service agreements.
- **Client training packages** : Teach clients how to update content, publish blogs and manage their websites confidently after launch.

## **How to find your first freelance web design client**


Your first client rarely comes from luck. It usually comes from consistently showing your work, building relationships and reaching out to businesses that genuinely need a better website.


- **Cold email local businesses** : Identify businesses with outdated or poorly performing websites, then send personalised emails explaining how a redesign could improve credibility, user experience and conversions. Include one relevant portfolio example and a clear next step.
- **Build in public on LinkedIn and X** : Share your design process, website redesigns, client wins and lessons learned. Consistent posting helps potential clients discover your expertise and builds trust before they ever contact you.
- **Join design communities** : Participate in Webflow, Framer and design communities where founders and creators regularly look for designers and recommend freelancers for upcoming projects.
- **Partner with agencies** : Marketing and branding agencies frequently outsource website projects during busy periods. Delivering quality work can lead to long-term partnerships and recurring referrals.
- **Start with your existing network** : Friends, family and business contacts may already know someone who needs a website. Your first paid project often comes through a warm introduction rather than a cold pitch.

## Frequently asked questions


### **Do I need to know how to code to be a freelance web designer?**


No. Modern platforms like Webflow, Framer and Squarespace allow you to build professional websites without writing code. However, understanding basic HTML and CSS can help you customise designs, troubleshoot issues and collaborate more effectively with developers on larger or more complex projects.


### **How much can a freelance web designer earn?**


Your earnings depend on your experience, niche and the types of clients you serve. Beginners often charge $1,000–$3,000 per website, while experienced freelancers regularly command $5,000–$20,000+ for custom projects. Monthly maintenance retainers can also create a steady stream of recurring income.


### **Which platform is best for freelance web designers?**


There's no single best platform, it depends on your target clients. Webflow is ideal for business websites, Framer suits modern marketing sites, WordPress remains the most flexible option for many businesses, while Squarespace works well for smaller brands that need simple, easy-to-manage websites.


### **How long does a typical freelance web design project take?**


A simple website can be completed in one to three weeks, while business websites usually take four to eight weeks. Larger custom projects may run for several months, depending on revisions, content readiness, client feedback and the complexity of the site's functionality.


### **Should I specialise in a niche as a freelance web designer?**


Yes. Focusing on industries such as SaaS, healthcare, coaches, law firms or e-commerce helps you build expertise and position yourself as a specialist. Clients are often willing to pay more for designers who understand their industry, customers and business goals.
