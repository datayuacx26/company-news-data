# International Roaming Charges US: How to Avoid Huge Bills | Grey

Landing in the United States is exciting until your phone reconnects to a local network and you return home to a surprisingly large bill. Many travellers assume they can use their phone abroad just as they do at home, only to discover that roaming charges for calls, texts and mobile data can add up far faster than expected.


The good news is that expensive roaming bills are no longer unavoidable. There are several ways to stay connected without paying excessive carrier fees. A bit of planning before you travel can save you hundreds of dollars.


Without a travel pass, some US roaming rates can reach $10 per MB of data and up to $3 per minute for calls. Fortunately, more affordable options are available, including carrier travel passes, travel eSIMs, local SIM cards and Wi-Fi calling. This guide explains the costs, compares your options and shows you how to stay connected while keeping your spending under control.


## **How international roaming charges work in the UK**


International roaming charges begin when your phone connects to a mobile network outside your home country. Depending on your provider and destination, you'll either be charged pay-as-you-use rates for every call, text and megabyte of data, or your usage will be covered by a daily travel pass. Bill shock usually happens because many phones continue using background data for app updates, cloud backups and notifications even when you're not actively using them.


With pay-per-use roaming, every activity is billed separately, making it the most expensive option for frequent travellers. A travel pass charges a fixed daily fee, allowing you to use your domestic allowance within selected countries. Before travelling, check which countries are covered, how long the pass lasts and whether any fair usage limits apply. Understanding these differences helps you choose the most cost-effective option and avoid unexpected charges when your trip ends.

## **Smart ways to avoid international roaming fees**


Avoiding roaming charges is easier than many travellers realise. The right option depends on your destination, how long you're staying and how much mobile data you expect to use*.*


- **Airplane mode + Wi-Fi:** The cheapest option. Turn on airplane mode and use hotel, airport or café Wi-Fi. Ideal for light users, but you'll have no mobile network.
- **Travel eSIM:** Activate a digital data plan before you arrive. Great for compatible phones, competitive pricing and instant setup, although voice calls may still require internet apps.
- **Local SIM card:** Buy a SIM after landing for local rates and generous data. Usually the lowest-cost choice for longer stays, but you'll temporarily use a different phone number.
- **Carrier travel pass:** Keep your existing number and use your home plan abroad for a fixed daily fee. Convenient, though it can become expensive on longer trips.
- **Dual SIM phones:** Use your home SIM for calls and a local SIM or eSIM for data. This provides flexibility while helping reduce roaming costs without changing devices.

## **What to do before travelling internationally**


Preparing your phone before departure can prevent expensive roaming charges and connectivity issues once you land. Complete these steps before boarding your flight.


- **Check your carrier's roaming policy:** Review your provider's roaming rates, travel pass options and supported destinations so you know exactly what charges and allowances apply before travelling.
- **Install your eSIM:** If you're using a travel eSIM, download and activate it before leaving home to avoid setup issues when you arrive in your destination.
- **Enable Wi-Fi calling:** Turn on Wi-Fi calling while you're still connected to your home network, allowing you to make and receive calls over Wi-Fi where supported.
- **Set mobile data limits:** Configure data usage alerts or limits on your phone to prevent unexpected roaming charges if your device accidentally connects to a mobile network.
- **Turn off background app refresh:** Disable background data for apps that automatically sync photos, emails and updates, helping reduce unnecessary mobile data usage while travelling abroad.

## **Frequently asked questions**


### **What is the cheapest US carrier for international roaming?**


For most travellers, T-Mobile offers the cheapest international roaming. Many Magenta and Go5G plans include free unlimited basic data and texting in more than 215 destinations, with calls charged separately. If you need faster speeds, you can purchase a high-speed data pass for your trip.


### **Does Wi-Fi calling work abroad?**


Yes. If your carrier supports Wi-Fi Calling, it works abroad much like it does at home. When connected to a Wi-Fi network, you can make and receive calls without using cellular roaming. Check with your carrier before travelling, as availability and charges can vary by provider and destination.


### **Are eSIMs better than local SIM cards?**


It depends on your priorities. Travel eSIMs are easier to set up, activate instantly and let you keep your existing SIM in your phone. Local SIM cards usually offer lower prices for larger data packages, making them a better choice for longer stays or heavy data users.


### **Can I use my US phone in Europe without roaming charges?**


Yes. You can avoid roaming charges by switching off data roaming, enabling airplane mode when needed and using a travel eSIM or local SIM for mobile data. Connecting to trusted Wi-Fi networks also helps reduce costs while keeping you connected throughout your trip.


### **How much data do I need for a one-week trip abroad?**


Most travellers only need 3 GB to 5 GB of mobile data for a one-week trip. This is usually enough for maps, messaging, emails, social media and occasional video streaming. If you regularly upload videos or join video calls, consider buying a larger data plan.
