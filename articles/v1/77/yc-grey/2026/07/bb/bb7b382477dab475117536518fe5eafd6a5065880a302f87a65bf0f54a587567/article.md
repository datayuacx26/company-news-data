# Holiday Work Permit Australia: How to get one and what you can earn

Australia is one of the most popular destinations for young travellers looking to combine work and travel. From working in cafés in Melbourne to harvesting fruit in regional Queensland or spending weekends exploring the Great Barrier Reef, a working holiday lets you earn money while experiencing life in Australia.


If you've searched for a holiday work permit Australia, you've probably come across terms like Working Holiday visa, Work and Holiday visa, and visa subclasses **417 and 462** . They all refer to Australia's Working Holiday Maker programme, but the visa you're eligible for depends on your nationality.


Not every country participates in the programme, and the requirements differ depending on the visa subclass. Understanding which visa applies to you is the first step before planning your trip.


This guide explains who can apply, how the process works, how much the visa costs, how much you can earn while you're in Australia, and what your options are if your country isn't currently eligible.

## What is the Australia working holiday permit?


Although many people search for a holiday work permit Australia, the country doesn't issue a document officially called a "work permit".


Instead, the right to work comes through the Working Holiday Maker programme, which includes two temporary visas: the *Working Holiday visa (subclass 417)* and the *Work and Holiday visa (subclass 462)* .


Both visas allow young travellers to work and travel across Australia for up to 12 months. During that time, you can take temporary jobs, travel between cities, and experience life in Australia while helping fund your stay.


The biggest difference between the two visas is who can apply. Your nationality determines which subclass you're eligible for, and each visa has its own eligibility requirements.


Many travellers also extend their stay by completing eligible regional work, such as farm work, fishing, construction, bushfire recovery, or tourism jobs in designated parts of Australia.


If your goal is to spend a year travelling while earning money to support your trip, the Working Holiday Maker programme is one of Australia's most flexible visa options.


## Who is eligible for an Australia working holiday visa?


Not everyone can apply for a working holiday visa. Australia only offers these visas to citizens of participating countries, and you'll also need to meet several personal requirements.


In general, applicants must:


- Be between 18 and 30 years old, although citizens of some countries can apply up to the age of **35**
- Hold a passport from an eligible country
- Apply from outside Australia (for most first-time applications)
- Have enough money to support themselves when they arrive
- Have enough funds to buy a return ticket or enough money to purchase one later
- Not be travelling with dependent children
- Meet Australia's health and character requirements


If you're applying for the Work and Holiday visa (subclass 462), you may also need to meet additional education, English language, or government support requirements, depending on your nationality.

## Working holiday visa Australia for Nigerians and Africans


One of the most common questions people ask is whether Nigerians can apply for an Australia working holiday visa.


As of 2026, the answer is no.


Nigeria is not currently included on the list of countries eligible for either the Working Holiday visa (subclass 417) or the Work and Holiday visa (subclass 462). That means Nigerian passport holders can't apply for Australia's Working Holiday Maker programme at this time.


The same applies to many other African countries. While Australia has expanded the programme over the years, eligibility remains limited to countries that have a formal agreement with the Australian Government.


That doesn't necessarily mean working in Australia is out of reach.


If you're from Nigeria or another African country that's not part of the Working Holiday Maker programme, you may still qualify through another visa pathway.


Some of the most common alternatives include:


- **Student visa:** Study at an Australian institution while working within your visa's employment conditions.
- **Skilled migration visas:** Available to people with qualifications and experience in occupations that Australia needs.
- **Employer-sponsored visas:** Australian employers can sponsor eligible workers for specific roles where skills shortages exist.


If you're considering working abroad but Australia isn't currently an option, you can also explore opportunities in other countries. For example, our **Luxembourg work permit guide** explains another pathway for living and working overseas.


Although Australia regularly reviews its visa programmes, you shouldn't assume your country will become eligible in the future. Always check the latest eligibility list before making travel plans.


## How to apply for an Australia working holiday visa


If you're eligible, applying for an Australian working holiday visa is a relatively straightforward process. Applications are completed online through the Australian Government's **ImmiAccount** portal.


Here's what to expect.


### Step 1: Check which visa you're eligible for


Before you begin your application, confirm whether you qualify for:


- Working Holiday visa (subclass 417), or
- Work and Holiday visa (subclass 462).


The requirements differ depending on your nationality, so make sure you're applying for the correct subclass.


### Step 2: Create an ImmiAccount


Visit the Australian Department of Home Affairs website and create a free[ImmiAccount](https://online.immi.gov.au/lusc/login) . This is where you'll complete your application, upload documents, pay your visa fee and track your application's progress


### Step 3: Gather your documents


Although requirements vary slightly depending on your nationality, you'll usually need:


- A valid passport
- A recent passport photograph
- Proof that you have enough money to support yourself when you arrive
- Evidence of onward travel funds or a return ticket
- Any required English language or education documents (subclass 462 applicants)
- Additional supporting documents if requested


Some applicants may also need to complete health examinations or provide police certificates during the assessment process.


### Step 4: Complete your online application


Fill in your personal information carefully and upload all required documents.


Take your time to review everything before submitting. Missing information or incorrect details can delay your application.


### Step 5: Provide biometrics if requested


Depending on your nationality and where you apply, you may receive instructions to provide biometric information, such as fingerprints and a photograph.


Not every applicant will need to complete this step.


### Step 6: Pay the application fee


As of **2026** , the application fee for a first Working Holiday Maker visa is **AUD 840** , although additional costs may apply for health examinations, police certificates, or biometrics. Fees can change, so always check the latest amount before submitting your application.


### Step 7: Wait for a decision


Processing times vary throughout the year and depend on factors such as application volumes, your nationality, whether additional documents are required, and health or character checks.


Because processing can take longer during busy periods, it's best **not to book flights or make non-refundable travel arrangements until your visa has been granted.**


### Tips for a smoother application


Before you submit your application:


- Double-check that your passport is valid
- Make sure every uploaded document is clear and readable
- Provide complete and accurate information
- Respond quickly if the Department of Home Affairs asks for additional documents


Small mistakes can slow down your application, so taking a little extra time before submitting can make the process much smoother.


## How much can you earn on an Australia working holiday?


One of the biggest advantages of Australia's Working Holiday Maker programme is the opportunity to earn enough to support your travels while gaining international work experience.


As of **1 July 2026** , Australia's National Minimum Wage is **AUD 26.44 per hour** for award-free adult employees. Many jobs are covered by industry awards, which may offer higher minimum pay depending on the role and location. Casual workers are also entitled to a casual loading, which increases their hourly rate.


How much you earn will depend on factors such as the type of job you do, where you work, whether you're employed full-time, part-time or casually, and whether you're entitled to overtime, weekend or public holiday rates.


Working holiday makers often find temporary jobs in hospitality, retail, tourism, warehouses and logistics, or on farms during harvest seasons. Construction and labouring roles are also common, particularly for those with relevant experience.


Seasonal work in regional Australia is especially popular, as it can help eligible travellers qualify for a second or third Working Holiday visa while allowing them to explore different parts of the country.


### Tax for working holiday makers


Working holiday makers don't pay tax in the same way as Australian residents.


If you're employed by a registered employer, the first **AUD 45,000** of your working holiday income is generally taxed at **15%** . Higher tax rates apply once your earnings exceed that threshold.


As with any country, it's worth understanding your tax obligations before you start working. Keeping copies of your payslips and making sure your employer is correctly registered can make tax time much easier.

## Frequently asked questions


### Can Nigerians get a working holiday visa in Australia?


No. As of 2026, Nigeria isn't part of Australia's Working Holiday Maker programme, so Nigerian passport holders can't apply for either subclass 417 or subclass 462. Alternative pathways, such as student, skilled migration and employer-sponsored visas, may still be available depending on your circumstances.


### How long can I stay on an Australia working holiday visa?


Your first Working Holiday visa allows you to stay in Australia for up to **12 months** . If you complete eligible specified work in regional Australia, you may qualify for a second and, in some cases, a third Working Holiday visa.


### How much does an Australia working holiday visa cost?


As of 2026, the application fee for a first Working Holiday Maker visa is **AUD 840** . Additional costs may apply for health examinations, police certificates or biometric appointments. Always check the latest fee before applying, as visa charges can change.


### What is the difference between subclass 417 and subclass 462?


Both visas allow eligible travellers to work and travel in Australia for up to one year. The main difference is eligibility. Subclass **417** is available to citizens of one group of participating countries, while subclass **462** applies to another group and often includes additional requirements such as education, English language proficiency or annual application caps.


### Can I extend my working holiday visa in Australia?


Yes. Many travellers qualify for a second or third Working Holiday visa after completing eligible specified work in regional Australia and meeting the relevant requirements.
