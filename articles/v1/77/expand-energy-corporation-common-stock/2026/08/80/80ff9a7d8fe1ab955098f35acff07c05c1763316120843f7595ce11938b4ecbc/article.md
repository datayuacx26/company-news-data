### Employee Spotlight


### Get to Know Surface Landman, Reid Croft


A lifelong Morgantown resident, Reid grew up watching the shale boom reshape Appalachia and comes from a family with generations of roots in the region’s energy industry. Today he works on the Surface Land team, serving as a key link between the company and the landowners whose property it operates on.


*****How did you get into the energy industry, and what brought you to Expand** ?***
Energy has been part of my family’s history for generations. My great-grandfather, my grandfather and other members of my family all worked in the energy sector. As a high school senior, I discovered West Virginia University’s Energy Land Management program at a career fair — pitched as the business side of oil and gas — and I was sold. I joined in Fall 2015, one semester after it launched, and interned with what was then Southwestern Energy in 2018 between the Jane Lew and Tunkhannock offices. The learning curve was steep, but it confirmed I’d found the right path. I joined the Surface Land team full-time in Morgantown afterward and have built my career here ever since.


*****Walk me through a typical day — how would you describe your job to a neighbor** ?***
I usually say it’s easier to describe a typical week than a typical day. The Surface Land team sits at the intersection of many parts of the business — operations, construction, facilities, environmental, regulatory — while also serving as a primary point of contact for landowners. A big part of my job is being the translator between them and the company. One day I might be acquiring easements for waterline infrastructure, and the next I’m answering, “Is the fracking on that pad three miles away causing this new crack in my driveway?” Simply put, I make sure the company has the land rights it needs while maintaining good relationships with the people who own and live on that land.


*****What do you enjoy doing outside of work** ?*** As much time outdoors with my family as possible — camping, hiking, biking, skiing, gardening or projects around our property. My wife would tell you that collecting hobbies is one of my hobbies; I’ve cycled through running, CrossFit, gravel biking, ping pong, kettlebells and more than a few others. I also enjoy amateur homesteading, and moving to a property with 10 acres has given me more room to expand that. And I like reading — or at least the audiobook version of it, since most of mine happens on long drives or while I’m working on a hobby.

### In the Community


During the past quarter, Expand Energy’s employees have been active members of their communities, supporting charitable initiatives in our operating areas. Highlights include:


- ****Expand Energy Helps Students Launch Rockets at River Elementary****
Students at River Elementary spent the day building and launching model rockets — 80 launches in all — on the school’s baseball field. The day was made possible by a grant from our STEM Education Partnership Fund, awarded in connection with the Foundation for Appalachian Ohio, and led by volunteers from the WV SOAR rocketry club. Students ran the launches themselves while volunteers walked them through the basic engineering and physics behind each flight.
- **Investing in Youth, Education and Workforce Development**
Our employees spent time at career fairs across our operating area, including the University High School Career Fair in Morgantown and an ‘Adult Day’ event with high school seniors from Wetzel and Tyler counties. During these activities, we talked with students about careers in oil and gas and the opportunities available in our community. Our team is proud to see the impacts of the STEM Education Partnership Fund at work in classrooms across West Virginia and Ohio.
- **Strengthening Local Emergency Response**
Our SW App team welcomed the directors of the regional Emergency Management Agencies (EMA) into Expand Energy’s Wheeling office to talk through safety, preparedness and how we can better partner with one another. Alongside those conversations, we were proud to present donations to six county EMAs and the Monroe County Sheriff’s Office to enhance their equipment, training and preparedness efforts
