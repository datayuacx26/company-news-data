# More than driving: Inside the critical role of a Nutrien driver

At first glance, the job title of “driver” may sound straightforward. But as part-time Nutrien driver Rob Tyler will tell you, there are more twists and turns on that road than you think.


Rob came to Nutrien in 2019 after a 34-year career in the U.S. Air Force, where he served as a crew chief on KC‑135 refueling aircraft. Accustomed to high‑stakes environments where timing, coordination and safety are non‑negotiable, he found more similarities than differences in his new role.


"I spent my entire career on the move, on the move, on the move," Rob says. "After I retired, I got bored."


A year later, a friend told him about a driving role in agriculture.


“In the Air Force, the tanker always had to be in the right place at the right time,” says Rob, who works out of the Nutrien Ag Solutions branch in Laurelville, Ohio. “It’s not that different here. You’re supporting farmers who are on tight timelines, and you want to keep their equipment moving — but you always do it safely.”


One piece of advice has followed Rob from the military into agriculture:


"Eyes open, mouth closed, watch and learn."


That balance — precision without rushing, urgency without cutting corners — is at the heart of what Nutrien drivers do every day.


### A Role Built on Awareness and Responsibility


Rob describes his early days behind the wheel as humbling. Despite decades of experience driving military vehicles, agricultural trucks presented a new level of complexity.


“These trucks are top‑heavy. You’re hauling up to 30 tons on narrow township roads, through rolling hills, sharp turns, traffic, farm equipment — sometimes all at once,” he explains. “You learn quickly that speed isn’t the goal. Getting there and back safely is.”


Drivers must constantly assess road conditions, terrain, weather, and traffic — often in rural areas where roads are narrow and visibility is limited. It’s a role that demands situational awareness, patience, and discipline.


That mindset aligns closely with Nutrien’s commitment to safety.


“There is no rush that’s worth taking a risk,” Rob says. “If you’re late, that’s fine. The priority is always safety.”


### More Than Driving: Wearing Many Hats


Another surprise for Rob was how much more the role involves beyond driving.


At many Nutrien locations — especially smaller operations — drivers are hands‑on contributors across the site. They help load liquid and dry products, manage chemical handling, understand hazmat requirements, communicate closely with applicators, and ensure loads are within legal weight limits.


“You need to know what you’re hauling, how it’s mixed, and how it’s applied,” Rob explains. “You’re working with chemicals, so safety glasses, gloves, procedures — those aren’t optional.”


Drivers must also be prepared for the unexpected: a hose failure, a narrow bridge, a muddy farm lane, or a last‑minute schedule change driven by weather. Every decision matters.


“It’s not just starting the truck and going,” Rob says. “There are a lot of variables, and you need to think ahead.”


### Keeping Agriculture Moving


What makes the role rewarding, Rob says, is knowing how essential it is.


Drivers play a critical role in keeping sprayers and spreaders moving from field to field – often racing against weather windows that can close quickly. When fertilizer arrives on time, farmers can move directly into planting, keeping their operations on track.


“You don’t want that equipment waiting,” Rob says. “But you also don’t push it. The goal is smooth, steady, safe coordination.”


That coordination mirrors military logistics — another reason Rob believes the role is well‑suited for veterans.


### A Natural Fit for Military Experience


Rob sees clear parallels between military service and driving for Nutrien: discipline, accountability, teamwork, and a strong safety culture.


“In the Air Force, you’re trained to think big picture,” he says. “You don’t just complain — you find solutions. That mindset works really well here.”


Veterans, he adds, are often comfortable with structure, procedures, and responsibility — qualities that are essential for safe, effective drivers.


“You’re trusted with a lot,” Rob says. “That trust matters.”


### Safety First—Always


Above all, Rob returns to one theme again and again: safety.


From navigating difficult terrain to mentoring younger drivers, Rob is deliberate about slowing things down and reinforcing safe habits.


“I tell the new guys all the time — slow down. We just want you from point A to point B and back,” he says. “That’s success.”


It’s a message that reflects Nutrien’s core values — and one Rob lives every day.


For those considering a career as a Nutrien driver, especially individuals with military backgrounds, Rob offers a simple takeaway: this is meaningful, challenging work that matters.


“It keeps you engaged. It keeps you thinking. And at the end of the day, you know you helped get the job done — safely.”
