## Data ownership and security for high-net-worth clients


Monaco solicitors handling high-net-worth clients face significant risks if confidential legal data is housed on third-party SaaS CRM platforms: sensitive case files, passport scans, financial disclosures, and client communications may be exposed to unauthorized access or international data transfers not easily controlled by the firm. Legal requirements for data sovereignty strictly mandate that client information is stored, accessed, and processed within approved jurisdictions, ensuring compliance with both UK and Monaco regulations. Failure to do so can result in serious reputational and legal consequences for legal practices.


Generic cloud CRM vendors rarely offer the infrastructure flexibility law firms require. With default public-cloud hosting, firms often rely on the vendor’s compliance claims rather than wielding direct control over data residency, storage architecture, or backup frequency. In contrast, managed hosting and client-owned infrastructure provide tangible advantages. Managed hosting means security and disaster recovery are handled by dedicated professionals, but ultimate control still lies with the firm to choose vendors and audit compliance. Opting for client-owned hosting allows law firms to enforce their own access controls and encryption standards, guaranteeing that only selected staff and contractors ever see confidential data.


When considering disaster recovery and granular access control, legal sector requirements are far more stringent than most CRM market segments. For Monaco solicitors, rapid restoration from securely isolated backups and the ability to tightly restrict database access, down to the case or user level, are baseline necessities.
