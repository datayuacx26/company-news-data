## Workflow fit: Where standard CRMs hit a wall


Pipedrive and HubSpot both structure their CRMs around a set of default pipeline models and standardized sales stages, which work well for straightforward sales teams but often struggle to accommodate businesses with unusual approval steps, cross-team handoffs, or non-linear processes. While both platforms offer some customization, like editable stages and custom fields, there are real limits. When company logic falls outside their built-in workflows, teams are frequently forced into manual workarounds that add friction, increase risk, and undermine data integrity.


Pipedrive’s pipelines are designed primarily for classic B2B sales cycles: leads move from qualification to proposal to close, using a series of stages such as “Contact Made,” “Meeting Scheduled,” and “Negotiation Started.” These pipelines can be duplicated or renamed, and custom fields allow for some flexibility in data capture, but the underlying logic remains a linear progression. Similarly,[HubSpot](https://hubspot.com/?utm_source=miniloop) employs deal pipelines that allow users to create custom properties and stages, but underlying automations and reporting favor single-track processes. For nuanced workflows, like deals with nested approval requirements, multiple product lines each with their own routes, or regulatory compliance records. These systems can only stretch so far before friction builds.


Customization in both tools typically means adding custom fields, defining pipeline names, or building basic automation with the platform’s native builder. HubSpot’s marketplace offers integrations for more complex requirements, but plugging multiple point solutions together often introduces yet another layer of manual oversight. Pipedrive also supports over 500 integrations and customizable reports, but truly unique logic, like multi-level approvals that trigger based on changing business rules, or specialized audit trails for compliance. Often requires cumbersome Zapier chains, third-party add-ons, or spreadsheet workarounds. These compensations are fragile, difficult to scale, and can break with any change to the underlying system.


Picture a growth-stage company whose business depends on coordinating internal approvals across finance, legal, and product before every high-value deal. In Pipedrive or HubSpot, such a team typically resorts to tracking stages through color codes, external checklists, or by creating parallel pipelines. None of which are natively enforced or reportable. Over time, these workarounds lead to inconsistent data and accountability gaps. For organizations with strict compliance mandates or those needing infrastructure control due to data sovereignty, the limitations are even sharper. Standard CRMs rely on the vendor’s cloud; options to host the system on your own infrastructure or implement custom security protocols are limited or nonexistent.


A custom CRM, designed specifically around a team’s actual workflow, avoids these bottlenecks. By tailoring both the data model and the user experience, it ensures approvals, data collection, and process enforcement match exactly what the business needs. This control is essential not just for operational efficiency, but for companies that require data ownership or must demonstrate compliance to auditors and regulators. Domains where out-of-the-box CRMs often fall short.
