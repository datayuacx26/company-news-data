## Migration: What to expect when switching from Zoho CRM


Switching from Zoho CRM to a new platform involves more than just exporting contacts and importing them elsewhere. A smooth migration depends on data prep, integration mapping, careful oversight, and active user transition planning. Key steps include cleaning and reformatting legacy data, ensuring vital information isn’t lost in translation, and managing inevitable integration or workflow mismatches that emerge between the old and new systems.


Data export from Zoho CRM often surfaces overlooked issues. Standard exports might leave out custom field values, attachments, or activity history, and some teams miss crucial audit logs or integration-specific metadata. Prior to importing into your new CRM, you’ll need to standardize, deduplicate, and clean data so information matches the structure and requirements of the new platform. Teams frequently underestimate the manual effort required to get datasets migration-ready. Messy or misaligned data during this stage can result in lost leads, misattributed deals, or unusable reports post-move.


Integration and workflow mapping present another source of friction. Zoho CRM may have custom functions or automation connecting to tools like email systems, quoting engines, or analytics suites. Replicating those in a new system isn’t always straightforward. Each CRM alternative offers different integration options, so your team should catalog mission-critical automations and plan for either equivalent replacements or workarounds. Failing to do this upfront risks disconnected systems or inefficient patchwork solutions.


Change management is critical for successful adoption. Even a technically perfect migration can falter if users aren’t comfortable with the new CRM’s navigation or processes. Establish training early, communicate why workflows may feel different, and gather ongoing feedback. Neglecting user onboarding leads to lost productivity and low engagement with the new tool.


Some of the most disruptive pitfalls include missing legacy data, mismatched integrations, and downtime during cutover. To minimize risk, conduct parallel runs, testing the new CRM while the old system remains live, and maintain a complete data backup before final switchover.
