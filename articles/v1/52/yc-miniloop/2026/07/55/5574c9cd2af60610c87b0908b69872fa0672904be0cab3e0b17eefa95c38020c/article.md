## Data Quality: Real-Time Enrichment vs Database Depth


Clearbit and ZoomInfo optimize for different things. Clearbit's 50 million company records are refreshed the moment a change is detected, using machine learning plus QA-trained review. The dataset intentionally excludes personal emails and merges duplicate company domains into a single record, trading raw count for precision. A third-party analysis cited on Clearbit's own site backs this up, calling it the top enrichment tool for accuracy and record coverage combined.


ZoomInfo takes the opposite bet: a much larger 200+ million contact database, verified by AI plus a dedicated data-science team, refreshed on a regular cycle rather than in real time. That size buys broader coverage across industries and company sizes, but accuracy gets more mixed for niche or smaller companies where ZoomInfo's proprietary sourcing has thinner coverage.


Net: Clearbit optimizes for precision per record. ZoomInfo optimizes for coverage across a bigger universe of contacts. Which one matters more depends on whether your target accounts are well-documented enterprises or smaller, faster-moving companies.


## Pricing: Free Tier vs Annual Contract


Neither company publishes a full price list, but the entry point looks very different.


Clearbit offers a free version, so you can test data quality against your own accounts before spending anything. Paid plan pricing is available on request rather than published outright, and some users have reported unexpected overcharges tied to unclear terms. worth clarifying usage limits before you commit.


ZoomInfo has no self-serve pricing at all. Plans are gated behind a custom quote, typically starting around $15,000 per year, and costs scale from there with user licenses, feature add-ons like technographics, and credit usage for extra data pulls like org charts. You sign a contract before you can validate whether the data fits your accounts.


For teams that want to test before spending, Clearbit's free tier is the lower-risk entry point. For teams that already know their outbound volume and budget for enterprise software, ZoomInfo's contract is a predictable line item rather than a surprise.


## Integrations and Ease of Use


Clearbit is built to connect fast: API access ties into Salesforce and HubSpot without heavy setup, and no-code activation gets most teams running in minutes. The integration list stays focused on core CRM and MAP connections rather than a sprawling app catalog.


ZoomInfo's integration footprint is broader, reaching across CRM and sales-engagement platforms including Salesforce, Outreach, and Pardot. That depth comes with a longer runway to get there. onboarding runs through a demo and signed contract before you're live.


For lean teams that want to move fast on their own, Clearbit's self-serve setup is the practical choice. For teams already deep in an enterprise stack that need broad connectivity across sales tools, ZoomInfo's integration depth can justify the longer ramp.

### How does Clearbit's data accuracy compare to ZoomInfo?

ZoomInfo covers a much larger database of 200+ million verified contacts, verified by AI and a data-science team, but accuracy gets more mixed for smaller or niche companies where its proprietary sourcing has thinner coverage. If your accounts are large, well-documented enterprises, ZoomInfo's coverage holds up well. If you're targeting smaller or fast-moving companies, Clearbit's real-time approach tends to stay more current.


### Is Clearbit cheaper than ZoomInfo?


Clearbit offers a free tier and paid plans available on request, without a published price list. ZoomInfo has no self-serve pricing at all. plans are gated behind a custom quote that typically starts around $15,000 per year, plus additional costs for user licenses, feature add-ons, and data-pull credits. For teams that want to test before spending anything, Clearbit's free tier is the lower-cost entry point.


### Can you use Clearbit and ZoomInfo together?


Yes. Whether that's worth the combined cost depends on how much your outbound motion actually relies on phone-based outreach.

No. ZoomInfo doesn't offer a self-serve trial; access requires a demo and a custom quote before you can evaluate the platform against your own accounts. Clearbit offers a free tier, so you can test data quality on your own list before committing to a paid plan.
