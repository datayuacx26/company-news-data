# How to Plan a Bridal Shower: The Complete Guide for 2026

Last Updated on August 12, 2026 by[Joy Editors](https://withjoy.com/)


To plan a bridal shower, start 8 to 12 weeks before the event. Choose a host, set a budget ($300 to $2,000 depending on venue and guest count), confirm the guest list with the bride, select a theme, and send invitations 4 to 6 weeks out. Finalize the menu, games, decorations, and favors by the week before the shower. Most showers last 2 to 3 hours.


Planning a bridal shower is one of the most meaningful pre-wedding responsibilities. Whether you are a maid of honor tackling this for the first time or a bridesmaid helping coordinate, this guide walks through every decision from the initial budget conversation to the post-shower thank-you notes.


Below, you will find a week-by-week timeline, real budget breakdowns, 2026 theme ideas, etiquette answers, and a sample day-of schedule you can adapt to any shower format.


## What Is a Bridal Shower (and How Is It Different from a Wedding Shower)?


A bridal shower is a pre-wedding celebration that honors the bride-to-be with gifts, games, and quality time with close friends and family. Traditionally, only women attend. A wedding shower (sometimes called a couples shower or Jack and Jill shower) includes partners and guests of all genders.


The core structure is the same for both formats. The difference comes down to the guest list and sometimes the theme. Coed wedding showers have become increasingly popular, particularly among couples who share friend groups or prefer a less traditional gathering. If you are planning a coed event, the timeline and logistics in this guide still apply. You may just want to expand the guest list and adjust the games to fit a mixed group.


## Who Hosts a Bridal Shower?


The maid of honor traditionally hosts the bridal shower, but there are no strict rules about who takes the lead. Common hosts include:


- **Maid of honor** (most common)
- **Bridesmaids** (often co-hosting together)
- **Mother of the bride or groom**
- **Close friends or aunts**
- **The bride herself** (perfectly acceptable in 2026)


When multiple people co-host, designate one person as the primary coordinator to avoid conflicting decisions. Split responsibilities early: one person handles the venue and food, another manages invitations and RSVPs, and a third takes on decorations and games.


**Pro tip:** Create a shared document or spreadsheet so every co-host can see the budget, task assignments, and vendor contacts in one place. A[free wedding website](https://withjoy.com/wedding-website) can also serve as a central hub where guests find shower details alongside other wedding information.


### Who Pays for a Bridal Shower?


The host traditionally covers the cost. When bridesmaids co-host, they typically split expenses equally. It is appropriate to set a per-person budget cap before planning begins so nobody feels stretched. If the bride’s family wants to contribute, accept graciously and assign them specific costs (flowers, cake, or venue deposit).

## Bridal Shower Planning Timeline: 12 Weeks to Day-Of


This timeline covers the full planning arc. Adjust the start date based on your wedding schedule, but aim to have the shower 4 to 8 weeks before the wedding.


### 8 to 12 Weeks Before the Shower


This is the foundation phase. Get the big decisions locked in before moving to details.


- **Confirm the host(s).** Decide who is leading and who is helping.
- **Set the budget.** Agree on a total spend and how costs will be divided.
- **Pick the date.** Coordinate with the bride and key guests (mother of the bride, wedding party). Saturdays and Sundays between 11 AM and 3 PM are the most common.
- **Choose the venue.** Options include a host’s home, restaurant private dining room, rooftop bar, park pavilion, spa, or rental event space.
- **Draft the guest list with the bride.** Every person invited to the shower must also be invited to the wedding. Ask the bride for her list and cross-reference it with the wedding[guest list manager](https://withjoy.com/guest-list) .


**Pro tip:** Keep the shower guest list to 20 to 40 people for the best balance of intimacy and energy. Larger showers (50+) can feel impersonal unless the venue and activities support that scale.


### 6 to 8 Weeks Before the Shower


With the basics set, this phase focuses on the creative vision and communication.


- **Choose a theme and color palette.** See the theme section below for 2026 ideas.
- **Send invitations.** Mail paper invitations or send digital ones 6 weeks before the shower. Include the date, time, location, dress code, RSVP deadline, and registry information. Set the RSVP deadline for 2 weeks before the event.
- **Confirm the registry.** Ask the bride if her[gift registry](https://withjoy.com/registry/) is ready. Include the registry link on invitations or on a shower-specific page on the wedding website.
- **Book vendors.** Reserve a caterer, baker, florist, or photographer if the budget allows.
- **Plan the menu.** Decide between brunch, lunch, afternoon tea, or cocktail format. Start sourcing recipes if you are cooking.


### 3 to 4 Weeks Before the Shower


This is the detail phase. Move from planning to execution.


- **Finalize the menu and order supplies.** Confirm catering orders or create a grocery list for DIY food.
- **Select games and activities.** Plan 2 to 3 games (see the activities section below). Print or purchase any materials you need.
- **Order or make decorations.** Balloons, banners, table linens, centerpieces, signage, and any themed props.
- **Choose party favors.** Small, practical items guests will actually use: candles, mini succulents, local honey jars, custom cookies, or seed packets.
- **Plan the playlist.** Create a 3-hour playlist that matches the theme and energy. Start upbeat, mellow out during gift opening, and bring it back up for the end.


### 1 to 2 Weeks Before the Shower


Final stretch. Confirm everything and prepare for the unexpected.


- **Follow up on RSVPs.** Text or call anyone who has not responded by the deadline.
- **Confirm the final headcount** with the venue and caterer.
- **Prepare game materials.** Print bingo cards, trivia sheets, or advice cards.
- **Create a setup plan.** Assign tasks to helpers: who is handling food, decorations, music, and gift tracking.
- **Prepare a gift log.** Designate someone to write down who gave what during the gift opening for thank-you notes.
- **Charge your camera or phone.** Assign one person as the unofficial photographer.

### Budget-Saving Tips


- **Choose brunch over dinner.** Brunch menus cost 30% to 50% less than dinner service at most restaurants.
- **Use digital invitations.** Free or low-cost digital invites save $50 to $100 and make RSVP tracking automatic.
- **DIY the centerpieces.** Grocery store flowers in simple vases look beautiful and cost a fraction of florist arrangements.
- **Skip the photographer.** Assign a friend with a good phone camera and set up a small photo backdrop.
- **Buy decorations you can reuse.** Choose neutral linens and reusable signage that work for the bachelorette party too.


## Bridal Shower Themes and Ideas for 2026


The right theme pulls everything together: invitations, decorations, menu, dress code, and activities. Here are themes that are trending in 2026, plus timeless options that always work.


### Trending Themes for 2026


- **Activity-based shower.** Replace the sit-down format with a group experience: flower arranging, pottery, cooking class, wine tasting, or a spa day. Guests get an experience instead of just attending a party.
- **Garden party.** Held outdoors with floral tablecloths, linen napkins, a grazing board, and a cocktail hour feel. Works well for spring and summer showers.
- **Sustainable and eco-friendly.** Reusable decorations, locally sourced food, seed packet favors, and digital invitations. A growing choice for environmentally conscious couples.
- **Couples shower (coed).** Everyone is invited. Think backyard barbecue, game night, or cocktail party. Great for couples who share a friend group.
- **Travel-themed.** Perfect for couples planning a destination honeymoon. Passport-style invitations, a map centerpiece, and a “fund the honeymoon” registry tie-in.


### Classic Themes That Always Work


- **Afternoon tea.** Tiered trays, finger sandwiches, scones, floral china, and a dress-up dress code.
- **Brunch and bubbly.** Mimosa bar, waffle station, fresh fruit, and a relaxed morning vibe.
- **Stock the kitchen.** Each guest brings a kitchen item from the registry. Pairs with a cooking demonstration or recipe card station.
- **Lingerie shower.** Guests gift lingerie or intimate items. Keep the tone fun and comfortable for all ages present.
- **Book-themed.** Each guest brings a favorite book with a personal inscription instead of (or in addition to) a card. Especially popular with book-loving brides.

### Activity Stations (2026 Trend)


Instead of structured games, set up self-guided stations guests can visit throughout the event:


- **Flower crown station.** Provide greenery, wire, ribbon, and small blooms. Guests make their own to wear.
- **Photo booth.** A backdrop with props (veils, signs, sunglasses). Use a phone on a tripod with a timer or a portable photo printer.
- **Custom cocktail bar.** Set up a build-your-own cocktail or mocktail station with recipe cards.
- **Advice and memory wall.** A board where guests pin written memories or marriage advice.


## Sending Invitations and Tracking RSVPs


Send bridal shower invitations 4 to 6 weeks before the event. Set the RSVP deadline for 2 weeks before the shower to give yourself time to finalize the headcount, seating, and food order.


### What to Include on the Invitation


- Bride’s name (and partner’s name for coed showers)
- Date, time, and address
- Host name(s)
- Dress code or theme
- RSVP deadline and method (phone, email, or digital RSVP link)
- Registry link or note about where to find it


### Paper vs. Digital Invitations


Both formats are acceptable in 2026. Paper invitations feel more formal and are preferred for traditional showers or older guest lists. Digital invitations are faster, cheaper, and make RSVP tracking automatic. Many hosts use digital invitations for the shower and reserve paper for the wedding itself.


If you use an online invitation platform, you can track RSVPs in real time without chasing responses by text.


## Bridal Shower Etiquette Rules Everyone Should Know


Etiquette questions come up frequently in shower planning. Here are the rules that actually matter.


### Guest List Etiquette


- **Every shower guest must also be invited to the wedding.** This is the single most important etiquette rule for bridal showers. No exceptions.
- **Do not invite people the bride does not know well.** The shower should feel intimate. Casual acquaintances belong at the wedding, not the shower.
- **Plus-ones are optional.** For a women-only shower, plus-ones are not expected. For coed showers, invite partners by name on the invitation.
- **Children are at the host’s discretion.** If the event is adults-only, note it on the invitation.


### Gift Etiquette


- **Sharing the registry is appropriate.** Include the registry link on the invitation or direct guests to the wedding website.
- **Gifts are expected but not mandatory.** Most guests will bring a gift. Some may contribute to a group gift instead.
- **The bride opens gifts at the shower.** Designate someone to keep a written log of who gave what.
- **Thank-you notes should go out within 2 weeks.** The bride is responsible for thank-you notes, but the host can help by providing the gift log.


### Other Common Etiquette Questions


- **Should the bride help plan?** It depends on the bride’s preference. Some brides want to be involved in theme and menu decisions. Others prefer to be surprised. Ask early.
- **Can you have more than one bridal shower?** Yes. A family shower in the bride’s hometown and a friend shower in the city where she lives is perfectly common. Different hosts, different guest lists.
- **Is a surprise shower a good idea?** Only if you know the bride would enjoy it. Many brides prefer to know about the shower so they can dress accordingly and mentally prepare to be the center of attention.


## Common Bridal Shower Planning Mistakes to Avoid


These are the errors that derail otherwise great showers. Avoid them and you are ahead of most hosts.


1. **Not consulting the bride on the guest list.** The bride should approve the final list. Surprises are fine for the theme and decorations, but not for who is in the room.
2. **Scheduling too close to the wedding.** A shower the week before the wedding adds stress instead of joy. Aim for 4 to 8 weeks before.
3. **Ignoring dietary restrictions.** Ask about allergies and dietary needs when collecting RSVPs. Offer at least one vegetarian and one gluten-free option.
4. **Overspending on decorations, underspending on food.** Guests remember the food and the company. They do not remember the $200 balloon arch.
5. **Planning too many games.** Two to three activities is the sweet spot. More than that and the event starts to feel like summer camp.
6. **Forgetting a gift log.** Without a written record of who gave what, the bride cannot write proper thank-you notes.
7. **Not having a rain plan for outdoor events.** Always have a backup location or tent option for outdoor showers.


## After the Shower: Thank-You Notes and Gift Tracking


The shower does not end when guests leave. A few post-event tasks make a big difference.


- **Send thank-you notes within 2 weeks.** Handwritten notes are the standard. Each note should reference the specific gift and how the couple plans to use it.
- **Share photos with guests.** Create a shared album and send the link within a few days. Guests love seeing photos from the event.
- **Track gifts for the bride.** If you kept the gift log during the event, share it with the bride immediately so she can start her thank-you notes.
- **Inform absent guests.** If someone could not attend but sent a gift, let the bride know so she can send a thank-you note.
- **Return rental items.** Return any rented linens, tableware, or equipment promptly to avoid extra charges.


## Virtual and Hybrid Bridal Showers


Virtual showers made a big entrance during 2020, and they remain relevant for destination weddings, long-distance friend groups, and brides with family spread across multiple time zones. A hybrid format (in-person event with a virtual component) gives remote guests a way to participate without requiring everyone to travel.


### Tips for a Successful Virtual Shower


- **Keep it shorter.** 60 to 90 minutes is the maximum for a virtual event before attention drops.
- **Mail shower boxes.** Send remote guests a small package with treats, a candle, or a mini bottle of champagne so they feel included.
- **Choose games that work on screen.** Trivia, advice cards (submitted beforehand), and “how well do you know the bride” all translate well to video.
- **Assign a tech host.** One person should manage the video call, mute/unmute guests, and keep things on schedule.
- **Open gifts on camera.** Ship gifts to the bride ahead of time so remote guests can watch the reactions live.

## Frequently Asked Questions


### Who traditionally hosts a bridal shower?


The maid of honor is the most common host, but bridesmaids, the mother of the bride, close friends, or aunts can all host or co-host. In 2026, there are no strict rules. The bride herself can also host. When co-hosting, designate one primary coordinator to keep decisions moving.


### How far in advance should you plan a bridal shower?


Start planning 8 to 12 weeks before the shower date. The shower itself should take place 4 to 8 weeks before the wedding. This gives guests enough time to RSVP and buy gifts without overlapping with final wedding preparations.


### How much does a bridal shower cost?


A home-hosted bridal shower for 20 guests typically costs $300 to $800. A restaurant or venue event for 30 guests ranges from $1,000 to $3,000. The biggest expenses are food and drinks, followed by venue rental (if applicable) and decorations.


### How long should a bridal shower last?


Most bridal showers last 2 to 3 hours. This gives enough time for mingling, a meal, 2 to 3 activities or games, gift opening, and dessert. Going beyond 3 hours risks losing guest energy, so plan to keep the schedule tight.


### What is the difference between a bridal shower and a bachelorette party?


A bridal shower is a daytime celebration focused on gifts, games, and spending time with friends and family of all ages. A bachelorette party is a night-out or weekend trip focused on fun with the bride’s closest friends. Most brides have both events, hosted by different people on different dates.


### Should you invite someone to the bridal shower who is not invited to the wedding?


No. Every person invited to the bridal shower must also be invited to the wedding. Inviting someone to a gift-giving event without including them at the wedding is considered poor etiquette and can damage relationships.
