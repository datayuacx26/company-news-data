# Rehearsal Dinner Planning Guide: Everything You Need to Know (2026)

Last Updated on August 5, 2026 by[Joy Editors](https://withjoy.com/)


**Rehearsal dinner planning** typically begins 3 to 6 months before the wedding. The event follows the ceremony rehearsal (usually the night before the wedding) and includes the wedding party, immediate family, the officiant, and often out-of-town guests. Average costs range from $1,500 for a casual backyard dinner to $15,000 or more for a formal venue. The groom’s family traditionally hosts, though many modern couples now share expenses or self-fund the evening.


## What Is a Rehearsal Dinner?


A rehearsal dinner is a gathering held after the wedding ceremony rehearsal, typically the evening before the wedding. It serves as a thank-you to the wedding party, a chance for both families to mingle, and a more intimate celebration before the big day.


Traditionally, the event follows a run-through of the ceremony at the venue. The wedding party practices the processional, recessional, and cue timing with the officiant, then heads to dinner together. The meal can range from a casual backyard barbecue to a multi-course seated dinner at an upscale restaurant.


In recent years, rehearsal dinners have evolved beyond a simple post-rehearsal meal. Many couples now treat the evening as a meaningful event in its own right, complete with personalized decor, toasts, slideshows, and activities that set the tone for the wedding weekend.


## Who Plans and Pays for the Rehearsal Dinner?


By long-standing tradition, the groom’s parents host and pay for the rehearsal dinner. This custom dates back to when the bride’s family covered the wedding reception, and the groom’s family took responsibility for the pre-wedding gathering.


Modern approaches vary widely. Here are the most common arrangements:


- **Groom’s parents host:** They choose the venue, handle the budget, and coordinate logistics. This remains the most common arrangement.
- **Shared hosting:** Both sets of parents split costs and planning duties, which is increasingly popular when families live in different cities.
- **Couple self-funds:** Many couples in their late 20s and 30s prefer to plan and pay for the event themselves, keeping full control over style and guest list.
- **Bride’s parents host:** In some cultural traditions or family situations, the bride’s parents take the lead.


No matter the arrangement, start the budget conversation early. A direct, respectful discussion 6 months before the wedding prevents misunderstandings. Frame it simply: “We are starting to plan the rehearsal dinner. Would you like to be involved, and if so, how?” For a deeper look at traditional and modern payment norms, search for detailed guides on “who pays for rehearsal dinner” etiquette.


**Editor’s Tip:** In our experience, the smoothest budget conversations happen when the couple proposes a specific number first, rather than asking open-ended questions. Come prepared with a ballpark figure and two or three venue options at different price points. It turns a potentially awkward talk into a practical planning session, and it signals to parents that you have done your homework.


## Rehearsal Dinner Planning Timeline


Use this month-by-month checklist to stay on track. Starting 4 to 6 months out gives plenty of buffer for popular venues and catering bookings.


### 4 to 6 Months Before the Wedding


- Confirm who is hosting and establish the budget range
- Set the date (almost always the night before the wedding)
- Coordinate with your officiant and venue on rehearsal time (typically 5:00 to 6:00 p.m.)
- Draft a preliminary guest list
- Begin researching venues near the ceremony or reception site


### 3 to 4 Months Before


- Book the venue or reserve the space (restaurants, private dining rooms, and event spaces fill quickly for Friday evenings)
- Choose a catering style: plated dinner, buffet, family-style, or food trucks
- Send save-the-date communication to out-of-town guests so they can plan travel
- Decide on a theme or dress code that complements (but does not upstage) the wedding


### 6 to 8 Weeks Before


- Send formal invitations (paper or digital) with venue address, start time, dress code, and parking details
- Finalize the guest list and collect RSVPs
- Confirm menu selections and communicate dietary restrictions to the caterer
- Plan any toasts, slideshows, or activities
- Order or plan decor (centerpieces, signage, photo displays)


### 2 to 3 Weeks Before


- Confirm final headcount with the venue or caterer
- Create a seating chart (if applicable) that mixes families and friend groups
- Brief toast-givers on time limits (3 minutes per person, 4 to 5 speakers maximum)
- Prepare any gifts for the wedding party
- Arrange transportation between the rehearsal site and dinner venue if they are in different locations


### 1 Week Before


- Send a detailed schedule to the wedding party with arrival times, addresses, and parking instructions
- Confirm all vendor details (flowers, AV equipment, musician or playlist)
- Prepare a printed or digital run-of-show for the host and any speakers
- Check weather forecasts if the event is outdoors and activate your backup plan if needed


### Day Of


- Arrive at the rehearsal venue 15 to 20 minutes early
- Run through the ceremony (processional order, cue timing, readings, recessional)
- Travel to the dinner venue (allow 20 to 40 minutes for transit)
- Enjoy dinner, toasts, and quality time with your closest people
- Wrap up by 9:30 to 10:00 p.m. so the couple gets enough rest before the wedding


## How to Set Your Rehearsal Dinner Budget


One of the biggest gaps in rehearsal dinner planning is specific budget guidance. Costs vary enormously depending on your guest count, location, and style. Use these tiers as a starting point:


Style Guest Count Estimated Total Cost Per-Person Range


Casual (backyard BBQ, pizza party, taco bar) 20 to 30 $1,500 to $3,000 $50 to $100


Mid-range (restaurant private dining, buffet catering) 30 to 50 $3,000 to $8,000 $75 to $160


Upscale (fine dining, full bar, plated multi-course) 40 to 75 $8,000 to $15,000+ $150 to $250+


### Budget Breakdown by Category


Here is how costs typically distribute across major categories:


- **Venue rental:** 0 to 25% (many restaurants waive fees with a food minimum; private homes cost nothing)
- **Food and catering:** 40 to 50% (the largest expense regardless of style)
- **Drinks and bar service:** 15 to 25% (open bar costs more; consider a limited cocktail menu or BYOB venue)
- **Decor and flowers:** 5 to 10% (keep it simple since the wedding is tomorrow)
- **Invitations:** 2 to 5% (digital invitations reduce this to nearly zero)
- **Entertainment and extras:** 5 to 10% (slideshow, games, welcome gifts)


### Money-Saving Tips


- Choose a restaurant with a private room instead of renting a separate event space. Many waive the room fee when you meet a food and beverage minimum.
- Host on a Thursday evening instead of Friday if your schedule allows. Venue and catering rates drop significantly on weeknights.
- Opt for family-style or buffet service, which costs 20 to 30% less than plated meals.
- Use digital invitations and a free online RSVP system to eliminate printing and postage costs.
- Skip elaborate floral arrangements. Simple candles, greenery, and photo displays create warmth without the expense.

## Building Your Guest List


The rehearsal dinner guest list is more selective than the wedding itself. Start with the essential core group, then expand based on budget and preference.


### Core Guest List (Always Invited)


- The couple
- Parents and stepparents of the couple
- Siblings (and their partners)
- Grandparents
- All members of the wedding party (bridesmaids, groomsmen, flower children, ring bearers) and their partners
- The officiant (and their partner)
- Wedding planner or coordinator (optional, but a nice gesture)


### Extended Guest List (Budget Permitting)


- Out-of-town guests who traveled for the wedding
- Close aunts, uncles, and cousins not in the wedding party
- Parents’ close friends (especially if parents are hosting)
- Readers, musicians, or other ceremony participants


A common point of etiquette: if you invite out-of-town guests, invite all of them. Selectively including only some can create awkwardness. ## Choosing the Right Venue


The venue sets the tone for the entire evening. Choose a space that reflects your style, accommodates your guest count comfortably, and works logistically with your rehearsal location.

### Questions to Ask Every Venue


- What is the minimum and maximum guest capacity for the space?
- Is there a room rental fee, or do you waive it with a food and beverage minimum?
- Can we bring our own decor, music, or AV equipment?
- What is the cancellation or change policy?
- Is there dedicated parking, and is it free?
- Do you offer a rain backup if the space is outdoors?


**Editor’s Tip:** Visit your top venue choices on the same day of the week and time of evening as your rehearsal dinner. A restaurant that feels spacious on a Tuesday afternoon can be packed and deafening on a Friday at 7 p.m. Pay attention to noise levels, lighting (will it feel warm enough for toasts?), and whether staff seem attentive during the rush. Those details matter more than the decor in photos.


### Destination Wedding Considerations


For destination weddings, the rehearsal dinner often doubles as a welcome event for guests arriving from out of town. Keep these logistics in mind:


- Book a venue within walking distance of the hotel block to simplify transportation. If you have negotiated[group hotel rates](https://withjoy.com/hotel-blocks) , choose a dinner spot that your guests can reach on foot.
- Consider a welcome cocktail party format instead of a formal seated dinner, which accommodates guests arriving at different times.
- Share detailed directions, parking, and dress code information in advance. A[wedding website with dedicated event pages](https://withjoy.com/wedding-website) keeps the full schedule, venue maps, and hotel block details in one place so guests can reference everything on their phones.


## Menu Planning and Dietary Accommodations


The food is often what guests remember most about the rehearsal dinner. Match the menu to the tone of the evening, and plan for dietary needs well in advance.


### Menu Styles by Formality


- **Casual:** BBQ, pizza, tacos, food trucks, or a potluck-style buffet. Great for outdoor or backyard settings. Budget-friendly and low-stress.
- **Semi-formal:** Buffet or family-style service at a restaurant. Allows variety while keeping costs moderate. Works well for groups of 30 to 60.
- **Formal:** Plated multi-course dinner with wine pairings. Best for smaller groups (under 40) at upscale restaurants or private dining rooms.


### Handling Dietary Restrictions


Collect dietary information when you gather RSVPs. Common needs to plan for include:


- Vegetarian and vegan options (plan for at least one of each)
- Gluten-free alternatives
- Nut allergies and other serious food allergies
- Kosher or halal requirements
- Children’s portions or a kid-friendly menu option


**Pro Tip:** Share the menu with the venue or caterer at least 2 weeks before the event. Most restaurants can accommodate special requests with advance notice but struggle with last-minute changes.


### Drink Service Options


- **Open bar:** Most generous but most expensive. Budget $30 to $60 per person for a 3-hour event.
- **Limited cocktail menu:** Offer 2 to 3 signature drinks plus beer and wine. Cuts bar costs by 30 to 40%.
- **Wine and beer only:** A refined but affordable option, especially if the venue charges high spirit markups.
- **BYOB venue:** Some restaurants and private spaces allow guests to bring their own alcohol. This can reduce drink costs to $10 to $15 per person (corkage fee only).


## Rehearsal Dinner Etiquette


Good etiquette keeps everyone comfortable and sets a gracious tone for the wedding weekend. Here are the guidelines that matter most.


### Dress Code


The rehearsal dinner should be one step less formal than the wedding reception. If the wedding is black tie, aim for cocktail attire at the rehearsal dinner. If the wedding is casual-elegant, go with smart casual. Always communicate the dress code clearly on the invitation.


### Invitation Timing and Wording


Send rehearsal dinner invitations 6 to 8 weeks before the event. Some couples include a rehearsal dinner card in the main wedding invitation suite. Digital invitations work particularly well for this event: they allow easy RSVP tracking, let you send updates if details change, and keep your rehearsal dinner responses separate from your main wedding count.


Keep the wording warm and straightforward. Include:


- Host names (“John and Mary Smith invite you to…”)
- Date, time, and location with address
- Dress code
- RSVP instructions and deadline
- Parking and transportation notes


### Gift-Giving at the Rehearsal Dinner


The rehearsal dinner is the traditional time for the couple to give thank-you gifts to the wedding party. It is also common for hosts to present a small token to the couple. Gifts are not expected from guests attending the dinner.


### Modern Etiquette Updates


Some traditional rules have relaxed in recent years:


- **Old rule:** Only the groom’s parents can host. **Modern take:** Anyone can host or co-host.
- **Old rule:** The rehearsal dinner must be a formal sit-down meal. **Modern take:** Cocktail parties, game nights, and casual cookouts all qualify.
- **Old rule:** The dinner must be the night before the wedding. **Modern take:** Two nights before works if the rehearsal is held earlier or if the couple wants a rest day.
- **One rule that still holds:** The rehearsal dinner should never be more lavish or more formal than the wedding reception itself. Keep it complementary, not competitive.


## Speeches and Toasts: Who Speaks and When


Toasts are one of the most memorable parts of a rehearsal dinner. Unlike the wedding reception, the rehearsal dinner is the right setting for longer, more personal stories and remarks from family.


### Recommended Speaker Order


1. **Host (traditionally groom’s parent):** Welcome guests, share a short story or sentiment about the couple
2. **Other parent(s):** Brief remarks from the bride’s parents or other family members
3. **Maid of honor and/or best man:** A preview or personal story (save the big speech for the reception)
4. **Other wedding party members or close friends:** Optional, by invitation only
5. **The couple:** Close with a thank-you to the hosts, wedding party, and guests


### Time Guidelines


Aim for 4 to 5 speakers total, with each keeping remarks under 3 minutes. That puts total toast time at 12 to 15 minutes, which is enough to feel meaningful without cutting into dinner conversation. Brief the speakers in advance so they know their time slot and the overall order.


**Pro Tip:** Schedule toasts during dessert or between courses rather than before the meal begins. Hungry guests are distracted guests.


**Editor’s Tip:** Assign someone (not the couple) to record the toasts on a phone propped at a good angle. Rehearsal dinner speeches are often more heartfelt and unguarded than reception toasts, and couples consistently tell us they wish they had captured them. A simple tripod and a phone in video mode is all it takes.

## Creative Themes and Ideas


The rehearsal dinner is a chance to show a different side of your personality than the wedding itself. Here are ideas organized by formality:


### Casual and Fun


- Backyard BBQ with lawn games (cornhole, bocce, croquet)
- Pizza party at a local pizzeria with a private room
- Taco bar or food truck night at a park or private property
- Clambake or seafood boil (perfect for coastal weddings)
- Cooking class where guests prepare the meal together


### Elegant and Classic


- Private dining room at a favorite restaurant
- Wine or whiskey tasting dinner with paired courses
- Garden party with a seated dinner under a tent
- Rooftop dinner with city views


### Unique and Memorable


- Progressive dinner that moves between 2 to 3 nearby locations (appetizers, entrees, dessert)
- Boat cruise dinner on a lake or river
- Farm-to-table dinner at a local farm
- Game night with trivia about the couple, board games, and prizes


**Editor’s Tip:** The best rehearsal dinner themes are not picked from a list. They grow from something specific to the couple. Think about where you had your first date, a shared hobby, or a trip that defined your relationship. A couple who bonded over hiking does not need a formal ballroom; a trailhead picnic with lanterns tells their story better than any Pinterest board could. Let the theme be a chapter of your life, not a production.

## Common Rehearsal Dinner Mistakes to Avoid


Even well-organized hosts make these errors. Learn from them before they happen:


1. **Starting too late:** Popular Friday-night restaurant spaces book 3 to 4 months ahead. Waiting until 6 weeks out limits options significantly.
2. **Outshining the wedding:** A rehearsal dinner that is more elaborate, more expensive, or more formal than the reception creates awkward comparisons. Keep it complementary.
3. **Forgetting dietary needs:** At least one guest will have a restriction. Ask about allergies and preferences when collecting RSVPs, not the week of the event.
4. **Not providing clear directions:** Guests traveling from out of town need more than just an address. Include parking instructions, transit time from the rehearsal, and dress code details.
5. **Letting toasts run long:** Without time limits, speeches can stretch past 45 minutes. Brief speakers in advance and designate someone to keep things moving.
6. **Keeping the couple up too late:** A rehearsal dinner that turns into a late-night party leaves the couple exhausted for their wedding day. Aim for a 9:30 to 10:00 p.m. wrap-up.
7. **Inviting a partial out-of-town group:** If you invite some out-of-town guests, invite all of them. Selective inclusion creates hurt feelings the day before the wedding.

## Frequently Asked Questions


### What is the order of events at a rehearsal dinner?


A typical rehearsal dinner follows this order: cocktails and mingling (30 minutes), a welcome toast from the host, dinner service (60 to 90 minutes), speeches and toasts during or after dessert (15 to 20 minutes), and then optional activities like gift-giving or a slideshow. The entire evening usually lasts 2.5 to 3.5 hours.


### Who is supposed to plan the rehearsal dinner?


Traditionally, the groom’s parents plan and host the rehearsal dinner. However, modern weddings often see the couple themselves, both sets of parents together, or even close friends coordinating the event. The key is to have an early conversation about roles, budget, and expectations so everyone is aligned.


### What is a reasonable budget for a rehearsal dinner?


Budget depends on guest count, location, and style. A casual backyard dinner for 20 guests might cost $1,500 to $3,000. A mid-range restaurant dinner for 40 guests typically runs $3,000 to $8,000. An upscale multi-course dinner for 50 or more guests can reach $8,000 to $15,000 or more. Food and drinks account for 55 to 75% of the total budget.


### How far in advance should you plan a rehearsal dinner?


Start planning 4 to 6 months before the wedding. Book the venue 3 to 4 months out, send invitations 6 to 8 weeks ahead, and finalize the headcount and menu 2 to 3 weeks before the event. Popular Friday-evening restaurant spaces fill quickly, so earlier is better for venue booking.


### Do you have to invite out-of-town guests to the rehearsal dinner?


It is not required, but it is a thoughtful gesture, especially if guests have traveled a long distance. The etiquette guideline is: if you invite some out-of-town guests, invite all of them. If your budget does not allow for a large group, consider hosting a separate, informal welcome gathering (drinks at a nearby bar, for example) for extended out-of-town guests.
