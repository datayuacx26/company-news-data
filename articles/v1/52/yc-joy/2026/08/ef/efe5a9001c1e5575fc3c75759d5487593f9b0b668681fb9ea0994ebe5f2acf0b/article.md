# How to Send Wedding Thank You Notes: Timeline, Etiquette & Wording Examples

Last Updated on August 3, 2026 by[Joy Editors](https://withjoy.com/)


Every wedding gift deserves a genuine, handwritten thank you — and every couple needs a plan to actually get them sent. This guide covers who needs a note, when to send it, what to write for every scenario, and how to stay organized so nothing slips through the cracks. At the end, we will show you how[Joy](https://withjoy.com/) can help you manage the entire process from one dashboard.


> **Quick answer:** A good wedding thank you note is 3–5 sentences. Mention the person by name, reference the specific gift, share how you plan to use it, and close with a warm sign-off. Send all notes within three months of your wedding — aim for eight weeks after returning from your honeymoon. Use a gift tracker to keep track of who gave what so nobody gets missed.


## Who Needs a Thank You Note?


The short answer: anyone who gave you something — a physical gift, cash, a cash fund contribution, or even their time and travel. Here is a quick breakdown:


- **Every guest who gave a gift** — whether they attended the wedding or not.
- **Cash fund contributors** — even small contributions deserve a personal note.
- **Group gift contributors** — each person gets their own individual note, not one card for the group.
- **Wedding party members** — bridesmaids, groomsmen, maid of honor, best man. They gave time, money, and emotional support.
- **Parents and close family** — especially those who contributed financially or helped plan.
- **Guests who traveled** — acknowledge the effort even if they did not give a separate gift.
- **Coworkers, your boss, neighbors, and family friends** — anyone who sent a gift or made a meaningful gesture.
- **Virtual wedding attendees** — they showed up from afar, and that counts.
- **Vendors (optional)** — photographers, planners, and caterers who went above and beyond appreciate a kind note.


If you are unsure whether someone needs a note, err on the side of sending one. A brief, genuine message is always welcome.


## Wedding Thank You Note Timeline


Timing matters. A prompt note feels thoughtful; a late one can feel like an afterthought. Here is the timeline to follow:


- **Pre-wedding gifts (shower gifts, early registry purchases):** Send a thank you within two weeks of receiving the gift.
- **Wedding day gifts and cards:** Send within one month of returning from your honeymoon.
- **Cash fund and registry gifts:** Send within eight weeks post-honeymoon.
- **Firm deadline:** All notes should be sent within three months of your wedding date.
- **Running late?** A late note is always better than no note. Acknowledge the delay briefly and move on — do not over-apologize.


> **Pro tip:** Set a weekly goal instead of trying to write everything at once. Ten notes per sitting keeps the quality high and the task manageable.


## Wedding Thank You Note Wording for Registry Gifts


Registry gifts are the easiest to write because you already know what the person gave. Mention the item by name and say something genuine about how you will use it.


### Template 1 — Kitchen or home item:


> Dear \[Name\],
>
>
> Thank you so much for the \[item\]. We have already put it to good use — \[brief personal detail, e.g., “made our first Sunday breakfast with it”\]. Your thoughtfulness means so much to us, and we are so glad you were there to celebrate with us.
>
>
> With love,
> \[Your Names\]


### Template 2 — Decor or keepsake:


> Dear \[Name\],
>
>
> The \[item\] is absolutely beautiful. We have found the perfect spot for it in our \[room\], and it will always remind us of this time in our lives. Thank you for celebrating with us and for your generosity.
>
>
> Warmly,
> \[Your Names\]


## Wedding Thank You Note Wording for Money or Cash Gifts


When someone gives cash or a check, do not mention the dollar amount. Instead, share what you plan to do with it. This keeps the note personal and avoids any awkwardness.


### Template 1 — General cash gift:


> Dear \[Name\],
>
>
> Thank you so much for your generous gift. We are putting it toward \[honeymoon / new home / a piece of furniture we have had our eye on\], and it will make such a difference. We are so grateful for your support and love.
>
>
> With love,
> \[Your Names\]


### Template 2 — Honeymoon fund contribution:


> Dear \[Name\],
>
>
> Thank you for contributing to our honeymoon fund. Because of you, we got to \[experience / upgrade to / enjoy\] \[specific detail, e.g., “a sunset dinner cruise in Santorini”\]. It was a memory we will carry with us always. We are so lucky to have you in our lives.
>
>
> Love,
> \[Your Names\]

## Wedding Thank You Note Etiquette: The Key Rules


- **Timeline:** Send notes within three months of your wedding date. Aim for eight weeks post-honeymoon as your personal deadline.
- **Handwritten vs. printed:** Handwritten is always the right choice for wedding thank you notes. Printed cards feel impersonal for this occasion.
- **Both names:** If you are married, both names should appear in the sign-off, even if only one person writes the notes.
- **Specific over generic:** “Thank you for the gift” is not enough. Name the item or gesture. This is the single most important rule.
- **Length:** Three to five sentences is ideal. Longer is not always better.
- **Do not mention dollar amounts:** For cash gifts, never write the amount. Reference what you plan to do with it instead.


## Common Thank You Note Mistakes to Avoid


Even well-intentioned couples stumble on these. Here are the most common mistakes — and how to sidestep them:


- **Using a generic template without personalizing it.** Every note should mention the specific gift and include at least one personal detail. Guests can tell when they received a copy-paste.
- **Mentioning dollar amounts for cash gifts.** Never write the amount. Say what you plan to do with it instead.
- **Sending one group note for a group gift.** Each contributor gets their own note. Always.
- **Waiting too long to start.** The longer you wait, the harder it gets. Start writing within a week of returning from your honeymoon.
- **Forgetting guests who did not give a physical gift.** People who traveled, attended virtually, or were part of the wedding party still deserve a note.
- **Over-apologizing in a late note.** One brief acknowledgment is enough. Spending half the note apologizing dilutes the gratitude.
- **Skipping the note because you thanked them in person.** A verbal thank you at the reception is great, but it does not replace a written note.
- **Not tracking who gave what.** Without a system, gifts get mixed up and people get missed. Use a gift tracker from day one.


## How to Stay Organized With Your Wedding Thank You Notes


The biggest challenge is not writing the notes — it is keeping track of who gave what. Here is how to stay on top of it:


- **Track gifts as they arrive.** Do not wait until after the wedding. Log every gift — registry items, cash, checks, group contributions — the moment it shows up. The longer you wait, the harder it is to remember who sent what.
- **Verify mailing addresses.** Before you start writing, make sure you have a current address for each recipient. Frequently Asked Questions


How long do you have to send wedding thank you notes?


The traditional guideline is within three months of your wedding date. Most etiquette experts recommend sending notes within eight weeks of returning from your honeymoon. Sending late is always better than not sending at all.


### What should you write in a wedding thank you note for money?


Do not mention the dollar amount. Instead, share what you plan to do with the gift — whether that is contributing to your honeymoon, a new home, or a specific purchase. This keeps the note personal and avoids any awkwardness around amounts.


### Do you have to send thank you notes to everyone at your wedding?


Yes. Anyone who gave a gift — whether they attended or not — should receive a thank you note. For guests who attended but did not give a gift, a note is a kind gesture but not required. Vendors who went above and beyond also appreciate a note.


### Can wedding thank you notes be typed or printed?


Handwritten notes are strongly preferred for wedding thank yous. Printed cards feel impersonal for this occasion. If your handwriting is difficult to read, write slowly and clearly — the effort matters more than perfect penmanship.


### Can you send thank you notes digitally?


Yes — digital thank you notes are becoming more common, especially for cash fund contributions and guests who attended virtually.[Joy lets you send personalized digital thank you notes](https://withjoy.com/help/en/articles/8328353-sending-thank-you-notes/) directly from your gift tracker, complete with a custom message and wedding photo.


### Should you send a thank you note to someone who attended but did not give a gift?


It is not strictly required, but it is a kind gesture — especially if the guest traveled a long distance or was part of your wedding party. A brief note acknowledging their presence and support is always appreciated.
