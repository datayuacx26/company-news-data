# How to Ask for Cash Instead of Gifts at Your Wedding (Politely)

Last Updated on July 30, 2026 by[Joy Editors](https://withjoy.com/)


**Asking for cash instead of gifts is completely acceptable — it is how you ask that matters.** More couples are choosing cash funds over traditional registries, especially those who already live together, are saving for a home, or want to fund a honeymoon. The key is framing the request as a personal, heartfelt note rather than a direct demand. Here is how to do it well.


## Is It Okay to Ask for Cash Instead of Gifts?


Yes, and it is far more common than most couples realize. Wedding gift etiquette has shifted significantly over the past decade. Cash funds, honeymoon registries, and experience-based registries are now mainstream. According to Joy platform data, cash funds are one of the most-added registry items among couples who use Joy.


Several factors drive this shift:


- **Couples already live together.** Unlike previous generations who often moved in together after the wedding, many couples today have fully stocked kitchens and linen closets long before their wedding day. A third toaster is not what they need.
- **Major life purchases take priority.** A first home, a renovation, paying off student loans, or building an emergency fund are real financial goals that cash contributions support directly.
- **Experiences matter more than things.** Many couples would rather put contributions toward a[honeymoon](https://withjoy.com/honeymoon-registry) , a bucket-list trip, or regular date nights than accumulate household goods.
- **Smaller living spaces.** Urban apartments and minimalist lifestyles leave little room for a full china set. Cash is practical when storage is limited.
- **Guest convenience.** Guests increasingly prefer the simplicity of contributing a specific dollar amount online over shopping for, wrapping, and shipping a physical gift.


The one rule that has not changed: never put gift or cash requests on the invitation itself. The invitation is a formal request to attend your wedding, not a shopping list. Instead, share your registry and cash fund details on your[wedding website](https://withjoy.com/wedding-website) , through word of mouth from family, and on a separate registry card if you include one in your invitation suite.

## 5 Ways to Ask for Cash Instead of Gifts


### 1. Set Up a Cash Fund or Honeymoon Registry


The cleanest way to ask for cash is to give guests a specific, named destination for their contribution. A[honeymoon fund](https://withjoy.com/honeymoon-registry) , a house down payment fund, or a “new adventures” fund feels more personal than a generic cash request.


Joy’s cash fund feature lets you create named funds with a direct contribution link. Here are some popular fund examples couples set up:


- **Honeymoon fund:** Break the trip into specific experiences — “Santorini sunset dinner,” “scuba certification in Bali,” “first-class upgrade.” Guests love contributing to something tangible. ([How to set up a honeymoon fund](https://withjoy.com/help/en/articles/8328215-adding-a-honeymoon-or-cash-fund-to-your-registry) )
- **First home fund:** Saving for a[down payment or closing costs](https://withjoy.com/honeymoon-registry) ? Create a “Our First Home” fund. Guests contributing $50-100 each adds up quickly across a 150-person guest list.
- **Home renovation fund:** Already own a home but want to redo the kitchen, finish the basement, or landscape the backyard? A renovation fund turns wedding gifts into something you will use every day.
- **Date night or experience fund:** “Date Night Fund,” “Concert Tickets for a Year,” or “Cooking Class Series” are fun, personal options that show guests exactly how their contribution will be enjoyed.


Guests contribute online, and funds transfer directly to your bank account. Joy charges no platform fee on cash fund contributions.


### 2. Add It to Your Wedding Website


Your[wedding website](https://withjoy.com/wedding-website) is the right place to share registry and cash fund information. Most guests check the wedding website before buying a gift. A short, warm note on your registry page is all you need:


> Your presence at our wedding is the greatest gift. If you would like to give something, we have set up a small registry and a honeymoon fund — both linked below.


Keep it brief and genuine. Guests appreciate honesty over elaborate justifications.


### 3. Include a Registry Card in Your Invitation Suite


A small registry card tucked into the invitation suite is standard practice. It can list your wedding website URL (where guests will find the cash fund link) or include a direct link to your registry. Keep the card simple — just the URL and a short line like “Registry and gift information available at \[website\].”


Do not put cash fund wording on the invitation card itself. The registry card is the appropriate place for this information.


### 4. Let Family Spread the Word


Word of mouth from parents and close family members is one of the most effective ways to communicate gift preferences, especially with older guests who may not check a wedding website. Ask your parents to mention your cash fund when guests ask what you need.


This approach works particularly well for guests who prefer giving a check or cash in a card at the reception rather than contributing online.


### 5. Set Up a Card Box at the Reception


Not every guest will feel comfortable with online contributions. A card box at the reception gives guests a place to leave a card with a check or cash. Label it clearly and keep it in a visible, attended location during the reception.


A card box also captures gifts from guests who forgot to send something in advance — which is more common than couples expect.


## Wording Examples for Asking for Cash


The tone should be warm, specific, and grateful. Avoid anything that sounds transactional or demanding.


**Honeymoon fund:**


> We have everything we need to start our life together — except the adventures ahead. If you would like to give a gift, a contribution to our honeymoon fund would mean the world to us.


**House down payment:**


> We are saving for our first home together. Your presence at our wedding is gift enough, but if you would like to contribute, we have set up a home fund linked on our wedding website.


**General cash fund:**


> Your presence is our present. If you would like to give something, a contribution toward our future — whether that is a trip, a home project, or a special dinner — would be deeply appreciated.


**Mixed registry (some physical items, some cash):**


> We have a small registry for a few things we still need, plus a honeymoon fund for those who prefer to give an experience. Both are on our wedding website.


## What to Avoid


- **Cash requests on the invitation.** This is the one hard rule. Keep it on the website and registry card.
- **Poems or rhymes asking for money.** These often come across as awkward. A direct, warm note works better.
- **Saying you “do not want gifts.”** If you set up a cash fund, you do want gifts — just in a different form. Be honest about it.
- **Vague requests.** “We have everything we need” without a specific fund or registry leaves guests without a clear path. Give them somewhere to go.


## How to Handle Guests Who Give Physical Gifts Anyway


Some guests will give physical gifts regardless of your preferences — and that is fine. Accept them graciously, send a personalized thank-you note within two weeks, and do not make guests feel bad for following their own instincts about gift-giving.


If you genuinely have no space for physical items, a brief note on your wedding website (“We are in a small apartment and have limited space — a cash contribution is the most helpful gift”) gives guests context without being rude.


## Frequently Asked Questions


### Is asking for cash rude?


No. Asking for cash is widely accepted at modern weddings. What matters is how you frame the request. A warm, specific note on your wedding website or registry card (“We are saving for our first home and would appreciate contributions to our house fund”) is perfectly polite. Putting a cash request directly on the invitation, or using demanding language, is what crosses the etiquette line.


### Should older guests be treated differently?


Not differently, but be aware that some older guests are more comfortable giving a physical gift or a check in a card. Offering both a cash fund and a small traditional registry covers all preferences. You can also ask parents to mention your cash fund when older relatives ask what you need, since they may not visit your wedding website.


### How much cash do guests usually give?


The most common range is $75-150 per guest or couple, though this varies by region, relationship to the couple, and the formality of the wedding. Close family members often give $200-500. Guests typically try to cover at least the cost of their plate. For a cash fund, there is no “wrong” amount — any contribution is generous.


### Can I have both a registry and cash fund?


Yes, and many couples do. A mixed approach works well: add a few physical items to a[traditional registry](https://withjoy.com/registry) for guests who prefer giving a tangible gift, and set up a cash fund for guests who prefer contributing money. List both on your wedding website so guests can choose whichever option feels right to them.


### Should I mention why I am asking for cash?


A brief explanation helps. Guests appreciate knowing their money is going toward something specific: “We are saving for a honeymoon to Italy” or “We are putting money toward our first home.” You do not need a long justification — one or two sentences give enough context to make the request feel personal rather than transactional.


### Can I ask for cash for a destination wedding?


Yes, and guests are generally understanding since they are already spending on travel. Keep wording sensitive to the fact that attending a destination wedding is itself a significant gift. Something like: “Your presence in Costa Rica means everything to us. If you would also like to give a gift, we have set up a small honeymoon fund on our website” acknowledges the expense guests are already incurring.


### What if someone gives a physical gift anyway?


Accept it graciously and send a personalized thank-you note. Some guests enjoy shopping for gifts, and their choice to give something physical reflects their own love language. Never make a guest feel bad about the form their generosity takes. If you receive duplicates or items you cannot use, most retailers accept returns within their standard return window.


### Can guests contribute after the wedding?


Yes. Most online cash fund platforms, including Joy, keep funds active after the wedding date. It is common for guests to contribute in the days and weeks following the event — especially those who attended the reception and were reminded about the fund. There is no need to close your fund immediately.


### Is a honeymoon fund considered a cash gift?


Technically, yes — the money goes to your bank account like any cash gift. But a[honeymoon fund](https://withjoy.com/honeymoon-registry) feels different to guests because it ties their contribution to a specific experience. Contributing $100 to “Dinner on the Amalfi Coast” feels more personal and memorable than dropping $100 in a generic cash fund. This is why naming your funds with specific activities or goals increases contributions.


### Should I include a QR code to my registry?


A QR code on your registry card or wedding website card is a practical touch that makes it easy for guests to find your registry from a printed card. It is especially useful for older guests or anyone who would rather scan than type a URL. Keep the QR code small and position it alongside the written URL so guests have both options. Many couples also include a QR code on reception signage near the card box.


### How do you politely ask for cash as a wedding gift?


Set up a named cash fund (honeymoon, home, adventures) on your wedding website and mention it there. Keep the wording warm and specific — tell guests what the money will go toward. Never put the request on the invitation itself.


### Can I ask for money instead of wedding gifts?


Yes. Cash funds and honeymoon registries are widely accepted. The etiquette rule is to keep the request off the invitation and share it through your wedding website and word of mouth instead.


### How do you say money instead of gifts at a wedding?


Be direct but warm: “We have set up a honeymoon fund in lieu of a traditional registry.” Pair it with a specific link so guests know exactly where to contribute.


### What is the best way to collect cash gifts at a wedding?


A combination works best: an online cash fund for guests who prefer to give in advance, and a card box at the reception for guests who prefer to give in person with a card and check.
