# Wedding Speech Examples for Every Role: Bride, Groom, Best Man, Maid of Honor & More

Last Updated on July 27, 2026 by[Joy Editors](https://withjoy.com/)


**Quick Answer**
A great wedding speech has five parts: introduce yourself, share one specific story, connect it to the couple’s relationship, offer genuine wishes for their future, and close with a toast. Aim for 300-600 words (2-4 minutes). The traditional speaking order is father of the bride, best man, maid of honor, then the couple. Below you will find fill-in-the-blank templates, full examples for every role, a writing timeline, toast lines, and a clear list of what not to say.


Wedding speeches are one of the most memorable parts of any reception, and one of the most nerve-wracking to write. Whether you are the best man scrambling for the right words, the maid of honor trying to balance funny and heartfelt, or the bride or groom wanting to thank the people who made the day possible, this guide has you covered.


Below you will find the speaking order, fill-in-the-blank templates, full speech examples for every role, a week-by-week writing timeline, quotable toast lines, and practical tips for delivering your words with confidence.


## Wedding Speech Order: Who Speaks and When


The speaking order matters more than most people expect. It controls the emotional arc of the evening: welcome, then warmth, then humor, then gratitude. Get it wrong and the room peaks too early, or the funny speech lands right after the tearful one and feels jarring.


Here is the traditional order, which still works at the majority of weddings:


Order Speaker Length Purpose


1 Father of the bride (or both parents of the bride) 3-5 min Welcomes guests, sets the emotional tone, welcomes the new partner into the family


2 Father of the groom or parents of the groom 2-4 min Thanks the hosts, welcomes the other family, offers a blessing


3 Best man 3-5 min Brings the humor, tells one great story, toasts the couple


4 Maid of honor 3-5 min Balances humor with real feeling, speaks to the bride directly


5 Groom (or the couple together) 2-4 min Thanks everyone: families, wedding party, guests who traveled


6 Bride 2-4 min Closes the block, speaks to her partner, raises the final toast


Most modern weddings adjust this. Blended families, two brides, two grooms, absent parents, and couples who simply do not want six speeches all call for a different shape. These variations are common and completely acceptable:


Situation Suggested Order


You want to keep it short (3 speeches total) Parent of the bride, best man or maid of honor (pick one), then the couple together


Two brides or two grooms One parent from each family, then each member of the wedding party, then each partner. Alternate families so neither goes first twice.


Blended or step-families Group parents and step-parents into one joint welcome, or split across the rehearsal dinner and reception so nobody is left out


A parent has passed away Have a sibling, aunt, uncle, or family friend speak in that slot, or add a brief moment of remembrance in the couple’s speech


Large wedding party who all want to speak Move extra speeches to the rehearsal dinner or welcome party. Cap the reception at four.


Very casual or micro wedding Open-mic style after dinner, with one designated person to start and close it


**Where speeches sit in the evening:** The safest slot is after the main course and before dessert. Guests have eaten, nobody is light-headed, and the bar has not been open so long that the room stops listening. Avoid scheduling speeches during cocktail hour (people are standing and distracted) or after dancing has started (you will never get the energy back).


### How Many Speeches Is Too Many


Four is the sweet spot. Five is fine. Six is the ceiling. Beyond that, guests lose attention regardless of how good the individual speeches are.


The practical constraint is total time, not the number of speakers. Budget 20 to 25 minutes for the entire speech block, including the pauses while people find the microphone and get settled. Two speakers at eight minutes each is worse than five speakers at four minutes each.


Number of Speeches Total Block Time Works Well For


2-3 10-15 min Micro weddings, courthouse celebrations, couples who want dinner to move quickly


4-5 18-25 min Most weddings. Enough voices to feel full without dragging.


6 25-30 min Large weddings with two full families to acknowledge. Split into two blocks if possible.


7+ 30+ min Not recommended at a reception. Move the overflow to the rehearsal dinner.


### Who Introduces Each Speaker


Somebody has to say “and now we will hear from the best man.” Do not leave this to chance, because the alternative is an awkward silence while five people look at each other.


- **A professional MC or the band leader.** Cleanest option. Give them the running order in writing, with correct name pronunciations.
- **The DJ.** Works well and costs nothing extra. Confirm they are comfortable doing it, since not all DJs are.
- **One member of the wedding party.** A good choice for smaller weddings. Ask someone who is not also giving a long speech.
- **A parent.** Traditional at some weddings: the father of the bride opens the block and hands off to each speaker.


Whoever does it should hold a printed list. Phones die, screens dim, and reading the order off a text message in a dark room does not work. If you are printing[wedding programs](https://withjoy.com/cards/wedding/programs) , including the speech order gives guests a heads-up and gently signals to speakers that the schedule is real.


Speakers need to know when they are on well before the day. Publishing a[schedule of events](https://withjoy.com/help/en/articles/14695827-manage-your-schedule-of-events) that lists the reception timeline means nobody is surprised, and the wedding party can plan around it. For the broader picture of how the speech block fits into the evening, see this guide to building a[reception timeline](https://withjoy.com/blog/wedding-reception-timeline/) , and these[practical tips for setting up speeches](https://withjoy.com/blog/helpful-tips-for-setting-up-speeches/) .


## The Anatomy of a Great Wedding Speech


Every memorable wedding speech, regardless of who is giving it, follows a similar structure. Use this as your framework:


Section What to Include Approximate Length


**Opening** Who you are, your relationship to the couple 30-60 seconds


**Story** One specific, vivid memory that shows who they are 60-90 seconds


**Connection** How that story reflects the couple’s relationship or values 30-45 seconds


**Wishes** Genuine hopes for their future together 30-45 seconds


**Toast** Short, punchy, glass-raising close 15-20 seconds


**The 50/30/20 Rule:** Spend 50% of your speech on the person you know best, 30% on the couple together, and 20% on the guests and occasion. This keeps the speech personal without ignoring the new partner or the celebration itself.


## Wedding Speech Templates You Can Fill In


If you are staring at a blank page, start here. These templates give you the skeleton: fill in the bracketed parts with your own specifics and you have a working draft in about twenty minutes. Read the result aloud and cut anything that sounds like a greeting card.


Template 1: Universal (works for any role)


Good evening. For those I have not met, I am \[your name\], and I am \[relationship to the couple\].


I want to tell you one thing about \[Name\]. \[One specific memory: a moment, a decision, a conversation. Two or three sentences. Include a detail only you would know.\]


That is who \[Name\] is. \[Explain what the story reveals about their character in one sentence.\]


When \[Name\] met \[Partner’s name\], \[describe the change you noticed. Be concrete: what did they say, how did they act differently.\]


\[Partner’s name\], \[say one sincere thing directly to them. Thank them for something specific.\]


Please raise your glasses. To \[Name\] and \[Partner’s name\]: \[one wish for their future\]. Cheers.


Template 2: Best Man


Good evening, everyone. I am \[your name\], and I have been \[Groom’s name\]’s \[friend, brother, cousin\] for \[number\] years.


\[One light, self-aware line about being asked to speak, or about how long you have known him.\]


Here is the story I want to tell you. \[One story, 60-90 seconds. Pick something that makes him look good in the end, not just funny.\]


I have watched \[Groom’s name\] \[describe a quality: loyalty, generosity, stubbornness in a good way\] for a long time. But something changed when \[Partner’s name\] showed up. \[Describe the specific change.\]


\[Partner’s name\], \[one sincere sentence to them. This is the line people remember.\]


\[Groom’s name\], \[one line directly to him.\]


Everyone, please raise your glasses. To \[Groom’s name\] and \[Partner’s name\]. Cheers.


Template 3: Maid of Honor


Hi, everyone. I am \[your name\], and I have known \[Bride’s name\] since \[when and how you met\].


\[One sentence that captures who she is. Specific, not generic.\]


\[One story, 60-90 seconds. Something that shows her character rather than just a funny situation.\]


When she told me about \[Partner’s name\], \[describe what you noticed. The best version of this line is about a change in her, not a description of them.\]


\[Partner’s name\], \[one sincere sentence directly to them.\]


\[Bride’s name\], \[one line to her. Say the thing you would say if the room were empty.\]


To \[Bride’s name\] and \[Partner’s name\]: \[one wish\]. Cheers.


Template 4: Parent of the Bride or Groom


Good evening, everyone. I am \[your name\], \[Name\]’s \[mother, father, parent\], and thank you all for being here tonight.


\[Thank the people who traveled, and anyone who helped make the day happen.\]


\[One memory of your child, ideally from childhood, that connects to who they are now. Two or three sentences.\]


\[Describe watching them become an adult, and what you admire about the person they are today.\]


\[Partner’s name\], \[welcome them into the family explicitly. Say the words “welcome to our family” if you mean them.\]


\[Optional: one sentence of remembrance for anyone not able to be here.\]


To \[Name\] and \[Partner’s name\]: \[your wish for them\]. Please raise your glasses.


Template 5: Bride or Groom


Thank you all for being here. \[One sentence about what it means to look out at this room.\]


To our families: \[thank them for something specific, not just “everything.”\]


To our wedding party: \[one line, ideally with a detail about what they actually did.\]


To everyone who traveled: \[acknowledge the effort directly.\]


\[Partner’s name\], \[the personal part. One thing you have learned from them, or one ordinary moment that told you everything. Keep it specific and keep it short: the room will feel it more than a long declaration.\]


Please raise your glasses. To all of you, and to us. Cheers.


Template 6: Very Short Toast (under 60 seconds)


I am \[your name\], \[relationship\]. I have known \[Name\] for \[number\] years, and in that time \[one thing you have watched them become\].


\[Partner’s name\], you are the reason for that.


To \[Name\] and \[Partner’s name\]. Cheers.


**How to use a template without sounding like one:** Fill it in, then delete the first sentence of every paragraph and see if the speech still works. It usually does, and it is tighter. Templates give you structure, but the specific details are what make the room go quiet.


## Best Man Speech Examples


The best man speech is expected to be funny, warm, and brief. One good story beats three mediocre ones. The job is not to be the funniest person in the room: it is to make the groom look good and make the couple feel celebrated. Humor is the delivery mechanism, not the point.


At a Glance Best Man


Typical length 3-5 minutes (400-600 words)


Position in the order Usually third, after the parents


Tone Funny opening, sincere finish. Land the plane on warmth.


Must include Your name, how you know the groom, one story, a direct line to the partner, a clear toast


Avoid Ex-partners, bachelor party details, inside jokes, anything you would not say in front of his grandmother


Common mistake Roasting for four minutes and never saying anything kind


Example 1: Classic Best Man


Good evening, everyone. For those I have not met, I am \[Name\], and I have had the privilege of being \[Groom’s\] best friend for the past twelve years. In that time, I have watched him make a lot of decisions: some excellent, some I will not mention tonight. Marrying \[Partner’s Name\] is, without question, the best one he has ever made.


I knew \[Partner’s Name\] was different the first time \[Groom\] talked about them. He did not just say “I met someone.” He said “I think this is the person.” That is not something he says lightly. And watching them together these past few years, I can tell you: he was right.


\[Groom\], you are one of the most loyal, generous people I know. \[Partner’s Name\], you make him even better. That is the highest compliment I can give anyone.


Please raise your glasses. To \[Groom\] and \[Partner’s Name\]: may your love be modern enough to survive the times, and old-fashioned enough to last forever. Cheers.


Example 2: Funny Best Man


Good evening. I am \[Name\], and I have been asked to keep this speech under five minutes. I have been asked to keep a lot of things under five minutes over the years, and I have never once succeeded. Tonight, I will try.


I have known \[Groom\] since we were both terrible at most things. He has improved significantly. \[Partner’s Name\] deserves a large share of the credit for that.


I have watched \[Groom\] fall in love before. It never looked like this. This looks like the real thing: the kind where you actually listen when the other person is talking, where you remember their coffee order, where you become a slightly better version of yourself just by being around them.


\[Partner’s Name\], thank you for making him that person. And \[Groom\], thank you for finally giving me a reason to buy a suit.


To the couple: may your arguments be short, your adventures be long, and your Wi-Fi always be strong. Cheers.


Example 3: Best Man Who Is Also the Brother


Good evening. I am \[Name\], and I am \[Groom’s\] younger brother, which means I have spent my entire life either following him around or arguing with him. Usually both in the same afternoon.


Here is what I know about my brother that most of you do not. When our family went through a hard year, he was nineteen, and he drove four hours every other weekend to be home. Nobody asked him to. He never mentioned it. He just kept showing up.


That is the thing about \[Groom\]. He shows up. Quietly, without needing credit for it.


\[Partner’s Name\], you are getting a person who will show up for you for the rest of your life. I know that because I have watched him do it. And I want to say thank you, because in the last few years I have watched him become lighter, funnier, and more himself than I have ever seen him.


Please raise your glasses. To my brother and \[Partner’s Name\]. Cheers.


## Maid of Honor Speech Examples


The maid of honor speech should balance humor with genuine emotion. You know the bride better than almost anyone. Let that show. One personal story, a moment of real feeling, and a warm toast lands better than a list of compliments.


At a Glance Maid of Honor


Typical length 3-5 minutes (400-600 words)


Position in the order Usually fourth, right after the best man


Tone Warm and personal, with humor woven through rather than front-loaded


Must include How you met, one story that shows her character, a direct line to her partner, a toast


Avoid Dating history, anything about her family that is not yours to share, lists of adjectives


Common mistake Talking only about the bride and forgetting the partner exists


Example 1: Classic Maid of Honor


Hi, everyone. I am \[Name\], and I have been \[Bride’s\] best friend since we were both \[age\] and had very questionable taste in everything. Some things have changed. Most things have not.


\[Bride\] is one of the most thoughtful people I have ever known. She remembers birthdays, she shows up when things are hard, and she gives advice that is actually useful. I have always admired that about her.


When she told me about \[Partner’s Name\], I noticed something different. She was not just happy: she was calm. Like she had found something she had been looking for without quite knowing she was looking. That is a rare thing.


\[Partner’s Name\], you make my best friend feel like the best version of herself. That is everything I could have asked for.


Please raise your glasses, to \[Bride\] and \[Partner’s Name\]. May every day together feel as good as today. Cheers.


Example 2: Funny Maid of Honor


Good evening, everyone. I am \[Name\], \[Bride’s\] maid of honor and, more importantly, the person who has heard every version of this love story since the very beginning.


I was there for the first text she sent me after their first date. I will not read it aloud, but I will say it involved a lot of capital letters and at least three exclamation points per sentence. That is not her normal style.


\[Bride\], you have always known exactly who you are and what you want. Watching you find someone who matches that energy, who makes you laugh as hard as you make everyone else laugh, has been one of the great joys of my life.


\[Partner’s Name\], welcome to the family. Fair warning: we are a lot. You are handling it beautifully.


To the couple: may your love story always be worth reading. Cheers.


Example 3: Maid of Honor Who Is Also the Sister


Hi, everyone. I am \[Name\], and I am \[Bride’s\] sister. I have known her longer than anyone in this room, including our parents, who technically arrived first but were not paying as close attention.


When we were kids, \[Bride\] used to organize everything. Our toys, our schedules, our arguments. She once made a written agreement about whose turn it was to sit in the front seat. She was seven. It was laminated.


I used to find that annoying. Now I understand it differently. She takes care of the people she loves by making sure nothing falls through the cracks. That is not control. That is love with a spreadsheet.


\[Partner’s Name\], you are marrying someone who will remember every important date, notice when you are quiet, and handle the hard things before you know they are happening. Do not take that for granted, and I do not think you will, because I have watched how you look at her.


To my sister and \[Partner’s Name\]. Cheers.


## Father of the Bride Speech Examples


The father of the bride speech is traditionally the first of the evening. It sets the emotional tone. Keep it warm, personal, and brief. Welcome the new partner into the family, thank the guests, and give the couple your blessing.


At a Glance Father of the Bride


Typical length 3-5 minutes (400-600 words)


Position in the order First. You open the block and set the tone.


Tone Warm, proud, a little emotional. Humor is welcome but not required.


Must include A welcome to all guests, one memory of your daughter, an explicit welcome to her partner, a blessing


Avoid Money talk, advice that sounds like instructions, jokes about “losing” a daughter


Common mistake Talking about your daughter for four minutes and mentioning her partner once, at the end


Example 1: Traditional Father of the Bride


Good evening, everyone. For those I have not had the pleasure of meeting, I am \[Name\], \[Bride’s\] father, and the proudest person in this room tonight.


I have watched \[Bride\] grow from someone who needed me for everything into someone who needs me for very little, and that is exactly how it is supposed to go. She is kind, she is capable, and she has always known her own mind. I cannot take full credit for that, but I will take some.


\[Partner’s Name\], I want you to know that you are not just gaining a spouse today. You are gaining a family that will show up for you, celebrate with you, and occasionally give you unsolicited advice. We come as a package. I hope that is acceptable.


To my daughter and her partner: your mother and I wish you a life full of the things that matter. Good health, real laughter, and the kind of love that gets better with time.


Please raise your glasses. To \[Bride\] and \[Partner’s Name\].


Example 2: Father of the Bride, Blended Family


Good evening. I am \[Name\], and I am lucky enough to be one of the people who helped raise \[Bride\].


I want to start by thanking every person in this room who had a hand in getting her here. Her mother, her stepmother, her grandparents, the teachers and friends who showed up along the way. It took a lot of people, and none of us did it alone.


Here is what I remember most. \[Bride\] was \[age\] when she decided she was going to \[specific childhood ambition\]. Everyone told her it would be difficult. She did it anyway, and she has been doing exactly that ever since. She decides, and then she does it.


\[Partner’s Name\], you have been part of this family for a while now, and today just makes it official on paper. You have been steady, you have been kind to her, and you have been patient with all of us. Welcome, formally.


To \[Bride\] and \[Partner’s Name\]: may you keep choosing each other, on the easy days and the difficult ones. Please raise your glasses.


## Father of the Groom Speech Examples


The father of the groom speech is shorter than the father of the bride speech and serves a slightly different purpose: thank the hosts, welcome the other family, and offer a blessing. If the bride’s family hosted, acknowledging that directly is the traditional courtesy.


At a Glance Father of the Groom


Typical length 2-4 minutes (250-450 words)


Position in the order Second, after the father or parents of the bride


Tone Gracious and appreciative. This is the thank-you speech.


Must include Thanks to the hosts, a welcome to the new family, one line about your son, a blessing


Avoid Repeating what the previous speaker just said, going longer than the bride’s father did


Common mistake Treating it as a second best man speech


Example: Father of the Groom


Good evening. I am \[Name\], \[Groom’s\] father, and I will be brief, because \[previous speaker\] said most of it better than I could.


First, thank you to \[Bride’s parents\] for tonight, for your generosity, and for raising a daughter we are genuinely thrilled to welcome into our family. That is not a formality. We mean it.


\[Groom\], I have watched you work hard for everything you have. You have never asked anyone to make it easier for you. I have always admired that, even when I wished you would ask for help occasionally.


\[Bride’s Name\], you have made him happier than I have seen him. A parent does not need much more than that.


To the two of you: build something that lasts, be kind to each other on the ordinary days, and come home for dinner now and then. Please raise your glasses.


## Mother of the Bride and Mother of the Groom Speech Examples


Mothers giving speeches used to be the exception. It is now common, and often the most affecting speech of the night. There is no separate etiquette to learn: the structure is the same as any parent speech. Speak to your child, welcome their partner explicitly, and thank the room.


At a Glance Mother of the Bride or Groom


Typical length 2-4 minutes (250-450 words)


Position in the order Either alongside your partner in the opening slot, or as your own speech in the first two positions


Tone Personal and specific. Parent speeches earn the right to be sentimental.


Must include One memory that is genuinely yours, a direct welcome to the new partner, a wish for their marriage


Avoid Advice about how to be married, comparisons to your own marriage, anything about grandchildren


Common mistake Reading a list of childhood milestones instead of telling one story well


Example 1: Mother of the Bride


Good evening, everyone. I am \[Name\], and I am \[Bride’s\] mother.


Thank you for being here. Some of you traveled a long way, and I know what that costs in time and effort. It means a great deal to us that you came.


When \[Bride\] was small, she used to narrate everything she did out loud, as if someone were taking notes. I remember thinking she was going to be someone who needed an audience. I was wrong about that. What she actually needed was to understand things by explaining them, and she still does it, and it is one of my favorite things about her.


\[Partner’s Name\], the first time you came to our house, you asked me a real question and then listened to the whole answer. I noticed. I have noticed a hundred things since then. You are exactly the kind of person I hoped she would find.


My wish for you both is not that it will always be easy, because it will not be. My wish is that you will keep turning toward each other when it is not.


Please raise your glasses. To \[Bride\] and \[Partner’s Name\].


Example 2: Mother of the Groom


Good evening. I am \[Name\], \[Groom’s\] mother, and I have been looking forward to this for a very long time.


To \[Bride’s\] family: thank you for welcoming us so warmly, and for raising someone who has become genuinely important to our family, not just to my son.


\[Groom\], you were a serious child. You read instruction manuals for fun. You asked questions that I could not answer. Somewhere along the way you turned all of that into a person people rely on, and I am prouder of that than of anything on your resume.


\[Bride’s Name\], you brought out a side of him I had not seen since he was very young. He is lighter with you. He laughs more. Thank you for that, sincerely.


To both of you: may your home be the place you are both most glad to return to. Please raise your glasses.

## Groom Speech Examples


The groom’s speech is a thank-you speech with one personal moment at the end. Thank the hosts, the families, the wedding party, and the guests who traveled, then say something real to your partner. Two to four minutes is plenty.


At a Glance Groom


Typical length 2-4 minutes (250-450 words)


Position in the order Near the end, before or alongside the bride


Tone Grateful, warm, direct. Nerves are fine and the room is on your side.


Must include Thanks to both families, thanks to the wedding party, thanks to travelers, one line to your partner


Avoid Trying to be funnier than the best man, thanking a long list by name and forgetting someone


Common mistake Making it entirely logistics and gratitude with nothing personal in it


Example 1: Groom, Simple and Sincere


I will be honest: I have rewritten this speech four times. Every version sounded either too formal or too much like a toast at a work event. So I am going to keep it simple.


Thank you. To our families, who gave us everything. To our friends, who showed up. To the people who traveled far to be here: we do not take that lightly.


\[Partner’s Name\], I have been trying to figure out how to say what I want to say, and I keep coming back to this: you make ordinary days feel significant. That is not a small thing. That is everything.


I am the luckiest person in this room. Please raise your glasses. To my partner, my best friend, and the person I get to spend my life with. Cheers.


Example 2: Groom, With a Story


Good evening, everyone, and thank you for being here. I want to thank both of our families, our wedding party for carrying us through the last year, and everyone who got on a plane to be in this room.


I want to tell you about a Tuesday. Not a big moment, not the proposal. A Tuesday about two years ago, when I had a bad day and came home and did not want to talk about it. \[Partner’s Name\] did not ask me any questions. She made dinner, put on something terrible on television, and sat next to me for three hours.


That was the day I knew. Not because of anything she said, but because of how easy it was to be a mess in front of her.


\[Partner’s Name\], I am not going to promise you a perfect life. I am going to promise you that when it is not perfect, I will be sitting next to you.


Please raise your glasses. To my wife. Cheers.


## Bride Speech Examples


Brides speak at most weddings now, either alongside their partner or on their own. The structure mirrors the groom’s speech: gratitude first, then one genuine thing said out loud to the person you married.


At a Glance Bride


Typical length 2-4 minutes (250-450 words)


Position in the order Last, closing the block with the final toast


Tone Whatever is actually true for you. Funny, tender, or plainspoken all work.


Must include Thanks to families and guests, acknowledgment of your wedding party, one line to your partner


Avoid Apologizing for being emotional, reading a long thank-you list, running past four minutes


Common mistake Assuming you do not need to prepare because you will “just speak from the heart”


Example 1: Bride, Gratitude First
I want to start by saying thank you. To everyone in this room for traveling, for celebrating with us, and for making today feel like the most loved we have ever been.


To our wedding party: you have been extraordinary. You showed up for every moment, every errand, every late-night panic. We see you and we are so grateful.


To our parents: everything good about us started with you. Thank you for teaching us what love looks like in practice, not just in words, but in the daily choices you made for your families.


And to \[Partner’s Name\]: I did not know it was possible to feel this sure about anything. You are my favorite person to talk to, to travel with, to disagree with, and to come home to. I cannot wait to see what we build together.


To everyone here: thank you for being part of the beginning of our story. Cheers.


Example 2: Bride, Short and Direct
I am going to keep this short, because I would rather spend the evening talking to all of you than talking at you.


Thank you to our families for everything you have done, most of which we will never fully know about. Thank you to our friends for showing up in every sense of the phrase.


\[Partner’s Name\], I used to think the goal was to find someone who agreed with me. It turns out the goal was to find someone I trust when we disagree. That is you, and I did not know to look for it.


Please raise your glasses. To all of you, to this ridiculous and wonderful day, and to my husband. Cheers.


## Short Wedding Speech Examples (Under 2 Minutes)


Not every speech needs to be long. A short, well-crafted toast can be more memorable than a rambling five-minute speech. These work for anyone giving a brief toast at the rehearsal dinner, the reception, or a smaller celebration.


Short Toast: Universal
I have known \[Name\] for \[X\] years, and in that time I have watched them become someone I am genuinely proud to call my friend. \[Partner’s Name\], you are the reason for that. Thank you for choosing each other. To the couple: cheers.


Short Toast: Sibling
Growing up, I always thought I knew everything about my \[brother/sister\]. Then I watched them fall in love, and I realized I had only seen part of who they are. \[Partner’s Name\] brought out the rest. I am grateful for that every day. To \[Name\] and \[Partner’s Name\]: cheers.


Short Toast: Parent
I have had \[number\] years of watching \[Name\] become the person standing here tonight, and I have loved every one of them. \[Partner’s Name\], welcome to our family. You are exactly what we hoped for. To both of you: cheers.


Short Toast: Longtime Friend of the Couple
I am in the unusual position of having been friends with both of them before they were friends with each other. I would like everyone to know that I take full credit for this. To \[Name\] and \[Partner’s Name\]: you make more sense together than either of you did apart. Cheers.


Short Toast: Rehearsal Dinner
Tomorrow is the big day, so tonight I only want to say one thing. Every person at this table is here because you two chose them. That says something about you, and it says something about the life you are building. To \[Name\] and \[Partner’s Name\]: sleep well, and we will see you tomorrow. Cheers.


## Wedding Speech Timeline: When to Write, Practice, and Deliver


Most bad wedding speeches are not written badly. They are written late. The speech that gets drafted the night before is almost always the one that runs long, wanders, and leans on generic praise because there was no time to find the real story.


Here is a schedule that works. It assumes about three hours of total effort, spread out so your brain has time to do the useful background work between sessions.


When What to Do Time Needed


**8 weeks out** Confirm you are speaking, how long you have, and where you sit in the order. Ask the couple if anything is off-limits. 10 minutes


**6 weeks out** Collect raw material. Open a note on your phone and add memories as they occur to you. Do not write yet, just gather. Ongoing, 5 minutes at a time


**4 weeks out** Pick one story. Write a messy first draft in one sitting without editing. Aim for 600-800 words and expect to cut. 45 minutes


**3 weeks out** Cut to 400-600 words. Delete anything that needs explaining. Read it aloud once and time it. 30 minutes


**2 weeks out** Send it to one trusted person, ideally someone who knows the couple. Ask specifically: is anything here unclear, unkind, or too long? 15 minutes


**1 week out** Final version. Print two copies on note cards in large type. Memorize your first line and your last line. 30 minutes


**3 days out** Practice out loud, standing, three times. Time each run. If it is over your limit, cut, do not speed up. 20 minutes


**Day before** One run-through. Put a printed copy in the pocket of whatever you are wearing tomorrow. Do not rewrite tonight. 10 minutes


**Wedding day** Read it once quietly in the morning. Then leave it alone until you are called up. 5 minutes


**If you have one week, not eight:** Do the 4-week step tonight (messy draft, one story, no editing), the 3-week cut tomorrow, and practice out loud three times over the remaining days. The single non-negotiable step is reading it out loud standing up. Skipping that is what causes speeches to run double their intended length.


### Wedding Day Timing: What Happens When


Knowing the shape of the evening keeps you calm. Here is the typical sequence at a reception with a seated dinner:


Approximate Time What Is Happening What Speakers Should Do


Cocktail hour Guests arrive, drinks are served Limit yourself to one drink. Locate the microphone and the person running the order.


Guests seated Couple enters, first course begins Confirm the running order has not changed. It often has.


After the main course Plates cleared, glasses topped up This is usually when the speech block starts. Have your cards in hand.


Speech block (20-25 min) Speakers go in order Stand where the couple can see you. Do not start until the room is quiet.


Dessert and cake Energy shifts, guests start moving Speeches are over. Do not add one now.


Dancing Music takes over Hand your printed copy to someone who can keep it. The couple often wants it later.


## How to Write Your Own Wedding Speech: Step by Step


1. **Start with one story.** Do not try to summarize the entire relationship. Pick one specific memory that captures who they are: a moment, a conversation, a decision they made that told you everything.
2. **Write the way you talk.** Read your draft aloud. If it sounds like an essay, rewrite it. The best speeches sound like a conversation.
3. **Keep it to 300-600 words.** At a comfortable speaking pace (about 130-150 words per minute), that is 2-4 minutes. Time yourself.
4. **Address both partners by name.** Even if you only know one of them well, acknowledge the other directly. It matters.
5. **Practice out loud at least three times.** Not in your head, out loud, standing up, at speaking pace. You will catch awkward phrases you would miss on paper.
6. **End with a clear toast line.** “Please raise your glasses” is your signal. Have your closing line memorized so you can deliver it with confidence.


## Opening Lines That Work


The first 15 seconds set the tone for everything that follows. Here are opening lines that work for different roles and styles:


- “I have known \[Name\] for \[X\] years, and in that time I have learned three things about them.” (Best man or maid of honor: sets up a list structure)
- “When \[Name\] asked me to speak today, I said yes immediately. Then I spent three weeks regretting it.” (Funny: relatable and self-aware)
- “There are people in your life who make you better just by being in it. \[Name\] is one of those people.” (Warm: works for any role)
- “I am \[Name\]’s \[role\], which means I have a front-row seat to one of the best love stories I know.” (Parent: sets up a narrative)
- “I have been trying to figure out what to say tonight, and I keep coming back to one word: grateful.” (Bride or groom: honest and direct)
- “I am going to tell you about a Tuesday.” (Any role: an unexpected opener that earns attention immediately)
- “There are two versions of this speech. The one I wrote, and the one I would have given if \[Name\] were not sitting eight feet away.” (Funny: works if you follow it with something genuinely kind)


## Wedding Speech Quotes and Toast Lines


These are original lines written to be borrowed. Use them as a closing toast, an opening hook, or the sentence that carries the emotional weight of your speech. Change the wording so it sounds like you, and never use more than one per speech: a stack of quotable lines reads as a greeting card rather than a person speaking.


### Heartfelt


- “Love is not the part where you find each other. It is the ten thousand ordinary days you choose each other afterward.”
- “The best marriages are not built on grand gestures. They are built on being the first person the other one wants to tell.”

- “A good partner does not remove the difficult parts of life. They just make you feel less alone inside them.”

### Funny

### Short and Classic

## What Not to Say in a Wedding Speech


Speeches rarely fail because of what was missing. They fail because of one line that should have been cut. Almost every wedding speech disaster falls into one of the categories below, and every one of them is avoidable in a single editing pass.


### The Hard No List


Do Not Say Why It Fails Say This Instead


Anything about an ex-partner There is no version of this that lands. It embarrasses the couple and puts the room on edge. Talk about what changed when they met their partner, without a comparison


“I am not good at speeches” Signals the audience to lower expectations before you have said anything Open directly with your name and your relationship to the couple


Bachelor or bachelorette party details Grandparents, colleagues, and children are in the room “We had a very good weekend, and that is all anyone needs to know”


Jokes about the marriage failing The “ball and chain” genre reads as bitter, not funny, to most of the room Humor about the couple’s quirks, not about marriage as an institution


Anything about money Cost of the wedding, who paid, salaries, or the value of gifts Thank the hosts for their generosity without naming figures


References to pregnancy or grandchildren You may be wrong, and even if you are right it is not your announcement Wish them a full life together and stop there


Reading a list of everyone’s names Loses the room in twenty seconds and you will still forget someone Thank groups: families, wedding party, everyone who traveled


Inside jokes for five people The other 145 guests sit in silence, which changes the energy of the whole speech Explain the context in one sentence, or cut the joke


Comparisons between the two families Even a compliment framed as a comparison creates a loser Say something specific and warm about each family separately


“I will keep this short” (then not doing it) The room starts counting. You have set a timer against yourself. Just be short. Do not announce it.


### Subjects to Handle Carefully, Not Avoid


Some topics are not off-limits, but they need to be handled deliberately rather than improvised:


- **A parent or loved one who has died.** One sentence, placed early, delivered plainly. “I know \[Name\] would have loved to be here tonight.” Do not build the speech around it, and do not skip it if the family would notice the absence.
- **Divorced or estranged parents.** Acknowledge everyone in a group phrase rather than individually. Check with the couple beforehand on names and titles: guessing at how to refer to a step-parent is the most common source of hurt feelings in parent speeches.
- **Faith and religion.** Follow the couple’s lead. If the ceremony was religious, a blessing fits. If it was not, keep it universal.
- **The couple’s difficult year.** Illness, job loss, a long-distance stretch. Only include it if it is genuinely part of their story and only if you have asked. What reads as resilience to you may read as private to them.
- **Politics, work grievances, and current events.** There is no upside. Cut them.


### Delivery Mistakes That Undo a Good Speech


- **Drinking to settle your nerves.** One drink is calming. Three is detectable from every seat in the room. Hold off until after you have spoken.
- **Reading from your phone.** The screen dims, the lighting is wrong, and it looks like you are checking messages. Use printed note cards in large type.
- **Going over time.** A five-minute speech at four minutes is charming. At nine minutes it is a problem the couple will remember.
- **Explaining a joke that did not land.** Move on immediately. The room forgets in four seconds if you do.
- **Adding “one more thing” after the toast.** The toast is the ending. Once glasses are up, you are finished.
- **Turning your back on the couple.** Stand so they are in your eyeline. Most of the speech should be delivered to them, not to the room.


**The one-question edit:** Read your final draft and ask, for every line, “would I be comfortable if the couple read this before the wedding?” If the answer is no anywhere, cut that line. A speech that only works as a surprise is a speech that should not be given.


### Make Sure Every Guest Knows When Speeches Start


A great speech deserves a great audience, and that comes down to logistics. Publish the reception timeline on your wedding website so speakers know their slot and guests are seated when the block begins. Managing RSVPs and the schedule in one place also means late changes reach everyone automatically, which matters more than most couples expect on the day. ## FAQ: Wedding Speeches


### What do you say for a wedding speech?


A wedding speech typically includes an introduction (who you are and your relationship to the couple), a personal story or memory, warm wishes for the couple’s future, and a toast. Aim for 2-4 minutes (300-600 words). Keep it heartfelt, specific, and positive.


### What is the order of speeches at a wedding?


The traditional order is: father of the bride, father of the groom, best man, maid of honor, groom, then bride. Most modern weddings shorten this to three or four speeches. The order matters because it controls the emotional arc of the evening: welcome first, humor in the middle, gratitude at the end.


### When during the reception should speeches happen?


After the main course and before dessert. Guests have eaten, the room is seated and attentive, and the bar has not been open long enough for attention to fade. Avoid cocktail hour (guests are standing and distracted) and anything after dancing has started.


### What is the best opening line for a wedding speech?


The best opening lines introduce yourself, establish your relationship to the couple, and hook the audience immediately. Try: “I have known \[Name\] for \[X\] years, and in that time I have watched them become the person standing here today.” Avoid opening with “I am not good at speeches,” which tells the room to expect less.


### How long should a wedding speech be?


Most wedding speeches run 2-4 minutes (300-600 words at a comfortable pace). Best man and maid of honor speeches typically run 3-5 minutes. Bride and groom speeches can be shorter. Avoid going over 5 minutes, since guests’ attention fades quickly. Budget 20-25 minutes for the entire speech block.


### How many speeches should there be at a wedding?


Four is the sweet spot and six is the ceiling. Total time matters more than the number of speakers: five four-minute speeches work better than two eight-minute ones. If more people want to speak, move the extra speeches to the rehearsal dinner or welcome party.


### Does the mother of the bride give a speech?


Often, yes. Mothers speaking at weddings is now common, either alongside their partner in the opening slot or as a separate speech. The structure is the same as any parent speech: one real memory, an explicit welcome to the new partner, and a wish for the marriage. Keep it to 2-4 minutes.


### What is the 50/30/20 rule for wedding speeches?


The 50/30/20 rule suggests spending 50% of your speech on the person you know best, 30% on the couple together, and 20% on the guests and occasion. It keeps the speech personal without ignoring the new partner or the celebration itself.


### When should I start writing my wedding speech?


Start collecting memories about six weeks out and write your first draft four weeks before the wedding. That leaves time to cut it down, get one round of feedback, and practice out loud. Total effort is around three hours. Speeches written the night before are the ones that run long and rely on generic praise.


### Can I use quotes in a wedding speech?


Yes, but use one at most, and place it where it carries weight: usually the closing toast. A speech built from borrowed lines feels like a greeting card. Adapt the wording so it sounds like something you would actually say out loud.


### What should you never say in a wedding speech?


Never mention ex-partners, bachelor or bachelorette party details, money, pregnancy speculation, or jokes about the marriage failing. Skip inside jokes that only a handful of guests understand, and do not compare the two families. If you would not be comfortable with the couple reading a line beforehand, cut it.


### Who gives speeches at a wedding?


Traditional wedding speeches come from the best man, maid of honor, father of the bride, and the couple themselves. Modern weddings often include the mother of the bride or groom, a sibling, or close friends. Blended families, two brides, or two grooms all call for a custom order, which is completely standard.
