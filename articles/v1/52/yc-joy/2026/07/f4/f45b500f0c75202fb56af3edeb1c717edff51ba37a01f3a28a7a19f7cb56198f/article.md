# How Many Hotel Rooms to Block for a Wedding: The Complete Guide

Last Updated on July 23, 2026 by[Joy Editors](https://withjoy.com/)


> **Quick answer:** Block rooms for roughly 30–50% of your total guest count, adjusted for how many are traveling from out of town. For a 100-guest wedding with 60 out-of-town guests, plan for 20–30 rooms. Use a courtesy block (10–20 rooms) if you want no financial commitment, or a contracted block for better rates and guaranteed availability.


Hotel room blocks are one of the most practical things you can do for your wedding guests, and one of the most confusing to set up. How many rooms do you actually need? What happens if guests do not fill the block? When should you book?


This guide answers all of it, including a general framework for estimating your needs and a table showing recommended room counts by wedding size.


## How to Estimate Your Room Block


There is no single correct number. The right block size depends on your guest list, how many people are traveling, and what kind of block you are setting up. Here is a general framework to work from:


- **Start with your out-of-town guests.** Look at your guest list and count everyone who will need to travel to your wedding. This is your starting number, not total headcount.
- **Assume most guests will share rooms.** Couples, families, and friends traveling together will typically book one room between them. For most weddings, you can roughly cut your out-of-town guest count in half to estimate room needs.
- **Not everyone will use your block.** Some guests will stay with local family, book their own hotels, or choose Airbnb. Expect around 70–80% of your out-of-town guests to actually book through your block.
- **Destination weddings need more rooms.** When nearly every guest is flying in, your room count will be much higher than a local wedding of the same size.
- **Build in a small buffer for contracted blocks.** If you are signing a contract with an attrition clause, estimate conservatively. It is better to add rooms later than to pay for rooms that go unfilled.


For example, a 100-guest wedding with 60 out-of-town guests might need somewhere in the range of 20–30 rooms. A smaller local wedding with only 20 travelers might only need 8–12. Use the table below as a starting reference, then adjust based on what you know about your guest list.

## Courtesy Block vs Contracted Block: Which Should You Choose?


### Courtesy Block


A courtesy block (also called a “soft block”) is an informal agreement where the hotel holds a set number of rooms for your guests. There is no financial commitment: if guests do not fill the rooms, you owe nothing. The tradeoff is that rooms are released back to general inventory on a rolling basis, and the hotel can cancel the arrangement with little notice.


Courtesy blocks typically range from 10–20 rooms. They work well for smaller weddings or when you are unsure how many guests will need accommodations.


### Contracted Block


A contracted block is a formal agreement with guaranteed room availability and a negotiated rate. The hotel holds the rooms until a cutoff date (usually 30 days before the wedding). The catch: most contracts include an attrition clause, meaning you owe a penalty if guests do not fill a minimum percentage of the block (typically 80–90%).


To protect yourself: negotiate the attrition threshold down to 70–75%, and set the contracted block size at 80% of what you actually expect to need. This gives you a buffer without overcommitting.


**Watch out for attrition clauses.** If your contract requires 80% pickup and only 60% of rooms are booked, you may owe the hotel for the difference. Always read the attrition terms before signing, and negotiate the threshold before you commit.


## How Many Hotel Blocks Should You Set Up?


Two to three hotels is the right range for most weddings. The standard approach:


- **One mid-range option** : the primary block, closest to the venue, accessible price point
- **One budget option** : for guests who want to spend less or are staying longer
- **One upscale option** (optional): for guests who want a nicer stay or are making a weekend of it


Offering multiple price points increases overall pickup and reduces the risk that any single block goes unfilled. It also accommodates the range of budgets in a typical guest list.


## When to Book Your Hotel Room Block


Timing depends on your wedding date and location:


- **9–12 months out:** Peak season (May through October), popular cities, destination weddings, holiday weekends
- **6–9 months out:** Off-peak dates, smaller markets, weekday weddings
- **Less than 6 months:** Risky for popular hotels: availability may be limited and rates higher


Book earlier than you think you need to. Hotels fill up on popular wedding weekends, and the best rates go to couples who commit early.


> **Pro tip:** Contact 3–5 hotels at the same time and compare their offers. Mention that you are comparing options: hotels will often improve their initial offer on rate or attrition terms when they know you are shopping around.


## Does It Cost Money to Block Hotel Rooms?


For courtesy blocks: no. There is no upfront cost, and you do not pay if rooms go unfilled.


For contracted blocks: some hotels require a deposit, which may or may not be refundable. You also take on financial risk through the attrition clause. Ask specifically: Is the deposit refundable? Can it be applied to room revenue after the event? What is the attrition penalty structure?


Guests book and pay for their own rooms. You are not paying for their accommodations. You are only financially responsible if you have a contracted block and guests do not fill it to the minimum threshold.

## Frequently Asked Questions


### Should you block hotel rooms for a wedding?


Yes, if you have out-of-town guests. A hotel block guarantees availability at a negotiated rate, which saves guests money and reduces the stress of finding accommodations. Even a small courtesy block (10–15 rooms) is worth setting up for weddings with 20 or more traveling guests.


### What is the 50/30/20 rule for weddings?


The 50/30/20 rule is a general budget guideline: allocate roughly 50% of your wedding budget to the venue and catering, 30% to photography, music, flowers, and decor, and 20% to everything else (attire, transportation, stationery, honeymoon). It is a starting framework, not a hard rule: priorities vary by couple.


### How far in advance should I block hotel rooms for a wedding?


9–12 months ahead for peak season dates and popular cities. 6–9 months is workable for off-peak or smaller markets. The earlier you book, the better your rate and room type selection.


### What happens if guests do not fill the hotel block?


For courtesy blocks: nothing. Rooms are released back to general inventory. For contracted blocks: you may owe an attrition penalty for rooms below the minimum pickup threshold. Always negotiate the attrition clause before signing and set your contracted block size conservatively.


### How many hotels should I block for a wedding?


Two to three hotels is the right range for most weddings. Offer a mid-range option near the venue, a budget option for cost-conscious guests, and optionally an upscale choice. Multiple price points increase overall pickup and reduce attrition risk.
