# Pet-Inclusive Weddings: How to Include Your Dog (or Cat) in Your Big Day

Last Updated on July 21, 2026 by[Joy Editors](https://withjoy.com/)


**Quick answer:** To include your pet in your wedding, start by confirming your venue allows animals, then assign a dedicated pet handler for the day. Most couples include dogs in the ceremony as ring bearers or walk-down companions, then have the handler take the pet home before the reception. Plan logistics early: temperament check, vet visit, rehearsal with the pet, and a backup plan if your pet gets overwhelmed.


More couples are including their pets in weddings than ever before. Dogs walk down the aisle as ring bearers, cats make cameo appearances in getting-ready photos, and some couples build their entire venue search around pet-friendliness. But pulling it off smoothly requires real planning: not every venue allows animals, not every pet handles crowds well, and not every wedding timeline works with a four-legged guest.


This guide covers everything you need to know to include your pet in your wedding, from venue logistics to day-of handling to the backup plan you should have ready.


## Is a Pet-Inclusive Wedding Right for You?


Before diving into logistics, honestly assess whether including your pet is the right call. Consider:


- **Temperament:** Is your pet comfortable around large groups of people, loud music, and unfamiliar environments? A dog that gets anxious at the park may not thrive at a 150-person wedding.
- **The timeline:** Most pets do best for a short window (30 to 60 minutes). Plan their appearance for a specific moment, not the entire day.
- **Guest comfort:** Some guests have allergies or fear of animals. A pet-friendly wedding should accommodate everyone.
- **Venue restrictions:** Many venues prohibit animals entirely. This needs to be confirmed before you book.


If your pet is social, calm in new environments, and you have someone who can handle them, including them in the wedding can be one of the most memorable and photographed moments of the day.


## How to Find a Pet-Friendly Wedding Venue


Not all venues that say “pet-friendly” mean it the same way. Some allow pets outdoors only. Others require a pet deposit or insurance rider. A few welcome animals everywhere on the property.


When evaluating venues, ask:


- Are pets allowed during the ceremony, the reception, or both?
- Are there breed or size restrictions?
- Is there a pet deposit or additional fee?
- Are there designated outdoor areas where pets can take breaks?
- Does the venue require proof of vaccinations or a pet liability waiver?


Outdoor venues (gardens, farms, estates, beaches) tend to be more accommodating than indoor ballrooms or historic buildings. If your venue does not allow pets during the event, some couples arrange for the pet to appear for photos only, before guests arrive.


When you find a venue that works, share all the details on your[wedding website](https://withjoy.com/) so guests know what to expect. If pets will be present during the ceremony, a brief note on your website helps guests with allergies or concerns plan accordingly.


## Roles Your Pet Can Play


The most common ways to include pets in weddings:

## The Pet Handler: Your Most Important Hire


A dedicated pet handler is non-negotiable. This is someone whose only job is managing your pet from arrival to departure. They are not a bridesmaid doubling as a dog-sitter. They are not a groomsman who “does not mind.” They are a person with one responsibility: your pet.


Options for pet handlers:


- **Professional pet attendant:** Some pet-sitting services specialize in wedding day care. They handle transport, grooming touch-ups, feeding, bathroom breaks, and departure. Cost: $150 to $500 depending on hours and location.
- **Trusted friend or family member:** Someone who knows your pet, is not in the wedding party, and is genuinely willing to leave the ceremony early if needed.
- **Dog walker or pet sitter you already use:** Your pet already trusts them, which reduces anxiety.


The handler should attend the rehearsal with the pet, know the exit plan, and have your vet’s number saved.


## Planning the Day-of Timeline for Your Pet


Most pets should be on-site for 1 to 2 hours maximum. Build a specific timeline:


**Sample pet timeline:**


- **2 hours before ceremony:** Pet arrives with handler. Walk, bathroom break, settle in.
- **30 minutes before:** Getting-ready photos with pet (if desired).
- **Ceremony:** Pet walks down the aisle or sits with handler in front row.
- **Immediately after ceremony:** Couple + pet photos (10 to 15 minutes).
- **Before cocktail hour:** Handler takes pet home or to boarding. Pet’s job is done.


This keeps your pet’s time short, reduces stress, and avoids the chaos of a dog at a crowded reception with loud music and food everywhere.


Share the full wedding schedule, including when the pet will be present, on your[wedding website](https://withjoy.com/) so guests and vendors know the plan.


## What to Pack for Your Pet


Create a pet day-of kit:


- Leash and collar (plus a backup)
- Water bowl and water
- Treats (high-value, for bribery during photos)
- Poop bags
- A towel or blanket for resting
- Any calming aids your vet recommends (not sedatives unless prescribed)
- A lint roller for post-cuddle wardrobe emergencies
- The pet’s outfit or accessories (floral collar, bow tie, bandana)


## Pet Attire and Accessories


Keep it comfortable. Your pet should be able to move, breathe, and see normally in whatever they wear. Popular options:


- **Floral collar or garland:** Coordinate with your florist. Use pet-safe flowers only (avoid lilies, baby’s breath, and chrysanthemums, which are toxic to dogs and cats).
- **Bow tie or bandana:** Simple, lightweight, easy to remove if the pet gets uncomfortable.
- **Custom leash:** Personalized with the couple’s names or “Best Dog” for photos.
- **“Ring bearer” sign:** A small sign attached to the collar or carried by the handler.


Test any outfit or accessory at home before the wedding. If your pet tries to remove it within 5 minutes, it is not going to work on the day.


## Rehearsal with Your Pet


If your pet has a role in the ceremony, a rehearsal is essential. Bring them to the rehearsal (or visit the venue beforehand) so they can:


- Get familiar with the space, sounds, and surfaces
- Practice walking down the aisle on leash
- Meet the handler in the venue environment
- Identify any triggers (slippery floors, loud echoes, other animals nearby)


Even calm, well-trained dogs can behave differently in an unfamiliar setting. A rehearsal gives you time to adjust the plan if needed.


## The Backup Plan


Every pet-inclusive wedding needs an exit strategy. Things that can go wrong:


- Your pet gets anxious, barks during vows, or refuses to walk
- Weather changes force the ceremony indoors (where pets may not be allowed)
- A guest has a severe allergy you did not know about
- Your pet has an accident on the venue floor


The backup plan is simple: the handler removes the pet calmly and quietly. No drama, no disruption. This is why the handler must be dedicated to this one role. Have the handler’s car nearby, packed with the pet kit, ready for an early departure at any point.


## Including Pets You Cannot Bring to the Venue


If your venue does not allow animals, or your pet is not suited for crowds, you can still honor them:


- **Photo charm:** Attach a small photo of your pet to your bouquet or boutonniere.
- **Signature cocktail:** Name a drink after your pet.
- **Table cards:** Feature your pet’s photo on escort cards or table numbers.
- **Cake topper:** A custom cake topper that includes your pet.
- **Wedding website feature:** Add a section about your pet on your[wedding website](https://withjoy.com/) , with a photo and a fun bio. Guests love it.


## Guest Communication


Let guests know in advance that a pet will be present. This is both a courtesy (allergies, phobias) and a way to build excitement.


Your[RSVP questions](https://withjoy.com/help/en/articles/8293305-customize-your-rsvp-questions) can include a note about pets being present, and your wedding website is the best place to share the details: which events the pet will attend, how long they will be present, and that a handler is managing them.


For guests with allergies, knowing the pet will only be present for the outdoor ceremony (and not the indoor reception) can make the difference between attending and declining.


## Photography Tips for Pets at Weddings


Pet photos are often the most shared images from a wedding. Set your photographer up for success:


- Brief your photographer on the pet timeline so they are ready
- Schedule dedicated pet portrait time (10 to 15 minutes)
- Have high-value treats available for attention-getting
- Use a squeaker toy behind the camera for ear-perked shots
- Accept candid over posed: the best pet photos happen naturally
- Get shots of the pet with each partner individually, and together


**Pro tip:** Ask your photographer if they have experience with animals. Some photographers love pet photos and know how to capture them; others find it stressful. If pet photos are a priority, look for a photographer who has them in their portfolio.


## Cats, Rabbits, and Other Pets


Dogs get most of the attention, but other pets can be included too:


- **Cats:** Best for getting-ready photos or a brief, controlled appearance. Most cats do not enjoy crowds, so a photo-only role works best. Use a carrier for transport and have a quiet room available.
- **Rabbits:** Can appear in outdoor photos. Keep them in a pen or held by the handler. Avoid loud environments.
- **Horses:** Popular for rustic or farm weddings. The couple arrives or departs on horseback. Requires a professional handler and venue coordination.


For any non-dog pet, prioritize the animal’s comfort over the photo opportunity. A stressed pet does not make for good photos or a good experience.


### Pet-Inclusive Wedding Checklist


- Confirm venue pet policy (fees, restrictions, outdoor areas)
- Book a dedicated pet handler
- Schedule a vet visit (vaccinations current, health check)
- Choose pet’s role and attire
- Test outfit/accessories at home
- Attend rehearsal with pet
- Pack a pet day-of kit
- Brief photographer on pet timeline
- Communicate pet presence to guests via wedding website
- Confirm backup/exit plan with handler
- Arrange post-ceremony pet transport home

## Frequently Asked Questions


### How do you include a dog in your wedding ceremony?


The most common way is as a ring bearer or aisle escort. Assign a dedicated handler to walk the dog down the aisle on a leash. Keep the dog’s appearance short (ceremony and photos only) and have the handler take them home before the reception.


### How much does a wedding pet handler cost?


Professional pet attendants for weddings typically charge $150 to $500, depending on hours, travel, and services. Some include grooming, transport, and boarding. A trusted friend or your regular pet sitter can fill this role for less.


### What flowers are safe for pets at weddings?


Roses, sunflowers, snapdragons, zinnias, and orchids are generally safe for dogs and cats. Avoid lilies (toxic to cats), baby’s breath, chrysanthemums, tulips, and daffodils. Always confirm with your florist and vet before putting flowers on your pet.


### Can you include a cat in your wedding?


Yes, but cats typically do best in short, controlled appearances: getting-ready photos or a brief portrait session in a quiet room. Most cats do not enjoy large crowds, loud music, or unfamiliar outdoor spaces. A photo-only role is the safest approach.


### What if my pet does not cooperate during the ceremony?


This is why you need a dedicated handler and a backup plan. If the pet gets anxious, barks, or refuses to walk, the handler quietly removes them. Brief your officiant and wedding party on this plan so everyone stays calm if it happens. The best pet wedding moments are often the unscripted ones.
