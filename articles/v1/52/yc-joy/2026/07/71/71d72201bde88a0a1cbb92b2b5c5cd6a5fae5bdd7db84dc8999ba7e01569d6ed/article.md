## What Is a Save the Date?


A save the date is the first formal communication your guests receive about your wedding. It is not the invitation — it is the advance notice that tells people your names, wedding date, and general location so they can block the date on their calendars, request time off work, and start planning travel.


Save the dates go out before formal invitations and typically include:


- Both partners’ names
- The wedding date
- The city or venue location (full address is not required yet)
- A note that a formal invitation will follow
- Your wedding website URL (so guests can find details as you add them)


Save the dates come in many formats: printed cards, magnets, postcards, digital designs sent via email or text, and even creative options like bottle openers or custom illustrations. The format you choose sets the tone for your entire wedding — a foil-pressed card signals a formal celebration, while a playful digital design suggests a more relaxed affair.


## When Should You Send Save the Dates?


Timing depends on your wedding type:


- **Local weddings:** 6 to 8 months before the wedding date.
- **Destination weddings:** 9 to 12 months in advance, giving guests extra time to book flights, accommodations, and passports.
- **Holiday weekend weddings:** 9 to 12 months out — holiday travel fills up fast and guests need lead time to adjust plans.


If your wedding is less than 4 months away, skip the save the date and send formal invitations directly. Sending save the dates too early (more than 12 months out) risks guests forgetting or details changing before the wedding.

## When to Send Save the Dates: A Complete Timeline


Getting the timing right ensures guests have enough notice without sending so early that your save the date gets lost or forgotten.


### Local Weddings


Send save the dates 6 to 8 months before the wedding. Most guests will be driving or taking a short flight, so they need enough time to request days off and arrange accommodations, but not so much that the date feels distant.


### Destination Weddings


Send save the dates 9 to 12 months in advance. Guests need extra time to book international or domestic flights, apply for passports or visas, arrange extended time off, and budget for travel expenses. The earlier notice also helps them take advantage of lower airfares.


### Holiday Weekend Weddings


Send save the dates 9 to 12 months out. Holiday weekends (Memorial Day, Labor Day, Fourth of July, Thanksgiving weekend) are popular travel times, and guests may already have competing plans. Early notice helps them prioritize your celebration.


### Save the Date Timeline Checklist


- **12 months out:** Finalize your wedding date and venue. Begin designing save the dates for destination or holiday weddings.
- **9 to 10 months out:** Send save the dates for destination and holiday weddings.
- **6 to 8 months out:** Send save the dates for local weddings.
- **4 to 6 months out:** Follow up with formal invitations (whether or not you sent save the dates).
- **Under 4 months:** Skip the save the date and send formal invitations directly.

## Frequently Asked Questions


### When should I send save the dates?


Send save the dates 6 to 8 months before the wedding for local celebrations, or 9 to 12 months for destination weddings and holiday weekends. If your wedding is less than 4 months away, skip the save the date and go directly to formal invitations.


### What information should I include on a save the date?


Include both partners’ names, the wedding date, the city or general location (a full address is not necessary yet), a note that a formal invitation will follow, and your wedding website URL. Do not include registry information on save the dates — save that for the invitation and wedding website.


### Do save the dates require an RSVP?


No. Save the dates are informational — they ask guests to hold the date, not to formally respond. The RSVP comes later with the formal invitation, typically 2 to 3 months before the wedding. Joy’s[online RSVP system](https://withjoy.com/online-rsvp) makes it easy to collect responses when the time comes.


### Should I include my wedding website URL on the save the date?


Yes. Your[wedding website](https://withjoy.com/wedding-website) is where guests go for travel details, hotel recommendations, registry links, and event information. Including the URL on the save the date gives guests a place to find answers immediately, and you can update the website as details are finalized.


### Can I send digital save the dates instead of paper?


Absolutely. Digital save the dates are a legitimate, increasingly popular option. They are instant, eco-friendly, budget-friendly, and allow guests to click directly to your wedding website or add the date to their calendar. Joy offers a range of[free and premium digital save the date designs](https://withjoy.com/cards/wedding/save-the-date) .


### Can I change my wedding date after sending save the dates?


Yes, though it requires sending an update to all guests. If you have a wedding website (and you should), update the date there immediately and send a brief email or message to your guest list with the new date. If you sent paper save the dates, a digital update via email is perfectly acceptable for the correction — you do not need to reprint and mail a new card.
