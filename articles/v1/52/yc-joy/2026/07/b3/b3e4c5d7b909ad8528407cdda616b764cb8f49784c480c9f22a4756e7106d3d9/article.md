# What to Put on Your Wedding Website: The Complete Checklist (2026)

Last Updated on July 30, 2026 by[Joy Editors](https://withjoy.com/)


Your wedding website needs seven essential sections: your names and wedding date, ceremony and reception details, RSVP, guest list management, travel and accommodations, registry, your story, and an FAQ. Everything else — dress code, schedule, wedding party, virtual streaming — is optional but recommended for weddings with out-of-town guests or multiple events.


> **Quick answer:** The non-negotiables are your names, wedding date, ceremony time and address, reception time and address, and a working RSVP form. Add travel info and hotel recommendations if more than 20% of your guests are traveling. Add registry links once the registry is live. Everything else depends on your wedding size and complexity.


## The Essential Sections of a Wedding Website


### 1. Names and Wedding Date


Your homepage should display your full names, wedding date, and ceremony time prominently. This is the first thing guests see — make it impossible to miss. Include the day of the week (Saturday, October 11, 2026) so guests can plan travel without having to look up the calendar.


### 2. Ceremony and Reception Details


Include the full address of both the ceremony and reception venues, even if they are at the same location. Add a Google Maps link for each address — do not make guests copy and paste. Include:


- Venue name
- Full street address with zip code
- Google Maps link
- Ceremony start time
- Reception start time (and end time if you have a hard stop)
- Parking instructions (valet, self-park, street parking, nearest garage)


If the ceremony and reception are at different locations, note the distance and travel time between them so guests can plan accordingly.


### 3. RSVP


An online RSVP form is the most important functional element of your wedding website. It saves you from tracking paper cards and gives you a real-time guest count. A good RSVP form collects:


- Guest name(s)
- Attending or not attending
- Meal choice (if applicable)
- Dietary restrictions or allergies
- Plus-one name (if you are allowing plus-ones)


Set a clear RSVP deadline — typically 3 to 4 weeks before the wedding. Display the deadline prominently on the RSVP page. Joy’s wedding website includes a built-in RSVP system that syncs directly with your guest list, so you never have to manually enter responses.


### 4. Manage Your Guest List


A digital guest list is the organizational backbone of your wedding website. Rather than juggling spreadsheets, sticky notes, and email threads, a digital guest list centralizes everything and keeps it automatically synced with your RSVP responses.


With a digital guest list, you can:


- **Track RSVPs in real time.** See who has responded, who hasn’t, and follow up with reminders — all from one dashboard.
- **Manage plus-ones.** Control who gets a plus-one, track their names, and keep your headcount accurate without manual back-and-forth.
- **Collect mailing addresses.** Gather addresses directly from your guests through the website for save the dates, invitations, and thank-you notes.
- **Handle meal selections.** Collect meal choices alongside RSVPs so you can send final counts to your caterer without a separate survey.
- **Keep everything synced.** When a guest RSVPs, their meal choice, plus-one, and attendance status update automatically — no re-entry needed.

### 5. Travel and Accommodations


Your wedding website can serve as a complete travel hub for your guests — especially if you are hosting a destination wedding or have a large number of out-of-town attendees. Go beyond basic hotel info and position this section as a one-stop resource for everything your guests need to get there and stay comfortably.


Include the following:


- **Airport information.** List the nearest airports with airport codes, and note which one is closest to the venue. If there are multiple options, recommend the best one for most guests.
- **Hotel room blocks.** Include the hotel name, a direct booking link or phone number, the group rate, and the deadline to book. Make it clear what happens if they miss the deadline.
- **Additional hotel recommendations.** Offer 2 to 3 alternatives at different price points — budget-friendly, mid-range, and upscale. Include distance from the venue for each.
- **Transportation options.** Cover shuttles, rideshare, rental cars, and whether the venue has on-site parking. If you are arranging group transportation, share the schedule and pickup locations.

### 6. Registry


Link to your registry from your wedding website — this is the standard way to share registry information. Do not include registry links on your invitations (that is considered poor etiquette). Your website is the right place.


Modern registries go well beyond traditional store registries. Today’s couples can mix and match:


- **Traditional store registries.** Classic kitchen, home, and bedding items from major retailers.
- **Cash funds.** Honeymoon funds, house down payment funds, date night funds, or any custom cash fund for experiences that matter to you.
- **Honeymoon registries.** Let guests contribute to specific honeymoon experiences — flights, excursions, hotel upgrades, dinners.
- **External registries.** Already have a registry at another store? Link it alongside your Joy registry so guests can find everything in one place.
- **Charity registries.** Donate to a cause you care about in lieu of traditional gifts.

### 7. Your Story


A short “how we met” section makes your website feel personal rather than purely logistical. It does not need to be long — 2 to 3 paragraphs covering how you met, your relationship, and the proposal is plenty. Include a photo from your engagement session or a candid photo that captures your relationship.


### 8. FAQ


An FAQ section reduces the number of questions you get via text and email. Common questions to answer:


- What is the dress code?
- Are children invited?
- Can I bring a plus-one?
- Is the venue indoors or outdoors?
- What time should I arrive?
- Is there parking at the venue?
- Will there be a shuttle?
- What should I do if I have dietary restrictions?


## Optional Sections Worth Adding


### Wedding Schedule


A day-of timeline helps guests plan their day, especially if there are multiple events (welcome dinner, ceremony, cocktail hour, reception, after-party). Include start times for each event and the location if it changes.


### Wedding Party


A wedding party page does more than list names — it introduces the people who matter most to you as a couple. Go beyond a simple roster and make it a section guests actually enjoy reading.


For each member of your wedding party, consider including:


- **Photos.** Individual portraits or candid shots that capture each person’s personality.
- **Their role.** Bridesmaid, groomsman, maid of honor, best man, flower girl, ring bearer, officiant, or any custom title.
- **A short bio.** Two to three sentences about who they are — not a resume, but a snapshot that helps guests connect.
- **How you know them.** College roommate, childhood best friend, the groom’s sister, coworker-turned-family. These personal details spark conversation at the wedding.


This helps guests become familiar with the wedding party before the big day, making introductions easier and the celebration feel more connected. ### Things to Do


A local guide with restaurant recommendations, activities, and attractions is especially useful for destination weddings or when many guests are traveling from out of town. Include a mix of price points and types of activities.


### Photo Gallery


Engagement photos, candid shots, and photos from your relationship make the website feel alive. Update this section after the wedding with a few professional photos to give guests a way to relive the day.


### Additional Events


If you are hosting a welcome dinner, rehearsal dinner, morning-after brunch, or other events that some or all guests are invited to, list them here with dates, times, locations, and who is invited. Be clear about which events are open to all guests versus invitation-only.


### Virtual Weddings and Live Streaming


Not every guest can make it in person — especially for destination weddings or when loved ones live far away. A virtual wedding or live streaming section on your website ensures those guests can still be part of your celebration.


Include the following for virtual attendees:


- **Livestream link.** Share the streaming link on a dedicated page of your wedding website. Test the link before the wedding day to make sure it works.
- **Schedule and timing.** Let virtual guests know exactly when to tune in, including time zone information. If the ceremony and reception stream at different times, specify both.
- **Viewing instructions.** Not everyone is tech-savvy — include a brief how-to for joining the stream (browser, app, or platform instructions).
- **Pre-event updates.** Keep virtual guests in the loop leading up to the wedding with updates, rehearsal clips, or behind-the-scenes content so they feel included.
- **Recordings after the event.** Share a recording of the ceremony for anyone who couldn’t watch live. Post it on your wedding website so it is easy to find.

### Privacy and Password Protection


Your wedding website contains personal details — venue addresses, guest lists, travel plans — that you may not want publicly accessible. Rather than leaving your site open to anyone who finds it, take a few minutes to configure your privacy settings.


Options to consider:


- **Password protection.** Require a password to access the website and share it with your save the dates or invitations. This keeps details private from anyone not on the guest list.
- **Guest-only access.** Restrict access so only people on your guest list can view the website — they log in with their name or email rather than a shared password.
- **Search visibility.** Control whether your wedding website appears in Google search results. If your guest list is small or your wedding is private, turn off search indexing.

### Use Your Wedding Website as Your Wedding Coordinator


Your wedding website is not just an information page — it can be the central command center for your entire wedding. Instead of juggling separate tools for guest communication, travel logistics, and event coordination, use your wedding website to manage everything from one place.


Here is how:


- **Timeline management.** Build and share your wedding weekend timeline so guests know where to be and when — from the welcome dinner to the morning-after brunch.
- **Multiple events.** If your wedding spans multiple days or includes separate events (rehearsal dinner, ceremony, reception, after-party), manage them all from one dashboard and display them clearly on your website.
- **Guest communication.** Send updates, reminders, and changes directly through your wedding website rather than mass-texting or group-emailing. This keeps your communication organized and in one place.
- **Travel updates.** As logistics change (shuttle times, hotel availability, road closures), update your travel section and notify guests — no chain of “reply-all” emails needed.
- **Day-of coordination.** Share real-time updates on the wedding day: ceremony running late, cocktail hour location change, last-minute schedule adjustments. Guests check the website instead of texting you.

## What Not to Put on Your Wedding Website


- **Registry links on the homepage.** Keep registry information in a dedicated section — do not lead with it.
- **Detailed seating chart information.** Guests do not need to know their table assignment in advance. Share this at the venue.
- **Vendor contact information.** Your photographer, florist, and caterer do not need to be listed on your guest-facing website.
- **Negative or exclusionary language.** Avoid “adults only” or “no children” phrasing that could feel harsh. Use neutral language: “This is an adult reception.”
- **Too much detail about the ceremony script.** A brief ceremony description is fine, but guests do not need a word-for-word rundown.


## When to Launch Your Wedding Website


Launch your wedding website when you send save the dates — typically 6 to 12 months before the wedding for local weddings, or 9 to 12 months out for destination weddings. At minimum, the website needs your names, date, venue, and a note that more details are coming.


Add the RSVP form 2 to 3 months before the wedding when you send formal invitations. Add registry links once your registry is live.


## Wedding Website Checklist


Use this checklist to make sure your website is complete before sending invitations:


- Names and wedding date on homepage
- Ceremony venue name, address, and Google Maps link
- Ceremony start time
- Reception venue name, address, and Google Maps link (if different)
- Reception start time
- Parking instructions
- RSVP form with deadline
- Guest list tool connected and syncing
- Hotel room block link and booking deadline
- 2 to 3 additional hotel recommendations
- Transportation and car rental info (if applicable)
- Registry links (including cash funds and external registries)
- Privacy and password settings configured
- Dress code
- FAQ section
- Wedding party with photos and bios
- Livestream link and instructions (if applicable)
- Contact information (email address for questions)


## Frequently Asked Questions


### What are common wedding website mistakes?


The most common mistakes: launching too late (after invitations go out), not including a Google Maps link for the venue, forgetting to set an RSVP deadline, and not updating the site when details change. Also common: including registry links on the invitation instead of the website.


### What FAQs should I put on a wedding website?


Cover dress code, children policy, plus-one policy, parking, shuttle availability, dietary restrictions process, and what time guests should arrive. These are the questions couples get most often via text and email — answering them on the website saves everyone time.


### Should I password-protect my wedding website?


Password protection keeps your wedding details private from people who were not invited. You can configure all of these from your[privacy settings](https://withjoy.com/help/en/articles/8310196-website-privacy-settings) . For most couples, password protection or guest-only access strikes the right balance between privacy and convenience.


### How long should a wedding website be?


Long enough to answer every guest question, short enough that guests actually read it. The essential sections cover everything most guests need. Add optional sections only if they are relevant to your wedding. Most couples end up with 5 to 10 pages or sections.


### Do you need a wedding website?


Not technically, but it makes wedding planning significantly easier. An online RSVP system alone saves hours of tracking paper cards. For weddings with out-of-town guests, a travel and accommodations page is essential. Most couples who skip a wedding website end up fielding the same questions hundreds of times via text.


### Can guests who can’t attend watch the wedding online?


Yes — if you set up live streaming. Add a livestream link to your wedding website with viewing instructions and timing details.
