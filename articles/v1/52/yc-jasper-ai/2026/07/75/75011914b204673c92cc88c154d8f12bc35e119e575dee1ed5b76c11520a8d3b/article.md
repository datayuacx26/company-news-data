# How to Earn Citations Across Off-Domain AI Search Sources

When AI search engines answer a question about your category, where do they point?


It’s almost never to your own website.


AI-generated answers draw from news articles, industry publications, analyst research, comparison sites, customer reviews, creator and community platforms, partner content, and other sources distributed across the web.


These off-domain sources have significant influence over how brands appear in AI search. A[2026 Muck Rack study](https://muckrack.com/blog/what-is-ai-reading-may-2026?utm_source=chatgpt.com) analyzing more than 25 million links cited by ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini found that earned media accounted for 84% of AI citations.


That changes the nature of brand visibility. Marketing teams can publish accurate, comprehensive content on their own sites and still leave much of their AI search presence in the hands of online surfaces they don’t control.


Brands can’t directly create most of those third-party signals. But they *can* earn them by giving those sources evidence and expertise worth citing.


## **The off-domain sources shaping AI search**


[AEO and GEO](https://www.jasper.ai/blog/geo-aeo) operate across an ecosystem of owned and third-party sources. Your website gives AI engines direct information about your brand, products, and expertise. Off-domain sources provide external signals that can validate, expand, challenge, or reshape that information.


Those sources don’t all establish authority in the same way. A trade publication may contribute independent editorial credibility. A review platform captures verified customer experiences. A YouTube demo shows how a product works. A Reddit thread surfaces first-hand opinions and candid category conversations.


Each source also has different standards for what it will publish, reference, or discuss. Earning citations in this kind of ecosystem requires understanding what each platform values, then publishing content it will want to use. Here’s a quick snapshot:


Off-domain surface Examples Content and evidence to develop How to activate it


News and trade media Business publications, technology news sites, and industry publications Proprietary research, trend data, executive commentary, fact sets, and expert analysis Media outreach, expert responses, contributed articles, and news announcements


Analyst and research sources Analyst firms, research organizations, and industry associations Category frameworks, product evidence, customer outcomes, and perspectives on market developments Analyst briefings, research participation, and association contributions


Review and comparison platforms G2, Capterra, TrustRadius, and category listicles Customer reviews, detailed product information, use-case evidence, and documented results Customer advocacy, review programs, accurate profiles, and reviewer education


Community platforms Reddit, Quora, industry forums, and professional communities Expert guidance, useful explanations, product context, and authentic customer experiences Expert participation, community engagement, and customer advocacy


Video and creator platforms YouTube, podcasts, creator newsletters, and webinar platforms Product demonstrations, interviews, tutorials, expert commentary, and original findings Creator relationships, executive appearances, collaborative content, and expert-led programming


Customer and partner sites Customer blogs, integration partners, agencies, and technology partners Case studies, integration stories, benchmark data, and joint perspectives Co-marketing, partner enablement, collaborative research, and customer storytelling


Reference and educational sources Wikipedia, glossaries, educational resources, and industry guides Company facts, category definitions, milestones, research, and third-party evidence Earned coverage, consistent public information, and appropriate factual corrections


The right mix of off-domain sources will look different for every brand. Teams should prioritize the surfaces with the greatest influence over their most important queries, then focus their efforts on earning citations where they can materially improve visibility.


## **How to operationalize off-domain citation opportunities**


Earning an off-domain citation starts with content actually worth citing: original evidence, expertise, and a clear point of view. Our guide to creating[non-commodity content](https://www.jasper.ai/blog/non-commodity-content-ai-search) for AI search breaks down how to build that into your production process.


The next move is turning that content into citation opportunities across the surfaces already shaping brand-relevant AI answers. The same underlying evidence can support several off-domain opportunities when it is adapted for each audience.


Proprietary research, for example, might inform a journalist’s trend coverage, strengthen an analyst briefing, give an executive a substantive point to discuss on a podcast. A customer outcome could support a partner story, an industry award submission, a review program, or contributed editorial coverage.


In each case, the evidence remains consistent while its presentation and activation reflect the needs of the source.


Operationalizing that means:


- Pinpointing where your brand needs stronger visibility in AI-generated answers
- Finding the sources already shaping those answers
- Matching the strongest available evidence and content to the opportunity
- Shaping it into the format each source actually uses
- Routing it through the team that owns that channel
- Tracking what gets picked up and feeding it into the next round


This process discipline is what makes off-domain content value compound. Every citation earned becomes evidence for what to try next, and the following push starts smarter than the last.
