## Is Wegovy approved for teens?


Yes. Wegovy is FDA-approved for teens ages 12 and older who have obesity.


A GLP-1 is a class of weight-management medicines that work with your body's own hunger signals to lower appetite. Wegovy (the brand name for semaglutide) won FDA approval for this age group in December 2022. The approval is for teens with obesity, which doctors define as a body mass index (BMI) at or above the 95th percentile for a child's age and sex. In plain terms, that means a teen's BMI falls in the top 5 percent compared with other kids the same age and sex.


Wegovy is one of two GLP-1-class medicines approved for this age group:


- Wegovy (semaglutide): a weekly injection, approved for ages 12 and older.
- Saxenda (liraglutide): a daily injection, approved for ages 12 and older since December 2020.


One drug you may have heard of is not approved for teens. Zepbound (tirzepatide) is approved only for adults. Its label states that safety and effectiveness have not been established in patients under 18. A study in teens is underway, but for now Zepbound is not an option for this age group.


These medicines are prescription-only. A teen should start one only after a full evaluation by a doctor who treats childhood obesity.

## Does insurance cover Wegovy for teens?


Sometimes, but[coverage for a teen's weight-management medicine is limited](https://www.blueberrypediatrics.com/health-tips/does-insurance-cover-glp1-teenagers) and varies a lot by plan. Many families are denied or face heavy paperwork first.


### Why pediatric GLP-1 coverage is so limited


Weight-loss medicines sit in a benefit category that many health plans treat as optional. Large employer plans and marketplace (ACA) plans often exclude or restrict them for obesity. Even when a plan does cover the drug, it usually requires prior authorization first. Prior authorization is when your insurer must approve a medicine before they will pay for it. For a teen, that often means the doctor has to send proof of the diagnosis and supporting records, and the plan can still say no.


How rare is access in practice? A 2025 analysis of electronic health records found that fewer than 1 in 100 teens with obesity were prescribed a GLP-1. The study also found teens on Medicaid or paying cash were far less likely to get one than teens with private insurance. The authors tie this partly to coverage limits and prior-authorization rules. This study is a preprint, meaning it has not yet finished peer review. Read it as a signal of how hard access is, not as a coverage rate.


### Medicaid, Medicare, and marketplace plans


Public coverage depends heavily on who pays and where you live:


- Medicare: By law, Medicare does not pay for drugs used for weight loss. This rarely affects teens, since Medicare mainly covers people 65 and older. We mention it only because "the government won't pay" is a common myth, and the rule that creates it is a Medicare rule.
- Medicaid: States choose whether to cover weight-loss drugs. As of January 2026, only 13 state Medicaid programs covered GLP-1 drugs for obesity. The rest did not, though they still cover these drugs for other approved uses like diabetes.
- A special rule for kids on Medicaid: A federal benefit called EPSDT can require Medicaid to cover care that is medically necessary for a child, even when the same benefit is optional for adults. This can help a teen's case. But it is not a guarantee. States and their plans still apply medical-necessity rules, step therapy, and prior authorization, so families may still face denials.


The honest takeaway: do not assume coverage. Call the number on your insurance card and ask directly whether your plan covers Wegovy for a teen, and what steps are required.

## Which cost path is right for your family?


The right path depends on your insurance, your budget, and how much support you want around the medicine.


A few simple guideposts:


- If your plan clearly covers it: Paying through insurance may be your lowest-cost option. Confirm the copay and any prior-authorization steps first.
- If you have private insurance but are not sure about coverage: Ask your plan directly, and ask the prescriber's office to help with prior authorization.
- If you have no coverage or keep hitting denials: A comprehensive program can remove the guesswork by bundling the care and helping you navigate the drug cost.
- If cost is your only concern and you already have a care team: A maker's self-pay program may be the simplest route, as long as you understand the price can change.


There is no single best answer for every family. The best path is the one that keeps your teen safe, supported, and able to stay on track.


## When to talk to a doctor about GLP-1s for your teen


Talk to a doctor if your teen has obesity and you are weighing a GLP-1, especially if you have already been told the medicine could help.


A good first step is a full evaluation with a doctor who treats childhood obesity. They can confirm whether a GLP-1 fits your teen, explain the risks and benefits, and start the insurance process. In our experience caring for Balance families, one of the most common questions parents ask is, "My teen's doctor recommended a GLP-1, but our insurance denied it, so what do we do now?" A denial is often the starting point, not the end of the road. The care team can file an appeal with added medical detail, which sometimes changes the answer.


These are prescription medicines


Wegovy and Saxenda require a prescription and ongoing medical supervision. Do not buy GLP-1 medicines from unverified online sellers, and do not start, stop, or change a dose without your doctor. These medicines carry a boxed warning about a rare thyroid tumor risk and should not be used by anyone with a personal or family history of medullary thyroid cancer or MEN 2.


Call your doctor promptly if your teen has any concerning symptoms while on a GLP-1, such as severe belly pain, repeated vomiting, or signs of an allergic reaction. When in doubt, reach out to your care team.


## Frequently Asked Questions


### Is Wegovy FDA-approved for teenagers, and at what age?


Yes. Wegovy is approved for teens ages 12 and older who have obesity (a BMI at or above the 95th percentile for age and sex). It has held this approval since December 2022.


### How much does Wegovy cost per month for a teen without insurance?

### Does insurance cover Wegovy for adolescents?


Sometimes, but coverage is limited and varies by plan. Many plans exclude or restrict weight-management drugs, and most require prior authorization. Call your insurer to ask about your specific plan.

Only if they have private (commercial) insurance. The savings card excludes people on government plans, so a teen on Medicaid or CHIP cannot use it.

Not necessarily. Pediatric guidelines say weight-management medicine works best as one part of a full care program with nutrition support and follow-up. A medicine-only path can skip the care that helps a teen succeed safely.
