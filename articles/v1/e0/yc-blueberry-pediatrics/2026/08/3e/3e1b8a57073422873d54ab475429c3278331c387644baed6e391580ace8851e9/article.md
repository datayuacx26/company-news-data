## What You Actually Pay for Virtual Pediatric Care


Virtual pediatric care is usually priced one of two ways: pay-per-visit or a flat monthly membership. Knowing the difference helps you compare the real cost.


With pay-per-visit care, you pay each time you talk to a doctor. Standalone apps like Teladoc and Amwell list visits at about $75 when you pay out of pocket. Through insurance, a virtual visit often costs less, and some plans cover it at no charge. The catch is that you pay again for every visit and often for each child.


A membership works differently. You pay one monthly fee, and visits are included. A membership can also cost far less over a year than paying per visit. This is especially true if you have more than one child or your kids get sick often.

## When You Still Need In-Person or Emergency Care


Virtual care is a great first stop for common illnesses. It is not a substitute for emergency care. The[American Academy of Pediatrics](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/health-management/Pages/Telehealth-Services-for-Children.aspx) says telehealth works best alongside your regular pediatrician, and that some visits still need to happen in person.


In-person care is usually needed for well-child checkups, shots, and anything that requires a hands-on exam, imaging, or lab work. For babies younger than 2, the AAP also recommends care through your own pediatrician or medical home rather than one-off telehealth services outside the medical home.


Call 911 or go to the emergency room now, do not wait for a virtual visit, if your child has any of these signs.


Trouble breathing, grunting, or bluish lips; a fever in a baby younger than 3 months; a seizure; no wet diaper or tears for 8 hours; repeated green or bloody vomiting; or a sudden, big change in how alert or responsive your child is.


If you are not sure whether a symptom is an emergency, a good rule is to call your pediatrician first. For a full list of childhood emergency warning signs, see this[MedlinePlus guide from the National Institutes of Health](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/patientinstructions/000594.htm) .
