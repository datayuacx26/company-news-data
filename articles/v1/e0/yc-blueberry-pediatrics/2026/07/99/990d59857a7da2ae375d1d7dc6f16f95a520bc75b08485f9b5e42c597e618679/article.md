# How Long Does a Sunburn Last? Pediatric Healing Timeline

Most sunburns heal on their own with time. A mild (first-degree) sunburn usually fades in about 3 to 7 days. A moderate burn that peels takes about a week. A blistering (second-degree) sunburn can take 2 weeks or more. Any sunburn on a baby under 1 needs a call to the doctor right away.


In our telehealth visits, sunburn is one of the most common summer worries parents message us about. Many are surprised by how fast a baby or toddler burns, sometimes after just a short time outside or through thin clothing. Below, a pediatrician walks you through the healing timeline by severity, what actually helps, what to skip, and the warning signs that mean it is time to call.

## Key Takeaways


- A mild sunburn usually heals in about 3 to 7 days. A moderate, peeling burn takes about a week. A blistering burn can take 2 weeks or more.
- Redness usually starts within a few hours of sun and peaks at 12 to 24 hours.
- A sunburn on a baby under 1 is urgent. Call the doctor right away.
- You cannot speed up your skin's repair. You can ease the pain and keep the burn from getting worse.
- For pain, use ibuprofen or acetaminophen. Never give aspirin to a child or teen.
- Do not pop blisters. They protect the healing skin underneath.
- The damage from sunburn stays even after it heals. Childhood sun exposure raises the lifelong risk of skin cancer.


## How long does a sunburn take to heal?


How long a sunburn lasts depends on how deep it is. Most heal in a few days to a couple of weeks without a doctor.


Sunburn is your skin's inflamed reaction to too much ultraviolet (UV) light. The deeper the burn, the longer it takes to heal. Here is the timeline by severity so you know what to expect.


Type of sunburn How long it lasts What you see


Mild (first-degree) About 3 to 7 days Red, sore skin with no blisters


Moderate (with peeling) About 1 week (7 to 10 days) Deeper redness, swelling, then peeling


Blistering (second-degree) 2 weeks or more Blisters, deeper pain, and swelling


### Mild (first-degree) sunburn: about 3 to 7 days


A mild, first-degree sunburn (a surface burn that is red and sore, with no blisters) usually heals in[about 3 to 7 days](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK534837/) .


This is the most common kind. The skin looks pink or red and feels hot, tight, and tender. It may peel a little as it heals. Mild sunburns almost always get better at home with cool comfort care and staying out of the sun.


### Moderate sunburn with peeling: about 1 week


A moderate sunburn takes about a week, and peeling can continue for 7 to 10 days.


The redness is deeper, and the skin may swell a bit. After a few days, the top layer peels as your body sheds sun-damaged cells. Peeling is normal. It does not mean the skin is infected. Let it come off on its own and do not pick at it.


### Blistering (second-degree) sunburn: 2 weeks or more


A blistering, second-degree sunburn (a deeper burn that forms blisters) can take 2 weeks or more to heal, often 2 to 3 weeks.


Blisters mean the burn reached a deeper layer of skin. These burns hurt more and may swell. Some can leave a lasting change in skin color. A large or badly blistering burn may need a doctor, so call if you are worried.


## What does the sunburn healing timeline look like day by day?


A sunburn follows a fairly predictable path: redness comes first, peaks within a day, then fades as the skin peels and repairs.


Here is how a typical sunburn unfolds:


- First few hours: Redness (called erythema) begins. Sunburn can be sneaky, so the skin may look worse over the next several hours.
- 12 to 24 hours: Redness, warmth, and soreness reach their peak. This is usually when a sunburn feels the worst.
- Around day 3: Peeling may start as the skin sheds damaged cells. This is a normal part of healing.
- About 1 week: Peeling can continue for 7 to 10 days, and any blisters usually begin to heal in that same window.


## How long do sunburn blisters last?


Sunburn blisters usually heal in[about 7 to 10 days](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK534837/) . Blisters are a sign of a deeper, second-degree burn. The blisters themselves usually close in about a week, though the deeper burn can take 2 to 3 weeks to fully heal.


A blister is a small pocket of fluid that forms to protect and heal the skin underneath. Give it time and keep it clean. Watch a blistering burn closely, especially in young children, and call your doctor if it looks severe or infected.


Do not pop sunburn blisters


Blisters form to help your skin heal and to protect it from infection. Leave closed blisters alone. A thin layer of plain petroleum jelly can protect them. If a blister breaks on its own, gently clean the area with mild soap and water and watch for signs of infection, like spreading redness, warmth, or pus. Call your doctor about large blisters (bigger than half an inch), many small blisters, or any blisters on the face.


## How can I make a sunburn heal faster?


You cannot rush your skin's repair, but you can ease the pain and keep the burn from getting worse. No cream, spray, or remedy speeds up how fast skin actually heals.


The goal is comfort while your body does the work. These supportive steps are safe to try for children:


- Cool the skin. Apply cool (not ice-cold) compresses, or give a cool bath or shower. Gently pat the skin dry afterward.
- Give extra fluids. A sunburn pulls fluid toward the skin and away from the rest of the body, so offer extra water to help prevent dehydration.
- Moisturize. Use a gentle moisturizer, or one with aloe vera or soy, to soothe tight, sore skin. Avoid products with dyes or heavy fragrance.
- Treat pain safely. Ibuprofen or acetaminophen can ease pain. Follow the dosing on the package for your child's age and weight.
- Try 1% hydrocortisone cream. A short course of over-the-counter 1% hydrocortisone can calm sore skin. Skip the face and any broken skin.


What to skip on a child's sunburn


Do not use numbing "-caine" sprays or creams (like benzocaine). The FDA warns they can cause a serious blood problem called methemoglobinemia, which can happen at any age and is most dangerous in young children. It is safest to skip these products in children. Never give aspirin to a child or teen, because it is linked to a rare but serious illness called Reye's syndrome. Do not give ibuprofen to a baby under 6 months without asking your doctor. Skip home remedies like apple cider vinegar, which can burn the skin, and do not pop blisters.

## How long does a sunburn last on a baby or toddler?


A baby's sunburn heals on the same timeline as anyone else's, but a young child's burn is more serious and needs closer attention.


Infant skin is thinner and more delicate than adult skin, so it burns more quickly, sometimes after only a short time outside. Babies also lose fluids and overheat faster, which raises the risk of dehydration and heat illness. In our summer visits, parents are often shocked at how fast a little one burned. It can happen through a hat or thin clothing, or from sun bouncing off water and sand.


Because of these risks, prevention matters most for the youngest children. Babies under 6 months should be kept out of direct and indirect sun. Shade, a hat, and lightweight clothing come first, with a small amount of sunscreen only when shade is not available. ## When should I see a doctor for a sunburn?


Call for a sunburn if your child is under 1, has a severe or blistering burn, or shows signs of dehydration, heat illness, or infection. When in doubt, reach out.


Most sunburns can be cared for at home, but some need a doctor or emergency care. Use these lists to help you decide.


A sunburn in a baby under 1 is an emergency


A baby under 1 year old with any sunburn needs to be seen by a doctor. Call your pediatrician right away. Also get emergency care (call 911) if a child of any age faints, is too weak to stand, seems confused, or is very hard to wake, since these can be signs of heatstroke.


Get care right away if your child has any of these:


- A sunburn and is under 1 year old
- Fainting, extreme weakness, confusion, or unusual sleepiness
- Signs of dehydration, such as very few wet diapers, no tears when crying, or a dry mouth
- A high fever, or a fever with skin that looks infected (spreading redness, warmth, or pus)
- A burn that covers a large area of the body


Call your doctor within a day if your child has:


- Severe pain that home care does not help
- Large blisters (bigger than half an inch), many small blisters, or blisters on the face
- Fever, chills, headache, or a general feeling of being unwell


Telehealth is a fast first step when you are worried and not sure what to do. It is not a replacement for emergency care. A baby under 1, or any child with severe blistering, a high fever, fainting, or signs of dehydration or heatstroke, needs an in-person or emergency visit right away.


## How can I prevent a sunburn in the first place?


The best way to prevent sunburn is to combine shade, clothing, and sunscreen, and to be extra careful with babies and toddlers.


Sun protection works best in layers. Here is what pediatricians recommend:


- Under 6 months: Keep babies out of direct and indirect sun. Use shade, a wide-brimmed hat, and lightweight clothing that covers the skin.
- 6 months and older: Use a broad-spectrum, water-resistant sunscreen with SPF 30 or higher on any skin not covered by clothing.
- Reapply often: Put sunscreen on about 15 to 30 minutes before going out, and reapply every 2 hours, or after swimming or sweating.
- Seek shade: Limit time in direct sun during peak hours, usually 10 a.m. to 4 p.m. Use umbrellas, stroller canopies, hats, and sunglasses.
- Watch reflected sun: Water, sand, and even snow bounce UV rays back onto the skin, and clouds do not block them all.

## Frequently Asked Questions


### How long does a sunburn last?


A mild sunburn usually heals in about 3 to 7 days. A moderate burn that peels takes about a week. A blistering, second-degree burn can take 2 weeks or more. The answer to how long does sunburn last depends mostly on how deep the burn is.


### How long do sunburn blisters last?


Sunburn blisters usually heal in about 7 to 10 days. Do not pop them. They protect the healing skin underneath and help prevent infection.


### How long does a second-degree sunburn take to heal?


A second-degree (blistering) sunburn usually takes about 2 to 3 weeks to heal. It sometimes leaves a lasting change in skin color.


### What does the day-by-day sunburn timeline look like?


Redness begins within a few hours and peaks at 12 to 24 hours. Peeling often starts around day 3 and can last 7 to 10 days, when blisters also tend to heal.


### How can I make a sunburn heal faster?


You cannot speed up how fast skin repairs itself. You can ease the pain with cool compresses, extra fluids, gentle moisturizer, and ibuprofen or acetaminophen, and keep the burn from getting worse.


### How long does a sunburn last on a baby or toddler?


The healing timeline is the same, but a young child's burn is more serious. A baby under 1 with a sunburn needs to see a doctor right away.


### When should I call a doctor or go to the ER for a sunburn?


Call right away for any sunburn in a baby under 1, signs of dehydration or heat illness, a high fever, or a large or badly blistering burn. Fainting or confusion is a 911 emergency.


### How do I prevent sunburn in kids?


Keep babies under 6 months out of the sun. For older kids, use shade, hats, clothing, and a broad-spectrum SPF 30 or higher sunscreen, and reapply every 2 hours.
