# A Website Optimization Checklist for Steady, Measurable Upkeep

A useful website optimization checklist is a repeatable inspection route: confirm that the site works, remove information that no longer helps, verify important actions, and record what changes afterward. Maintenance becomes manageable when each finding receives an owner, a priority, and a review date.


Four activities hold that route together: reviewing technical website health, updating outdated website content, testing important conversion elements, and monitoring ongoing performance improvements. A broken mobile layout can weaken a strong offer, stale pricing can invalidate a clean form, and a successful test can disappear without follow-up.


## Prepare the website optimization checklist before opening the editor


*Novaro Template from Strikingly*


Begin outside the editor. Choose the pages with the greatest business impact: the homepage, top landing pages, core service or product pages, contact path, checkout, and high-traffic resources. Record the current page goal and one meaningful signal before changing it. Treat the website optimization checklist as an operating record, not a cosmetic review.


### Use one record for every finding


1. **Evidence:**


note the URL, device, date, and what actually happened.


2. **Impact:**


state whether the issue blocks access, confuses a decision, or weakens measurement.


3. **Action:**


describe the smallest complete fix rather than writing “optimize page.”


4. **Verification:**


name the metric that will confirm the repair while monitoring ongoing performance improvements.


5. **Owner and date:**


make the next review visible instead of relying on memory.


This log connects reviewing technical website health with updating outdated website content because it shows whether a problem belongs to the page, the message, or both. It also gives testing important conversion elements a baseline and gives monitoring ongoing performance improvements a place to store results. Without the log, the website optimization checklist becomes a series of edits with no reliable history.


## Start by reviewing technical website health


Reviewing technical website health begins with access. Open each priority page in a private window, follow the navigation, and click essential links. Submit forms with a labeled test address. For a store, follow the cart path without paying and confirm that shipping, quantity, and checkout controls respond. The Strikingly


[pre-publish checklist](https://support.strikingly.com/hc/en-us/articles/214364618-Pre-publish-Checklist)


also covers images, menus, mobile views, and search-friendly details.


Review loading behavior rather than relying on one score. Look for oversized media, multiple videos, third-party embeds, sliders, and custom code on the first screen. Strikingly's guide to


[improving page load speed](https://support.strikingly.com/hc/en-us/articles/206051562-Improving-Your-Site-Page-Load-Speed)


recommends lightweight pages and selective media. Reviewing technical website health should identify the resource delaying useful content, not merely label the page “slow.”


### Technical pass criteria


Confirm successful loading, working navigation, valid form delivery, secure destinations, readable error states, and sensible behavior on a slower connection. Reviewing technical website health also includes checking title tags, descriptions, headings, and image alt text because discoverability and accessibility are part of the page's operating condition.


Use the website optimization checklist to separate urgent failures from planned improvements. A broken inquiry form needs immediate repair. A decorative image may be scheduled for compression. An old script may need an owner to verify whether it is still required. This triage keeps reviewing technical website health actionable and prevents broad work from delaying testing important conversion elements.


## Retire stale promises and missing details


*Strikingly Page Title and Page Name Comparison*


Updating outdated website content starts with facts that can damage a decision: prices, availability, service areas, team details, specifications, policies, and response times. Compare each page with the source used by the business today. If the claim, offer, or process changed, the visible explanation and supporting metadata should change together.


Review the page title, navigation name, heading, and body as separate fields. They may have been written at different times. Strikingly explains the distinction between search-facing titles and menu labels in its guide to


[SEO page titles and meta descriptions](https://support.strikingly.com/hc/en-us/articles/115000094561-Adding-SEO-Page-Titles-and-Meta-Descriptions-Tags)


. Updating outdated website content means aligning these fields with the page's current job instead of repeating one phrase everywhere.


Look for pages that overlap, contradict one another, or answer questions the audience no longer asks. Updating outdated website content may mean merging two thin resources, redirecting an obsolete campaign page, or rewriting an old service description around the current process. The Strikingly article on


[step-by-step website maintenance](https://www.strikingly.com/blog/posts/website-maintenance-made-easy-a-step-by-step-guide)


connects regular content updates with broken-link checks, security, and performance rather than treating freshness as a cosmetic task.


Finish the content pass by reading only the links and calls to action. Confirm that labels describe their destinations and downloads are current. Updating outdated website content should reduce contradictions across the journey. It also improves testing important conversion elements because the test no longer compares buttons attached to inaccurate promises. Record every revised page in the website optimization checklist so monitoring ongoing performance improvements has context.


## Inspect the full path on a real phone


*Strikingly Mobile View Editor*


A responsive template does not guarantee that every edit remains comfortable on a small screen. Reviewing technical website health on mobile means checking the published path with a real device: open the menu, scan headings, expand FAQs, use forms, dismiss overlays, and tap the primary action. Pay attention to sticky tools that cover content and to long labels that wrap into confusing shapes.


Strikingly's


[Mobile View in Editor](https://support.strikingly.com/hc/en-us/articles/900004779883-Mobile-View-in-Editor)


helps owners inspect and reorganize content in a phone preview, while the guide for a


[site that looks unusual on mobile](https://support.strikingly.com/hc/en-us/articles/215046907-Website-looks-strange-on-mobile)


identifies domain forwarding and background image settings as possible causes. Use both preview and live-device checks; they answer different questions.


Mobile review often reveals where updating outdated website content is also necessary. A desktop paragraph may become an exhausting wall of text, an old comparison may require side-to-side scrolling, or a once-short button label may no longer fit. Add these findings to the same website optimization checklist. They are not a separate “mobile project” if they affect the same visitor decision.


## Use a test ticket when testing important conversion elements


*Strikingly Site History Restore View*


Testing important conversion elements begins after failures and factual errors are resolved. Choose one meaningful decision point: a headline, primary button, form, pricing explanation, proof block, product image, or checkout instruction. Write the hypothesis before editing. For example, “Naming the response time beside the inquiry button will reduce uncertainty and increase completed forms.”


A test ticket should include the page, current version, changed version, audience, primary metric, start date, and review date. Testing important conversion elements one variable at a time makes the result easier to interpret. Strikingly's article on


[website optimization testing](https://www.strikingly.com/blog/posts/use-website-optimization-testing-to-learn-what-visitors-actually-do)


recommends setting one goal, changing one element, and comparing the same metric before and after.


Keep a rollback path. Strikingly allows owners to


[edit a published site](https://support.strikingly.com/hc/en-us/articles/214364558-Edit-Site-After-Publishing)


, and its


[Site History](https://support.strikingly.com/hc/en-us/articles/16516124304539-Undo-Redo-Buttons-and-Site-History)


can restore eligible published versions for supported plans. Testing important conversion elements is safer when the previous copy, layout, and date are recorded. Do not depend on memory or a screenshot with no URL.


Stop or reverse a change if it blocks access, creates misleading information, or harms a critical action. Testing important conversion elements does not require waiting through obvious damage. For smaller differences, let the test gather enough comparable activity and note external factors such as campaigns, holidays, or pricing changes. Monitoring ongoing performance improvements begins during the test, not after someone announces a winner.


## Use a maintenance cadence for monitoring ongoing performance improvements


Monitoring ongoing performance improvements requires a compact set of signals connected to page jobs. A service page might track qualified form submissions. A store page might track product views, cart additions, and completed purchases. A resource page might track engaged reading and the next relevant click. Avoid turning the website optimization checklist into a dashboard inventory with no decisions attached.


Strikingly's


[Google Analytics event tracking guide](https://support.strikingly.com/hc/en-us/articles/214364518-Using-Google-Analytics-Event-Tracking)


explains tracked form submissions and button clicks. For broader traffic analysis, connect


[Google Analytics 4 with Strikingly](https://support.strikingly.com/hc/en-us/articles/15275247898395-Using-Google-Analytics-with-Strikingly-GA4)


or begin with


[built-in analytics](https://support.strikingly.com/hc/en-us/articles/215752348-Using-Your-Strikingly-Built-in-Analytics)


. Monitoring ongoing performance improvements works when the team can connect a metric change to a dated repair, content update, or test.


Use weekly checks for sudden failures, monthly reviews for patterns, and quarterly reviews for structural decisions. Monitoring ongoing performance improvements should compare equivalent periods and annotate unusual events. If a form conversion drops after traffic quality changes, the form may not be the cause. If engagement rises after updating outdated website content, inspect which revised pages earned the change before expanding the approach.


## Finish every review with a three-level queue


- **Fix now:**


broken access, inaccurate claims, failed forms, unsafe links, and serious mobile problems.


- **Plan next:**


high-impact content revisions and tests with clear evidence, owners, and review dates.


- **Watch:**


uncertain patterns that need more comparable data before a page is changed.


Run a short weekly version of the website optimization checklist on critical paths. Reserve a monthly session for reviewing technical website health and updating outdated website content across priority pages. Use a quarterly review for testing important conversion elements that require longer observation and for monitoring ongoing performance improvements across connected journeys.


The value of the website optimization checklist is not the number of boxes marked complete. It is the quality of the next decision. Reviewing technical website health protects access, updating outdated website content protects accuracy, testing important conversion elements replaces guesswork, and monitoring ongoing performance improvements shows whether the work held. Keep the log current, close verified findings, and carry only evidence-backed priorities into the next inspection.
