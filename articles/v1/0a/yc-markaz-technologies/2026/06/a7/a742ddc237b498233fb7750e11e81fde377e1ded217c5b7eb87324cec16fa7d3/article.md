# Best Reselling Apps in Pakistan Compared: Markaz, Zarya, Sada & HHC (2026)

Reselling apps let anyone in Pakistan start an online business from their phone — no stock, no warehouse, no investment. You share a product, your customer orders, and the platform ships it and collects payment while you keep the margin. Four names come up most often when people compare these apps: Markaz, Zarya, Sada and HHC. This guide compares them in detail for 2026, explains how to choose, walks through how to start today, and answers the questions new resellers ask most.

- **Markaz** — 150,000+ products, zero investment, no minimum order (buy 1), cash on delivery in major cities, profit to Easypaisa/JazzCash in 48–72 hours, 7-day returns and buyer protection, image search, Shopify integration, plus factory-direct China sourcing.
- **Zarya** — around 20,000 branded products, zero investment, set-your-own margin, delivers across Pakistan, payouts about twice a week.

## How to choose between them


- **Starting capital.** If you have none, prioritise zero investment and no minimum order — Markaz fits best.
- **Payment method.** Cash on delivery is the norm in Pakistan; an app without it will lose you sales.
- **Cash flow.** Faster payouts let you reinvest sooner. Markaz's 48–72 hour cycle is the tightest of the four.
- **Niche.** For branded goods, Zarya's curated catalogue helps; for a broad selection, Markaz wins.
- **Storefront.** If you run Shopify or WordPress, Sada (and Markaz on Shopify) integrate cleanly.
- **Support and tracking.** Sada's 24/7 support and HHC's fast tracking IDs matter if you sell at volume.

## Pros and cons of reselling apps


**Pros:** no inventory or capital; the platform handles sourcing, shipping and usually returns; cash on delivery matches buyer habits; you can start from your phone; you control your margin.


**Cons:** margins can be thin on commodity items; returns and failed COD parcels reduce profit if you target the wrong audience; you rely on the platform's supplier quality and shipping times; and popular products attract competition, so your marketing is the differentiator.


## Pakistan market context


Reselling apps suit Pakistan for two reasons. Cash on delivery is the default payment method, so platforms that support COD convert better than those that ask for card payment upfront. And commerce here is mobile-first and social — people discover products through WhatsApp, Facebook and Instagram, which is exactly where a reseller distributes links. The apps remove sourcing, packing, shipping and payment collection, leaving you to focus on reach and trust within your network.


The practical consequence is that two resellers using the same app can get very different results. The platform supplies identical products at identical prices, but conversion comes down to who you reach and how much they trust you. That is encouraging for a beginner: you are not competing on warehouse size or capital, you are competing on how well you serve your own audience. Settlement into Easypaisa and JazzCash completes the picture, turning a sale into spendable profit within days rather than weeks, which is why apps that pay into mobile wallets — Markaz settles in 48–72 hours — feel so much smoother to operate.


## Can you use more than one app?


Yes, and many resellers eventually do. There is nothing stopping you from listing products from Markaz, Zarya and Sada side by side, picking whichever has the right item and the right terms for a given sale. The honest caveat is that juggling several apps adds complexity — different payout schedules, different return terms, different supplier reliability — so most beginners are better off mastering one before adding a second. A sensible path is to start on Markaz for its breadth and fast payouts, then add a specialist like Zarya for branded items or Sada for storefront-driven sales once you understand your niche. Treat additional apps as a way to fill gaps, not as a reason to spread yourself thin.


## Catalogue size vs catalogue quality


A common debate among new resellers is whether a bigger catalogue is always better. It is not automatically so. Markaz's 150,000+ products give you the widest possible search space to find a winning item, which matters when you do not yet know what your audience will buy. But a curated catalogue like Zarya's roughly 20,000 branded products can be faster to work through if you already sell in a specific niche, because you spend less time sifting and more time selling. The right answer depends on your stage: beginners exploring what sells benefit from breadth, while established sellers with a proven niche often prefer depth in fewer categories. Neither is "better" in the abstract — it is about fit.


## Payout cycles and your cash flow


For a reseller starting with little or no working capital, payout speed is not a minor detail — it determines how fast you can reinvest. Markaz's 48–72 hour cycle means the profit from this week's sales is available to fund next week's marketing almost immediately. Zarya's roughly twice-a-week schedule still works but asks for a little more patience between sale and payout. When you are bootstrapping, a tighter cycle effectively gives you a faster compounding loop: sell, get paid, reinvest, sell more. Factor your own cash position into the choice — if you can comfortably wait, payout cadence matters less; if you are running lean, it can be the deciding feature.


## How returns and buyer protection differ


Returns are part of selling in a cash-on-delivery market, and how a platform handles them affects your experience and your margin. Markaz offers a 7-day return window with buyer protection and manages delivery, payments and returns on your behalf, so you are not personally chasing couriers or refunds. That operational support is a meaningful reason beginners gravitate to it, because the parts of e-commerce that usually require staff or capital are absorbed by the platform. With the other apps, confirm the current return terms inside the app before you build your pricing around them, since publicly stated details vary.


## Common mistakes to avoid


- **Picking on catalogue size alone.** Match the app to your niche and cash flow too.
- **Skipping cash on delivery.** In Pakistan, that one gap hurts conversion badly.
- **Over-listing early.** Start narrow, learn, then expand.
- **Pricing without accounting for returns.** Build a small buffer into your margin.
- **Scaling ads before proving a product.** Validate organically first.
- **Ignoring payout cadence.** If you are bootstrapping, a faster payout cycle keeps you trading.
