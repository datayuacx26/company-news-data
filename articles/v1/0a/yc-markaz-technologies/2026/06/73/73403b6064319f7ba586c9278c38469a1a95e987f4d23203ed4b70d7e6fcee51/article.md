# Zarya vs Markaz: Which Reseller App Is Better in 2026?

Zarya and Markaz are two of Pakistan's best-known reseller apps. Both let you sell from your phone with no investment and no inventory — but they differ in catalogue size, payout speed and how much they handle for you. This guide gives a clear, fact-based comparison for 2026, explains how each app works, and helps you decide which one suits the way you want to sell.


## What is a reseller app, and why are they growing in Pakistan?


A reseller app lets you sell products you don't own and never have to stock. You pick items from the app's catalogue, add your own profit margin, and share them with customers over WhatsApp, Facebook and Instagram. When someone buys, the app's supplier dispatches the product and you keep the margin. There's no warehouse, no upfront purchase and no packing — which is exactly why these apps have taken off in Pakistan.


Two things are driving the trend. First, more Pakistanis shop and sell through their phones and social feeds every year, so the audience is already there. Second, cash on delivery (COD) remains the payment method most buyers trust, and the best reseller apps build COD in so first-time customers can order without a card or wallet. Together, these remove the two biggest barriers to starting a business: capital and logistics.

## How catalogue size changes the way you sell


The gap between around 20,000 products (Zarya) and 150,000+ (Markaz) isn't just a bigger number — it changes your day-to-day. A larger catalogue means you can test more niches without running out of options, react to seasonal demand (Eid, wedding season, winter), and replace any product that stops selling without leaving a gap in your store. It also makes it easier to bundle complementary items, which lifts your average order value.


A smaller, brand-focused catalogue like Zarya's has its own logic: fewer choices can mean less decision fatigue and a clearer identity if your audience specifically wants recognised labels. The right answer depends on your strategy. If you plan to experiment and scale across categories, breadth wins. If you've found a tight branded niche that converts, depth in that niche matters more than raw count. Markaz adds a third lever on top —[factory-direct sourcing from China](https://www.markaz.app/china) — which lets you reach for products and margins that locally stocked catalogues often can't match, accepting a longer 10–17 day delivery window in exchange.


## Understanding payout cycles and your cash flow


Payout timing sounds like a small detail until you're trying to grow. With Markaz, profit reaches your Easypaisa or JazzCash within 48–72 hours of a completed sale; with Zarya, payouts run roughly twice a week. Faster access to your earnings means you can recycle profit into ads or restocking sooner, and the compounding effect over a busy month is real — a reseller running paid promotions can fund the next campaign from the last sale rather than waiting for a fixed payout day.


It also affects how you handle COD returns. Because a portion of cash-on-delivery orders are refused, you want a system where the money and the returns are reconciled cleanly. Markaz handles delivery, payments and returns end to end, so what lands in your wallet already reflects completed, paid orders. When you compare any two reseller apps, look past the headline features and ask how and when you actually get paid — it's one of the biggest day-to-day differences between platforms.

## Marketing your products without an ad budget


The first sales for most Pakistani resellers come from their own network, not paid ads. Set up a WhatsApp Business number, sort customers into broadcast lists by interest, and post a few products a day to your status with a clear price and a one-line reason to buy. On Facebook and Instagram, short product videos and honest photos consistently outperform polished but generic posts. With Markaz's image search you can also turn a customer's photo into a matching product in seconds, which makes responding to "do you have something like this?" effortless.


Once you have a few sales, reinvest a small portion of profit into boosting the posts that already work rather than guessing at new ones. Social proof — screenshots of delivered orders shared with permission, plus a visible returns promise — converts hesitant buyers better than blanket discounts. Because Markaz pays profit within 48–72 hours, you can recycle earnings into the next round of promotion quickly, while a slower payout cycle forces you to wait before reinvesting.


## Common mistakes to avoid when reselling


- **Chasing too many products at once.** Start with a small, focused range you can market well, then expand.
- **Setting margins by guesswork.** Factor in delivery cost and any service fee before you decide your selling price.
- **Ignoring payout timing.** If you need cash quickly, a 48–72 hour payout (Markaz) behaves very differently from a twice-weekly cycle.
- **Over-promising delivery.** Be honest about timelines, especially for China-sourced items that take around 10–17 days.
- **Not using returns as a selling point.** Buyer protection and 7-day returns reassure first-time buyers — mention them.


## Why cash on delivery and fast payouts matter


In Pakistan, COD is still the default for a large share of online shoppers because it removes the trust barrier — the customer only pays once the parcel is in their hands. For you as a reseller, that means more orders close, especially from first-time buyers. Fast payouts matter on the other side of the ledger: when your profit arrives within 48–72 hours rather than on a weekly cycle, you can reinvest in ads or new products sooner, which compounds over time. Both factors favour a platform that builds COD in and pays quickly.


## What "handled for you" really means


One phrase decides how much time a reseller app actually saves you: how much it handles on your behalf. With Markaz, delivery, payment collection and returns are all managed end to end, backed by 7-day returns and buyer protection. In practice that means you don't arrange couriers, you don't chase COD cash, and you don't process reverse logistics when an item comes back — Markaz does. When the platform owns that whole chain, there's one place to look and one party responsible. When you weigh Zarya against Markaz, look past the catalogue numbers and ask how much of the messy operational work each app takes off your plate — for most beginners, that's the difference between a side income that runs itself and one that eats your evenings.
