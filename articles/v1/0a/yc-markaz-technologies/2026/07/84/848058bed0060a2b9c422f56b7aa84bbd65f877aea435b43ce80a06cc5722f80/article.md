# AliExpress vs Alibaba vs Markaz: Pakistan Guide

Both carry the same Alibaba Group logo, both promise cheap goods from China — and Pakistani buyers mix them up constantly. **AliExpress** and **Alibaba.com** are built for opposite jobs: one sells single retail pieces to consumers, the other sells wholesale lots to businesses. This guide compares them honestly for buying from Pakistan — order sizes, payments, delivery, customs — and shows where the[managed route in rupees](https://www.markaz.app/china) fits between them. (Selling ON these platforms is a different question — we cover that in[selling on AliExpress vs Markaz](https://www.markaz.app/blog-post/selling-on-aliexpress-vs-markaz) .)


## AliExpress in a nutshell


AliExpress is the Alibaba Group’s **retail** marketplace: single pieces, consumer listings in English, card payment at checkout. For a Pakistani buyer the catch is the last mile: shipping options to Pakistan are limited and often cost more than the item, delivery commonly takes several weeks, payment needs an internationally-enabled card, and customs duty can be collected before your parcel is released. It works — many Pakistanis use it for niche items — but it is a patience-and-card game, with returns to China being effectively impractical.


## Alibaba in a nutshell


Alibaba.com is the **export wholesale** marketplace: factories and trading companies selling by the carton with minimum order quantities, USD payments, and freight you arrange or negotiate. It is the right tool for genuine import volume — and a poor one for buying five pieces to test. Our full[Alibaba in Pakistan guide](https://www.markaz.app/blog-post/alibaba-in-pakistan) and the step-by-step[ordering walkthrough](https://www.markaz.app/blog-post/how-to-order-from-alibaba-in-pakistan) cover MOQs, payments and the landed-cost math in detail.


## Head to head for a Pakistani buyer


- **Order size** — AliExpress: single pieces. Alibaba: carton/MOQ wholesale.
- **Payment** — both need international payment (card or USD transfer); neither takes rupees or cash on delivery.
- **Delivery** — AliExpress: commonly several weeks, limited courier options to Pakistan. Alibaba: freight timelines, plus customs clearing you (or an agent) manage.
- **Customs** — both can land you with duty and clearing charges after purchase; the sticker price is never the landed price.
- **Recourse** — platform dispute systems exist on both, but returning goods to China from Pakistan is rarely worth the freight; see[ordering safely from China](https://www.markaz.app/blog-post/is-it-safe-to-order-from-china-pakistan) .


## The problems both share in Pakistan


Strip the branding and the two platforms share the same three walls for a buyer here: **payment** (an international card or USD wire, which many resellers don’t have or don’t want to expose), **waiting** (weeks, with tracking that goes quiet mid-ocean), and **the landed-cost surprise** (freight + duty + clearing added after you’ve committed). ## Frequently asked questions


### Does AliExpress deliver to Pakistan?


Yes, though courier options are limited and shipping can cost more than the item; delivery commonly takes several weeks and customs duty may be collected before release.


### How do I pay on AliExpress or Alibaba from Pakistan?


With an internationally-enabled card (AliExpress) or USD payment/transfer terms (Alibaba). Neither platform supports rupees or cash on delivery — if you want COD, you need a managed marketplace.


### Is Alibaba cheaper than AliExpress?


Per unit, usually — Alibaba is wholesale and AliExpress is retail over the same factory base. But Alibaba’s minimum orders, freight and duty mean its advantage only materialises at volume; on small orders the landed costs converge.


### Do I pay customs on AliExpress orders in Pakistan?


Often, yes — duty and handling can be assessed on arrival, and your parcel waits until they’re paid. Factor it into the real price before ordering.
