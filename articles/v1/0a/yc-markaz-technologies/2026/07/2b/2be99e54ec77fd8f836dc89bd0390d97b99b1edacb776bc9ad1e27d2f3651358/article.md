# Best Products to Source From China in 2026

Most "best products to import" lists are guesses dressed as advice. This one starts from what is actually in the catalogue, because a product idea is worthless if there is no depth of supply behind it — one listing at one price is a gamble, while two thousand competing listings is a market.


Here is how the Markaz China catalogue is distributed right now, and how to use that distribution to choose.

The lesson from that shape is straightforward: **fashion, accessories and everyday household goods are where China sourcing is strongest for Pakistan.** If your first product sits in a category with tens of thousands of options, you have room to find a better price, a better variant, or a supplier with a photo set worth using. If it sits in a category with a few hundred, you are betting on one listing.


## The four tests before you commit


Depth tells you where to look. These four questions tell you whether a specific product is worth selling.


### 1. Does it survive the wait?


China orders arrive door-to-door in about 10 to 17 days. A product a customer wants rather than urgently needs survives that; a product tied to a date does not. Sell the necklace, not the wedding shoes needed on Friday.


### 2. Does the margin justify the patience?


A product that saves your customer fifty rupees is not worth a two-week wait to them, and the margin will not be worth the messaging effort to you. Look for items where the factory-direct price is meaningfully below what is available locally.


### 3. Is it hard to buy locally?


Your entire advantage is offering something the shop down the road does not have. A product freely available in every local market puts you in a price war you cannot win.


### 4. Is it forgiving on size and fit?


Returns hurt more when the parcel has travelled from China. Accessories, bags, gadgets and home items are forgiving; fitted clothing and footwear are not, unless you are prepared to guide sizing carefully.


## Categories worth a first order


**Clothing accessories** — the deepest part of the catalogue and the most forgiving on sizing. Scarves, hair accessories, belts, small jewellery. Light, cheap to ship, easy to photograph well.


**Bags and small leather goods** — 20,000 products deep, universally wanted, no sizing problem, and the visual difference between a factory-direct bag and a local-market bag is obvious in a photograph.


**Phone and computer accessories** — the digital category runs to nearly 8,000 products. Cables, stands, cases and chargers are compact, rarely returned and constantly replaced.


**Home and kitchen gadgets** — household appliances and storage products are where the "I didn't know this existed" reaction lives, and that reaction is what makes social selling work.


**Beauty** — deep at 4,600 products, but the most caution is warranted here: anything applied to skin deserves a sample order to yourself before you sell it to anyone else.
