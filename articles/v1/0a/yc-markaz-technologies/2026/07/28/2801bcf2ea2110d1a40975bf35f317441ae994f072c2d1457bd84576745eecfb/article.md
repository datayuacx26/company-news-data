# Is Dropshipping Halal and Legit in Pakistan?

Two questions stop more would-be resellers in Pakistan than any technical problem: *is this allowed in Islam?* and *is this a real business or another online scam?* Both deserve straight answers — so here they are, without hype.


## The Islamic question, honestly


The classical concern scholars raise about dropshipping is selling something you do not yet own or possess — the Prophet ﷺ instructed against selling what is not with you. Whether a given dropshipping arrangement crosses that line depends on how it is structured, and scholars discuss several structures that address the concern:


- **Agency (wakalah):** you act as a marketing agent for the supplier and earn a known commission or margin for bringing the sale — you are being paid for your effort, not selling goods you don't hold.
- **Order-then-buy with disclosure:** you openly take the customer's order as a promise, purchase the item, and it is delivered to them — with no deception about price, condition or delivery time.


What is agreed on across the board: **deception is never permissible** . Fake photos, hidden charges, inflated "discounts" and false delivery promises make any model problematic, dropshipping or not. We are not muftis and this is not a fatwa — for your personal situation, ask a scholar you trust and describe the exact model you plan to use. ## The legitimacy question


Dropshipping is a real, working business model in Pakistan — thousands of resellers earn from it monthly. What makes it feel scammy is the ecosystem around it: fake gurus selling courses, suppliers demanding registration fees, and stores that never deliver. The test of a legitimate platform is simple:


- **Cash on delivery** — the customer pays when the parcel arrives, so nobody can take money and vanish.
- **Returns** — a real 7-day return window means the platform stands behind the products.
- **No joining fees** — a supplier that earns when YOU sell doesn't need to charge you for the privilege of selling.
