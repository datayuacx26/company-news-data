# No Minimum Order: Start China Sourcing With One Unit

Yes, you can buy from China one unit at a time. Minimum order quantity — MOQ — is a supplier's rule, not a law of importing, and several routes into the Chinese market ignore it completely: paid samples, agent-bought 1688 and Taobao items, and dropshipping catalogues that ship a single piece to your customer's door.


The catch is price per unit. A factory quotes its lowest rate at volume, so one piece always costs more per piece than a carton would. What that premium buys you is information — whether the product actually sells, whether the quality survives the trip, and whether Pakistani buyers accept it at the door — before you put real money into stock.


This guide covers what MOQ really is, four ways around it from Pakistan, what a single unit costs once it lands, how to negotiate a smaller first order, and when bulk is the better call.


## What MOQ means, and why suppliers set one


MOQ is the smallest quantity a supplier will sell in one order. It is quoted per product, and often per variant — a supplier may accept a small total order but insist on a set quantity per colour or size.


The reasons are usually real, not greed:


- **Setup cost.** Changing a machine over to your product takes time whether you order fifty pieces or five thousand. That cost is spread across the run.
- **Materials.** Fabric comes on rolls, plastic in bags, boxes in print runs. Small orders leave awkward leftovers.
- **Packaging.** Printed cartons and labels have their own minimums, often higher than the product's.
- **Admin.** Quoting, invoicing, packing and export paperwork cost the same on a tiny order as on a large one.


Which is why MOQ varies so much by who you are actually talking to. A factory that manufactures the item wants a production run. A trading company that buys from that factory and holds stock can break a carton. A wholesale market seller in Yiwu or Guangzhou will sell you a handful. And a marketplace that has already imported the goods will sell you one.


So the useful question is never "what is the MOQ for this product?" It is "who is the cheapest person in this chain who will sell me the quantity I need?"


## Four ways to buy a single unit from China


### 1. Order a sample


Nearly every serious supplier on Alibaba sells samples, and this is the most direct answer to the no-minimum question. You pay a higher per-piece price, often plus courier, and you get the exact item you were about to commit to. Many suppliers will credit the sample cost against a later bulk order — ask before you pay, and get the answer in writing on the platform.


Samples are worth the premium because they answer questions a listing photo cannot: real material, real weight, real finish, real packaging. ### 2. Buy through a China-based agent


1688 and Taobao are China's domestic platforms. Prices sit close to wholesale and minimums are often tiny or non-existent, because these sites were built for Chinese buyers rather than exporters. The obstacles are practical: Chinese-language listings, Chinese payment methods, and sellers who only ship to a domestic address.


Sourcing agents and freight forwarders exist to bridge exactly that. The agent buys on your behalf, receives the item at their warehouse, checks it, and forwards it to Pakistan — usually consolidating several purchases into one shipment. You pay a service fee plus domestic and international shipping on top of the product price.


This route gives you the widest product range at the lowest sticker price, and the highest number of moving parts. ### 4. Split an order with other resellers


If a supplier will not go below a set quantity and you only want a share, group buying is the old workaround: three or four sellers combine to hit the MOQ and split the carton on arrival.


It works, and it goes wrong in predictable ways. Agree in advance who pays the freight, who holds the goods, who deals with the supplier if a batch is faulty, and what happens if one person's share does not sell. Do it with people you actually know, not names from a group chat.


## What one unit really costs to land in Pakistan


The product price is the smallest part of a single-unit import. The number that matters is landed cost per unit, and it stacks up like this:


- The item price, at sample or single-piece rate rather than bulk rate.
- Domestic shipping inside China, from the seller to your agent's warehouse.
- The agent's service fee, if you used one.
- International freight — priced by actual or volumetric weight, whichever is greater, and usually with a minimum billable weight.
- Duties and taxes on arrival, which depend on the product category and the declared value.
- Last-mile delivery inside Pakistan, plus any COD handling on the way back out.
- Payment and currency conversion costs.


Freight is what punishes single units hardest. A courier's minimum billable weight and fixed handling charges do not shrink because your parcel is small, so the per-unit share of shipping on a one-piece order can rival the item itself — especially for anything bulky and light, where volumetric weight decides the bill.


*Worked example — use your own live quotes, never numbers copied from an article:* take the supplier's single-piece price. Add the domestic China leg and the agent fee. Add the international freight for that parcel, using the greater of actual and volumetric weight. Add duty and taxes as quoted for that category. Divide the total by one, because you bought one. Now compare that figure with the same product's wholesale price in a Pakistani market, and with the landed PKR price of the nearest item in a[China product category](https://www.markaz.app/china/categories) . Sometimes the import wins. On heavy, cheap, commodity items, it very often does not — and it is far better to learn that on paper than after paying freight.

## How to get a supplier to lower its minimum


Published MOQs are an opening position more often than sellers assume. A few things move them:


- **Ask with a number.** "What is your MOQ?" invites the standard answer. "Can you do a first order of X pieces, with a larger repeat order if it sells?" invites a real one.
- **Take stock colours and sizes.** Custom variants force a production run. Whatever is already on the shelf can be broken into small quantities.
- **Drop the branding on the first order.** Printed boxes and logo work carry their own minimums, and they are usually what is holding the MOQ up. Brand later, once the product has proven itself. Our guide to[packaging and branding for small online sellers](https://www.markaz.app/blog-post/packaging-branding-for-small-online-sellers-in-pakistan) covers what can be done locally instead.
- **Pay the sample price without haggling.** Arguing over a sample marks you as a buyer who will argue over everything. Paying it cleanly buys goodwill on the real order.
- **Go one step down the chain.** If the factory will not break its run, its stockist or a trading company probably will, at a slightly higher per-piece price.
- **Say what you are doing.** Suppliers deal with test buyers constantly. "I am testing this product in Pakistan and will reorder if it sells" is a normal, credible message.
- **Keep it all on the platform.** Chat, quotes, agreed specification, payment. The moment a conversation moves to a private wallet or a personal account, your protection goes with it — the warning signs are listed in[is it safe to order from China in Pakistan](https://www.markaz.app/blog-post/is-it-safe-to-order-from-china-pakistan) .


## The single-unit test loop


A one-piece order is not a purchase, it is an experiment. Run it like one.


1. **Pick one product with a clear buyer.** Not five. One test at a time is the only way to know what the answer applies to.
2. **Order the single unit** by whichever of the four routes above fits the product.
3. **Inspect it properly when it lands.** Build quality, does it match the listing, how it survived the box, size and weight against your courier's slabs, and anything that needs a plug, a voltage or an instruction sheet a Pakistani buyer can read.
4. **Shoot your own photos and one video** with that unit. Supplier images sell a product nobody believes; your own hand holding it sells one they do.
5. **List it and run it for two weeks** on the channels you already use, at a price built from your real landed cost.
6. **Count the outcomes.** Enquiries, orders, and how many of those orders were actually accepted at the door. A product with orders and heavy refusals is not a winner.
7. **Then decide.** Reorder in quantity, retest at a different price, or drop it and keep the unit as a demo piece.

## When buying in bulk is the right answer


Single-unit sourcing is a testing tool, not a business model. Switch to volume when:


- **The product has proven demand.** Once orders are steady, the per-unit freight premium is pure lost margin.
- **Freight dominates the item price.** Heavy or bulky goods only make sense consolidated into one shipment.
- **You want your own branding.** Custom packaging and logos need a production run, full stop.
- **Timing is fixed.** Seasonal stock has to be here before the season, not somewhere over the Arabian Sea during it.
- **Your buyers will not wait.** Shipping from China runs around 10 to 17 days door to door; local Markaz stock runs 3 to 5 days. If your customers expect a parcel this week, you need stock in Pakistan — the trade-offs are laid out in[how long China-to-Pakistan delivery takes](https://www.markaz.app/blog-post/china-to-pakistan-delivery-time) .


The sequence that wastes the least money is simple: test single units, sell from a no-minimum catalogue while demand is unproven, and place a bulk order only for the products that have already earned it.

## Mistakes that make single-unit sourcing expensive


- **Testing ten products at once from ten suppliers.** You pay the fixed freight and handling cost ten times. Consolidate through one agent, or test in sequence.
- **Testing heavy, cheap items.** The shipping on a single bulky unit can exceed the product's retail price here. Start light.
- **Pricing retail off the sample cost.** The sample rate is not your future cost base, and it is not your bulk rate either. Price the test to learn, and reprice on real numbers when you reorder.
- **Skipping the drop test.** Anything glass, ceramic or hinged has to survive a Pakistani last-mile delivery, not just the flight. Find that out on one unit.
- **Paying outside the platform.** A discount for paying into a personal account is not a discount.
- **Never photographing the unit.** If the test ends and you have no photos, video or measurements of your own, you paid for the item and threw away the reason you bought it.
