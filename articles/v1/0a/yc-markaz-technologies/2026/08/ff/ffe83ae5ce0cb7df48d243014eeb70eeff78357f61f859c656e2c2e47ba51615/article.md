# Dropshipping in Islamabad & Rawalpindi

Dropshipping in Islamabad and Rawalpindi works like it does across Pakistan, with one local edge: the twin cities sit on the country's densest courier and cash-on-delivery network, so orders move quickly and COD collection is reliable. You don't need a shop, stock or upfront investment. You pick products from a wholesale supplier, share them with buyers in Islamabad, Rawalpindi and beyond, and the supplier ships each order cash-on-delivery while you keep the margin. This guide covers how to start from the twin cities, which products sell here, where to source them, how COD and delivery work locally, the pricing maths that keeps each order profitable, and the mistakes that cost new resellers money.


## How dropshipping works from the twin cities


The model is the same whether you're in F-10, Saddar or Bahria Town. You don't buy stock. You list a supplier's products at your own price, share them on WhatsApp, Facebook or Instagram, and when someone orders you place that order with the supplier. They pack it, ship it to your customer, collect the cash on delivery, and pay you the difference. Your only job is finding buyers and pricing well.


Because there's no inventory, your risk on each product stays at zero until a customer has already agreed to buy. You never carry dead stock, and you never pay for a product before you've sold it. That's what makes dropshipping realistic for a student in H-11 or someone selling part-time from a job in Blue Area. ## Why Islamabad and Rawalpindi are a strong base to sell from


Three things make the twin cities easy to sell from. First, courier coverage: every major logistics company runs pickup across Islamabad and Rawalpindi, and delivery to the rest of Pakistan is typically 3–5 days. Nothing about your location slows fulfilment down. Second, buyers here are used to ordering online and paying cash on delivery, which keeps conversion high because customers pay only when the parcel arrives at their door.


Third, you sit between two cities with different buyer profiles, and that widens your pool without any extra effort. A single product you post reaches Blue Area office workers, students in the university belt, and Raja Bazaar shoppers in the same feed. ## Step by step: setting up in the twin cities


### 1. Pick a niche you can speak to


Start with something you actually understand or use. If you follow local fashion, sell ladies' wear. If you're into phones, sell mobile accessories. Knowing the product means you answer buyer questions fast, and fast replies close sales. Pick one narrow niche first rather than listing everything.

Your supplier decides your delivery speed, return handling and payout reliability, so vet them before you list a single item. ## Where to source: local markets vs no-minimum app vs China-direct


### Local wholesale markets


Raja Bazaar in Rawalpindi and the nearby wholesale lanes still offer the cheapest per-unit rate if you buy in bulk. The trade-offs are real: you pay cash up front, you travel and inspect each piece, and whatever doesn't sell is your loss. For a reseller testing several products, buying a dozen of each turns a small budget into inventory risk before the first sale.


### No-minimum wholesale apps


This route fits how most resellers actually work: sell first, buy second. With a no-minimum supplier you order a single unit only after a customer has paid, cash-on-delivery, with your margin paid out to you. No upfront stock, no dead inventory. The per-piece rate sits close to what the markets charge for dozens, because the platform buys at volume and passes the tier down. ## Pricing and margin: a worked example


Price to the market, not to your cost. Check what a product sells for on local pages and marketplaces, subtract your all-in cost including delivery, and only list items that leave a clear margin. Example numbers for a winter shawl: your wholesale cost is PKR 700, delivery is included, and similar shawls sell locally for PKR 1,200–1,400. List at PKR 1,299 and you keep roughly PKR 500–600 per order after cost. Aim for at least PKR 200–500 net per order so returns and the occasional undelivered parcel don't wipe out your profit. Because a no-minimum supplier ships one unit at a time, your startup budget can be close to zero — your real investment is the time spent posting and replying. ## COD, delivery timelines and returns


Cash on delivery is the default in Pakistan and it's the biggest trust lever you have — buyers who won't prepay will happily order COD. The supplier handles collection and remits your margin, so you never chase payments. Local Islamabad and Rawalpindi orders are often next-day; the rest of Pakistan is typically 3–5 days. Returns are the trade-off with COD: some parcels come back undelivered, which is normal, so price for it rather than fearing it. ## Marketing to twin-city buyers


You don't need ad spend to start. The cheapest channels convert best here: post the product on your WhatsApp status daily, list it on Facebook Marketplace and local Islamabad and Rawalpindi buy-and-sell groups, and record a 15-second Reel or TikTok showing it in use. Reference familiar areas — delivery to Saddar, G-11, Bahria Town, DHA — so buyers feel it's local and reliable. Reply within minutes; in COD selling, the fastest responder usually gets the order. Once a product sells consistently, put a small budget behind the post that's already working rather than guessing with a new one.


## Common mistakes to avoid


A few errors sink new resellers. Listing too many products at once, so you can't answer questions on any of them. Pricing at cost-plus-a-little instead of to the market, leaving no room for returns. Ignoring return rates until they eat the month's profit. And picking a supplier on price alone — a cheap rate with slow delivery or poor return handling costs more than it saves. ## Quick answers


### Can I start dropshipping in Islamabad or Rawalpindi with no money?


Yes. With a no-minimum, cash-on-delivery supplier you don't buy stock upfront — you only order after a customer has paid, so the startup cost is close to zero.


### How long does delivery take from the twin cities?


Local Islamabad and Rawalpindi orders are often next-day, and delivery to the rest of Pakistan is typically 3–5 days through standard couriers.


### What sells best in Islamabad and Rawalpindi?


Winter wear and heating accessories in the cold months, and year-round ladies' fashion, mobile gadgets, kitchen and home tools, and kids' items.


### Do I need to register a business to start?


No. You can start as an individual reseller sharing products and collecting orders. Formal registration only becomes worthwhile once your volume grows.


### Is it better to source locally or from China?


Source locally for fast-moving basics you can buy near Raja Bazaar, and go China-direct for gadgets and trend items Pakistan doesn't make — comparing landed cost, not sticker price.
