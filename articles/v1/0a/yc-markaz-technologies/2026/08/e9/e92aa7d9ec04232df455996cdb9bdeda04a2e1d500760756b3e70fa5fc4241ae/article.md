# Packaging & Branding for Small Online Sellers in Pakistan

For a small online seller in Pakistan, packaging and branding are two cheap jobs that often get confused with each other. Packaging is getting the parcel to the buyer intact and looking like it came from a real shop. Branding is making sure the buyer remembers who sent it. Neither one needs a designer or a big budget.


The short version: buy a courier flyer or a small carton that fits the product, add one protective layer, seal it properly, and stick on a clear address label. Then add one branded thing — a sticker, a rubber stamp, or a small thank-you card carrying your name and WhatsApp number. That is the entire starter kit, and it costs a few rupees per order.


The rest of this guide is the detail: what to buy, what it costs you in courier weight, what to skip, and how branding works when a supplier packs the parcel for you.


## Packaging and branding are two different jobs


Keep them separate in your head, because they fail in different ways and they cost different amounts.


- **Packaging fails at the door.** A cracked bottle, a creased shirt box, a torn flyer with the address rubbed off. The result is a refused parcel, a return leg you pay for, and a customer who will not order again.
- **Branding fails later.** The product arrives fine, the customer is happy, and three weeks on they cannot remember where they bought it. The second order goes to whoever posted a video that day.


Packaging is a physical problem with a fixed cost per order. Branding is a memory problem, and most of it is free.


## What to actually buy for packaging


You can price all of this at any wholesale packaging market — Jodia Bazar in Karachi, the Shah Alam Market area in Lahore, and the general packaging bazaars in Faisalabad and Rawalpindi all stock the same items. Buy in a few hundred units, not thousands, until you know your product mix.


### 1. The outer layer


Two real options for a small seller:


- **Courier flyers (poly mailers).** Plastic bags with a self-seal strip. Cheapest per unit, lightest, waterproof, and they have a pouch for the invoice. Right for clothes, fabric, scarves, socks, soft toys, unbreakable accessories. Wrong for anything that can be crushed.
- **Corrugated cartons.** Cardboard boxes, priced by size and ply. Heavier and more expensive, but the only sane choice for glass, bottles, ceramics, electronics, watches, cosmetics and anything with a rigid retail box you do not want dented.


Buy two or three flyer sizes and two carton sizes. More than that and you will spend your evenings guessing which one fits.


### 2. The protective layer


Whatever is inside the outer layer has to not move. A parcel is thrown into a bag, ridden on a bike, stacked, and thrown again.


- **Bubble wrap** for anything fragile — one full wrap, not a decorative strip.
- **Air pillows or crumpled paper** to fill the empty space in a carton. If the box rattles when you shake it, it is not packed.
- **Small zip bags** for loose parts, screws, chargers, spare buttons. Losing one small part turns a good order into a full return.
- **A plastic sleeve** around clothing before it goes into the flyer, so a burst flyer does not mean a ruined shirt.


### 3. Sealing and labelling


Cheap tape is where small sellers lose parcels. Buy proper packing tape and run it along the seam and over both ends of a carton. For flyers, press the whole self-seal strip down, then tape across it.


The address label matters more than any of it. Print it if you can. If you write by hand, use a permanent marker, write the phone number twice, and cover the label with clear tape so rain and handling do not erase it. Riders do not chase illegible addresses — they mark the parcel undelivered.


### 4. Weight, because that is what you pay for


Couriers charge by weight, and for large light parcels they charge by volumetric weight — the size of the box converted into a weight figure. So an oversized carton with air in it costs you money on every single order, forever.


Two rules that pay for themselves: pick the smallest outer layer the product safely fits into, and weigh a packed sample of each product on a kitchen scale before you set your price. Packaging that pushes a parcel into the next weight slab is the most common invisible margin leak in a small operation.


## What a parcel has to survive in Pakistan


Most orders here are cash on delivery, which changes the job. The buyer has not paid yet. They are deciding at the door, holding the parcel, with a rider waiting. Every packaging decision either helps that moment or hurts it.


- **It has to look sealed and untouched.** A re-taped flyer reads as "someone opened this".
- **It has to survive rain and dust.** Bikes, no covered vans, monsoon weeks.
- **It has to be openable without a knife.** Many buyers ask to check the parcel before paying. A carton wrapped in six layers of tape irritates everyone standing there.
- **The outside should not advertise the contents.** For personal-care items, intimate wear or expensive electronics, a plain outer layer is the courtesy that gets the parcel accepted.


Packaging is also one of the few return causes you fully control. Wrong item, damaged item and "the box was open" are all packing-table problems, not courier problems. ### The open-parcel question


Decide your policy before your first order, write it in your bio, and repeat it on every confirmation message. Either the buyer may open and check before paying, or they may not. Both are workable. What loses money is discovering the disagreement while the rider is on the phone from the customer's gate.


## Branding on a small seller's budget


Branding for a new online seller is not a logo project. It is four decisions, and you can make all of them this week.


### Pick a name you can spell over the phone


Short, easy to say in Urdu and English, and searchable. Check that the Instagram handle, the Facebook page name and the WhatsApp Business display name are all available in the same spelling before you commit — one name spelled three ways is how a customer fails to find you again.


### One logo, one colour, one font


A wordmark — your name set in a clean font — is a perfectly good logo for a reselling business, and it beats a complicated illustration at sticker size. Pick one accent colour and use it on your sticker, your profile picture and your video captions. Repetition is what makes a small brand look established. ### The three cheapest branded items, in order


1. **A printed sticker roll.** Your name, handle and phone number. One sticker on every parcel turns any plain flyer into your packaging. Cheapest per order by a distance and the one thing every seller should buy first.
2. **A rubber stamp.** One-time cost, unlimited uses, works on cartons, flyers and paper bags. Ugly on glossy plastic, excellent on brown card.
3. **A thank-you card.** A business-card-sized slip: thank you, your WhatsApp number, and one line telling them what to do if something is wrong. That last line prevents more angry posts than any refund policy.


Printed flyers and custom boxes come later — after you know which products repeat, and after the order volume makes a print run worth it. Ordering 5,000 branded boxes for a product you have sold eleven times is the classic first-year mistake.


### What to skip at the start


- Custom-printed courier flyers with your logo — high minimum order, and your product mix will change.
- Ribbons, wax seals and tissue paper on low-margin items. They add weight and add nothing to a COD buyer's decision.
- An expensive logo. Nobody has ever refused a parcel over typography.
- A brand name so clever nobody can spell it after hearing it once.


## Most of your branding is not on the box


The parcel arrives once. Your page is where the buyer meets you before and after, so that is where most of the branding work sits.


- **Consistent product photos.** Same background, same lighting, same crop. A grid that looks like one shop sells more than a grid of borrowed supplier images with six different watermarks.
- **One voice in every reply.** Whatever it is — formal, friendly, Roman Urdu — keep it the same in comments, DMs and confirmation calls.
- **Product copy that answers the real questions.** Size, material, what is in the box, delivery time, COD. Our guide to[writing product descriptions that sell](https://www.markaz.app/blog-post/how-to-write-product-descriptions-that-sell) has the structure worth copying.
- **A visible delivery and return promise.** Written once, repeated everywhere. This is the single most valuable brand asset a COD seller owns.
- **The follow-up message.** Two days after delivery: "Did it reach you fine?" It costs nothing, catches problems before they become public complaints, and it is the reason people order twice.


If short video is where your buyers are, the same consistency rules apply on camera — same handle, same colour, same promise in every clip. ## Who packs the parcel when you dropship?


This is the part most branding advice skips, and it decides which of the ideas above apply to you.


If you hold stock, you pack. You control the flyer, the sticker, the card and the unboxing, and every branding idea in this guide is available to you. You also pay for the stock upfront and carry whatever does not sell.


If you dropship, the supplier packs and ships to your customer. You do not touch the parcel, so packaging is not your cost or your decision — and it is not your risk either. What you own instead is everything around the parcel: the product page, the photos and captions, the confirmation message, the delivery promise and the follow-up. ## Costing it properly


Packaging is a per-order cost and it belongs in your price, not in your surprise. Before you set a price, write down every line for one packed order:


- Outer layer — flyer or carton
- Protective filler — bubble wrap, paper, zip bag
- Tape and label
- Sticker, stamp ink or card
- The courier's weight slab for the packed weight, including any volumetric bump


Add that total to the product cost and the courier's COD handling, then set your price. As a labelled example of the method, not a price list: weigh a packed sample, look up the slab it falls into, and check whether a smaller carton drops it a slab. If it does, that saving repeats on every order you ever ship. ## Mistakes that cost money


- **Oversized boxes.** You pay for air on every parcel, and the product rattles.
- **Cheap tape on a heavy carton.** The seam opens in transit and the parcel comes back.
- **Handwritten labels with no tape over them.** One rainy afternoon and the address is gone.
- **Branding before selling.** Six weeks on a logo, zero orders. Post the product, take the orders, print the sticker after.
- **Bulk-ordering printed packaging early.** Product mix changes fast in the first year and printed stock does not.
- **Different names on different platforms.** The customer who wants to reorder cannot find you.
- **No note inside the parcel.** A slip with your WhatsApp number turns a one-time buyer into someone who can reach you.


## Start with this


Buy two flyer sizes, one carton size, a roll of proper packing tape, bubble wrap, and one printed sticker roll with your name and number. Weigh a packed sample of each product. Write your delivery and return promise in one paragraph and put it in your bio. Then pack ten orders and note what went wrong.


That is a full packaging and branding setup for a small seller in Pakistan, and it costs less than one bad return. Everything fancier — printed boxes, tissue paper, a designed logo — is a decision to make once you know which products keep coming back.
