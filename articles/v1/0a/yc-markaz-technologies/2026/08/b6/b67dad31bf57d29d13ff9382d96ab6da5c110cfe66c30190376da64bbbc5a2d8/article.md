# Factory-Price Wholesale: Lowest Rates for Pakistani Resellers

The lowest real wholesale rate in Pakistan depends on one thing: how much you can buy at once. If you can commit to bulk — dozens of pieces per article — the classic wholesale markets like Azam Cloth Market in Lahore or Jodia Bazaar in Karachi still beat everyone on per-unit price. If you resell one order at a time, a no-minimum wholesale app gets you factory-tier rates without the travel, the haggling or the dead stock. And if you want products your local market doesn't have, China-direct sourcing wins — but only when you count the landed cost, not the sticker price. This guide walks through all three routes, the maths that separates a real factory price from a marketing line, and how resellers with small budgets get bulk-market rates from the first order.


## What "factory price" really means


Every supplier calls their rate a factory price. In practice there are three tiers. The mill or factory gate rate is what a distributor pays for full production lots. The wholesale market rate is that plus the market trader's margin — still cheap, but you're expected to buy in dozens. The retail-wholesale rate is what most "wholesale" websites actually charge: a discount off retail, nowhere near the factory gate.


Two questions expose which tier you're being offered. First: what is the minimum order quantity? A genuine factory-tier rate usually demands volume — if there's no minimum and the price is still low, the seller is aggregating many small buyers into bulk purchasing power, which is the model that works best for new resellers. Second: what's included? A rate that excludes delivery, or quietly adds "handling", is not comparable to an all-in price.


## Route 1: the physical wholesale markets


Azam Cloth Market (Lahore), Jodia Bazaar and Boulton Market (Karachi), Ghanta Ghar (Multan) — these markets have supplied Pakistani shopkeepers for generations, and for true bulk they remain the cheapest per unit. The trade-offs are real, though: you pay cash up front, you travel, you inspect quality piece by piece, and whatever doesn't sell is your loss. For a reseller testing ten products, buying a dozen of each turns a PKR 15,000 budget into inventory risk before the first sale.

## A worked example of landed cost


Say you're pricing a ladies' handbag. Example numbers: local market rate PKR 850 per piece at a dozen minimum, so PKR 10,200 up front. A no-minimum wholesale app lists it at PKR 900 with delivery included and no stock risk. A China platform shows PKR 600, but agent, freight and customs bring it to PKR 950 landed. The market wins only if you're confident of selling all twelve; the app wins on cash flow; China wins when the bag isn't available locally at all. Lowest rate is a per-product decision, not a loyalty decision.

## Quick answers


### Where are the cheapest wholesale rates in Pakistan?


For bulk buyers: the traditional wholesale markets of Lahore, Karachi and Multan. For single-unit buyers: no-minimum wholesale apps, where the per-piece rate tracks the bulk market without the inventory risk.


### Is buying from China cheaper than local wholesale?


On sticker price, almost always. On landed cost, only sometimes — duty and freight can double the price. Compare landed cost per piece, and use China-direct for products with no good local source.


### Can I get factory prices without a minimum order?


Yes — platforms that pool thousands of small resellers buy at volume tiers and pass the rate down per unit. That's the model Markaz runs for both its Pakistan and China catalogues.
