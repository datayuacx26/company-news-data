# Product Bundling: Raise Your Average Order Value

Bundling means selling two or more items together as one offer at one price, so a single delivery carries more value. It is the cheapest way for a small seller in Pakistan to earn more without finding a single new customer.


The reason it works here is arithmetic. Your cost per order — the courier charge, the COD handling fee, the packing, the confirmation call, your time — is mostly fixed no matter what is inside the parcel. Sell one item and that cost stack eats a full margin. Sell two in the same parcel and the second item carries almost none of it. Average order value goes up, cost per order barely moves, and the difference is profit.


This guide covers the bundle types that sell in Pakistan, how to price one without giving away your margin, what bundling does to your COD refusal rate, and how to run bundles when a supplier ships for you.


## What average order value is, and why it is the number to move


Average order value is your total sales divided by your number of orders. If ten orders bring in a certain amount, the average is that amount divided by ten. Work it out for last month before you read further — most sellers have never calculated it and are surprised by how low it is.


There are only three ways to grow: more customers, more orders per customer, or more value per order. The first costs money in ads. The second takes months of trust. The third you can change this afternoon by rewriting your offer.


And it compounds with everything else. A higher average order value means each rupee of ad spend has more to earn back, each delivery run is worth more, and a refused parcel is a smaller share of a bigger month.


## Why bundles work harder under cash on delivery


COD selling has costs that a prepaid model does not, and every one of them is charged per parcel rather than per item:


- **Delivery.** Couriers price by parcel and weight slab. A second small item often rides in the same slab for nothing.
- **COD handling.** Charged on the order, not the line item.
- **Packing.** One bag, one label, one strip of tape.
- **Your time.** One confirmation call, one dispatch, one follow-up — whether the parcel holds one item or four.
- **Refusal risk.** One parcel is one chance of being refused. Two separate orders are two.


That last one matters more than sellers expect. Every parcel you send carries a risk of coming back, and the return leg is paid whether or not the buyer takes it. Fewer, fuller parcels is a quieter way to run the same revenue. ## Five bundle types that sell in Pakistan


### 1. The complete-the-job bundle


Everything needed to finish the task the buyer came for. A phone case with a screen protector and a cleaning cloth. A hair dryer with a heat-protection spray and a round brush. A baby feeding set with the bottle brush.


This is the strongest bundle type because it is not a discount, it is a favour. The buyer was going to need the second item anyway; you saved them a second search and a second delivery charge.


### 2. The multi-pack


The same item, more than one. Socks, undergarments, kitchen cloths, storage boxes, hair ties, phone cables. Anything that gets used up, lost, or is needed by more than one person in a house.


Multi-packs are the easiest bundle to launch because you do not need a second product, only a second unit — and buyers already expect a per-unit saving on quantity, so the offer explains itself.


### 3. The starter kit


A curated set for someone doing something for the first time. A skincare starter set. A first sewing kit. A car-cleaning set. Sell the outcome, not the parts: someone buying their first set does not know what to choose, and a kit removes the choosing.


### 4. The variety or family pack


Mixed sizes, colours or flavours in one box. It solves the buyer's biggest hesitation — picking wrong — and it lets you move slow-selling variants alongside popular ones without discounting them separately.


### 5. The add-on offer


Not a fixed bundle. One extra item offered at the moment of ordering: "Add a spare filter for a small amount extra?" On WhatsApp this is one message after the buyer confirms, and it is the single highest-return habit in this whole guide because you are asking someone who has already decided to buy.


Keep the add-on cheap relative to the main item, obviously related, and offered once. Ask twice and you sound like a call centre.


## Pricing a bundle without giving away the margin


The discount on a bundle comes out of the second item's margin, never out of your costs. Build the price from the bottom:


1. **Add up the landed cost of every item** in the bundle — what you pay the supplier, plus anything you pay to get it into the parcel.
2. **Add the per-order costs once** , not once per item: courier, COD handling, packing, and a realistic allowance for refused parcels.
3. **Add your target margin** on the whole bundle, in PKR rather than as a percentage. Percentages hide small numbers.
4. **Compare with the buy-separately total.** Your bundle price should sit visibly below it, but the saving comes from the per-order costs you no longer pay twice — not from selling anything below its landed cost.
5. **Check the floor.** If the bundle price is under the combined landed cost plus per-order costs, you are paying customers to order. Redo it.


*Worked example — use your own figures, never numbers copied from an article:* take the supplier price of item A and item B, add them. Add your courier charge, COD fee and packing cost once. Add your margin. That is the bundle price. Now add up what those two items would cost bought separately, including two delivery charges. The gap between the two numbers is what you show the buyer. If there is no gap, the bundle is not a real offer and buyers can tell.

### Free delivery as the bundle trigger


The simplest version of bundling is a free-delivery threshold: spend above a set amount and delivery is on you. Set the threshold above your current average order value — somewhat above, not double — so it is a stretch the buyer can actually make, and make sure the margin at that threshold covers the delivery you just gave away.


## Choosing what to bundle


Do not guess. The answer is in orders you have already taken.


- **Read your own order history.** Which two items keep appearing in the same chat? That pair is your first bundle, and it is already proven.
- **Ask why the buyer bought.** The job they hired the product for tells you what else the job needs.
- **Bundle a fast seller with a slow one** — but only where the pairing makes sense. Random pairs read as clearing old stock, because they are.
- **Watch weight and size.** A bundle that pushes into the next courier weight slab can cost more to deliver than the extra item earns.
- **Do not bundle two fragile items.** One breakage refuses the whole parcel, and you lose both margins plus the return leg.
- **Keep it to two or three items.** Longer bundles are harder to photograph, harder to price and harder to restock.

## Presenting the bundle so it actually sells


A bundle that is not obvious is not a bundle. Four things do most of the work:


- **One photo with everything laid out.** All items visible in one frame, on a plain background. Buyers must see what they get without reading.
- **A name, not a list.** "Kitchen Starter Set" beats "chopper + peeler + grater". Name the outcome.
- **One price, with the saving stated once.** Show the bundle price and what the items cost separately. Say it plainly and move on.
- **A description that covers every item.** Sizes, materials, what is in the box — for each piece. Vague bundle listings cause the "this is not what I expected" refusal at the door. ## Bundling when the supplier ships for you


Dropshippers can bundle, with one rule: check how the items dispatch before you sell them as one offer.


On[Markaz](https://www.markaz.app/shop) you order per item with no minimum, the supplier ships, and cash on delivery is collected and settled to you in PKR. Two items from the same supplier usually travel together. Two items from different suppliers may arrive on different days, and a buyer who paid one price for one bundle will not accept half of it at the door.


So, three checks before you list a bundle you do not pack yourself:


- **Same supplier where possible.** One dispatch, one parcel, one cash collection.
- **Same delivery window.** Local stock runs 3–5 days; items coming from China run roughly 10–17 days. Bundling one of each means the buyer waits for the slower one — say so up front, or do not pair them.
- **Same cash amount at the door.** The rider collects one figure. Make sure it matches the figure you quoted, for the whole bundle.


Where the delivery windows do not line up, sell the bundle as a pre-order with the honest date, or build it entirely from[China deals](https://www.markaz.app/china/deals) so the whole set arrives on the same timeline. Track the dispatches in the[dropshipping portal](https://www.markaz.app/dropshipping-portal) rather than in a chat thread — a split bundle is the order you most need to be able to look up.


## What bundling does to your refusal rate


Higher order value means more cash at the door, and more cash at the door means a slightly higher chance the buyer changes their mind. Bundles pay for themselves anyway, but do not pretend the effect is zero. Three things keep it small:


- **Confirm the total before dispatch.** One message stating the items, the bundle price and the delivery window. Never let the amount be a surprise.
- **Show the saving in the confirmation.** Reminding the buyer what they saved is the cheapest way to keep an order warm.
- **Do not bundle upward too fast.** Doubling your average order value in one step raises refusals more than a steady climb. Move the number, then hold it.

## Mistakes that make bundles lose money


- **Discounting from the retail price instead of building from cost.** The saving must come from the per-order costs you avoid, not from your margin.
- **Bundling items nobody buys together.** A pairing that makes no sense reads as stock clearance and gets ignored.
- **Ignoring the weight slab.** Check the courier's slabs before you set the price, not after the first month's bill.
- **Too many items.** Three is plenty. More is a photograph nobody can read.
- **Running every product as a bundle.** Keep single units available. Some buyers only want the one thing, and a forced bundle loses that sale entirely.
- **Never measuring.** If you do not know your average order value before and after, you have no idea whether the bundle worked.


## A two-week test


1. **Day 1.** Work out last month's average order value. Write the number down.
2. **Day 2.** Read your order history and find the two items that keep appearing together.
3. **Day 3.** Price that pair from cost upward, and check it against the buy-separately total.
4. **Day 4.** Shoot one flat-lay photo of the bundle and name it after the outcome.
5. **Day 5.** Pin it to your catalogue and post it once.
6. **Days 6–13.** Offer the add-on to every buyer who confirms an order. One message, once.
7. **Day 14.** Recalculate the average order value. Compare it with day 1, and check your refusal rate moved with it.


If the number went up and refusals held steady, build the second bundle. If refusals climbed, the bundle price outran what your buyers are ready to hand over at the door — bring it down a step and try again.
