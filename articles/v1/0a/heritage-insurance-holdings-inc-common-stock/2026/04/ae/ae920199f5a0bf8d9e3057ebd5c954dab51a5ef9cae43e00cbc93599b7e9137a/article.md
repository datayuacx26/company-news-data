# Spring Home Maintenance

Home maintenance is an essential part of being a homeowner. After months of colder weather, spring is the perfect time for homeowners to inspect, clean, and maintain their homes. Winter weather can take a toll on your roof, gutters, landscaping, and other key systems, and addressing small issues now can help prevent larger, and more expensive problems later.


### ****


### **Inspect Your Roof for Winter Damage**


Your roof is one of the most important parts of your home. Winter storms, snow, and ice can loosen or damage shingles, leaving your roof vulnerable to leaks. It is important to have your home and roof inspected after winter. Ensure that your inspection includes looking for missing or cracked shingles, damaged flashing around chimneys and vents, signs of sagging areas, and debris buildup from branches and leaves. If you notice damage or areas of concern on your own, you may want to contact a professional to complete an inspection. Catching issues early can help prevent water damage and costly repairs down the line.


### **Clean Your Gutters and Downspouts**


Gutters play a crucial role in directing rainwater away from your home’s foundation. During the winter, gutters often collect leaves, debris, and even ice buildup that can clog the system. Spring is the ideal time to remove leaves, twigs, and debris from gutters. Downspouts are how water clears smoothly off your roof. Make sure that downspouts are clear and directing water away from the home. Properly flowing gutters and downspouts will help prevent foundation damage, basement flooding, and exterior water intrusion.


### **Service Your HVAC System**


As temperatures rise, your air conditioning system will soon be working overtime. Spring is the perfect time to ensure your HVAC system is running properly and efficiently, before it gets too hot. Now is a good time to replace the air filters in your home and clean any dust and buildup that may have collected on or around the vent. Test your thermostat and if something seems off, schedule a professional HVAC tune-up. Routine maintenance can extend the life of your system, improve indoor air quality, and help reduce energy costs during warmer months.


### **Clean Your Yard and Landscaping**


Winter weather can leave your yard looking a little rough. Plan to trim back overgrown trees and branches. Inspect trees for damage or rotting from the winter months and consider hiring a licensed professional to cut them down. Dead or rotting trees are more likely to blow over in heavy winds during a spring or summer storm. Keep healthy trees and bushes trimmed back away from your home and any powerlines. Inspect outdoor sprinkler heads for damage from snowplows or cold weather. You may also want to adjust your sprinkler timers to align with your region’s rainfall and watering needs. A tidy, well-maintained landscape enhances curb appeal and the overall wellbeing of your home. Early attention helps your yard and landscaping thrive during a busy season.


### **Fixing Leaky Pipes**


Freezing temperatures in winter months can cause both major and minor pipe damage. Pinhole leaks and hairline cracks are the most common kinds of damage resulting from winter weather. Both kinds of damage start with small drips of water but eventually lead to costly damage over time. Listening for dripping sounds or steadily increasing water bills can be a tell-tale sign that a pipe leak has occurred. Beyond that, you can proactively inspect your pipes throughout the year to catch a leak before it causes too much damage. Start by examining visible pipes in basements, under sinks, and in utility areas for signs of corrosion, discoloration, or mineral buildup, which are all indicators of slow leaks. You can also always watch out for more obvious signs of a leak like water stains, bubbling paint, or warping wood. These are the most common visual indicators of a leak. Don’t wait until it’s too late, and the water damage in your home is severe. Be proactive and call a professional to complete an inspection.


### **Creating a Home Maintenance Plan**


Developing a regular home maintenance schedule can prevent problems from occurring. Spring is a great time to create a structured schedule to help you keep your property in good condition throughout the year. Your home maintenance schedule should have different cadences of events for different tasks. Seasonal maintenance tasks like inspecting your roof in the spring for damage from the winter months or clearing the gutters to remove leaves in the fall after summer storms can easily help address weather-related issues. Monthly tasks you may want to complete are testing smoke and carbon monoxide detectors, changing and cleaning HVAC filters, inspecting for plumbing leaks under the sinks and around the toilets, looking for signs of pest and insect activity, and ensuring that drains and gutters are flowing properly. Annually you can have larger services completed by a professional like a roof inspection, plumbing inspection, electrical system check, water heater maintenance, dryer vent cleaning, and HVAC inspection and tune up.
