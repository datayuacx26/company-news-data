## What an AI executive assistant actually is, and is not


The seven tools here are not the same kind of thing. Three only touch the calendar. One only takes meeting notes. One only works in the inbox. One is a general chatbot you prompt task by task, and one tries to run the whole role. What separates the real assistants from a clever chatbot is simple: a real one connects to your email and calendar and acts on them, rather than sitting in a separate window waiting for the next prompt. Where each tool stops against that bar, connecting and acting versus only drafting, is what decides which fits you.


If you want the category itself explained first, our[guide to what an AI executive assistant is and how to choose one](https://www.marblism.com/blog/ai-executive-assistant) covers the types, the limits, and the questions worth asking before you shop.


## What actually matters in an AI executive assistant


What matters in an AI executive assistant is whether it finishes the job, stays on the leash you give it, sounds like you, bills predictably, and leaves the final call to you. These tools all look good in a demo. What actually gets one dropped a couple of months in comes down to those five things, and you can see every one of them in the bad reviews. Check a tool against all five before you hand over your calendar.


### 1. It finishes the job


The tools worth paying for send the reply and book the meeting. If a tool only hands you a draft to check, fix, and send, you are still doing the job yourself. Scheduling works the same way: a tool that proposes times you confirm one by one has not taken the work off you. Did the reply actually go out, and did the meeting land on your calendar, without you finishing it by hand?


### 2. It does what you asked, without taking over


The complaint that comes up most for AI schedulers is overcorrecting. You ask to move one meeting, and the tool spends the next hour rearranging your whole week and pinging you about every change. This is the single most common gripe about auto-schedulers. A good assistant does the specific thing you asked, leaves the rest alone, and escalates only when something genuinely conflicts.


### 3. It sounds like you and gets the facts right


The headline promise of an AI email assistant is that it learns your voice. In practice, many drafts still read like a robot and slip in errors a client will notice, so you rewrite every one. A reply that needs heavy editing is slower than writing it yourself. Send a week of real replies through a drafting tool and count how many you could send untouched. That number, not the demo, tells you whether it sounds like you.


### 4. The price is predictable


Pricing is what these tools get slammed for most, usually the ones that bill by credit or by task. A single background agent can burn a month of credits, and people drop the tool the day the bill stops being predictable. Two patterns cause it. One is a credit allowance that resets monthly, so a busy fortnight leaves you rationing. The other is a usage cap that quietly throttles you once you cross it, which is the same problem without the invoice. Four of the seven run some kind of allowance rather than pure flat pricing, Marblism's pooled hours included. Model your busiest month against whatever the cap is before you commit.


### 5. Your data and your decisions stay under your control


An executive assistant sees your email, your calendar, and your contacts, so where that data goes matters more than with most software. Check two things. First, whether the vendor trains its models on your content or keeps it private. The good ones say plainly that they do not train on your data, and several now put the opt-out on the free plan. Second, whether the tool acts on its own or asks first. For anything client-facing you want an approval step, so a confident and wrong AI cannot send the email or book the meeting before you see it.


## AI assistant vs hiring a human EA


The parts of the role these tools genuinely finish are the recurring ones: email triage, scheduling, meeting notes, reminders, and first-draft replies, for a tiny fraction of a salary. What none of them does is the judgment, the discretion, and the relationships. That is handling a sensitive client with care, deciding what matters most when everything is urgent, or representing you in a conversation that carries real weight. An AI assistant clears the admin so a person is free for the part that needs a person.


The cost gap is large enough to change the math for most owners, which is why most never make the hire. Base pay for a full-time executive assistant in the US sits near $76,000 a year, on[Bureau of Labor Statistics wage data](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/43-6011.00) . Add the 25 to 40 percent that benefits and payroll tax typically pile on, and the real cost is past $95,000 before anyone has answered an email. The single-purpose AI tools here run from free to roughly $30 a month for the plans most owners use, with heavier tiers above that. An all-in-one AI Executive Assistant like Marblism starts at $24 a month. Even a full stack of three or four tools rarely passes $150 a month, which is under 3 percent of that annual cost.


The realistic comparison is not AI versus a $76,000-a-year human assistant. It is AI versus the nothing most founders currently have. There is a fuller breakdown of[how an AI executive assistant stacks up against a human virtual assistant](https://www.marblism.com/blog/ai-executive-assistant-vs-virtual-assistant-who-handles-your-inbox-better) , and of[what a virtual assistant actually costs](https://www.marblism.com/blog/virtual-assistant-cost) if you are weighing the hire. Hand the repeatable admin to AI, keep the judgment calls for yourself, and you get most of the relief at almost none of the cost.


## AI calendar assistants to plan and protect your time


AI calendar assistants are the tools that plan and defend your time, and three own the category from different angles. Motion auto-schedules your whole day. Reclaim.ai defends focus time and habits around the meetings you already have. Akiflow keeps you in manual control while pulling every task into one place. The right one depends on how much of your calendar you want an AI to run: Motion rebuilds the day, Reclaim.ai defends time around the meetings you already have, and Akiflow leaves the scheduling to you.


### 1. Motion, best for letting AI own the whole day


[Motion](https://www.usemotion.com/) is the most automated calendar tool here: you hand it your tasks and deadlines, and it decides when each one happens. For an owner whose day has no structure, the appeal is waking up to a calendar that already made those decisions. The trade is that it wants to own the whole day, not help with part of it.


#### Key Features


- Auto-scheduling that time-blocks tasks into open calendar slots
- Automatic re-planning when meetings or priorities change
- Combined view of Google, Outlook, and iCloud calendars
- AI notetaker with action items and follow-up email drafts
- Project and task management with an AI project builder
- AI docs, wiki, and notes alongside the planner


#### Pros


- Removes the daily decision of what to work on next
- Pulls tasks, projects, and calendars into one place
- Re-plans around disruptions without you touching it
- Strong fit for founders tracking many deadlines at once
- Doubles as a project manager, not only a scheduler


#### Cons


- The interface is dense, and reviewers describe a real learning curve
- Two to four weeks before the auto-scheduling clicks
- Long-term subscribers say the price keeps climbing while the interface stays the same
- The mobile app lags well behind the desktop version
- No permanent free plan, only a trial


#### Pricing


There is no free plan, only a free trial.[Motion's Pro AI plan is $19 per seat a month](https://www.usemotion.com/pricing) with 7,500 AI credits, and Business AI is $29 per seat a month with 15,000 credits, both on annual billing. The AI runs on that monthly credit allowance, and extra credits cost 25 cents per 100 on Pro. Heavy months draw down faster, so this is the metered kind of pricing worth modelling before you commit.

The founder with more tasks and deadlines than they can track, who wants an AI to own the calendar and decide the order of the day. ## AI notetaker to capture every meeting


Neither the calendar nor the email tools capture a meeting. For a week of back-to-back calls a dedicated notetaker is often the highest-value thing to add, because you cannot take good notes and pay attention at the same time.

The company had a breakout year, raising[a $125 million round at a $1.5 billion valuation in March 2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/25/granola-raises-125m-hits-1-5b-valuation-as-it-expands-from-meeting-notetaker-to-enterprise-ai-app/) as it expanded from notetaker toward a wider AI workspace.

#### Pros

## Frequently asked questions


### Choosing between the tools


#### Is there a free AI executive assistant?


There are free single-purpose ones, but no free assistant that covers the whole role. Reclaim.ai's Lite plan is free forever for one person, capped at one calendar sync and a one-week scheduling range. Granola's Basic plan is free with limited meeting history, Shortwave has a free plan for individuals, and ChatGPT's free tier handles most on-demand asks. Motion and Akiflow run trials rather than permanent free plans. Every tool that takes the whole executive-assistant role is paid, Marblism included, because running the inbox and calendar continuously costs real compute.


#### Is there an AI executive assistant app?


Most of these ship as desktop and mobile apps rather than a browser tab alone. Motion, Akiflow, and Shortwave have desktop and mobile apps, Granola now runs on Mac, Windows, iOS, and Android, and ChatGPT is on every platform. Marblism runs in the browser, because Eva works in the background rather than being something you sit in front of. If you want one you can run from your phone, check the mobile app specifically, since a few of these are noticeably weaker there than on desktop.


#### Which AI assistant is best for a solo founder?


A solo founder with no assistant usually has two good routes. One is the single tool that fixes their worst bottleneck, often Reclaim.ai for the calendar or Shortwave for the inbox. The other is a single assistant that covers the whole role instead of one task. Marblism fits the second case: Eva handles inbox, calendar, and scheduling from $24 a month, which is cheaper than running three separate tools and far below any human hire.


#### What is the best AI assistant for a CEO?


For a CEO with a human assistant already, the answer is usually the tools that make that person faster: Reclaim.ai for delegated calendar control, which lets an assistant run the diary from their own login, and Granola for meeting capture. For a CEO or founder without one, the bottleneck is the whole role rather than any single task, which is the case for an assistant like Marblism's Eva that runs inbox, calendar, and scheduling together. The deciding question is not seniority, it is whether anyone else is currently absorbing the admin.


#### Do these AI assistants work with Outlook and Gmail?


The calendar tools, Reclaim.ai, Motion, and Akiflow, all support both Google and Outlook calendars. Email is stricter: Shortwave connects only to Gmail and Google Workspace and cannot link a Microsoft 365 or Exchange account at all, so an Outlook shop is better served by Copilot built into Microsoft 365.


### Cost and setup


#### How much does an AI executive assistant cost?


Reckon on the stack, not one tool, because most people end up running two or three. A working meetings-and-inbox pair, Granola at $14 plus Shortwave at $18, comes to about $32 a month. Add a calendar tool and you are near $42 with Reclaim.ai or $51 with Motion. A single all-in-one assistant like Marblism is $24 a month for the whole role, which is why the stack maths usually favours it once you pass two tools. Even a full three or four tool stack rarely passes $150 a month, a small fraction of a human assistant's cost.


#### Will AI replace an executive assistant?


Not the whole role. These tools finish the recurring parts, the email triage, the scheduling, the meeting notes, the reminders, and the first-draft replies. What none of them does is the judgment, the discretion, and the client relationships, which is the part that still needs a person. For an owner who never had an assistant, the honest comparison is not AI versus a human, it is AI versus doing all the admin themselves.


#### How quickly can I get one set up?


Most take minutes to start. Calendar and inbox tools like Reclaim.ai, Granola, and Shortwave connect to your existing Google account and work the same day. Auto-schedulers like Motion take a couple of weeks before the planning feels right, and Reclaim.ai works far better once you have actually tuned your habits and priorities. An all-in-one assistant like Marblism is set up by describing your business rather than wiring up integrations. Whichever you pick, give it a week of real meetings and real inbox volume before you judge it, since Motion and Reclaim.ai both need about that long to tune.
