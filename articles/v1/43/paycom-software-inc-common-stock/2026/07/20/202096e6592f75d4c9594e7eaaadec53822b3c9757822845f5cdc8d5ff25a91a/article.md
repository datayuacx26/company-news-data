# What Is Human Capital Management (HCM)?

Human capital management is a critical business function that centers on optimizing the value of an organization’s employees. Essentially, HCM is the comprehensive set of practices and tools used to recruit, manage and develop a workforce. It treats employees as valuable assets and focuses on enhancing their skills, increasing their satisfaction and promoting their growth within the company.


Effective human capital management strategies are pivotal to attracting top talent, fostering a productive work culture and driving organizational success. Below, we examine the components of HCM, how it differs from adjacent terms, its impact on employees and how to select the software that supports it.


## What is human capital management?


Human capital management is the practice of managing the entire employee life cycle as a strategic investment. This includes a range of methods and tools used to attract, recruit, train, develop, manage and retain an effective workforce.


HCM refers to both a business strategy and a category of software organizations use to manage their people from hire to retire. This covers everything from recruiting and payroll to benefits, performance and workforce planning, making it the broadest of the common HR technology categories.


## HCM vs. HR vs. HRIS vs. HRMS vs. talent management


These terms overlap and are often used interchangeably, but each describes something distinct. HCM is the umbrella; the others are either narrower systems within it or the broader strategy it sits inside.


**Term** **What it covers** **How it relates to HCM**


Human resources (HR) Core administrative functions: hiring, job and position management, compliance, recordkeeping and reporting. The traditional, task-focused foundation. HCM is the strategic layer built on top


HCM The full employee life cycle (recruiting, payroll, benefits, talent, performance and workforce planning), managed as a strategic investment. The umbrella strategy and software category. The broadest term here.


Human resources information system (HRIS) The system of record for employee data: records, policies and core HR workflows. A core component within HCM; narrower and data-focused. See[the difference between an HRIS and broader HCM](https://www.paycom.com/resources/blog/what-is-hris/) .


Human resource management system (HRMS) The applications that automate HR processes across the employee life cycle. Often used interchangeably with HCM software, HRMS emphasizes strategy over administration.


Talent management Sourcing, performance, learning, career development and succession. A subset of HCM focused on developing and retaining people.[SHRM frames it](https://www.shrm.org/topics-tools/topics/talent-management) as integrating every stage of the employee life cycle into one strategy.


Workforce management Time and labor, scheduling and absence management. A subset of HCM focused on time-based operations.


For larger organizations weighing a system of record against a full HCM suite, see[what an enterprise HRIS is and where it fits](https://www.paycom.com/resources/blog/what-is-enterprise-hris/) .


## What does human capital management include?


Human capital management is a multifaceted operation, with several components working together to strengthen and optimize a workforce. Here are the essential HCM functions.


### *Workforce planning*


Workforce planning is the strategic process of aligning a company’s human resources with its business goals. This is used to forecast future staffing needs, identify skills gaps and implement plans to find the right talent. The stakes are real: U.S. employers recorded roughly[5 million job separations in a single month](https://www.bls.gov/jlt/news.htm) . Without proactive planning, that churn turns into unfilled roles, lost institutional knowledge and reactive hiring.


### *Recruiting and hiring*


Recruiting and hiring is the process of attracting, evaluating and selecting candidates to fill roles — a crucial step in building a skilled, diverse workforce that aligns with a company’s culture and vision.


### *Benefits administration*


Benefits administration involves managing and delivering employee benefits, including health insurance, retirement plans and paid leave. This is not a minor line item: Benefits accounted for[30.1% of total compensation costs](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/ecec.nr0.htm) for private-industry employers. Solid benefits administration is key to attracting and retaining talent while accurately managing one of an employer’s largest cost categories.


### ***Payroll management***


Payroll management is the accurate, timely processing of employee compensation (wages, taxes and other deductions). In practice, that means handling real-world complexity: retroactive pay after a midcycle raise, off-cycle payments, multistate tax withholding for remote employees, shift differentials and wage garnishments. Getting these right protects[payroll compliance](https://www.paycom.com/resources/blog/payroll-compliance/) and employee trust; getting them wrong invites penalties, rework and audit exposure.


**Training, learning and development, reporting and analytics, performance management, employee experience (EX) and compensation planning** round out the core HCM functions.


## What are the benefits and challenges of human capital management?


Human capital management delivers measurable organizational value, but it comes with real challenges, including competition in the labor market, high training costs, change management and the need to maintain engagement and retention. With the right strategy, organizations can maximize their workforce’s potential while streamlining essential HR functions.


Key benefits include streamlining recruitment, identifying skills gaps, improving retention and boosting operational efficiency.


### *Streamlining the recruitment process*


An effective HCM strategy increases the speed at which an organization can recruit and onboard prospective candidates. It streamlines the process by empowering hiring managers to easily manage and track applicants.


### *Identifying skills gaps*


Human capital management helps businesses identify potential skills gaps in relation to their overall strategy. Organizations can create a proactive plan to source or develop talent to help fill in the gaps.


### *Improving employee retention*


The right HCM strategy empowers employees to use their skills and training to reach their career goals. Employers have a better understanding of their workforce’s needs and can provide opportunities and training to help employees succeed.


### *Boosting operational efficiency*


Ultimately, human capital management allows an organization to enhance its operational effectiveness, driving efficiency and productivity by meeting the needs of its employees.

## Human capital management: FAQ


### *What is the difference between HR and HCM?*


HR takes a more traditional approach to workforce management, focusing primarily on the administrative functions of hiring, compensating and maintaining employees. HCM goes beyond the traditional functions of HR to develop, engage and optimize an organization’s talent and meets its overall objectives.


### *What does a human capital management system do?*


A human capital management system offers a comprehensive suite of tools that streamline the entire employee life cycle. From recruiting potential candidates to fostering their professional development, HCM systems facilitate the attraction, retention and growth of talent within an organization.


### *Why is human capital management important?*


In today’s ultra-competitive and rapidly changing business environment, effective human capital management helps organizations attract top talent, foster a productive work culture and drive organizational success.


### *What does HCM stand for?*


HCM stands for human capital management, which is both the strategy and the software organizations use to manage the full employee life cycle, from hire to retire.


### *What is HCM in HR?*


In an HR context, HCM is the strategic framework and supporting technology that turns administrative HR tasks into a coordinated approach to recruiting, developing, paying and retaining employees.


### *What is the difference between HCM and HRIS?*


An HRIS is the system of record for employee data and core HR workflows. HCM is broader: It includes the HRIS plus payroll, benefits, talent and performance management as one strategic approach. Learn more about[the role of an HRIS](https://www.paycom.com/resources/blog/what-is-hris/) .
