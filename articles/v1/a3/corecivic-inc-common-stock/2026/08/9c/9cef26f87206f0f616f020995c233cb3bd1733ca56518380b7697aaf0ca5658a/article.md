# Resident Art Contest Benefits American Legion Post in Tennessee

Artist Pablo Picasso oncesaid , “Art washes away from the soul the dust of everyday life.”


While this can be true for manypeople , it may be especially true for incarcerated individuals, as opportunities for creative expression can be more limited. This summer at CoreCivic's **South Central Correctional Center** in Clifton, Tennessee, one case manager is helping residents find a creative outlet while also supporting the facility's American Legion Post 295 by organizing a resident art contest.


Case Manager Renee Lutz recognized that there were several talented residents at the facility, so it was no surprise when she presented the art contest opportunity to them, she received 54 carefully hand-drawn and painted designs.


Lutz says it was clear those who participated put a great deal of effort into their work.


“This art contest went beyond showcasing talent — it gave participants the opportunity to use their creativity to support a meaningful cause,” Lutz says. “As they worked on their artwork, they were developing patience, focus and pride in accomplishing something that benefits veterans and the community."


The artists worked on their pieces during their free time and in between regularly scheduled activities. Lutz says creating artwork provided them with a sense of peace and time for personal reflection.


“I've seen firsthand how creative expression can help participants shift their focus from daily stressors to accomplishing something meaningful," Lutz recalled. "Completing a piece of artwork builds confidence and reminds them they are capable of creating something of which they can be proud.”


Those who entered a piece of art say they are eager to learn how their work stacked up to that of their peers. During the contest, Lutz described the atmosphere as hopeful and fun.


“They have been boasting to each other — friendly rivalry — and it has given them a boost of morale to be able to participate in something like this.”


The artwork will be auctioned in September at the local American Legion Post in Waynesboro, Tennessee, with proceeds benefitting


[South Central's American Legion Post 295](https://www.corecivic.com/news/post-295-corecivics-south-central-correctional-center-charters-first-incarcerated-veterans-american-legion-post-in-tennessee) , which is the first active post within the Tennessee Department of Correction system. Post 295 was chartered in August 2025 and is comprised solely of incarcerated veterans. Like all other American Legion Chapters, Post 295 is held to the same standards and structure, requiring leadership roles to be filled, annual membership dues paid, membership meetings held, and efforts made to support the local community.
