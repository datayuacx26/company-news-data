# Equities update | August 7th, 2026


5 min read


Week 32 saw a strong rebound across global equities as investors looked through the previous week's deleveraging and renewed confidence in the AI investment cycle following another broadly positive set of corporate earnings. Risk appetite was further strengthened supported by softer-than-expected US payroll data that reduced expectations for a September rate hike and contributed to further weakness in the US dollar. Against this backdrop, earnings across Bitcoin miners were generally weak, as lower Bitcoin prices and depressed hashprice led to multiple miners mining below their cost curve. Nevertheless, adoption across stablecoins and tokenised financial infrastructure continued to broaden, even as momentum behind the CLARITY Act stalled, with meaningful legislative progress now increasingly likely to be pushed into the post-election period or the next Congress.


## Week 32 key developments in Blockchain Equities:


-


Index performance:


The Index declined 0.8% during the week, underperforming Bitcoin, which rose 4.1%. Macro sentiment improved into the end of the week following a materially weaker-than-expected US labour-market report, with non-farm payrolls falling by 23k in July versus expectations for an 80k increase, while June payrolls were also revised sharply lower. Although unemployment edged down to 4.1%, this largely reflected a decline in labour-force participation. The softer employment data reduced expectations for a September Federal Reserve rate increase, with market-implied odds falling from roughly 55–60% to around 40–45%, supporting risk assets.

BLOCK Index July 2026 rebalance – diversifying and refining exposure across the blockchain ecosystem:


The July rebalance reflected the continued evolution of the blockchain-equity universe, with Bitdeer Technologies and Bullish added to the Index, while Canaan, SoftBank and Ceres were removed. Bitdeer strengthens exposure within Mining Operations through its large-scale self-mining capacity and proprietary SEALMINER hardware platform, while Bullish broadens exposure to institutional digital-asset infrastructure across trading, custody, market data and indices through CoinDesk, alongside emerging tokenisation opportunities. The removals largely reflected declining thematic relevance: SoftBank’s blockchain investments have remained dormant, Canaan remains heavily exposed to the challenged ASIC hardware market with comparatively limited mining scale, and Ceres’ blockchain exposure was materially reduced following SBI Holdings’ acquisition of its Bitbank stake. Overall, the rebalance continues to improve the Index’s thematic purity by increasing exposure to businesses with more direct, scalable and strategically relevant participation in the evolving blockchain ecosystem.
