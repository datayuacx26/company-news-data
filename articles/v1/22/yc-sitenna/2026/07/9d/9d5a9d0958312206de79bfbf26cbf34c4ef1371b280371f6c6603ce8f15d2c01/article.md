# The Hidden Cost of a Services-Heavy Rollout

Every infrastructure team that’s evaluated deployment software has heard some version of the same pitch: a powerful platform, endless configurability, built on a trusted enterprise foundation. What the pitch doesn’t always spell out is who’s going to build it, how long that takes, and what it costs once the initial contract is signed.


That’s the pattern we keep seeing across the market. It’s not that these platforms are bad. They’re heavy. And heavy platforms come with a services bill that rarely shows up in the first conversation.


### **Where the hidden cost actually lives**


Plenty of deployment platforms are built on top of large CRM ecosystems, which means most meaningful configuration work runs through a systems integrator, not your own team. Want to change a workflow, add a new asset type, or adjust how a milestone triggers a handoff? That’s a change request, a scoping call, and a line item.


For an enterprise fiber operator running thousands of sites, that might be a manageable cost of doing business. For a mid-market telecom contractor or a regional utility running a few hundred, it’s a different story. You’re paying enterprise-grade implementation overhead for a mid-market problem, and every adjustment after go-live means going back to the same well. The result is a platform that technically does what you need, but slowly, and at a price that keeps climbing after the deal closes.


### **Time-to-value is the real metric**


Ask any ops leader what they actually want from a new system and the answer is rarely “more configurability.” It’s speed. They want their field teams entering data on day one, their PMs seeing accurate status without chasing spreadsheets, and their leadership getting a real portfolio view without waiting on a quarterly BI refresh.


Services-heavy implementations work against all three. A typical CRM-based rollout can take six to nine months before a team is fully live, and that’s before you count the follow-on projects for reporting, integrations, or process changes six months later.


Compare that to a platform built to be configured by the people who actually run the work, not a certified partner on retainer. Sitenna customers are typically live within four weeks, using asset templates and workflows their own team set up, not a consultant’s interpretation of what they described in a discovery call.


### **Why this shows up hardest at mid-market scale**


Enterprise operators can absorb services overhead because the scale justifies it, and they usually have dedicated admins managing the platform full time. Mid-market teams, running anywhere from a few hundred to a couple thousand employees, don’t have that luxury. They need a platform that flexes with them without a change-order process attached to every adjustment.


That’s the gap worth naming directly if you’re running a services-heavy platform today. Ask yourself honestly: is the complexity you’re paying for solving a problem you actually have, or is it complexity you inherited because it came bundled with the platform.


### **What good looks like instead**


A rollout platform should get out of your team’s way, not become a second project to manage. That means:


- Configuration your own ops team can do without a partner.
- Reporting that reflects your actual site, project, and asset structure from day one.
- A go-live measured in weeks, with the ability to keep adjusting the platform yourself as your process evolves, not on a quarterly services calendar.


None of this is a knock on any particular vendor. Some of these systems are capable, and plenty of large operators use them well. But if you’re feeling the drag of a services-heavy model at your current scale, it’s worth asking what a faster, self-managed alternative could look like, especially if implementation costs and change-request timelines have started to outweigh the flexibility you were sold on.


Speed to value isn’t a nice-to-have. For a lean team running a growing site portfolio, it’s the difference between software that helps you move and software you’re stuck managing.
