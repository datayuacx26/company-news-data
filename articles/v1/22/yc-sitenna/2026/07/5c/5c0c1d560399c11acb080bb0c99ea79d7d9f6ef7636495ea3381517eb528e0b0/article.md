# 3 Handoffs That Break Infrastructure Rollouts

Talk to enough infrastructure teams, whether they’re running fiber, wireless towers, utility comms, or EV charging, and the same three problems come up regardless of vertical. They don’t show up because of bad people or bad intent. They show up because rollouts involve constant handoffs between teams, and every handoff is a place where information can get lost, delayed, or duplicated.


### **Handoff one: fragmented site data**


Site acquisition teams know things that construction teams need and often don’t get, at least not in a form they can use. A lease detail, a wayleave condition, a site access constraint, all captured somewhere, but rarely in the same system the field crew is working from. So the field crew shows up without the full picture, loses a day, and the information gets recaptured a second time, badly.


This is the most common failure point in distributed-site rollouts, and it scales in direct proportion to how many sites and stakeholders are involved. A 20-person towerco running a lean, distributed-site portfolio feels this constantly, because there’s no dedicated ops layer to catch the gaps before they cause a delay in the field.


### **Handoff two: grid connection delays**


For anything tied to grid capacity, whether that’s a battery storage site, an EV charging depot, or a utility comms build, the interconnection queue is the clock everyone is racing against. Delays here aren’t usually caused by the utility being slow for no reason. They’re caused by incomplete or inconsistent information moving between the developer, the grid operator, and whoever’s tracking restudy timelines and capacity offers internally.


When that information lives in someone’s inbox instead of a shared, current system of record, a routine restudy request can sit unanswered for weeks longer than it needs to, simply because nobody had a clear view of what stage each project was actually at.


### **Handoff three: poor rollout visibility**


This is the one that shows up last but costs the most. Leadership needs to know, in real time, which projects are on track, which are stuck, and why. Too often, that answer requires someone manually pulling status from three or four sources, formatting it into a deck, and presenting a picture that’s already a week out of date by the time it reaches the room.


Manual reporting isn’t just slow. It’s a tax on every rollout, paid every week, by someone who could be doing higher-value work instead of chasing status updates.


### **Why this pattern repeats across every vertical**


Fiber, wireless, utility comms, EV charging, battery storage: the specific milestones differ, but the underlying failure mode doesn’t. Data gets captured in one place and needed in another. Nobody owns the full picture. And the fix people usually try first, more spreadsheets, more manual check-ins, more status meetings, adds process without adding visibility.


### **What actually breaks the cycle**


The fix isn’t a bigger team or a stricter reporting cadence. It’s a system where site data, grid status, and rollout progress live in one place that every stakeholder, from field crew to leadership, can see and trust without a manual handoff in between.


If any of these three problems sound familiar, whether you’re running a lean 20-person towerco or a much larger deployment operation, the diagnosis is usually the same regardless of vertical: the pain isn’t a people problem.


It’s a visibility problem, and it’s solvable.
