# 51 PowerPoint Ideas to Make Your Next Presentation Stand Out

Every idea on this list does the same job: it gives the whole deck one idea to organize around. I usually land on mine once I know the story I'm trying to tell.


These are the concepts I reach for most:


1.


Storytelling arc:


Setup, turning point, resolution. It's the oldest structure in the book, and it still works best for pitches. People usually stick around to see how the story ends.


2.


Problem and solution:


Open with the problem, then walk straight into how you solve it. Sales and product decks live or die on this concept, and it's the one I reach for most.


3.


Before and after:


Pick a shift, whether that's a metric, a process, or a design, and frame the whole deck around it. Every slide gets an obvious reference point to build from.


4.


Journey or roadmap:


Lay out your content as a path with stops along the way. Onboarding decks practically write themselves once I frame them this way.


5.


Comparison-driven:


Build the entire deck around comparing two or more things, like tools, strategies, or time periods. Every single slide reinforces the same comparison.


6.


Data-first concept:


Let a handful of numbers drive the structure instead of building a narrative around them. I only pull this one out when the numbers are strong enough to carry the deck on their own.


7.


Case study format:


Center the whole deck on one real example, then walk through what happened and what it means. A strong example carries this concept almost by itself.


8.


Single-metaphor concept:


Pick one metaphor, like a race, a recipe, or a journey, and carry it through every slide. It turns an ordinary topic into something people remember. Don’t worry about being corny; worry more about being relatable.


9.


Interactive concept:


Build in a moment where you ask the audience a question or run a quick poll. Even a small break like this keeps a long deck from losing people.


10.


Countdown or ranked list:


Structure the deck as a ranked list, from least to most important or vice versa. This gives people a reason to keep watching to see what lands at number one.


11.


FAQ-style concept:


Build the deck around answering the questions your audience is most likely to ask. It works especially well when you already know the objections you're facing.


12.


Milestone concept:


Frame the deck around a series of milestones hit (or ones still ahead). This one holds up well for progress updates where the timeline itself is the story.
