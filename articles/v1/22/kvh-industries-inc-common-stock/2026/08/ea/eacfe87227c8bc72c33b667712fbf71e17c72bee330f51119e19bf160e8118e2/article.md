# What Is NIST and Why Should Maritime Companies Care?

**TL;DR:** NIST is a U.S. government cybersecurity framework that sets the foundation for the maritime rules you already must follow, including USCG and IMO requirements. It organizes cybersecurity into six functions: Govern, Identify, Protect, Detect, Respond, and Recover. Understanding it helps you go beyond checkbox compliance and know what good security looks like.


You’ve probably seen the letters NIST show up in a meeting, a vendor pitch, or a compliance document, and nodded along as if you knew exactly what it meant. Some people do, but most people have no idea.


Don’t worry. By the end of this, you will.


**What Is NIST?**


NIST is the National Institute of Standards and Technology, a U.S. government agency that published the Cybersecurity Framework (CSF). The CSF is a playbook that any organization can use to protect itself from cyber threats.


Here’s the key thing to understand: NIST is not a law. It’s not something you get fined for ignoring. It’s a framework, meaning it’s a structured way of thinking about cybersecurity that the smartest people in the field agreed on. And because it’s so well thought out, regulators all over the world have used it as the foundation for their own rules, which include the ones that apply to you.


**Why Maritime Companies Should Pay Attention**


If you operate vessels on U.S. waters, you’re already dealing with the new USCG cybersecurity rules. If you operate internationally, you’re likely aware of the IMO’s Maritime Cyber Risk Management guidelines. Both are built on the same foundation: NIST.


When your auditor or your IT partner talks about NIST, they’re not speaking a different language. They’re describing the exact same things you’re already required to do, just with a common vocabulary that everyone in the industry uses.


Understanding NIST means understanding why the rules are written the way they are, not just what the rules say. And that makes compliance a whole lot easier.


**The Six Functions of NIST CSF 2.0, in Plain English**


1. **Govern.** Most people skip this step, but it’s the most important. Governance ensures that organizational leaders understand the company’s cyber risks and make informed decisions. Cybersecurity is not just an IT problem; it’s a business issue that starts at the top. Consider this: a vessel does not operate without a captain who understands the risks of the route. Likewise, an organization’s cybersecurity should not operate without leadership that understands the risks.
2. **Identify.** Before you can protect anything, you need to know what you have. This means cataloging every device, system, and connection on your vessels and in your offices, including navigation systems, satellite communications, crew devices, and shore-based networks. You can’t protect what you don’t know exists.
3. **Protect.** This is the function most people think of when they hear “cybersecurity.” It encompasses controlling access permissions, keeping software up to date, training your crew to recognize threats, and putting safeguards in place so that one mistake doesn’t take down your whole operation.
4. **Detect.** Having protections in place is great. Knowing when something slips through anyway is just as important. Detection involves having monitoring in place so that if something unusual happens on your network, you find out about it quickly, rather than weeks later. In maritime terms, think of it like a bilge alarm. You hope you never need it. But you absolutely want it there.
5. **Respond.** When something goes wrong, and it will at some point, you need a plan. Clear steps ensure your team knows exactly what to do, who to call, and how to contain the problem before it gets worse. A good response plan is the difference between a bad day and a catastrophic one.


6. **Recover.** Getting back to normal operations as fast as possible after an incident is its own discipline. It requires maintaining backups, following restoration procedures, and having a clear plan to get your vessel or business back up and running. Many organizations fall short, focusing on prevention but failing to plan for recovery.

**The Bottom Line**


NIST isn’t just alphabet soup for IT people. It’s the common language of cybersecurity, and it’s the backbone of the rules maritime operators are already required to follow. If you understand the six functions, you understand what good cybersecurity looks like, and you’re in a much stronger position to ask the right questions, work with the right partners, and protect what matters.
