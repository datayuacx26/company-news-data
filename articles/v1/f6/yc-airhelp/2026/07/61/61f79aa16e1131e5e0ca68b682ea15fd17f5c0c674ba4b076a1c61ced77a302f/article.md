# Can you bring a shaving razor on a plane? Hand luggage rules (2026)

**Short answer: yes, most razors are allowed in hand luggage.** Disposable razors, cartridge razors, and electric shavers all pass through security without issue. The exception is anything with an exposed or detachable blade, like a traditional safety razor or a straight razor — those need to go in checked luggage. Here's the full breakdown by razor type, plus the liquid-rule catch that trips people up even when their razor itself is cleared. For the full picture on what else you can pack, see our guide to[what you can bring on a plane.](https://www.airhelp.com/en-int/blog/what-can-you-bring-on-plane/)

## The core rules, explained by razor type


Aviation security rules focus on whether an item has an exposed cutting edge that could be used as a weapon, not on what the item is technically called. That's why a disposable razor with an enclosed cartridge sails through security, while a safety razor's exposed double-edge blade doesn't. Here's the direct verdict for each type.


### Can I bring a disposable razor on a plane?


Yes. Disposable razors are allowed in both hand luggage and checked luggage. The blade is fixed and enclosed, so there's no safety concern.

### Can I bring an electric razor on a plane?


Yes. Electric razors and shavers are treated as electronics, not blades, so they're allowed in hand luggage without restriction. What matters more than the blade is the power source:


-


Corded razors with no internal battery: allowed in either hand luggage or checked luggage.


-


Battery-powered or rechargeable razors: pack them in hand luggage where possible, especially if they use a lithium battery — most airlines prefer lithium batteries to travel in the cabin rather than the hold.


-


Removable batteries: if the battery can be taken out, the razor itself can go in either bag, but keep the battery in your hand luggage.

### Can I put any razor in my checked luggage?


Yes. Every razor type covered above — disposable, cartridge, electric, safety, straight, or loose blades — is allowed in checked luggage with no restrictions. Checked luggage is always the safe fallback if you're unsure about a specific razor.

## Exceptions and edge cases


Straight razors and multi-tool razors with an exposed blade follow the same rule as safety razors — checked luggage only, no exceptions.


If you're connecting through multiple airports on one trip, remember that security rules are enforced by the airport you're currently in, not your final destination. An item accepted in your hand luggage at your first airport can still be confiscated at a connecting airport with stricter local enforcement, so when in doubt, pack borderline items in checked luggage from the start.


Rules can also differ slightly outside the EU and US, so if your trip involves a transfer through a country with its own aviation security authority, a quick check of that airport's rules is worth the two minutes it takes. Our broader[hand luggage rules](https://www.airhelp.co.uk/blog/hand-luggage-allowance/) guide covers sharp objects and other borderline items beyond just razors, and our[luggage and security rules hub](https://www.airhelp.com/en-int/blog/?category=luggage-security-rules) rounds up more packing guides.


It's also worth remembering that security officers make judgment calls in the moment, not just from a printed list. A pocket knife disguised as a novelty razor, a multi-tool with a blade attachment, or anything with a spring-loaded or locking blade is treated as a weapon first and a grooming item second — when an item looks ambiguous, expect it to be confiscated rather than waved through.


## Practical tips: don't forget the liquid rule


Your razor being allowed doesn't mean your whole shaving kit is in the clear. Shaving cream, gel, and aftershave all count as liquids under[aviation security rules](https://www.airhelp.com/en-int/blog/liquids-on-a-plane/) . In the EU, that generally means containers of 100ml or less, packed in a single clear resealable bag.


That said, the rules are shifting: a growing number of European airports have installed newer CT (computed tomography) scanners that allow liquids up to 2 liters per container, with no need to bag them separately. The rollout is uneven — some airports have it in every lane, some only in a few, and some haven't started at all — so check your specific departure airport's current rules before you pack, rather than assuming either the old or new limit applies.


A solid shaving stick or bar counts as a solid, not a liquid, and sidesteps the whole question if you'd rather not think about it.


## Best razors for travel


If you're packing light or just want to avoid any back-and-forth with security, a few practical choices make the whole question moot.


-


Disposable razors are the simplest option for short trips — cheap enough to leave behind afterward, and no security ambiguity at all.


-


Cartridge razors with a travel cap give a closer shave than disposables without adding any restriction, and the cap protects both the blade and your other belongings.


-


A compact electric shaver is the best choice for longer trips or frequent flyers, since it needs no shaving cream, no water, and clears security as an electronic item rather than a blade.


-


If you're committed to a safety razor at home, consider a travel-specific disposable or cartridge razor just for the trip, and leave the safety razor packed in checked luggage rather than testing your luck at the gate.


Whichever razor you choose, a small protective case keeps the blade or head covered, prevents damage to the rest of your toiletries bag, and makes security's job easier when they glance inside.


## FAQ


### Can I bring a safety razor in hand luggage?


No — a safety razor with its blade fitted has an exposed edge that isn't allowed in hand luggage. Pack it in checked luggage instead.


### Are electric razors allowed in carry-on?


Yes. Electric razors and shavers are treated as electronics and are allowed in hand luggage without restriction. If the razor is battery-powered, pack it in your hand luggage rather than checked luggage, especially for lithium batteries.


### What about disposable razors?


Disposable and cartridge razors are allowed in both hand luggage and checked luggage, since their blades are enclosed rather than exposed.


### What razors are not allowed in hand luggage?


Safety razors with the blade fitted, straight (cut-throat) razors, and any loose replacement blades are not allowed in hand luggage. All three are allowed in checked luggage instead.


### Can I pack shaving cream in hand luggage?


Yes, as a[liquid](https://www.airhelp.com/en-int/blog/liquids-on-a-plane/) — which usually means 100ml containers in a clear bag under EU rules, though some airports with newer scanners now allow up to 2 liters. Check your departure airport's current rule before you fly.
