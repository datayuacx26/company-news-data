## 2. Flightradar24: Visuals and data


If flight tracking were a spectator sport, Flightradar24 would be the broadcast network. The app covers a wide range of transmitter-equipped aircraft and displays them on a live, zoomable map that turns raw aviation data into something fun to watch.


**Standout features:**


-


**Tap any plane on the map** to see tail number, aircraft type, altitude, speed, full route, and photos of the actual aircraft


-


**Augmented Reality** mode lets you point your phone at the sky and instantly identify any plane overhead


-


**Historical flight playback** (up to 365 days) and weather layers on premium tiers


-


**Near-complete global coverage** , including helicopters and some private aviation


Flightradar24 handles the basics too — departure and arrival times, delay info, gate data — but it's not where the app shines brightest. If you need proactive disruption alerts or compensation tracking, you'll want to pair it with something else. Where Flightradar24 is unmatched is depth of aircraft-level data: it's the app you open when you want to understand what's happening in the sky.


**Price:** Free; the Gold plan (~$34.99/year) unlocks the full experience.


**Best for:** Aviation enthusiasts and visually oriented travelers who want the most detailed, interactive real-time map available.


## 3. FlightAware: Predictive intelligence


FlightAware has long been the go-to desktop tool for flight tracking, and the mobile app carries that same data-rich approach. Its key differentiator is thinking one step ahead: instead of just tracking your flight, it tracks the inbound aircraft that's supposed to become your flight.


**Standout features:**


-


**"Where is my plane?"** tracks your inbound aircraft and recalculates your estimated departure — sometimes giving you a heads-up 30 minutes or more before the airline confirms a delay


-


**The Misery Map:** a color-coded overview of airports experiencing weather-related delays and cancellations across an entire region


-


**Push notifications** for departures, arrivals, gate changes, and cancellations


-


**NEXRAD weather radar** overlays on premium plans


FlightAware's strength is predictive intelligence: tools like the Misery Map help you decide whether a connection is worth risking, and inbound-aircraft tracking can flag delays before the airline confirms them. It won't help you file a compensation claim, but it might help you avoid the disruption in the first place. The free tier is solid; premium plans add extended alert history and advanced weather data.


**Price:** Free; premium plans available with additional data layers.


**Best for:** Travelers who want to predict delays before the airline announces them.


## 4. FlightStats: The clean, professional choice


FlightStats strips flight tracking down to its most functional form. The interface is deliberately minimal: a timeline view of your flight's progress, current status, gate information, and weather conditions — all displayed without clutter.


**Standout features:**


-


**Historical on-time performance data** for any flight number, so you can check a route's track record before booking


-


Clean, functional **home-screen widgets** that let you glance at your flight's status without opening the app


-


**Timeline-based flight progress** view with gate and weather info


FlightStats isn't trying to wow you with maps or gamification. It's the app you open when you need a fast, reliable answer: is my flight on time, and what gate do I go to? The historical performance data is its secret weapon, invaluable for business travelers choosing between two connections or deciding how much buffer to leave between meetings and landing.


**Price** : Free; premium tools available.


**Best for:** Business travelers who need fast, no-frills logistics and want to check a flight's reliability before booking.


## 5. Flighty: The early warning system


Flighty is an iOS only app (there is no Android version), and its main goal is to provide the most accurate flight information often faster than the airline and predict aircraft delays and schedule changes using machine learning, FAA and Eurocontrol data․


Standout features:


-


**"Where's My Plane?"** shows your incoming planes for flights up to 25 hours before they're scheduled to depart, so you see if delays are building up earlier in the route


-


**ML-powered** delay predictions that explain why a delay is happening: late aircraft, air traffic control, ground stops, weather


-


**Connection Assistant** recommends minimum layover times and flags potentially tight connections


-


**Push notifications** , including gate changes, cancellations, or baggage claim notifications


-


**Flighty Passport:** a travel journal, with personal statistics, maps and annual reports


The app is generally fast with delay alerts, and it also works offline and updates via basic messaging Wi-Fi while you're in the air, so you can spot connection issues before you land. The trade-off: Flighty is iOS and macOS only, with no Android version available. And unlike AirHelp, it won't tell you whether a disruption entitles you to compensation. It makes sure you know about problems early, but what you do next is up to you.


**Price:** There is a free-tier with basic functionality, or you can subscribe to Flighty Pro, starting at roughly $59․99/year․ First flight tracked gets temporary Pro access․


**Best for:** iOS users who want more detailed predicted delay information and notifications than an airline app typically provides․
