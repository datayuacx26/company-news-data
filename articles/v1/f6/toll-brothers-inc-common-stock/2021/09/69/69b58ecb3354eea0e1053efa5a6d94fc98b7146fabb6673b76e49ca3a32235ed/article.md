# Benefits of Natural Light: How Daylighting Enhances Your Home Design

Last updated on July 27th, 2026


When searching for a new home, every homeowner has a unique wish list – spacious kitchens, walk-in closets, entertaining areas, or luxurious primary suites. Yet one of the most impactful design elements often goes overlooked: the benefits of natural light.


In modern home design, the strategic use of natural light, commonly called daylighting, plays an essential role in creating spaces that feel brighter, more connected, and more comfortable to live in. Beyond aesthetics, daylighting can help reduce dependence on artificial lighting, improve energy efficiency, and support overall well-being.


At Toll Brothers, maximizing natural light has long been a priority through thoughtful architecture, expansive glass, and seamless indoor-outdoor living spaces that enhance homeowners’ daily experience.


## Quick Summary


- Natural light enhances well-being, supporting mood, productivity, and overall comfort.
- Daylighting can improve energy efficiency by reducing reliance on artificial lighting.
- Large glass doors and windows create seamless indoor-outdoor living spaces.
- Modern glass technology allows for bigger openings with improved insulation and glare reduction.
- Covered outdoor living areas help regulate temperature while increasing usable living space.


## What Is Daylighting in Home Design?


Daylighting is the intentional use of natural light in architecture and home design to improve comfort, efficiency, and livability. By strategically incorporating windows, moving walls of glass, and open layouts, homeowners can enjoy brighter interiors while reducing the need for artificial lighting throughout the day.


As Tim Gehman, director of design at Toll Brothers, explains: “The best thing we can do is provide as many opportunities for homeowners to control natural light on their own as they experience it.”

## How Does Natural Light Improve Indoor-Outdoor Living?


One of the greatest benefits of natural light is its ability to strengthen the connection between indoor and outdoor spaces. Toll Brothers incorporates large moving walls of glass from Western Window Systems into many home designs, helping create seamless transitions between interior living spaces and outdoor environments.


### Benefits of Expansive Glass and Moving Walls


- Increased natural light throughout the home
- Enhanced outdoor views
- Greater openness and visual flow
- Flexible entertaining spaces
- Improved connection to nature


Modern advancements in glass technology also allow for:


- Better insulation values
- Reduced solar glare
- Larger expanses of glass without sacrificing comfort

## Why Are Large Glass Doors Trending in Modern Homes?


Homeowners continue to prioritize larger glass openings because they create brighter, more connected living spaces. Multi-slide and[bi-fold doors](https://westernvolumeprogram.com/classic-line/aluminum-multi-slide/) allow indoor and outdoor areas to function as one cohesive environment. When opened, these expansive glass panels often disappear into wall pockets, maximizing usable living space and allowing fresh air and daylight to flow freely through the home.


“The value of the door is their absence,” Gehman says. “They can go away and completely open up those spaces.”

## Improve Energy Efficiency with Natural Light


Maximizing natural light can also support a more energy-efficient home design. Thoughtfully designed[outdoor living spaces](https://tollbrothers.com/blog/make-most-of-your-outdoor-living-area/) help regulate temperature and reduce energy usage. Covered patios paired with large glass openings can:


- Protect interiors from direct sunlight
- Allow cooler evening air to circulate naturally
- Improve airflow throughout the home


This design approach helps reduce reliance on artificial cooling while maintaining comfort year-round. High-end screening systems can also extend the usability of outdoor spaces by helping regulate temperature and creating comfortable environments in a range of climates.
