# Monday CRM Alternative for Individuals: Dex vs Monday


We've all heard of monday.com. For teams of different sizes, it sounds like the perfect cure for CRM fatigue: one Work OS for projects, marketing, and sales, endlessly customizable, with AI sprinkled on top. And for teams, a lot of that promise holds up.


If you're a founder tracking investor conversations, a job seeker juggling recruiters, or just someone trying not to lose touch with people who matter, you'll feel that mismatch within the first week.


This post breaks down where monday CRM works, where it doesn't, and why Dex is the Monday CRM alternative most individuals end up choosing.


## What monday CRM does well


Credit where it's due: monday CRM is a capable sales product.


It sits inside Monday.com's broader "Work OS," which means you can run your project management, marketing operations, and sales CRM from one platform. For a team of five to 50 people managing leads through defined pipeline stages, it handles the job. Customizable boards, shared dashboards, automations that trigger when deals move between stages, sales forecasting on the Pro plan, and a growing set of AI tools including their Sidekick assistant and an app builder called monday vibe.


The 2026 updates are real. Monday.com added AI agents through an Agent Factory (still in early access), linked quotes and invoices directly to deals, and built an MCP integration that lets you query meeting transcripts in natural language. For revenue teams, these are useful additions.


But every one of those features assumes you have a sales motion. Leads coming in. Deals moving through stages. A team that needs shared visibility. If that describes your work, monday CRM earns its price. If it doesn't, you're paying for machinery you'll never turn on.


## Where monday CRM falls short as a personal tool


The problems start with pricing. monday CRM charges per seat with a three-seat minimum on every paid plan. Even if you're one person, you're paying for three. The Basic plan is $12 per seat per month billed annually, which means your real entry cost is $36 per month, for a tool that caps you at 1,000 active contacts on that tier.


Then there's the data model. Everything in monday CRM orbits around deals, leads, and accounts. That works for tracking a prospect through qualification to close. It doesn't work for remembering that you met someone at a dinner party, they're launching a company, and you said you'd introduce them to your friend who does brand design. Personal relationships don't have pipeline stages.


The integration gaps are the other issue. monday CRM connects to Gmail, Outlook, and business tools. It doesn't sync with[LinkedIn](https://getdex.com/integrations/linkedin) ,[WhatsApp](https://getdex.com/integrations/whatsapp) ,[iMessage](https://getdex.com/integrations/imessage) ,[Instagram](https://getdex.com/integrations/instagram) , or[Facebook](https://getdex.com/integrations/facebook) . For most people I know, professional relationships don't live exclusively in email. They're spread across DMs, texts, and social platforms. A CRM that only sees your inbox is looking at maybe half the picture.


And there are no[keep-in-touch reminders](https://getdex.com/docs/workflows/keep-in-touch) . No recurring nudge that says "you haven't reached out to Mark in two months." monday CRM tracks deals forward. It doesn't track relationships over time.

## How the two tools work day to day


### Getting set up


**Dex:** Sign up, connect your accounts ([LinkedIn](https://getdex.com/integrations/linkedin) , Gmail, calendar, social), and your contacts start flowing in. Most people are running within 10 minutes. The integrations hub puts all your connections on a single page.


**monday CRM:** You build boards, define pipeline stages, connect integrations, configure automations, and set up team permissions. It takes longer because there's more to configure — and more to maintain as your process changes.


### After meeting someone new


**Dex:** Add the contact (or let the integration pull them in). Write a quick note. Set a[follow-up reminder](https://getdex.com/docs/workflows/follow-up-features) . When the reminder surfaces, you have the full context — past notes, emails, meetings — in one timeline.


**monday CRM:** Add the person as a lead on a board. Fill in deal and account fields. Assign a pipeline stage. Log the activity. Set up or trigger automations. It's thorough, but it's a lot of steps for "I met someone interesting and want to stay in touch."


### Staying in touch over months and years


This is where the gap is widest. Dex is built around the problem of maintaining relationships over time.[Keep-in-touch reminders](https://getdex.com/docs/workflows/keep-in-touch) surface people on your chosen cadence.[Pre-meeting briefs](https://getdex.com/docs/workflows/pre-meeting) email you a summary before calls so you walk in prepared.[AI Assist](https://getdex.com/docs/ai/ai-assist) suggests conversation starters based on your notes and their recent activity.


monday CRM is built around moving deals forward. It tracks pipeline progress and logs sales activities. Periodic "how's it going?" check-ins with non-sales contacts aren't really what it's designed for.

### What is a personal CRM?


A personal CRM helps you manage personal and professional relationships as an individual, not a sales team. It organizes your network with notes, reminders, and interaction history, and modern personal CRMs add AI that suggests follow-ups and surfaces context from past conversations. Think of it as a private system that combines your address book, calendar, and notes with the memory you wish you had.
