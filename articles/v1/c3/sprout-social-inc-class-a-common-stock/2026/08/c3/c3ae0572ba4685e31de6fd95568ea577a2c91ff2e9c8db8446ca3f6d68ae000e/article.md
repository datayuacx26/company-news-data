If you’re not already partnering with YouTube influencers, it’s time to change that.


[Sprout Social’s 2026 Content Strategy Report](https://sproutsocial.com/insights/data/2026-social-media-content-strategy-report/) revealed that consumers are increasingly turning to YouTube to inform their buying behavior. Approximately 24% find new products on the network, with consumers almost as likely to seek out product education as they are entertainment. And YouTube influencers have a powerful role to play in both spaces.


With built-in audiences of thousands or millions, influencers on YT have a unique ability to build awareness, demonstrate real-world value and spark lasting interest. When you leverage[YouTube influencer marketing](https://sproutsocial.com/insights/youtube-influencer-marketing/) , you’re backing your brand with the network’s most trusted voices.


To help you get started, we’ve rounded up 10 YouTube influencer marketing examples, why they work and how to use them for your business.


## 1. Unboxing


Unboxing involves opening new products on camera, showcasing packaging and features. Oftentimes, creators simultaneously share their first impressions on look, feel and functionality to give viewers a better idea of what to expect.


That’s what Marques Brownlee did when he unboxed the iPhone 17, iPhone 17 Pro and iPhone Air on his YouTube channel. Upon opening the products, he compared their designs and battery capabilities, then provided his hot take on the models—a piece of intrigue he strategically included in the title.


### When and why it works


Unboxing content is ideal for product-based brands in sectors like technology, beauty and fashion. They’re the perfect format for highly anticipated launches, especially when led by influencers.


Creators often receive products before the average consumer, meaning their videos provide the first sneak peek at what’s inside the box. This early access creates hype around a product and fosters a sense of exclusivity; viewers feel like they’re part of a moment they can’t experience anywhere else. And even if they don’t get the products pre-launch, their content can still help increase awareness over time and convert consumers on the fence.


## 2. Tutorials and how-tos


Most YouTube influencer marketing campaigns aren’t complete without how-to content. As the name suggests, these videos see creators provide step-by-step guidance on how to perform a specific process, whether it’s changing a tire or perfecting a smoky eye.


With the right expertise, influencers can lend credibility and increase consumer confidence. With over two million subscribers and several years of experience as a makeup artist, UK influencer Lisa Eldridge offers an impactful mix of skill and reputation. Her buy-in instantly makes products feel more professional, reliable and worth investing in.


### When and why it works


Users love tutorials for their practicality and accessibility. They learn new skills on a free platform and in a format they can stop, start and rewatch as many times as needed.


For brands, tutorials don’t just promote products but actively teach consumers how to use them. This is ideal for consumers in the awareness and consideration phases because it helps them understand the product’s purpose, see it in action and build confidence before buying.


## 3. Product reviews


In this style of YouTube content, creators share their overall impressions of specific products, listing pros, cons and key considerations for their audience. While these videos carry some similarities with unboxings, they’re generally more comprehensive in rating the item’s overall quality.


For example, health influencer Doctor Mike shared a taste-test video on his channel featuring fellow influencers Joe Santagato and Frankie Alvarez, with the trio rating viral protein snacks. And since not every product receives glowing feedback, the ones that do stand out even more in consumers’ minds.


### When and why it works


Much like traditional reviews and testimonials, product review videos can be a valuable form of[social proof](https://sproutsocial.com/insights/social-proof/) . They give your audience insight into how other consumers feel about your business—in their words.


A positive review could ease a consumer’s hesitation in the consideration phase and nudge them towards conversion. Plus, brands don’t necessarily need exclusivity to stand out in product reviews, making them more versatile and suitable for brands of various sizes and budgets.


## 4. Get ready with me (GRWM)


GRWM videos are one of the top[influencer marketing trends](https://sproutsocial.com/insights/influencer-marketing-trends/) in 2026. Creators film themselves performing their daily grooming routines while talking directly to the camera or via voiceover.


YouTuber lay luv is proof that GRWMs aren’t reserved for mega-influencers heading to red-carpet events. She’s found success sharing her preparation process for an average day of high school—making her extremely relatable, especially with young audiences.


### When and why it works


GRWM videos are a goldmine for natural product integration, particularly from brands in the cosmetic, skincare, fragrance and fashion industries. Because these items slot seamlessly into influencers’ everyday routines, they feel like authentic recommendations rather than overt product placements.


## 5. Makeover transformations


Another effective form of YouTube influencer marketing is makeover content. This entails creators revamping a particular space—be it their kitchen, office or backyard—using a brand’s products.


Lone Fox—aka Drew Michael Scott—is a popular home design and DIY YouTuber who regularly shares makeover content. Whether it’s a living room refresh or a closet declutter, he takes his audience along for the ride as he transforms a space, then wows them with the before-and-after.


### When and why it works


Makeover content is ideal for brands that specialize in furniture, household appliances or home decor. These products thrive on visual impact—and few formats convey that better than a before-and-after reveal.


Not to mention, makeovers also tap into a content framework that viewers love: progress. Makeovers rarely happen overnight; usually, it’s a slow and steady unfolding that builds towards a major transformation.


This lends itself well to[episodic content series](https://sproutsocial.com/insights/social-media-content-series/) , which is becoming increasingly popular among social users for the predictability and connection it provides. Per Sprout’s Q2 2025 Pulse Survey, 57% of users want brands to actively prioritize this content.


## 6. Contests and giveaways


[Contests and giveaways](https://sproutsocial.com/insights/social-media-contests/) see brands partner with creators to gift viewers products or services. In exchange for providing free goods or services, brands get increased visibility and awareness.


Take MrBeast, who often holds large-scale giveaways on his channel. On one occasion, he gifted 50 subscribers with brand-new laptops—and offered some high-level exposure for the brands backing the technology, Lenovo and Intel.


### When and why it works


Giveaways are highly versatile, making them suitable for brands across industries. Whether it’s a one-year software subscription or a limited-edition product, every business provides some form of value that can double as a prize.


Most contests offer low-effort entry; to partake, users only have to follow an account, like a post or leave a comment. This is particularly impactful for consumers in the awareness stage because it ensures that their first touchpoint with your company is all value, no cost. Even if they don’t win, they’ll remember your business as something worth winning and one that gives to its community.


## 7. Silent reviews and aesthetic montages


In some YouTube influencer marketing campaigns, saying nothing says it all. Silent reviews and aesthetic montages involve no talking. Instead, creators use facial expressions and gestures, text overlay, sound effects or visuals to captivate and communicate.


Take this silent book review from Ireland-based YouTuber Bookgirlnextdoor. In it, she gives her honest opinions on popular novels—without uttering a single word.


### When and why it works


In general, silent reviews and montages are best suited to products with high visual or sensory appeal—think beauty, skincare, homewares and fashion.


But brands outside these industries can still get in on the fun, with a silent book review being a key example. It subverts expectations by turning a typically verbal format into something totally silent. Because the delivery is untraditional, it feels fresh and captivating—which can help drive brand interest and engagement.


## 8. Challenges and stunts


A popular niche on YouTube sees influencers perform or coordinate high-risk, unconventional or extreme feats. Oftentimes, these tasks are gamified, making the content even more entertaining for viewers.


Aussie YouTubers Brett Stanford, Derek Herron and Scott Gaunson have leveraged stunt-based content to build a following of millions. On their channel, you’ll regularly see them attempting outlandish challenges, such as launching a giant frisbee off a cliff or[trying to crush a car using only water](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NofSr-fh32w) .


### When and why it works


This content sees people testing their limits, trying new things and redefining what’s possible. So, it’s a great fit for brands that do the same. Companies built around adventure and innovation slot seamlessly into challenge videos because their ethos matches the premise of the video. As a result, their integration feels organic, not forced.


## 9. Hauls


In haul videos, creators show off items they recently purchased, either from one or multiple retailers.


For instance, Irish influencer Sophie Murray focused on a single retailer in a haul posted to her YouTube channel: H&M. As she showed off each product, she discussed key details—such as sizing and pricing—and included B-roll of her wearing it.


### When and why it works


Whether it’s fashion, beauty, technology or homewares, hauls are best suited to product-based brands due to the content’s “show-and-tell” format.


They’re also ideal for consumers in the consideration phase who want to see products up close before buying. Additionally, many creators—like Sophie—feature[affiliate marketing](https://sproutsocial.com/insights/affiliate-influencer-marketing/) links in their video descriptions to facilitate conversion.


## 10. Day in the life


Day-in-the-life content gives viewers an authentic, intimate look at an influencer’s day, documenting everything from work to relationships to hobbies.


French influencer Shera takes her two million subscribers through her everyday life in her vlogs. In the one below, she captures how she balances her personal life (i.e. raising her children) and professional life (i.e. running a business) in a 24-hour period.


### When and why it works


Making breakfast, running errands, putting the kids to bed—day-in-the-life content taps into the relatability of everyday life.


And relatability is an invaluable currency for brands. Per the[2025 Sprout Social Index™](https://sproutsocial.com/insights/index/) , consumers crave authenticity and relatability from brands on social more than anything else.


Because they have high levels of audience trust, influencers can dial that resonance up even further. When they show how a product or service fits into their routine, it helps viewers visualize how it could slot into their lives.


## 11. Parodies and creative skits


These videos involve creators acting out fictional scenarios, usually in a comedic or satirical way that pokes fun at a particular topic or trend. Because they’re typically short-form, skits are ideal for[YouTube Shorts](https://sproutsocial.com/insights/youtube-shorts/) .


YouTube influencer Ryan George is known for his tongue-in-cheek skits, which cover a wide range of topics. Whether it’s AI, pop culture or unspoken social rules, he can find a comedic spin for just about anything.


### When and why it works


Punchy and playful, skits are highly captivating and versatile. Creators like Ryan are skilled at finding the funny side of any product or service—including the ones you offer.


With their humor, you can hook people in the awareness stage instantly and start familiarizing them with your brand. From there, you can subtly and comedically educate them on the specific pain points your business addresses for them.


## Key brand takeaways from examples of YouTube influencer marketing


Now that you’ve seen examples of YouTuber influencer marketing in action, let’s take a look at the most important insights to inform your strategy:


### 1. The “first 60 seconds” rule


While[long-form content](https://sproutsocial.com/insights/youtube-shorts-vs-long-form-content-strategy/) is king on YouTube, you still need to cater to short attention spans. Make sure your hook (i.e. the first 60 seconds) is compelling enough to entice viewers to stick around.


Open your video with a thought-provoking question. Piece together several high-energy, short-form clips from the video in a trailer-style format to give viewers a sneak peek at what’s to come. Display bold animated text on the screen that immediately piques people’s interest.


### 2. Integrated product utility over product placements


It’s not enough for your products or services to simply appear in a video—audiences want to see real, practical examples of how they’ll fit into their lives. In other words, show don’t tell.


Demonstrating your product in action is the best way to underscore its value and emphasize the problem you’re solving.


### 3. Partner with influencers aligned to your brand identity


If an influencer’s values and aesthetics don’t align with your brand’s, your messaging won’t resonate. Even worse, you could erode audience trust or damage your credibility.


[Sprout Social Influencer Marketing](https://sproutsocial.com/influencer-marketing/) ensure you’re collaborating with the right creators. For instance, the[Brand Fit Score](https://sproutsocial.com/influencer-marketing/features/brand-fit-score/) quantifies how a creator’s audience and content coincide with your brand. Or, you can use[Recruit](https://sproutsocial.com/influencer-marketing/features/recruit/) to create a branded application page and let influencers come to you.


### 4. Optimize for social media search


Users are actively using social networks as search engines and YouTube is the OG network for social search. Optimize your YouTube account for[social media search](https://sproutsocial.com/insights/social-media-search/) by creating dedicated videos specifically geared towards long-tail keyword queries.


While broad search terms may garner larger monthly search volumes, the competition is also higher. By targeting long-tail keywords, you can narrow down your audience to high-intent users. For example, instead of making a video titled “how to make coffee”, consider a more niche option like “how to make a cookie butter iced latte without an espresso machine”.


### 5. Multi-platform repurposing multiplies ROI


[Repurposing social media content](https://sproutsocial.com/insights/repurposing-content-for-social-media/) for multiple platforms is an excellent way to maintain cross-channel consistency, build share of voice and strengthen ROI.


Make the most of your long-form YouTube videos by extracting the most compelling moments in short-form clips. You can then share these across various formats and networks, such as YouTube Shorts, TikTok and Instagram Reels—turning one piece of content into multiple social touchpoints.


### 6. Comments are a goldmine for real-time research and development


Audience feedback is one of the most valuable tools for content refinement—and your comment section is brimming with these insights.


Use Sprout Listening to scan your comments regularly to understand what viewers enjoy, what they want to see more of or issues they’re having (e.g. broken description links). Then, use this data to guide your future content initiatives.

## Frequently asked questions


Which format drives the most actual sales?


Product reviews and tutorials typically drive the most sales because they target consumers later in the buyer journey. These viewers are actively researching and comparing product options, making them more likely to convert after watching.


How do we stop viewers from skipping the sponsored part of a vlog?


Weave it into the narrative. Instead of a forced 30-second spiel about why your product is great, incorporate it into the story the video is telling.


Should brands give creators a strict script for a review?


No. While you can give them key talking points as a high-level guide, let them say things in their own voice. Otherwise, the partnership will feel unnatural and fail to resonate with their audiences.
