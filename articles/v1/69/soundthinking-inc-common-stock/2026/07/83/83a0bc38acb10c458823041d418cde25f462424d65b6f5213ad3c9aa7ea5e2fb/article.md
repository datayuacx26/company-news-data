# How CrimeTracer Case Folders Help Agencies Work Human Trafficking Cases

## How CrimeTracer Case Folders Help Agencies Work Human Trafficking Cases


Human trafficking is rarely a local crime. Victims move. Suspects move. Evidence surfaces in counties, states, and sometimes regions that have no established working relationship with each other. For the investigator trying to put it all together, the challenge is less about knowing where to look and more about having a system that keeps up when the case does not stay put.


In 2023,[80 jurisdictions reported](https://www.state.gov/reports/2025-trafficking-in-persons-report/united-states/) 2,640 sex trafficking incidents and 771 labor trafficking incidents, significantly increasing from 2022. Those numbers only reflect what was reported and captured in statistical data. The actual scope of trafficking activity and the number of agencies that may hold a piece of the investigative puzzle are considerably larger.


The bigger challenge in multi-jurisdictional trafficking cases is rarely a shortage of data. The data exists in separate systems, managed by separate agencies across multiple jurisdictions, with no mechanism to surface the connections that already exist. A suspect generating records in three counties may never appear on any single investigator's radar until someone happens to make a call.


CrimeTracer's™ case folders were built for exactly this kind of investigation. They give investigators a structured, collaborative workspace to organize records, search results, and entities such as people, vehicles, and even locations of interest in one place. Case folders are designed to support large amounts of data, including the cross-agency components that human trafficking cases almost always require.


## What a Case Folder in CrimeTracer Actually Does


CrimeTracer supports over 1 billion records from RMS, CAD, LPRs, ShotSpotter, JMS, court records, warrants, etc., and connects to both Thomson Reuters CLEAR® and N-DEx to compile data from your agency and other jurisdictions nationwide. Case folders draw from that data foundation, which means the people and connections surfaced inside a folder are grounded in law enforcement records, within CJIS-compliant boundaries, not only your agency’s documents.


A case folder in[CrimeTracer](https://www.soundthinking.com/law-enforcement/crime-data-solution-crimetracer/) is not a static file. It is a curated environment where you add people, vehicles, reports, pawn slips, a variety of documents, and even search results as the investigation develops. As records are searched and moved into a case folder, it becomes easier to see the overall scope of the case, the number of connections, and types of information in the folder, such as associated individuals, vehicles, and locations within the case folder. The core of CrimeTracer lies in connected data, which links cases, people, vehicles, locations, and more. Case folders help investigators keep case-related findings in one centralized location.


For a human trafficking investigation, that matters. A suspect who appears in RMS records in one county, is associated with a vehicle record in another, and has a warrant in a third jurisdiction may not trigger any single alarm individually. When a single individual appears in dozens of reports across agencies and over many years, investigators need a single consolidated record. CrimeTracer merges these into unified entities for people, vehicles, and locations. This consolidation creates clearer linkages across cases and reduces the chances of missing critical associations buried in separate systems.


## Connecting Evidence Across Jurisdictions


A trafficking investigation is usually lengthy, with a long list of names involved, potential connections, locations, vehicles, phone numbers, and more. CrimeTracer natively identifies connections between disparate records and across jurisdictions through link analysis. Case Folders lets investigators house those connections, search results, and even notes, with other CrimeTracer users, including those in other jurisdictions, who may be working on the same trafficking case.


Without a centralized workspace, those connections depend on investigators knowing each other, sharing information manually, and piecing it together outside any system. A Case Folder in CrimeTracer holds all of that evidence in one place, keeping it organized and visible to every authorized CrimeTracer investigator or task force member working that case. As new records are added, the folder reflects the current state of the investigation. Case Folders also allow notes, so investigators can add case updates.


Users with access to that Case Folder will also receive an alert when updates are made to it.


## Multi-Agency Sharing and Alerts


CrimeTracer's case folder sharing capability is designed to support an enhanced collaborative structure. Investigators from different agencies can be granted access to the same folder, allowing them to view the same evidence, search results, and the criminal network's organizational structure – and contribute case-related information. For agencies where task forces routinely involve county sheriff's offices, municipal police departments, and state investigators working the same trafficking corridor, shared access reduces duplication and keeps everyone working from the same version of the case.


Alert functionality in CrimeTracer lets investigators set notifications tied to their searches. When a search runs and new results appear, the investigator is notified. For trafficking cases where a suspect's activity might surface across multiple agencies over weeks or months, those alerts keep investigators current without requiring them to re-run searches manually every day.


## Why This Approach Fits Trafficking Investigations


The number of persons referred to U.S. attorneys for human trafficking offenses in fiscal year 2023[increased 23 percent](https://bjs.ojp.gov/library/publications/human-trafficking-data-collection-activities-2025) from a decade earlier, and the number prosecuted in U.S. district court grew by 73 percent over the same period. More cases are moving through the system. That increased volume puts pressure on investigators and prosecutors alike to collect, organize, and present evidence efficiently.


Most trafficking cases do not fall apart for lack of evidence. They fall apart because the evidence lives in too many places, managed by too many agencies, with no shared system to hold it together through the full arc from initial lead to courtroom. Closing that gap is what case folders are designed to do.
