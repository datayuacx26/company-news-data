# How to File Payroll Tax Forms 940, 941, and 944 for Growth-Stage Payroll Operators

Three IRS forms, three deadlines, and one dependency chain create most payroll tax confusion for growth-stage employers. Form 941 or Form 944 covers quarterly or annual federal withholding and FICA. Form 940 covers annual FUTA. SUTA payments made to the state determine your FUTA credit. This guide sequences five procedures in execution order, from form determination through annual Form 940 reconciliation, and links out to[why FUTA calculations break down for growth-stage payroll teams](https://www.asuresoftware.com/why-futa-calculations-break-down-and-how-growth-stage-payroll-teams-fix-them/) for the per-employee wage-base mechanics behind the FUTA deposit calculations below.


## Determine Your Filing Obligations First


Before any deposit or filing action is valid, you need to know which forms apply to your business and which states you owe unemployment tax to. These first two procedures establish that foundation.


### How to Determine Which IRS Payroll Tax Forms Apply to Your Business


How to Determine Which IRS Payroll Tax Forms Apply to Your Business is the procedure for mapping your payroll profile to the correct federal filing obligations, Form 941, Form 944, or Form 940, before your first payroll run or after a business structure change. The payroll owner or HR and payroll admin executes it during initial payroll setup, producing a confirmed form set with filing frequencies. Use it when setting up payroll for the first time or after receiving an unexpected IRS notice about a form change.


**Prerequisites:**


- Confirm your EIN is issued and active with the IRS.
- Calculate your prior-year total federal payroll tax liability, or a projected annual liability if you are a new employer.
- Gather any IRS correspondence on file, including a Form 944 designation letter if you have received one.
- Confirm your employee count and payroll frequency.


**Ordered steps:**


1. **Confirm your EIN status.** Verify your EIN is active and matches the name and entity type on file with the IRS to avoid form-routing errors.
2. **Calculate your projected annual federal payroll tax liability.** Add your estimated federal income tax withheld to employee and employer Social Security and Medicare taxes for the year.
3. **Apply the Form 944 threshold test.** If your projected annual liability is $1,000 or less and the IRS has invited or designated you for annual filing, you file Form 944 instead of Form 941, per the[IRS's guidance on annual employment tax filing](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/certain-taxpayers-may-file-their-employment-taxes-annually) as of July 2026.
4. **Default to Form 941 if no 944 designation exists.** Absent an IRS invitation to file annually, you file Form 941 quarterly regardless of your liability size.
5. **Confirm your Form 940 obligation separately.** FUTA applies to nearly every employer with active payroll, so check your specific filing threshold against the[IRS's Form 940 filing and deposit requirements](https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc759) as of July 2026.
6. **Document your confirmed form set.** Record the applicable forms, Form 940 plus either Form 941 or Form 944, their filing frequencies, and the basis for each determination in your payroll compliance log.


**Expected outcome:** A written payroll compliance form map specifying Form 941 or Form 944, with frequency, plus Form 940, with the eligibility basis documented for audit reference.


**When to use:** Use this procedure at payroll setup or after any IRS notice changing your filing designation. Do not use it as a substitute for a tax advisor review if your business has multi-state nexus or household employee payroll.


**Common pitfalls:**


- Assuming Form 944 is optional. Form 944 is an IRS designation, not an employer election made without IRS involvement, so filing Form 941 after being designated for Form 944 triggers a mismatch notice.
- Conflating Form 940 with Form 941. Form 940 reports FUTA, an employer-only unemployment tax, while Form 941 reports withheld income tax plus Social Security and Medicare. The two are never interchangeable.
- Missing the Form 944 election window. If you meet the $1,000 threshold but have not received an IRS invitation, you must request Form 944 status by calling the IRS at 800-829-4933 by April 1 of the tax year, or by sending a written request postmarked by March 15; missing that window means filing Form 941 for the year instead, per the[IRS's guidance on annual employment tax filing](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/certain-taxpayers-may-file-their-employment-taxes-annually) as of July 2026.

### How to Register for SUTA and Confirm State Unemployment Tax Obligations


How to Register for SUTA and Confirm State Unemployment Tax Obligations is the procedure for establishing state unemployment insurance accounts in every state where you have employees working, confirming your assigned tax rates, and linking state payments to the FUTA credit offset calculation on Form 940. The payroll owner or HR admin executes it during initial payroll setup or when hiring in a new state, producing active SUTA account numbers and confirmed rates per state.


**Prerequisites:**


- Confirm the list of states where at least one employee physically works.
- Confirm your EIN and entity legal name as registered with the IRS.
- Estimate quarterly payroll by state for rate-bracket determination.
- Complete the form determination described in the procedure above.


**Ordered steps:**


1. **Identify every state with payroll nexus.** List all states where at least one employee performs work, including remote workers whose work state differs from your company's headquarters.
2. **Register with each state's workforce agency.** Submit a new employer registration application to the state unemployment insurance agency in each nexus state, most of which offer online registration through their workforce or labor department portal.
3. **Record your assigned SUTA account number and new-employer rate.** Document the account number and initial tax rate each state agency issues upon registration confirmation.
4. **Verify your SUTA wage base by state.** Confirm the annual taxable wage base for each state, since it differs from the federal FUTA wage base, and configure your payroll system accordingly.
5. **Set up your SUTA payment schedule.** Determine each state's deposit frequency, most commonly quarterly, and add the due dates to your payroll compliance calendar.
6. **Confirm timely SUTA payment protects your full FUTA credit.** Document that SUTA taxes must be paid by the Form 940 due date to claim the standard FUTA credit offset described later in this guide.


**Expected outcome:** Active SUTA account numbers and confirmed tax rates for all nexus states, with payment schedules loaded into your payroll compliance calendar and the FUTA credit dependency documented.


**When to use:** Use this procedure when hiring your first employee in any state or expanding into a new state. Do not use it for household employers, who follow different SUTA rules, or for independent contractors, since SUTA does not apply to contractor payments.


**Common pitfalls:**


- Missing remote-worker nexus. An employee working remotely in a state creates SUTA nexus in that state even if your company has no physical office there.
- Confusing SUTA with FUTA. SUTA is paid to the state; FUTA is paid to the federal government through EFTPS deposits. They are separate obligations with separate accounts.


Multi-state SUTA registration is an area where growth-stage companies often underinvest, particularly once remote hiring puts employees in states the company never planned to operate in. ## Execute Your Deposit and Quarterly Filing Cycle


Once your forms are confirmed and your SUTA accounts are active, two cycles run throughout the year. FUTA deposits and Form 941 or Form 944 filings operate on separate but parallel schedules.


### How to Schedule and Execute FUTA Deposit Payments


How to Schedule and Execute FUTA Deposit Payments is the procedure for calculating your quarterly FUTA liability, determining whether a deposit is required, and submitting payment through EFTPS before the quarterly deadline. The payroll owner or payroll admin executes it quarterly, producing a confirmed EFTPS deposit record. Use it each quarter when your cumulative FUTA liability exceeds $500.


**Prerequisites:**


- Confirm your EFTPS enrollment and active login credentials.
- Gather quarterly gross wages per employee.
- Track FUTA taxable wages per employee against the annual wage base cap.
- Confirm SUTA account numbers and payment status from the procedure above.


**Ordered steps:**


1. **Calculate your FUTA taxable wages for the quarter.** Sum gross wages paid to each employee up to $7,000 for the year, excluding wages already taxed in prior quarters of the year.
2. **Apply the FUTA tax rate.** Multiply your FUTA taxable wages by 6.0%, the gross federal rate before any SUTA credit, to determine your gross FUTA liability for the quarter, per[IRS Tax Topic 759](https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc759) as of July 2026.
3. **Apply the SUTA credit estimate.** Reduce the 6.0% rate by up to 5.4% for a net effective rate of 0.6%, and only apply the credit if your SUTA payments are current, since an overdue state balance disqualifies the credit for that state.
4. **Determine whether a deposit is required.** If your cumulative FUTA liability for the year exceeds $500 at the end of any quarter, a deposit is due by the last day of the month following that quarter, per[IRS Tax Topic 759](https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc759) .
5. **Submit your deposit through EFTPS.** Log in to EFTPS, select Form 940 as the tax form, enter the deposit amount, and schedule payment at least one business day before the due date.
6. **Record your EFTPS confirmation number.** Save the confirmation number in your payroll compliance log alongside the quarter, deposit amount, and payment date for Form 940 reconciliation.
7. **Carry forward liability under $500.** If your cumulative FUTA liability does not exceed $500, carry the balance forward to the next quarter without depositing; a fourth-quarter balance of $500 or less can be paid directly with your Form 940 filing instead.


**Expected outcome:** A confirmed EFTPS deposit record for the quarter with the confirmation number logged, or a documented carry-forward entry if the $500 threshold was not met.


**When to use:** Use this procedure every quarter in which your cumulative FUTA liability crosses $500. Do not use it for Form 941 deposits, which follow a separate monthly or semiweekly schedule based on a different lookback period, covered next.


### How to Prepare and File Form 941 or Form 944 Each Period


How to Prepare and File Form 941 or Form 944 Each Period is the procedure for reconciling your quarterly (Form 941) or annual (Form 944) federal payroll tax deposits against withheld income tax, Social Security, and Medicare liabilities and submitting your return to the IRS on time. The payroll owner or payroll admin executes it, producing a filed return with either zero balance due or a documented adjustment. Use it for every Form 941 quarter or at year-end for Form 944 filers.


**Prerequisites:**


- Pull your payroll register for the filing period, including gross wages, withholdings, and employer tax shares.
- Gather your EFTPS deposit history and confirmation numbers for the period.
- Reference your prior period's Form 941 or Form 944 for liability carryover.
- Confirm your form designation from the procedure described earlier in this guide.


**Ordered steps:**


1. **Pull the payroll register for the period.** Export gross wages, federal income tax withheld, employee Social Security and Medicare withheld, and employer Social Security and Medicare contributions for every pay date in the quarter, or the year for Form 944.
2. **Complete the wage and tax lines of Form 941 or Form 944.** Enter your employee count, wages, federal income tax withheld, and Social Security and Medicare tax amounts using your payroll register totals.
3. **Calculate total taxes before adjustments.** Sum federal income tax withheld plus total Social Security and Medicare taxes, combining employee and employer shares.
4. **Reconcile your deposits against total liability.** Compare total taxes against the EFTPS deposits made during the period; the difference is either a balance due or an overpayment.
5. **Complete the deposit schedule section.** Semiweekly depositors, those whose four-quarter lookback total exceeded $50,000, complete Schedule B; monthly depositors, those at $50,000 or less, complete the monthly summary lines on the form itself, per[IRS Instructions for Form 941](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i941) as of July 2026.
6. **Review for fractions-of-cents and sick pay adjustments.** Apply any required adjustments to account for rounding differences or third-party sick pay.
7. **Submit your return by the deadline.** File electronically or by mail by the last day of the month following the quarter, April 30, July 31, October 31, or January 31 for 2026 per[IRS Instructions for Form 941](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i941) , or by January 31 for Form 944 filers; employers who made all required deposits in full and on time get an additional 10 calendar days to file.
8. **Save the IRS acknowledgment.** Retain the electronic filing acknowledgment or mailed confirmation as proof of timely filing.


**Expected outcome:** A filed Form 941 or Form 944 with IRS acknowledgment on record, no unexplained balance due, and deposit reconciliation documented in your payroll compliance log.


**When to use:** Use this procedure every quarter for Form 941 filers, or once annually by January 31 for Form 944 filers. Do not use it to report FUTA; FUTA is reported exclusively on Form 940.


**Common pitfalls:**


- Reporting FUTA on Form 941. FUTA belongs on Form 940 only. In Asure's experience, conflating the two is among the more common misfiling errors encountered when onboarding new payroll clients.
- Missing the deposit schedule section. Omitting Schedule B for semiweekly depositors triggers an IRS notice even when the underlying tax liability is correct.
- Missing the next-day deposit rule. If you accumulate $100,000 or more in employment tax liability on any single day, deposit it by the next business day; doing so also converts you to a semiweekly depositor for the rest of the current calendar year and all of the following calendar year, per[IRS Instructions for Form 941](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i941) as of July 2026.

## Reconcile FUTA Credits and File Form 940 Annually


The annual close-out connects everything upstream. Form 940 cannot be completed correctly until all four quarters of SUTA payments are confirmed.


### How to Reconcile SUTA Credits and File Form 940 Annually


How to Reconcile SUTA Credits and File Form 940 Annually is the procedure for verifying your state unemployment tax payments, calculating your allowable FUTA credit offset, completing Form 940, and submitting any remaining FUTA balance due by January 31. The payroll owner or payroll admin executes it at year-end, producing a filed Form 940 with SUTA credit documentation. Use it once per calendar year after all four quarters of SUTA payments are confirmed.


**Prerequisites:**


- Compile your annual FUTA taxable wages per employee from your payroll system.
- Confirm SUTA payments for all nexus states for the full calendar year, from the two procedures described earlier.
- Gather your EFTPS FUTA deposit history and confirmation numbers for all quarters.
- Review state SUTA rate notices and any credit reduction notices on Schedule A (Form 940) for states with outstanding federal loans.


**Ordered steps:**


1. **Compile your annual FUTA taxable wages.** Sum wages paid to each employee up to $7,000 across all four quarters, using your payroll register.
2. **Calculate your gross FUTA liability at 6.0%.** Multiply your total FUTA taxable wages by 6.0% to establish gross federal liability before any SUTA credit, per[IRS Tax Topic 759](https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc759) .
3. **Verify SUTA payments for all nexus states.** Confirm that state unemployment taxes were paid in full and on time for every state on your nexus list; late or unpaid SUTA disqualifies the credit for that state.
4. **Apply the SUTA credit offset.** Reduce your gross FUTA liability by up to 5.4% for each state where SUTA was paid timely, yielding the standard net FUTA rate of 0.6% on qualifying wages.
5. **Check for credit reduction states.** Review Schedule A (Form 940) to identify any states subject to a FUTA credit reduction because of outstanding federal unemployment loans, and add the credit reduction amount to your net FUTA liability. For the 2025 tax year, filed by January 31, 2026, California employers faced a 1.2% credit reduction and U.S. Virgin Islands employers faced a 4.5% credit reduction, while Connecticut and New York avoided any reduction by repaying their loan balances before the November 10, 2025 deadline, per the[Department of Labor's Federal Register notice on FUTA credit reductions](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-01-12/html/2026-00342.htm) . The list for the 2026 tax year will not be finalized until after the comparable November 2026 deadline, so confirm the current list directly with the Department of Labor before filing.
6. **Subtract quarterly FUTA deposits already made.** Deduct all EFTPS FUTA deposits made during the year from your net FUTA liability to determine the remaining balance due or overpayment.
7. **Complete and file Form 940.** Enter all calculated amounts on Form 940, attach Schedule A if any credit reduction states apply, and file electronically or by mail by January 31; employers who deposited all FUTA tax when due get an automatic extension to file by February 10, per[IRS Tax Topic 759](https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc759) .
8. **Pay any remaining FUTA balance.** If your year-end balance due exceeds $500, it should already have been deposited quarterly; pay any residual balance by January 31 to avoid penalty.
9. **Retain your filed Form 940 and SUTA payment documentation.** Archive the filed return, EFTPS confirmation numbers, and state SUTA payment receipts together for future reference.


**Expected outcome:** A filed Form 940 with IRS acknowledgment, SUTA credit documentation for all nexus states, and no unexplained FUTA balance due, with supporting records archived.


**When to use:** Use this procedure once per calendar year after your December 31 payroll is closed. Do not use it if you had no employees and paid no wages during the calendar year, since no Form 940 filing obligation exists.


**Common pitfalls:**


- Skipping Schedule A for credit reduction states. If any state on your nexus list carried a federal unemployment loan balance, omitting Schedule A understates your FUTA liability and triggers a notice.
- Assuming SUTA payments automatically generate the FUTA credit. The credit requires timely SUTA payment; payments made after the Form 940 due date do not qualify for the current-year credit.

## How to Sequence These Procedures


Execute these five procedures in order. Start with form determination before any deposit or filing action, since the wrong form designation invalidates all downstream work. Complete SUTA registration immediately after, because SUTA account numbers and confirmed rates are prerequisites for both the FUTA deposit procedure and the annual Form 940 reconciliation. Run FUTA deposit scheduling every quarter throughout the year, and run Form 941 or Form 944 filing on its own quarterly or annual cycle in parallel. At year-end, execute annual Form 940 reconciliation only after all four quarters of SUTA payments are confirmed. If you are a new employer partway through the year, start with form determination immediately rather than waiting for your first filing deadline to force the decision.

## Related Questions


**Is FUTA reported on Form 941?** No. FUTA is reported exclusively on Form 940, filed annually. Form 941 reports withheld federal income tax plus employee and employer Social Security and Medicare taxes and does not include FUTA. Reporting FUTA on Form 941 is one of the more common payroll misreporting errors Asure encounters when onboarding new payroll clients.


**Is SUTA the same as SUI?** Yes. SUTA and SUI refer to the same employer-paid state unemployment tax; the terminology varies by state. The obligation, registration process, and relationship to the FUTA credit offset are identical regardless of which term your state uses.


**Do I have to file both Form 940 and Form 941?** Most employers file both. Form 941 is filed quarterly to report withheld income tax and Social Security and Medicare taxes, while Form 940 is filed annually to report FUTA. The exception is employers designated by the IRS for Form 944, who file that instead of Form 941 but still file Form 940 annually for FUTA.


**Who pays FUTA, the employer or the employee?** FUTA is paid entirely by the employer. Employees do not contribute to FUTA. The employer pays 6.0% on the first $7,000 of each employee's wages, reduced to an effective 0.6% after the standard 5.4% SUTA credit offset for employers current on state unemployment tax payments, per[IRS Tax Topic 759](https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc759) .


**What is the difference between FUTA and SUTA?** FUTA is the federal unemployment tax, reported on Form 940 and paid to the IRS. SUTA is the state unemployment tax, paid to the state workforce agency where employees work. Both are employer-only taxes, and timely SUTA payment is what unlocks the credit that reduces your FUTA rate from 6.0% to 0.6%.
