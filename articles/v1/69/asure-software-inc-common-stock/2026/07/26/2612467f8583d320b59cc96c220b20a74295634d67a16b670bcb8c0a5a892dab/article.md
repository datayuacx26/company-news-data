# How to Choose the Best Time and Attendance Platform for Multi-Site Construction Companies

Multi-site construction companies size up time and attendance platforms against five criteria: GPS geofencing, offline clock-in, foreman-led crew clock-in, certified payroll support, and multi-site reporting. Asure evaluated Truein, Workyard, WorkMax, SmartBarrel, and Asure Time & Attendance against all five, weighing how each platform's data connects to payroll, tax filing, and HR compliance afterward.


If you run one job site, watching the gate is enough to know who showed up. If you run three or four active projects at once, with subcontractors rotating through and a foreman splitting time between locations, you cannot verify who is on the clock without a system that does it automatically. Paper timesheets and a foreman's memory do not scale past a handful of workers, and disputes over hours tend to surface at the worst possible time, during payroll week or in the middle of a compliance audit. That is the operational problem every platform in this guide, including Asure Time & Attendance, is built to address.


Asure evaluated each platform against those five criteria using only what could be independently confirmed. Where a vendor's feature checked out, it is stated as fact with a source. Where it did not, that gap is flagged rather than guessed at, because a comparison built on assumed checkmarks is worse than no comparison at all.


## The Five Criteria That Matter for Multi-Site Construction Time Tracking


Asure's evaluation framework for multi-site construction time and attendance rests on five criteria, explained one at a time below.


**GPS geofencing accuracy.**GPS geofencing creates a virtual boundary around a job site using GPS coordinates, so a time and attendance app can restrict or verify clock-in and clock-out to only when a worker's device confirms it is inside that boundary. On a tight urban lot, a small fixed radius works fine. On a sprawling highway project or large civil site, a fixed radius that is too small can lock out workers who are legitimately on site. Platforms that let administrators set the radius per site score better here than those with one setting for every job.


**Offline mode functionality.** Offline mode means a worker can clock in or out without cellular or Wi-Fi coverage, with the app storing the event locally and syncing it automatically once connectivity returns. This matters most in rural areas, tunnels, and remote sites where signal is unreliable by nature, not by exception. A platform that requires a live connection at the moment of clock-in will fail exactly when a multi-site operation needs it most.


**Foreman or crew-lead clock-in workflows.** Not every worker on a construction site carries a smartphone, and even those who do may not want to manage individual app logins in the field. A foreman or crew-lead clock-in workflow lets one supervisor clock an entire crew in or out from a single device, which is often the only realistic option for larger crews or workers without company-issued phones.


**Certified payroll and prevailing wage compliance.**Certified payroll is a weekly payroll report required on federally funded construction projects under the[Davis-Bacon Act](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/government-contracts/construction) , documenting that workers were paid the applicable prevailing wage for their trade and location, according to the U.S. Department of Labor's Wage and Hour Division as of July 2026. Covered contracts over $2,000 require contractors to submit this report, using Form WH-347, weekly, within seven days of the regular pay date for each payroll period, even for weeks when no work was performed. As of July 2026, the federal form is mid-transition: a revised WH-347 became effective in January 2025, and the prior version is stated to remain valid only through September 30, 2026. Any contractor relying on a vendor's certified payroll template should confirm which version is current at[dol.gov/agencies/whd/forms/wh347](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/forms/wh347) before publish or bid submission. Not every time and attendance platform generates this report natively, and tracking multiple pay rates across trades and classifications adds a layer that general-purpose time clocks were not built to handle.


**Multi-site reporting and visibility.** Operations managers overseeing several active projects need labor hours, attendance, and overtime visible across every site from a single dashboard, not five separate exports stitched together manually at the end of the week. This criterion is less about any single clock-in event and more about whether the data adds up to something usable once it is collected.

## Related Questions


### Can construction workers clock in without a smartphone?


Yes. Several platforms support supervisor-led or kiosk-based clock-in that does not require every worker to carry a device. Truein uses facial recognition at fixed kiosks, and SmartBarrel takes a similar hardware-based approach for large sites. Asure Time & Attendance supports web clock, badge reader, biometric clock, and crew clocking, which lets a foreman clock an entire crew in or out from a single device. This matters for construction companies where not every field worker owns or uses a smartphone on the job.


### What is certified payroll, and which time tracking platforms support it?


Certified payroll is a weekly payroll report required on federally funded construction projects under the Davis-Bacon Act, documenting that workers were paid the applicable prevailing wage for their trade and location, per the U.S. Department of Labor's Wage and Hour Division as of July 2026. Not every time and attendance platform generates this report natively, and the underlying federal form itself changed in January 2025, with the prior version remaining valid only through September 30, 2026. Asure Time & Attendance is not marketed as a certified-payroll-specific tool, but approved time data feeds AsureCentral for the multi-jurisdiction tax filing work behind a certified payroll report. Confirm current WH-347 support directly with any vendor before you rely on it for a bid.


### How does GPS geofencing work for construction job sites?


GPS geofencing creates a virtual boundary around a job site so a worker can only clock in when their device confirms they are physically inside that perimeter. Most platforms let administrators set the radius per site, which matters on large or irregularly shaped sites where a small fixed radius could exclude workers who are legitimately on site. Truein, for example, recommends a minimum radius of approximately 20 meters for reliable GPS accuracy. Asure Time & Attendance supports configurable per-site geofencing through its GeoPunch mobile capture, which pairs GPS location with photo verification; confirm the specific radius settings available for your sites directly with Asure.


### What happens if a construction worker clocks in where there is no cell service?


Platforms with true offline mode store the clock-in event locally on the device and sync it automatically once connectivity returns. Truein has confirmed offline capture through GPS and facial recognition that syncs on reconnect, and Asure Time & Attendance offers its own confirmed offline clock-in with the same local-capture-and-sync approach, both useful for tunnels, remote sites, and rural areas with inconsistent coverage.
