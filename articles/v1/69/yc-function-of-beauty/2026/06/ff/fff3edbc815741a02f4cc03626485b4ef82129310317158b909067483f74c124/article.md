# Dry Scalp and Oily Hair: Why It Happens and How to Fix It

# Dry Scalp and Oily Hair: Why It Happens and How to Solve Your Hair's Behavior


Do you have an oily scalp and dry ends?


At first glance, those two concerns seem contradictory. How can your scalp feel dry while your hair looks oily?


.


## Why Do I Have a Dry Scalp and Oily Hair?


A dry scalp and oily hair often occur when your scalp's moisture barrier becomes compromised while your sebaceous glands continue producing oil.


Your scalp contains thousands of sebaceous glands that produce sebum, a natural oil designed to lubricate both the scalp and hair fiber.


When the scalp becomes irritated, dehydrated, or stripped of moisture, the skin barrier can become disrupted. In response, the scalp may continue producing oil while still feeling tight, dry, itchy, or flaky.


The result?


- Dry or irritated scalp


- Oily roots


- Greasy hair


- Flaking


- Itchiness


- Hair that feels heavy at the roots but dry at the ends


## What Causes a Dry Scalp and Oily Hair?


### Seasonal Changes


Cold weather, indoor heating, and low humidity can pull moisture from the scalp in the same way they affect the hair fiber.


Meanwhile, your sebaceous glands may continue producing their normal amount of oil, creating the feeling of a dry scalp and oily roots simultaneously.


### Hair Type


Straight hair is particularly prone to looking oily.


Why?


Straight hair has a smooth, direct path from scalp to ends. Oil can travel easily down the hair shaft, making buildup more visible.


Curly and coily hair types often experience the opposite challenge. Natural oils have a harder time traveling through bends and curves, which can leave lengths feeling drier even when the scalp produces adequate oil.


## How to Support a Dry Scalp and Oily Hair


### 1. Stop Overwashing


If you're washing every day, consider gradually extending the time between wash days.


This allows your scalp's natural oil production to normalize and may reduce excess oil over time.


For oily hair types, focus shampoo on the scalp rather than the lengths of your hair.


### 2. Use Daily Lightweight Hydration & Weekly Treatments


Oily hair still needs moisture. Oil and moisture are two different things. Oil is a barrier that protects the hair and seals moisture in, but it does not provide hydration itself.


Lightweight conditioners and personalized Leave-In Conditioners can help support hydration without weighing hair down.


A weekly hair mask can help support moisture retention, improve manageability, and protect the hair cuticle from environmental stressors. The ideal frequency depends on your hair's structure and how quickly it loses moisture. One to two times a week is optimal.


### 3. Avoid Extremely Hot Water


Hot water can strip away protective oils and disrupt the scalp's moisture barrier.


Instead, wash with lukewarm water to help maintain a healthier scalp environment.


### 4. Support Your Hair's Moisture Balance


The ends of your hair are often the oldest and most vulnerable part of the fiber.


A personalized Leave-In Conditioner can help support moisture retention while maintaining lightweight movement.


For straight and wavy hair, a Custom Hair Serum can help smooth the cuticle without adding heaviness to the mid-lengths to ends.


### 5. Keep Your Scalp Environment Healthy


Your scalp is skin.


Just like the skin on your face, it responds to changes in humidity, temperature, products, and cleansing habits.


To keep your scalp environment healthy for oily hair, prioritize gentle cleansing of the scalp, avoid over-stimulating the sebaceous glands, and prevent heavy products from accumulating at the roots. A balanced scalp discourages excess sebum production and reduces greasiness over time. Supporting a balanced scalp environment often leads to healthier hair behavior over time.
