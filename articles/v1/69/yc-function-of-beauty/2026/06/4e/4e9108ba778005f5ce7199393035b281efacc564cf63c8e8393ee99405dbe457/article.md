## What Chlorine, Salt Water, and Sun Actually Do to Hair


Hair is made primarily of protein called keratin and protected by an outer layer called the cuticle. The cuticle acts as the hair's first line of defense, helping regulate moisture retention and protecting the inner structure of the fiber.


Chlorine weakens keratin, which is the structural protein that gives hair its elasticity, resulting in split ends and breakage.


Exposure to saltwater and UV rays physically pulls moisture from your strands through osmosis, leaving them dehydrated. Salt acts as a sponge, pulling water out of the hair shaft, which gives it a parched, straw-like texture. When this happens, moisture can escape more easily, the hair surface may become rougher, and strands can appear drier, duller, or more prone to frizz. Color-treated hair may also experience faster fading when exposed to the sun and pool water.


Using the right products can help reduce breakage and recover from the summer stressors that we put on our hair.


The goal is to understand how your hair's structure responds to summer conditions and support it accordingly.


## Sun, Salt Water, and Chlorine Affect Every Hair Type Differently


Hair type isn't just how your hair looks. It's how it functions.


The shape of the hair fiber influences how oils travel, how moisture is retained, and how the hair responds to environmental stressors like chlorine, UV exposure, humidity, and salt water.


### Straight Hair


To protect and repair straight hair from chlorine and saltwater, focus on lightweight hydration and smoothing the cuticle. Straight hair shows structural damage quickly through frizz, limpness, and a straw-like texture, but it also weighs down easily if you use products that are too heavy.


Avoid rubbing your hair with heavy beach towels, which roughs up the hair cuticle and causes extreme frizz. Instead, gently blot your hair or wrap it in a soft microfiber towel or cotton t-shirt.


### Wavy Hair


Wavy hair (Type 2) sits between straight and curly hair in terms of moisture distribution.


The bends in the hair strand make it slightly harder for oils to travel evenly, which can make waves more susceptible to frizz and moisture loss during the summer months.


Sun exposure, chlorine, and humidity can disrupt wave definition, leaving hair looking less uniform and more prone to expansion.


For wavy hair, protecting your pattern means balancing heavy moisture retention with curl-safe detangling. Wavy hair is naturally more porous and prone to frizz than straight hair, meaning chlorine and salt water can completely flatten your wave definition and turn your texture coarse or stringy.


### Curly Hair


Curly hair (Type 3) naturally experiences greater challenges with moisture distribution because oils must travel around multiple curves and bends in the hair fiber.


Protecting your curls from chlorine and saltwater is all about maximizing moisture barriers and preventing severe shrinkage and matting. Curly hair (Type 3) is naturally highly porous, meaning it absorbs damaging pool chemicals and salt like a sponge, leading to extreme frizz and hair damage.


### Coily Hair


Coily hair (Type 4) has the most complex strand geometry of all hair types, making moisture retention especially important.


Because natural oils have the hardest time traveling from root to end, coily hair often relies heavily on conditioning and moisture-focused routines to maintain softness and manageability.


After swimming or prolonged sun exposure, coily hair may experience increased dryness and require additional hydration to help support the cuticle and maintain moisture balance.
