# How to Transition Your Hair Routine From Summer to Fall, by Hair Type

August is when your hair can start noticing fall before you do.


Summer asks your hair to navigate heat, humidity, UV exposure, sweat, and frequent styling. As the season changes, cooler temperatures and lower humidity change the environment around the hair fiber, too. By September and October, a routine designed primarily to manage summer humidity may no longer be addressing what your hair needs most.


But that doesn't mean you need an entirely new routine.


It means understanding


how your hair structure responds to its environment


and adjusting where your routine puts its focus.


Because hair isn't random. Its behavior is largely determined by its structure, and that structure responds to moisture, friction, heat, humidity, and the condition of the cuticle.


## Why the Summer-to-Fall Transition Matters for Your Hair


The outer layer of your hair, the cuticle, acts as a protective barrier. Along with the hair's natural lipid layer, it helps regulate how moisture moves into and out of the fiber.


When that surface system is intact, hair is better able to resist excess water. When it becomes compromised by heat, chemical processing, friction, or environmental stress, moisture movement becomes less controlled. That's when you may notice roughness, dryness, frizz, or a loss of definition.


Humidity adds another layer to the equation.


Inside the cortex, hydrogen bonds are temporary connections that help maintain the shape of the hair. Water can break and reform these bonds, which is why humidity can change how hair behaves. When moisture enters the fiber unevenly, the hair can swell and shift shape, creating the frizz and flyaways associated with humid weather.


As the air becomes drier, the problem can change.


Instead of primarily managing excess moisture coming from the environment, your routine increasingly needs to help


retain moisture within the hair


. Dry air also makes static more noticeable because electrical charges don't dissipate as easily. Friction from sweaters, hats, scarves, pillowcases, and brushing can make those flyaways even more obvious.


That doesn't mean every hair type needs the same amount of moisture. In fact, this is where hair type matters most.


## Straight Hair: Keep Cleansing Balanced, Add Lightweight Protection


Straight hair has a relatively uniform fiber structure, so sebum can travel easily from the scalp toward the ends. That's one reason straight hair can become oily or look weighed down faster than other hair types. At the same time, over-washing can contribute to dryness, particularly through the lengths and ends.


As fall approaches, the goal isn't to suddenly stop cleansing. It's to


balance cleansing with moisture retention


.


Continue focusing shampoo on the scalp, where oil and buildup accumulate, while keeping heavier conditioning products away from the roots. Depending on scalp activity, straight hair may need cleansing every one to three days.


Then add lightweight protection to the lengths.


A leave-in treatment on damp hair can provide light moisture and help reduce flyaways without overwhelming the strand. For a finishing step, a lightweight serum can smooth the surface, add shine, and provide a protective layer against dryness and static.


Your fall focus:


hydration without weight, balanced cleansing, and protection for dry ends.


Try:


Custom Straight Hair Shampoo + Conditioner, Straight Hair Mask once a week, Straight Hair Leave-In, and Custom Hair Serum.


## Wavy Hair: Move From Humidity Control to Moisture Balance


Wavy hair sits in the middle of the spectrum. Its S-shaped pattern creates bends along the fiber, which means sebum doesn't travel as evenly as it does along straight hair. The result can be familiar: oily roots, dry ends, and a wave pattern that seems to have a mind of its own.


In summer, humidity can disrupt those waves by changing the temporary bonds inside the cortex. In fall, lower humidity can shift the problem toward dryness, static, and loss of definition.


The answer isn't simply adding the heaviest product you can find.


Wavy hair benefits from


balanced moisture that supports the wave without stretching it out


. Keep cleansing gentle and focus shampoo on the scalp. On days when your hair doesn't need a full wash, refreshing with conditioner or a lightweight leave-in can help maintain moisture without repeatedly cleansing the strand.


As the air gets drier, your mask can also become more important. The Wavy Hair Mask with vegan squalane provides deeper conditioning for waves that are feeling dry or more prone to frizz.


For styling, apply your wave-defining product to soaking-wet or damp hair and scrunch rather than brushing through afterward. That helps preserve the natural wave pattern instead of breaking it apart.


Your fall focus:


balanced moisture, wave definition, and less friction.


Try:


Custom Wavy Hair Shampoo + Conditioner, Wavy Hair Leave-In Treatment, Wavy Hair Mask one to two times a week as needed, and Zero-Gravity Styling Mousse for definition.


## Curly Hair: Prioritize Moisture Retention, Not Just Hydration


Curly hair has a structural challenge that doesn't disappear when summer ends: the bends and curves in the fiber make it harder for natural oils to travel from the scalp to the ends.


That's why curly hair can feel dry even when the scalp doesn't.


The shape of the fiber also means the cuticle is less uniformly aligned, which can make moisture move in and out more readily. Dryness isn't a sign that your hair is doing something wrong.


It's geometry.


Fall makes moisture retention even more important.


Rather than simply adding more water-based hydration, think about what happens


after


hydration. A leave-in treatment helps maintain moisture throughout the day, while a curl cream conditions the surface of the strand and helps support definition. Layering products allows you to build the amount of moisture and control your individual curls need.


As fall progresses, make deep conditioning a consistent part of your routine. The Curly Hair Mask, formulated with coconut oil and shea butter, provides more intensive conditioning for curls that are feeling dry or less defined.


And keep friction low. Avoid dry brushing, detangle while conditioning, and consider a silk or satin pillowcase to reduce friction and moisture loss while you sleep.


Your fall focus:


moisture retention, definition, low friction, and consistent conditioning.


Try:


Custom Curly Hair Shampoo + Conditioner, Curly Hair Mask, Curly Leave-In Treatment, and Super Shape Curl Cream.


## Coily Hair: Protect the Moisture You've Worked to Keep


Coily hair has the most complex pathway for natural oils to travel. Tight bends and turns create more points of friction and stress along the strand, while the cuticle is more separated and less uniformly aligned.


That makes coily hair particularly susceptible to moisture loss, dryness, and breakage. Again, this isn't a flaw.


It's the physics of the fiber.


When humidity drops, moisture retention becomes even more important.


For coily hair, the goal should be less about repeatedly adding moisture and more about


keeping that moisture in the fiber


. Gentle cleansing helps preserve the hair's surface lipids, while rich conditioning and leave-in products provide ongoing support.


Coily hair generally benefits from less frequent cleansing—often every five to seven days depending on scalp needs and lifestyle—with shampoo concentrated primarily on the scalp.


After conditioning, layer your leave-in while the hair is still wet, followed by your styling product. For additional protection, a serum can provide another surface layer that helps reduce moisture loss, friction, and static.


As fall moves toward winter, protective styling and low-manipulation routines become increasingly useful for minimizing mechanical stress.


Your fall focus:


moisture retention, lipid support, low manipulation, and protection from friction.


Try:


Coily Hair Shampoo + Conditioner, Coily Hair Mask, Coily Leave-In Treatment, and Custom Hair Serum.


## One Important Note About Porosity


You may have heard that straight hair is low porosity and curly or coily hair is high porosity.


There's some structural reasoning behind those tendencies, but


hair type and porosity aren't the same thing


.


Porosity describes how easily hair absorbs and retains moisture, and it's influenced by the condition of the cuticle. Damage from chemical processing, heat, friction, and environmental stress can change how porous hair behaves. Two people with the same curl pattern can therefore have very different moisture needs.


That's why your routine should respond to both


your hair type and your hair's current condition


.


If your waves suddenly feel dry, your curls aren't holding, or your straight hair feels rough at the ends, don't assume you need an entirely different routine. Look at what your hair is telling you about moisture retention, surface condition, buildup, and friction.


## The Fall Hair Rule: Follow the Structure


The biggest seasonal shift isn't about replacing your entire summer routine.


It's about changing what your routine is designed to do.


In summer, your hair may need more support against humidity, heat, UV exposure, and environmental moisture. In fall, lower humidity and increasing friction can make moisture retention, conditioning, and static management more important.


And the right adjustment depends on your hair's structure.


Straight:


Keep it light. Balance cleansing with hydration and protect the ends.


Wavy:


Keep the balance. Add conditioning without sacrificing your wave pattern.


Curly:


Layer moisture and focus on retaining it.


Coily:


Prioritize moisture sealing, low manipulation, and protection.


Your hair doesn't need to follow the calendar perfectly.


It just needs a routine that understands


how its structure responds when the environment changes


.


Because great hair care isn't about fighting your hair.


It's about understanding it.
