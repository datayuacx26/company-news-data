## What a Skills Gap Is and Why It Matters


A skills gap is the distance between the skills an organization needs from its people and the skills those people actually have. Simple concept, but the implications run deep.


At the individual level, it might be a manager who has never led a remote team, or a rep who cannot speak to a new product line. At the organizational level, it is what happens when a company changes strategy faster than its workforce can follow, and the people executing the plan are not equipped to execute it well.


Every organization has some version of this. The difference between companies that grow and companies that stall often comes down to whether they can see their gaps clearly and close them quickly.


## Common Types of Skills Gaps in the Workplace


Not every skills gap looks the same, and knowing the type you are dealing with changes how you respond.


There are two categories that show up most consistently across organizations:


### Technical Skills Gaps


These are the most visible.[AI adoption](https://arist.com/solutions/ai-upskilling) , data literacy, cybersecurity, software proficiency: when these are missing, work simply does not get done right. Technical gaps tend to grow fastest because tools and systems evolve faster than most training programs can keep pace with.


### Soft Skills Gaps


Harder to measure, but just as damaging. Communication breakdowns, poor adaptability, and thin leadership benches can quietly sink teams even when technical competency is high. These gaps often surface in performance reviews and manager feedback long before anyone names them as training problems.

Four forces are driving that number up:


-

-

-


Schools frequently graduate people trained for roles as they existed years ago, not as those roles exist now.


-


[Treating training as a one-time event](https://arist.com/resources/blogs/how-to-design-training-courses-for-actionable-change) cannot keep pace with how fast roles and expectations evolve.

## How to Conduct a Skills Gap Analysis


Running a skills gap analysis doesn't need to be complicated. Most organizations follow four core steps.


### The Four-Step Process


Start with a clear picture of where you are and where you need to go.


-


Define the skills your organization needs to meet its goals, broken down by role or department.


-


Assess your current workforce using surveys, performance data, manager input, or structured assessments.


-


Compare what exists against what's required to identify the gaps.


-


Focus on which gaps matter most based on business impact and[close them through targeted training](https://arist.com/resources/blogs/how-to-improve-employee-training-proven-strategies) , hiring, or role redesign.


### Choosing Your Methods


Different situations call for different approaches. Surveys work well at scale. One-on-one assessments go deeper. Performance reviews surface gaps that employees may not self-report. The right method depends on your team size, data availability, and timeline.


## Skills Gap Analysis Methods and Tools


Organizations typically rely on a few core methods to identify where skill gaps exist.


Surveys and questionnaires ask employees and managers to self-report confidence and competency across role-relevant skills. Interviews and focus groups go deeper, surfacing context that surveys miss. Performance reviews and competency assessments compare actual output against defined role expectations. Observation and job analysis watch how work actually gets done and reveal gaps that self-reporting often obscures.


### Choosing the Right Tool


The method you choose should match your organization's size, budget, and urgency. Options range from spreadsheet-based skills gap analysis templates in Excel to dedicated software that maps competencies at scale. Simpler tools work well for smaller teams; larger organizations benefit from structured reporting and role-based competency frameworks.

## Strategies for Closing Skills Gaps


Closing a skills gap requires action at the organizational level, beyond individual effort alone. The most effective approaches combine honest assessment with structured delivery.


-


Conduct a skill gap analysis to map current capabilities against what roles actually require. Without this baseline, training efforts tend to miss the mark.


-


[Build learning into the flow of work](https://arist.com/resources/blogs/build-effective-learning-strategy) . Training that happens separately from daily tasks rarely sticks. Short, frequent practice tied to real job contexts produces better retention.


-


Use manager involvement to reinforce learning. Employees who discuss new skills with their managers are far more likely to apply them.


-


Hire and partner strategically when internal development takes too long. Upskilling works well for incremental gaps; critical shortfalls sometimes require outside expertise.


-


[Revisit and repeat](https://arist.com/resources/blogs/the-role-of-continuous-learning-in-talent-retention-in-the-corporate-world) . Skills gaps are not static. Regular reassessment keeps training aligned with where the business is actually heading.

A skills gap analysis is a structured process that compares the skills your organization needs against the skills your workforce currently has.
