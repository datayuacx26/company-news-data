# Proven Pressure Telemetry Applications, Reimagined with SoHo

Implantable pressure telemetry has helped researchers answer complex physiological questions for decades. From cardiovascular disease models to reproductive physiology, respiratory monitoring, neurological research, and specialized pressure endpoints, DSI[pressure-sensing technology](https://www.datasci.com/solutions/cardiovascular/pressure-sensing-technologies) has supported peer-reviewed studies where continuous data from conscious, freely moving animals were essential.


[SoHoTM](https://www.datasci.com/products/implantable-telemetry/soho-telemetry-system) builds on that legacy.


As DSI’s newest implantable pressure telemetry platform, SoHo is designed to make pressure monitoring more flexible, approachable, and refined. Its smaller system footprint, streamlined setup, and support for group-housed animals give researchers new ways to bring established pressure-sensing applications into their study designs.


The publications highlighted below were conducted using legacy DSI pressure-sensing technology, not SoHo. But they demonstrate the breadth of applications that pressure telemetry has already made possible — and the types of studies researchers can now reimagine with SoHo.


##


### Why Pressure Telemetry Matters


Pressure-based endpoints are often highly dynamic. Blood pressure, left ventricular pressure, pulmonary artery pressure, right ventricular pressure, intrauterine pressure, intracranial pressure, and[other pressure signals](https://www.datasci.com/solutions/unique-applications) can shift with disease progression, therapeutic intervention, circadian rhythm, activity, pregnancy, stress, or behavior.


For researchers, this creates a challenge. Snapshot measurements may miss important physiological changes, while repeated handling, restraint, or anesthesia can introduce variability and alter the very signals being studied.


Implantable telemetry helps address these limitations by enabling continuous pressure monitoring in conscious, freely moving animals. This allows researchers to follow physiological changes over time, capture richer datasets, and reduce reliance on repeated invasive or stressful procedures.


SoHo advances that approach with a platform designed for modern telemetry workflows — helping researchers collect pressure data in a system that is easier to set up, more space-conscious, and compatible with refined study designs.


### What SoHo Changes About Pressure Telemetry Studies


DSI pressure telemetry has already supported a wide range of pressure-based applications. SoHo’s value is not that it creates these applications from scratch. Its value is that it gives researchers a new way to run them.


With SoHo, researchers can bring established pressure endpoints into study environments that may have been harder to support with traditional telemetry systems. A smaller system footprint can make it easier to incorporate telemetry into different laboratory spaces or study configurations. Streamlined setup can help reduce workflow complexity and make continuous pressure monitoring more approachable for research teams. Support for group-housed animals can help researchers preserve social interaction and align studies with refinement and animal welfare goals.


Together, these advantages create new flexibility in how implantable pressure telemetry studies are designed and executed.


### Cardiovascular and Pulmonary Pressure Monitoring


[Cardiovascular applications](https://www.datasci.com/solutions/cardiovascular) are among the most recognized uses for implantable pressure telemetry. Blood pressure and left ventricular pressure are common endpoints, but DSI pressure-sensing technology has also supported more specialized cardiovascular and pulmonary vascular measurements.


In pulmonary hypertension research, Sebkhi et al. used DSI pressure-sensing technology to continuously monitor pulmonary artery pressure in conscious rats during the development of hypoxia-induced pulmonary hypertension and treatment with sildenafil. This approach allowed researchers to follow disease progression and therapeutic response over time without repeated anesthesia.


Handoko et al. also demonstrated the value of chronic pressure telemetry in pulmonary hypertension research by developing and validating a refined technique for monitoring right ventricular or pulmonary artery pressure in rats. By combining telemetry with echocardiography, the researchers were able to follow cardiovascular changes longitudinally in conscious animals.


These studies show the power of continuous pressure monitoring for understanding progressive cardiovascular disease. With SoHo, researchers can consider how these established pressure endpoints may fit into more flexible study environments, including group-housed designs.


### Respiratory Rate Derived from Blood Pressure Signals


Pressure telemetry can also provide value beyond the primary pressure endpoint.


Kramer et al. showed that respiratory rate could be extracted from the blood pressure waveform using DSI software. This allowed researchers to derive an additional physiological parameter from the same implanted transmitter already measuring blood pressure, without adding hardware or increasing animal handling.


For researchers designing studies with SoHo, this type of application highlights the potential value of collecting more information from a single implanted device. When pressure signals can support additional physiological insights, research teams may be able to simplify study design while still capturing meaningful longitudinal data.


### Intrauterine Pressure and Uterine Contractility


Some pressure endpoints are especially valuable because they capture dynamic physiological events that cannot be fully understood through isolated measurements.


In reproductive physiology, McCloskey et al. implanted DSI pressure telemetry catheters into the uterine horn of pregnant mice to continuously record intrauterine pressure in conscious, freely moving animals. This enabled researchers to study uterine contractility throughout pregnancy and connect changes in uterine excitability to a specific potassium channel involved in uterine function.


This application demonstrates how pressure telemetry can support research questions outside traditional cardiovascular studies, particularly when real-time physiological monitoring is essential.


For future studies, SoHo offers a new way to think about how continuous pressure endpoints may be incorporated into refined and flexible telemetry workflows.


### Intracavernosal Pressure in Sexual Function Research


DSI pressure telemetry has also supported specialized physiological research where pressure changes are central to the experimental question.


In sexual function research, miniaturized implantable pressure telemetry devices have been used to record intracavernosal pressure in conscious rats across multiple erectile contexts, including copulation, reflexive erection, and psychogenic erection. These studies helped researchers better understand the coordination of neural, vascular, and muscular events involved in erectile function, as well as the impact of spinal cord injury on penile reflexes.


These applications show how implantable pressure sensing can help researchers study complex physiological responses in conscious animals and capture dynamic pressure changes as they occur.


### Intracranial Pressure Applications


Pressure telemetry can also support neurological and ophthalmic research where elevated pressure contributes to disease processes.


For example, Nusbaum et al. studied the effects of elevated intracranial pressure on optic nerve and retinal ganglion cell degeneration in mice. Applications like this demonstrate the broader relevance of pressure-sensing approaches beyond cardiovascular and pulmonary research.


As researchers continue to explore pressure-related mechanisms across disease areas, SoHo provides a platform for bringing continuous pressure monitoring into more adaptable study designs.


### A More Flexible Future for Proven Pressure Endpoints


The applications described here are not theoretical. They have already been demonstrated using DSI pressure-sensing technology in peer-reviewed research.


[SoHo](https://www.datasci.com/products/implantable-telemetry/soho-telemetry-system) builds on that scientific foundation with a system designed for greater flexibility, easier implementation, and refined study design. Its compact footprint can help laboratories incorporate telemetry into more spaces and configurations. Its streamlined setup can reduce operational complexity. Its support for group-housed animals can help researchers preserve social interaction while collecting continuous physiological data.


For researchers asking what they can study with pressure telemetry, decades of peer-reviewed work provide the answer.


For researchers asking how they can run those studies in a more flexible, refined, and accessible way, SoHo offers a new path forward.
