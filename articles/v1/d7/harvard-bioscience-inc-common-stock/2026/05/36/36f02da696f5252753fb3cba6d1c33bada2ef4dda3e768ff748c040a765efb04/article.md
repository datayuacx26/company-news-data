# BTX User Spotlight: Streamlining CRISPR Genome Editing with Electroporation

Genome engineering workflows continue evolving toward faster, simpler, and more reproducible approaches. While CRISPR-Cas9 has transformed genetic engineering across many model organisms, efficient editing can still be challenging in organisms that naturally favor non-homologous end joining (NHEJ) over homologous recombination.
This has been particularly true for fungal systems such as *Cryptococcus neoformans* , an important pathogenic yeast used extensively in fungal biology and infectious disease research. Traditional genome engineering approaches in *C. neoformans* have often relied on biolistic transformation methods and long homology arms, workflows that can be technically demanding and difficult to scale.


A recent study published in Genetics demonstrated how combining an organism-optimized CRISPR-Cas9 system with electroporation-based DNA delivery can simplify genome editing workflows in *C. neoformans* , enabling efficient editing with short homology arms and streamlined donor construction.


### Optimizing Cas9 for *Cryptococcus neoformans*


In the study, “Short homology-directed repair using optimized Cas9 in the pathogen *Cryptococcus neoformans* enables rapid gene deletion and tagging,” researchers designed a *C. neoformans* -optimized Cas9 construct, referred to as CnoCAS9.


The construct incorporated:


- *C. neoformans* -preferred codons
- A consensus *C. neoformans* intron
- TEF1 promoter and terminator sequences
- A nuclear localization signal


The resulting system enabled reliable genome editing in the KN99α strain of *C. neoformans* . The authors also demonstrated that stable genomic integration of CnoCAS9 further improved both transformation efficiency and homologous recombination efficiency without affecting virulence in animal models.


### Efficient Editing with Short Homology Arms


One of the study’s most important findings was the ability to perform genome editing using donor DNA constructs containing only 50 bp homology arms.


Traditional fungal homologous recombination workflows often require substantially longer homology regions, increasing cloning complexity and extending experimental timelines. By pairing precise CRISPR-induced double-strand breaks with optimized Cas9 expression, the researchers achieved efficient homologous recombination using donor constructs generated through synthetic oligonucleotides and PCR amplification.


This simplified approach enabled:


- Rapid gene deletion workflows
- Epitope tagging strategies
- Streamlined donor template generation
- Reduced cloning burden


The study also introduced a universal tagging module containing codon-optimized mNeonGreen and tandem CBP-2X FLAG tags for localization and protein purification studies.


### Electroporation as a Flexible DNA Delivery Method


DNA delivery remains one of the most critical components of successful genome editing workflows. In *C. neoformans* , biolistic transformation has historically been a common method for introducing genetic material, but the required instrumentation and workflow complexity can limit accessibility for some laboratories.


The study instead utilized electroporation-based transformation for delivery of CRISPR components and donor DNA. Electroporation works by applying controlled electrical pulses that transiently permeabilize cell membranes, allowing nucleic acids to enter cells efficiently.


In this work, researchers performed electroporation using a[BTX Gemini X2 Twin Wave Electroporator](https://btxonline.com/products/electroporation-systems/gemini-twin-wave/gemini-twin-wave-electroporators.html) with the following parameters:


- 500 V
- 400 Ω
- 250 µF


The use of electroporation helped support a streamlined transformation workflow while avoiding the complexity associated with particle bombardment systems.


### Supporting Broader Trends in Genome Engineering


Although the study focused specifically on *C. neoformans* , the findings reflect broader trends shaping modern genome engineering workflows.


Researchers increasingly seek approaches that:


- Reduce cloning complexity
- Improve editing efficiency
- Shorten experimental timelines
- Enable scalable workflows
- Expand genome engineering into non-model organisms


Optimized CRISPR systems paired with instrument-driven delivery technologies such as electroporation are helping move the field in this direction.


For laboratories working in fungal genetics, functional genomics, or antifungal drug discovery, these advances may help lower technical barriers while improving experimental flexibility and reproducibility.


### A More Accessible Path to Fungal Genome Engineering


The study highlights how organism-specific optimization and simplified donor design can substantially improve genome engineering workflows in organisms traditionally viewed as genetically difficult to manipulate.
By combining a *C. neoformans* -optimized Cas9 system with electroporation-based delivery and short-homology donor constructs, the researchers established a more streamlined approach for rapid gene deletion and tagging in *C. neoformans.*


As CRISPR workflows continue expanding into new organisms and applications, flexible delivery platforms like electroporation are likely to remain an important part of enabling efficient and reproducible genome engineering.
