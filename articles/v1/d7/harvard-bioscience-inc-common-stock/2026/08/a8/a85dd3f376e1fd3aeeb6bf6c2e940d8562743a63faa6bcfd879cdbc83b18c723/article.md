# Isolated Perfused Lungs: Bridging the Gap Between In Vitro and In Vivo Research

Respiratory researchers often face a difficult tradeoff when selecting an experimental model.


Cell cultures and lung tissue slices offer valuable control and cellular-level insight, but they cannot fully reproduce the interactions among ventilation, perfusion, airway mechanics, vascular function, and the alveolar barrier. Whole-animal models preserve this complexity, but systemic circulation, metabolism, immune responses, and effects on other organs can make it harder to isolate what is happening specifically within the lung.


The isolated perfused lung, or IPL, model helps bridge this gap.


By maintaining an intact lung outside the body under controlled temperature, ventilation and perfusion, researchers can study lung-specific physiology, pharmacokinetics, toxicology, and injury responses without many of the systemic variables present in an in vivo study.


### What Is an Isolated Perfused Lung Model?


In an IPL preparation, the lung is removed from the body while preserving its internal architecture and functional structures, including the airways, alveoli, pulmonary vasculature, endothelium, epithelium, and resident immune cells.


The organ is then ventilated and perfused with a physiological solution under carefully controlled conditions. Depending on the study design, researchers can regulate variables such as:


- Ventilation mode and respiratory rate
- Tidal volume and airway pressure
- Perfusion pressure or flow
- Perfusate composition
- Temperature
- Oxygen and carbon dioxide concentrations
- Delivery of drugs, aerosols, nanoparticles, or other test substances


Researchers may continuously measure pulmonary arterial pressure, airway resistance, lung compliance, perfusion flow, gas exchange, pO2, pH, pCO2 and changes in lung weight associated with edema.


This combination of preserved organ architecture and precise experimental control distinguishes IPL from simpler in vitro models. The lung continues to function as an integrated respiratory organ, while the absence of systemic circulation allows pulmonary responses to be observed more directly.


### IPL and EVLP: Related Models with Different Applications


Isolated perfused lung and ex vivo lung perfusion, or EVLP, are closely related approaches, although the terms are often used in different research contexts.


IPL commonly refers to isolated lungs from mice, rats, or other laboratory animals used in pulmonary physiology, pharmacology, inhalation toxicology, and drug-delivery research.


EVLP is frequently associated with lung preservation and transplantation. In these studies, lungs are ventilated and perfused outside the body so researchers can assess organ function, study ischemia-reperfusion injury, compare preservation strategies, or evaluate treatments intended to restore injured lungs.


Both approaches share the same core advantage: they allow researchers to study an intact, functioning lung under controlled conditions outside the systemic environment.


### Studying Pulmonary Drug Absorption and Pharmacokinetics


For inhaled therapies, the amount of drug introduced into an exposure system does not necessarily reflect how much reaches the lung, remains in pulmonary tissue, crosses the alveolar barrier, or enters circulation.


IPL models allow researchers to investigate processes such as:


- Pulmonary absorption
- Tissue retention
- Clearance
- Metabolism
- Transfer across the alveolar-capillary barrier
- Appearance of a drug or metabolite in the perfusate


A test substance can be delivered directly to the ventilated lung as an aerosol or liquid. Researchers can then collect perfusate and tissue samples over time to characterize how the substance moves through the organ.


Fraunhofer ITEM uses an isolated perfused rat lung model to study the uptake and transfer of inhaled substances. During these experiments, investigators can monitor physiological parameters such as tidal volume, dynamic airway resistance, dynamic compliance, and pulmonary arterial pressure while measuring the concentration of a test article appearing in the perfusate.


This type of whole-organ data can provide greater physiological relevance than simplified in vitro absorption models while avoiding the additional distribution and metabolism that occur elsewhere in the body.


For inhaled drug development, IPL can therefore contribute valuable information for formulation comparison, candidate selection, and pulmonary pharmacokinetic modeling.


### Investigating Pulmonary Physiology, Barrier Function, and Edema


Because IPL preserves the relationships among pulmonary vessels, airways, alveoli, and resident cells, it is well suited to mechanistic studies of lung physiology and injury.


Researchers can use these models to study:


- Hypoxic pulmonary vasoconstriction
- Pulmonary vascular resistance
- Airway resistance
- Lung compliance
- Surfactant function
- Endothelial and epithelial barrier integrity
- Inflammatory mediator release
- Pulmonary edema


One study used isolated perfused mouse lungs to investigate the role of TRPV4 channels in alveolar barrier function and protection from pulmonary edema.


The model allowed the researchers to connect a specific molecular mechanism with whole-organ outcomes, including vascular permeability, barrier integrity, and edema formation. This illustrates an important strength of IPL: researchers can move beyond observing a cellular response and evaluate how that response affects the function of the intact lung.


These models may also be used to assess acute responses to aerosols, nanoparticles, chemicals, and environmental pollutants. Functional changes can be evaluated alongside biochemical and histological endpoints, including:


- Changes in airway resistance or compliance
- Increased pulmonary vascular pressure
- Lung weight gain
- Increased vascular permeability
- Inflammatory biomarkers
- Epithelial or endothelial injury


This enables researchers to connect a controlled pulmonary exposure with immediate changes in respiratory mechanics, vascular function, and tissue integrity.


### Supporting Lung Preservation and Transplantation Research


EVLP provides a controlled platform for studying donor-lung preservation, ischemia-reperfusion injury, graft assessment, and organ-reconditioning strategies.


Researchers can compare preservation solutions, perfusates, temperatures, ventilation approaches, and therapeutic interventions while continuously monitoring lung performance.


In a 2026 study, investigators used the[HUGO SACHS ELEKTRONIK IPL-2 system](https://www.hugo-sachs.de/isolated-organ/isolated-lung-perfusion-systems/ipl-2-isolated-perfused-lung-systems-for-rat-and-guinea-pig.html) to compare lung preservation solutions following 18 hours of cold ischemia in rats.


During seven hours of EVLP, the researchers assessed:


- Pulmonary edema
- Lung compliance
- Pulmonary vascular resistance
- Inflammatory responses
- Endothelial injury


The experimental preservation solution was associated with reduced edema, improved compliance, lower pulmonary vascular resistance, and reduced markers of inflammation compared with one of the clinically used comparator solutions.


This study demonstrates how EVLP can support more than simple organ maintenance. It can provide a controlled environment for comparing preservation strategies and evaluating interventions intended to improve lung function before transplantation.


### Supporting the 3Rs in Pulmonary Research


Isolated organ models also align with the principles of Replacement, Reduction, and Refinement.


Although IPL still requires an animal-derived organ, one preparation can generate multiple types of physiological, biochemical, and tissue data under tightly controlled conditions. Researchers may be able to use IPL to screen compounds, refine doses, investigate mechanisms, or compare experimental conditions before proceeding to more complex in vivo studies.


IPL models may help researchers:


- Reduce unnecessary whole-animal studies
- Refine subsequent in vivo study designs
- Identify unsuitable compounds or conditions earlier
- Collect multiple complementary endpoints from one organ
- Separate direct pulmonary effects from systemic responses


IPL should not be viewed as a universal replacement for either in vitro or in vivo research. Instead, it can serve as a strategically positioned model within a tiered research program.

### An Intact-Organ Perspective on Pulmonary Research


No single experimental model can answer every question in respiratory science.


Cell cultures provide accessibility and cellular specificity. Lung slices preserve localized tissue interactions. Whole-animal studies capture systemic biology.


Between them, the isolated perfused lung offers something distinct: a functioning, intact pulmonary organ studied under conditions that researchers can directly control.


For investigations involving pulmonary drug absorption, respiratory mechanics, vascular function, toxicology, edema, inflammation, lung preservation, and transplantation, IPL and EVLP models can provide the whole-organ perspective needed to translate simplified experimental findings into more physiologically relevant insight.
