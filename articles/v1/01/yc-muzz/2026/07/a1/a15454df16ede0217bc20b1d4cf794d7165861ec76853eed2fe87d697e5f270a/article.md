Throughout the tournament, Spain became a team that a lot of Muslims felt connected to. This connection was influenced by Spain’s broader public position on Palestine, including its decision in 2024 to **[formally recognise the State of Palestine alongside Ireland and Norway](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=p3Rj3676Grw)** . The move was described by Spanish Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez as part of a push towards supporting a peaceful resolution to the conflict.


Moments of solidarity also resonated with[Spanish footballer Lamine Yamal](https://www.bbc.co.uk/sport/football/articles/c0m7zx38v43o) drawing global attention after[waving a Palestinian flag during Barcelona’s La Liga title celebrations](https://www.aljazeera.com/sports/2026/5/12/lamine-yamals-palestine-flag-wave-hailed-by-fans-activists-and-athletes?utm_source=chatgpt.com) , an act that was praised by many Palestinian supporters, football fans and activists online.


Yamal’s background also reflected the multicultural identity of modern Spain. Born in Catalonia to a Moroccan father and Equatorial Guinean mother, the 19 year old has previously spoken about his[Muslim identity and experiences with Islamophobia in football.](https://www.theguardian.com/football/2026/apr/01/spain-fans-islamophobic-and-xenophobic-chants-egypt-police-investigate)


Football brings people together in this unique sense, creating a space where **people can find connection in unexpected places** .
