# Muslims Don’t Date, We Marry: What That Really Means

“Muslims don’t date, we marry.” It’s a phrase you’ve probably heard a dozen times when referencing Muslims finding a spouse, but it’s often misunderstood. The reality isn’t that Muslims skip getting to know someone; rather, many choose to do so with a clear intention from the start: finding a compatible life partner through marriage. For Muslims in the UAE and around the world, this approach helps people build meaningful connections while keeping marriage as the shared goal.


That intention changes everything. Instead of casual, undefined dating, the focus is on finding someone you can build a future with. You’re not just “seeing where it goes”; you’re getting to know someone to understand whether you’re aligned in your values, goals, and expectations. This is often what people mean by “halal dating”: getting to know someone in a way that is intentional, respectful, and with a clear purpose in mind.

Conversations usually happen in comfortable, public settings, with clear boundaries. Family may be involved early, never to pressure, but to signal seriousness. It removes ambiguity and makes intentions clear from the start. Instead of vague questions like “what are we?”, people ask what actually matters: where do you see yourself in five years, how many kids do you want, what does modesty mean to you? These aren’t intense, but they’re important conversations when considering someone as a potential life partner.


This approach also cuts out something many people are tired of: wasted time. There’s no need for months of confusion or emotional limbo. If you’re aligned, you move forward. If you’re not, you move on.


So when people say “Muslims don’t date, we marry,” what they really mean is this: they get to know someone with intention, honesty, and clarity because the goal is to find a meaningful, lasting relationship.
