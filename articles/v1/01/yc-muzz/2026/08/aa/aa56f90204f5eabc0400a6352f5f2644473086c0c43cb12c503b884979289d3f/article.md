### **Does Muzz verify every profile?**


Yes. Selfie verification is mandatory on Muzz, not an optional badge and not a paid feature.


Every member submits a selfie, which is compared against their profile photo to confirm the profile belongs to a real person. That selfie is never shared with anyone – not with other members, not with third parties – and is used only for security.


Muzz also reviews profiles manually. Photos that don’t meet the guidelines are rejected, and every member has to show their face in their verification selfie. Manual review is slower than pure automation, and that’s deliberate.


Muzz also monitors behaviour, not just profiles. Accounts that like or message at volume – fast, indiscriminate, the pattern of someone working through a list rather than looking for a spouse – get flagged automatically and reviewed by a person.


Muzz founder Shahzad Younas has written publicly about how the company approaches this, and he’s blunt about it: it’s a permanent cat-and-mouse game, and he won’t claim it’s solved. *(ref:*[https://x.com/ShahzadYounas_/status/1889686271515058626?s=20](https://x.com/ShahzadYounas_/status/1889686271515058626?s=20) *)*


Some context from that thread worth repeating: Scammers target relationship apps specifically, because people there are emotionally open and are often too embarrassed to report being defrauded. These aren’t opportunists – Muzz has encountered organised operations running scams as full-time work from dedicated offices.


What Muzz uses against them: device identification, selfie and facial-similarity recognition, and further methods the company deliberately doesn’t publish. A significant focus is stopping blocked scammers from simply signing up again – the single measure that most affects whether a removal actually sticks.


Muzz tracks how long a suspected scammer exists on the platform before being detected and removed, and that window has narrowed meaningfully as these measures have come in. The goal is removal before the account can match or message anyone at all.


Shahzad has also noted the uncomfortable part: AI now makes some deception nearly impossible to spot by eye, which means increasingly using AI to detect AI.


Honorable mention: **Only women review verification selfies.** Muzz’s community team is all women, by design. For sisters who wear hijab or niqab, or who simply don’t want their face reviewed by a man, verification shouldn’t mean a trade-off — so it isn’t one. The selfie you submit is seen by a woman on the Muzz team, and by no one else. It never appears on your profile, is never shown to other members, and is never shared outside Muzz.


### **Are there fake profiles and catfish on Muzz?**


Any platform with millions of members attracts people acting in bad faith. Pretending otherwise would be the least trustworthy thing an app could do.


What Muzz does about it: mandatory selfie verification at signup, manual profile review, and an investigation process for impersonation reports. If you think someone is impersonating another person, report them and Muzz will investigate.


### **How to spot a fake profile**


- **One photo, or several that look like the same shot.** Real profiles have variety – different settings, different lighting, different days.
- **A bio with nothing specific in it.** Generic virtues and no details about their actual life is a signal.
- **They refuse a video call.** Muzz has free in-app video calling. Someone serious about marriage will use it. Someone who won’t, after weeks of chatting, is telling you something.
- **They want to move off the app immediately.** Wanting your snapchat or WhatsApp within the first few messages usually means they want to be somewhere with no verification, no moderation and no reporting.
- **The story escalates fast.** Intense declarations early, then a crisis, then a request. That’s the shape of a romance scam.
- **Money enters the conversation at all.** An emergency, an investment opportunity, a visa problem, a gift card. No exceptions, no matter how good the reason sounds.


### **How do you block or report someone on Muzz?**


Blocking is free, takes seconds, and works two ways.


**From their profile:** open the profile, scroll to the bottom, select Block, choose a reason, submit.
**From your chat:** open the chat, tap the three dots in the top right, and under Safety Options select Block, then confirm.


Two things worth knowing, both documented publicly by Muzz:


- **They are not notified.** The person you block is never told.
- **It works both ways.** They can’t see or message you, and you won’t see them either.


Reporting is separate from blocking, and you should do both when someone’s behaviour breaks the rules rather than just being a bad match. Reports go to the moderation team for review, and Muzz operates a ban appeals process so decisions can be contested.

### **Why does Muzz limit you to five conversations?**


Muzz caps active conversations at five. Once you have five going, browsing pauses until you unmatch someone. You can have unlimited matches – the limit is on conversations, not connections.


This has been documented in the Muzz help center since the feature launched, and the reason is straightforward: five real conversations that go somewhere are worth more than fifty that don’t.


**The limit applies to everyone, including Gold members.** It isn’t a paywall and there is no version of Muzz you can buy that removes it.


Muzz also prompts you when a conversation goes quiet from either side, to check whether you want to keep talking.


### **What Muzz is doing about ghosting**


Relationship apps have a problem, and everyone using one already knows what it is.


You send a message. Nothing. You send another. Seen, two weeks ago. Eventually you stop asking. ** Ghosting isn’t a safety issue in the way harassment or scams are. Nobody is in danger. But on an app built for marriage, being left hanging with no answer is its own kind of disrespect, and it is the single most common complaint we hear.


So Muzz built accountability into the mechanic. If you have unanswered conversations, browsing stops until you deal with them – reply, or unmatch. Both are valid. Walking away is allowed. Disappearing while someone waits is not.


Ending a conversation respectfully is a feature, not a failure. Not every match is the one, and saying so is kinder than silence.

### **How to stay safe on any Muslim marriage app**


This applies whether you use Muzz or not.


1. **Video call before you meet.** The single most effective anti-catfish step there is, and it’s free on Muzz.
2. **Stay on the app early.** Verification, moderation and reporting only work while you’re on the platform.
3. **Never send money.** Not for an emergency, not for a flight, not for an investment. There is no version of this that ends well.
4. **Meet in public the first time (with a mahram).** A café, a restaurant, somewhere with people. Bring someone if you want to.
5. **Tell someone where you’re going.** A friend, a sibling, a parent — the person’s name, the place, the time.
6. **Guard your details.** Home address, workplace, financial information — none of it belongs in an early conversation.
7. **Trust the feeling.** If something is off, you don’t owe anyone an explanation. Unmatch, block, report.

Muzz requires mandatory selfie verification for every member, manually reviews profiles, and offers free blocking and reporting to all users. Photo privacy is available on the free tier. **Does Muzz verify its users?**
Yes. Every member must complete selfie verification, which compares a submitted selfie against the profile photo to confirm a real person is behind the account. The selfie is never shared with anyone and is used only for security. Verification is not a paid feature.


**Are there fake profiles on Muzz?**

**How do I block someone on Muzz?**
From their profile: scroll to the bottom, select Block, choose a reason, submit. From a chat: tap the three dots, then Block under Safety Options. They are not notified, and the block works both ways — they can’t see or message you, and you won’t see them either.

Reports go to the moderation team for review, which can result in a ban. Muzz operates an appeals process so banned members can contest a decision.

Muzz caps active conversations at five so members focus on real conversations rather than collecting matches. Reply or unmatch to free a slot. Matches are unlimited – only conversations are capped, and the limit applies to Gold members too.


**Does Muzz Gold remove the five-conversation limit?**
No. The limit applies to every member regardless of subscription. Gold adds filters, likes, Profile Boosts, Rematch and a VIP badge — it does not change conversation limits or unlock safety features.


**Is Muzz free?**
Yes. Matching and chatting are free and Muzz has committed to keeping them free. Gold is optional and adds visibility and convenience features.
