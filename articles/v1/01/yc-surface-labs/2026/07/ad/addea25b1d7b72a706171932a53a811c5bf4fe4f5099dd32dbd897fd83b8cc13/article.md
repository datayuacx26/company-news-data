# Launching Ad Analytics

# 70 to 80% faster.


That's how much sooner you'll know whether an ad is actually working, once you grade it on lead quality instead of ad platform metrics.


Here's why the gap is that wide.


## The ad platforms grade their own homework


Meta wants to show you conversions. LinkedIn wants to show you leads. Google wants to show you clicks. Every one of those numbers is a report card the platform wrote about itself.


So the dashboard looks great even when the leads never close. A Meta campaign can be your top performer in Ads Manager and your top source of pipeline that never books a demo. You usually don't find out until month three, when someone in RevOps finally stitches spend against pipeline in a spreadsheet.


## What Surface sees that the platforms can't


Surface is where the leads actually land, get enriched, get scored, and get routed. That position gives us signals Meta and LinkedIn never touch:


- Which leads matched your ICP
- Which ones engaged after signup
- Which ones a rep called qualified
- Which ones booked a meeting
- Which ones closed
- Which ones churned inside 30 days


Every one of those ties back to the ad it came from. Not ad to click. Ad to an actual dollar of revenue.


## Why you learn faster


Two reasons.


First, you don't have to wait on pipeline data. If an ad is bringing in leads that are visibly off-ICP or low intent, you know that on day two, not after the whole sales cycle plays out.


Second, you get a peer benchmark. Surface sees ad performance across hundreds of B2B companies, so when your CAC or your lead quality sits well outside the norm for companies like yours, we can tell you. Anonymized and aggregated. That's a comparison against reality, not just your own trend line drifting.


Put those together and "is this ad working" goes from roughly 60 days to roughly 10.


## The story your CFO has been asking for


Most marketing teams can't tell this story:


> This $80K campaign brought in 340 leads. 88 were ICP fit, 22 booked demos, 6 became opportunities, and 2 have closed for $180K in ARR. Payback in 30 days.


That used to take a dedicated RevOps hire and a warehouse pipeline. In Surface it's the default report.


## The real lever is killing the losers


Everyone obsesses over finding the winning campaign. The bigger win is cutting the losing one early. Every quarter a bad campaign stays live is money you don't get back.


Surface flags it in days instead of months. You reallocate, your pipeline math improves, and you spend less to produce more.
