## **How to Compare WEX Fleet Card Competitors**


Evaluating and choosing the best WEX alternatives requires looking beyond headline rebates. A card built for growing fleets should balance fuel savings, spend controls, reporting capabilities, fraud prevention, and network acceptance based on how your fleet operates.


### **Compare Actual Fuel Savings, Not Just Advertised Rebates**


Advertised fuel discounts and per-gallon savings do not always reflect the value fleets ultimately realize. Fuel volume, network participation, and where drivers purchase fuel can all affect the value of a fleet card.


When comparing providers, estimate actual savings using the truck stops and fueling locations your drivers visit most often rather than relying on advertised discount figures alone.


### **Compare Controls, Fraud Protection, and Reporting**


Driver PINs and spending limits help fleet managers control purchases, while advanced reporting tools provide greater visibility into how drivers fuel across different routes.


Advanced providers also offer tools that help businesses[prevent fuel card fraud](https://www.atob.com/blog/how-to-prevent-fuel-card-fraud) and improve visibility into fleet spending.


Look for these advanced features:


- Telematics-based location validation
- Fuel level tracking
- Suspicious activity monitoring
- Automated[IFTA fuel tax reporting](https://www.atob.com/blog/ifta-fuel-tax-reporting)
- Real-time transaction visibility


Strong reporting tools can help businesses monitor fuel spending, identify unusual activity, and support day-to-day fleet management.


### **Evaluate Total Fees and Network Coverage Together**


When comparing fleet card providers, consider the full operating cost rather than fuel savings alone. Compare annual fees, transaction fees, late fees, out-of-network fees, and overall operating costs rather than focusing only on fuel discounts.


Open-loop cards function as[fuel cards that can be used anywhere.](https://www.atob.com/blog/fuel-cards-that-can-be-used-anywhere) These allow businesses to make vehicle-related purchases anywhere Mastercard or Visa is accepted, providing broader acceptance across fuel stations and truck stops. Closed-network cards may offer tighter purchase controls, but can limit where drivers purchase fuel.


Before selecting a provider, compare your fleet's typical routes, fueling locations, and operational needs to determine the right fit.
