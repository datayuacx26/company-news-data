# 3 Best Fleet Cards with Maintenance Options Built-In

Most fleets manage fuel and maintenance with separate payment methods, creating reconciliation gaps that hide the true cost of operating each vehicle. Fleet cards with built-in maintenance options help consolidate fuel, repairs, parts, and other vehicle-related expenses into a single payment solution with unified controls and reporting.


This guide explains what to look for in a fleet card with maintenance capabilities and compares three programs designed to simplify fleet spending. Beyond fuel purchases, today's fleet cards can also help businesses manage maintenance, parts, reporting, and other vehicle-related expenses from a single platform.


## **What to Look for in a Fleet Card with Maintenance Options**


A maintenance-enabled fleet card should support repair purchases, parts purchases, fuel transactions, and approved vehicle expenses under one control framework. The right fuel card helps fleet operators reduce fuel costs while simplifying fleet expense management across every vehicle. Here is what to look for:


### **Built-in Visibility Across Fuel, Repairs, and Driver Expenses**


True cost-per-vehicle tracking requires seeing fuel purchases, repair invoices, parts transactions, and fuel data in a single view. Bringing this information together helps fleet operators compare fuel usage, repair frequency, maintenance costs, and the amount of fuel consumed by each vehicle.


Fleet cards that allow businesses to make vehicle-related purchases anywhere Mastercard is accepted support maintenance purchases at many repair shops, parts suppliers, tire dealers, and truck stop service centers. Unlike some branded fuel card providers, broader acceptance gives fleets greater flexibility when job sites, routes, or truck stop networks change.


Platforms like AtoB consolidate fuel, maintenance, parts, and driver spending into one dashboard, helping businesses lower fuel expenses while improving visibility into the fleet's operating costs.


### **Controls That Prevent Unauthorized Fuel and Maintenance Spend**


Without proper spend controls, a maintenance-enabled fleet card functions much like a standard business credit card or business charge card, making it more difficult to monitor and control employee spending. Fleet managers need granular controls that help protect margins while still giving drivers flexibility.


Key control types to evaluate:


- **Merchant category restrictions –** Limit purchases by fuel type, repairs, parts, and other approved vehicle expenses
- **Per-transaction caps –** Set dollar limits by expense type to flag unusual charges
- **Driver-level authorization –** Assign cards to specific drivers with individual rules
- **Vendor whitelisting –** Approve or block specific repair shops and parts suppliers


These capabilities help businesses purchase fuel, manage maintenance costs, and reduce unauthorized spending across the fleet.


### **Reporting That Supports Preventive Fleet Maintenance Management**


Fleet cards capture odometer readings and fuel data during each fuel transaction. Combined with **GPS** and telematics data, this information can support preventive maintenance scheduling, International Fuel Tax Agreement (IFTA) reporting, and better visibility into fuel volume across the fleet.


Declining fuel efficiency or higher fuel consumption than expected may indicate maintenance issues before they become costly repairs. When a vehicle suddenly uses more fuel per mile, it may indicate engine problems, tire wear, or other[fleet maintenance](https://www.atob.com/blog/what-is-fleet-maintenance) needs.


Reporting tools help businesses reduce fuel waste, lower fuel expenses, and improve long-term operating efficiency while providing better insight into monthly fuel trends.
