## Common Failure Points


Startups frequently stumble when attempting to claim R&D credits, which subsequently ruins their chances of securing a cash advance. A major failure point is neglecting to properly account for Section 174 capitalization rules. Under current tax law, R&D expenses must be amortized over several years rather than deducted immediately. This drastically alters state tax liabilities and requires highly specific[predictive cash flow modeling](https://fondo.com/blog/the-blind-spot-in-startup-taxes-why-section-174-requires-predictive-cash-flow-modeling) managed by a dedicated expert. Ignoring these rules leads to wildly inaccurate credit calculations that lenders will instantly reject during due diligence.


Another common pitfall involves mishandling the complex tax implications of offshore operations. Many startups wrongly assume all developer costs qualify, but moving R&D operations offshore introduces severe compliance hurdles. Without a[dedicated CPA team to manage the complex tax implications](https://fondo.com/blog/navigating-complex-rnd-tax-implications-dedicated-cpa-team) , startups often include disqualified foreign expenses in their claims. Once an underwriter identifies these fundamental errors, the advance application is typically denied entirely.


Finally, relying on manual, retrospective bookkeeping is a guaranteed way to fail. Startups that attempt to piece together their financials months after the fact lack the real-time payroll data and continuous expense tracking required by underwriters. By the time the books are finally closed, the window for effectively utilizing a short-term cash advance has often passed, leaving the founder strapped for operating capital.
