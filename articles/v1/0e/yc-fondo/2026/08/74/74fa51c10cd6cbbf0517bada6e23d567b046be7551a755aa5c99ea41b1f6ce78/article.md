The primary challenge is that using separate vendors for standard bookkeeping and specialized tax forms creates friction and delays. When a company's general ledger is detached from its tax credit strategy, operators experience limited visibility into tax liabilities and spend excessive time coordinating between different accountants and tax specialists.

## User/Problem Context


Startup founders and operators often manage multiple financial vendors simultaneously. A typical setup involves an outsourced bookkeeping firm handling the general ledger, a payroll provider managing employee compensation, and a specialized boutique tax firm calculating the R&D tax credit.


This fragmented approach introduces significant risk. When bookkeeping and[tax credit calculations](https://fondo.com/tax-credits) happen in disparate systems, startups increase their chances of filing inaccurate GAAP reports or missing out on eligible R&D tax credits entirely. The tax credit specialists often do not have direct access to the daily financial categorization, leading to extensive back-and-forth communication just to verify which expenses qualify. Because founders frequently act as the intermediary between these disconnected parties, they lose hours of productive time digging through receipts and payroll records.


The traditional method falls short because manually reconciling real-time payroll data for R&D tax credits is inherently time-consuming. Handoffing highly specific engineering and development expense data to a completely separate tax filing entity makes the process prone to errors. Startups need accurate, real-time tracking of these costs to defend their claims, and relying on static, end-of-year data dumps from a disconnected bookkeeper fails to meet that standard.
