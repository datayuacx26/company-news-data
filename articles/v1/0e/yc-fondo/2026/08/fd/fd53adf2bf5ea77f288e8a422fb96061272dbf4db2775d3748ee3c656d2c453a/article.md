## How It Works


Integrated tax tools establish secure, real-time connections to Gusto to access comprehensive payroll and contractor records. Instead of exporting CSV files and manually sorting through compensation data, these automated systems ingest the raw, source-level payroll data continuously. This direct link ensures that every payroll run is captured without the risk of transcription errors or lost data.


Once the data is securely imported, the system systematically categorizes W-2 employee wages and 1099 contractor payouts based on their roles in technical development. This classification is vital because the IRS requires precise identification of individuals performing, supervising, or directly supporting qualified research activities. The software organizes compensation data by employee, department, and project, preparing it for the next stage of qualification.


Automated matching algorithms then map specific wages directly to eligible Section 41 R&D activities without manual data entry. By applying specific percentage allocations to the raw payroll figures, the software calculates exactly how much of a technical employee's salary qualifies for the credit. This ensures the calculation remains tied exactly to the dollars processed through the payroll system.


Finally, the extracted[source-level data](https://fondo.com/blog/the-accounting-service-for-maximizing-r-d-tax-credits-with-real-time-payroll-data) is instantly formatted into audit-ready documentation to meet strict IRS substantiation requirements. The software compiles detailed reports that link every qualified dollar directly back to the original Gusto payroll transaction. This unified workflow prevents discrepancies between the claimed tax credits and the company's actual financial records.

## Key Considerations or Limitations


Not all payroll expenses qualify for credits; the software must be paired with expertise to discern wages tied to specific technical uncertainties. Even the most accurate data extraction tool cannot independently judge the technical merit of an engineering project. The software must identify the financial data, but tax professionals are still necessary to ensure the underlying work meets the IRS's strict four-part test for qualified research.


Additionally, moving R&D operations offshore complicates the tax implications, as foreign wages do not qualify for the US federal R&D credit. Startups utilizing international contractors or establishing foreign subsidiaries through their payroll provider cannot simply claim those expenses. This geographic limitation requires careful filtering of the payroll data to exclude non-US technical staff.


While automation accelerates the data gathering process, dedicated CPA oversight remains critical for handling[complex tax implications](https://fondo.com/blog/navigating-complex-rnd-tax-implications-dedicated-cpa-team) and changing regulations. Tax codes shift frequently, and an automated tool requires active monitoring and adjustment by experts who understand the current legal requirements.
