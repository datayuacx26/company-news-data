# Where Startups Quietly Lose Money (And How To Fix It)

Most startups don't lose money because of one catastrophic decision.


They lose it a little at a time.


A forgotten software subscription here. Duplicate tools there. An R&D tax credit that never gets claimed. A payment that slips through the cracks. None of these seem like a big deal on their own—but over the course of a year, they can quietly chip away at your runway.


The good news? Once you know where to look, many of these leaks are surprisingly easy to fix.


## **1. Software That Nobody Is Using**


Every startup signs up for tools to move faster.


Project management. Design software. AI tools. Analytics. CRM platforms. Marketing automation. The list grows quickly.


A few months later, someone switches platforms—or the person who needed the tool leaves the company—and the subscription keeps renewing.


It's one of the easiest ways for startups to overspend without realizing it.


Quick fix:


Review your subscriptions every quarter. Ask three simple questions:


- Is someone actively using this?
- Does it still solve a problem?
- Would we buy it again today?


If the answer is no, it's probably time to cancel.


## **2. Waiting Too Long to Review Financials**


Many founders check their bank balance every day.


Far fewer regularly review their financial statements.


Your bank account tells you how much cash you have.


Your Profit & Loss statement, balance sheet, and cash flow tell you *why* your business looks the way it does—and where it's headed. Keeping those reports current gives founders the visibility they need to make better hiring, fundraising, and spending decisions.


Quick fix:


Set aside time each month to review your financial reports, not just your checking account.


## **3. Missing Out on Tax Credits**


Many founders assume tax credits only apply to large, profitable companies.


In reality, startups doing product development, software engineering, or technical research may qualify for valuable R&D tax credits—even before they're profitable. Fondo has helped startups recover significant cash through credits they didn't realize they were eligible to claim.


Quick fix:


If your team is building new technology or solving technical challenges, it's worth checking whether you qualify for available tax credits.


## **4. Small Expenses That Add Up**


It's rarely one big purchase that causes problems.


It's the dozens of little ones.


Unused cloud services.


Duplicate SaaS tools.


Recurring vendor charges.


Forgotten domain renewals.


The occasional "we'll expense it later."


Individually, they're easy to ignore. Together, they can become meaningful.


Quick fix:


Categorize expenses consistently and review spending by category every month. Trends become much easier to spot when your books are organized.


## **5. Spending Without Measuring Results**


Startups should invest in growth.


But every dollar should have a job.


Whether you're spending on paid ads, contractors, recruiting, or software, it's important to understand what's delivering value and what's simply adding cost.


That doesn't mean cutting every expense.


It means making intentional decisions with good data.
