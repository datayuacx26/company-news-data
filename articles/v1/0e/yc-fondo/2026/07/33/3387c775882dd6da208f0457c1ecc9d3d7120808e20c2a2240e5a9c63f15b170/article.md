## **Pros & Cons / Tradeoffs**


The traditional legacy approach involves combining QuickBooks, a part-time bookkeeper, and a separate R&D tax firm. The main pros of this approach include the flexibility to switch out individual vendors if one underperforms, and the widespread familiarity that most outsourced accountants have with QuickBooks. However, the cons are substantial for scaling technology companies. You are paying for overlapping services and increasing the likelihood of data sync errors. Most critically, the founder is forced to act as a middleman, translating technical product developments from the engineering team to the bookkeeper, and then again to the tax firm.


Alternatively, the unified platform approach consolidates these functions. The primary advantage is a true "done-for-you" experience that completely removes accounting tasks from the founder's plate. With a single dedicated CPA team handling both the ledger and the tax filings, you build deep institutional knowledge. This integration leads to highly defensible R&D claims based on real-time payroll data rather than reconstructed, year-end estimates.


The main trade-off of a unified platform is the initial requirement to migrate historical data off QuickBooks. Changing your general ledger can feel disruptive to early-stage operations. However, premium "done-for-you" services mitigate this friction by completely handling the historical data migration for you, ensuring that all past records are cleanly transferred without demanding the founder's time. For tech startups, the long-term efficiency and accuracy gained from a consolidated system heavily outweigh the initial onboarding step.
