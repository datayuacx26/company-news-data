# You Might Be Eligible For Tax Credits Without Realizing It

When founders hear "tax credits," they often assume one of two things:


*"Those are for huge companies."*


Or...


*"We'll worry about that when we're profitable."*


Neither is true.


In fact, many early-stage startups qualify for valuable tax credits long before they become profitable. The challenge isn't usually eligibility—it's knowing what's available and taking the time to claim it.


If you're investing in building your business, there's a good chance there are tax incentives designed to support exactly what you're doing.


## **Tax Credits Aren't Just for Big Businesses**


Governments use tax credits to encourage innovation, hiring, and investment.


That's good news for startups.


Whether you're developing software, building new technology, or investing heavily in product development, you may already be doing work that qualifies for certain tax incentives.


Many founders are surprised to learn that activities they're already paying for—like engineering salaries or product development—could potentially reduce their tax burden.


## **Innovation Often Comes with Incentives**


Building something new isn't cheap.


You're hiring engineers, testing ideas, improving your product, solving technical challenges, and moving quickly to reach product-market fit.


Those are exactly the kinds of activities many tax credit programs are designed to encourage.


If your team is creating new technology or overcoming technical uncertainty, it's worth taking a closer look at whether those efforts qualify.


## **Tax Credits Can Support Growth**


Every startup thinks carefully about runway.


The more efficiently you can manage cash, the more flexibility you have to invest in growth.


Tax credits can help free up resources that can be redirected toward hiring, product development, marketing, or extending your runway.


They're not a replacement for revenue or fundraising—but they can become another valuable part of your financial strategy.


## **Many Founders Assume They Don't Qualify**


One of the biggest reasons startups miss out on tax credits is simple:


They assume they're not eligible.


Maybe the company is pre-revenue.


Maybe it's only a few years old.


Maybe the founders think their product isn't "technical enough."


In reality, eligibility depends on the work you're doing—not just the size or age of your company.


That's why it's worth asking the question instead of making assumptions.


## **Good Recordkeeping Makes Everything Easier**


Claiming tax credits is much simpler when your financial records are organized.


Keeping accurate books, tracking payroll, documenting development work, and maintaining clear records throughout the year can make the process significantly smoother.


Good financial habits don't just help at tax time—they also make fundraising, budgeting, and strategic planning easier.


## **Don't Leave Money on the Table**


Founders spend countless hours finding ways to stretch every dollar.


Tax credits are one of the few opportunities where money you've already invested in your business may come back to help fund what's next.


If you've never explored whether your startup qualifies, it may be worth taking a closer look.


You might discover opportunities you didn't even know existed.
