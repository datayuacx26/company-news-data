# Toxic wildfire pollution threatens communities as millions urged to stay indoors

## Wildfire Smoke Crisis Spreads Across Five US States as Officials Warn of Serious Health Risks


A major wildfire smoke emergency is affecting millions of residents across the western United States, with dangerous levels of air pollution spreading across several states. Health officials and weather authorities are warning people to limit outdoor exposure as smoke from large-scale wildfires creates unhealthy air conditions in parts of Idaho, Montana, Nevada, Oregon, and Washington.


The National Weather Service (NWS) has issued air quality alerts across wide areas of the Pacific Northwest as multiple wildfires continue to burn. Officials are urging residents to protect themselves, especially vulnerable groups such as children, older adults, pregnant women, and people living with heart or lung conditions.


The ongoing wildfire outbreak has created thick smoke plumes carrying harmful pollutants across state borders. In several locations, air quality levels have reached unhealthy and very unhealthy categories, increasing concerns about breathing difficulties and serious health complications.


## Wildfire smoke contains dangerous microscopic particles


One of the biggest health concerns linked to wildfire smoke is fine particulate matter known as PM2.5. These tiny particles are so small that they can travel deep into the lungs and may enter the bloodstream.


According to environmental health experts, exposure to PM2.5 pollution can worsen existing health problems, including asthma, chronic lung conditions, and cardiovascular diseases. Long-term or heavy exposure has also been associated with increased risks of heart attacks and premature death among people with existing health vulnerabilities.


The US Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) has warned that wildfire smoke exposure can contribute to several health issues, including reduced lung function, irregular heartbeat, aggravated respiratory conditions, and increased cardiovascular stress.


Officials say people should avoid unnecessary outdoor activities when smoke levels become dangerous. Keeping windows and doors closed, using air filtration systems when available, and monitoring local air quality reports are among the recommended steps.


## Thousands evacuated as fires continue spreading


The wildfire situation has forced emergency evacuations in parts of Oregon. Thousands of residents in central and eastern Oregon were ordered to leave their homes after lightning storms triggered new fires across the region.


More than one million acres have been affected by wildfires in Oregon, with emergency crews working to control several major fire zones. Firefighters are facing difficult conditions due to dry vegetation, high temperatures, and changing weather patterns.


Oregon officials have warned that the wildfire season could become increasingly challenging. Emergency teams are preparing for continued fire activity as drought conditions and extreme weather create favorable conditions for additional outbreaks.


Lieutenant Colonel Nathan Edgecomb of the Oregon National Guard said officials expect a difficult fire season and are preparing resources to respond to worsening conditions.


## Air quality reaches dangerous levels


The Air Quality Index (AQI) is used to measure pollution levels and ranges from 0 to 500. Lower numbers indicate cleaner air, while higher numbers represent increasing health risks.


An AQI level between 0 and 50 is considered good. Levels between 51 and 100 are generally acceptable but may affect sensitive individuals. Scores above 150 indicate unhealthy conditions where outdoor activities should be limited.


In some areas near the Oregon and Idaho border, air quality monitoring systems recorded pollution levels above 200, placing conditions in the very unhealthy category.


Officials warn that when AQI levels enter the hazardous range, everyone should avoid outdoor physical activity and take immediate precautions to reduce exposure.


Communities in cities such as Boise and Twin Falls in Idaho, along with Billings, Missoula, and Great Falls in Montana, have experienced worsening smoke conditions as wildfire pollution moves through the region.


## Weather conditions increase wildfire danger


The wildfire threat is not only caused by existing fires. Weather conditions are also increasing the risk of new outbreaks.


Red flag warnings have been issued in parts of Idaho because forecasters expect thunderstorms, dry conditions, low humidity, and strong winds. Lightning strikes during these conditions can quickly ignite new fires in drought-affected areas.


A red flag warning means weather conditions are highly favorable for wildfire development and rapid spread. Authorities advise residents to avoid activities that could accidentally start fires during these periods.


Emergency officials are closely monitoring weather patterns because even small fires can become difficult to control when combined with strong winds and dry landscapes.


## Who is most at risk from wildfire smoke?


While wildfire smoke can affect everyone, some groups face higher health risks. Experts identify the following groups as particularly vulnerable:


- Children and teenagers
- Older adults
- Pregnant women
- People with asthma or breathing difficulties
- Individuals with heart disease
- People with diabetes or other chronic health conditions
- Outdoor workers and athletes


Health experts recommend that vulnerable individuals remain indoors whenever smoke levels are high. People who must spend time outside should reduce physical activity and consider using properly fitted protective masks designed to filter fine particles.


## Debate over wildfire assistance funding


The wildfire crisis has also created debate over emergency funding and federal assistance.


Oregon lawmakers have raised concerns that federal support was not immediately approved for some major fires, including the Rowe Creek Complex and East Evans Creek Fire. Officials argued that additional resources are needed before fires become large-scale disasters.


Members of Oregon’s congressional delegation said federal assistance programs should help communities prevent fires from becoming catastrophic rather than waiting until destruction reaches extreme levels.


The Federal Emergency Management Agency (FEMA) responded that wildfire assistance requests are reviewed based on established legal requirements and available information provided by states.


The agency stated that Fire Management Assistance Grant decisions follow criteria established by Congress and that officials continue working with Oregon authorities to monitor changing wildfire conditions.


## Residents urged to stay informed


As wildfire activity continues across the western United States, authorities are encouraging residents to stay updated through official weather alerts and local emergency services.


Air quality conditions can change quickly as winds move smoke across large distances. Even communities far from active flames may experience unhealthy pollution levels.


Experts say preparation is essential during wildfire season. Residents should monitor air quality reports, prepare emergency supplies, and follow evacuation instructions if authorities issue warnings.


The current wildfire smoke emergency highlights the growing challenges communities face as extreme weather events become more frequent and intense. Protecting public health requires quick action, accurate information, and cooperation between residents, emergency responders, and government agencies.
