# PT Organics Fruit Pouch Recall: What Parents Need to Know About Possible Plastic Contamination

## PT Organics Recalls Over 380,000 Fruit Pouches Due to Packaging Concern


Parents and caregivers are being advised to check their pantry shelves after PT Organics announced a nationwide recall of more than 380,000 Pumpkin Tree Peter Rabbit Organics Banana & Strawberry fruit pouches. The recall was issued after the company discovered a potential packaging problem that could allow small strands of soft plastic material to enter the fruit purée.


The affected products were distributed across the United States and sold through major retailers, including Kroger, Meijer, and Target stores. Consumers who have purchased these fruit pouches are encouraged to review the product information carefully and avoid using any items that match the recalled lot details.


## Why Were the Peter Rabbit Organics Fruit Pouches Recalled?


According to PT Organics, the issue was linked to an internal packaging defect. The company stated that the lining inside certain pouches could potentially break down during use, causing thin strands of food-grade plastic material to mix with the purée when the pouch is squeezed.


Although the material is described as food-grade plastic, the company initiated the recall as a precaution to protect consumers and prevent possible risks associated with consuming contaminated products.


Fruit pouches are especially popular among families with young children because they are convenient, portable, and easy to consume. However, because these products are often eaten directly from the package, any packaging failure can create additional concerns for parents.


## Which Products Are Included in the Recall?


The recall applies only to specific Pumpkin Tree Peter Rabbit Organics Banana & Strawberry 4-ounce (113-gram) pouches. Consumers should check the details printed on the packaging before deciding whether to keep or discard the product.


The recalled products include the following identifying information:


- Product: Pumpkin Tree Peter Rabbit Organics Banana & Strawberry 4-ounce pouches
- Barcode: 8 15367 01078 0
- Lane number: The number “4” printed on the rear right-hand side seam
- Best-before dates:


- 01/19/2027
- 01/20/2027
- 03/17/2027
- 03/18/2027
- 05/14/2027
- 05/15/2027


Only products matching these specific details are included in the recall. Other Peter Rabbit Organics products or different expiration dates are not part of this action.


## Where Were the Recalled Fruit Pouches Sold?


The recalled fruit pouches were available nationwide at several major retail chains. Customers may have purchased the affected products at:


- Kroger stores
- Meijer stores
- Target stores


The products were reportedly sold between March 6 and July 13. Because these retailers serve customers across multiple regions, consumers throughout the country should check their supplies if they regularly purchase fruit snacks or baby food pouches.


## What Should Consumers Do If They Have the Recalled Product?


Consumers who find a recalled pouch should not eat or serve the product. Instead, they should follow the instructions provided by PT Organics or the relevant retailer regarding disposal or possible refund procedures.


Parents should also check other stored baby and toddler food products to ensure they are not confusing recalled items with unaffected products. Keeping packaging information, including barcodes and expiration dates, can make it easier to identify recalled products.


If someone believes they consumed a recalled product and experiences unusual symptoms or concerns, they should contact a healthcare professional for guidance.


## Why Food Recalls Matter for Families


Food recalls are an important part of the safety system designed to protect consumers. Manufacturers, retailers, and government agencies monitor products and respond when potential safety issues are discovered.


For families with young children, food safety is particularly important because infants and toddlers may be more vulnerable to contamination risks. Parents often rely on packaged foods for convenience, making it essential for companies to maintain strict quality controls throughout manufacturing and packaging.


Packaging defects can sometimes be difficult to detect because the product may appear normal from the outside. This is why recall notices provide specific identification details such as lot numbers, dates, and barcodes.


## How to Stay Updated on Food Recalls


Consumers can reduce risks by regularly checking official recall announcements from trusted sources. The U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) provides updates about recalled foods, beverages, and supplements through its public safety notices.


It is also helpful to:


- Register for retailer recall notifications when available
- Check expiration dates before consuming packaged foods
- Keep receipts when purchasing specialty food products
- Follow manufacturer announcements regarding product safety issues


Staying informed can help families respond quickly when a recall affects products they may already have at home.


## Final Thoughts


The PT Organics fruit pouch recall highlights the importance of checking food products carefully before consumption. More than 380,000 Pumpkin Tree Peter Rabbit Organics Banana & Strawberry pouches have been included in the recall because of a possible packaging-related plastic contamination issue.


Consumers should review the barcode, lane number, and best-before dates on their pouches to determine whether they have an affected product. If the item matches the recall information, it should not be consumed.


Food safety updates may seem inconvenient, but they play a critical role in protecting families and ensuring that potential problems are addressed quickly.
