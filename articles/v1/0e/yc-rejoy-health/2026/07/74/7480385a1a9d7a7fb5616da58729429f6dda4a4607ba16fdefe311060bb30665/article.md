# More Than 12,000 Pounds of Bacon Recalled in US Over Import Inspection Issue

Consumers across parts of the Pacific Northwest are being urged to check their refrigerators and freezers after more than 12,000 pounds of imported bacon were recalled in the United States. The recall involves specific smoked bacon products that entered the country without completing the required federal import reinspection process.


Although there have been no confirmed reports of illness linked to the recalled products, federal food safety officials have classified the action as a Class I recall, which is the most serious type of food recall. Here is everything shoppers need to know about the affected products, why the recall was issued, and what to do if you have purchased the bacon.


## More Than 12,000 Pounds of Bacon Recalled


Maple Leaf Foods, Inc. has recalled approximately **12,036 pounds of smoked bacon products** that were imported from Canada into the United States. According to the U.S. Department of Agriculture's Food Safety and Inspection Service (FSIS), the products entered the country without undergoing the mandatory import reinspection process.


The issue was identified during routine inspection activities conducted by federal officials. Once the oversight was discovered, the recall was initiated to prevent potentially affected products from remaining in consumers' homes or on store shelves.


Import reinspection is a standard requirement for meat products entering the United States. It helps verify that imported foods meet U.S. food safety standards before they are distributed for sale.


## Which Bacon Products Are Included?


The recall affects bacon products produced during June 2026 under two different brand names.


The recalled products include:


- **Royale Natural Applewood Smoked All Natural Uncured Bacon**


- 12 ounce packages
- Sell by dates:


- September 1, 2026
- September 7, 2026


- **TOP VALU Uncured Hardwood Smoked Bacon**


- 12 ounce packages
- Sell by dates:


- September 1, 2026
- September 2, 2026
- September 4, 2026
- September 5, 2026
- September 7, 2026


Consumers can also identify the recalled products by checking for:


- Canadian establishment number **EST. 1** printed on the package
- Health certificate number **2026-S732971612** printed on master case boxes


Checking these details carefully can help determine whether your bacon is part of the recall.


## Where Were the Products Sold?


According to FSIS, the recalled bacon products were distributed to **Grocery Outlet** stores in three states:


- Idaho
- Oregon
- Washington


Customers who purchased bacon from Grocery Outlet locations in these states during recent weeks should review the product labels and sell by dates.


While the distribution was limited to these states, consumers should remain cautious if products were transported or shared beyond the original purchase location.


## Why Was the Recall Issued?


Unlike many food recalls that involve contamination, bacteria, or foreign materials, this recall was issued because the products **did not receive the required import reinspection after entering the United States** .


The inspection process is designed to ensure imported meat products comply with all applicable federal regulations before reaching consumers.


A spokesperson for Maple Leaf Foods stated that the recall was **solely related to the missed import reinspection requirement** and emphasized that there were **no additional food safety concerns** that prompted the recall.


This distinction is important because the recall does not stem from confirmed contamination or known health hazards within the bacon itself.


## Why Is This a Class I Recall?


The USDA classified this action as a **Class I recall** , the agency's highest recall category.


A Class I recall is issued when there is a reasonable probability that consuming the affected product could cause serious adverse health consequences or even death.


Even though no illnesses have been reported in connection with this recall, federal regulators often use this classification when products fail to meet critical regulatory requirements involving food safety oversight.


The classification underscores the importance of removing affected products from circulation quickly.


## Have Any Illnesses Been Reported?


As of the latest announcement, **no confirmed illnesses or injuries** have been linked to the recalled bacon.


However, FSIS advises consumers who believe they became ill after consuming one of the affected products to contact a healthcare provider for evaluation.


Consumers experiencing food related symptoms should not ignore potential signs of illness, particularly if they suspect they consumed a recalled product.


## What Should Consumers Do?


Federal officials recommend that anyone who purchased the recalled bacon should **not eat it** .


Instead, consumers should:


- Throw the product away safely.
- Return it to the place of purchase for a refund, if applicable.
- Check refrigerators and freezers for affected packages.
- Inform family members who may have stored or purchased the products.


Retailers are also expected to remove all recalled products from store shelves as part of the recall process.


## How FSIS Handles Food Recalls


When a recall is announced, FSIS performs effectiveness checks to confirm that manufacturers and distributors have properly notified retailers and removed affected products from commerce.


The agency also works with manufacturers throughout the recall process to reduce the chance that recalled products remain available to consumers.


Officials expressed concern that some customers may still have the bacon stored at home because refrigerated and frozen products are often kept for extended periods.


That is why consumers are encouraged to verify product information carefully rather than assuming older purchases are unaffected.


## Food Recall Awareness Matters


Food recalls occur for a variety of reasons, including labeling errors, contamination concerns, regulatory violations, and manufacturing issues. While many recalls never result in reported illnesses, acting promptly helps reduce potential risks.


Consumers can stay informed by monitoring recall announcements from federal food safety agencies and checking product labels whenever recalls are announced.


Taking a few moments to compare sell by dates, establishment numbers, and product names can prevent accidental consumption of recalled food products.


## Final Thoughts


The recall of more than 12,000 pounds of smoked bacon serves as a reminder that regulatory safeguards play an important role in maintaining the integrity of the food supply. Although officials have not identified contamination or reported illnesses associated with these products, the absence of the required import reinspection prompted regulators to take precautionary action.


Anyone who purchased Royale Natural Applewood Smoked All Natural Uncured Bacon or TOP VALU Uncured Hardwood Smoked Bacon from Grocery Outlet locations in Idaho, Oregon, or Washington should inspect the packaging carefully. If the product matches the recall details, it should be discarded or returned to the retailer rather than consumed.


Staying informed about food recalls and following official guidance can help protect consumers and ensure recalled products are removed from homes as quickly as possible.
