# Cyclospora Cases Rise Among UK Travellers Returning From Mexico: Symptoms, Risks, and Prevention Tips

Recent reports have highlighted an increase in Cyclospora infections among people returning to the United Kingdom after holidays in Mexico. Health authorities are encouraging travellers to stay informed about food and water safety after a noticeable rise in travel-related cases during the 2026 summer season.


Although Cyclospora infection, also known as cyclosporiasis, is rarely life-threatening, it can cause severe digestive illness that may disrupt travel plans and require medical attention. Understanding the symptoms, how the parasite spreads, and the best ways to reduce your risk can help you stay healthy while travelling abroad.


## What Is Cyclospora?


Cyclospora is a microscopic parasite called *Cyclospora cayetanensis* that infects the small intestine. People become infected after consuming food or water contaminated with the parasite. Unlike many common stomach bugs, Cyclospora infections are not typically spread directly from one person to another.


The parasite is more commonly found in tropical and subtropical regions where sanitation and food handling conditions can allow contamination to occur. Imported produce has also been linked to outbreaks in several countries over the years.


## Why Are UK Health Officials Concerned?


Health officials in the UK have recently reported an increase in Cyclospora infections among travellers returning from Mexico. Many of the reported cases involved people who had stayed in popular tourist destinations and consumed meals as part of all inclusive holiday packages.


Authorities stress that the increase does not mean travelling to Mexico is unsafe. Instead, it highlights the importance of following food and water safety precautions while abroad, regardless of whether you are staying in luxury resorts or budget accommodation.


Travellers should remain aware of potential health risks and seek medical advice if symptoms develop after returning home.


## Common Symptoms of Cyclospora Infection


Symptoms usually appear several days to around two weeks after consuming contaminated food or water. Some infected individuals experience mild illness, while others develop more persistent digestive problems.


Common symptoms include:


- Frequent watery diarrhoea
- Severe stomach cramps
- Bloating
- Nausea
- Vomiting
- Fatigue
- Loss of appetite
- Weight loss
- Increased gas
- Mild fever


One of the most commonly reported symptoms is sudden, frequent watery diarrhoea that can become severe enough to interfere with normal daily activities.


Without treatment, symptoms may continue for several weeks or may disappear and return.


## How Does Cyclospora Spread?


Cyclospora spreads primarily through contaminated food and water. Fresh produce is considered one of the most common sources because fruits and vegetables can become contaminated before reaching consumers.


Foods that have previously been associated with outbreaks include:


- Fresh herbs
- Leafy salads
- Soft fruits
- Berries
- Fresh vegetables


The parasite requires time in the environment before becoming infectious, making direct person to person transmission extremely unlikely.


## Who Is Most at Risk?


Anyone can develop Cyclospora infection after consuming contaminated food or water. However, some individuals may experience more serious illness.


Higher risk groups include:


- Older adults
- Young children
- Pregnant women
- People with weakened immune systems
- Individuals receiving immune suppressing medications


People with weakened immune systems may require prescription antibiotics and closer medical monitoring.


## How Can Travellers Reduce Their Risk?


Good hygiene and careful food choices remain the most effective ways to reduce the risk of infection.


Experts recommend:


- Drink bottled or properly treated water.
- Eat food that has been thoroughly cooked and served hot.
- Wash hands frequently with soap and water.
- Avoid unwashed fresh produce.
- Peel fruit yourself whenever possible.
- Be cautious with raw herbs, salads, and berries.
- Choose reputable restaurants with good hygiene standards.


Even travellers staying at luxury resorts should continue to practice safe food habits because contamination can occur anywhere within the food supply chain.


## What Should You Do If You Become Sick?


If you develop persistent diarrhoea after returning from international travel, contact a healthcare professional as soon as possible. Tell your doctor about your recent travel history because this information can help guide appropriate testing and treatment.


Healthcare providers may recommend laboratory testing to confirm the diagnosis. While some mild infections improve on their own, others require antibiotics to completely eliminate the parasite.


Early diagnosis may also help prevent dehydration and reduce the duration of illness.


## Is There a Risk Within the UK?


Current health guidance indicates that Cyclospora is not naturally established in the United Kingdom. Most reported infections are associated with international travel rather than local transmission.


Since the parasite is generally acquired through contaminated food or water in affected regions, routine community spread within the UK remains unlikely.


Travellers returning from affected destinations should simply remain aware of symptoms and seek medical advice if illness develops after their trip.


## Final Thoughts


An increase in Cyclospora infections among UK travellers serves as an important reminder that foodborne illnesses remain a risk during international travel. Fortunately, simple precautions such as drinking safe water, choosing freshly cooked meals, and practicing good hand hygiene can significantly reduce the likelihood of infection.


Most people recover fully, particularly when the illness is identified early and treated appropriately. If digestive symptoms persist after travelling abroad, consult a healthcare provider promptly and mention your recent destination.
