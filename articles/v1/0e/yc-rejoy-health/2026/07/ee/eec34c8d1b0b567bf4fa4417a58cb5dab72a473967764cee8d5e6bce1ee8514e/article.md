# Milton Baby Bottle Cleaner Recall: What Parents Need to Know About Insect Contamination Warning in the UK

## Milton Baby Bottle Cleaner Removed From UK Shelves After Contamination Discovery


Parents across the UK are being urged to check their cupboards after a popular baby hygiene product was recalled following the discovery of insects inside some bottles.


Milton Baby Bottle Cleaner, a widely used product among families for cleaning baby bottles, breast pumps, feeding equipment, and household items, has been removed from shelves after a small number of bottles were found to contain insect contamination.


The recall affects specific batches of Milton’s 500ml Baby Bottle Cleaner. Customers who have purchased the product are advised to check the batch number and expiry date printed on the packaging to find out whether their bottle is included in the recall.


The company behind the product, Milton International SAS, confirmed that affected products were immediately removed from sale after the issue was identified. Retailers, including Waitrose, have also withdrawn the impacted batches from stores.


## Which Milton Baby Bottle Cleaner Products Are Affected?


Only certain batches of Milton Baby Bottle Cleaner 500ml bottles are included in the recall. Customers should check their product details carefully.


The affected batch codes and expiry dates are:


- 6030 1103M, expiry January 2029
- 6077 1103M, expiry March 2029
- 6110 1103M, expiry April 2029
- 6111 1103M, expiry April 2029
- 6112 1103M, expiry April 2029
- 6133 1103M, expiry May 2029


Milton has confirmed that no other batches are affected.


Anyone who has a bottle matching one of these details should stop using the product and follow retailer or manufacturer instructions regarding returns and refunds.


## What Did Milton Say About the Recall?


Milton said that the safety of babies and the confidence of parents remain its highest priorities. The company explained that it launched an investigation after discovering insects in a small number of bottles.


The manufacturer stated that affected products were removed from sale immediately and that laboratory testing was carried out according to recognised standards.


According to Milton, testing showed no risk to babies’ health or safety. However, the recall was issued as a precautionary measure to protect consumers and maintain product safety standards.


The company has apologised to customers for the inconvenience caused and said it is working with retail partners to restore normal supplies as quickly as possible.


## Why Baby Product Recalls Are Taken Seriously


Products designed for babies are subject to strict safety expectations because infants are more vulnerable to potential contamination risks.


Baby bottle cleaners are used to clean items that come into direct contact with milk, formula, and feeding equipment. Parents rely on these products to maintain hygiene and reduce the risk of harmful bacteria developing on bottles and accessories.


Even when testing suggests there is no immediate danger, manufacturers and regulators often take a cautious approach when there is any uncertainty surrounding a product.


A recall allows companies to remove potentially affected items from circulation while investigations continue.


## What Should Parents Do If They Own This Product?


Parents who have Milton Baby Bottle Cleaner at home should check the packaging before using it again.


If the bottle matches one of the recalled batch numbers, customers should stop using it and return it according to retailer guidance.


Customers who purchased the affected product from Waitrose can return it to their nearest store for a full refund.


People who bought the cleaner from other retailers should check with the seller or Milton for further instructions.


Consumers should avoid throwing away recalled products immediately unless advised to do so, as retailers or manufacturers may require product details to process refunds or investigate the issue.
