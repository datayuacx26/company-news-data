# Scientists Who Received NIH Funding Nominated Anthony Fauci for Awards, Newly Released Emails Show

## Scientists Receiving NIH Grants Nominated Fauci for Prestigious Awards, Emails Reveal


Recently released emails made public by Sen. Rand Paul's office have renewed discussion about former National Institute of Allergy and Infectious Diseases (NIAID) Director Dr. Anthony Fauci and the prestigious awards he received before and during the COVID-19 pandemic. The documents indicate that several scientists who had received substantial funding from the National Institutes of Health (NIH) nominated Fauci for high profile awards, raising fresh questions about potential conflicts of interest and government ethics.


The emails, released on July 24 by the office of Sen. Rand Paul, chairman of the U.S. Senate Committee on Homeland Security and Governmental Affairs, have sparked debate over whether recipients of NIH funding should have participated in nominating the longtime public health official for lucrative international prizes.


## Emails Show Award Nominations from NIH Funded Scientists


According to the released correspondence, Dr. Charles Dinarello, a professor of medicine and immunology at the University of Colorado School of Medicine, nominated Anthony Fauci for the Dan David Prize in 2020. The Dan David Prize is an internationally recognized award that, at the time, carried a total value of $1 million, with $900,000 awarded directly to the recipient.


In one email, Dinarello stated that Fauci and his longtime aide, Greg Folkers, completed much of the work required for the nomination package. Fauci ultimately received the award.


The emails also include congratulatory messages exchanged among colleagues after the ethics review was completed. One employee joked about Fauci becoming wealthy from the prize, while another humorously requested a percentage of the winnings.


## Ethics Review Cleared Acceptance of Prize


Before Fauci accepted the Dan David Prize, government ethics officials reviewed whether accepting the award would violate constitutional restrictions regarding foreign gifts.


According to the review, officials concluded that accepting the prize did not violate the U.S. Constitution because the Dan David Prize operates independently of Tel Aviv University, despite being based there. Officials determined that the foundation had sufficient independence to allow acceptance of the award.


However, not everyone agreed with that conclusion. Former law professor E. Donald Elliott argued in a published opinion article that because Tel Aviv University receives significant financial support from the Israeli government, acceptance of the prize should have been considered unconstitutional under the Constitution's foreign emoluments provisions.


The differing legal interpretations have remained part of the broader public discussion surrounding the award.


## Other Prize Money Was Restricted


The released documents also reveal that ethics officials did not approve every prize opportunity presented to Fauci.


In some cases, government ethics advisers determined that Fauci could not accept prize money from organizations that received funding from NIAID, the institute he led from 1984 until 2022.


One example involved the Georgia Institute of Technology, where officials reportedly concluded that accepting prize funds would create an ethics concern because the institution was an NIH grant recipient.


These decisions demonstrate that ethics reviews were conducted individually depending on the circumstances surrounding each award.


## Funding Records Draw Attention


Public funding records indicate that Dr. Charles Dinarello received millions of dollars in research grants from NIAID over several decades, beginning in the mid 1980s.


The records cited in the released documents show that additional NIH funding continued after Dinarello nominated Fauci for the Dan David Prize.


While the timing has attracted public attention, the emails themselves do not provide evidence that funding decisions were directly connected to the award nominations.


## Fauci Also Discussed Other Award Nominations


The released emails include additional exchanges involving other prestigious scientific awards.


In 2021, Dinarello contacted Greg Folkers asking whether Fauci had been invited to nominate someone for the Shaw Prize.


Fauci responded that if Dinarello wanted to be nominated, he would be willing to submit his name. Fauci also suggested that Dinarello prepare much of the nomination paperwork, similar to previous nominations.


The correspondence illustrates the professional relationships that existed among prominent medical researchers involved in major scientific awards.


## Barton Haynes Also Nominated Fauci


Another scientist mentioned in the emails is Dr. Barton Haynes, a professor of medicine at Duke University School of Medicine.


According to the documents, Haynes nominated Fauci for multiple awards over several years, including the Dan David Prize and the Heinz Award for the Human Condition.


One email indicates that Fauci personally asked Haynes to serve as his nominator for the Heinz Award. Fauci explained that Haynes would first need to register as an official nominator before the paperwork could be submitted.


Haynes agreed and replied that he believed the award would be a good fit.


Despite the nomination, Fauci did not receive either the Heinz Award or the Shaw Prize.


## NIH Funding to Haynes Also Highlighted


Funding records referenced in the released materials indicate that Barton Haynes had received more than $100 million in NIAID research grants before one of Fauci's award nominations.


Additional funding reportedly continued afterward.


The documents have prompted questions from critics regarding whether researchers receiving federal grants should participate in award nominations involving senior agency officials.


However, the released emails do not establish that grant funding was influenced by those nominations.


## Sen. Rand Paul Raises Questions


Sen. Rand Paul said the newly released records raise concerns about the appearance of a possible conflict of interest.


In a public statement accompanying the document release, Paul said the records raise questions about whether a quid pro quo may have existed given the significant financial value associated with some of the awards.


Neither the NIH, Anthony Fauci, nor Barton Haynes responded to media requests for comment before the original report was published, according to the source article.


## Broader Public Discussion Continues


The release of these emails has added another chapter to the ongoing public debate surrounding Anthony Fauci's leadership during his decades at NIAID and throughout the COVID 19 pandemic.


Supporters argue that Fauci's awards reflected decades of scientific contributions and public service. Critics maintain that the relationships between grant recipients and agency leadership deserve closer examination to ensure transparency and maintain public confidence in government institutions.


Whether additional investigations or reviews will follow remains uncertain, but the documents have generated renewed interest in federal ethics procedures and the nomination process for prestigious international awards.


## Conclusion


The newly released emails reveal that several scientists who had received NIH funding nominated Anthony Fauci for major awards, including the million dollar Dan David Prize. While government ethics officials approved Fauci's acceptance of that award, the documents have fueled renewed debate about transparency, conflicts of interest, and the appearance of impartiality in federal agencies.


As lawmakers continue reviewing the records, the discussion is likely to remain part of the broader conversation about public accountability, government ethics, and the relationships between federal research agencies and the scientists they support.
