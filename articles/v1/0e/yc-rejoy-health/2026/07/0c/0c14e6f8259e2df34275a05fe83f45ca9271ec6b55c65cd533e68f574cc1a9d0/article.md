# Amazon Pool Drain Cover Recall Raises Safety Concerns for Home Pool Owners

Two pool drain cover products sold through Amazon have been recalled after federal safety officials determined that they may fail to provide proper protection against dangerous underwater suction hazards. The recalled products, sold under the names Sviyatp and OeyUoc, were manufactured in China and distributed as replacement drain covers for residential swimming pools.


The U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission (CPSC) announced the recalls on July 23, 2026, after finding that both products did not comply with federal safety requirements designed to prevent swimmer entrapment. The affected covers were sold online through Amazon.com between March and June 2026, with each product priced at approximately $18.


Although no injuries or accidents have been reported in connection with either product, safety officials are urging pool owners to immediately stop using pools equipped with these drain covers until the recalled parts are removed.


## Recalled Pool Drain Covers Did Not Meet Federal Safety Standards


The recalled products failed to meet the requirements established under the Virginia Graeme Baker Pool and Spa Safety Act, a federal law created to reduce the risk of swimmers becoming trapped by powerful suction forces from pool and spa drains.


Pool drain covers play a critical role in preventing accidents because they are designed to distribute suction pressure and stop body parts, hair, or clothing from becoming trapped against a drain opening. If a drain cover is poorly designed or does not meet required standards, it may increase the risk of serious injury or drowning.


According to the CPSC, approximately 670 Sviyatp pool drain covers and around 404 OeyUoc drain covers were sold to consumers. Both products were marketed as replacement drain covers for home swimming pools.


The Sviyatp drain cover was sold by Shenzhen Meilanxiang Trading Co., Ltd., operating under the Sviyatp name. The OeyUoc product was sold by Shenzhen Yuanxinxiang Trade Co., Ltd., operating as Yuanxinxiang. Both companies are based in China, and the recalled products were manufactured there.


## How Consumers Can Identify the Recalled Products


One of the challenges with this recall is that neither drain cover has identifying information printed directly on the product itself. This means consumers may have difficulty determining whether they own one of the recalled covers after installation.


The CPSC advises customers to check the original packaging if it is still available.


The Sviyatp product packaging includes the following identifying information:


- Product codes: X0050114ZZ and SV-SP1030-001
- Product description: “Sviyatp Dual Suction Outlet”
- Manufacturing label: “Made in China”


The OeyUoc product packaging includes:


- Product codes: X00501CQ7P and OY-WG1048E-1P
- Product description: “OeyUoc WG1048…Skimmer Lid SP1030”
- Manufacturing label: “Made in China”


Both recalled products were shipped with two stainless steel screws.


## What Pool Owners Should Do Now


Consumers who believe they may have one of these drain covers installed should stop using the pool immediately until the product is removed.


The CPSC recommends that owners take the following steps:


1. Remove the recalled drain cover from the pool.
2. Mark the product with the word “RECALL” using permanent marker.
3. Take a photograph of the removed cover.
4. If possible, cut or damage the outer structure of the cover so it cannot be installed again.
5. Contact the appropriate company to request a full refund.


Owners of Sviyatp drain covers should send identification photographs toSviyatpRecall@qq.com .


Owners of OeyUoc drain covers should send photographs toOeyUocChina@163.com .


Consumers should avoid reinstalling the products or giving them to another person, as doing so could create a safety risk.


## Why Pool Drain Safety Rules Matter


The safety requirements involved in this recall are connected to the Virginia Graeme Baker Pool and Spa Safety Act, which was created following a tragic accident involving a young child.


The law was named after Virginia Graeme Baker, a 7-year-old girl who died in 2002 after becoming trapped by the suction of a hot tub drain. Following her daughter’s death, her mother, Nancy Baker, worked with safety organizations and lawmakers to push for stronger protections in swimming pools and spas.


The legislation was signed into law in December 2007 and became effective in 2008. It established national safety requirements for drain covers and other anti-entrapment measures, particularly in public pools and spas.


While the law primarily focuses on public aquatic facilities, its safety principles are also important for residential pool owners. Properly designed and installed drain covers can help reduce the risk of dangerous suction-related accidents.


## Online Marketplace Safety Concerns


The recall also highlights growing concerns about safety standards for products sold through online marketplaces. Consumers often purchase replacement pool parts, accessories, and maintenance equipment online because of convenience and lower prices. However, product safety information can sometimes be difficult to verify, especially when items are imported from overseas manufacturers.


Experts recommend that pool owners purchase replacement parts from trusted suppliers, verify that products meet recognized safety standards, and keep product packaging or receipts for future reference.


Pool equipment should not be treated as a simple household accessory. Components such as drain covers, filters, ladders, and safety barriers can directly affect swimmer protection.


## Final Reminder for Pool Owners


The recall of the Sviyatp and OeyUoc pool drain covers serves as an important reminder that even small replacement parts can have major safety consequences. Consumers who have installed either recalled product should stop using the pool, remove the cover, and contact the manufacturer for refund instructions.


Regular inspection of pool equipment and awareness of safety recalls can help prevent accidents and protect swimmers, especially children.


Pool owners are encouraged to stay informed through official safety agencies and check whether their equipment meets current safety requirements before allowing anyone to swim.
