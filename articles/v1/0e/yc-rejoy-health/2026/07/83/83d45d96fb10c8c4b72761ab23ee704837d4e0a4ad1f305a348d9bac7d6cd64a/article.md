# Florida Attorney General Opens Investigation Into Anthony Fauci Over Potential State Offenses

Florida Attorney General James Uthmeier has launched an investigation into Dr. Anthony Fauci over potential state offenses not covered by a federal pardon. Here is what happened and what it could mean.


Florida Attorney General James Uthmeier announced that his office is opening an investigation into former National Institute of Allergy and Infectious Diseases Director Dr. Anthony Fauci. The announcement came after Fauci repeatedly invoked his Fifth Amendment right against self-incrimination during a Senate hearing on July 29, 2026.


The investigation centers on whether Fauci may have committed any state offenses that would not be covered by the federal pardon issued by former President Joe Biden in January 2025. The development has reignited debate over government accountability, pandemic-era decisions, and the legal limits of presidential pardons.


## Why Is Florida Investigating Anthony Fauci?


Attorney General James Uthmeier announced the investigation through a post on X shortly after Fauci testified before the Senate Committee on Homeland Security and Governmental Affairs.


Uthmeier criticized Fauci's repeated use of the Fifth Amendment during questioning, describing his refusal to answer as concerning. He stated that his office intends to determine whether any state laws may have been violated during Fauci's leadership of federal public health agencies throughout the COVID-19 pandemic.


The investigation follows increasing calls from some lawmakers for additional scrutiny of Fauci's actions during the pandemic.


## What Happened During the Senate Hearing?


During the Senate hearing, senators questioned Fauci about decisions made while he served as director of the National Institute of Allergy and Infectious Diseases and as a leading federal health adviser during the COVID-19 crisis.


On the advice of his attorneys, Fauci repeatedly invoked his Fifth Amendment right against self-incrimination instead of answering numerous questions.


Several Republican lawmakers argued that his federal pardon may have changed his legal position regarding self-incrimination. Others maintained that invoking the Fifth Amendment remains a constitutional right regardless of political criticism.


The hearing quickly became one of the most closely watched congressional proceedings related to the government's pandemic response.


## Rep. Anna Paulina Luna Calls for State Action


Florida Representative Anna Paulina Luna urged attorneys general and governors in Florida, Texas, West Virginia, and Arkansas to pursue potential state-level legal action against Fauci.


In a post on X, Luna argued that Biden's federal pardon does not protect Fauci from possible state prosecutions or civil lawsuits. She also alleged that accountability should continue through state legal systems if applicable laws were violated.


Luna did not explain why she specifically identified those four states.


## Understanding the Federal Pardon


On January 20, 2025, former President Joe Biden issued a preemptive federal pardon covering potential federal offenses involving Fauci from 2014 through 2025.


Legal experts note that presidential pardons apply only to federal crimes. They do not automatically prevent state investigations or prosecutions if state laws are allegedly violated.


However, whether any state criminal charges could be brought depends on the evidence, applicable statutes, and each state's laws.


## Legal Experts Say State Cases Could Face Challenges


Legal analysts say that launching a state investigation does not necessarily mean criminal charges will follow.


Ohio defense attorney and former prosecutor Mike Allen noted that any potential prosecution could face significant legal obstacles. One major issue is the statute of limitations, which limits how long prosecutors have to file charges after an alleged offense.


Allen explained that many pandemic-related decisions occurred in 2020 or earlier, meaning time limits may already have expired for numerous potential offenses.


He also emphasized that prosecutors must pursue any investigation in good faith and within the limits of state law.


## Can Someone Invoke the Fifth Amendment After Receiving a Pardon?


One of the legal questions raised during the hearing involves whether a person who has received a federal pardon can still invoke the Fifth Amendment.


Some lawmakers argued that because the pardon removes the risk of federal prosecution, Fauci should have answered the committee's questions.


However, constitutional scholars have noted that Fifth Amendment protections may still apply if truthful answers could expose someone to state criminal liability, civil consequences, or other legal risks. The exact application depends on the specific circumstances and legal questions involved.


Because the Florida investigation concerns possible state offenses, the legal debate remains significant.


## Other States Continue Reviewing Fauci's Conduct


Florida is not the only state examining Fauci's pandemic-related actions.


South Carolina Attorney General Alan Wilson continues leading a coalition of 17 state attorneys general that has sought information from Congress regarding Fauci's conduct during the pandemic.


Following Fauci's Senate testimony, Wilson stated that Americans deserve transparency and accountability from public officials whose decisions affected millions of people.


The coalition has not announced whether any specific state investigations will result in criminal charges.


## Fauci's Attorney Declines Comment


David Schertler, Fauci's attorney, declined to comment following the Senate hearing and the announcement of Florida's investigation.


As of publication, no criminal charges have been filed against Fauci in Florida or any other state.


The Florida Attorney General's announcement marks the beginning of an investigation rather than a legal finding. Investigators will first determine whether sufficient evidence exists to support any potential legal action.


## What Happens Next?


The investigation remains in its early stages.


Florida authorities will review available evidence and determine whether any alleged conduct falls under Florida law and whether prosecution would be legally possible.


Any future legal action would likely face extensive constitutional and procedural challenges, including questions surrounding jurisdiction, statutes of limitations, and evidentiary standards.


For now, the investigation represents another chapter in the ongoing political and legal debates surrounding the federal government's response to the COVID-19 pandemic.


## Conclusion


Florida Attorney General James Uthmeier's decision to investigate Dr. Anthony Fauci has added a new legal dimension to ongoing scrutiny of pandemic-era leadership. While a federal pardon shields Fauci from certain federal prosecutions, legal experts note that it does not automatically prevent state investigations. Whether the Florida inquiry leads to any formal charges remains uncertain, and experts caution that significant legal hurdles would need to be overcome before any prosecution could proceed.
