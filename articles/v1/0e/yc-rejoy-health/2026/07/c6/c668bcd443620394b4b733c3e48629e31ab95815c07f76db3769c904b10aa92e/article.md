# UK Gel Nail Polish Ban 2026: What the New TPO Rules Mean for Salons and Customers

## UK to Restrict TPO in Gel Nail Products From August 2026


The UK beauty industry is preparing for an important regulatory change that will affect nail salons, beauty professionals, retailers, and consumers. From August 15, 2026, new restrictions will limit the sale of gel nail products containing trimethylbenzoyl diphenylphosphine oxide, commonly known as TPO.


The decision follows growing concerns about the chemical's potential health risks and aligns the UK more closely with European safety standards. While many manufacturers have already begun reformulating their products, salon owners and nail technicians will need to understand the new timeline and prepare for the transition.


Here is everything you need to know about the upcoming gel nail polish ban, why it is happening, and what it means for customers.


## What Is TPO?


Trimethylbenzoyl diphenylphosphine oxide, or TPO, is a photoinitiator used in many gel nail products. Its primary role is to help gel polish harden quickly when exposed to ultraviolet (UV) or LED nail lamps.


The ingredient has been widely used because it provides several benefits, including:


- Fast curing times
- Durable gel finishes
- A smooth and glossy appearance
- Reliable performance during professional manicures


Although it has been common in gel nail formulas for years, scientific evaluations have raised concerns about its safety under certain exposure conditions.


## Why Is the UK Restricting TPO?


The upcoming restrictions are based on concerns about the chemical's hazardous properties. Research has suggested that TPO may pose risks to reproductive health if exposure reaches high enough levels.


The European Union previously classified TPO as a reproductive toxic substance and introduced restrictions on its use in cosmetic products during 2025. The UK is now implementing similar measures to reduce the presence of the ingredient in professional and consumer nail products.


Industry experts have emphasized that the classification is based on hazard assessments rather than evidence showing that salon customers are experiencing widespread health problems from standard gel manicures.


Even so, regulators have chosen to adopt a precautionary approach by removing the ingredient from cosmetic products.


## Key Dates for the UK Gel Nail Product Changes


Businesses involved in the nail industry should pay close attention to the implementation schedule.


### August 15, 2026


- Manufacturers can no longer place TPO-containing gel nail products on the UK market.
- New supplies containing the ingredient cannot be introduced for sale.


### February 14, 2027


- Nail professionals will no longer be able to purchase TPO-based products from distributors.
- Retailers will no longer be allowed to sell home gel manicure kits containing TPO.


Existing stock that salons already own may still be used after this date, according to the current transition guidance.


## What Does This Mean for Nail Salons?


Salon owners should begin reviewing their inventory and discussing alternative products with suppliers well before the restrictions take full effect.


Many manufacturers anticipated regulatory changes and have already developed new formulations that do not contain TPO. In many cases, customers are unlikely to notice a significant difference in curing speed or overall finish.


Salon operators may also need to:


- Update product inventories
- Train staff on replacement products
- Verify ingredient lists before purchasing
- Inform customers about any changes to products used during treatments


Preparing early can help businesses avoid disruptions once the restrictions become fully enforceable.


## Will Customers Notice Any Difference?


For most people, the experience of receiving a gel manicure is expected to remain largely unchanged.


Many replacement products use alternative photoinitiators that deliver similar curing performance and shine. As a result, customers should continue to enjoy long-lasting gel manicures without major differences in appearance or durability.


The biggest change will happen behind the scenes as manufacturers update product formulations to comply with new regulations.


## Are Gel Manicures Still Safe?


Health authorities have not advised consumers to stop getting gel manicures altogether.


Instead, experts recommend following good safety practices when visiting nail salons, including:


- Choose reputable salons that follow hygiene standards.
- Ensure technicians use approved products.
- Avoid getting gel polish on damaged skin.
- Wear sunscreen on your hands before UV lamp exposure if recommended by your dermatologist.
- Follow proper removal techniques instead of peeling off gel polish.


These simple precautions can help reduce unnecessary exposure while maintaining healthy nails.


## Why Are Manufacturers Reformulating Products?


The nail care industry has responded quickly to regulatory changes.


Many brands now use alternative ingredients such as TPO-L or BAPO, which can provide curing performance similar to traditional TPO-based formulations.


This means salons can continue offering gel manicures without sacrificing quality while remaining compliant with updated regulations.


Consumers may gradually notice new product labels or ingredient lists as manufacturers complete the transition.


## What Should Consumers Do?


There is no immediate action required for most customers.


If you regularly receive professional gel manicures, you can ask your nail technician whether the salon has already switched to TPO-free products. Many businesses have already made the transition following earlier European regulatory changes.


If you purchase home gel manicure kits, it may also be worth checking ingredient labels before buying new products after the regulations come into force.


Remaining informed and choosing reputable brands can help ensure a safe manicure experience.


## Final Thoughts


The UK's upcoming restrictions on TPO-containing gel nail products represent another step toward strengthening cosmetic safety regulations. While the changes may require adjustments for manufacturers and salon owners, consumers are unlikely to experience significant differences in the quality of professional gel manicures.


With many brands already offering TPO-free alternatives, the beauty industry appears well prepared for the transition. As the deadlines approach, both professionals and customers should stay informed and ensure that products used comply with the latest regulations.
