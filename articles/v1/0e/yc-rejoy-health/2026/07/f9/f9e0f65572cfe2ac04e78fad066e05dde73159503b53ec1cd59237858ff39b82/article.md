# Salmonella Egg Recall Sickens Nearly 100 Across the US as Health Officials Issue Fresh Warning

## Salmonella Outbreak Linked to Recalled Eggs Spreads Across 17 States


A growing salmonella outbreak in the United States has left nearly 100 people sick across 17 states, prompting federal health authorities to urge consumers to check their refrigerators for recalled eggs. While no deaths have been reported, dozens of people have required hospitalization, highlighting the serious health risks associated with foodborne illnesses.


According to the U.S. Food and Drug Administration, 98 confirmed infections have been recorded between November 21 and June 30. Health investigators believe the outbreak is linked to eggs recalled by Midwest Poultry Services, although officials say the company's products do not account for every reported illness.


The recall has drawn nationwide attention as food safety agencies continue to investigate the full scope of the outbreak and work to prevent additional infections.


## Nearly 1.6 Million Dozen Eggs Recalled


Midwest Poultry Services previously recalled nearly 1.6 million dozen eggs that were produced or distributed from farms in Texas between June 6 and July 3. The recall includes both white and brown cage-free eggs sold under multiple brand names.


The affected eggs carry sell-by or best-by dates ranging from July 20 through August 17. Consumers are strongly advised to inspect any recently purchased eggs and compare them with the official recall information provided by federal authorities.


The recalled products were distributed to several grocery chains across multiple states. Among the retailers identified are Kroger stores in Texas and Louisiana, as well as Brookshire Grocery locations serving customers in Texas, Oklahoma, Arkansas, Louisiana, New Mexico, and Mississippi.


Health officials recommend immediately disposing of any recalled eggs or returning them to the place of purchase for a refund.


## Genetic Testing Confirms Salmonella Connection


The FDA reported that routine testing conducted at Midwest Poultry farms in Texas detected salmonella bacteria in collected samples. Laboratory analysis and genetic sequencing revealed that some of these bacterial samples matched the strain responsible for infecting patients during the outbreak.


This scientific evidence strengthened investigators' conclusion that the recalled eggs are a significant source of the illnesses. However, officials continue to investigate because not every reported case appears to be connected to the recalled products.


Additional testing and interviews with affected individuals are ongoing to determine whether other food sources may also be contributing to the outbreak.


## What Is Salmonella?


Salmonella is one of the most common causes of foodborne illness worldwide. The bacteria typically spread through contaminated food products, especially raw or undercooked eggs, poultry, meat, and unpasteurized dairy products.


Symptoms generally begin within six hours to six days after exposure and can last between four and seven days. Most healthy individuals recover without medical treatment, although the illness can become severe in vulnerable populations.


Common symptoms include:


- Diarrhea
- Fever
- Stomach cramps
- Nausea
- Vomiting
- Dehydration


People experiencing severe dehydration, persistent vomiting, bloody diarrhea, or prolonged fever should seek medical attention immediately.


## Who Faces the Highest Risk?


Although many salmonella infections resolve without complications, certain groups face a much higher risk of serious illness.


These include:


- Young children
- Adults over the age of 65
- Pregnant women
- Individuals with weakened immune systems
- People undergoing cancer treatment
- Organ transplant recipients


For these individuals, salmonella infections may spread beyond the intestines and require hospitalization or intensive medical treatment.


The current outbreak has already resulted in 26 hospitalizations, underscoring the importance of food safety precautions.


## What Consumers Should Do


Food safety experts recommend taking immediate action if you recently purchased eggs from affected retailers.


Consumers should:


- Check egg cartons for brand names and best-by dates included in the recall.
- Throw away recalled eggs or return them for a refund.
- Wash and disinfect any surfaces, containers, or refrigerator shelves that may have come into contact with the eggs.
- Wash hands thoroughly after handling raw eggs.
- Cook eggs until both the yolk and white are firm.


Proper food handling significantly reduces the risk of salmonella infection.


## Another Foodborne Illness Is Also Spreading


The salmonella outbreak is not the only food safety concern currently affecting the United States.


Federal health officials are also monitoring a significant outbreak of cyclosporiasis, a parasitic illness that causes severe gastrointestinal symptoms.


Recent federal data show more than 4,100 confirmed cases across 41 states, with thousands of additional reports awaiting laboratory confirmation.


Michigan has reported the largest number of cases, where investigators have linked many infections to contaminated lettuce.


Unlike salmonella, cyclosporiasis is caused by the parasite *Cyclospora cayetanensis* . Symptoms often include severe diarrhea, fatigue, nausea, vomiting, body aches, fever, and dehydration.


Experts note that infections can last for weeks if left untreated and often require prescription antibiotics.


## Food Safety Remains a Public Health Priority


Large-scale food recalls serve as an important reminder of how quickly contaminated products can reach consumers across multiple states. Modern food distribution networks allow products to travel long distances, making early detection and rapid recalls essential for protecting public health.


Health agencies continue to encourage consumers to stay informed about food recalls through official government announcements and to practice safe food preparation habits at home.


Routine testing, genetic sequencing, and coordinated investigations between federal and state agencies have become valuable tools for identifying outbreak sources more quickly than in previous decades.


While the current salmonella outbreak remains under investigation, public awareness and prompt action by consumers can help prevent additional illnesses.


## Final Thoughts


The ongoing salmonella outbreak linked to recalled eggs has already affected nearly 100 people and led to more than two dozen hospitalizations across the United States. Although no fatalities have been reported, health officials continue to investigate the outbreak while urging consumers to check for recalled eggs and follow food safety recommendations.


At the same time, another widespread foodborne illness linked to contaminated lettuce highlights the continuing importance of monitoring food safety and responding quickly to public health alerts.


Consumers should remain vigilant by checking recall notices, practicing safe food handling, and seeking medical advice if symptoms of foodborne illness develop after consuming potentially contaminated products.
