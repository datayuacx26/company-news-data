# COVID-19 Cases Rising Across 37 US States, CDC Data Shows New Summer Infection Trend

COVID-19 infections are showing signs of growth across much of the United States, according to the latest estimates from the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC). New epidemic trend data indicates that COVID-19 activity is increasing or likely increasing in 37 states, while no states are currently showing a clear decline.


Although infections are rising in several areas, health officials note that the overall impact on hospitals and emergency departments remains low compared with earlier phases of the pandemic. The latest figures highlight the continuing need for public awareness, monitoring, and preventive health measures as the virus continues to circulate.


## CDC Reports COVID-19 Growth in Multiple States


The CDC’s latest epidemic trend estimates, based on data collected between June 23 and July 21, suggest that COVID-19 transmission is increasing nationwide, with a 91 percent probability that infections are growing across the country.


According to the agency’s estimates, COVID-19 infections are:


• Growing or likely growing in 37 states
• Showing no significant change in 11 states
• Declining in none of the states included in the assessment


Several states identified as experiencing growth include Texas, Louisiana, Mississippi, and Hawaii. Other states categorized as likely growing or showing mixed growth patterns include Alabama, Arkansas, Georgia, North Carolina, South Carolina, Virginia, Maryland, Delaware, New Jersey, Connecticut, Massachusetts, Rhode Island, New Hampshire, Maine, Nevada, and Utah.


The CDC noted that some states did not have enough available information for a clear classification, while others were listed as having stable infection trends.


## Hospital Impact Remains Low Despite Rising Cases


While COVID-19 infections appear to be increasing, the current impact on emergency healthcare services remains limited. The CDC reported that COVID-19 accounts for approximately 0.2 percent of emergency department visits nationwide, a level considered “very low” by the agency.


Health experts continue to monitor several indicators, including infection rates, hospital admissions, wastewater surveillance, and virus variants. These measurements help officials understand whether changes in transmission are temporary fluctuations or part of a larger trend.


The current situation differs significantly from the early years of the pandemic, when hospitals experienced severe pressure due to widespread infections and high numbers of serious cases.


## Global COVID-19 Activity Continues


COVID-19 activity is also being reported internationally. The World Health Organization (WHO) recorded 17,860 COVID-19 cases during a 28-day reporting period ending July 5.


The reported cases represented an increase compared with the previous reporting period. Thailand reported approximately 9,900 cases, followed by Brazil with about 5,000 cases. Other countries reporting cases included Colombia, the United Kingdom, and Mexico.


Because testing and reporting practices vary widely between countries, official case numbers may not represent the complete level of transmission in every region.


## Understanding COVID-19 and Its Continued Spread


COVID-19 is caused by the SARS-CoV-2 virus, which was first identified in Wuhan, China, in late 2019. The virus primarily spreads through respiratory particles released when infected individuals breathe, speak, cough, or sneeze.


Symptoms can vary widely. Some people experience mild illness, while others may develop more serious complications. Common symptoms can include fever, cough, fatigue, sore throat, and breathing difficulties.


Public health agencies continue to recommend awareness of symptoms, staying home when sick, and following medical guidance based on individual health risks.


## Ongoing Debate Over COVID-19 Origins


The origins of COVID-19 remain a subject of international discussion and scientific investigation. Different researchers and governments have presented different views regarding how the virus first emerged.


The White House has previously stated that COVID-19 likely originated from a laboratory-related incident involving gain-of-function research. The administration has cited concerns regarding research practices and biosafety conditions at laboratories in Wuhan.


Other scientists and organizations have continued to examine multiple possible explanations, including natural transmission from animals to humans. The scientific community has not reached universal agreement on the origins of the virus.


The debate has remained politically and scientifically significant, with discussions involving laboratory safety, international cooperation, and pandemic preparedness.


## US Government Criticism of WHO Pandemic Response


The US government has also criticized aspects of the World Health Organization’s response during the COVID-19 pandemic.


Officials have raised concerns about the organization’s handling of information, international coordination, and proposed changes to global health regulations. The United States previously rejected certain amendments to the WHO’s International Health Regulations, arguing that they could increase international authority during future health emergencies.


Supporters of stronger international health cooperation argue that global coordination is important for responding to future outbreaks. Critics have raised concerns about national decision-making authority and public health policy control.


## Why Monitoring COVID-19 Trends Still Matters


Even with low emergency department numbers, tracking COVID-19 trends remains important. Respiratory viruses can change quickly, and infection patterns can shift depending on seasons, immunity levels, vaccination rates, and emerging variants.


Public health agencies use surveillance systems to identify changes early and provide updated guidance when necessary.


Individuals at higher risk of severe illness, including older adults and people with certain medical conditions, may benefit from staying informed about local infection trends and discussing preventive options with healthcare professionals.


## Conclusion


The latest CDC estimates show COVID-19 infections increasing or likely increasing across 37 US states, signaling continued viral activity during the summer months. However, current healthcare impacts remain low, with COVID-19 representing a small percentage of emergency department visits.


As COVID-19 continues to circulate globally, health officials emphasize the importance of monitoring trends, understanding available information, and relying on trusted medical sources. The ongoing discussion surrounding the virus’s origins and global pandemic response also highlights the importance of transparency, scientific research, and preparedness for future health challenges.
