# Ramipril Shortage in the UK Extended Until October 2026: What Patients Need to Know

The UK has extended the shortage protocol for 1.25mg ramipril capsules until October 2026. Learn why supplies remain limited, what it means for patients, and the steps to take if your medication is affected.


Thousands of people across the United Kingdom who rely on ramipril for managing high blood pressure and other heart conditions continue to face limited medicine supplies. Health authorities have extended the Serious Shortage Protocol (SSP) for 1.25mg ramipril capsules until October 30, 2026, meaning pharmacies will continue dispensing only one month's supply at a time.


The shortage has raised concerns among healthcare professionals, pharmacists, and patients alike. While alternative medicines and formulations are available in some cases, pharmacists warn that ongoing supply issues are placing additional strain on healthcare services and creating uncertainty for people who depend on this commonly prescribed medication.


This article explains what the shortage means, why it is happening, and how patients can manage their prescriptions safely.


## What Is Ramipril?


Ramipril is a prescription medicine widely used to treat several cardiovascular and kidney conditions. It belongs to a group of medicines known as ACE inhibitors, which help relax blood vessels and reduce blood pressure. By lowering blood pressure, ramipril helps reduce the risk of serious complications such as heart attacks, strokes, heart failure, and kidney damage.


It is commonly prescribed for:


- High blood pressure (hypertension)
- Heart failure
- Protection after a heart attack
- Certain kidney conditions, especially in people with diabetes


Because hypertension often develops without noticeable symptoms, many patients rely on medications like ramipril for long term control.


## Why Has the Shortage Been Extended?


The shortage of 1.25mg ramipril capsules was first identified earlier in 2026. Health authorities introduced a Serious Shortage Protocol to help ensure available supplies were distributed fairly among patients.


The protocol has now been extended until the end of October because supply has not yet returned to normal levels.


Under the current arrangements, pharmacists are instructed to provide only one month's supply at a time. This temporary measure is intended to reduce the risk of some patients receiving more medication than they immediately need while others struggle to access treatment.


Although only the 1.25mg capsule strength is affected under the protocol, healthcare professionals continue to monitor supplies of other formulations and alternative medicines.


## Why Are Medicine Shortages Becoming More Common?


Medicine shortages have become increasingly common across many countries over the past few years. According to pharmacy organizations, several factors may contribute to supply disruptions, including:


- Global manufacturing delays
- Limited production capacity
- Increased patient demand
- Supply chain disruptions
- Transportation challenges
- International geopolitical instability
- Shortages of pharmaceutical ingredients


When one medicine becomes difficult to obtain, patients may be switched to alternatives. This can increase demand for substitute medicines and create additional pressure on supplies.


## What Does This Mean for Patients?


For most patients, the shortage does not mean treatment will suddenly stop. Instead, pharmacies are carefully managing available stock to ensure as many people as possible continue receiving their medication.


Patients may notice that:


- They receive only a one month supply.
- Their pharmacy needs extra time to source stock.
- Their doctor may recommend a different strength or formulation.
- Their prescription could be changed to an alternative medication if appropriate.


Healthcare professionals stress that patients should continue taking their medication exactly as prescribed unless advised otherwise by their doctor or pharmacist.


## Do Not Stop Taking Ramipril Without Medical Advice


One of the biggest concerns during medicine shortages is that some patients may consider stopping treatment on their own.


Stopping blood pressure medication without professional guidance can increase the risk of uncontrolled hypertension and serious complications.


These complications may include:


- Heart attack
- Stroke
- Heart failure
- Kidney damage


If your pharmacy cannot immediately supply your medication, speak with the pharmacist or your prescribing clinician. They can discuss suitable alternatives if necessary.


## The Importance of Managing High Blood Pressure


High blood pressure is often called a silent condition because many people experience no symptoms for years.


Without treatment, consistently elevated blood pressure can damage blood vessels and increase the risk of:


- Heart disease
- Stroke
- Kidney disease
- Vision problems
- Dementia


Regular blood pressure monitoring and taking prescribed medicines consistently remain essential for reducing these risks.


## Lifestyle Habits That Support Healthy Blood Pressure


Medication is only one part of blood pressure management. Healthy lifestyle choices can also help improve cardiovascular health.


Some recommended habits include:


### Reduce Salt Intake


Eating less salt may help lower blood pressure. Many processed foods contain significant amounts of sodium, so checking food labels can be beneficial.


### Stay Physically Active


Regular exercise supports heart health and circulation. Walking, cycling, swimming, and other moderate activities can contribute to better blood pressure control.


### Maintain a Healthy Weight


Achieving and maintaining a healthy body weight can reduce strain on the heart and improve overall cardiovascular health.


### Limit Alcohol


Excessive alcohol consumption may increase blood pressure over time. Following recommended drinking guidelines can help reduce health risks.


### Stop Smoking


Smoking damages blood vessels and increases the risk of cardiovascular disease. Quitting smoking offers significant long term health benefits.


### Attend Regular Health Checks


Adults should have their blood pressure checked periodically, particularly if they have risk factors such as obesity, diabetes, or a family history of hypertension.


## What Pharmacists Are Saying


Pharmacy organizations have expressed concern that ongoing medicine shortages continue to create additional workloads for community pharmacies.


Pharmacists often spend considerable time searching for available stock, contacting prescribers, and arranging alternative treatments when medicines become unavailable.


Healthcare professionals continue to encourage improved coordination across the medicine supply chain to reduce disruptions and ensure patients receive timely treatment.


## Frequently Asked Questions


### Is ramipril being recalled?


No. The current issue relates to limited supply of certain 1.25mg capsules rather than a product recall.


### Can I switch to another medicine?


Only your doctor or prescribing clinician should decide whether an alternative medication is appropriate for your condition.


### Should I stockpile ramipril?


No. Patients should collect medicines only as prescribed. Stockpiling may worsen shortages for others.


### What should I do if my pharmacy has no stock?


Speak with your pharmacist. They may be able to source stock from another supplier or contact your prescriber about suitable alternatives.


## Final Thoughts


The extension of the Serious Shortage Protocol for 1.25mg ramipril capsules highlights the ongoing challenges affecting medicine supplies across the healthcare system. While temporary restrictions may be frustrating, they are intended to help ensure fair access for patients who rely on this important medication.


Patients should continue taking their medicine as prescribed, stay in contact with their pharmacy, and seek medical advice before making any changes to their treatment. With careful management and collaboration between healthcare providers, most patients can continue receiving appropriate care while supply issues are addressed.
