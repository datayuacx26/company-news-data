# Rising Ectopic Pregnancy Admissions in England: How Age, Health Inequality and Lifestyle Factors May Be Linked

Ectopic pregnancy cases requiring hospital care in England have increased in recent years, with researchers highlighting several possible reasons behind the trend. A new analysis suggests that changing patterns in maternal age, health inequalities, and access to early pregnancy care may all be contributing factors.


An ectopic pregnancy is a serious pregnancy complication where a fertilised egg implants outside the uterus, most commonly inside a fallopian tube. Because the pregnancy cannot develop safely outside the womb, medical treatment is usually required to prevent potentially dangerous complications.


Recent research from experts at the University of Liverpool has examined hospital admission patterns and found that ectopic pregnancy admissions have risen significantly, particularly since 2021.


## Ectopic Pregnancy Admissions Show Concerning Increase


According to research published in The Lancet Regional Health Europe, hospital admissions linked to ectopic pregnancies increased steadily between 2004 and 2012, with an average yearly rise of around 3 percent.


After remaining relatively stable between 2012 and 2021, admission rates began increasing again. Since 2021, researchers have observed an annual rise of more than 4 percent.


The study suggests that several factors could explain this increase. One important factor is the growing number of women becoming pregnant at older ages. While many people have healthy pregnancies later in life, increasing maternal age is recognised as a factor associated with a higher risk of certain pregnancy complications, including ectopic pregnancy.


Researchers also highlighted changes in hospital care practices and the possible influence of infections and wider health trends.


## Older Maternal Age and Pregnancy Trends


The timing of pregnancy has changed significantly in recent decades. Many women are choosing to start families later due to education, careers, financial considerations, and personal circumstances.


The research found that women aged between 25 and 34 accounted for the largest proportion of ectopic pregnancy hospital admissions between 2013 and 2024, representing approximately 45 to 55 percent of cases each year.


Women aged 35 to 39 accounted for around 20 to 25 percent of admissions, while women aged 20 to 24 contributed approximately 15 to 18 percent.


The most noticeable increase was among women aged 40 and above. Their share of ectopic pregnancy admissions increased from around 7 percent in 2013 to approximately 12 percent in 2024.


Experts suggested that this reflects broader national changes in fertility patterns, where pregnancy is increasingly occurring at older ages compared with previous generations.


## Deprivation and Health Inequality Linked to Higher Risk


The research also identified significant differences between women living in more deprived areas and those in wealthier communities.


Women from the most deprived areas of England experienced more than twice the number of hospital admissions for ectopic pregnancy and miscarriage compared with women from the least deprived areas.


Researchers said this gap may be linked to several health and social factors, including higher rates of smoking, obesity, and reduced access to early maternity services.


For example, smoking rates were reported as significantly higher among women in the most deprived areas compared with those in the least deprived areas. Obesity levels also showed a similar pattern.


These differences highlight the importance of improving access to healthcare, pregnancy support, and preventative health services across all communities.


## Common Symptoms of Ectopic Pregnancy


Recognising the warning signs of an ectopic pregnancy is important because early diagnosis can help prevent serious complications.


Symptoms usually appear between the fourth and twelfth weeks of pregnancy. Common signs may include:


- Vaginal bleeding, which may come and go and can appear dark brown
- Lower abdominal pain, often on one side
- Shoulder tip pain
- Discomfort or pressure when using the toilet
- Pain during bowel movements or urination


In some cases, an ectopic pregnancy can grow large enough to cause the fallopian tube to rupture. This is a medical emergency.


Signs of a possible rupture include:


- Sudden and severe abdominal pain
- Feeling faint or extremely dizzy
- Feeling sick or unwell


Anyone experiencing these emergency symptoms should seek urgent medical help by calling emergency services or attending the nearest emergency department.


## Treatment Options for Ectopic Pregnancy


An ectopic pregnancy cannot continue safely because the fertilised egg is not located inside the uterus. Treatment depends on factors such as the pregnancy size, symptoms, hormone levels, and overall health.


Doctors may recommend medication to stop the pregnancy from developing further. In other situations, surgery may be required, especially if there is a risk of rupture or internal bleeding.


Early diagnosis often provides more treatment options and can reduce the risk of serious complications.


## The Impact of Wider Health Changes


Researchers also examined miscarriage admissions and found different patterns over recent years. Hospital admissions for miscarriage declined between 2018 and 2021, followed by signs of a modest increase after 2021.


Experts suggested that several influences may have affected pregnancy outcomes, including Covid-19 infections, changes in healthcare services during the pandemic, increasing maternal age, rising obesity rates, and broader lifestyle and environmental factors.


These findings show that pregnancy health is influenced by a combination of medical, social, and economic factors.


## Improving Pregnancy Care and Awareness


The rise in ectopic pregnancy admissions highlights the importance of accessible early pregnancy care. Regular medical support, awareness of symptoms, and timely diagnosis can make a major difference in outcomes.


Healthcare systems may need to focus on reducing inequalities by improving access to maternity services, supporting healthier lifestyles, and ensuring women in all communities receive appropriate pregnancy care.


Understanding the factors linked to ectopic pregnancy can help healthcare professionals develop better prevention strategies and improve support for women during early pregnancy.


## Conclusion


Ectopic pregnancy remains one of the most common serious complications during early pregnancy, affecting thousands of women each year in England. Recent increases in hospital admissions may be connected to delayed childbearing, health inequalities, lifestyle factors, and changes in healthcare patterns.


While many pregnancies progress normally, recognising symptoms early and seeking medical advice quickly can help protect maternal health. Continued research and improved access to healthcare services are essential for addressing rising ectopic pregnancy rates and supporting women across England.
