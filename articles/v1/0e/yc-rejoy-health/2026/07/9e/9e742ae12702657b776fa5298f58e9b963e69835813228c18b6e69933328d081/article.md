# Severe Flooding in Northern China Leaves Communities Facing Massive Losses, Unsafe Homes, and Livestock Disaster

## Flood Aftermath Creates New Challenges for Residents in Jilin Province


Communities in northern China’s Jilin Province are facing serious challenges after days of heavy rainfall and flooding damaged homes, destroyed farmland, and caused widespread losses for residents and local businesses.


As floodwaters gradually disappeared from affected areas, residents reported scenes of destruction, including abandoned vehicles, unsafe buildings, damaged agricultural land, and large numbers of dead animals left behind.


The flooding affected areas including Dunhua City and nearby towns, where residents said the impact extended beyond temporary water damage. Many families are now struggling with housing problems, economic losses, and uncertainty about how they will rebuild their lives.


## Heavy Rainfall and Reservoir Discharges Linked to Rising Water Levels


According to local accounts, continuous rainfall began affecting Jilin Province in mid-July 2026. Residents reported that water levels increased rapidly after multiple reservoirs released floodwaters, contributing to severe flooding in several areas.


Jilin Province reportedly contains more than 100 large and medium-sized reservoirs, while Dunhua City has numerous smaller reservoirs spread across surrounding areas.


Residents said flooding began quickly in some villages, leaving little time for preparation. Zhou Huijuan, a villager from Shaheyan Town in Dunhua City, described the event as one of the most difficult experiences she had faced.


She said floodwaters entered the area during the evening, affecting several nearby villages. Many homes in rural parts of northeastern China are single-story structures, making them particularly vulnerable during major floods.


According to her account, some houses were almost completely covered, with only rooftops visible in areas where the water was shallower. Other buildings disappeared beneath deeper floodwaters.


## Homes Declared Unsafe After Floodwaters Recede


Although the water levels eventually dropped, the damage left behind created new dangers for residents.


Many rural houses were reportedly damaged to the point that they could no longer safely be occupied. Structural problems became a major concern as families returned to inspect their properties.


Residents described damaged walls, destroyed household items, and buildings that had become unsafe because of prolonged exposure to floodwater.


Flood damage was also reported in urban areas. Zhou said some parts of Dunhua City experienced severe water accumulation, with some buildings seeing water levels rise significantly inside lower floors.


Affected areas reportedly included Shaheyan Town, Dashitou Town, Heishi Township, Huangnihe Town, and E'mu Town.


For many residents, the biggest concern was not only repairing homes but also recovering their ability to earn income.


## Farmers Face Severe Agricultural Losses


The flooding caused major damage to agricultural production in the region. Residents said crops in fields were destroyed, creating additional financial pressure for farming families.


For many rural households, agriculture and livestock provide their main source of income. Losing crops means losing both immediate earnings and future harvests.


Zhou explained that families with savings may have more ability to recover, while elderly residents and households with limited financial resources could face much greater difficulties.


The disaster has highlighted the economic vulnerability of rural communities when extreme weather events damage homes, farmland, and sources of income at the same time.


## Livestock Farmers Report Millions in Losses


One of the most serious impacts was reported by livestock farmers in Dashitou Town.


Han Chunmei, a livestock farmer in the area, said her farm lost hundreds of pigs during the flooding. She estimated that the financial damage reached several million yuan.


According to Han’s account, rising water levels overwhelmed her farm, causing the deaths of most of her livestock.


She said she had raised more than 800 pigs, but after the flood only a portion of the animals remained. Many piglets also died during the disaster.


After the floodwaters withdrew, she described finding large numbers of animal carcasses around the farm area.


Livestock losses can create long-term problems because farmers must deal not only with lost animals but also with cleanup costs, disease prevention concerns, and the challenge of restarting operations.


## Local Businesses Also Experience Major Damage


The flooding affected more than farms. Residents said many local businesses suffered losses, including factories, restaurants, and other small enterprises.


Dashitou Town reportedly has many livestock operations as well as businesses connected to local industries. Flood damage disrupted economic activity throughout the area.


Residents said damaged equipment, destroyed inventory, and contaminated facilities could make recovery difficult for business owners.


For families who rely on small businesses or farming, rebuilding may require significant financial support.


## Concerns Over Disaster Assistance and Recovery Support


Residents interviewed about the flooding expressed concerns about the availability of disaster assistance.


Han said she had heard reports of government relief funding but claimed she had not personally received direct assistance. She also said she attempted to seek attention online by sharing videos about the damage.


According to her account, she later received requests to remove those posts.


The claims made by individual residents could not be independently verified from the information available in the original report. However, their experiences reflect the frustration some disaster victims may feel when waiting for recovery support after a major emergency.


## Long Recovery Ahead for Flood-Affected Communities


The flooding in Jilin Province has left many residents facing a difficult recovery period.


Repairing damaged homes, restoring farmland, replacing lost livestock, and rebuilding businesses could take considerable time and resources.


For rural communities especially, recovery is closely connected to restoring economic activity. Without adequate support, families who have lost homes and income sources may face continued hardship.


As communities continue cleanup efforts, the long-term impact of the flooding will depend on reconstruction efforts, financial assistance, and measures taken to reduce future flood risks.
