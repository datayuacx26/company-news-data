# Mercury-Free Dental Fillings Gain Momentum as HHS Urges States to Phase Out Amalgam in Medicaid

HHS has urged states to phase out mercury-containing dental fillings in Medicaid programs by 2027. Learn what the policy means, why it matters, and how it could impact patients and providers.


The U.S. Department of Health and Human Services (HHS) has announced a major initiative encouraging states to eliminate the use of mercury-containing dental fillings within Medicaid programs. Through a notification sent by the Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services (CMS), state Medicaid directors have been urged to restrict or discontinue coverage for dental amalgam procedures and transition toward mercury-free alternatives.


The policy aligns with the federal government's broader health objectives and reflects growing concerns about the health and environmental impacts associated with mercury exposure. While the transition is expected to take place over the next few years, states have been encouraged to complete the phase out by 2027.


## What Are Mercury-Containing Dental Fillings?


Dental amalgam has been used for more than 150 years to restore cavities. It is made by combining liquid mercury with metals such as silver, tin, and copper. Because of its durability and affordability, amalgam has long been a popular choice for treating tooth decay.


However, advances in dental technology have made mercury-free materials, particularly resin-based composite fillings, more widely available. These alternatives closely match the natural color of teeth and are increasingly used in modern dentistry.


Health officials say that the availability of these alternatives makes it possible to reduce reliance on mercury-containing materials while maintaining high standards of dental care.


## CMS Encourages States to Update Medicaid Coverage


CMS has advised every State Medicaid Director to review dental coverage policies and consider limiting or ending reimbursement for dental amalgam procedures. Instead, states are encouraged to support the use of resin-based composite fillings whenever appropriate.


The agency specifically referenced dental procedure codes associated with amalgam fillings and suggested replacing them with composite filling codes. According to CMS, most state Medicaid plans do not require formal amendments to implement these policy changes, making the transition administratively simpler.


The guidance does not impose a federal mandate. Instead, it encourages states to voluntarily adopt policies that prioritize mercury-free dental treatments.


## Why Is Mercury a Concern?


Mercury is recognized as a toxic heavy metal. Although dental organizations have generally maintained that dental amalgam is safe for most patients, public health agencies acknowledge that certain populations may be more vulnerable to mercury exposure.


According to the CMS notification, groups that may face increased health risks include:


- Pregnant women
- Women planning pregnancy
- Children younger than six years old
- Individuals with neurological disorders
- People with impaired kidney function
- Other medically vulnerable populations


Health officials believe reducing unnecessary exposure to mercury is an important preventive measure, especially when effective alternatives are available.


## Statements From Federal Health Officials


HHS Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. described the initiative as part of the administration's effort to modernize healthcare practices.


According to Kennedy, mercury-containing dental materials are outdated given the widespread availability of safe and effective mercury-free options. He emphasized that taxpayer-funded healthcare programs should prioritize treatments that minimize exposure to potentially harmful substances.


CMS Administrator Dr. Mehmet Oz also supported the initiative, stating that Americans should have confidence that healthcare systems promote the safest possible treatment options. He said encouraging Medicaid programs to move away from dental amalgam represents an important step toward improving patient care.


## Connection to the Indian Health Service Decision


The CMS notification also reminded state officials that the Indian Health Service previously announced plans to stop using mercury-containing dental amalgam in its facilities by 2027.


Federal officials said this earlier decision reflects increasing awareness of mercury's environmental impact and ongoing efforts to reduce its use throughout healthcare systems.


The Indian Health Service serves millions of American Indians and Alaska Natives, making its policy shift an important milestone in the transition toward mercury-free dentistry.


## International Efforts to Reduce Mercury Use


The United States is also participating in broader international efforts to reduce mercury use.


CMS noted that the country is a party to the Minamata Convention on Mercury, an international treaty designed to protect human health and the environment from mercury pollution.


Under commitments associated with the convention, participating countries aim to phase out the use of dental amalgam by 2034.


Although individual countries may adopt different timelines and implementation strategies, the agreement signals growing global support for reducing mercury use across industries, including healthcare.


## What This Means for Medicaid Patients


For most Medicaid beneficiaries, the transition is expected to increase access to mercury-free fillings rather than reduce dental services.


Patients may increasingly receive composite resin fillings instead of amalgam when receiving treatment for cavities. Composite materials offer several advantages, including:


- Tooth-colored appearance
- Better cosmetic results
- Less removal of healthy tooth structure
- No mercury content


Dentists will continue to evaluate which material is appropriate for each patient's clinical needs.


Patients currently with amalgam fillings generally do not need to have them removed unless recommended by their dentist for medical or dental reasons.


## Potential Impact on Dentists and State Programs


If states choose to follow CMS guidance, dental providers participating in Medicaid may gradually shift toward using composite materials for cavity restorations.


State Medicaid agencies will need to review reimbursement policies, update billing guidance, and communicate changes to participating providers.


Because CMS believes most states can implement these changes without submitting formal State Plan Amendments, officials expect the transition process to be relatively straightforward.


Individual states will ultimately determine how and when they adopt the recommendations.


## The Broader "Make America Healthy Again" Initiative


HHS described the phase out of mercury-containing dental fillings as part of the administration's broader "Make America Healthy Again" (MAHA) agenda.


According to the department, the initiative seeks to modernize healthcare practices, reduce exposure to potentially harmful substances, and promote preventive health measures across federal health programs.


Advancing mercury-free medical interventions has been identified as one component of these broader public health goals.


## Looking Ahead


The CMS guidance marks another step in the ongoing shift away from mercury-containing dental materials in the United States. While states are not federally required to eliminate amalgam coverage immediately, many may begin reviewing their Medicaid dental policies over the coming months.


As mercury-free materials continue to improve in durability, appearance, and affordability, experts expect their use to become increasingly common in both public and private dental care.


The transition also aligns with international environmental commitments and reflects a broader trend toward minimizing unnecessary mercury exposure whenever suitable alternatives exist.


## Frequently Asked Questions


### Why is HHS encouraging states to stop using mercury dental fillings?


HHS says mercury-free alternatives are widely available and reducing mercury exposure may improve health protection, particularly for vulnerable populations.


### Will existing mercury fillings need to be removed?


No. Current guidance focuses on reducing the future use of dental amalgam rather than removing existing fillings. Patients should consult their dentist before considering replacement.


### What are the alternatives to dental amalgam?


The primary alternative is resin-based composite filling material, which is mercury free and matches the natural color of teeth.


### Is the phase out mandatory?


CMS has encouraged states to restrict or end Medicaid coverage for amalgam fillings, but implementation decisions remain with individual state Medicaid programs.


### When could the transition be completed?


CMS has encouraged states to phase out the use of mercury-containing dental fillings by 2027, while international efforts under the Minamata Convention target a broader phase out by 2034.
