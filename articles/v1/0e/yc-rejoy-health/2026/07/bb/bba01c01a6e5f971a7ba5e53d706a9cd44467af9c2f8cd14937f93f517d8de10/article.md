# Grammy-Nominated R&B Singer Nivea Shares Chronic Myeloid Leukemia Diagnosis and Inspires Fans With Positive Update

Despite facing a serious health challenge, the 44-year-old artist says her treatment has been progressing well. She remains optimistic while continuing to focus on her music career, family, and personal growth.


The announcement has prompted an outpouring of support from fans and fellow artists who have followed her career for more than two decades.

## What Is Chronic Myeloid Leukemia?


Chronic myeloid leukemia, commonly called **CML** , is a type of blood cancer that begins in the bone marrow, where blood cells are produced.


Unlike acute forms of leukemia that develop rapidly, chronic myeloid leukemia often progresses slowly. Some individuals may have the disease for months or even years before noticeable symptoms appear.


CML develops when abnormal white blood cells are produced in the bone marrow. These cells do not function properly and can eventually interfere with the production of healthy blood cells.


Modern treatments have significantly improved outcomes for many people living with CML. Targeted therapies have allowed numerous patients to manage the disease successfully for many years while maintaining an active lifestyle.


## Common Symptoms of Leukemia


Symptoms of leukemia can vary depending on the specific type and stage of the disease. Some people experience very few symptoms during the early stages, especially with chronic leukemia.


Common symptoms may include:


- Persistent fatigue
- Frequent infections
- Unexplained weight loss
- Easy bruising or bleeding
- Night sweats
- Fever
- Swollen lymph nodes
- Bone or joint pain


Health experts recommend consulting a medical professional if these symptoms persist or become concerning, as they can also be associated with many other medical conditions.


## What Causes Chronic Myeloid Leukemia?


Researchers continue studying the exact causes of chronic myeloid leukemia.


Medical experts believe the disease develops because of genetic changes within blood-forming cells in the bone marrow. Most cases are not inherited, and there is currently no evidence that lifestyle alone causes CML.


Some factors that may increase risk include exposure to high levels of radiation and certain environmental or medical factors. However, many people diagnosed with CML have no known risk factors.


Advances in research have led to treatments that specifically target the abnormal cells responsible for the disease, dramatically improving survival rates compared with previous decades.
