# Supermarket Pasta Salad Recall: Listeria Warning Issued for ASDA and Morrisons Products

**A food safety warning has been issued over three ready-to-eat pasta products sold in major UK supermarkets after a possible contamination with Listeria monocytogenes was identified. Consumers are being advised not to eat the affected products and to return them for a full refund.**


Shoppers in England, Scotland and Wales are being urged to check their refrigerators after food producer Greencore announced a recall involving pasta salad products sold through **ASDA and Morrisons** .


The recall relates to a possible presence of **Listeria monocytogenes** , a type of bacteria that can cause listeriosis. While listeriosis is often mild in healthy people, the infection can become serious and, in vulnerable individuals, may result in complications including sepsis and meningitis.


The warning is particularly important for pregnant women, older adults, newborn babies and people whose immune systems are weakened.


## Which supermarket products are affected?


Consumers should check the product name, pack size, use-by date and product code before eating any of the recalled items.


The affected products supplied by Greencore are:


- **ASDA Cheese & Tomato Pasta Salad** , 300g, use-by date **10 August** , product code **5063089221990**
- **ASDA Nacho Cheese Pasta Salad** , 500g, use-by date **9 August** , product code **5063717198311**
- **Morrisons Chicken and Bacon Pasta** , 300g, use-by date **7 August** , product code **5010251790587**


The recall applies to products sold in **England, Scotland and Wales** .


Anyone who has one of the affected products at home should **not eat it** , even if it looks and smells normal.


Instead, the product should be returned to the supermarket for a full refund. According to the food safety advice supplied with the recall, customers do not need to provide a receipt.


## Why is Listeria dangerous?


Listeria monocytogenes is a bacterium that can contaminate food. Eating contaminated food can result in an infection known as **listeriosis** .


For many healthy people, listeriosis may cause relatively mild symptoms. These can include a high temperature, muscle aches, nausea, diarrhoea and other flu-like symptoms.


However, the infection can occasionally become invasive. In serious cases, bacteria can spread through the bloodstream or reach the nervous system.


This can lead to potentially life-threatening complications such as **sepsis or meningitis** .


The risk is particularly concerning for people with weakened immune systems. This can include individuals undergoing certain cancer treatments, people with some long-term illnesses and older adults.


Pregnancy is another important risk factor. Listeria infection during pregnancy can have serious consequences for the unborn baby, even when the mother experiences relatively mild symptoms.


## Food can look perfectly normal


One reason food recalls involving Listeria are taken seriously is that contaminated food does not necessarily provide an obvious warning.


Food containing Listeria may not look spoiled, smell unusual or taste different.


That means consumers cannot reliably determine whether a recalled product is safe simply by examining it.


This is particularly relevant to ready-to-eat foods, which may be eaten without an additional cooking step that could kill the bacteria.


Ready-to-eat products can include deli meats, smoked fish, pre-packaged sandwiches and certain prepared salads. Listeria can also be associated with some soft cheeses, pâté and pre-cut fruit.


## Who faces the greatest risk?


Although anyone can potentially develop listeriosis, severe illness is more likely in certain groups.


People considered particularly vulnerable include:


- Pregnant women
- Newborn babies
- Adults aged 65 and over
- People with weakened immune systems
- People receiving chemotherapy or other treatments that affect immunity
- People with certain underlying medical conditions


For these groups, food safety precautions are especially important.


However, the recall applies to everyone who purchased the affected products, regardless of their age or health.


## What should you do if you bought a recalled product?


If you find one of the affected pasta products in your refrigerator, do not eat it.


Check the **product name, pack size, use-by date and product code** against the recall information. If all the details match, the safest course of action is to follow the recall instructions and return the product to the relevant supermarket for a refund.


Consumers should also avoid serving the affected food to other people or using it as an ingredient in another meal.


If you have already eaten one of the recalled products, there is no need to assume that you will become ill. Listeria contamination does not mean every person who consumes a contaminated product will develop listeriosis.


However, anyone who develops symptoms should seek appropriate medical advice, particularly if they belong to a higher-risk group.


## What are the symptoms of listeriosis?


Symptoms can vary considerably from person to person.


Milder illness may resemble flu and can include:


- Fever
- Muscle aches
- Nausea
- Diarrhoea
- Tiredness


More serious infection can cause symptoms associated with bloodstream or nervous-system involvement.


Signs of severe illness should be treated urgently. Symptoms such as severe headache, confusion, a stiff neck, difficulty staying awake or other signs of serious infection can require immediate medical assessment.


Pregnant women should also contact a healthcare professional if they develop symptoms that could be associated with listeriosis.


## How can consumers reduce the risk?


Good food hygiene can reduce the risk of foodborne infections.


Consumers should follow storage instructions on packaging, keep refrigerators at the recommended temperature and observe use-by dates. Hands, utensils and food preparation surfaces should also be kept clean.


People in higher-risk groups may be given additional dietary advice by healthcare professionals, particularly concerning foods that have a higher risk of carrying Listeria.


It is also important to remember that refrigeration does not necessarily eliminate Listeria. The bacterium can survive and grow under refrigerated conditions, which is one reason manufacturers and food safety authorities take contamination concerns seriously.


## Food safety recalls are designed to prevent illness


A product recall does not necessarily mean that a person who consumed the food will become sick.


Instead, recalls are an important precaution used when there is a potential safety problem. Removing affected products from sale and advising consumers not to eat them can help prevent additional exposure.


For shoppers, the most important step is therefore straightforward: **check your refrigerator and do not consume any product covered by the recall.**


The affected pasta salads have specific product codes and use-by dates, so consumers should check those details rather than assuming that every pasta salad sold by ASDA or Morrisons is affected.


## Final takeaway


The Greencore recall highlights the importance of paying attention to supermarket food safety alerts, particularly when ready-to-eat foods are involved.


The three recalled products are the **ASDA Cheese & Tomato Pasta Salad, ASDA Nacho Cheese Pasta Salad and Morrisons Chicken and Bacon Pasta** listed above.


If you have purchased one of the affected products, do not eat it. Return it to the relevant store for a refund and follow official food safety guidance.


People who have eaten a recalled product and subsequently develop symptoms should seek medical advice. Those at increased risk of severe listeriosis should be especially cautious.
