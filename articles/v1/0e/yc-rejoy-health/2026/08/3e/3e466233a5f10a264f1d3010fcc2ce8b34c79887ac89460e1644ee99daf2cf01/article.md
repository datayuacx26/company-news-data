# Cyclospora Health Alert: Experts Warn Contaminated Produce Could Be Linked to Rising US Parasite Outbreak

## Growing concerns over a widespread foodborne illness


A growing public health concern in the United States has prompted warnings that the current Cyclospora outbreak may involve more than just one type of food. Health experts have raised concerns that commonly eaten fruits and vegetables, including blueberries and raspberries, could potentially be connected to infections caused by the parasite responsible for cyclosporiasis.


The warning comes as thousands of people across the country have reported illness linked to Cyclospora, a microscopic parasite that can cause severe digestive symptoms. While health officials initially focused investigations on contaminated shredded lettuce, experts say other fresh produce items may also require closer examination.


Former Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) Director Dr Robert Redfield recently warned that the outbreak could involve a wider range of foods. He highlighted that previous Cyclospora outbreaks have been associated with several types of imported produce, including leafy greens, herbs, berries, and other fresh vegetables.


## What is Cyclospora and why is it dangerous?


Cyclospora cayetanensis is a parasite that affects the digestive system. People become infected when they consume food or water contaminated with microscopic parasite particles.


Unlike some common foodborne illnesses that disappear within a few days, cyclosporiasis can last much longer. Many patients experience symptoms that improve temporarily before returning again.


Common symptoms include:


- Persistent watery diarrhea
- Stomach cramps
- Bloating
- Nausea
- Vomiting
- Loss of appetite
- Fatigue and weakness


In some cases, the illness can continue for several weeks without proper treatment. Severe diarrhea can also lead to dehydration, which may be especially dangerous for older adults, young children, and people with existing health conditions.


## Why experts are looking beyond lettuce


Health investigators originally focused on shredded lettuce after cases were connected to products supplied to restaurants and retailers. However, officials have also investigated other foods because Cyclospora contamination is not limited to one crop.


Fresh produce can become contaminated when it comes into contact with water containing human waste. This can happen during farming, irrigation, harvesting, washing, or processing.


Dr Redfield explained that previous outbreaks have often involved imported produce. He advised consumers to consider locally grown foods when possible while investigations continue.


Health officials have previously linked Cyclospora outbreaks to products such as:


- Cilantro
- Parsley
- Basil
- Raspberries
- Salad mixes
- Other fresh vegetables


Because many fruits and vegetables are eaten raw, there may be fewer opportunities to eliminate the parasite before consumption.


## Could blueberries and raspberries be involved?


Berries have been investigated in previous Cyclospora outbreaks. In the past, contaminated raspberries imported from overseas were linked to cases of cyclosporiasis.


Health experts have noted that berries can be difficult to clean thoroughly because the parasite may remain attached to the surface. While washing produce under running water is recommended, it may not completely remove Cyclospora.


The concern does not mean that all blueberries or raspberries are unsafe. Instead, experts are warning that certain contaminated batches of imported produce could contribute to outbreaks if contamination occurred before reaching consumers.


## Thousands affected as investigations continue


Public health agencies have reported thousands of confirmed Cyclospora cases across the United States, with many more suspected infections still under review.


Investigators face challenges because symptoms often appear one to two weeks after exposure. This delay makes it difficult for patients to remember exactly what foods they consumed and where they purchased them.


Health departments often need to interview patients about recent meals, grocery purchases, and restaurant visits to identify possible sources.


The investigation has involved multiple states, with some areas reporting significantly higher numbers of infections than others.


## How Cyclospora infections are diagnosed and treated


Doctors can diagnose cyclosporiasis by testing stool samples for evidence of the parasite. Because the parasite may not always appear in every sample, healthcare providers may request more than one test.


The infection can usually be treated with prescription antibiotics. A commonly used medication combination containing trimethoprim and sulfamethoxazole is considered effective against Cyclospora.


Without treatment, symptoms may continue for weeks or return repeatedly.


Anyone experiencing prolonged diarrhea, severe stomach problems, signs of dehydration, or worsening symptoms should contact a healthcare professional.


## Safety tips for handling fresh produce


Although health officials continue investigating the outbreak, consumers can take several precautions to reduce their risk of foodborne illness.


Experts recommend:


1. Wash fruits and vegetables thoroughly under running water before eating.
2. Avoid consuming produce that looks damaged, spoiled, or contaminated.
3. Keep fresh produce separate from raw meat and seafood.
4. Wash hands before preparing food.
5. Clean kitchen surfaces and utensils regularly.
6. Consider cooking produce when appropriate, as heat can destroy Cyclospora.


According to health experts, heating food to an internal temperature of around 158°F can eliminate the parasite.


## What consumers should know


The ongoing Cyclospora outbreak highlights the challenges of tracking foodborne illnesses in a global food supply chain. Fresh produce can travel long distances before reaching supermarkets and restaurants, making investigations more complicated.


Experts stress that consumers should not panic or avoid all fruits and vegetables. Produce remains an important part of a healthy diet. Instead, people should follow food safety practices and stay informed about official health alerts.


As investigations continue, public health agencies are working to identify the exact sources of contamination and prevent additional illnesses.
