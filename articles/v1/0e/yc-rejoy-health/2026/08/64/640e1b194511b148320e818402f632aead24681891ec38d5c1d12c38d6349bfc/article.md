# How Heatwaves Affect Your Body and Brain: The Hidden Health Risks of Extreme Temperatures

## Introduction: Heat Is More Than Just Uncomfortable


Heatwaves are becoming a more common part of summer life, bringing rising temperatures, weather warnings, and growing concerns about public health. While many people associate hot weather with sunburn, dehydration, or heat exhaustion, research shows that extreme temperatures can affect the human body in many less obvious ways.


High temperatures can influence energy levels, sleep quality, concentration, mood, and even physical pain. The impact of heat is not limited to very dangerous conditions such as heatstroke. Scientists have found that the body and brain can begin responding negatively even before temperatures reach their highest levels.


Understanding how heat affects us can help people take better precautions and reduce the health risks linked to increasingly frequent heatwaves.


## The Connection Between Heat and Physical Pain


Recent research has highlighted a surprising effect of hot weather: increased reports of physical pain.


A study analysing daily health data from more than two million people in the United States found that rising temperatures were linked with a greater likelihood of people experiencing general physical pain. The strongest effects appeared during very hot conditions, particularly when temperatures reached around 32°C or higher.


The research suggested that certain groups may be more vulnerable to heat-related pain, including middle-aged adults and people facing economic disadvantages.


Pain during hot weather may happen for several reasons. High temperatures can place additional stress on the body, affecting hydration levels, circulation, inflammation, and other internal processes. These changes may worsen existing discomfort or trigger new physical symptoms.


For people living with ongoing conditions such as joint discomfort, muscle problems, or other sources of chronic pain, hot weather may create additional challenges.


## Why Hot Weather Can Make Pain Feel Worse


Scientists believe there are several ways heat can influence the body’s experience of pain.


One important factor is dehydration. When the body loses more fluids through sweating, it can affect normal physical functions. Dehydration may contribute to tiredness, headaches, muscle discomfort, and reduced ability to regulate body temperature.


Heat can also increase physical stress. When the body works harder to stay cool, systems such as the heart and blood vessels face extra pressure. These changes can affect how the body processes discomfort.


Another major factor is inflammation. High temperatures may influence inflammatory responses in the body, which can play a role in worsening certain types of pain.


However, heat does not only affect pain directly. It can also influence several lifestyle factors that are closely connected to how people feel physically.


## The Role of Sleep During Heatwaves


One of the biggest problems during hot weather is poor sleep.


Many people struggle to rest when nighttime temperatures remain high. A warm bedroom, increased sweating, and difficulty cooling down can lead to shorter and less effective sleep.


Sleep and pain are strongly connected. Poor sleep can make people more sensitive to pain, while physical discomfort can make it harder to fall asleep or stay asleep.


This creates a difficult cycle. Hot weather can disturb sleep, poor sleep can increase pain sensitivity, and increased pain can further reduce sleep quality.


Over time, this pattern may affect overall wellbeing, emotional health, productivity, and quality of life.


## How Heat Affects the Brain and Mental Performance


Extreme temperatures do not only affect the body. They can also influence how the brain functions.


Many people report experiencing “brain fog” during hot weather. This may include difficulty concentrating, slower thinking, reduced motivation, and problems making decisions.


Pain can contribute to these effects because the brain uses significant attention and energy to manage discomfort. When the body is dealing with pain, fewer mental resources may be available for tasks that require focus and memory.


Heat can also affect mood. Disrupted sleep, physical discomfort, and reduced ability to complete normal activities can increase stress and frustration.


For students, workers, and older adults, these effects may have a noticeable impact on daily performance.


## Why Heat Can Sometimes Help Pain


Interestingly, heat itself is not always harmful when it comes to pain.


Many people use targeted heat treatments, such as warm compresses or heating pads, to relax muscles and reduce specific discomfort. Controlled heat applied to a small area can have therapeutic benefits.


However, there is a major difference between targeted heat therapy and being exposed to high temperatures for long periods.


A heating pad affects a specific area of the body under controlled conditions. A heatwave affects the entire body and forces multiple systems to work harder to maintain balance.


This is why extreme outdoor temperatures can increase discomfort rather than provide relief.


## The Economic Impact of Heat-Related Health Problems


The effects of extreme heat extend beyond individual health.


Research suggests that increased pain linked to higher temperatures can create significant economic costs through healthcare needs, reduced productivity, and lower quality of life.


As climate change contributes to more frequent and intense heatwaves, communities may face greater challenges in protecting vulnerable populations and adapting to hotter conditions.


The impact may be especially significant in areas where people have limited access to cooling systems, green spaces, or healthcare support.


## How to Protect Your Health During Hot Weather


Although heatwaves can create serious challenges, simple actions can reduce the risks.


Here are practical ways to protect your body during periods of extreme heat:


• Drink enough water throughout the day, especially when spending time outdoors.


• Avoid intense physical activity during the hottest parts of the day.


• Choose cooler times, such as early morning or evening, for exercise and outdoor tasks.


• Spend time in shaded or air-conditioned spaces when temperatures are high.


• Keep sleeping areas as cool as possible to improve rest.


• Check on older adults, children, and people with health conditions who may be more vulnerable to heat.


Small changes can make a significant difference when temperatures rise.


## Conclusion: Heatwaves Are a Growing Health Concern


Heat affects the human body in complex ways. Beyond causing discomfort, high temperatures can influence pain levels, sleep, concentration, mood, and daily performance.


As summers become hotter, recognising these hidden health effects is increasingly important. Protecting ourselves from extreme heat is not only about avoiding immediate dangers such as dehydration or heatstroke. It is also about reducing the wider effects that hot weather can have on physical and mental wellbeing.


By understanding how our bodies respond to heat and taking sensible precautions, we can better manage the challenges of a warming world.
