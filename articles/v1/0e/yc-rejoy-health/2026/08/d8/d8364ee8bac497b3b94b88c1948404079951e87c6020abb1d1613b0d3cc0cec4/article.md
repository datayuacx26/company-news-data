# New York City Braces for Extreme Heat and Strong Storms Across the Northeast

New York City officials are urging residents to take precautions as a period of intense heat, high humidity, thunderstorms, and potential flooding threatens the city and other parts of the Northeast.


City authorities have activated emergency preparations in response to the forecast, with officials encouraging New Yorkers to stay informed, limit exposure to dangerous heat, and prepare for the possibility of severe weather.


The National Weather Service has issued a heat advisory covering all five New York City boroughs. According to information provided by city officials and the weather service, the advisory began Thursday and was scheduled to remain in effect through Friday evening, with the possibility that the heat threat could continue through the weekend and into early next week.


Heat index values, which account for both temperature and humidity, could reach approximately 104 degrees Fahrenheit in New York City. Such conditions can be particularly dangerous because high humidity makes it harder for the human body to cool itself through sweating.


## City Activates Emergency Preparations


New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani and New York City Emergency Management announced preparations ahead of the expected combination of extreme heat and severe storms.


Officials said the city was taking steps to prepare infrastructure and emergency services while also making cooling resources available to residents.


The city's preparations reportedly include efforts to clear catch basins, position emergency personnel, and deploy COOL vans to help check on vulnerable residents, including older adults.


Mamdani urged residents to prepare before severe weather arrives and to pay attention to neighbors who may need assistance.


City officials have also encouraged New Yorkers to check on family members, friends, and neighbors who may be particularly vulnerable to extreme heat. Older adults, people with disabilities, and individuals who lack access to reliable air conditioning can face increased risks during prolonged periods of high temperatures.


## Heat Creates Health Risks


Extreme heat can become hazardous quickly, particularly when temperatures remain high for several days.


High humidity can make conditions feel considerably hotter than the actual air temperature. People who spend extended periods outdoors, exercise during the hottest part of the day, or lack access to cooling facilities may be at greater risk of heat-related illness.


Officials are advising residents to drink plenty of water and reduce unnecessary outdoor activity during periods of peak heat.


Residents are also encouraged to wear appropriate clothing, seek air-conditioned locations when necessary, and avoid leaving children or vulnerable individuals in parked vehicles.


Signs of serious heat-related illness can include confusion, dizziness, difficulty breathing, rapid heartbeat, nausea, and vomiting. Officials have advised people to seek emergency medical assistance if severe symptoms occur.


## Cooling Centers Available


To help residents cope with the heat, New York City officials said hundreds of cooling centers would be available in air-conditioned facilities.


Additional options for cooling down can include public pools, outdoor spray showers, and beaches.


Residents who plan to use public cooling facilities should check current city information for locations, opening hours, and any access requirements before traveling.


City Emergency Management Commissioner Christina Farrell has also encouraged New Yorkers to take simple steps to prepare and to look after people who may be more vulnerable to extreme temperatures.


The availability of cooling resources can be especially important for people living in apartments without adequate air conditioning or ventilation.


## Storms Could Bring Flash Flooding


The heat is not the only concern facing New York City.


According to the information supplied by city officials, the chance of thunderstorms is expected to increase, bringing the possibility of heavy rainfall and flash flooding.


The greatest risk for heavy rainfall was identified for Friday afternoon and evening. However, weather conditions can change quickly, meaning residents should continue monitoring official forecasts and emergency alerts.


Flash flooding can occur rapidly, particularly in areas where drainage systems become overwhelmed by intense rainfall.


Officials have specifically warned people living in basement apartments to prepare for potential flooding. Residents in low-lying locations should remain alert to changing conditions and be prepared to move to safer areas if flooding becomes a threat.


## Travel Could Become Dangerous


Severe storms can create additional hazards for motorists, pedestrians, cyclists, and public transportation users.


Heavy rain can reduce visibility and make roads slippery. Flooded streets can also become difficult or dangerous to navigate.


Residents have been urged to avoid downed power lines and fallen trees following severe weather. Even if a power line does not appear to be active, it can still present a serious danger.


Drivers should avoid attempting to cross flooded roadways. Water can be deeper or moving faster than it appears, and flooded streets can conceal damaged pavement or other hazards.


People traveling during storms should allow additional time, monitor transportation updates, and consider postponing unnecessary travel when conditions become dangerous.


## Threat Extends Beyond New York City


The weather threat is not limited to New York City.


Parts of New York state and several surrounding states are also facing elevated heat risks. Weather advisories have been reported for portions of Connecticut, Massachusetts, Rhode Island, New Jersey, Pennsylvania, and Delaware.


Temperatures in some areas could rise into the upper 90s, while certain locations could potentially experience triple-digit conditions.


The combination of extreme heat, humidity, and thunderstorms means residents throughout the region should monitor local forecasts rather than assuming conditions will be the same everywhere.


Weather systems can affect neighboring communities differently, and storm intensity can vary considerably over relatively short distances.


## How Residents Can Prepare


Residents can take several practical steps before the most dangerous conditions arrive.


First, people should make sure they have access to drinking water and identify a nearby air-conditioned location if their home becomes excessively hot.


Second, residents should check on vulnerable friends, relatives, and neighbors. A simple phone call or visit can help determine whether someone has access to cooling and understands the current weather threat.


Third, people should keep phones charged so they can receive emergency notifications during storms or power outages.


Residents should also secure loose outdoor objects that could be blown around by strong winds and avoid unnecessary outdoor activity during the hottest portion of the day.


Those living in flood-prone or basement-level homes should review their plans before heavy rainfall begins.


## Stay Informed as Conditions Change


Extreme weather forecasts can change as conditions develop. Residents should therefore rely on current information from official weather and emergency management agencies.


Emergency alerts can provide important updates about heat advisories, thunderstorms, flooding, transportation disruptions, and other hazards.


The central message from New York City officials is preparation. Taking precautions before dangerous weather arrives can reduce the risks associated with both extreme heat and severe storms.


For New Yorkers, that means staying hydrated, finding reliable cooling options, checking on vulnerable people, preparing for potential flooding, and exercising caution when traveling.


With the possibility of prolonged heat and additional storms across the Northeast, residents should continue monitoring official forecasts and be ready to adjust their plans as conditions evolve.
