# USDA Issues Health Alert for Costco Burritos Over Undeclared Egg Allergen, Consumers Urged to Check Freezers

The U.S. Department of Agriculture's Food Safety and Inspection Service (FSIS) has issued a public health alert for certain Red's All Natural frozen burritos sold through Costco's same-day delivery service. The warning follows the discovery of a labeling mistake that resulted in products containing egg being packaged inside cartons labeled as a different variety without declaring the allergen.


While no illnesses or injuries have been reported, federal officials are urging consumers to check their freezers and avoid eating the affected products if they have an egg allergy or sensitivity. The alert highlights the ongoing importance of accurate food labeling and allergen awareness, especially for people with food allergies that can trigger severe reactions.


## Why the USDA Issued the Health Alert


According to the FSIS, the issue was identified after a consumer noticed that the contents inside a carton labeled as **Red's Steak Cilantro and Lime Burritos** did not match the packaging. The burritos inside were actually a different product that contained egg, a major food allergen that was not listed on the outer label.


The consumer reported the discrepancy to the manufacturer, Red's All Natural, based in North Sioux City, South Dakota. The company then notified federal regulators, prompting the USDA to issue a public health alert.


Unlike a traditional food recall, the affected products are no longer available for sale. However, officials believe some consumers may still have the burritos stored in their home freezers, creating a potential health risk for individuals with egg allergies.


## Products Included in the Alert


The public health alert applies to:


- Red's All Natural Steak Cilantro and Lime Burritos
- 3-pound, 2-ounce cartons
- Ten individually wrapped burritos per package
- Produced on **June 19, 2026**
- Distributed through Costco's same-day delivery service


Consumers who purchased these burritos are encouraged to inspect the packaging carefully and avoid consuming the product if they suspect it may be affected.


## Understanding the Allergen Risk


Egg is recognized as one of the nine major food allergens that manufacturers are legally required to declare on food labels in the United States.


The Food and Drug Administration identifies the following major allergens:


- Egg
- Milk
- Fish
- Crustacean shellfish
- Tree nuts
- Peanuts
- Wheat
- Soybeans
- Sesame


For most consumers, accidentally eating a product containing egg may not cause any health issues. However, for people with an egg allergy, even small amounts can trigger symptoms ranging from mild skin irritation to severe allergic reactions, including anaphylaxis, which requires immediate medical attention.


Proper allergen labeling plays a critical role in helping consumers make safe food choices and preventing potentially life-threatening reactions.


## No Reported Illnesses So Far


As of the latest update from the USDA, there have been no confirmed reports of illnesses, injuries, hospitalizations, or deaths associated with the mislabeled burritos.


Although no adverse events have been reported, federal officials issued the alert as a precautionary measure to ensure that consumers who may have purchased the product are informed of the potential risk.


## What Consumers Should Do


The FSIS advises consumers who have the affected burritos in their freezer not to eat them if they have concerns about the labeling.


Instead, consumers should:


- Dispose of the product safely.
- Return it to the place of purchase if possible.
- Contact the retailer or manufacturer for additional information if needed.


Anyone who experiences symptoms of an allergic reaction after consuming the product should seek medical attention immediately.


## Why This Is a Public Health Alert Instead of a Recall


Many consumers may wonder why the USDA issued a public health alert rather than a formal recall.


According to federal guidance, recalls generally apply to products that remain available in the marketplace. In this case, the affected burritos are reportedly no longer being sold through Costco's distribution channels.


However, because frozen products often remain in household freezers for weeks or months, the USDA determined that consumers still needed to be notified about the labeling error.


Public health alerts serve as an important tool to inform the public when potentially hazardous food products may still be in consumers' possession.


## Another Burrito Allergen Alert Issued Recently


The Costco-related alert follows another recent allergen-related action involving frozen breakfast burritos.


Fresh & Ready Foods LLC voluntarily recalled two varieties of spicy breakfast burritos after discovering the presence of undeclared soy. The affected products included:


- Sprig & Sprout Spicy Breakfast Burrito
- Fresh and Ready Spicy Breakfast Burrito


These products were distributed through grab-and-go markets and vending machines across several southeastern states.


The U.S. Food and Drug Administration later classified the recall as a **Class I recall** , the agency's highest risk category. A Class I recall indicates there is a reasonable probability that consuming the product could result in serious health consequences or death for individuals with the relevant allergy.


The incident demonstrates how undeclared allergens remain one of the leading causes of food recalls and public health alerts in the United States.


## Importance of Food Allergen Labeling


Food manufacturers are required to accurately list major allergens on product packaging because millions of Americans live with food allergies.


Even a single labeling mistake can expose consumers to unexpected allergens, creating potentially serious health risks. Companies routinely investigate reports of packaging errors and work with federal agencies to notify consumers when necessary.


Consumers with food allergies are encouraged to carefully inspect product labels before consumption and remain alert for food safety announcements from federal agencies.


## Final Thoughts


The USDA's latest public health alert serves as a reminder that labeling accuracy is essential for consumer safety. Although the affected Red's All Natural burritos are no longer available for purchase, individuals who bought them through Costco's same-day delivery service should check their freezers and avoid consuming the products if they have an egg allergy.


With no illnesses reported, the alert is intended to prevent future allergic reactions by ensuring consumers are aware of the packaging error. As food allergy awareness continues to grow, timely communication between manufacturers, regulators, and consumers remains an important part of protecting public health.
