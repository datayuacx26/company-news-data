# RFK Jr Urges Parents to Vaccinate Children Against Measles as US Cases Reach a 35 Year High

The United States is experiencing one of its largest measles outbreaks in decades, prompting an unexpected public statement from Health and Human Services Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. Despite being widely known for years of skepticism toward vaccines, Kennedy has now publicly encouraged parents to ensure their children receive the measles vaccine before returning to school.


His comments have drawn national attention because they appear to mark a significant departure from his previous public messaging on vaccines. At the same time, the statement comes as measles infections continue to rise across the country, raising concerns among public health officials about the future of disease prevention in the United States.


## Measles Cases Continue to Rise Across America


According to the latest public health data, the United States has recorded more than 2,300 confirmed measles cases in 2026. This surpasses the total number reported during the previous year and represents the highest annual case count seen in approximately 35 years.


Health experts warn that the increase is particularly concerning because measles was officially declared eliminated in the United States in 2000. While imported cases have occurred periodically, widespread outbreaks have remained relatively uncommon until recent years.


The growing number of infections has renewed concerns that the country could lose its measles elimination status if transmission continues to spread.


## RFK Jr Calls for Childhood Measles Vaccination


During an interview on CNN's *State of the Union* , Robert F. Kennedy Jr. addressed parents directly regarding measles prevention.


He stated that parents should vaccinate their children against measles and acknowledged that the vaccine is highly effective. Kennedy also referenced the Measles, Mumps, and Rubella (MMR) vaccine, encouraging families to receive the combined immunization that has long been recommended by health authorities.


The remarks surprised many observers because Kennedy has spent years questioning vaccine safety and effectiveness. Before joining the federal government, he became one of the most recognizable voices within the vaccine skepticism movement, often promoting claims that were rejected by scientific research.


## A Major Shift From Previous Vaccine Messaging


Kennedy's latest comments stand in sharp contrast to his previous public positions.


For years, he questioned vaccine safety, criticized immunization programs, and repeatedly raised concerns about childhood vaccines. Many public health organizations argued that his statements contributed to increased vaccine hesitancy among parents.


Even after becoming the nation's top health official, Kennedy often described vaccination as a personal decision rather than strongly encouraging routine immunization.


Because of this history, many medical experts view his latest endorsement of the measles vaccine as a notable shift in messaging, although questions remain about whether it represents a broader change in his views.


## Public Health Experts Welcome the Recommendation


Many health professionals welcomed Kennedy's statement encouraging measles vaccination, regardless of his previous record.


The MMR vaccine has consistently been shown to provide strong protection against measles. According to health authorities, two doses offer approximately 97 percent effectiveness in preventing infection.


Medical experts continue to stress that widespread vaccination remains the most effective way to prevent outbreaks and protect vulnerable populations, including infants, individuals with weakened immune systems, and those who cannot receive vaccines for medical reasons.


Public health officials also emphasize that maintaining high vaccination rates helps create community immunity, reducing opportunities for the virus to spread.


## Critics Say the Message Came Too Late


Although Kennedy's recommendation was welcomed by some experts, others argued that it arrived after months of rapidly increasing infections.


Throughout the outbreak, critics accused the administration of failing to aggressively promote vaccination campaigns. Some believe stronger messaging earlier in the year could have encouraged more families to vaccinate their children before outbreaks expanded across multiple communities.


Health officials continue monitoring dozens of active outbreaks nationwide while urging eligible individuals to receive recommended vaccinations.


## Heated Television Interview Draws Attention


The interview itself also became a major news story.


CNN anchor Dana Bash repeatedly questioned Kennedy about vaccine safety and his responsibility for increasing vaccine hesitancy. During the exchange, Kennedy denied any responsibility for the nationwide rise in measles cases.


Instead, he suggested that vaccine refusal within certain religious communities and broader international health conditions contributed to the outbreaks.


The conversation became increasingly tense as Kennedy criticized Bash's questions and challenged several of her statements. Bash responded by expressing frustration that misinformation surrounding vaccines continues to influence public opinion while preventable diseases are spreading.


The exchange quickly generated widespread discussion across television, social media, and political commentary.


## Additional Health Claims Spark Controversy


Beyond discussing measles, Kennedy also made several claims involving other public health issues.


During the interview, he questioned aspects of COVID-19 vaccination policy and repeated controversial statements regarding conditions such as Lyme disease, respiratory syncytial virus (RSV), and autism.


Many scientists and medical organizations have stated that these claims are not supported by established scientific evidence. Researchers continue to emphasize that decades of peer reviewed studies support the safety and effectiveness of recommended childhood vaccines.


Public health experts caution that inaccurate medical information can reduce public confidence and make disease prevention more difficult.


## Why Vaccination Remains Important


Measles is among the most contagious infectious diseases known.


The virus spreads easily through coughing, sneezing, and airborne particles that can remain in indoor environments for extended periods. Individuals who are not vaccinated face a much higher risk of infection during outbreaks.


Complications can include pneumonia, brain inflammation, hospitalization, permanent disability, and in rare cases, death.


Routine vaccination has dramatically reduced measles cases worldwide and remains one of the most successful public health interventions ever developed.


Health authorities continue recommending that eligible children receive the full MMR vaccination schedule according to established immunization guidelines.


## Looking Ahead


The coming months will likely determine whether the United States can control the current outbreak before schools fully reopen nationwide.


Kennedy's endorsement of measles vaccination may encourage some hesitant parents to reconsider immunization. However, many experts argue that sustained public education, transparent communication, and strong support from healthcare professionals will be necessary to restore confidence and reduce future outbreaks.


As measles cases continue to climb, public health officials remain focused on increasing vaccination coverage and preventing additional community transmission.
