# Bank of America Reports Spending $250 Million Annually on GLP-1 Weight Loss Drugs for Employees

The rapid rise of GLP-1 weight loss medications is transforming employee healthcare benefits across the United States. One of the clearest examples comes from Bank of America, where CEO Brian Moynihan recently revealed that the company now spends more than **$250 million each year** on GLP-1 medications for eligible employees.


The announcement highlights how major employers are increasingly investing in prescription weight loss treatments such as Ozempic and Wegovy. While these medications come with significant costs, many organizations believe they could deliver long term health improvements and reduce future healthcare expenses.


## Why Bank of America Is Investing in GLP-1 Medications


Speaking during a CNBC interview, Bank of America CEO Brian Moynihan explained that spending on GLP-1 drugs has grown from virtually nothing just a few years ago to around $250 million annually.


According to Moynihan, the company's strategy focuses on improving employee health rather than simply covering another prescription benefit. He pointed to growing clinical evidence suggesting that GLP-1 medications may help lower the risk of serious cardiovascular conditions in addition to supporting weight management.


The bank believes that healthier employees could experience fewer chronic illnesses over time, potentially reducing long term healthcare costs while improving overall wellbeing.


## What Are GLP-1 Drugs?


GLP-1 medications belong to a class of drugs originally developed to help people manage Type 2 diabetes. They work by mimicking a naturally occurring hormone that regulates blood sugar levels, slows digestion, and helps people feel full for longer periods.


Over the past several years, these medications have also become widely prescribed for obesity and weight management.


Some of the best known GLP-1 medications include:


- Ozempic
- Wegovy
- Mounjaro
- Zepbound


Many patients using these treatments report significant weight loss when the medications are combined with healthy eating habits and regular physical activity.


## Growing Popularity Across America


The popularity of GLP-1 medications continues to increase rapidly.


Recent Gallup survey findings indicate that approximately 11 percent of American adults now report taking a GLP-1 medication for weight loss. That represents a dramatic increase from only 3 percent in 2024.


This sharp rise reflects growing physician acceptance, expanding patient awareness, and increasing demand for effective obesity treatments.


Healthcare experts have described GLP-1 drugs as one of the most significant advances in obesity treatment in recent decades.


## Employers Are Expanding Coverage


Bank of America is not alone in covering these medications.


According to recent survey data from the International Foundation of Employee Benefit Plans, more than one third of corporate employers now provide insurance coverage for GLP-1 medications used for both diabetes management and weight loss.


Large employers are weighing the immediate expense against potential future savings.


Obesity is associated with several costly chronic conditions, including:


- Heart disease
- High blood pressure
- Type 2 diabetes
- Sleep apnea
- Joint disorders
- Certain forms of cancer


If GLP-1 medications help reduce the occurrence of these illnesses, employers could eventually see lower medical claims and healthier workforces.


## Potential Health Benefits Beyond Weight Loss


Medical researchers continue to study the broader health effects of GLP-1 medications.


Recent clinical research suggests these drugs may provide benefits beyond reducing body weight. Some studies have found they can lower the risk of:


- Heart attacks
- Stroke
- Cardiovascular death
- Heart failure


These findings have increased interest among healthcare providers, insurers, and employers looking for treatments that improve long term health outcomes.


Although research continues, many experts believe these medications may become an important part of chronic disease prevention for eligible patients.


## The Financial Challenge


Despite growing enthusiasm, GLP-1 medications remain expensive.


The annual cost of treatment can reach thousands of dollars per patient depending on insurance coverage and pricing.


Brian Moynihan noted that Bank of America actively negotiates with pharmaceutical companies and healthcare providers to secure lower prices for these medications.


Large employers often have greater bargaining power than individual consumers, allowing them to reduce prescription drug costs through negotiated agreements.


However, medication pricing continues to be one of the biggest concerns for employers considering expanded coverage.


## Why Some Patients Stop Taking GLP-1 Drugs


Although demand continues to rise, not everyone remains on treatment.


Previous survey results published by health policy organization KFF found that cost remains one of the leading reasons patients discontinue GLP-1 medications.


Side effects also play a significant role.


Commonly reported side effects include:


- Nausea
- Vomiting
- Diarrhea
- Constipation
- Stomach discomfort


More serious complications, although less common, may include severe gastrointestinal problems or vision changes. Because of these risks, patients should only use GLP-1 medications under the supervision of a qualified healthcare professional.


## Could Employer Healthcare Benefits Continue to Change?


The rapid adoption of GLP-1 medications may reshape employer healthcare benefits over the coming years.


As more evidence emerges regarding their long term effectiveness, additional companies could decide that covering these medications represents a worthwhile investment in employee health.


At the same time, employers must balance rising prescription drug costs with overall healthcare budgets.


Questions remain about long term medication use, insurance eligibility, patient adherence, and future pricing. Pharmaceutical competition and expanded manufacturing may eventually lower costs, making these treatments more accessible.


For now, Bank of America's substantial annual investment demonstrates just how important GLP-1 medications have become in conversations about employee health benefits.


## Final Thoughts


The growing popularity of GLP-1 medications reflects a broader shift in how obesity is treated within modern healthcare. Instead of focusing solely on lifestyle changes, many healthcare providers and employers are incorporating prescription therapies into comprehensive wellness strategies.


Bank of America's reported annual spending of $250 million illustrates the scale of this transformation. While the financial commitment is considerable, company leadership believes the investment may deliver healthier employees and lower healthcare costs over time.


As research continues and more employers evaluate their benefit programs, GLP-1 medications are likely to remain a major topic in healthcare, insurance, and workplace wellness discussions.
