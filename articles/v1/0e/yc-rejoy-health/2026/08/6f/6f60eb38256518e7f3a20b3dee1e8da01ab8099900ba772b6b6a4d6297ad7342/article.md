# Candida Auris Superbug Cases Rise Across Southern US States, Raising New Health Concerns

## Drug-Resistant Fungus Spreads in Hospitals as Experts Urge Stronger Infection Control Measures


A dangerous drug-resistant fungus known as **Candida auris (C. auris)** is continuing to spread across parts of the United States, with health officials warning that rising cases could create serious challenges for hospitals and long-term care facilities.


New data from the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) show that several states, including Tennessee, Ohio, Louisiana, and Virginia, have experienced significant increases in confirmed Candida auris cases compared with the previous year. The rise has renewed concerns among healthcare professionals because some strains of this fungus are resistant to commonly used antifungal medications.


Candida auris is considered a serious public health threat because it can cause severe infections, including bloodstream infections, wound infections, and ear infections. These infections are especially dangerous for patients who are already vulnerable, including people in intensive care units, long-term acute care hospitals, and nursing facilities requiring specialized medical support.


## What Is Candida Auris and Why Is It Called a Superbug?


Candida auris is a type of yeast that can infect humans. Unlike many common fungal infections, some strains of Candida auris are resistant to multiple antifungal treatments. This resistance makes infections difficult to manage and has led health authorities to classify it as a “superbug.”


Standard antifungal medicines, including fluconazole, may not work against certain strains. When available treatments fail, doctors may have limited options to control the infection.


According to health experts, Candida auris can survive on surfaces and spread easily in healthcare environments. The fungus can remain on medical equipment, hospital beds, and other areas where patients receive care. This makes strict cleaning procedures and infection prevention practices essential.


The CDC has identified Candida auris as an urgent public health threat because of its ability to spread quickly and resist available treatments.


## States Seeing the Largest Increase in Cases


CDC surveillance data show that several states recorded major increases in confirmed Candida auris infections in 2026.


Tennessee experienced one of the sharpest increases, rising from 10 reported cases in 2025 to 283 cases in 2026. Virginia also reported a notable increase, with cases climbing from 77 in 2025 to 132 in 2026.


Ohio and Louisiana each recorded an increase of 76 confirmed cases during the same period.


While some states reported only a small number of infections, others accounted for a much larger share of overall cases. California and Texas continue to report some of the highest numbers of confirmed clinical cases, with 873 and 433 cases respectively for the week ending July 25.


Other states reporting high numbers include Tennessee with 283 cases, Ohio with 279 cases, and Georgia with 193 cases.


Health officials say the geographic expansion of Candida auris infections highlights the need for improved monitoring, faster testing, and stronger infection control measures.


## Who Is Most at Risk From Candida Auris?


Candida auris does not typically affect healthy individuals. The greatest risks are seen among people who are already seriously ill or have weakened immune systems.


Patients most vulnerable to severe infections often include:


• People staying in intensive care units
• Individuals using ventilators
• Patients with long hospital stays
• Older adults with multiple health conditions
• People receiving care in long-term medical facilities


CDC data from 2022 through 2024 showed that most clinical cases involved patients older than 45 years of age. Many infections occurred among patients receiving acute medical care.


Because symptoms can sometimes be difficult to recognize, early detection is extremely important. Healthcare workers rely on laboratory testing to identify Candida auris and prevent further transmission.


## Why Early Detection and Prevention Matter


Experts emphasize that infection prevention remains the most effective tool against Candida auris.


CDC officials recommend several measures to reduce the spread of the fungus, including:


• Rapid identification of infected patients
• Proper cleaning and disinfection of healthcare environments
• Screening high-risk patients
• Following infection control procedures
• Expanding laboratory testing capabilities


Dr. Tom Chiller, chief of the CDC’s Mycotic Diseases Branch, has emphasized that early detection and infection control in hospitals and nursing facilities are critical to limiting transmission.


Healthcare experts also warn that delays in diagnosis can allow Candida auris to spread to other patients and healthcare workers.


## Rising Cases Highlight Global Health Challenges


Candida auris was first identified in the United States in 2016. Since then, CDC officials have monitored its spread and classified it as an emerging health threat.


The growth of drug-resistant infections is part of a larger global concern involving antimicrobial resistance. When bacteria, viruses, and fungi become resistant to available medicines, treating infections becomes more complicated.


Researchers continue to study Candida auris to better understand how it spreads, how resistance develops, and how healthcare systems can respond more effectively.


## What Can People Do to Stay Protected?


For most people, Candida auris does not represent an immediate everyday risk. However, individuals with loved ones in hospitals or long-term care facilities should be aware of infection prevention practices.


Visitors and caregivers can help reduce risks by:


• Following hospital hygiene guidelines
• Washing hands regularly
• Avoiding unnecessary contact with medical equipment
• Asking healthcare providers about infection prevention steps


Patients and families should also communicate openly with medical teams if they have concerns about possible infections or exposure risks.


## Conclusion


The increase in Candida auris cases across several U.S. states is creating renewed concern among health officials. The fungus remains a major challenge because of its ability to resist some antifungal treatments and spread within healthcare settings.


Although researchers and public health agencies continue working to control its impact, experts say prevention, early diagnosis, and strict infection control remain the strongest defenses.


As drug-resistant infections continue to emerge worldwide, monitoring threats like Candida auris will remain an important priority for healthcare systems and communities.
