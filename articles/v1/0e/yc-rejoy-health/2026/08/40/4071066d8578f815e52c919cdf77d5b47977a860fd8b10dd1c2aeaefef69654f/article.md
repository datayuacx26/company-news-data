# Premium Dog Food Recalled After Reports of Serious Eye Problems in Pets

A premium fresh dog food company has recalled its products in the UK after receiving dozens of reports that dogs developed symptoms associated with dry eye disease. The company, Years, said it had identified a possible connection between the condition and buckwheat, an ingredient used in some of its meals.


The recall has caused concern among pet owners who use fresh food subscriptions because they often choose these products with the expectation that they offer a high-quality alternative to conventional pet food.


## Years identifies possible link to eye problems


Years, a subscription-based dog food company that provides fresh meals designed around individual pets, contacted customers with an urgent warning after investigating reports of eye problems in dogs.


According to information released by the company, 57 suspected cases of dry eye had been reported by 16 August. Years said the figure represented around 0.1 per cent of its customer base.


The company explained that it had begun receiving multiple reports in late July involving dogs that had developed sudden dry eye symptoms affecting both eyes. An investigation was subsequently launched on 1 August.


Years said investigators had found a potential link involving buckwheat, which is a common and approved ingredient used in its recipes. The company has not stated that buckwheat has definitively been proven to cause the condition. Instead, it has described the relationship as a potential link while its investigation continues.


As a precaution, customers have been advised to stop feeding affected Years products to their pets.


The company has also paused subscriptions for an initial six-week period. During that time, Years said it plans to prepare its meals using quinoa instead of buckwheat.


## What symptoms should dog owners look for?


Years has advised customers to pay close attention to their dogs' eyes and seek veterinary advice if they notice anything unusual.


Possible symptoms can include redness, cloudy-looking eyes, frequent blinking and squinting. Dogs may also repeatedly paw at their eyes or develop thick discharge.


Other signs can include general discomfort around the eyes. Some dogs may appear reluctant to open their eyes or may become noticeably distressed when their eyes are touched or exposed to bright light.


These symptoms can have several possible causes, so owners should not automatically assume that food is responsible. Eye conditions can require prompt veterinary attention, particularly when there is pain, discharge, cloudiness or a sudden change in vision.


Years has encouraged customers who notice these symptoms to contact a vet promptly and tell the veterinary practice about the product recall.


## One owner's experience highlights the concern


Among those affected is Cookie, a six-year-old spaniel cross owned by David Felton in north London.


Felton told the Press Association that Cookie began experiencing eye problems in April after eating Years food for approximately two and a half years.


Initially, the problem was diagnosed as a corneal abrasion, which is a scratch or injury affecting the surface of the eye. After around two weeks of treatment without sufficient improvement, Cookie was referred to a specialist.


The specialist subsequently diagnosed bilateral dry eye disease, meaning the condition was affecting both eyes.


Felton said his dog had experienced considerable discomfort and required several different treatments. He estimated that veterinary care had cost approximately £1,500.


Cookie is currently receiving treatment twice each day, including a steroid-based eye treatment alongside drops and gel intended to provide relief.


Felton stopped feeding Cookie Years products after receiving the company's recall communication.


For pet owners, cases such as Cookie's can be particularly worrying because food-related problems are not always immediately obvious. A dog may have been eating the same food for months or even years before symptoms appear, making it difficult for an owner to identify a possible connection.


## Why the recall matters for fresh pet food customers


The incident also raises broader questions about the expectations surrounding premium pet food.


Fresh dog food has become increasingly popular among owners looking for meals made with recognisable ingredients. Companies in this sector often promote recipes designed around individual dogs, with meals prepared and delivered directly to customers.


Years markets meals featuring recipes such as pork with apple, turkey with cranberry and steak with kidney. The company's positioning is aimed at owners who want an alternative to traditional dried or processed pet food.


The recall demonstrates that premium positioning does not eliminate the possibility of an ingredient-related problem.


At the same time, it is important to distinguish between a reported association and a confirmed cause. The available information indicates that Years is investigating a possible relationship between buckwheat and dry eye symptoms. It does not establish that buckwheat is definitively responsible for every reported case.


The company's decision to remove buckwheat from its meals while the investigation continues is therefore a precautionary measure.


## What should Years customers do now?


Customers who have received the recall notification should follow the company's instructions and stop feeding the affected Years food to their animals.


Owners should also monitor their pets for changes involving the eyes. If a dog develops redness, discharge, squinting, excessive blinking, cloudiness or obvious discomfort, professional veterinary advice should be sought.


Pet owners should keep details of the food their animal has been eating, including product names and dates where possible. This information may help a vet assess the situation.


Owners who believe their dog has developed an adverse reaction should also report the problem to Years, particularly if they have not already done so. Keeping veterinary records and receipts may also be useful where treatment has been required.


Years has said it intends to publish the findings of its investigation once it has more information.


## A developing situation for pet owners


The recall is likely to remain under close attention as Years continues investigating the reports. With approximately 40,000 customers, the company's warning has the potential to affect a significant number of households, even though the reported cases represent a small proportion of its customer base.


For now, the central message is one of caution. Owners should not panic, but they should take unusual eye symptoms seriously and seek professional advice rather than attempting to diagnose the cause themselves.


The situation also serves as a reminder that even carefully selected pet foods can sometimes be associated with unexpected adverse reactions. Companies may need to investigate individual ingredients when patterns of illness emerge, while pet owners should remain alert to changes in their animals' health.


As the investigation progresses, further information should help determine whether buckwheat is genuinely responsible for the reported cases or whether another factor is involved.
