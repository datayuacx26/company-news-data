# Deli Meal Recall: Kroger, Publix and Other Stores Affected Across Three States

Consumers in several states are being urged to check their refrigerators and freezers after federal food safety officials announced a recall involving thousands of pounds of prepared deli meals.


Ukrop’s Homestyle Foods, a Richmond, Virginia-based food producer, has recalled approximately 22,880 pounds of prepared food products because some items may contain small pieces of metal. The recalled foods were sold through several major retailers, including Kroger, Publix, Food Lion and Wegmans.


The recall affects multiple prepared foods made by the company, including baked spaghetti, chicken cobbler and bread pudding products. While officials have not reported any injuries connected to the recall, consumers should avoid eating products that may be affected.


## Why Were the Deli Meals Recalled?


According to information released by federal food safety authorities, the potential contamination involves small pieces of aluminum.


The issue was identified after a consumer reportedly discovered a metal fragment in one of the company's fully cooked products. During a subsequent review, officials determined that a small piece of aluminum had been found in a baking pan used to prepare several products.


As a precaution, Ukrop’s Homestyle Foods withdrew six varieties of food from the marketplace.


The recalled products were prepared at Ukrop’s Kitchen between July 1 and July 30, 2026. Some of the products carried expiration dates extending from July 8 through Aug. 5.


Although no injuries have been reported, the presence of metal in ready-to-eat food can create a potentially serious safety concern.


## Which Products Are Included?


The recall covers six different product varieties and package sizes.


Consumers should check whether they purchased any of the following:


- 14.8-ounce packages of baked spaghetti
- 11.6-ounce packages of chicken cobbler
- 13.8-ounce bread pudding with vanilla sauce
- 48-ounce bulk packages of baked spaghetti
- 48-ounce bulk packages of chicken cobbler
- 4-pound bread pudding with vanilla sauce kits


The products were sold through retail stores in Virginia, North Carolina and West Virginia. Some were also distributed through military retail channels and online sales.


Other retailers associated with the recalled products include Harris Teeter, Ukrop’s Market Hall and Libbie Market.


Because recalled foods can remain in household refrigerators or freezers after retailers remove them from shelves, consumers should check products they purchased during the affected period.


## What Should Consumers Do?


Anyone who believes they have one of the recalled products should not eat it.


Instead, consumers should follow the recall instructions provided by the manufacturer or federal food safety authorities. If the product is still in the refrigerator or freezer, consumers should handle it carefully and dispose of it according to the applicable recall guidance.


People who have questions about a recalled item can compare the product information on the packaging with the details provided by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration or the U.S. Department of Agriculture's Food Safety and Inspection Service.


Consumers should also remember that the absence of an injury report does not mean a recalled product is safe to consume. Foreign objects such as metal fragments can cause injuries if swallowed.


## Where Were the Products Sold?


The recalled foods reached consumers through several grocery and retail channels.


Major retailers named in connection with the recall include Kroger, Publix, Food Lion and Wegmans. The products were also sold through Harris Teeter and other retail outlets.


Some affected products were distributed to military retail locations, while certain items were available through online sales.


The geographic scope includes Virginia, North Carolina and West Virginia.


For shoppers who regularly purchase prepared meals from grocery store deli sections, this recall serves as another reminder to pay attention to food safety announcements. Product recalls can sometimes involve specific package sizes, production dates or expiration dates, so consumers should review the full recall information instead of assuming every product from a particular brand is affected.


## Federal Officials Classify the Recall as Class I


The USDA's Food Safety and Inspection Service classified the recall as a Class I event.


Under the federal food recall system, a Class I classification is used when there is a reasonable possibility that consuming or using the affected product could result in serious health consequences or death.


That classification does not mean that every person who consumed a recalled product will become ill or injured. Rather, it reflects the potential severity of the identified hazard and helps determine the urgency of the recall.


In this case, the concern centers on possible metal fragments in prepared food.


## Company Takes Action After Complaint


The company said consumer safety was its highest priority and indicated that the supplier of the baking pans had been notified.


According to federal information, steps were also initiated to remove potentially affected pan lots from distribution.


The recall followed the discovery of a metal piece in a cooked product, highlighting how consumer complaints can play an important role in identifying potential food safety problems.


Consumers who notice an unusual object in packaged or prepared food should avoid consuming the product and report the issue through the appropriate food safety or regulatory channels.


## Another Food Recall Involves Bacon


The Ukrop’s recall is not the only recent food safety action involving products distributed in the United States.


Federal officials also announced a separate recall involving more than 12,000 pounds of smoked bacon products manufactured by Maple Leaf Foods Inc.


That recall involved not-ready-to-eat bacon products that entered the United States without undergoing a required import reinspection.


The affected bacon included certain 12-ounce packages of Royale Natural Applewood Smoked All Natural Uncured Bacon and TOP VALU Uncured Hardwood Smoked Bacon.


The products were distributed to Grocery Outlet locations in Idaho, Oregon and Washington.


Unlike the Ukrop’s recall, the bacon issue was related to federal import inspection requirements rather than the presence of a foreign object.


## What This Means for Shoppers


Food recalls can happen for a variety of reasons, including contamination, undeclared allergens, manufacturing problems, labeling errors and foreign objects.


For consumers, the safest approach is to take recall notices seriously and check product information carefully.


If you recently purchased prepared meals from a major grocery chain, look at the product name, package size and date information before eating it. When a recall applies, do not rely on appearance or smell to determine whether the food is safe.


Prepared foods can also be shared with family members or stored in another household's refrigerator, so consumers may want to notify anyone who could have received an affected product.


The Ukrop’s recall is a useful reminder that food safety checks continue after products reach stores. A single consumer complaint can lead to an investigation and, when necessary, a broader recall designed to protect the public.
