# Taylor Farms Recalls Salsa and Guacamole Over Salmonella Risk: What Consumers Need to Know

Taylor Farms has announced a voluntary recall of more than a dozen prepared food products because of concerns about possible **Salmonella contamination** linked to jalapeño peppers used in some of the products.


The recall affects prepared foods including **salsa, guacamole and other items containing jalapeños** that were distributed to retailers. Consumers who recently purchased Taylor Farms products are being advised to check their refrigerators and packaging carefully to determine whether any recalled items are included.


The company said it initiated the voluntary recall after its jalapeño supplier, Coast Citrus Distributors, became involved in a separate recall concerning peppers potentially connected to a Salmonella outbreak.


## Taylor Farms Salsa and Guacamole Recall


Taylor Farms, a California-based food supplier, said it voluntarily recalled products containing the affected jalapeño peppers. The company also said it has stopped sourcing peppers from the farmer in Sinaloa, Mexico, who supplied the peppers to Coast Citrus Distributors.


The supplier said it is looking for alternative sources while the investigation and food safety review continue.


The recall is significant because Taylor Farms products are widely distributed and may be sold through multiple retail and food service channels. Consumers should therefore rely on the specific product information and recall notices rather than assuming that every Taylor Farms salsa or guacamole product is affected.


Not every product made by Taylor Farms has been recalled.


Consumers should check product names, package information, dates, lot codes and other identifying details against the official recall information before deciding whether a product should be discarded.


## Why Were the Products Recalled?


The Taylor Farms recall is connected to concerns about jalapeño peppers that may have been contaminated with Salmonella.


U.S. health authorities are investigating a Salmonella outbreak associated with jalapeño peppers imported from Mexico. According to the information provided in the original report, the outbreak had been associated with hundreds of illnesses across multiple states and dozens of hospitalizations.


Because the recalled Taylor Farms foods contain jalapeños from the affected supply chain, the company chose to remove potentially affected products from the marketplace as a precaution.


Taylor Farms said it was not aware of any reported illnesses associated with the products included in its voluntary recall.


A voluntary recall does not necessarily mean that every item included in the recall is contaminated. Instead, companies may remove products from sale when there is a reasonable food safety concern or when an ingredient used in the products is connected to a contamination investigation.


## What Taylor Farms Products Could Be Affected?


The recall involves more than a dozen prepared food products containing jalapeños. These include products such as **salsas and guacamole** , although consumers should not assume that every variety or package size is part of the recall.


The affected products may have been distributed through retailers and other food channels. Packaging information is particularly important because similar products can have different production dates, lot codes or distribution details.


If you have Taylor Farms salsa, guacamole or another prepared food containing jalapeños in your refrigerator, check the product against the company's official recall information.


Do not rely solely on the appearance or smell of the food. Food contaminated with Salmonella may look and smell normal.


## What Should Consumers Do?


Consumers who discover that they have a recalled Taylor Farms product should avoid eating it.


The safest approach is to follow the instructions provided in the official recall notice. Depending on the recall instructions, consumers may be told to discard the product or return it to the retailer where it was purchased.


If you are unsure whether a product is included, keep it separate from other food until you can verify the recall information.


Consumers should also wash their hands after handling potentially contaminated food packaging. If the recalled food has been stored in a refrigerator or on surfaces that come into contact with other foods, cleaning those areas can help reduce the possibility of cross-contamination.


Families should take extra care when handling recalled food around young children, older adults and people with weakened immune systems.


## What Is Salmonella?


Salmonella is a type of bacteria that can cause a foodborne illness known as salmonellosis.


Common symptoms can include **diarrhea, fever and stomach cramps** . Symptoms may develop after consuming contaminated food, although the timing and severity can vary between individuals.


Some people recover without medical treatment, while others can develop more serious illness. Young children, older adults and people with weakened immune systems can be at greater risk of severe complications.


Anyone who becomes seriously ill after consuming a recalled food should seek appropriate medical advice.


It is important to remember that having eaten a recalled product does not automatically mean that someone will become sick. The recall is a precaution designed to reduce the risk of exposure to potentially contaminated food.


## Restaurant Industry Also Affected


The jalapeño recall has implications beyond grocery store products.


Restaurants that received peppers from the affected supply chain have also taken steps to prevent potentially contaminated ingredients from reaching customers.


Restaurant chains including Chipotle Mexican Grill and QDOBA reportedly received affected peppers and stopped using them after the recall was announced.


Food suppliers, restaurants and retailers routinely rely on detailed ingredient tracking to identify products that may have used a particular ingredient. When a supplier issues a recall, businesses can use those records to identify potentially affected products and remove them from sale.


## Why Food Recalls Matter


Food recalls are an important part of the food safety system. They allow manufacturers, suppliers, retailers and health authorities to respond when an ingredient or finished product may pose a health risk.


A recall can occur because of confirmed contamination, a potential contamination risk, labeling problems, undeclared allergens or other safety concerns.


In this case, the Taylor Farms recall highlights how a problem involving a single ingredient can affect multiple prepared foods. Jalapeños may be used in several recipes, meaning one recalled ingredient can result in multiple finished products being removed from stores.


For consumers, checking food recall information regularly can be a simple way to reduce exposure to potentially unsafe products.


## Taylor Farms Recall: Key Takeaways


The Taylor Farms recall involves more than a dozen prepared food products containing jalapeños, including certain salsa and guacamole products.


The recall is connected to jalapeño peppers supplied through Coast Citrus Distributors and a broader Salmonella investigation involving peppers imported from Mexico.


Taylor Farms said it voluntarily recalled the affected products and stopped sourcing peppers from the implicated farmer while seeking alternative suppliers.


Consumers should check their Taylor Farms products against the official recall details and avoid eating products confirmed to be part of the recall.


At this time, Taylor Farms said it was unaware of reported illnesses connected specifically to the voluntarily recalled products.


As investigations continue, consumers should pay attention to updated recall information from food safety authorities, retailers and the manufacturer.
