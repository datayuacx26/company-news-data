# FDA Recalls Vegan Cheddar Puffs Over Undeclared Milk Allergy Risk

The U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) has announced a recall involving Frankie’s Organic Plant Based Vegan Cheddar Puffs after the company discovered that the snack may contain undeclared milk. The recall affects a limited quantity of the product distributed in Washington, Florida, and South Carolina.


According to an FDA public health alert issued on Aug. 12, 2026, Frankie’s Organic of Quebec, Canada, withdrew 94 cases of its 4-ounce bags of Frankie’s Organic Plant-Based Vegan Cheddar Puffs from the market. The concern centers on an ingredient used in the seasoning that contains milk, an allergen that was not properly declared on the product label.


For most consumers, consuming the recalled snack is not expected to cause a health problem related to the undeclared ingredient. However, people who have a milk allergy could experience an allergic reaction after eating the product. For that reason, consumers with milk allergies are urged to check their packages carefully and avoid eating the affected product.


## What Prompted the Recall?


The recall followed two complaints involving allergic reactions to related products sold in Canada. During an investigation, the company identified the likely source of the problem as an ingredient supplied for use in the seasoning.


Federal officials said the root cause was traced to the supplier level. The seasoning ingredient contained milk, but the presence of the allergen was not appropriately declared for the affected product.


Undeclared allergens are a significant food safety concern because consumers with allergies often rely on product labels to determine whether a food is safe for them to eat. Even a relatively small amount of an allergen can cause symptoms in a sensitive individual.


Frankie’s Organic said consumer safety remains its highest priority and that it is taking corrective action following the investigation.


## Which Product Is Affected?


The recalled item is Frankie’s Organic Plant-Based Vegan Cheddar Puffs, sold in 4-ounce bags.


Consumers who have purchased the product should look for the following identifying information:


- Product: Frankie’s Organic Plant-Based Vegan Cheddar Puffs
- Package size: 4 ounces
- UPC: 816929000089
- Best-by or expiration date: May 16, 2027
- Recall quantity: 94 cases


The product was distributed through Earth Fare stores in Florida and South Carolina, PCC Markets in Washington state, and selected independent retailers in the affected regions.


Consumers who do not have a milk allergy may not face the same allergy-related risk, but anyone who is unsure whether a package is part of the recall should avoid consuming it until the product information has been verified.


## Why Undeclared Milk Matters


Milk is one of the major food allergens recognized by the FDA. Food manufacturers are required to clearly identify major allergens on food labels so consumers can make informed decisions about what they eat.


The FDA recognizes nine major food allergens in the United States. They are milk, eggs, fish, crustacean shellfish, tree nuts, peanuts, wheat, soybeans, and sesame.


An allergic reaction can vary from mild to severe. Possible symptoms can include hives, swelling, stomach discomfort, vomiting, breathing difficulties, or other reactions. Severe allergic reactions can become medical emergencies.


People with known food allergies should therefore treat recall notices seriously, even when a recalled food appears safe based on its name or packaging. A product described as plant-based or vegan may still present a risk when an allergen is accidentally introduced through an ingredient or manufacturing process.


## What Consumers Should Do


Consumers who have the recalled cheddar puffs should check the package information before eating the product. People with a milk allergy should not consume the affected snack.


If you have purchased a recalled package, follow the instructions provided by the retailer or manufacturer regarding returns or disposal. Consumers can also review the FDA recall notice for the latest official information.


Anyone who develops symptoms after consuming a food containing an undeclared allergen should seek appropriate medical attention, particularly if symptoms are severe or rapidly worsening.


Consumers should also remember that food recalls can be updated as investigations continue. Checking the official FDA notice is one way to confirm whether additional products, lot numbers, or distribution areas have been added.


## A Broader Reminder About Food Recalls


The Frankie’s Organic recall is another reminder that food safety problems can occur at different points in the supply chain. Ingredients may pass through several suppliers and manufacturers before they become part of a finished product.


In this case, the investigation identified an ingredient at the seasoning supplier level as the source of the undeclared milk. Such incidents demonstrate why manufacturers maintain quality-control procedures and why regulatory agencies monitor products after they reach the market.


The FDA has also recently been involved in other snack-related recall actions. In a separate case, Utz Quality Foods voluntarily recalled limited varieties of Zapp’s and Dirty brand potato chips after concerns that Salmonella could be present in a seasoning containing dry milk powder.


The two situations involve different hazards. The Frankie’s Organic recall concerns an undeclared milk allergen, while the Utz recall involved potential Salmonella contamination. Both cases nevertheless highlight the importance of following official food safety notices.


## What to Check Before Eating Recalled Food


Consumers can take several simple steps when they hear about a food recall. First, check the product name and package size. Next, compare the UPC, lot information, and expiration or best-by date with the details listed in the official recall notice.


It is also important not to rely solely on a product's description. A food marketed as plant-based, vegan, dairy-free, or free from a particular allergen may still become subject to an allergy alert if an ingredient is incorrectly labeled or unintentionally introduced.


When a recall affects someone with a known food allergy, the safest approach is to avoid the product until its status is confirmed.
