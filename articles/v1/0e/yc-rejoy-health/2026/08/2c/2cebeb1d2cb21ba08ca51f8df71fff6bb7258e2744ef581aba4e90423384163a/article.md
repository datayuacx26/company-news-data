# Why Trump Chooses McDonald’s While Traveling, According to RFK Jr.

President Donald Trump’s long-standing preference for McDonald’s has once again drawn attention after Health and Human Services Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. explained why the president reportedly turns to major fast-food chains when traveling.


According to Kennedy, Trump’s choice is not simply about taste or convenience. He said Trump prefers familiar national restaurant chains while on the road because he is concerned about getting food poisoning from unfamiliar establishments.


The comments have renewed interest in **Donald Trump’s diet, McDonald’s, food safety, and the Make America Healthy Again initiative** , particularly because Kennedy is leading the federal government’s health agenda.


## RFK Jr. Explains Trump’s McDonald’s Habit


Kennedy discussed Trump’s eating habits during an appearance on the *Net Positive* podcast published in August 2026. He said Trump generally enjoys high-quality food but changes his approach when traveling.


Kennedy described seeing large quantities of McDonald’s food aboard the presidential aircraft and said Trump has confidence in national chains because he believes their standardized operations provide a more predictable food experience.


The explanation adds another dimension to Trump’s well-known relationship with McDonald’s. The president has spoken publicly for years about his preference for the restaurant, while photographs and public appearances have frequently shown him with fast food.


Trump’s choice has often been associated with convenience and familiarity. Kennedy’s latest comments suggest that food safety concerns may also influence those decisions.


That distinction is important. The claim is about Trump’s personal reasoning, rather than evidence that McDonald’s is universally safer than independent restaurants. Food safety can vary between individual restaurants, food suppliers, preparation practices, and local conditions.


## Trump’s Diet Changes When He Is at Home


Kennedy also drew a contrast between Trump’s meals while traveling and what he eats at the White House or Mar-a-Lago.


According to Kennedy, Trump has access to fresh food, chefs, and locally sourced produce when he is at his regular residences. In those settings, Kennedy characterized the president’s diet as considerably better than what he consumes while traveling.


The contrast helps explain why Kennedy’s comments should not necessarily be interpreted as saying Trump eats fast food for every meal.


Instead, the description suggests that travel creates a different set of priorities. Trump reportedly values consistency and familiarity when he is away from his usual kitchens and food suppliers.


For a president who spends substantial time traveling for political events, diplomatic meetings and official duties, food logistics can also be more complicated than they are for an ordinary traveler.


## Trump’s Long History With McDonald’s


Donald Trump’s McDonald’s habit is hardly new.


During his 2024 presidential campaign, Trump made headlines when he worked behind the counter at a McDonald’s restaurant in Pennsylvania. His campaign appearances have also frequently incorporated fast food, reinforcing his public image as a longtime fan of the chain.


Trump has previously pointed to consistency and cleanliness as reasons for his preference. His affinity for McDonald’s has become a recognizable part of his public persona, much like his well-known preference for Diet Coke.


Kennedy has previously discussed Trump’s consumption of fast food, sweets and Diet Coke. Earlier comments also highlighted a distinction between Trump’s travel habits and his meals at the White House and Mar-a-Lago.


The latest remarks therefore fit into a broader pattern Kennedy has described rather than representing a completely new claim about the president’s eating habits.


## Why the Comments Matter for the MAHA Agenda


Kennedy’s comments are particularly notable because of his leadership of the **Make America Healthy Again, or MAHA, initiative** .


The administration has emphasized reducing reliance on highly processed foods and encouraging Americans to consume what it describes as “real food.” The federal health agenda has also focused heavily on chronic disease, nutrition, food ingredients and the American food supply.


The official HHS MAHA website says the initiative is aimed at addressing what the department describes as the country’s chronic disease crisis. HHS currently cites several health statistics, including its claim that 40 percent of the U.S. population is dangerously close to becoming diabetic or prediabetic.


The department also highlights efforts involving food policy, nutrition and artificial food dyes. Its MAHA information page says 40 percent of the U.S. food and beverage industry has committed to removing artificial dyes, while the department also reports that multiple states have adopted policies restricting certain food purchases through SNAP.


That creates an obvious point of public interest when the health secretary discusses the eating habits of the president he serves.


Kennedy, however, has indicated that he does not intend to act as a personal “scold” toward Trump. His comments have generally presented the president’s food choices as a personal matter while emphasizing the broader policy goal of improving Americans’ diets.


## Food Safety Versus Nutrition


The Trump-McDonald’s discussion also raises an interesting distinction between **food safety and nutritional quality** .


A restaurant meal can be prepared safely while still being high in calories, sodium, saturated fat or other nutrients that consumers may want to limit depending on their individual dietary needs.


Likewise, choosing a familiar chain because of concerns about food poisoning does not automatically make every menu item a healthy choice.


Food safety involves preventing contamination and illness. Nutrition involves the overall composition of a person’s diet. The two issues overlap, but they are not the same.


For travelers, the practical lesson is that familiarity may influence restaurant choices, but consumers should still consider balanced meals, portion sizes and dietary variety.


## Kennedy Promotes Cooking at Home


During his podcast appearance, Kennedy also discussed the importance of preparing food at home. He argued that Americans should regain confidence in basic cooking and make healthier meals without spending excessive amounts of money.


That message aligns with the broader MAHA emphasis on changing eating habits rather than focusing exclusively on individual medical treatments.


The administration has increasingly framed nutrition as an important part of addressing chronic disease. HHS says its MAHA initiative is intended to identify and address root causes of chronic health problems.


Whether those policies ultimately produce measurable improvements in national health remains an ongoing subject of debate.


## What RFK Jr.’s Comments Reveal About Trump’s Diet


Kennedy’s latest comments offer a relatively simple explanation for one of Trump’s most recognizable eating habits.


When traveling, Trump reportedly prefers national chains such as McDonald’s because he trusts their consistency and worries about becoming sick from unfamiliar food. At the White House and Mar-a-Lago, Kennedy says the president has access to fresher foods and locally sourced ingredients.


The story is therefore less about one fast-food restaurant and more about the complicated relationship between **food safety, convenience, personal preference and nutrition** .


Trump’s affection for McDonald’s has been public for decades, but Kennedy’s explanation provides additional insight into why the president continues to choose the chain while traveling.


It also highlights an unusual contrast within the administration. The president is known for fast food and Diet Coke, while his health secretary is leading an initiative focused on improving American nutrition and reducing chronic disease.


For now, Kennedy says he is not trying to police Trump’s personal menu. Instead, his focus remains on the broader MAHA effort to encourage Americans to rethink what they eat and how their food is produced.
