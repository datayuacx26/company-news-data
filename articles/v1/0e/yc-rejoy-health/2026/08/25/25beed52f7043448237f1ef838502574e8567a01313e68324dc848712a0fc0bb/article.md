# Why Are There Fewer Wasps in the UK This Summer? Scientists Explain the Possible Reasons

Discover why wasp numbers appear unusually low in the UK this summer. Experts explain how extreme weather, heatwaves, food shortages, and climate change may be affecting wasp populations.


## The Strange Disappearance of Wasps During the UK Summer


For many people, summer means outdoor meals, garden activities, festivals, and warmer evenings. It also usually means dealing with unwanted visitors such as wasps. These insects are often associated with picnic interruptions, buzzing around drinks, or building nests in roofs and sheds.


However, this summer has been different. Across many parts of the UK, people are noticing fewer wasps than usual. Even pest control professionals have reported a quieter season, with fewer calls from homeowners needing wasp nests removed.


For scientists who study insects, this decline is more than just a pleasant change for people who dislike wasps. Wasps play an important role in nature, and a significant drop in their numbers could affect ecosystems.


## Early Signs Suggest a Difficult Year for Wasps


A lack of wasp sightings alone does not confirm a population decline. Reliable monitoring data is still needed, including results from national surveys that track wasp numbers. However, several observations suggest that wasps may have experienced a challenging year.


In previous warm summers, wasp populations often increase rapidly. Colonies grow larger, worker numbers rise, and wasps become more visible as they search for food. This often leads to reports of large numbers of wasps around homes, gardens, and outdoor events.


This year, the usual summer increase appears to be missing. Researchers and pest controllers have noticed fewer nests and fewer wasp-related complaints compared with previous years.


## How Weather Changes May Have Affected Wasp Colonies


The development of a wasp colony begins in spring when young queens emerge from hibernation. These queens must survive alone while building nests, laying eggs, collecting food, and raising the first generation of workers.


A warm and dry spring usually creates good conditions for queen wasps. Plenty of flowers provide nectar, while other insects provide food for developing larvae. Once the first workers emerge, the colony can expand quickly because the queen no longer has to complete all tasks herself.


However, this year’s weather patterns may have created problems. Although the spring began with warm and dry conditions, it was followed by sudden cold periods, damp weather, and unusual temperature changes.


These unpredictable conditions may have caused some queens to abandon nests or fail to survive. As a result, fewer colonies may have developed successfully.


## Extreme Heat Could Be Putting Wasps Under Pressure


The UK has experienced increasingly frequent heatwaves, and extreme temperatures can create challenges for many insects.


Every insect species has a limit known as its critical thermal maximum. This is the temperature beyond which the insect struggles to survive. When temperatures become too high, insects can suffer from dehydration, reduced movement, and changes in behaviour.


Wasps are generally considered resilient insects. They have adapted to many environments around the world and can regulate nest temperatures through behaviours such as wing fanning. Their nest structures also provide some protection from outside temperature changes.


Despite these adaptations, extreme heat may still affect them. Very high ground temperatures can create dangerous conditions, especially for insects that spend time searching for food outside the nest.


Repeated heatwaves may reduce wasp activity, affect their ability to find food, and make it harder for colonies to develop normally.


## Why Are Some Wasps Smaller Than Usual?


Another unusual observation this summer is that many of the wasps being seen appear smaller than expected.


The size of a wasp is mainly determined during its larval stage. Adult wasps do not grow after they leave their pupal stage. The amount and quality of food available while they develop affects their final size.


Early-season worker wasps are often smaller because the queen has limited resources while raising them alone. Later in the summer, colonies usually become stronger, food supplies improve, and larger workers are produced.


If small wasps are still common later in the season, it may suggest that colonies have struggled to access enough food. Limited insect prey or fewer available resources could reduce larval nutrition.


Heat may also play a role. Research suggests that insects developing in warmer conditions can mature more quickly but may reach adulthood at a smaller size.


Scientists have observed that climate change may already be influencing insect body sizes. The smaller wasps seen this year could be another example of how changing temperatures are affecting wildlife.


## Why Wasps Matter to the Environment


Although wasps are often unpopular, they provide valuable services to ecosystems.


They are natural pest controllers because they feed on many insects that can damage plants and crops. They also contribute to pollination while visiting flowers for nectar. In addition, wasps help with decomposition by breaking down organic material.


A major decline in wasp numbers could therefore have wider effects on gardens, farms, and natural habitats.


Understanding changes in insect populations is important because insects are indicators of environmental health. When insect numbers change, it can reveal problems linked to climate, habitat loss, pollution, or food availability.


## How People Can Help Wasps


If you see a wasp near your garden or picnic, it may not be looking to attack. Often, it is simply searching for food.


During periods when natural food sources are limited, wasps may be attracted to human food because they need protein to feed their young or sugar for their own energy.


Instead of immediately trying to remove a wasp, people can observe its behaviour. Avoid sudden movements, keep food covered when possible, and consider planting flowers that provide nectar throughout the year.


Supporting biodiversity in gardens can help many insects, including wasps, bees, and butterflies.


## Final Thoughts


The unusually low number of wasps in the UK this summer may be linked to a combination of factors, including unstable spring weather, repeated heatwaves, reduced food availability, and rising temperatures.


More research and monitoring will be needed to confirm exactly what has happened to wasp populations. However, the situation highlights a wider issue: climate change is influencing even familiar species that many people take for granted.


While fewer wasps may make outdoor activities more enjoyable, their absence could signal changes happening across the natural world.
