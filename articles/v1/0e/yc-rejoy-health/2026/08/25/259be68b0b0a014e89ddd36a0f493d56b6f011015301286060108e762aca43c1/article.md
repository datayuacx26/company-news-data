# Typhoon Dolphin Strikes China’s Eastern Coast as More Than 1 Million People Evacuate

Typhoon Dolphin has made landfall on China’s eastern coast, bringing powerful winds, intense rainfall and a heightened threat of flooding and landslides. The storm, described by authorities as the strongest tropical cyclone to affect China so far this year, has prompted large-scale evacuations and widespread disruption across several provinces and major cities.


The typhoon reached the coast near Yuhuan in Zhejiang province at around 5:30 p.m. local time on Sunday, according to China’s meteorological authorities. At the time of landfall, Dolphin had maximum sustained winds of about 42 meters per second, or approximately 151 kilometers per hour.


The storm has also affected parts of Japan, where it moved through the southern Okinawa prefecture before reaching China. Six people were reportedly injured in Okinawa, while more than 50,000 buildings experienced power outages.


## More Than 1 Million People Evacuated


The arrival of Typhoon Dolphin has triggered one of the largest emergency responses associated with a storm in the region this year. More than 1 million people have been moved away from potentially dangerous areas as authorities prepare for flooding, landslides and other storm-related hazards.


Zhejiang province has been among the hardest-hit areas in terms of preparations. In Wenzhou, more than 900,000 residents were relocated, while officials established more than 1,000 emergency shelters.


Authorities in Fujian province also evacuated nearly 99,000 people from locations considered vulnerable to the storm. The province raised its emergency response to Level III as Dolphin approached, while Zhejiang, Shanghai and Jiangsu implemented similar emergency measures.


The evacuations have focused particularly on coastal communities, construction sites, mountainous locations and areas exposed to landslides or sudden flooding.


Officials have also instructed offshore workers to move to safer locations and ordered vessels to return to port. Reservoirs, mountain streams, tourist destinations and areas with a history of landslides have been placed under closer observation.


## Heavy Rain Raises Flooding Concerns


While strong winds are an immediate danger, authorities are also closely monitoring the enormous amount of rainfall expected from Typhoon Dolphin.


Forecasters expect heavy precipitation through August 10 across a broad area of eastern and central China. The affected regions include Zhejiang, Shanghai, northern Fujian, northeastern Jiangxi, central and southern Anhui, and large parts of Jiangsu.


Some areas of central and eastern Zhejiang could receive between 250 and 500 millimeters of rain, according to weather forecasts. Such rainfall over a short period can overwhelm drainage systems, cause rivers to rise rapidly and increase the likelihood of flash floods.


The country’s water resources authorities have warned that several major rivers, including the Qiantang, Yong, Jiao and Shuiyang, could experience significant rises in water levels.


Smaller rivers in areas receiving the heaviest rainfall may also rise above warning thresholds.


The combination of saturated ground, steep terrain and continued rainfall could make mountainous communities particularly vulnerable. Authorities have issued geological disaster warnings for parts of Zhejiang and have urged residents in areas covered by red-level mountain torrent warnings to follow evacuation instructions.


## Shanghai Faces Major Travel Disruption


China’s largest commercial city has also experienced substantial disruption as the typhoon approaches.


Shanghai cancelled around 1,500 flights, according to flight-tracking data provider VariFlight. At least 30,300 people in the city were evacuated as authorities prepared for the storm.


The cancellations left thousands of travelers dealing with uncertain schedules and alternative arrangements.


One traveler from Guangdong, identified by his surname Chen, said his family was stranded in Shanghai after their scheduled Sunday flight was cancelled because of the typhoon.


The family was reportedly rebooked on a flight scheduled for Monday night, although Chen said there was uncertainty over whether the replacement flight would operate.


For people living in southern China, typhoons are a familiar part of the summer weather pattern. Chen noted that residents from Guangdong may view a storm of this strength differently because of their experience with tropical cyclones.


However, the potentially prolonged rainfall associated with Dolphin remains a significant concern even if wind speeds decline after landfall.


## Ferry Services and Offshore Construction Suspended


Authorities in Fujian have taken additional measures to reduce risks along the coast.


Maritime officials suspended 55 coastal passenger ferry routes as the storm approached. All 115 offshore construction projects in the province were also halted, while 290 construction vessels were moved into sheltered waters.


These precautions are intended to reduce the risk of accidents caused by high winds, large waves and rapidly changing sea conditions.


Construction projects and offshore operations can be especially vulnerable during tropical cyclones because equipment, workers and vessels may be exposed to severe weather.


Officials have therefore prioritized moving people and equipment away from areas where conditions could deteriorate rapidly.


## Where Will Typhoon Dolphin Go Next?


Following landfall in Zhejiang, Typhoon Dolphin is expected to move westward across China. Forecasters anticipate that the system will slow down as it reaches central and southwestern parts of the country before gradually weakening.


However, weakening winds do not necessarily mean the danger will immediately disappear.


A slower-moving tropical system can continue producing substantial rainfall even after losing some of its strength. This could extend the period of flooding and landslide risks across areas already affected by heavy precipitation.


China’s National Meteorological Centre has warned that the storm’s movement could therefore prolong hazardous weather conditions.


Residents in affected provinces are being urged to monitor official weather updates, follow evacuation orders and avoid unnecessary travel during periods of severe weather.


## Typhoon Dolphin Highlights China’s Summer Storm Risks


Typhoon Dolphin is the latest major weather event to demonstrate the risks posed by tropical cyclones in East Asia during the summer months.


China’s eastern coastline contains major cities, industrial centers, ports and densely populated communities. Severe storms can therefore have consequences far beyond direct wind damage, affecting aviation, shipping, construction, tourism and everyday travel.


The extensive evacuations and transportation restrictions surrounding Dolphin demonstrate how authorities are attempting to limit casualties before the worst conditions arrive.


For residents and travelers, the biggest risks over the coming days are expected to include flooding, landslides, flash floods and transportation delays.


As the storm moves inland, weather conditions are likely to change rapidly. People in affected regions should rely on local government announcements and official meteorological information when making decisions about travel or evacuation.
