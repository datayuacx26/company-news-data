# Rising Infant Formula Prices Are Putting UK Babies and Families Under Pressure

Rising infant formula prices are creating financial and emotional pressure for UK families. Explore the risks, causes and possible solutions.


The rising cost of living has affected almost every household in the UK, but for families with babies, some expenses cannot simply be reduced or removed. Infant formula is one of them. For parents who rely on formula to feed their babies, rising prices can create serious financial pressure and, in some circumstances, may affect infant safety and parental wellbeing.


Recent research into the experiences of parents struggling to afford infant formula highlights the scale of the problem. Families have described cutting back on their own food, reducing heating, taking on debt and returning to work earlier than planned simply to make sure they could continue buying milk for their babies.


## Why is infant formula becoming so expensive?


Food prices have risen sharply in recent years. UK food price inflation reached 19.1% in March 2023, but infant formula prices increased even more rapidly.


Between 2021 and 2023, the average price of infant formula increased by around 22%. More recently, the average price of an 800g tin reportedly rose by 46p between May 2025 and May 2026, reaching approximately £12.45.


For families with limited incomes, even relatively small increases can make a significant difference. Babies need to be fed regularly, meaning parents cannot simply buy less formula or postpone the purchase until prices fall.


This makes infant formula different from many other household products. Families can often switch from expensive foods to cheaper alternatives, but parents cannot compromise on their baby's nutritional needs.


## The hidden impact on parents


The financial consequences extend beyond household budgets. Parents struggling to afford formula can experience anxiety, guilt, stress and isolation.


Research involving 281 parents found that financial difficulties associated with infant formula affected mental and physical wellbeing, relationships and family life. Some mothers described feeling that they had failed their babies, particularly when they had originally hoped to breastfeed but had been unable to do so.


This emotional pressure can be particularly difficult during the postnatal period, when parents may already be coping with sleep deprivation, major lifestyle changes and the demands of caring for a newborn.


The problem is therefore not simply about the price printed on a formula tin. It is also about whether parents feel secure, supported and able to provide basic necessities for their children.


## What happens when families cannot afford formula?


When money becomes extremely tight, some parents may resort to unsafe feeding practices. These can include using less formula powder than recommended or attempting to make supplies last longer.


Such practices can be dangerous because infant formula must be prepared according to the manufacturer's instructions. Adding too much water can reduce the nutritional value of a feed and may put a baby at risk.


Other parents may keep prepared formula for longer than recommended or obtain opened formula products through informal online marketplaces. These approaches can increase the risk of contamination and illness.


Importantly, these situations should not automatically be interpreted as parental neglect. They can be symptoms of financial hardship and a lack of accessible support. Parents experiencing this kind of pressure need practical help rather than judgement.


## Does a more expensive formula mean better nutrition?


Another important issue is the difference between formula brands and prices.


Marketing can create the impression that premium or more expensive infant formula provides significant advantages for a baby's health or development. However, infant formula is tightly regulated, and products must meet nutritional requirements.


There can be substantial differences in retail prices, with some products costing considerably more than others. Yet higher prices do not necessarily mean that a formula provides superior nutritional benefits.


For parents already worried about money, understanding this distinction can help reduce unnecessary financial pressure. Healthcare professionals can also provide guidance about appropriate infant feeding choices.


## Could government action reduce formula costs?


The UK Competition and Markets Authority has examined the infant formula market, including manufacturer and retailer margins. Its findings have contributed to wider debate about whether the current market provides sufficient protection for families.


One potential approach is stronger regulation of prices or profits. Other countries have considered measures designed to limit the cost of essential infant formula products.


A broader strategy could include better financial support for low-income families, improved access to breastfeeding assistance for parents who want to breastfeed, and stronger protections for working parents.


Support should also recognise that not every parent can breastfeed, and some parents who want to breastfeed may not receive adequate practical assistance. Formula feeding should not be treated as a failure. The priority should be ensuring that every baby can be safely fed.


## Building a better support system


Addressing infant formula affordability requires more than one solution. Families need reliable ways to obtain formula when they experience an unexpected financial crisis.


Benefits and food support schemes can play an important role, but eligibility requirements may leave some working families without adequate assistance. Parents who earn slightly above a threshold can still struggle significantly with housing, childcare, energy and food costs.


Better information is also essential. Parents should feel able to speak honestly with health visitors, midwives, GPs and other professionals if they are struggling to afford formula. Early support may prevent families from reaching a crisis point.


Ultimately, infant nutrition should not depend on whether a household has enough money left at the end of the month.


## What parents can do if formula becomes unaffordable


Parents who are struggling to buy enough formula should seek help as soon as possible rather than changing the preparation instructions or reducing feeds.


In England and Wales, eligible families may be able to receive support through the Healthy Start scheme. Families in Scotland may qualify for Best Start Foods. Local services, health professionals and food support organisations may also be able to identify additional sources of assistance.


Parents should never feel ashamed about asking for help. Financial hardship can affect families from many different backgrounds, including households where one or both parents are working.


## Conclusion


The rising cost of infant formula is more than a consumer price issue. It is a family wellbeing issue and, in some circumstances, a child health and safety concern.


When parents have to choose between buying formula and paying for food, heating or housing, the problem extends beyond individual household budgeting. It raises questions about how society supports families during the earliest and most demanding stages of parenthood.


Reducing the cost of essential formula, improving financial assistance and ensuring accessible feeding support could help relieve pressure on families. Most importantly, parents should be able to feed their babies safely without experiencing shame, fear or financial crisis.
