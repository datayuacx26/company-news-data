# Urgent Dunnes Stores Recall: 43 Products Recalled Over Salmonella Risk

Irish shoppers are being urged to check their fridges and cupboards after more than 40 products sold at Dunnes Stores were recalled over concerns about possible Salmonella contamination.


The recall affects a range of Baxter & Greene products, including sandwiches, wraps, salads, lunchboxes and sausage rolls. Several products sold through selected Dunnes Stores salad bars are also included in the warning.


The Food Safety Authority of Ireland (FSAI) issued the recall after Salmonella was detected in sesame seeds used in the affected products. Customers have been advised not to eat any of the recalled products and to return them to a Dunnes Stores location for a refund.


## Dunnes Stores recall affects 43 products


The food recall covers 43 Baxter & Greene products containing sesame seeds. The affected foods include both pre-packed items and products sold from salad bars.


Among the recalled foods are popular ready-to-eat options such as turkey and ham dinners, vegetarian nut loaf, gourmet sausage rolls, sandwiches, wraps and several salads.


Some salmon products and lunchboxes are also included in the recall.


The affected products have specific use-by dates, meaning shoppers should check the packaging carefully before consuming anything from the Baxter & Greene range.


Dunnes Stores has removed the affected products from sale, while recall notices are being displayed at points of purchase.

## Why have the Dunnes products been recalled?


The recall was issued because Salmonella was detected in sesame seeds used in the affected foods.


Salmonella is a type of bacteria that can cause food poisoning. People who become infected can develop symptoms including diarrhoea, fever, headache and abdominal cramps.


According to the information supplied with the recall, symptoms commonly develop between 12 and 36 hours after infection, although the incubation period can range from six to 72 hours.


Most people recover within four to seven days, but Salmonella infection can sometimes lead to more serious illness.


Young children, older people and people with weakened immune systems are among those who can be at greater risk of serious complications.


## What should shoppers do?


Anyone who has purchased one of the recalled Dunnes Stores Baxter & Greene products should check the product name and use-by date carefully.


If the product matches the recall, customers should not eat it.


Instead, the recalled item should be returned to any Dunnes Stores location for a refund. Customers are advised not to consume the food even if it looks or smells normal, as contaminated food may not show obvious signs of spoilage.


People who have already eaten an affected product should be aware of the possible symptoms of Salmonella infection. Anyone who becomes unwell should seek appropriate medical advice, particularly if symptoms are severe, persistent or affect someone considered vulnerable to foodborne illness.


## Sesame seed products also recalled


The wider issue also involves specific batches of sesame seed products.


The recalled products include Gem Sesame Seeds 1kg with batch codes 155 and 177, as well as several Sysco Sesame Seeds 1kg batches.


The affected Sysco batches include codes 147, 169, 189 and 203. Sesame Seeds 25kg lots 32308 and 32704 are also included, along with Sowans Sesame Seeds 100g batch code 155.


The affected sesame seed products have best-before dates extending into 2027.


Wholesalers and distributors have been asked to contact affected customers and recall the implicated batches. Caterers have also been advised not to use the affected sesame seed products.


## Dunnes Stores customers urged to check food at home


The recall is particularly important for customers who regularly buy ready-made lunches, salads and other convenience foods from Dunnes Stores.


Because many of the recalled items are ready-to-eat foods, customers may have purchased them for immediate consumption or stored them in a refrigerator at home.


Anyone who has a recalled product should avoid eating it and follow the refund instructions.


Food recalls can be updated as further information becomes available, so consumers should continue checking official food safety announcements if they are concerned about a particular product.
