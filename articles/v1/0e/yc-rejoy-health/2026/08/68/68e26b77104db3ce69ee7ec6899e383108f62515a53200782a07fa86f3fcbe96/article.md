# UK Salmonella Outbreak: London and Yorkshire Among Regions Reporting the Most Cases

A growing **salmonella outbreak in the UK** has prompted an investigation by public health authorities after more than 200 infections were recorded over the past year. Health officials are examining a possible connection with eggs imported from outside the UK, while warning the public about the symptoms of salmonella and ways to reduce the risk of infection.


According to figures reported by the UK Health Security Agency (UKHSA), 207 cases were identified between 11 August 2025 and 1 August 2026. Most of the infections were recorded in England, with smaller numbers reported in Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland.


The outbreak has also raised concerns because a significant proportion of those infected required hospital treatment.


## Which parts of the UK have recorded the most salmonella cases?


England has recorded the overwhelming majority of cases linked to the current outbreak.


Of the 207 infections identified during the period, **199 were reported in England** . Scotland recorded six cases, while Wales and Northern Ireland each reported one.


Within England, **London recorded the highest number of confirmed cases, with 47 infections** . Yorkshire and the Humber followed with 38 cases.


The figures indicate that infections have been detected across different parts of the country rather than being restricted to one local area. People affected have ranged from babies to adults aged up to 90, highlighting that salmonella can affect people across a wide age range.


Health authorities continue to investigate the source and possible routes of transmission.


## What is behind the UK salmonella outbreak?


The UKHSA is investigating a particular strain of salmonella involved in the outbreak. Officials are working with the Food Standards Agency and other public health organisations to establish how the infections are connected.


One possibility being examined is a link to **eggs imported from outside the UK** .


Salmonella bacteria can contaminate food and cause illness when contaminated products are eaten or handled. Although food is the most common route of infection, transmission between people can occur in some circumstances.


The investigation is therefore important because identifying the source can help authorities determine whether additional food safety measures, product recalls or public warnings are necessary.


At this stage, consumers should follow official food safety advice and pay attention to any product warnings or recalls issued by the relevant authorities.


## What are the symptoms of salmonella?


**Salmonella symptoms** can vary from person to person, but common signs include diarrhoea, stomach cramps, vomiting, fever and a general feeling of being unwell.


Symptoms can begin within several hours or a few days after exposure to contaminated food, although the timing can vary.


For many otherwise healthy people, salmonella infection is unpleasant but temporary. Recovery commonly takes around four to seven days, with maintaining adequate fluid intake being particularly important because diarrhoea and vomiting can lead to dehydration.


However, salmonella can become more serious in some people.


Young children, older adults and people with weakened immune systems can face a greater risk of complications. In the current UK outbreak, **38 per cent of identified cases required hospital admission** , according to the information reported by The Independent.


Two people also developed bloodstream infections described as potentially life-threatening.


These figures make the current investigation particularly significant, even though salmonella infections are often relatively mild.


## How can salmonella be prevented?


There are several straightforward measures households can take to reduce the risk of salmonella and other foodborne illnesses.


**Wash your hands thoroughly:** Hands should be washed with soap and water after using the toilet and before preparing or eating food. Hand hygiene is particularly important when handling raw ingredients.


**Cook food properly:** Thorough cooking can help destroy harmful bacteria. Follow cooking instructions carefully, particularly when preparing foods that may carry a higher risk of contamination.


**Take care when handling eggs:** Consumers should follow current food safety guidance when storing, preparing and cooking eggs. If an egg or other food product has been officially recalled, it should not be consumed.


**Avoid preparing food for others when ill:** People experiencing symptoms such as diarrhoea or vomiting should avoid handling food for other people wherever possible. This can help limit the spread of infection.


**Prevent cross-contamination:** Raw foods should be kept separate from ready-to-eat foods, while utensils, chopping boards and kitchen surfaces should be cleaned appropriately after food preparation.


These precautions can help reduce the risk of salmonella as well as other forms of food poisoning.


## Why the outbreak is being closely monitored


The number of cases alone does not provide the full picture of the outbreak. Health authorities are also examining the strain involved, where infections have occurred, when people became ill and whether individual cases share a common source.


The relatively high hospitalisation rate has added to concerns surrounding the outbreak.


Investigators are working to establish whether imported eggs or another food source could explain the infections. Establishing a clear connection can be challenging because people may consume contaminated food days before symptoms develop, making it difficult to immediately identify the product responsible.


The investigation will therefore rely on epidemiological information, laboratory testing and food safety investigations.


## What should people do if they develop symptoms?


Anyone experiencing symptoms consistent with salmonella should take steps to avoid spreading the infection. This includes practising careful hand hygiene and avoiding food preparation for other people while unwell.


People who are concerned about their symptoms should contact their GP or an appropriate out-of-hours medical service for advice.


Anyone experiencing severe symptoms, signs of significant dehydration or other worrying changes should seek medical attention promptly, particularly if they are in a group considered more vulnerable to serious infection.


It is also important not to assume that every case of diarrhoea or vomiting is caused by salmonella. A range of infections and other conditions can cause similar symptoms, which is why medical advice and testing may be necessary in some circumstances.


## UK salmonella outbreak: what happens next?


The UKHSA and partner agencies are continuing to investigate the outbreak and its possible source. Further findings could provide more information about whether imported eggs are responsible and whether additional action is required.


For consumers, the key message is to follow official food safety advice, maintain good kitchen hygiene and remain aware of product recalls or public health announcements.


With **207 salmonella cases recorded across the UK during the period investigated** , the outbreak is being closely monitored. London currently has the highest number of reported infections among English regions, followed by Yorkshire and the Humber.


While most salmonella infections resolve without hospital treatment, the current outbreak demonstrates why food hygiene and early attention to symptoms remain important.
