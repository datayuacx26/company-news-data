# Brain Cancer Breakthrough: New Study Suggests Earlier Chemotherapy After Surgery Could Improve Glioblastoma Treatment

## Researchers Discover Critical Time Windows That May Help Fight Glioblastoma More Effectively


A new scientific study has revealed a promising approach that could transform the treatment of glioblastoma, one of the most aggressive and deadly forms of brain cancer. Researchers have identified specific periods immediately after brain tumor surgery when chemotherapy may work more effectively than under current treatment practices.


The findings suggest that giving chemotherapy within minutes or a few days after surgery could improve drug delivery to the brain and potentially reduce the risk of the cancer returning. Although the research is still in its early stages and has not yet been tested in large human clinical trials, experts believe it could eventually change the standard approach to glioblastoma care.


## What Is Glioblastoma?


Glioblastoma is the most aggressive type of brain tumor. It grows rapidly and often spreads into nearby brain tissue, making it extremely difficult to remove completely through surgery.


Each year, approximately 3,200 people in the United Kingdom are diagnosed with glioblastoma. Unfortunately, survival rates remain very low, with only a small percentage of patients living five years or longer after diagnosis.


Because microscopic cancer cells usually remain after surgery, the disease frequently returns even after patients receive chemotherapy and radiotherapy.


## Why Timing May Be the Key


Current medical practice usually involves waiting four to six weeks after surgery before starting chemotherapy and radiation treatment. This delay allows patients time to recover from the operation.


However, scientists from The University of Manchester believe there may be a better approach.


Their research found that surgery temporarily disrupts the blood brain barrier, a protective layer that normally prevents many medicines from reaching brain tissue. During this brief period, chemotherapy drugs may gain better access to areas where cancer cells remain.


Researchers identified two important treatment windows:


- Approximately 15 minutes after surgery.
- Around 48 to 72 hours following surgery.


According to the study, delivering treatment during these periods could improve the effectiveness of cancer drugs before the blood brain barrier returns to its normal protective state.


## Understanding the Blood Brain Barrier


The blood brain barrier acts as the body's natural defense system, protecting the brain from harmful substances circulating in the bloodstream.


While this protection is essential for healthy brain function, it also creates a major obstacle for doctors trying to treat brain tumors. Many chemotherapy drugs cannot easily pass through this barrier, reducing their ability to reach cancer cells.


The latest findings indicate that surgery temporarily creates an opportunity for medicines to cross into the brain more efficiently. Researchers believe this short period could become a valuable treatment window for future therapies.


## How the Study Was Conducted


The research, published in *Science Translational Medicine* , used laboratory models designed to closely mimic the surgical removal of glioblastoma tumors.


Scientists tested medicines that would normally struggle to enter the brain unless the blood brain barrier had been disrupted.


The results showed that treatment delivered during the identified time windows significantly reduced tumor regrowth in the laboratory models.


Although these findings are encouraging, researchers emphasize that additional studies involving human patients are necessary before this strategy becomes part of routine medical care.


## Expert Insights


Lead researcher Dr. Thomas Kisby explained that the study demonstrates, for the first time, that glioblastoma surgery briefly creates a vulnerability that doctors may be able to use to their advantage.


Researchers believe that carefully timed treatment during these periods could slow or even prevent the cancer from returning.


Professor Kostas Kostarelos also noted that this strategy may allow doctors to use advanced therapies, including targeted medicines, nanoparticle treatments, immunotherapy, and gene therapy, more effectively after surgery.


Instead of relying only on developing entirely new drugs, physicians may be able to improve outcomes by delivering existing treatments at the most beneficial time.


## Why This Research Matters


Glioblastoma remains one of the most challenging cancers to treat because even the most successful surgeries cannot remove every cancer cell.


Cancer cells that remain near the surgical site often begin growing again within months.


If future clinical trials confirm these findings, doctors could introduce treatment much earlier than they do today, increasing the chances of destroying these remaining cancer cells before they spread.


This approach could represent a major advancement in brain cancer treatment without necessarily requiring completely new medicines.


## Medical Experts Urge Caution


Independent cancer specialists welcomed the research while stressing that it is still in its early stages.


Experts say the findings provide valuable insight into improving drug delivery to the brain, but human clinical trials will be necessary before treatment guidelines can change.


Researchers must confirm that earlier chemotherapy is both safe and effective for patients recovering from brain surgery.


Only after successful clinical testing would hospitals consider updating current treatment protocols.


## Growing Calls for More Brain Cancer Research


The publication of this study comes at a time when awareness of brain cancer is increasing across the United Kingdom.


Scottish swimmer Archie Goodburn, who was diagnosed with brain cancer at the age of 22, has recently urged the UK government to invest more heavily in brain cancer research and improve patient access to innovative clinical trials.


His public appeal has highlighted the urgent need for greater funding, faster research, and improved treatment options for patients facing one of the deadliest forms of cancer.


## Symptoms of Glioblastoma


Glioblastoma symptoms can vary depending on the location of the tumor but commonly include:


- Persistent headaches
- Memory problems
- Personality or behavior changes
- Difficulty speaking or understanding language
- Vision problems
- Weakness or numbness
- Extreme fatigue
- Seizures
- Nausea caused by increased pressure inside the skull


Anyone experiencing persistent neurological symptoms should seek prompt medical evaluation.


## Looking Ahead


While this breakthrough is not yet a cure, it offers hope for improving glioblastoma treatment in the future.


The discovery that chemotherapy may work better during specific periods immediately after surgery opens new possibilities for doctors and researchers worldwide.


Future clinical trials will determine whether these promising laboratory findings translate into improved survival and better outcomes for patients living with glioblastoma.


If successful, this strategy could become an important step toward making one of the world's most difficult cancers more treatable.
