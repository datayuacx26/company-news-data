# Puerto Rico Water Crisis Deepens as San Juan Faces 48-Hour Water Rationing Amid Severe Drought

Puerto Rico is facing a growing water crisis as severe drought conditions force authorities to introduce water rationing measures across San Juan and several other major cities. Thousands of residents are preparing for scheduled water interruptions as officials attempt to manage declining water supplies during one of the island’s most challenging environmental periods in recent years.


Beginning Friday, more than 180,000 customers will experience rotating water cutoffs lasting up to 48 hours. The restrictions are being introduced as prolonged dry weather, record-breaking temperatures, and existing infrastructure problems combine to create a serious public water challenge.


The situation has raised concerns among residents, healthcare providers, and government officials, with many warning that vulnerable communities could face increasing difficulties if conditions continue to worsen.


## Severe Drought Forces Puerto Rico to Introduce Water Restrictions


Puerto Rico’s government has announced that water rationing will affect areas across San Juan and nearby municipalities, including Carolina, Juncos, Gurabo, Trujillo Alto, Canóvanas, and Loíza.


Governor Jenniffer González has acknowledged the seriousness of the situation, saying that current weather conditions have created circumstances that are difficult for authorities to control. She warned that the crisis could become more severe if drought conditions continue.


The latest restrictions come after July recorded the lowest rainfall levels ever documented in San Juan in more than a century. The month was also among the hottest periods recorded in the capital city.


According to drought monitoring data, nearly one quarter of Puerto Rico is currently experiencing severe drought conditions, while more than one third of the island is affected by moderate drought.


Meteorologists have warned that rainfall levels are expected to remain below average in the coming weeks, increasing concerns about water availability during the remainder of the summer.


## Extreme Weather and Climate Conditions Behind the Crisis


Weather experts have linked Puerto Rico’s worsening drought to broader climate patterns affecting rainfall and temperatures across the region.


María Novoa García, a meteorologist with the National Weather Service in San Juan, said forecasts indicate that dry conditions could continue until at least September. She explained that changing ocean temperatures and strengthening climate patterns are contributing to reduced rainfall.


The combination of limited rainfall, extreme heat, and increased demand for water has placed additional pressure on Puerto Rico’s water system.


Officials have also highlighted that drought is not the only factor behind the island’s water challenges. Long-standing infrastructure problems have made it harder for communities to receive a reliable water supply.


## Water Shortages Affect Communities Across Puerto Rico


For many residents, the current rationing announcement is not the beginning of the water crisis. Some neighbourhoods have already experienced repeated shortages for months, and in some cases for nearly a year.


Community leaders say that scheduled water cuts may create additional hardship for residents who already struggle with inconsistent access.


Marcia Soler París, a community leader in San Juan, described the situation as chaotic. She raised concerns about elderly residents and people with mobility issues who may find it difficult to collect and transport heavy water containers during shortages.


Many families are now being forced to store water whenever supplies return, while others depend on emergency distribution points and water delivery services.


## Authorities Prepare Emergency Water Support


Puerto Rico’s water and sewer authority has announced measures to reduce the impact of rationing. Officials plan to use large water trucks to distribute drinking water to affected communities.


Priority will be given to hospitals, healthcare facilities, and homes with elderly residents who may face greater risks during prolonged shortages.


Healthcare officials have also attempted to reassure the public that hospitals are prepared to manage the crisis. Tania Conde Sterling, president of Puerto Rico’s Association of Health Services Administrators, said medical facilities have emergency procedures and plans in place for water supply disruptions.


Hospitals typically maintain backup systems to protect essential services, although officials continue to monitor the situation closely as the drought develops.


## Infrastructure Problems Add Pressure to Water Emergency


While drought has triggered the latest restrictions, Puerto Rico’s water problems have existed for years due to ageing infrastructure and insufficient maintenance.


San Juan Mayor Miguel Romero filed legal action against the island’s Water and Sewer Authority earlier in the year over ongoing water service problems. The lawsuit highlighted concerns about unreliable supplies and repeated disruptions affecting residents.


Government officials have admitted that decades of underinvestment have weakened the island’s water infrastructure.


Puerto Rico Representative Domingo J. Torres García has also warned that further legal action could follow if authorities fail to provide clear explanations and solutions regarding continuing water shortages.


He said residents deserve transparency about the causes of the crisis, government plans, and the actions being taken to restore reliable water services.


## Previous Water Crises in Puerto Rico


This is not the first time Puerto Rico has experienced major water restrictions. Similar rationing measures were introduced in 2015 and 2020 during periods of drought and limited water availability.


During the 2015 crisis, hundreds of thousands of customers experienced scheduled water interruptions, with some households receiving water only every few days.


These previous events demonstrate the ongoing challenges faced by Puerto Rico’s water system and highlight the need for long-term investment in infrastructure, conservation, and climate resilience.


## What Happens Next for Puerto Rico?


Officials have not confirmed how long the current rationing measures will remain in place. Much depends on future rainfall levels and whether drought conditions improve in the coming months.


Residents are being encouraged to conserve water, store supplies safely, and follow guidance from local authorities.


The crisis has renewed debate about Puerto Rico’s ability to manage extreme weather events and protect communities from future water emergencies. Experts say improving infrastructure, maintaining water systems, and preparing for climate-related challenges will be essential to preventing similar situations in the future.


As the island faces another difficult period of drought, the immediate priority remains ensuring access to safe drinking water while authorities work toward longer-term solutions.
