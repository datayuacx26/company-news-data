# Bubble Sunscreen Pack Stopped at UK Border Over Labelling and Traceability Concerns

A popular Bubble Skincare sunscreen pack has been stopped at the UK border after regulators identified issues with its cosmetic labelling and product traceability requirements.


The Bubble Cosmetics Sunscreen Pack was classified as a high-risk product by the UK Office for Product Safety and Standards (OPSS). According to the regulator's notice, the imported pack did not meet several mandatory requirements under the UK's applicable cosmetics rules.


The action has raised questions about what the decision means for consumers who may have purchased individual Bubble sunscreen products in the UK.


## Why Was the Bubble Sunscreen Pack Stopped?


The OPSS identified concerns involving the information supplied with the sunscreen products. In particular, the pack did not provide adequate instructions explaining best practice for using the sunscreen.


Sunscreen is a product where clear usage information is particularly important. Consumers need to understand how much product to apply, how often it should be reapplied and how it should be used alongside other sun protection measures.


Regulators also identified problems with product traceability. Traceability information allows authorities to identify and follow a cosmetic product through the supply chain, helping them respond effectively if a safety or compliance issue arises.


The OPSS therefore determined that multiple mandatory cosmetic labelling requirements had not been satisfied.


## What Products Were Included?


The affected Bubble Cosmetics Sunscreen Pack contained three products designed to provide mineral sun protection.


These included a Tinted Daily Mineral Sunscreen, a Mineral Sunscreen Stick and Bubble Skincare Solar Mate Mineral Sunscreen. The products in the pack were reported as having SPF ratings ranging from 30 to 40.


The products are associated with Bubble Skincare, a brand known for skincare products aimed at younger consumers and people looking for modern, lightweight formulations.


Individual Bubble sunscreen products have also been sold through UK retailers, including Boots. Prices for the products were reported to range from approximately £14 to £19 individually.


However, the specific sunscreen pack that was stopped at the UK border cannot be sold in the UK as a result of the regulatory action.


## Does This Mean All Bubble Sunscreen Is Banned?


Not necessarily.


The regulatory action relates specifically to the Bubble Cosmetics Sunscreen Pack identified in the OPSS notice. Consumers should not automatically assume that every Bubble sunscreen product available in the UK has been banned or declared unsafe.


The issue described by the regulator concerns compliance with cosmetic labelling and traceability requirements. That is different from a finding that the sunscreen ingredients themselves are dangerous.


This distinction is important because a product can be stopped from entering a market because its packaging, instructions or traceability information does not meet legal requirements.


Consumers who own a Bubble sunscreen product should therefore check the exact product name, packaging and batch information rather than assuming that the regulatory notice applies to every Bubble item.


## What Does "High Risk" Mean?


The term "high risk" can sound alarming, but it should be understood in the context of product safety enforcement.


In this case, the regulator's concerns included missing or inadequate information required for consumers to use the product appropriately, as well as weaknesses in traceability.


Proper cosmetic labelling is an important part of consumer protection. Labels can provide essential details about how a product should be used, who is responsible for placing it on the market and how the product can be identified if regulators need to investigate it.


For sunscreen, instructions are particularly relevant because incorrect application can reduce the level of protection consumers expect from the SPF stated on the packaging.


## What Should Consumers Do?


If you have a Bubble sunscreen product, start by checking exactly which product you have and where it was purchased.


If it is part of the specific sunscreen pack identified in the regulatory notice, consumers should pay close attention to any guidance issued by the retailer, manufacturer or relevant UK authorities.


It is also sensible to check the product packaging for usage instructions and traceability information. If important information appears to be missing, consumers can contact the retailer or manufacturer for clarification.


When using sunscreen generally, follow the instructions provided on the product and do not rely on sunscreen alone. Seeking shade, wearing protective clothing and avoiding prolonged exposure to intense sunlight are also important parts of sun protection.


## Why Sunscreen Labelling Matters


Sunscreen products are designed to protect skin from ultraviolet radiation, but their effectiveness depends partly on correct use.


A product labelled with an SPF rating must be used according to its instructions to provide the intended level of protection. Applying too little sunscreen, failing to reapply it or overlooking areas of exposed skin can all reduce protection.


That is why regulatory requirements surrounding instructions, warnings and traceability are more than a packaging issue. They help consumers make informed decisions and give regulators the information they need to monitor products in the marketplace.


## What Happens Next?


The border action means the identified Bubble Cosmetics Sunscreen Pack cannot be sold in the UK.


The OPSS notice highlights the importance of meeting the UK's cosmetic requirements before products enter the market. For consumers, the key point is that the action concerns a specific product pack and identified compliance issues.


Anyone concerned about a particular Bubble sunscreen should check the exact product details rather than relying on headlines or social media posts.
