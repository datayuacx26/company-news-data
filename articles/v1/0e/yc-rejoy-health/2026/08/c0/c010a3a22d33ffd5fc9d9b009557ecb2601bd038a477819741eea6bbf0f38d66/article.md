# Deli Meal Recall Expands Across Three States After Metal Contamination Concern

## Thousands of Ready to Eat Meals Pulled From Major Grocery Chains


Consumers in several states are being urged to check their refrigerators after thousands of ready to eat deli meals were recalled because of possible metal contamination. The affected products were distributed through popular grocery retailers including Kroger, Publix, Food Lion, and Wegmans. Federal food safety officials say the recall was initiated after a customer discovered a piece of metal in one of the prepared meals.


The recall involves more than 22,000 pounds of food produced by Ukrop's Homestyle Foods, a Virginia based company known for prepared meals and baked products. Although no injuries have been reported, health authorities are advising consumers not to eat the affected products.


## Why the Deli Meals Were Recalled


According to federal officials, the issue was identified after a consumer reported finding a metal fragment inside a fully cooked meal. A subsequent investigation found that a small piece of aluminum had come from a baking pan used during food production.


The contamination risk involves tiny metal slivers that may have entered several products manufactured at Ukrop's Kitchen between July 1 and July 30. Because foreign objects in food can pose a choking hazard or cause internal injuries, the recall has been classified as a serious food safety event.


The U.S. Department of Agriculture's Food Safety and Inspection Service classified the incident as a Class I recall. This category is reserved for situations where there is a reasonable chance that consuming the product could result in serious health consequences or even death.


## Products Included in the Recall


The recall covers six different prepared food products sold in various package sizes. Consumers should check for the following items:


- 14.8 ounce baked spaghetti
- 48 ounce bulk baked spaghetti
- 11.6 ounce chicken cobbler
- 48 ounce bulk chicken cobbler
- 13.8 ounce bread pudding with vanilla sauce
- 4 pound bread pudding with vanilla sauce kit


The products carry expiration dates ranging from July 8 through August 5, depending on the production date.


## Stores That Sold the Recalled Products


The recalled meals were available through several well known grocery retailers and specialty markets. These include:


- Kroger
- Publix
- Food Lion
- Wegmans
- Harris Teeter
- Military retail stores
- Ukrop's Market Hall
- Libbie Market


The products were also sold through online ordering channels and distributed to Department of Defense locations.


## States Affected by the Recall


The recalled products were primarily distributed in:


- Virginia
- North Carolina
- West Virginia


Consumers who purchased prepared meals from participating stores in these states should carefully inspect product packaging and expiration dates to determine whether they may have purchased an affected item.


## What Consumers Should Do


Anyone who has purchased one of the recalled products should avoid eating it. Instead, consumers should either throw the product away or return it to the place of purchase for a refund if permitted by the retailer.


If someone believes they have consumed a recalled product and experiences symptoms such as pain, difficulty swallowing, or signs of internal injury, they should seek immediate medical attention.


Food safety experts also recommend checking refrigerators and freezers since some consumers may have stored these ready to eat meals for later use.


## Company Response


Ukrop's Homestyle Foods stated that consumer safety remains its highest priority. Company officials said the supplier responsible for the baking pans has already been notified, and steps are being taken to remove any potentially affected baking pan lots from circulation.


The company also noted that no injuries have been reported in connection with the recall. However, it chose to voluntarily recall the products out of caution after identifying the potential contamination source.


## Another Food Recall Happened Recently


This recall follows another recent food safety action involving bacon products distributed in the Pacific Northwest.


Federal regulators announced that Maple Leaf Foods recalled more than 12,000 pounds of smoked bacon after determining that the products entered the United States from Canada without undergoing the required import reinspection.


The recalled bacon products included Royale Natural Applewood Smoked All Natural Uncured Bacon and Top Valu Uncured Hardwood Smoked Bacon sold at Grocery Outlet stores in Idaho, Oregon, and Washington.


Although that recall involved import inspection requirements rather than contamination, it highlights the continued importance of routine food safety monitoring and regulatory oversight.


## Why Food Recalls Matter


Food recalls play an important role in protecting public health. Whether the issue involves bacterial contamination, undeclared allergens, packaging defects, or foreign materials like metal fragments, prompt recalls help remove potentially dangerous products from store shelves before more consumers are affected.


Consumers are encouraged to stay informed by monitoring recall announcements from federal agencies and checking products already stored at home. Taking quick action after a recall announcement can significantly reduce the risk of injury or illness.


## Final Thoughts


The latest deli meal recall serves as another reminder that even fully cooked and packaged foods can occasionally present unexpected safety concerns. While no injuries have been reported, the presence of metal fragments prompted regulators and the manufacturer to act quickly by removing thousands of pounds of products from the market.


Anyone who recently purchased baked spaghetti, chicken cobbler, or bread pudding from participating grocery stores in Virginia, North Carolina, or West Virginia should verify whether their products are part of the recall. Following recall guidance can help protect both individuals and families from unnecessary health risks.
