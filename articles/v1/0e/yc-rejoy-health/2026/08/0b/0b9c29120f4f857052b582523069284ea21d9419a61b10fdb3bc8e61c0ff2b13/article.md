# NHS Flu Vaccine 2026: Older Adults in England to Receive Jabs From October

The NHS has confirmed that the 2026 to 2027 seasonal flu vaccination programme for older adults in England will begin on October 1. The rollout is designed to provide protection closer to the period when flu is expected to circulate most widely during the winter months.


Adults aged 65 and over will be among those eligible for a free NHS flu vaccine from October. The programme will also cover several other groups considered to be at greater risk from serious complications associated with influenza.


## When will the NHS flu vaccine programme begin?


According to NHS England, the adult flu vaccination programme will start on October 1, 2026. The timing follows advice from the Joint Committee on Vaccination and Immunisation, known as JCVI.


The decision reflects evidence that protection from the flu vaccine can gradually decline in adults. By starting the programme in October rather than earlier in the autumn, health officials aim to provide stronger protection during the period when flu is most likely to be circulating.


Flu activity generally reaches its highest levels around December and January. Officials expect most eligible adults to receive their vaccination by the end of November, giving people protection before the main winter period.


The timing is particularly relevant for older adults, who can face a greater risk of serious illness following influenza infection.


## Who will be eligible for a free flu jab?


The NHS flu vaccine is offered free of charge to eligible groups in England. From October 1, the adult programme will include people aged 65 and over.


Other groups expected to be included from the same date are adults aged 18 to 65 who belong to a clinical risk group. People living in long-stay residential care homes will also be eligible.


The October programme will additionally cover carers who receive Carer's Allowance, close contacts of people who are immunocompromised and frontline social care workers.


Eligibility rules can change, so people should check the latest NHS guidance or wait for information from their GP or vaccination provider.


## Children and pregnant women will be vaccinated earlier


The NHS seasonal vaccination programme will not begin on the same date for everyone.


A separate programme is scheduled to start on September 1, 2026, for pregnant women and children who meet the relevant eligibility requirements. This includes children aged two or three on August 31, primary and secondary school-aged children, and children aged from six months to under 18 who are in clinical risk groups.


The earlier start for children is linked to the way flu tends to spread through the population. Flu circulation among children can occur before cases rise among adults.


Health officials also say protection from the vaccine lasts longer in children. Vaccinating children earlier may therefore help reduce transmission and indirectly protect older adults and other vulnerable people.


## Why is the adult flu vaccine being offered in October?


The timing of the 2026 flu vaccination programme is based on the expected seasonal pattern of influenza and the duration of vaccine protection.


Flu commonly becomes more widespread during the colder months. People also tend to spend more time indoors and closer to others during winter, creating more opportunities for respiratory viruses to spread.


Vaccination does not guarantee that someone will avoid influenza, but it is an important measure for reducing the risk of serious illness.


The decision to begin adult vaccinations in October is intended to balance early protection with maintaining that protection during the period of highest flu activity.


This approach follows JCVI advice and reflects concerns about waning vaccine effectiveness in adults. Health officials believe an October start can provide more effective coverage during the winter peak.


## Why are older people encouraged to get vaccinated?


Influenza can affect people of all ages, but older adults are among the groups that can be more vulnerable to severe illness.


Most people recover from flu with rest and supportive care. However, influenza can sometimes lead to complications, including serious respiratory illness, particularly among people who are older or have certain underlying health conditions.


For eligible adults, the NHS flu vaccination provides an opportunity to reduce the risk of becoming seriously ill during the winter season.


Vaccination can also contribute to wider protection by helping reduce the spread of influenza within communities.


## How will people get their flu vaccine?


Eligible people will be contacted by the NHS during the autumn and winter vaccination period. Depending on local arrangements, vaccinations may be available through GP surgeries or participating pharmacies that provide NHS flu vaccination.


People who believe they are eligible should look out for invitations and local NHS information.


It is also important to check current eligibility information because vaccination arrangements can vary between groups and may be updated as the programme progresses.


Those who have questions about their eligibility can contact their GP surgery or check official NHS information.


## What should people know before winter?


The 2026 to 2027 flu vaccination programme is part of the NHS's wider effort to prepare for the annual increase in respiratory viruses during autumn and winter.


For older adults, October 1 marks the planned start of the free NHS flu vaccination programme. With flu activity typically increasing later in the year, health authorities want the majority of eligible people to be vaccinated by the end of November.


The different vaccination dates for adults and children are based on the expected timing of flu circulation and the way influenza spreads through different age groups.


People who are eligible should consider taking up the offer when invited, particularly if they are at higher risk of complications from flu.


## Final thoughts


The NHS flu vaccine rollout for 2026 is expected to begin with children and pregnant women from September 1, followed by the wider adult programme from October 1.


For people aged 65 and over in England, the October start date is intended to provide protection closer to the winter period when influenza is generally most prevalent. Other eligible adults, care home residents, carers, close contacts of immunocompromised people and frontline social care workers will also be included in the October programme.


As the winter season approaches, eligible people should watch for NHS vaccination invitations and use official NHS channels for the latest information about appointments and eligibility.
