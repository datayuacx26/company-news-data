# CKM Syndrome Explained: Warning Signs, Risk Factors and How to Protect Your Heart and Kidneys

Learn what CKM syndrome is, its warning signs, stages, major risk factors and practical ways to protect your heart, kidneys and metabolic health.


## What Is CKM Syndrome?


Cardiovascular-kidney-metabolic syndrome, commonly called **CKM syndrome** , is a health condition that highlights the close relationship between the heart, kidneys and metabolic system.


Rather than treating heart disease, kidney problems, obesity and type 2 diabetes as completely separate conditions, doctors increasingly recognize that these problems can influence one another. A problem in one area can increase the strain placed on the others.


The issue is widespread. The American Heart Association and American College of Cardiology reported in their 2026 clinical guideline that nearly **90% of US adults have at least one risk factor associated with CKM syndrome** . These risk factors include excess weight, high blood pressure, abnormal cholesterol, elevated blood sugar and reduced kidney function.


That does not mean that 90% of Americans have advanced disease. Instead, the figure shows how common the individual risk factors are.


Understanding CKM syndrome can therefore be useful even for people who currently feel healthy.


## Why Heart, Kidney and Metabolic Health Are Connected


Your body does not operate as a collection of isolated organs.


The heart circulates blood throughout the body, while the kidneys filter waste and help regulate fluid, blood pressure and other important processes. Metabolic health involves how the body handles energy, blood sugar and related processes.


Conditions such as obesity, insulin resistance, high blood pressure, abnormal cholesterol, diabetes and kidney disease can place additional stress on these systems.


According to the Cleveland Clinic, CKM syndrome brings together cardiovascular disease, kidney disease, obesity and type 2 diabetes because these conditions share important risk factors and can contribute to one another.


This interconnected approach is also reflected in the latest medical guidance. The 2026 CKM guideline emphasizes earlier screening and coordinated care instead of waiting until serious cardiovascular or kidney complications appear.


## Common CKM Syndrome Risk Factors


Several health conditions can increase the likelihood of developing or progressing through CKM syndrome.


### Excess Weight and Abdominal Fat


Excess body fat, particularly around the abdomen, is an important risk factor. Increased body fat can be associated with inflammation, insulin resistance and other metabolic changes that affect cardiovascular and kidney health.


### High Blood Pressure


High blood pressure can damage blood vessels over time and increase the workload placed on the heart. It is also an important risk factor for kidney disease.


Because hypertension can exist without obvious symptoms, regular blood pressure checks can be particularly important.


### Abnormal Cholesterol


High triglycerides and unhealthy cholesterol levels are among the metabolic factors associated with CKM syndrome. Cholesterol problems can contribute to plaque buildup in arteries and increase cardiovascular risk.


### High Blood Sugar and Diabetes


Insulin resistance, prediabetes and type 2 diabetes can all contribute to metabolic problems. Persistently elevated blood sugar can also damage blood vessels and affect kidney function.


### Kidney Disease


Reduced kidney function is another important component of CKM syndrome. Kidney problems can make it harder for the body to regulate blood pressure and other processes, creating additional cardiovascular risk.


## Possible Warning Signs


CKM syndrome does not always produce obvious symptoms in its early stages. Some of the underlying risk factors, including high blood pressure and abnormal cholesterol, may cause no noticeable warning signs.


When symptoms do occur, they can vary depending on the conditions affecting an individual.


Potential symptoms associated with CKM syndrome and its related conditions include:


- Unusual tiredness
- Shortness of breath
- Chest discomfort or pain
- Swelling in the feet, legs, hands or ankles
- Needing to urinate more frequently
- Changes in appetite
- Leg pain when walking
- Dry skin
- Sleep apnea
- Gout


The Cleveland Clinic lists these among possible symptoms associated with CKM syndrome.


However, these symptoms are not specific to CKM syndrome. Having one or more does not automatically mean that someone has the condition.


Chest pain, severe shortness of breath, fainting or other potentially serious symptoms should receive urgent medical attention.


## CKM Syndrome Has Different Stages


One of the most important developments in understanding CKM syndrome is the use of stages.


The American Heart Association describes a progression beginning with risk factors and potentially advancing to cardiovascular disease.


**Stage 0:** No significant CKM risk factors are present. The priority is prevention and maintaining healthy habits.


**Stage 1:** Excess weight, insulin resistance or prediabetes may be present.


**Stage 2:** Metabolic risk factors such as high blood pressure, abnormal triglycerides, type 2 diabetes, metabolic syndrome or chronic kidney disease may be present.


**Stage 3:** Evidence of cardiovascular or kidney-related damage or a particularly high cardiovascular risk may be identified, even when symptoms are absent.


**Stage 4:** Cardiovascular disease has been diagnosed alongside other CKM risk factors. Conditions can include coronary heart disease, heart failure, stroke, peripheral artery disease or atrial fibrillation.


The purpose of staging is not to label people. It helps healthcare professionals understand risk and determine which preventive or treatment strategies may be appropriate.


## Can CKM Syndrome Be Prevented?


There is no single solution that prevents every case of CKM syndrome. However, several lifestyle choices can improve cardiovascular and metabolic health.


### Focus on a Balanced Diet


A nutritious eating pattern can help with weight management, blood pressure, blood sugar and cholesterol.


Rather than focusing on one so-called miracle food, aim for a balanced diet containing vegetables, fruits, whole grains, appropriate protein sources and healthy fats. Reducing excessive intake of highly processed foods, added sugars and excess sodium may also be beneficial.


### Stay Physically Active


Regular movement supports cardiovascular and metabolic health.


Adults are generally encouraged to accumulate around 150 minutes of moderate-intensity aerobic activity each week, along with muscle-strengthening activities. The most appropriate amount and intensity can vary depending on age, fitness and medical conditions.


You do not necessarily need an intensive gym routine. Walking, cycling, swimming and other activities can contribute to an active lifestyle.


### Prioritize Sleep


Sleep is another important part of overall health. Poor sleep can make it harder to maintain healthy weight, energy levels and metabolic function.


For most adults, health authorities recommend getting at least seven hours of sleep each night, although individual needs vary.


### Know Your Numbers


Preventive healthcare can identify risk factors before they become major problems.


Depending on your circumstances, your healthcare professional may monitor:


- Blood pressure
- Blood glucose
- Cholesterol and triglycerides
- Body weight and waist measurements
- Kidney function
- Overall cardiovascular risk


The 2026 CKM guideline recommends more comprehensive risk assessment and uses the PREVENT equations to help estimate cardiovascular risk in appropriate patients.


## Treatment Depends on the Individual


CKM syndrome does not have one universal treatment.


Someone with early-stage risk factors may primarily need lifestyle support and monitoring. Another person may require medication for blood pressure, cholesterol, diabetes or weight management.


The latest guidelines also recognize a role for certain medications, including GLP-1-based therapies and SGLT2 inhibitors, for selected patients. Metabolic or bariatric surgery may also be considered in appropriate circumstances.


Treatment should therefore be based on an individual's health history, test results, risk level and medical needs.


## The Bigger Message About CKM Syndrome


The most important lesson from CKM syndrome is that heart, kidney and metabolic health should not be considered separately.


A person may have high blood pressure today, developing insulin resistance tomorrow and kidney or cardiovascular complications later. Identifying those connections earlier can provide an opportunity to address risk before serious disease develops.


The good news is that many CKM risk factors can be monitored and treated. Lifestyle changes, preventive screening and appropriate medical care can all play a role in protecting long-term health.


If you have concerns about your blood pressure, cholesterol, blood sugar, weight, kidney function or cardiovascular risk, speaking with a qualified healthcare professional is a practical place to start.
