# Michael Sheen Discovers Toxic Forever Chemicals in His Blood While Filming BBC Documentary

Michael Sheen has revealed that testing carried out during the filming of his new BBC documentary found toxic chemicals in his blood, a discovery that left the Welsh actor deeply shocked.


The 57-year-old actor is investigating alleged environmental contamination in Wales in the upcoming two-part documentary *Buried with Michael Sheen* . The programme examines claims surrounding the disposal of industrial chemicals and the potential impact on communities living near contaminated sites.


Sheen agreed to have his blood tested during the investigation because he expected the results to provide a useful comparison. Instead, the results brought the issue much closer to home.


## Michael Sheen discovers PCBs in his blood


During the documentary investigation, Sheen learned that his blood contained polychlorinated biphenyls, commonly known as PCBs.


PCBs are a group of industrial chemicals that were once widely used because of their resistance to heat and chemical breakdown. Their durability made them useful in electrical equipment and a range of industrial applications.


However, that same durability has created a major environmental problem. PCBs can remain in the environment for decades and can accumulate in living organisms.


The chemicals are now internationally recognised as highly hazardous substances. The World Health Organization's International Agency for Research on Cancer classifies PCBs as carcinogenic to humans.


Speaking about his test results, Sheen said he had expected the test to show that he had not been exposed. The discovery therefore came as an unexpected shock.


He explained that the results made the issue feel considerably more personal and reinforced the documentary's wider questions about how widespread exposure to persistent chemicals may be.


## What are forever chemicals?


The term "forever chemicals" is often associated with PFAS, a large family of synthetic chemicals that are extremely persistent in the environment. PCBs are chemically different from PFAS, although they are also highly persistent pollutants and are sometimes discussed in the broader context of long-lasting environmental contaminants.


The persistence of these substances is a major concern because they can remain in soil, sediment and other parts of the environment for long periods.


The documentary focuses on claims that historic industrial waste has contributed to contamination in parts of Wales and north-west England.


One location examined by Sheen is Brofiscin Quarry in south Wales. The site has previously been investigated because of concerns about PCB contamination.


## Investigation into Brofiscin Quarry


Sheen became interested in the issue after discovering research by farming consultant and whistleblower Douglas Gowan.


According to the claims explored in the programme, large quantities of industrial chemicals were deposited at sites across Wales and north-west England over several decades.


The documentary also meets residents who live close to affected areas. Some residents reportedly have elevated levels of PCBs in their blood, while the programme explores concerns about serious health conditions within local communities.


It is important to note that the presence of a chemical in someone's blood does not, by itself, establish that a particular contaminated site caused an illness. Establishing cause and effect requires detailed scientific and medical evidence.


That distinction is particularly important when discussing environmental pollution and cancer.


## Sheen describes becoming worried after visiting the site


The investigation also appears to have changed the way Sheen viewed everyday exposure to potentially contaminated material.


After visiting Brofiscin Quarry during filming, the actor recalled becoming concerned that he could accidentally carry contaminated material away from the site.


He described returning to his car and removing his boots before cleaning them because he was worried about bringing potentially harmful substances home.


The experience demonstrated how investigating environmental contamination can affect people psychologically as well as physically. For Sheen, the concern was no longer simply an abstract environmental story. His own test results had shown that exposure was already a reality for him.


## Calls for greater responsibility


A central theme of *Buried with Michael Sheen* is accountability.


Sheen has argued that those responsible for historical dumping should be required to take responsibility for the consequences. He has also highlighted the difficult position facing local authorities when contaminated land requires expensive investigation, management or remediation.


Local councils may face significant financial challenges if they become responsible for dealing with contaminated sites, particularly when historic pollution involves multiple parties and decades-old industrial activity.


The actor believes the issue requires a solution that does not leave local communities or councils carrying the burden alone.


Natural Resources Wales has previously investigated concerns relating to PCB leaks at Brofiscin Quarry. According to the BBC, the regulator concluded that the leaks did not represent a sufficient risk to justify further action at that time.


The documentary could therefore reignite discussion about how historic contamination is monitored and managed and whether communities living near former industrial sites have received adequate protection.


## Michael Sheen's environmental activism


The documentary is another example of Sheen using his public profile to draw attention to social and environmental issues.


The actor, who grew up in south Wales, has previously been involved in projects highlighting economic hardship and inequality in his home community.


In 2025, he attracted widespread attention after helping clear approximately £1 million of debt owed by people in his hometown of Port Talbot. That project was explored in the documentary *Michael Sheen's Secret Million Pound Giveaway* .


His involvement in *Buried with Michael Sheen* continues that pattern of using television to investigate issues affecting communities in Wales.


## When will *Buried with Michael Sheen* air?


*Buried with Michael Sheen* is scheduled to premiere on BBC Two, BBC One Wales and BBC iPlayer on **Monday 17 August at 9pm** .


The two-part programme follows Sheen as he investigates the history of chemical contamination, speaks to residents and examines claims about the consequences of industrial waste.


The discovery of PCBs in his own blood adds an especially personal dimension to the documentary.


Rather than simply reporting on concerns raised by others, Sheen has now found evidence of chemical exposure in his own body. Whether that exposure can be connected to any particular source is a separate scientific question, but the discovery clearly changed his perspective on the investigation.


As the programme prepares to air, it is likely to generate renewed discussion about PCB contamination in Wales, the legacy of industrial pollution and who should ultimately pay for cleaning up potentially contaminated land.


For Sheen, the documentary is ultimately about more than chemicals. It is about communities, accountability and whether people affected by historic environmental decisions should be left to deal with the consequences themselves.
