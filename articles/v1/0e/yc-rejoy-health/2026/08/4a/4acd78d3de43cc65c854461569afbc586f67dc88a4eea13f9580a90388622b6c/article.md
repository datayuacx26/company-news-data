# Marine Veteran, 44, Left With Permanent Brain Damage After Eating Raw Oysters, Lawsuit Claims

A recent lawsuit in Illinois has drawn attention to the potentially serious health risks associated with eating raw oysters after a 44-year-old Marine veteran and former firefighter reportedly suffered catastrophic brain damage following a severe illness.


According to the lawsuit, Joe Belgio became seriously ill after eating raw oysters at Gibson’s Bar & Steakhouse in Oak Brook, Illinois, in June 2025. His attorneys allege that the oysters were contaminated with harmful pathogens and that the resulting illness eventually contributed to a cardiac arrest that left him with a severe anoxic brain injury.


The case highlights the importance of **food safety, raw oyster risks and shellfish contamination** , particularly for people who may be more vulnerable to foodborne infections.


## What happened after Belgio ate the oysters?


Belgio reportedly visited the steakhouse with his fiancée, Melanie Stayer, and their five-month-old daughter. During the meal, he ate raw oysters.


The lawsuit alleges that Belgio later developed a serious infection. His condition deteriorated dramatically, and in July 2025 he suffered cardiac arrest. According to statements from his attorney reported by ABC7 Chicago, Belgio was without oxygen for at least eight minutes before being resuscitated.


A prolonged lack of oxygen can cause an anoxic brain injury, a serious condition in which the brain is deprived of the oxygen it needs to function normally.


Belgio reportedly remained in a coma for months and now requires extensive assistance with everyday activities. His family and legal representatives say he requires around-the-clock care.


The financial consequences have also been substantial, with his fiancée reportedly facing medical expenses reaching seven figures.


## Which pathogens are mentioned in the lawsuit?


The legal complaint identifies two pathogens that the attorneys say were involved in Belgio's illness: **Enteropathogenic E. coli, commonly known as EPEC, and Plesiomonas shigelloides** .


Both organisms can be associated with gastrointestinal illness, although the exact circumstances of an individual infection require medical and laboratory evaluation.


Raw and undercooked shellfish can carry microorganisms from contaminated marine environments. Oysters are particularly relevant because they filter large quantities of water while feeding. If harmful microorganisms are present in the surrounding water, they can accumulate inside the shellfish.


This is one reason public-health authorities generally warn that eating raw oysters carries a greater foodborne illness risk than eating oysters that have been thoroughly cooked.


## What does the restaurant say?


Gibsons Restaurant Group has disputed the allegations.


In a statement reported by FOX21, the company described Belgio's cardiac event as tragic and said it wished him a full recovery. The company also stated that food safety is a major priority and said its internal investigation found no evidence that the restaurant caused Belgio's condition.


The restaurant group also said there had been no other reported cases of illness and expressed confidence that the legal process would ultimately support its position.


Those statements are important because the allegations contained in a lawsuit have not necessarily been established as facts in court. The case will need to proceed through the legal process, where evidence concerning the source of the oysters, food handling, medical records and potential causes of illness can be examined.


## Why can raw oysters be risky?


Raw oysters can contain bacteria, viruses and other microorganisms even when they appear fresh and smell normal.


Contamination is not necessarily visible to a customer. An oyster can look perfectly healthy while carrying pathogens capable of causing illness.


Cooking oysters thoroughly can substantially reduce the risk associated with many foodborne pathogens. However, simply adding hot sauce, lemon juice or alcohol does not reliably eliminate harmful microorganisms.


People with certain medical conditions, older adults, young children and individuals with weakened immune systems can face a higher risk of severe complications from some seafood-related infections.


Anyone who develops severe symptoms after eating raw or undercooked shellfish should seek medical advice promptly, particularly if symptoms include persistent vomiting, severe diarrhoea, confusion, difficulty breathing, weakness or signs of dehydration.


## The broader food-safety lesson


The Illinois lawsuit serves as a reminder that foodborne illness can sometimes have consequences far beyond a temporary stomach upset.


Most cases of food poisoning resolve without long-term complications, but severe infections can result in hospitalisation and, in rare circumstances, life-threatening complications.


For consumers, one practical way to reduce the risk is to choose thoroughly cooked seafood instead of raw or undercooked shellfish. People in higher-risk groups should be particularly cautious about consuming raw oysters.


Restaurants and seafood suppliers also play an important role in maintaining the cold chain, following hygiene procedures, sourcing shellfish from approved suppliers and complying with food-safety requirements.


## A family facing an uncertain future


For Belgio's family, the consequences described in the lawsuit extend far beyond questions about food safety.


His fiancée has described him as a dedicated Marine veteran and firefighter who spent much of his life serving others. A fundraising campaign established to support his recovery describes his military service in Iraq and his career helping people in emergencies.


Stayer has also spoken publicly about the continuing impact of his condition, saying that despite the severity of his injuries, she believes he remains present and continues to fight his way through recovery.


The family's legal action is seeking compensation for the extensive costs associated with Belgio's medical treatment and ongoing care.


## What consumers should remember about raw oysters


The case does not mean that every raw oyster is contaminated or that every person who eats raw shellfish will become seriously ill. However, it illustrates why raw oysters should not be considered risk-free.


Consumers can reduce their exposure to foodborne illness by choosing cooked shellfish, keeping seafood properly refrigerated and following food-safety guidance. When purchasing oysters for home preparation, it is also important to obtain them from reputable, approved sources and follow applicable cooking instructions.


The allegations against the Illinois restaurant remain allegations unless and until they are established through the legal process. The restaurant has denied responsibility and maintains that its investigation found no evidence linking the establishment to Belgio's medical condition.


As the lawsuit develops, further evidence may clarify what caused Belgio's illness and whether contaminated oysters were responsible.


For now, the case is a stark reminder of the potential dangers associated with consuming raw shellfish and the importance of t aking food safety seriously.
