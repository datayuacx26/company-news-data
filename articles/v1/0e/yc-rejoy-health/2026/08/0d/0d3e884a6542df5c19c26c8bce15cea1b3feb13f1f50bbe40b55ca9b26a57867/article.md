# Levothyroxine and Slow Heart Rate: Hypothyroidism Symptoms and Warning Signs

Learn about the link between an underactive thyroid, levothyroxine, and a slow heart rate. Discover symptoms of hypothyroidism, why medication monitoring matters and when to seek medical advice.


An underactive thyroid can affect many parts of the body, and some symptoms may be easy to overlook. Tiredness, dry skin, constipation, changes in weight and hair loss are commonly associated with hypothyroidism. However, a slow heart rate can also occur when thyroid hormone levels are too low.


A recent discussion by NHS GP and television doctor Dr Amir Khan highlighted the potential connection between an underactive thyroid and a slower heartbeat. His comments, shared through his podcast and social media, stressed that people should not dismiss changes in their health simply because hypothyroidism is a common condition.


For people taking levothyroxine, understanding the condition and keeping up with recommended blood tests can be an important part of ongoing treatment.


## What is an underactive thyroid?


An underactive thyroid, medically known as hypothyroidism, occurs when the thyroid gland does not produce enough thyroid hormone. These hormones play an important role in regulating how quickly many processes in the body work.


When thyroid hormone levels are low, bodily functions can slow down. This can contribute to symptoms such as persistent tiredness, weight gain, low mood, dry skin, constipation and hair loss.


Because thyroid hormones also influence the cardiovascular system, a person with an underactive thyroid may experience a slower heart rate. The source article explains that this can sometimes be difficult to recognise because people may focus on more obvious symptoms, such as feeling exhausted or noticing increased hair loss.


## Can hypothyroidism cause a slow heart rate?


A slow heart rate, sometimes called bradycardia, can be associated with an underactive thyroid. When thyroid hormone levels fall significantly, the body's processes may slow, including the rate at which the heart beats.


Dr Amir Khan warned that a severely reduced thyroid hormone level could become a serious medical problem. According to the discussion reported in the source, an extremely slow heart rate may contribute to unconsciousness and could require emergency medical attention.


This does not mean that everyone with hypothyroidism will develop a dangerous heart problem. However, an unexplained change in heart rate should not simply be ignored, particularly when it occurs alongside other symptoms of thyroid dysfunction.


If you experience fainting, severe weakness, chest pain, significant breathing difficulties or a sudden deterioration in your condition, seek urgent medical attention.


## What is levothyroxine?


Levothyroxine is a prescription medicine commonly used to treat an underactive thyroid. It provides the body with a synthetic form of thyroxine, also known as T4, which replaces thyroid hormone that the body is not producing adequately.


The body can convert T4 into the active thyroid hormone T3 when needed. Doctors select an appropriate levothyroxine dose based on individual circumstances and use blood tests to assess whether the dose is suitable.


The aim of treatment is to restore thyroid hormone levels and control symptoms while avoiding an excessively high or low dose.


## Why does levothyroxine dosage matter?


Finding the right levothyroxine dosage is an important part of managing hypothyroidism. The amount a person needs can change throughout their life, meaning that a dose that has worked well previously may eventually need to be reviewed.


The source article highlights several circumstances that can influence thyroid hormone requirements, including ageing, pregnancy, changes in body weight and changes in physical activity.


Unlike a healthy thyroid gland, which can adjust hormone production according to the body's changing needs, someone taking a fixed medication dose relies on medical monitoring to determine whether that dose remains appropriate.


This is one reason regular thyroid blood tests are important.


## Why are thyroid blood tests important?


Regular blood tests allow healthcare professionals to check thyroid hormone levels and determine whether a person's levothyroxine dose remains appropriate.


The NHS information cited in the source explains that people taking levothyroxine generally have blood tests to ensure they are receiving the correct dose. An unsuitable dose can cause side effects, which may include nausea, diarrhoea, headaches or sleep problems.


Medication requirements can change over time, so people should not assume that their original prescription will always remain suitable.


If symptoms return or new symptoms develop, speak with a GP, endocrinologist or pharmacist rather than changing the levothyroxine dose yourself.


## Common symptoms of an underactive thyroid


Hypothyroidism can produce a range of symptoms. Some of the symptoms highlighted in the source include:


- Persistent tiredness
- Weight gain
- Low mood
- Hair loss
- Dry skin
- Constipation
- A slower heart rate


Symptoms can vary considerably between individuals. Having one of these symptoms does not automatically mean that you have hypothyroidism, as many other health conditions can cause similar problems.


If several symptoms occur together, particularly if they persist or worsen, discussing them with a healthcare professional may be appropriate.


## Should people taking levothyroxine be worried?


For most people, levothyroxine is an established treatment for hypothyroidism and can help control symptoms when prescribed and monitored appropriately. The concern is not that everyone taking levothyroxine is at immediate risk of a heart problem.


Instead, the key message is that hypothyroidism needs ongoing management. A person's thyroid hormone requirements can change, and symptoms should be discussed with a healthcare professional rather than being automatically attributed to ageing, stress or lifestyle.


The source also emphasises that people taking thyroxine can live comfortable and healthy lives, although maintaining the correct dose can sometimes require ongoing monitoring and adjustments.


## When should you speak to a doctor?


Anyone concerned about symptoms of hypothyroidism or a change in their heart rate should seek medical advice. This is particularly important for people who already have a diagnosed thyroid condition and notice a significant change in their usual symptoms.


Do not stop taking prescribed levothyroxine or change the dosage without speaking to a healthcare professional.


A slow heartbeat can have many possible causes, and only an appropriate medical assessment can determine whether thyroid hormone levels, medication dosage or another condition is responsible.


## Final thoughts


Hypothyroidism is a common condition, but it still requires careful management. Symptoms such as tiredness, hair loss, dry skin and constipation may seem relatively minor, while changes in heart rate can be less obvious.


The recent warning discussed by Dr Amir Khan highlights why people with an underactive thyroid should pay attention to changes in their health and attend recommended blood tests.


For people taking levothyroxine, maintaining the appropriate dose is an important part of treatment. Because hormone requirements can change with factors such as age, pregnancy, weight and lifestyle, regular medical review can help ensure treatment remains suitable.


If you have concerns about your thyroid, levothyroxine treatment or heart rate, speak with your GP or another qualified healthcare professional. Prompt medical advice is particularly important if symptoms are severe, sudden or associated with fainting or loss of consciousness.
