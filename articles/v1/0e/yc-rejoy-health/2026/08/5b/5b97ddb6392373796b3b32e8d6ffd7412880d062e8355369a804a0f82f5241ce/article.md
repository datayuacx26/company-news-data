# Typhoon Dolphin Hits Southern Japan: Flights Cancelled as Storm Threatens Earthquake-Hit Region

Typhoon Dolphin is bringing powerful winds, heavy rain and widespread disruption to southern Japan, just days after a deadly earthquake struck the same region. Hundreds of flights have been cancelled, residents have been ordered to evacuate, and authorities are warning of flooding and landslides as the storm moves across the southern islands toward China.


The typhoon is creating an especially serious challenge for communities in Kyushu, including Kumamoto prefecture, where thousands of people remain in evacuation centres following last week's devastating earthquake.


## Typhoon Dolphin Brings Dangerous Weather to Japan


Typhoon Dolphin passed close to Okinoerabu on Friday morning as it moved westward across Japan's southern island chain. According to weather authorities, the storm was travelling at approximately 25 kilometres per hour, with powerful sustained winds around its centre.


A particularly strong gust was recorded on Tokunoshima, highlighting the dangerous conditions facing communities across the region.


Okinawa has already experienced injuries linked to the storm. Three men in their seventies were reportedly slightly injured after being affected by the powerful winds.


The storm has also forced major changes to transport across southern Japan. Naha Airport, the main aviation gateway to Okinawa, was closed, with hundreds of domestic and international flights cancelled.


For residents and travellers, the disruption is expected to continue while authorities monitor the typhoon's path and intensity.


## Nearly 260,000 People Ordered to Evacuate


The threat is particularly concerning because parts of southern Japan are still dealing with the consequences of a magnitude 7.1 earthquake that struck on 28 July.


The earthquake caused extensive damage and resulted in dozens of deaths. Many buildings were damaged, while some residents have been unable to return home.


Around 6,700 people in Kumamoto prefecture were still staying in evacuation centres as Typhoon Dolphin approached. Some residents had been using plastic sheeting to protect damaged roofs from further rain, but the arrival of a powerful tropical storm could make recovery considerably more difficult.


Evacuation orders have been issued across parts of Kagoshima and Okinawa prefectures. Officials have warned that wind gusts could become strong enough to damage buildings, making already weakened structures particularly vulnerable.


The combination of earthquake damage and severe weather increases the risks for people who remain in affected communities.


## Heavy Rain Could Trigger Flooding and Landslides


Strong winds are not the only concern associated with Typhoon Dolphin. Forecasters are predicting significant rainfall across southern Japan.


Some areas could receive up to 200 millimetres of rain during the 24 hours leading into Saturday morning.


Weather officials have also warned about the possibility of a linear rain band. These narrow areas of persistent thunderstorms can remain over the same location for an extended period, producing intense rainfall in a short time.


Such conditions can quickly overwhelm drainage systems and increase the risk of flash flooding, landslides and dangerous road conditions.


The situation is particularly sensitive in areas already affected by the earthquake. Damaged roads, unstable slopes and weakened buildings can make heavy rainfall considerably more hazardous.


At the same time, southern Japan is experiencing high temperatures, with some locations expected to reach around 36C. Authorities have therefore warned residents about heatstroke while also preparing for severe storm conditions.


## Toyota Suspends Production at Japanese Factories


Typhoon Dolphin is also affecting Japanese industry.


Toyota reportedly suspended production at nine factories in Japan because of concerns that the storm could interfere with shipping operations and the movement of finished vehicles.


The decision demonstrates how severe weather can have effects far beyond the immediate storm zone. Japan's manufacturing sector relies heavily on complex transport networks, ports and supply chains, all of which can be disrupted by extreme weather.


Flight cancellations, port restrictions and road closures could create further delays if the storm continues to affect transportation routes.


## Typhoon Dolphin Tracking Toward China


After passing through the waters south of Japan, Typhoon Dolphin is expected to cross the East China Sea before reaching China's eastern coastline.


Forecasts indicate that the storm could make landfall between southern Zhejiang and northern Fujian from Sunday afternoon into early Monday.


China's National Meteorological Centre has issued a yellow alert, the country's third level within its four-stage weather warning system.


Authorities in Zhejiang and Fujian have already taken precautionary measures. Offshore construction projects have been suspended, vessels have been moved to safer locations and thousands of people have been relocated from exposed islands.


Heavy rain is also expected in parts of Fujian, with some areas potentially receiving several hundred millimetres over multiple days.


The exact path of the storm after landfall remains uncertain. Meteorologists have suggested that changes in the surrounding atmospheric pressure systems could influence whether Dolphin weakens quickly or remains over southern China for an extended period.


## Taiwan and South Korea Also Feeling the Effects


Typhoon Dolphin's influence extends beyond the areas directly along its projected path.


Northern Taiwan is expected to experience significant rainfall as the storm moves westward.


The system is also influencing weather conditions on the Korean Peninsula. In South Korea, meteorologists have linked changes in wind patterns associated with Dolphin to unusually hot conditions in Seoul and surrounding areas.


The typhoon has therefore become a major regional weather event, affecting conditions across several parts of East Asia even where the storm itself is not expected to make landfall.


## What Happens Next?


The immediate priority for authorities in Japan is protecting residents from dangerous winds, flooding and landslides while maintaining emergency support for communities already affected by the earthquake.


For people in evacuation centres, the arrival of another major weather event could complicate relief operations and delay rebuilding work.


Authorities are expected to continue monitoring Typhoon Dolphin closely as it crosses the East China Sea. Residents in southern Japan, Taiwan and eastern China are being urged to follow official weather warnings and evacuation instructions.


The storm's eventual strength and track will determine the scale of its impact. However, its arrival at a time when parts of southern Japan are still recovering from a devastating earthquake makes the situation particularly challenging.

Typhoon Dolphin is adding another major emergency to a region already dealing with the aftermath of a deadly earthquake. From cancelled flights and evacuation orders in Japan to preparations along China's eastern coast, authorities across East Asia are taking precautions as the storm moves west.


The biggest concerns remain flooding, landslides, destructive winds and further damage to communities whose homes and infrastructure may already be weakened.
