# Lightning Strike During Thai Football Match Claims Player’s Life, Highlights Growing Need for Weather Safety Awareness

A local football match in southern Thailand turned into a tragic scene after a sudden lightning strike hit the pitch, killing a young footballer and injuring several other players. The incident has raised concerns about the dangers of playing outdoor sports during unstable weather conditions and serves as a reminder of how quickly severe weather can become life threatening.


The incident happened during the Golok FA Cup tournament at Santiphap Stadium in Sungai Golok, Narathiwat province, southern Thailand. The match was being played between amateur teams when a powerful lightning bolt struck near the players on the field.


According to local authorities, 24-year-old Sofwan Awae suffered serious injuries from the strike. Emergency responders rushed to provide medical assistance, but despite efforts to save him, he later died. Police officials confirmed the tragic outcome and said that medical teams attempted to revive him following the strike.


Video footage from the match reportedly showed the terrifying moment when lightning flashed across the field. Players were seen reacting instantly as the strike occurred, with several teammates rushing toward Awae to provide help. The sudden explosion and bright flash shocked everyone present at the stadium.


Several other footballers were also affected by the strike. Authorities said that around 12 players suffered injuries and were taken to hospital for medical treatment. One of those injured was reportedly a Malaysian player who was participating in the local tournament.


Sofwan Awae was not only a player in the community tournament but also a rising football talent. He played as a winger for SAMCOLTS and had recently signed with Yala FC, a Thai third-tier football club. His death has caused sadness among teammates, supporters, and the wider football community.


Yala FC expressed condolences to Awae’s family, describing him as a new member of the club and sharing sympathy with those affected by his passing. Many supporters have also paid tribute to the young player, remembering his passion for football and his contribution to the sport.


The tragedy has also renewed attention on lightning safety in Thailand. Lightning strikes are a recurring natural hazard in the country, especially during periods of heavy rain and thunderstorms. According to Thailand’s Department of Disease Control, hundreds of people were affected by lightning incidents between 2020 and 2024, with many losing their lives.


Thailand’s tropical climate means thunderstorms are common, particularly during the rainy season. Outdoor workers, farmers, athletes, and people taking part in outdoor activities are often among those most exposed to lightning risks.


Experts advise people to avoid open areas during thunderstorms because lightning can strike the ground even when rainfall appears limited. Sports fields, agricultural land, beaches, and large open spaces can become dangerous locations during storms.


Safety officials often recommend stopping outdoor games and activities when thunder is heard. Taking shelter inside a solid building or a fully enclosed vehicle is considered one of the safest options during lightning storms. Standing under trees is not recommended because trees can attract lightning and may cause serious injuries to people sheltering beneath them.


The death of Sofwan Awae is not the first recent lightning-related tragedy in Thailand. Earlier incidents have also resulted in fatalities during sudden storms. In one case, lightning killed an engineering student and his aunt while they were inside a small shelter in Lampang province. In another incident, a pregnant woman in Uthai Thani province died after seeking protection under a tree during a storm.


The danger of lightning is not limited to Thailand. On the same day as the football tragedy, lightning strikes in India’s eastern Jharkhand state reportedly killed at least 14 people, many of whom were agricultural workers outside in open fields.


These incidents highlight the importance of greater awareness about extreme weather risks. While lightning strikes cannot be prevented, many deaths can potentially be avoided through better preparation, early weather warnings, and quick decisions to stop outdoor activities when storms approach.


For sports organisers, the incident underlines the need for clear safety procedures. Matches and training sessions should be paused when thunderstorms are nearby, and players should have access to safe shelters. Amateur tournaments, where safety planning may sometimes receive less attention than professional events, can especially benefit from stronger weather monitoring systems.


Football fans and communities around the world have expressed sympathy following the loss of Sofwan Awae. His death is a painful reminder that even a normal sporting event can become dangerous when nature changes suddenly.


As climate patterns continue to create unpredictable weather conditions in many parts of the world, awareness and preparedness remain essential. Whether on a football field, a farm, or any outdoor location, recognising the warning signs of severe weather can help protect lives.
