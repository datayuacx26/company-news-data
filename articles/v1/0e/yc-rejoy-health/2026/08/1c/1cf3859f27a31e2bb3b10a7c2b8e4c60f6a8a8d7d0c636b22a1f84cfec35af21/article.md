# Are Screens Replacing Imagination? How Digital Habits Are Changing Children’s Play

Childhood has always been associated with creativity, curiosity, and imaginative play. From building forts in the living room to creating outdoor adventures with friends, children traditionally found endless ways to entertain themselves. However, a new concern is emerging as screens become the first choice for many children when they feel bored.


Phones, tablets, televisions, and gaming devices are increasingly competing with traditional toys and creative activities. New research suggests that many children are choosing digital entertainment over activities that encourage imagination, problem-solving, and independent play.


While technology has many benefits for learning and communication, experts are raising questions about whether excessive screen use is reducing opportunities for children to develop creativity through play.


## Children Are Choosing Screens Over Creative Activities


A recent survey involving 2,000 children between the ages of four and nine revealed a significant shift in how young children respond to boredom. More than one quarter of children said they would choose an electronic device when they needed something to do.


In comparison, a much smaller number said they would create their own games or use their imagination to find entertainment. Only a small percentage of children said they would choose outdoor play when they had nothing planned.


These findings highlight a major change from previous generations. Children who grew up before smartphones and widespread internet access also watched television, but they often spent more time creating their own games, inventing stories, and exploring their surroundings.


Today, digital entertainment is often available instantly, making it harder for children to experience boredom as an opportunity for creativity.


## Traditional Toys and Imaginative Play Are Declining


Classic childhood activities such as playing with toy cars, trains, puzzles, board games, craft projects, and pretend games appear to be losing popularity among many children.


Many parents reported that activities their children once enjoyed have been replaced, at least partly, by screen based entertainment. Instead of creating imaginary worlds with toys or acting out stories, some children are spending more time watching videos, playing digital games, or using apps.


Experts say imaginative play is an important part of childhood development. When children create their own games, they learn how to solve problems, communicate ideas, develop language skills, and understand different perspectives.


Unlike passive entertainment, imaginative play allows children to become creators rather than only consumers.


## Parents Want More Time for Creative Play


The research also showed that many children would like their parents to become more involved in pretend play and creative activities.


Nearly four in ten children said they wished their parents would join them more often in imaginative games. This suggests that children still value shared experiences and creative interaction, even in a world filled with digital distractions.


However, many parents admitted that finding time and ideas for imaginative activities can be difficult. Busy work schedules, household responsibilities, and modern lifestyle pressures often leave families relying on screens to keep children occupied.


More than one in five parents said they sometimes struggled to think of new creative games. Others said everyday pressures made it harder to spend quality time playing with their children.


## Why Imagination Matters in Childhood Development


Child development specialists emphasize that imagination is not just a form of entertainment. It plays an important role in helping children develop essential skills.


Creative play allows children to experiment with ideas, create stories, explore emotions, and practise decision making. When a child pretends to be a teacher, doctor, explorer, or superhero, they are learning about the world around them.


Child development psychologist Amanda Gummer explained that when children immediately turn to screens whenever they feel bored, they may lose opportunities to practise creating their own stories and games.


Imaginative activities give children the freedom to control their own experiences. They can change the rules, invent characters, and explore new possibilities without restrictions.


## The Role of Screen Time During School Holidays


School holidays can make the challenge even greater. Many parents say that when they are working or managing daily responsibilities, screens become a convenient way to keep children entertained.


The research found that a significant number of children spend several hours each day using screens during holiday periods.


Technology itself is not the problem. Educational apps, online resources, and digital tools can support learning when used responsibly. The concern is when screen time replaces activities that encourage physical movement, creativity, social interaction, and independent thinking.


A balanced approach can help children enjoy the benefits of technology while still protecting time for traditional play.


## How Parents Can Encourage More Imaginative Play


Parents do not need expensive toys or complicated activities to encourage creativity. Simple changes can make a big difference.


Children can be encouraged to:


- Build creative projects using household materials.
- Spend more time outdoors exploring nature.
- Create stories, drawings, or pretend scenarios.
- Play traditional board games and puzzles.
- Invent new games with simple rules.
- Spend device free time with family members.


Parents can also join children in their imaginary worlds. A few minutes of pretend play can help strengthen family connections and encourage children to continue developing their creativity.


The goal is not to remove technology completely but to create a healthier balance between digital entertainment and real world experiences.


## The Future of Childhood Play in a Digital World


Technology will continue to be a major part of children’s lives, and digital skills are becoming increasingly important. However, creativity, imagination, and social skills remain essential for personal growth.


The challenge for families is finding a balance where children can benefit from technology without losing the ability to entertain themselves, create new ideas, and explore the world around them.


Boredom may feel uncomfortable, but it can also be the starting point for creativity. Giving children space to think, experiment, and invent may help protect one of the most valuable parts of childhood: imagination.
