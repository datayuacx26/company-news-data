# UK's Biggest Solar Eclipse in Nearly 30 Years: Best Places to Watch, Timings, and Safe Viewing Guide (2026)

The UK is preparing for one of the most spectacular astronomical events in decades. On **12 August 2026** , millions of people across Britain will have the chance to witness the country's biggest solar eclipse since 1999. As the Moon passes between the Earth and the Sun, much of the Sun's surface will be covered, creating an unforgettable evening sky.


For skywatchers, photographers, and families, this rare event offers an exciting opportunity to experience one of nature's most impressive displays. Whether you are planning to travel to the southwest coast or simply step outside your home, knowing the best viewing locations, exact timings, and essential safety advice will help you make the most of this once in a generation spectacle.


In this guide, we explain everything you need to know about the **UK solar eclipse 2026** , including where to see it, when it happens, and how to watch safely.


## What Is a Solar Eclipse?


A solar eclipse happens when the Moon moves directly between the Earth and the Sun, blocking some or all of the Sun's light. This alignment is rare because the Moon's orbit does not perfectly match the Earth's orbit around the Sun.


Depending on your location, you may experience one of three types of solar eclipse:


- **Partial solar eclipse:** Only part of the Sun is covered.
- **Total solar eclipse:** The Moon completely blocks the Sun.
- **Annular solar eclipse:** The Moon appears slightly smaller than the Sun, leaving a bright ring around it.


For most people in the UK, the August 2026 event will be a **partial solar eclipse** , although it will be one of the deepest partial eclipses seen in decades.


## Where Is the Best Place to See the UK Solar Eclipse?


The southwest of England will offer the most impressive views.


People in areas including:


- Cornwall
- Devon
- Somerset
- South Wales


will experience up to **96% of the Sun covered by the Moon** , making this the closest the UK has come to a total solar eclipse since the famous eclipse of 1999.


Residents across England, Scotland, Wales, and Northern Ireland will still be able to see the eclipse, although the percentage of the Sun covered will vary depending on location.


Outside the UK, the eclipse will also be visible across much of Europe, including:


- France
- Belgium
- Switzerland
- Northern Italy


Meanwhile, observers in **Iceland, Greenland, and northern Spain** will enjoy a **total solar eclipse** , where the Sun is completely hidden behind the Moon.


Parts of northeastern Canada and the northeastern United States will also experience a smaller partial eclipse.


## UK Solar Eclipse Timings


The eclipse takes place during the evening on **Wednesday, 12 August 2026** .


For London, the key times are:


Event Time


First contact 6:17 pm


Maximum eclipse 7:12 pm


Eclipse ends Shortly after 8:00 pm


These times vary slightly across different parts of the UK by only a few minutes.


Because the eclipse occurs in the evening, many people may enjoy dramatic sunset views as the partially eclipsed Sun moves closer to the horizon.


## Why Is This Eclipse So Special?


Solar eclipses require an incredibly precise alignment between the Earth, Moon, and Sun.


Although partial eclipses happen somewhere around the world every year, seeing such a deep eclipse from the UK is much less common.


The last major eclipse visible across Britain occurred in **August 1999** , when parts of Cornwall and Devon experienced totality.


For many younger people, the 2026 eclipse will be the first opportunity to witness such an extraordinary astronomical event without travelling abroad.


## How to Watch the Solar Eclipse Safely


Watching a solar eclipse is exciting, but it must always be done safely.


Looking directly at the Sun without proper eye protection can permanently damage your eyesight, even when much of the Sun appears covered.


### Use Certified Eclipse Glasses


The safest option is to wear certified eclipse glasses that meet recognised international safety standards.


These glasses are specially designed to block harmful solar radiation while allowing you to view the eclipse safely.


Always inspect eclipse glasses before use. If they are scratched, torn, or damaged, replace them.


### Do Not Use Regular Sunglasses


Normal sunglasses, no matter how dark they appear, do not provide enough protection for viewing the Sun.


They allow dangerous levels of solar radiation to reach your eyes and should never be used during an eclipse.


### Avoid Old Eclipse Glasses


If you still own eclipse glasses from previous events, check their condition carefully.


Protective filters can degrade over time or become damaged during storage, reducing their effectiveness.


## Alternative Ways to Watch Without Looking at the Sun


If you do not have eclipse glasses, there are several safe methods for viewing the eclipse indirectly.


Popular options include:


- Making a simple pinhole projector using cardboard.
- Using a pinhole viewer.
- Projecting the Sun's image onto white paper.
- Looking at sunlight filtered through tree leaves.
- Using a kitchen colander to create multiple crescent-shaped projections on the ground.


These methods allow you to enjoy the eclipse without exposing your eyes to direct sunlight.


## What Will the Eclipse Look Like?


As the Moon gradually covers the Sun, daylight will begin to fade.


Many people notice:


- Cooler air temperatures.
- Strange shadows on the ground.
- Changes in bird and animal behaviour.
- An unusual twilight effect despite it being early evening.


Although the UK will not experience complete darkness in most locations, the atmosphere will still feel remarkably different from an ordinary summer evening.


Photographers may also capture dramatic landscapes as the partially eclipsed Sun approaches sunset.


## What If the Weather Is Cloudy?


British weather is always unpredictable, and cloud cover may affect visibility in some areas.


If skies are overcast, you still have several options:


- Check live weather forecasts before travelling.
- Move to a nearby area with clearer skies if possible.
- Watch livestreams from observatories broadcasting the eclipse online.


Many astronomy organisations and observatories will provide high quality live coverage, allowing viewers around the world to enjoy the event regardless of local weather conditions.


## Tips for the Best Eclipse Viewing Experience


To make the most of the event:


- Arrive at your viewing location early.
- Choose an open area with a clear western horizon.
- Bring certified eclipse glasses.
- Check weather forecasts throughout the day.
- Carry a chair, water, and warm clothing for the evening.
- Avoid using cameras or telescopes without proper solar filters.


If photographing the eclipse, use approved solar filters specifically designed for camera equipment to prevent both eye injury and equipment damage.


## Final Thoughts


The **12 August 2026 solar eclipse** promises to be one of the UK's most memorable astronomical events in nearly three decades. With up to **96% of the Sun obscured** in parts of southwest England, millions of people will have the chance to witness an extraordinary natural phenomenon.


Whether you are an experienced astronomer or simply curious about the night sky, preparing in advance by choosing a good viewing location, checking the weather, and using proper eye protection will ensure you enjoy this rare event safely.


Do not miss this unique opportunity to witness one of nature's most spectacular celestial displays.
