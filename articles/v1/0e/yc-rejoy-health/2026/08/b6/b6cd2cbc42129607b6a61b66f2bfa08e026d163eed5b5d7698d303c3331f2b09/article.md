# Lettuce Crisis in the US: Food Safety Scare and Rising Costs Put Restaurants Under Pressure

The US restaurant industry is facing a difficult period as concerns over lettuce safety combine with rising ingredient costs. A nationwide Cyclospora outbreak linked to shredded iceberg lettuce has caused thousands of illnesses and increased worries among customers, especially those who regularly visit salad-focused restaurants and fast-food chains.


The outbreak, which began in late June 2026, has affected consumer confidence at a time when restaurants are already dealing with higher food expenses. While health officials continue investigating the exact sources of contamination, businesses that depend heavily on fresh lettuce have experienced a noticeable impact.


According to health authorities, the initial investigation connected the outbreak to shredded iceberg lettuce served at Taco Bell locations in several US states, including Indiana, Kentucky, Michigan, Ohio, and West Virginia. As more cases were reported, investigators expanded their focus to additional states, including Illinois, Kansas, Oklahoma, and Pennsylvania.


Taco Bell responded by voluntarily removing shredded iceberg lettuce supplied by Taylor Farms from its US restaurants on July 17. The company’s decision was part of efforts to protect customers while health officials continued their investigation.


## Restaurants Experience Declining Customer Visits


The lettuce-related health concerns have affected restaurant traffic, particularly for businesses associated with fresh salads and vegetable-based meals.


Market research data from Placer.ai showed that visits to Taco Bell locations across the country dropped significantly by late July. The decline reflected growing customer caution following reports of illnesses connected to contaminated lettuce.


Food safety experts have suggested that the situation may involve more than one source. Craig Hedberg, a food safety expert at the University of Minnesota, noted that the increasing number of reported cases indicated a more complicated outbreak investigation.


As officials continue gathering information, other restaurant chains that rely heavily on fresh produce have also experienced customer concerns, even though they have not been directly linked to the outbreak.


Salad-focused brands, including Chopt and Sweetgreen, reported changes in customer behavior. Chopt saw a decline in visits during the outbreak period, while Sweetgreen experienced lower weekly spending compared with the same period in the previous year.


Sweetgreen stated that none of its ingredients had been connected to the outbreak and emphasized that the company follows strict food safety procedures, including ingredient tracking from farms to restaurants.


## Rising Lettuce Prices Add More Pressure


The food safety concerns come during a challenging time for the lettuce supply chain. Restaurants have already been dealing with increased ingredient costs caused by weather conditions, transportation expenses, and supply shortages.


Government data shows that iceberg lettuce prices increased significantly during the first half of 2026. Fresh lettuce prices were also considerably higher compared with the previous year, adding pressure to restaurants that use large quantities of greens.


Several factors contributed to the price increase. Agricultural experts explained that unusually hot weather in Arizona affected lettuce production earlier in the year. The warmer conditions caused crops to mature faster than expected, creating supply shortages before California’s growing season could fully increase availability.


Transportation costs have also played a role. Lettuce requires careful handling and continuous refrigeration from farms to grocery stores and restaurants. Higher fuel and logistics expenses have made the entire supply chain more expensive.


For restaurants, the combination of higher prices and customer concerns creates a difficult situation. Businesses must maintain food quality and safety standards while managing increased operational costs.


## Lettuce Has Faced Food Safety Challenges Before


The current Cyclospora outbreak is not the first time lettuce has been connected to major food safety concerns.


In 2018, romaine lettuce was linked to multiple E. coli outbreaks associated with growing regions in Arizona and California. Those incidents led to recalls, consumer warnings, and increased discussions about improving safety practices throughout the produce industry.


These previous events have encouraged restaurants, farmers, and regulators to strengthen monitoring systems. Modern food safety efforts include better traceability programs, improved farming practices, and faster responses when contamination is suspected.


However, leafy greens remain challenging to manage because they are often eaten raw. Unlike foods that are cooked at high temperatures, salads and fresh vegetables may not have a final preparation step that eliminates harmful bacteria or parasites.


## Consumers Become More Careful About Fresh Foods


Following reports of illnesses, many consumers are becoming more selective about where they buy salads and fresh produce. Food safety has become an increasingly important factor when choosing restaurants.


Experts say transparency and communication are essential during food-related health events. Restaurants that provide clear information about ingredient sourcing, safety procedures, and response actions may be better positioned to maintain customer trust.


The outbreak also highlights the importance of supply chain monitoring. From farms and transportation companies to restaurants and retailers, every stage plays a role in protecting consumers.


## What This Means for the Restaurant Industry


The lettuce crisis demonstrates how quickly food safety concerns can affect businesses. A single ingredient issue can influence customer behavior across an entire category, even impacting companies that are not involved in the outbreak.


Restaurants may need to review their supplier relationships, improve tracking systems, and prepare stronger communication strategies for future incidents.


At the same time, customers are likely to continue demanding safer and more transparent food production practices. As fresh food remains a major part of modern dining trends, maintaining confidence in ingredients will remain a priority for the restaurant industry.


The ongoing investigation into the Cyclospora outbreak will determine the full scope of the contamination and whether additional sources are identified. Until then, restaurants and consumers are closely watching developments while navigating a challenging period for one of America’s most common food ingredients.
