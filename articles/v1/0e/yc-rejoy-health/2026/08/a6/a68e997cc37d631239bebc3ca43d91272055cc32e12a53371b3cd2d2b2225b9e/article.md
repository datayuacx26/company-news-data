# Raw Dog Food Recall 2026: Oma’s Pride Chicken Recipe Pulled Over Salmonella Concerns

Pet owners are being urged to check their freezers after Oma’s Pride announced a recall of one lot of its frozen raw dog food because of possible **Salmonella contamination** . The recall affects a specific 6-pound package of Woof Complete Canine Chicken Recipe distributed across several U.S. states.


The U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) published the company’s recall announcement on August 12, 2026. According to the FDA, the affected product was recalled after testing identified Salmonella contamination. The recall involves one specific lot, meaning consumers should carefully check the product details before deciding whether their dog food is affected.


## Which Dog Food Has Been Recalled?


The recalled product is **Oma’s Pride Woof Complete Canine Chicken Recipe** , a frozen raw dog food sold in a 6-pound bag.


The affected packages contain 12 individually wrapped portions, with each portion weighing 8 ounces. The product was distributed through retail and wholesale accounts and was also shipped directly to customers who placed online orders.


The FDA says the recall involves **639 bags** from a single lot.


Consumers should look for these identifying details:


- **Product:** Woof Complete Canine Chicken Recipe
- **Package size:** 6-pound bag
- **Item or SKU:** F-WOOFC-6
- **Lot number:** BB012729
- **Manufacturing date:** January 27, 2026
- **Best-by date:** January 27, 2029
- **UPC:** 8 7938400145 9


The FDA states that no other Oma’s Pride products, package sizes, or lots are included in this particular recall.


## Where Was the Recalled Dog Food Sold?


The recalled raw dog food was distributed in 11 states.


The affected states are:


**Arizona, California, Indiana, Kentucky, Louisiana, Maryland, New Jersey, Nevada, New York, Pennsylvania, and Virginia.**


Because the product was also sold through direct online orders, pet owners who purchased frozen dog food online should check their product information even if they did not buy it from a traditional pet store.


The distribution information comes from the company announcement published by the FDA.


## Why Is Salmonella a Concern?


Salmonella is a type of bacteria that can cause illness in animals and humans. Contaminated raw pet food can create a potential health risk not only for pets that consume it, but also for people who handle the food, feeding dishes, storage containers, or surfaces that may have come into contact with it.


Dogs exposed to Salmonella may develop symptoms such as diarrhea, vomiting, fever, lethargy, decreased appetite, or abdominal discomfort. Diarrhea can sometimes contain blood. However, some infected animals may show few or no obvious symptoms.


An important concern is that an infected animal can potentially carry and spread Salmonella to other animals or people. That makes careful handling and cleaning particularly important when a recalled product has been present in a household.


The FDA advises pet owners to contact a veterinarian if a dog that consumed the recalled food develops symptoms associated with Salmonella infection.


## What Are the Possible Risks to People?


People can also become ill after exposure to Salmonella. Common symptoms can include nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, abdominal cramps, and fever.


Anyone who develops symptoms after handling the recalled dog food should consider contacting a healthcare professional, particularly if symptoms are severe or persistent.


People with weakened immune systems, older adults, young children, and other individuals who may be more vulnerable to foodborne infections should take extra precautions around potentially contaminated pet products.


The recall does not mean that everyone who handled the product will become sick. Rather, the concern is that contaminated food can provide an opportunity for exposure, which is why the recalled product should not be fed to pets or handled unnecessarily.


## What Should Pet Owners Do?


If you have the recalled Oma’s Pride product, the first step is to **stop feeding it to your dog immediately** .


Do not sell, donate, or give the recalled food to another pet owner. Instead, follow safe disposal practices and contact Oma’s Pride regarding a refund.


Pet owners should also thoroughly clean and sanitize items that may have touched the recalled food. This includes food bowls, preparation areas, storage containers, utensils, and other surfaces.


Hands should be washed thoroughly after handling the recalled product or anything that may have come into contact with it.


These precautions are particularly important because raw pet food can potentially contaminate household surfaces if it is handled improperly.


## What Did Oma’s Pride Say?


Oma’s Pride said it is voluntarily recalling the affected lot and is investigating the situation further. The company described food safety as a priority and said the recall is being conducted in coordination with the FDA.


According to the FDA notice, Oma’s Pride is a fourth-generation, family-owned company with more than 75 years of food manufacturing experience. The company also stated that its operations follow applicable FDA and USDA requirements and include sanitation, traceability, lot-control, and food-safety procedures.


Consumers with questions can contact Oma’s Pride at **1-800-678-OMAS** , Monday through Friday from 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. EST, or by email athello@omaspride.com .


## What If Your Dog Already Ate the Recalled Food?


If your dog consumed the affected product, do not panic, but monitor the animal closely.


Watch for symptoms including vomiting, diarrhea, bloody diarrhea, fever, lethargy, reduced appetite, or signs of abdominal discomfort. If symptoms appear, contact your veterinarian and explain that your dog may have been exposed to a product recalled because of Salmonella contamination.


It is also important to tell your veterinarian about the specific product and lot number when possible. This can help provide useful information when assessing your pet.


People in the household who handled the food should similarly monitor themselves for symptoms and contact a healthcare provider if they become ill.


## Why Checking Pet Food Recalls Matters


Pet food recalls can happen for several reasons, including bacterial contamination, foreign material, incorrect labeling, or other potential safety problems. The FDA maintains recall information to help consumers identify products that may pose risks.


This latest recall is a reminder that checking lot numbers and product labels can be just as important as checking the brand name. A recall may apply to only one production lot rather than an entire product line.


For this reason, pet owners should avoid assuming that every product from the same company is affected. In this case, the FDA specifically says that other Oma’s Pride products, sizes, and lots are not part of the recall.


## Bottom Line for Dog Owners


The **Oma’s Pride Woof Complete Canine Chicken Recipe 6-pound frozen raw dog food** recall affects lot **BB012729** and involves a Salmonella contamination concern.


If your package matches the recalled product details, stop feeding it to your pet, dispose of it safely, clean potentially contaminated surfaces and feeding equipment, and contact Oma’s Pride for refund information.


If your pet becomes ill after eating the recalled food, contact a veterinarian. People who develop possible Salmonella symptoms after handling the product should contact a healthcare professional.


Pet owners can also check the FDA’s official recall information for updates as the investigation continues.
