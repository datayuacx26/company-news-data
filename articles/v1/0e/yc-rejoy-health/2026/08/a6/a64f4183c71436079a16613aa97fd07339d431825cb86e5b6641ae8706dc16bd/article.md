# Rectal Bleeding Before Age 50 May Be a Major Warning Sign of Colorectal Cancer, Study Finds

## Early Symptoms of Colorectal Cancer Should Not Be Ignored


Colorectal cancer has traditionally been viewed as a disease that mainly affects older adults, but recent research shows a worrying increase among younger people. A new study suggests that one symptom in particular may help doctors identify younger patients who could be at higher risk: rectal bleeding.


Researchers found that adults under the age of 50 who experienced rectal bleeding were significantly more likely to be diagnosed with early-onset colorectal cancer compared with those who did not report this symptom. The findings highlight the importance of taking unusual changes in bowel health seriously, even among younger adults who may not consider themselves at risk.


According to a report published by Page Six on October 3, 2025, researchers from the University of Louisville reviewed medical records of younger patients who underwent colonoscopies and identified rectal bleeding as one of the strongest indicators linked to colorectal cancer diagnosis.


## Study Reveals a Strong Link Between Rectal Bleeding and Cancer Risk


The study examined 443 patients under the age of 50 who received colonoscopies between 2021 and 2023 at University of Louisville Health. Nearly half of the patients were diagnosed with early-onset colorectal cancer.


Among the symptoms reviewed, rectal bleeding stood out as a major warning sign. Researchers reported that younger patients with bleeding symptoms had a much higher likelihood of being diagnosed with colorectal cancer compared with those without that symptom.


The research suggests that rectal bleeding should not automatically be dismissed as a minor issue, such as hemorrhoids or temporary irritation. While many cases of bleeding are caused by non-cancerous conditions, doctors say it is important to investigate persistent or unexplained bleeding.


Dr. Sandra Kavalukas, a colorectal surgeon at the University of Louisville School of Medicine and the lead author of the study, said the findings may help doctors determine which younger patients with symptoms should receive further evaluation, including colonoscopy screening.


## Colorectal Cancer Rates Are Rising Among Younger Adults


Colorectal cancer rates among younger adults have been increasing for decades. While overall cancer rates in older populations have improved due to better screening practices, early-onset colorectal cancer continues to grow among people under 50.


Experts believe several factors may contribute to this increase, including lifestyle changes, dietary patterns, obesity rates, reduced physical activity, and environmental influences. However, researchers are still studying the exact reasons behind the trend.


One major concern is that younger patients are often diagnosed later because symptoms may be overlooked. Many people assume they are too young to have colorectal cancer, which can delay testing and treatment.


Early detection plays a critical role in improving outcomes. When colorectal cancer is found at an earlier stage, treatment options are generally more effective.


## Family History Is Not the Only Risk Factor


A common misconception is that colorectal cancer mainly affects people with a family history of the disease. While genetics can increase risk, the study found that many younger patients diagnosed with colorectal cancer did not have relatives with the condition.


Researchers reported that approximately 70% of younger cancer patients in the study had no family history of colorectal cancer. Only a small percentage had genetic markers commonly associated with inherited cancer syndromes.


This means people without a known family history should still pay attention to warning signs. Personal health history, lifestyle factors, and symptoms can all play a role in determining cancer risk.


## Other Factors Linked to Early-Onset Colorectal Cancer


The study also identified smoking history as a potential risk factor. Former smokers were found to have a higher risk of early-onset colorectal cancer compared with people who had never smoked.


In addition, health experts have pointed to several lifestyle factors that may influence colorectal cancer risk, including:


• Low physical activity levels
• Diets high in processed foods
• Excess body weight
• Limited consumption of fiber-rich foods
• Certain environmental exposures


These factors do not guarantee that someone will develop cancer, but maintaining healthy habits may help reduce overall health risks.


## When Should Younger Adults Consider Screening?


Current screening recommendations generally advise average-risk adults to begin colorectal cancer screening at age 45. However, doctors emphasize that people younger than the recommended screening age may need evaluation if they experience concerning symptoms.


Warning signs that should be discussed with a healthcare professional include:


• Blood in the stool or rectal bleeding
• Persistent changes in bowel habits
• Unexplained weight loss
• Ongoing abdominal discomfort or pain
• Unusual fatigue
• Changes in stool appearance


Doctors say symptoms should not be ignored simply because someone is young. A healthcare provider can determine whether additional testing is appropriate based on a person’s symptoms and medical history.


## Colonoscopy Remains an Important Detection Tool


Colonoscopy is one of the most effective methods for detecting colorectal cancer and preventing it. During the procedure, doctors can identify abnormal growths called polyps and remove them before they have a chance to become cancerous.


Other screening methods, including stool-based tests, may also help detect signs of colorectal cancer. However, people with symptoms such as rectal bleeding may require a more detailed evaluation.


The goal of research like this is not to encourage unnecessary testing but to help identify younger patients who may benefit from earlier medical attention.


## The Importance of Listening to Your Body


The rise of colorectal cancer among younger adults serves as a reminder that age does not eliminate health risks. Changes in digestion, bowel habits, or unexplained bleeding deserve attention.


Many people delay seeking medical advice because they fear being seen as overly concerned. However, early conversations with a healthcare professional can make a significant difference when it comes to detecting serious conditions.


Understanding symptoms, knowing personal risk factors, and following recommended screening guidelines are important steps in protecting long-term health.
