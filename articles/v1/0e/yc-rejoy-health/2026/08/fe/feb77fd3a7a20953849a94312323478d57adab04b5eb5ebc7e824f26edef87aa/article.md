# NHS Issues Urgent Warning About Fake Medicines: How UK Patients Can Stay Safe Online

## Fake medicines are putting UK patients at risk


The NHS has issued an urgent warning to people across the UK about the growing threat of fake medicines being sold through unregulated websites, social media platforms, and other unofficial sellers.


As more people turn to the internet to purchase healthcare products, weight loss treatments, supplements, and medical devices, criminals are increasingly using fake online pharmacies and copied websites to sell unsafe products. These counterfeit medicines may not contain the correct ingredients, may be contaminated, or could be completely different from what is advertised.


Health professionals are urging patients to be cautious and only buy medicines from trusted and registered sources. A simple check before purchasing can help prevent serious health risks and ensure that people receive safe and effective treatment.


## Why fake medicines are becoming a growing problem


Online shopping has made it easier than ever to access healthcare products, but it has also created opportunities for dishonest sellers. Some websites are designed to look like legitimate pharmacies, making it difficult for customers to identify whether they are genuine.


Fake online pharmacies may use professional-looking websites, misleading advertisements, and unrealistic offers to attract buyers. Some even copy the branding and appearance of trusted healthcare providers.


Weight loss medicines have become a particular concern, with many products being promoted through social media advertisements and unofficial websites. Some sellers claim their products offer quick results without requiring medical checks or a prescription.


However, medicines that are supplied without proper medical assessment can be dangerous. Patients may not know what ingredients they are taking, whether the product is suitable for their health condition, or whether it could interact with other medicines.


## NHS advice: Always use registered healthcare providers


NHS Thames Valley is encouraging people to avoid buying medicines and medical devices from websites or businesses that are not regulated.


Medicines that require a prescription should only be obtained through a qualified healthcare professional, such as a GP or registered pharmacy. Buying these products from unknown sources can expose patients to serious risks.


Olivier Picard, a Thames Valley pharmacist and Chair of the National Pharmacy Association, highlighted the increasing sophistication of fake online pharmacies. Criminal sellers are becoming better at creating websites that appear genuine and trustworthy.


He warned that medicines advertised online, especially weight loss treatments, may be counterfeit, contaminated, unsafe, or contain ingredients that are different from those listed in advertisements.


The advice from healthcare experts is clear: if an offer appears too good to be true, it may not be safe.


## How to identify a safe online pharmacy


Before purchasing any medicine online, patients should take time to check whether the seller is legitimate.


The General Pharmaceutical Council (GPhC) maintains a register of approved pharmacies operating in the UK. Checking this register is one of the most important steps consumers can take before buying medicines online.


A trustworthy online pharmacy should:


• Be registered with the General Pharmaceutical Council
• Clearly display registration details
• Require appropriate medical checks for prescription medicines
• Provide access to qualified healthcare professionals
• Avoid making unrealistic claims about products


Customers should be especially careful when websites offer prescription medicines without asking for medical information or provide large discounts that seem unusual.


## Social media advertisements can be misleading


Many fake medicine sellers now use social media platforms to promote their products. Advertisements may feature dramatic claims, customer testimonials, or promises of fast results.


While some online health information can be useful, social media advertisements are not always reviewed by healthcare professionals. A product promoted online does not automatically mean it is safe, approved, or effective.


Health experts recommend avoiding medicines purchased through social media sellers unless the seller can be verified as a registered healthcare provider.


Patients should also avoid sharing personal health information with unknown sellers or websites that do not have proper security and privacy measures.


## What to do if you have bought a suspicious medicine


People who are concerned about a medicine they purchased online should not ignore the issue. Speaking with a community pharmacist is a good first step.


Pharmacists are trained healthcare professionals who can provide advice about medicines, identify potential concerns, and recommend what action should be taken.


Patients should seek medical advice if they experience unusual symptoms after taking a medicine from an unfamiliar source. They should also keep any packaging, receipts, or information about where the product was purchased, as this may help healthcare professionals investigate the issue.


If a medicine looks different from expected, has unusual packaging, contains spelling mistakes, or arrives from an unknown supplier, it may be a warning sign.


## Protecting your health when buying medicines online


The internet can provide convenient access to healthcare products, but convenience should never come at the cost of safety.


Before buying any medicine online, patients should ask themselves a few important questions:


• Is the website registered and regulated?
• Does the seller require proper medical checks?
• Is the price unusually low compared with trusted providers?
• Are there clear contact details and professional information?
• Are claims being made that sound unrealistic?


Taking a few minutes to verify a seller could prevent serious health problems and protect people from counterfeit medicines.


Healthcare professionals continue to encourage patients to seek advice from GPs, pharmacists, and regulated healthcare providers whenever they have concerns about medicines or treatments.


## Final thoughts


The NHS warning about fake medicines highlights the importance of being careful when purchasing healthcare products online. Counterfeit medicines are not just ineffective, they can also pose serious risks to people’s health.


As online scams become more advanced, checking the legitimacy of a pharmacy has become an essential step for every patient. Using registered providers, seeking professional medical advice, and avoiding suspicious online offers can help ensure that people receive safe and effective treatment.


Your health should always come before convenience. When it comes to medicines, choosing a trusted source is one of the simplest ways to stay protected.
