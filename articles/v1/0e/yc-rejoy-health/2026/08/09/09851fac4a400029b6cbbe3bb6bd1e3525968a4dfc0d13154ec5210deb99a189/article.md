# Chemmy Alcott Hospitalised After Lake Jump Leaves BBC Presenter With Collapsed Lung

Former Olympic skier and BBC presenter **Chemmy Alcott** has revealed that a holiday adventure in the French Alps ended with an unexpected trip to hospital after she suffered a collapsed lung.


The 44-year-old Ski Sunday presenter shared details of the accident with her followers after jumping from a five-metre platform into Lake Annecy. What began as an adventurous moment during her holiday quickly turned into a medical emergency when she landed badly and subsequently experienced difficulty breathing.


Alcott later posted images from hospital, including a photograph showing her connected to medical equipment and another showing treatment involving a chest drain.


## Chemmy Alcott describes frightening holiday accident


According to the report supplied by The Sun, Alcott said she attempted a front flip from the platform but got the rotation wrong. The landing resulted in a serious chest injury and a collapsed lung.


Rather than describing the incident in overly dramatic terms, the presenter used humour when explaining what happened to her followers. She acknowledged that most people might simply get winded after making a mistake during a jump, but her accident had much more serious consequences.


She explained that the incident resulted in a collapsed lung and the insertion of a chest drain, as well as several additional nights in hospital.


Alcott also reflected on her experience in Annecy, saying she had thoroughly enjoyed the destination despite the accident. Her social media update appeared to combine humour with an honest account of what had happened.


The photographs she shared provided a stark contrast to the holiday footage. Instead of the picturesque Alpine setting, followers saw Alcott receiving hospital treatment and recovering from the injury.


## Fans rally around BBC presenter


Following the announcement, Alcott's followers quickly sent messages wishing her well.


Several people expressed surprise that the jump appeared relatively harmless when viewed on video. Others praised her for sharing the experience and wished her a speedy recovery.


The incident has attracted attention partly because of Alcott's background as an elite winter sports athlete. She has spent much of her career dealing with the physical risks associated with high-speed skiing, making her latest accident an unexpected development during what had been a leisure trip.


## Who is Chemmy Alcott?


Chemmy Alcott is a former British alpine skier who represented Great Britain at four Winter Olympics. She became particularly well known for competing in downhill and other speed events before later developing a career in television.


Her sporting career was marked by significant injuries. She previously suffered multiple fractures to her leg and eventually faced a difficult decision about whether continuing competitive skiing was worth the long-term risks.


Earlier in 2026, Alcott revealed that she had retired from competitive skiing following medical advice.


The decision was influenced by the extensive damage to her leg after repeated injuries. According to comments attributed to Alcott in the supplied report, a surgeon warned her that another serious fracture could potentially have resulted in an amputation below the knee.


That warning ultimately helped bring her competitive skiing career to an end.


## From Olympic skier to television presenter


Although Alcott stepped away from professional competition, she has remained closely connected to winter sports through broadcasting.


She has established herself as a television presenter and was involved in BBC coverage of the 2026 Winter Olympics in Milan and Cortina.


Her transition from athlete to broadcaster has allowed her to continue working in a sport she knows exceptionally well while moving away from the physical demands of elite competition.

Her latest accident demonstrates that the risks associated with adventure activities are not limited to professional athletes. Even experienced people can suffer serious injuries when a jump, dive or other manoeuvre goes wrong.


## What is a collapsed lung?


A collapsed lung, medically known as a pneumothorax, occurs when air enters the space between the lung and the chest wall. The trapped air can put pressure on the lung and cause part or all of it to collapse.


Symptoms can include sudden chest pain and shortness of breath. The severity can vary considerably, and treatment depends on the cause and extent of the problem.


In some cases, a small pneumothorax may be monitored while the body gradually reabsorbs the trapped air. More serious cases can require medical intervention, including the insertion of a chest tube or drain to remove air and allow the lung to expand again.


Anyone experiencing sudden breathing difficulties or significant chest pain after an accident should seek urgent medical attention.
