# The Simple Stair-Climbing Habit That Could Support a Longer, Healthier Life

For many people, improving their health can feel like it requires a major lifestyle overhaul. Joining a gym, following a strict exercise programme or finding extra time for long workouts can be difficult when daily schedules are already busy. But new research suggests that one of the simplest forms of everyday movement may offer meaningful health benefits: taking the stairs.


A recent review of research has found that regularly climbing stairs is associated with a lower risk of cardiovascular problems and premature death. The findings suggest that something as ordinary as choosing stairs instead of a lift or escalator could help people build more physical activity into their daily routines without needing to set aside a separate hour for exercise.


The research was carried out by academics from the University of East Anglia and specialists at Norfolk and Norwich University Hospital. Researchers reviewed five studies involving more than 450,000 people to examine the relationship between stair climbing and health outcomes.


According to the findings reported by The Independent, people who regularly climbed stairs had a 24 percent lower risk of dying from any cause compared with those who used stairs less frequently. They also had a 39 percent lower risk of dying from cardiovascular disease.


The research also pointed towards potential benefits involving several major cardiovascular conditions, including heart attacks, heart failure and strokes.


## Why climbing stairs can make a difference


Stair climbing may look like a small activity, but it can provide a surprisingly demanding form of movement. Unlike walking on a flat surface, climbing stairs requires the muscles of the legs to work against gravity while the heart and lungs respond to the increased physical demand.


That means a short climb can raise the heart rate relatively quickly. For people who spend much of the day sitting, these brief periods of movement may provide a useful way to interrupt long periods of inactivity.


One of the most appealing aspects of stair climbing is that it does not necessarily require a change of schedule. People can take the stairs at work, in shopping centres, at railway stations or in their homes. The activity can therefore become part of an existing routine rather than another appointment that has to be fitted into an already busy day.


Dr Sophie Paddock of the University of East Anglia's Norwich Medical School and Norfolk and Norwich University Hospital highlighted this practical advantage. She said stair climbing can be incorporated into everyday life and may be particularly useful for people who have limited time or access to conventional exercise.


This is important because lack of time is one of the barriers that can prevent people from being physically active. A short walk or stair climb may appear insignificant when considered on its own, but repeated throughout the week, these small bursts of movement can add up.


## Small amounts of activity can still matter


The research also adds to a wider understanding of how physical activity can be accumulated throughout the day.


Exercise does not always have to involve a long session at the gym or a structured workout. Everyday activities such as walking, climbing stairs, carrying shopping and moving around at work can all contribute to overall physical activity.


One of the studies included in the review indicated that climbing around six flights of stairs, equivalent to approximately 60 steps, could be associated with health benefits. This does not mean everyone should immediately aim for a particular number of steps, especially if they are not accustomed to strenuous activity. Instead, it illustrates how relatively short periods of stair climbing can form part of an active lifestyle.


For someone who normally takes the lift every day, choosing the stairs for one journey could be an easy starting point. Over time, that decision could become a habit.


The key is consistency. A single stair climb cannot transform cardiovascular health overnight. The potential benefit comes from making movement a regular part of everyday life.


## Cardiovascular health remains a major concern


The importance of regular physical activity becomes clearer when considering the global impact of cardiovascular disease.


Professor Vassilios Vassiliou of the University of East Anglia's Norwich Medical School said cardiovascular disease remains the leading cause of death worldwide and emphasised that many cardiovascular risk factors can be influenced through lifestyle.


Physical activity is one part of a broader approach to protecting heart health. Eating a balanced diet, avoiding smoking, maintaining a healthy weight and managing conditions such as high blood pressure, high cholesterol and diabetes can all play important roles.


Stair climbing should therefore not be viewed as a replacement for other healthy behaviours. Instead, it can be considered one practical tool within a wider approach to staying active.


The findings are also relevant to stroke prevention. Juliet Bouverie, chief executive of the Stroke Association, said the research contributes to growing evidence linking physical health and stroke risk.


She also highlighted the importance of exercise alongside other preventative measures, including a healthy diet, avoiding smoking and monitoring alcohol consumption. Blood pressure is particularly important because high blood pressure is a major risk factor for stroke.


## Making the stairs part of your routine


For people who are able to climb stairs safely, introducing more stair climbing does not have to be complicated.


A simple approach is to look for opportunities that already exist during the day. Taking the stairs instead of the lift at work, walking up a flight or two in a public building, or choosing stairs when entering or leaving a station can gradually increase daily movement.


It is also worth remembering that physical ability varies from person to person. Anyone with mobility difficulties, significant health concerns or symptoms such as chest pain or unusual breathlessness should seek appropriate medical advice before increasing strenuous activity.


For everyone else, the broader message from the research is encouraging. Health improvements do not necessarily require dramatic changes. Small decisions repeated consistently may contribute to better long-term health.


The stairs may be one of the most accessible opportunities because they are already present in many people's daily environments. There is no membership fee, specialist equipment or complicated routine required.


## A small choice with potential benefits


The latest research does not prove that stair climbing directly causes a reduction in premature death. The studies reviewed examined associations between stair use and health outcomes, so other differences between people who climb stairs regularly and those who do not may also contribute to the results.


Nevertheless, the findings reinforce an important public health message: regular movement matters.


For people looking for an uncomplicated way to become more active, choosing the stairs more often could be a realistic place to begin. It is a small change, but because it can be repeated during ordinary daily activities, it may be easier to maintain than a demanding exercise plan.


Ultimately, better health is rarely built through one decision. It is usually the result of many choices repeated over time. Taking the stairs may be one of those choices, offering a simple reminder that even everyday movement can have a meaningful place in a healthy lifestyle.
