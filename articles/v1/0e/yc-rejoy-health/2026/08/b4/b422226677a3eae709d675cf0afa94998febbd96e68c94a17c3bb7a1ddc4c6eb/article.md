# Holiday Stomach Bug Turns Out to Be Life-Threatening Sepsis After Appendix Burst

A young woman who initially appeared to have a routine stomach bug while on holiday later faced a life-threatening medical emergency after her appendix burst and caused sepsis.


Lottie Perks, now 20, became seriously ill after developing severe abdominal pain and vomiting during a family holiday in Brazil. At first, her symptoms appeared to improve, leaving her family to believe she had suffered from a stomach infection.


However, after returning home to London, her condition deteriorated rapidly. Her mother, Laura, said she became increasingly concerned when Lottie began shaking uncontrollably and developed extremely cold hands and feet, blue lips and unusually low body temperature.


The frightening experience has since encouraged the family to raise awareness of sepsis symptoms and the importance of seeking urgent medical attention when an illness appears to be getting worse.


## Severe stomach pain initially appeared to be a holiday illness


According to the family's account, Lottie developed crippling stomach pain and vomiting several days into their holiday.


After around 24 hours, she appeared to return to normal. The family therefore continued with their trip, believing the illness had passed.


The situation changed after they arrived back in London. Lottie began experiencing severe stomach pain and vomiting again, alongside diarrhoea and bleeding.


Her mother said Lottie was extremely pale and weak and could not stop vomiting, even when there was nothing left in her stomach.


The family went to an accident and emergency department, where Lottie was given fluids through an intravenous drip and monitored for several hours.


She was subsequently discharged with what doctors believed was a gastrointestinal illness. Her mother said blood tests had not shown elevated inflammation levels at that stage.


But Lottie's condition did not improve.


## Symptoms became increasingly alarming


Over the following hours, Lottie became much weaker. Laura said she contacted the hospital and asked whether appendicitis could be responsible for her daughter's symptoms.


The family later returned to hospital when Lottie's condition deteriorated further.


According to Laura, doctors initially believed dehydration was responsible. The situation became more concerning when an unusual rash and changes in the colour of Lottie's legs were noticed.


Her legs reportedly developed a mottled purple appearance, while her body became severely swollen.


Doctors then carried out further investigations, including a CT scan.


The scan revealed that Lottie's appendix had burst and that the resulting infection had led to sepsis.


## Burst appendix led to septic shock


A ruptured appendix can allow infected material to spread inside the abdomen. In severe cases, an infection can trigger sepsis, a potentially life-threatening reaction in which the body's response to infection can cause serious damage to organs.


Lottie's condition had progressed to septic shock by the time she collapsed.


Laura recalled seeing her daughter's eyes roll back as she collapsed into her arms. Her blood pressure had fallen dramatically while her heart rate became dangerously high.


Emergency treatment followed, including surgery and antibiotics.


According to the family's account, doctors warned that Lottie's organs were at risk of failing and that the situation was extremely serious.


Her recovery then became a waiting game.


After spending time in intensive care and requiring medication and machines to support her body, Lottie eventually began to improve. She was gradually taken off the treatments that had been keeping her alive.


Despite the severity of the illness, she eventually recovered sufficiently to return to university and successfully complete her first year.


## Recovery continued long after hospital treatment


Although Lottie survived the ordeal, the experience did not simply end when she left hospital.


She continues to experience health problems including episodes of sickness, abdominal pain, dizziness, fatigue and difficulty keeping warm, according to the family's account.


She also described the physical impact of her time in intensive care.


Having previously been active, she recalled becoming exhausted after walking only one lap of the intensive care unit during her recovery. The loss of muscle strength was another difficult part of returning to normal life.


The emotional consequences have also remained with both Lottie and her mother.


Laura said the experience had resulted in nightmares and had changed the family's attitude towards travelling abroad. Although they still want to enjoy holidays and explore new places, the possibility of a medical emergency now weighs more heavily on their minds.


## Why recognising sepsis symptoms matters


The family is now working with the Sepsis Trust to encourage people to take concerning symptoms seriously.


Sepsis can develop following an infection and can become life-threatening if it is not identified and treated promptly. Symptoms can vary considerably, which can make the condition difficult to recognise.


Possible warning signs can include confusion, extreme shivering, unusual changes in body temperature, breathing difficulties, a racing heart rate, mottled or discoloured skin, extreme weakness and a significant deterioration in someone's condition.


A person does not necessarily need to have an obvious wound for sepsis to develop. Infections affecting different parts of the body can potentially lead to sepsis.


Lottie's experience also highlights why persistent or worsening symptoms should not necessarily be dismissed simply because an earlier assessment was reassuring.


Her story is particularly notable because she was young and initially believed she had a common stomach illness.


Lottie said she had initially assumed she was experiencing food poisoning or a gastrointestinal infection. As the pain became worse, however, she became increasingly aware that something was seriously wrong.


She also described feeling frustrated that her symptoms were difficult to explain and that several other possibilities were considered before the rapidly progressing infection was identified.


## Listening to your body


One of the key lessons Lottie says she took from the experience is the importance of advocating for yourself when something does not feel right.


She explained that she now feels more confident about speaking up when she believes something is wrong.


The experience has also made her more aware that serious illnesses can sometimes look like more common conditions, particularly during the early stages.


For families, the story demonstrates how quickly a person's condition can change. Someone who initially appears to have a stomach bug can become seriously unwell if an underlying infection develops.


That does not mean every case of vomiting or abdominal pain is sepsis. Most stomach illnesses are not life-threatening. However, severe, persistent or rapidly worsening symptoms warrant medical attention, particularly when accompanied by unusual changes in temperature, skin colour, consciousness, breathing or circulation.


Lottie's recovery has given her family a chance to reflect on how close the situation came to becoming fatal.


Today, she is continuing her studies and rebuilding her strength while living with the longer-term effects of her illness.


Her family hopes that sharing the story will encourage others to recognise when an illness is no longer following an ordinary pattern.


The message is simple: if someone becomes dramatically more unwell, develops worrying new symptoms or you believe something is seriously wrong, seek urgent medical help and clearly explain what has changed.
