# El Niño Could Push 49 Million More People Into Hunger, UN Warns

Climate change continues to reshape weather patterns across the globe, and one of the most concerning developments is the expected return of a powerful El Niño event. According to the United Nations World Food Programme (WFP), a very strong El Niño could leave nearly 49 million additional people facing acute hunger by the end of next year.


The warning highlights the growing connection between extreme weather, food security, and humanitarian crises. While global food production has remained relatively stable in recent years, changing climate conditions combined with ongoing conflicts and economic challenges could place millions of vulnerable families at greater risk.


## What Is El Niño?


El Niño is a naturally occurring climate phenomenon caused by unusually warm sea surface temperatures in the central and eastern Pacific Ocean. Although it begins in the Pacific, its effects are felt worldwide.


During an El Niño event, many regions experience significant changes in rainfall and temperature patterns. Some areas suffer prolonged droughts that damage crops and reduce water supplies, while others experience severe flooding that destroys farmland, homes, and infrastructure.


Scientists are currently monitoring Pacific Ocean temperatures closely, and there is a high probability that this developing El Niño could become one of the strongest recorded in modern history.


## UN Warns of Rising Global Hunger


The World Food Programme estimates that nearly 49 million more people could experience acute food insecurity if the current forecasts become reality. This would increase the total number of people facing severe hunger across 45 vulnerable countries to approximately 274 million.


Acute food insecurity refers to situations where people cannot consistently access enough safe and nutritious food, putting their health and livelihoods at serious risk.


The warning comes as humanitarian organizations prepare for the potential impact of widespread weather disruptions affecting agriculture and food distribution.


## Regions Expected to Face the Greatest Impact


Several parts of the world are expected to experience especially severe consequences if the El Niño pattern strengthens.


### Central America


Central America could see one of the sharpest increases in food insecurity. Reduced rainfall may affect staple crops such as maize and beans, leaving farming communities with lower harvests and reduced incomes.


Many households in the region already struggle with high food prices and economic uncertainty, making them particularly vulnerable.


### Southern Africa


Southern Africa is another region expected to face significant challenges. Drought conditions may reduce agricultural production, affecting both commercial farms and smallholder farmers.


Millions of people rely on seasonal harvests for food and income, meaning poor rainfall can quickly lead to food shortages.


### South America and the Caribbean


Parts of South America and the Caribbean may also experience worsening food insecurity. Agricultural losses, changing rainfall patterns, and disruptions to local food systems could increase the number of people requiring humanitarian assistance.


### East Africa and the Sahel


Forecasts suggest below average rainfall across parts of East Africa and the Sahel. Reduced precipitation may damage crops, lower livestock productivity, and increase pressure on already fragile communities.


Conversely, countries such as Somalia could face heightened flooding risks, creating another layer of humanitarian concern.


## Climate Events Can Disrupt Global Food Systems


Extreme weather does not only affect farmers. It has consequences throughout the entire food supply chain.


When crops fail due to drought or floods, food supplies decline. Lower production often leads to higher prices in local and international markets. Families with limited incomes are typically the first to struggle with rising food costs.


Transport infrastructure can also suffer during extreme weather events. Flooded roads and damaged bridges make it harder to deliver food and humanitarian aid to affected communities.


As a result, climate events often trigger a combination of reduced food availability, higher prices, and limited access for vulnerable populations.


## Food Supplies Are Currently More Stable


Despite the concerning forecast, there is some positive news.


The World Food Programme noted that global food supplies are entering this El Niño period in relatively good condition following several strong harvest seasons. This stronger starting point may help reduce some immediate pressure on international food markets.


However, experts caution that this advantage could quickly disappear if extreme weather becomes widespread or if geopolitical conflicts continue disrupting trade and agricultural production.


## Funding Challenges Could Slow Humanitarian Response


One of the major concerns raised by the World Food Programme is the decline in funding available for humanitarian operations.


Reduced financial support from major international donors has affected the agency's ability to collect food security data and monitor vulnerable populations.


Accurate and timely information is essential during climate emergencies because it allows governments and aid organizations to identify emerging crises before they escalate.


Without sufficient monitoring, humanitarian responses may be delayed, potentially placing more lives at risk.


## Preparing Before Disaster Strikes


Rather than waiting until communities face severe food shortages, humanitarian organizations increasingly focus on anticipatory action.


This strategy involves providing assistance before the worst impacts occur.


Support may include cash transfers, emergency food assistance, agricultural resources, and early warning systems that help communities prepare for changing weather conditions.


According to the WFP, anticipatory aid programs have already reached more than 1.3 million people across multiple countries this year.


These early interventions can help families protect their livelihoods, preserve livestock, and reduce the long term impacts of climate related disasters.


## Why This Matters for the World


Food insecurity is not an isolated problem affecting only individual countries.


Large scale hunger can contribute to increased poverty, displacement, migration, political instability, and greater demand for international humanitarian assistance.


Climate related food shortages also have economic consequences, affecting global food prices and agricultural markets.


As climate change increases the frequency and intensity of extreme weather events, experts expect governments, aid agencies, and international organizations to invest more heavily in resilience, disaster preparedness, and sustainable agriculture.


Improving irrigation systems, developing drought resistant crops, strengthening early warning systems, and expanding humanitarian funding are among the strategies that could help reduce future risks.


## Looking Ahead


The coming months will be critical as scientists continue monitoring Pacific Ocean temperatures and weather developments.


If forecasts remain accurate, many vulnerable regions will need rapid support to prepare for potential droughts, floods, and declining agricultural production.


Although stronger global food supplies provide some reassurance, millions of people remain vulnerable to the combined effects of climate change, conflict, and economic pressures.


The United Nations and humanitarian organizations continue urging governments and international donors to act early, emphasizing that proactive measures can save lives and reduce the long term impact of future food crises.


## Conclusion


The warning from the World Food Programme serves as a reminder that climate events like El Niño can have far reaching consequences beyond changing weather patterns. They can threaten food production, increase hunger, and place millions of families at risk.


Preparing vulnerable communities before disasters occur, strengthening food systems, and maintaining humanitarian support will be essential if the world hopes to reduce the impact of this potentially historic El Niño event.
