# Chipotle Salmonella Lawsuit: Woman Hospitalized With Sepsis After Jalapeño-Linked Burrito Bowl

A Minnesota woman who was hospitalized with sepsis after developing a severe Salmonella infection has filed a lawsuit against Chipotle, claiming that contaminated jalapeño peppers in a burrito bowl caused her illness.


The lawsuit comes as federal health officials investigate a Salmonella outbreak linked to jalapeño peppers sourced from Mexico. According to health authorities, hundreds of people across the United States have been infected with the outbreak strain, with dozens requiring hospitalization.


## Minnesota woman files lawsuit against Chipotle


Kristen Behne, a Minnesota resident, says she became seriously ill after eating at a Chipotle restaurant in Roseville, Minnesota, on June 24.


According to the lawsuit, Behne began experiencing symptoms several days later, including fever, chills, and vomiting. Her condition reportedly worsened rapidly, and she eventually developed severe diarrhea and sepsis.


Her attorneys claim that laboratory testing found Salmonella in Behne that matched the strain associated with the ongoing outbreak.


The lawsuit alleges that the food she consumed at Chipotle contained jalapeño peppers connected to the Salmonella outbreak. It seeks more than $75,000 in compensatory damages.


The allegations have not been proven in court, and Chipotle has not been found legally responsible for Behne's illness.


## Salmonella outbreak linked to jalapeño peppers


The lawsuit comes amid a broader Salmonella investigation involving jalapeño peppers distributed to restaurants across the United States.


Federal health officials have reported 345 illnesses across 27 states as of August 5, with 36 people hospitalized. Minnesota and Colorado have reported some of the highest numbers of cases.


No deaths connected to the outbreak had been reported as of the latest federal data cited in the report.


The Food and Drug Administration has said the illnesses were associated with jalapeño peppers sourced from Mexico. Investigators also found that many people who became sick reported eating at Mexican restaurants before developing symptoms.


According to federal investigators, approximately 93 percent of nearly 200 interviewed patients said they had eaten at a Mexican restaurant before becoming ill.


Restaurants mentioned in connection with the investigation include Chipotle and Qdoba.


## Severe symptoms reported in Chipotle lawsuit


The lawsuit describes a rapid progression of illness after Behne's reported Chipotle meal.


According to her attorneys, she began experiencing fever, chills, and vomiting on June 27. She reportedly lost consciousness and developed a temperature of approximately 102 degrees Fahrenheit.


The following day, the lawsuit says she experienced more than 10 episodes of diarrhea within 24 hours.


Behne was subsequently admitted to a hospital on June 28. She was discharged two days later, according to the complaint.


Her lawyers say that she continues to experience effects associated with the Salmonella infection and the septic episode.


The lawsuit also claims that she could face an increased risk of complications, including reactive arthritis and post-infectious irritable bowel syndrome.


## What is Salmonella?


Salmonella is a type of bacteria that can cause foodborne illness. Infection commonly results from consuming contaminated food or water, although transmission can also occur through contact with infected people or animals.


Typical Salmonella symptoms include diarrhea, abdominal cramps and fever. Nausea, vomiting, chills and headache can also occur.


Many people recover without serious complications. However, some infections can become severe, particularly when bacteria spread beyond the digestive system.


Sepsis is one of the potentially life-threatening complications that can occur when the body's response to an infection becomes extreme and causes organ dysfunction.


People experiencing severe symptoms, dehydration, persistent high fever, bloody diarrhea, confusion or other signs of serious illness should seek urgent medical attention.


## Chipotle says affected peppers were removed


Chipotle has said that protecting the health and safety of customers and employees is its highest priority.


The company stated that it has a system designed to trace ingredients through its supply chain. After learning about a potential Salmonella outbreak affecting wholesalers and restaurants, Chipotle said it identified the potentially affected jalapeño peppers and removed them from restaurants where they had been distributed.


The company also said it replaced the peppers with products from different growers.


Chipotle reportedly changed its jalapeño supplier for affected restaurants beginning July 20. Qdoba also stopped serving the peppers approximately a week later.


Federal officials have said they do not currently believe there is an ongoing risk associated with the affected jalapeños at Chipotle and Qdoba because the restaurants removed the potentially contaminated products.


## What customers should know about the outbreak


The investigation highlights the difficulty consumers face when dealing with foodborne pathogens.


Salmonella contamination cannot necessarily be detected by looking at or smelling food. Contaminated food may appear normal, making it difficult for customers to identify a potential problem before eating it.


Health officials continue to investigate the source and distribution of the contaminated peppers as they monitor reported illnesses.


Consumers who believe they may have developed Salmonella after eating at a restaurant should contact a healthcare professional, particularly if symptoms are severe or persistent.


People who become seriously ill may also want to keep records of where and when they ate, their symptoms, medical treatment and any relevant receipts or other documentation. Such records can be important during public health investigations or potential legal proceedings.


## Lawsuit alleges Chipotle was responsible


Behne's legal complaint argues that she reasonably relied on Chipotle to provide food that was safe to eat.


Her attorneys contend that she could not have detected Salmonella in the food herself because the bacteria cannot be identified simply by looking at, smelling or tasting a contaminated ingredient.


The lawsuit therefore seeks compensation for the consequences the plaintiff says resulted from her infection, hospitalization and septic episode.


However, filing a lawsuit does not establish that the allegations are true. Chipotle may contest the claims, and the legal process will determine whether the company can be held responsible for the illness described in the complaint.


## The broader food safety concern


The Chipotle case is part of a wider series of foodborne illness investigations affecting restaurants in the United States.


The current Salmonella investigation involving jalapeño peppers has raised concerns about how contaminated ingredients can move through complex food supply chains before the source of an outbreak is identified.


Restaurants and food suppliers rely on traceability systems to identify potentially affected products and remove them from circulation. When contamination is discovered, rapid action can help reduce the risk of additional infections.


For consumers, the outbreak is also a reminder that foodborne illness can sometimes develop into a serious medical emergency.


As federal agencies continue investigating the Salmonella outbreak, additional information could emerge about the source of the contaminated peppers, the distribution chain, and the restaurants that received affected products.
