# What Does Air Conditioning Really Do to Your Body? Doctors Explain the Hidden Health Effects

As summer temperatures climb and heatwaves become increasingly difficult to escape, air conditioning can feel like an essential part of staying comfortable. Whether you are working in an air-conditioned office, sleeping in a cool bedroom, travelling in a car or staying in a hotel, a blast of cold air can provide welcome relief from the heat.


However, spending long periods in air-conditioned environments may also have some less obvious effects on your body. According to doctors quoted in recent health guidance, reduced humidity, cold airflow and poorly maintained air-conditioning systems can contribute to problems affecting the skin, eyes, nose, throat and respiratory system.


The good news is that many of these effects can be reduced with a few simple changes.


## 1. Air conditioning can make your skin dry


One of the most noticeable effects of prolonged exposure to air conditioning is dry skin.


Air-conditioning systems commonly reduce humidity indoors. While lower humidity can make hot temperatures feel more comfortable, it can also increase moisture loss from the skin. People who already have sensitive or dry skin may notice the effects more quickly.


Common symptoms can include tightness, flaking, irritation and chapped lips. For some people, existing skin conditions such as eczema may also become more uncomfortable.


A simple way to protect your skin is to apply moisturiser regularly, particularly before spending several hours in an air-conditioned environment. Products containing humectants such as glycerin or hyaluronic acid can help attract and retain moisture.


## 2. Your eyes may become dry and irritated


Dry indoor air can affect the eyes as well as the skin.


People spending long periods directly beneath an air-conditioning vent may notice burning, stinging or a gritty sensation. Contact-lens wearers can be particularly susceptible to discomfort because lenses can make existing dryness more noticeable.


Blinking regularly can help keep the surface of the eyes lubricated. If dryness continues, suitable lubricating eye drops may provide relief. Contact-lens users may also find it more comfortable to reduce the amount of time they wear their lenses while spending long periods in very dry environments.


If you experience persistent eye pain, significant redness or changes in vision, it is important to seek professional medical advice rather than assuming air conditioning is the cause.


## 3. A dry nose and throat can become uncomfortable


Your nose and throat rely on moisture to function comfortably. When indoor air becomes particularly dry, these areas can become irritated.


This may result in a dry or sore throat, blocked nose or hoarse voice. In some cases, dryness inside the nose can cause the delicate tissue to crack, making minor nosebleeds more likely.


Dr Lucy Hooper, a London-based GP quoted in the source article, also highlighted the role of mucus in the body's natural defence system. When nasal passages become dry, clearing mucus can become more difficult.


Staying hydrated throughout the day can help. It can also be useful to avoid having cold air blowing continuously across your face.


## 4. Poorly maintained air conditioning can affect breathing


The condition of an air-conditioning system matters.


A properly maintained system can help make indoor environments more comfortable, but poorly maintained units may circulate particles such as dust, pollen and mould. These irritants can be particularly troublesome for people who have allergies or respiratory conditions.


For anyone with asthma or another existing lung condition, exposure to airborne irritants may potentially worsen symptoms.


Home air-conditioning units should therefore be cleaned and serviced according to the manufacturer's instructions. Filters should also be replaced or cleaned when required.


In offices, hotels and other shared buildings, maintenance is generally outside an individual's control. If you notice that an air-conditioned environment consistently causes coughing, wheezing or breathing difficulties, consider moving to a different area and speak to a healthcare professional if symptoms persist.


## 5. Cold air and dehydration may contribute to aches and headaches


Have you ever left a heavily air-conditioned room with a headache or felt stiff after spending hours in a cold environment?


There can be several possible explanations. Dehydration is one important factor, particularly during hot weather when your body is already losing fluid. Cold airflow may also contribute to muscle tension, especially when it is directed continuously at one part of the body.


Rather than assuming the air conditioning itself is solely responsible, consider your overall environment. Are you drinking enough water? Have you been sitting in the same position for hours? Is cold air blowing directly onto your neck or shoulders?


Small adjustments can make a difference.


## How to reduce the health effects of air conditioning


You do not necessarily need to stop using air conditioning during hot weather. Instead, focus on making your environment more comfortable.


**Stay hydrated.** Keep water nearby and drink regularly throughout the day, especially during heatwaves.


**Moisturise your skin.** Apply a moisturiser before and after spending extended periods in dry indoor air.


**Protect your lips.** A nourishing lip balm can help reduce dryness and cracking.


**Look after your eyes.** If your eyes become dry, take regular breaks from screens, blink frequently and consider appropriate lubricating eye drops.


**Redirect the airflow.** In a car, avoid positioning vents directly at your face or eyes. At home, adjust vents so cold air is not constantly hitting your body.


**Keep filters clean.** Regular servicing and filter maintenance can help reduce the circulation of dust and other airborne particles.


**Consider indoor humidity.** If your home becomes particularly dry, a humidifier may help restore some moisture to the air. It should be cleaned regularly to prevent microbial growth.


**Use air conditioning strategically.** You may not need to run the system continuously. On some days, opening windows during cooler periods or using air conditioning only when temperatures rise may be enough to keep an indoor space comfortable.


## Should you stop using air conditioning?


For most people, air conditioning remains a useful way of managing extreme heat. Keeping cool is particularly important during heatwaves, when excessive heat can pose genuine health risks.


The key is balance. Very cold temperatures, dry air, direct airflow and poorly maintained equipment can make some people uncomfortable, but these issues can often be managed through hydration, skincare, ventilation, sensible temperature settings and regular maintenance.


If you develop persistent breathing problems, severe headaches, significant eye symptoms or another concerning health issue, do not assume that air conditioning is the only explanation. Seek medical advice when appropriate.


### Final thoughts


Air conditioning can be a valuable tool during hot weather, but spending hours in cooled indoor environments can have some unexpected effects on the body. Dry skin, irritated eyes, a sore throat, nasal discomfort and respiratory irritation are among the problems that may occur, particularly when humidity is low or equipment is poorly maintained.


By staying hydrated, protecting your skin and eyes, maintaining your air-conditioning system and avoiding unnecessary direct airflow, you can enjoy the benefits of staying cool while reducing some of the potential downsides.
