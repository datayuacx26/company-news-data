# Florida Leprosy Cases Increase: Symptoms, Causes, Prevention, and What You Should Know

Leprosy, also known as Hansen's disease, is once again making headlines after health officials reported an increase in cases across Florida. Although the disease is considered rare in the United States, recent data has raised public interest and prompted experts to remind people about the symptoms, transmission, and prevention of this ancient bacterial infection.


Despite its long history and the stigma often associated with it, leprosy is now a treatable disease when diagnosed early. Understanding how it spreads and who is most at risk can help reduce unnecessary fear while encouraging timely medical care.


## What Is Leprosy?


Leprosy, medically known as Hansen's disease, is a chronic bacterial infection caused by *Mycobacterium leprae* . The disease mainly affects the skin, peripheral nerves, eyes, and the lining of the nose and respiratory tract. If left untreated, it can lead to permanent nerve damage, muscle weakness, skin lesions, vision loss, and disability.


Contrary to popular belief, leprosy is not highly contagious. Most people have a natural immune defense against the bacteria, making infection relatively uncommon even after exposure.


## Florida Reports Higher Number of Cases


Health authorities in Florida have observed a rise in reported leprosy cases over recent years. According to state health data, dozens of cases were confirmed last year, marking the highest annual total recorded since official tracking began in the early 1990s.


Across the United States, health agencies continue to monitor Hansen's disease, although the overall number of infections remains low compared to many other infectious diseases.


Researchers are still investigating the reasons behind the increase in reported cases. While no single explanation has been confirmed, experts believe multiple environmental and human factors may contribute to the trend.


## How Does Leprosy Spread?


Leprosy spreads much more slowly than many infectious diseases. The bacteria usually require prolonged and close contact with an untreated infected person before transmission occurs. Casual interactions such as shaking hands, sharing meals, or sitting near someone generally do not spread the disease.


Another possible source of infection in parts of the southern United States is the nine banded armadillo. Some armadillos naturally carry the bacteria responsible for Hansen's disease.


People may be exposed when handling infected armadillos or disturbing soil around their burrows. However, health experts emphasize that the overall risk of contracting leprosy from armadillos remains very low.


## Symptoms of Hansen's Disease


Symptoms often develop very slowly. In many cases, signs may not appear for several years after exposure. Some people may not develop symptoms until 20 years later.


Common symptoms include:


- Light or reddish skin patches
- Numbness in affected areas
- Thickened nerves
- Muscle weakness
- Skin lumps or nodules
- Reduced sensation to heat or pain
- Eye problems that may affect vision


If untreated, nerve damage can become permanent, making early diagnosis extremely important.


## Who Is Most at Risk?


Approximately 95 percent of people naturally resist infection because of their immune system. This means that only a small percentage of individuals are susceptible to developing the disease after exposure.


People who may face a higher risk include:


- Individuals with weakened immune systems
- People living in or traveling to regions where leprosy is more common
- Those with prolonged close contact with untreated patients
- Individuals who frequently handle wild armadillos or work in areas where they live


Even among these groups, the overall likelihood of infection remains relatively low.


## Can Leprosy Be Prevented?


Although there is no routine vaccine specifically for Hansen's disease in most countries, several practical precautions can help reduce the already low risk of infection.


Health experts recommend:


- Avoid handling wild armadillos.
- Do not disturb soil around armadillo burrows without proper protection.
- Seek medical advice if you have prolonged contact with someone diagnosed with untreated leprosy.
- Monitor unusual skin changes or persistent numbness and consult a healthcare provider promptly.


Early evaluation allows doctors to diagnose the condition before complications develop.


## Is Leprosy Curable?


Yes. Hansen's disease is completely curable with modern multidrug antibiotic therapy.


Treatment usually involves a combination of antibiotics taken over several months to two years, depending on the severity of the infection. Once treatment begins, the risk of spreading the disease drops significantly.


Prompt diagnosis also prevents permanent nerve damage and other long term complications.


## Addressing the Stigma


Historically, leprosy has carried significant social stigma, often leading to discrimination and isolation of patients. Modern medical knowledge has changed our understanding of the disease.


Healthcare professionals stress that Hansen's disease is treatable, only mildly contagious under specific conditions, and should not be associated with fear or social exclusion. Public awareness and accurate information are essential to reducing misconceptions that have existed for centuries.


## Why Public Awareness Matters


Although Hansen's disease remains rare in the United States, increased reporting highlights the importance of public health surveillance and education. Recognizing symptoms early and understanding how the disease actually spreads can help individuals seek treatment sooner while reducing unnecessary concern.


Researchers continue studying environmental factors, animal reservoirs, and transmission patterns to better understand why some areas, including Florida, have experienced an increase in reported cases.


For most people, the risk remains extremely low. Maintaining good hygiene, avoiding unnecessary contact with wildlife that may carry the bacteria, and consulting a healthcare professional for unusual symptoms are sensible precautions.


## Final Thoughts


The recent rise in Florida's reported leprosy cases has renewed attention on one of history's oldest infectious diseases. While the increase is being closely monitored by health authorities, experts emphasize that Hansen's disease remains uncommon and is highly treatable with modern antibiotics.


Understanding the facts is the best way to separate myth from reality. Most people are naturally protected against infection, transmission requires prolonged close exposure in most cases, and early treatment leads to excellent outcomes. Staying informed and following basic preventive measures can help reduce risk while supporting accurate public awareness.
