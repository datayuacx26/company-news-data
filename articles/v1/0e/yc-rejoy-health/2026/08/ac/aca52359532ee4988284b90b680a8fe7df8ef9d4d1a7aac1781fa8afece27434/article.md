# Omalizumab vs Oral Immunotherapy for Multiple Food Allergies: Which Treatment Shows Better Results?

Food allergies affect millions of children and adults worldwide, making everyday activities like grocery shopping, dining out, and attending social events stressful. For people with allergies to multiple foods, the challenges become even greater because avoiding several allergens at once significantly increases the risk of accidental exposure.


A new randomized clinical trial published in *JAMA Pediatrics* compared two leading treatment options for multifood allergies: **omalizumab** and **multiallergen oral immunotherapy (MOIT)** . The findings provide valuable insights into how these treatments differ in effectiveness and safety, helping patients and healthcare providers make more informed decisions.


## Understanding Multifood Allergies


A multifood allergy occurs when an individual is allergic to more than one food. Common allergens include peanuts, milk, eggs, wheat, tree nuts, and other foods that are frequently found in everyday meals.


Living with multiple food allergies often requires constant vigilance. Even small amounts of an allergen can trigger reactions ranging from mild symptoms to severe anaphylaxis, which can become life threatening without immediate treatment.


Traditional management has focused on strict avoidance and carrying emergency medications such as epinephrine. However, newer therapies aim to reduce the likelihood of severe reactions following accidental exposure.


## What Is Omalizumab?


Omalizumab is a monoclonal antibody that targets immunoglobulin E (IgE), the antibody responsible for triggering allergic reactions. By lowering the body's allergic response, omalizumab can reduce the severity of reactions caused by accidental exposure to food allergens.


In 2024, omalizumab received approval in the United States to help reduce allergic reactions, including anaphylaxis, resulting from accidental exposure to one or more food allergens.


Unlike oral immunotherapy, omalizumab does not require patients to consume allergenic foods daily.


## What Is Multiallergen Oral Immunotherapy?


Multiallergen oral immunotherapy, commonly known as MOIT, gradually exposes patients to very small amounts of multiple allergenic foods. Over time, these doses are slowly increased to help build tolerance.


The goal is not to cure food allergies but to increase the amount of allergen a person can safely tolerate before experiencing a reaction. However, treatment requires frequent dose adjustments, careful monitoring, and consistent daily dosing.


Because allergenic foods are intentionally consumed during treatment, side effects and allergic reactions are relatively common.


## How the Study Was Conducted


Researchers enrolled 117 participants between the ages of 1 and 55 years who were allergic to peanuts and at least two additional foods, including milk, eggs, wheat, walnuts, hazelnuts, or cashews.


Participants were randomly assigned to receive either:


- Omalizumab with placebo oral immunotherapy
- Omalizumab-facilitated multiallergen oral immunotherapy


The trial was conducted across 10 academic medical centers in the United States.


Researchers evaluated whether participants could tolerate significantly larger amounts of their allergenic foods by the end of treatment while also monitoring adverse events and treatment discontinuation rates.


## Key Findings


The study found that omalizumab outperformed multiallergen oral immunotherapy in the primary intention-to-treat analysis.


Approximately 36 percent of participants receiving omalizumab successfully reached the primary treatment goal compared with 19 percent of those receiving MOIT.


Researchers also observed that participants receiving omalizumab generally tolerated higher doses of multiple food allergens by the end of treatment.


However, the difference in treatment success was strongly influenced by the high number of participants who discontinued oral immunotherapy because of treatment-related side effects.


Among participants who completed the study, both treatments produced similar effectiveness.


## Safety Differences Were Significant


One of the most important findings involved treatment safety.


Participants receiving multiallergen oral immunotherapy experienced substantially higher rates of allergic reactions during treatment.


Compared with omalizumab, MOIT was associated with:


- More serious adverse events
- More allergic reactions requiring epinephrine
- Higher treatment discontinuation rates
- Increased cases of anaphylaxis
- Several cases of eosinophilic esophagitis


By contrast, no serious adverse events related to omalizumab were reported during the study.


These safety differences were one of the main reasons why more participants completed treatment with omalizumab.


## Why Did More Patients Stop Oral Immunotherapy?


Researchers suggested several possible reasons for the higher dropout rate among patients receiving MOIT.


Participants in this study had severe allergies to multiple foods, making treatment more challenging than typical oral immunotherapy studies.


Additional contributing factors included:


- Higher maintenance dose targets
- Simultaneous treatment with multiple allergens
- Treatment-related allergic reactions
- Difficulty consuming large amounts of allergenic food products over an extended period


These factors likely increased both the physical and emotional burden of treatment.


## What Do These Results Mean?


The findings suggest that omalizumab may be a better option for many patients who want protection against accidental food exposure while avoiding the frequent allergic reactions associated with oral immunotherapy.


That does not necessarily mean oral immunotherapy is ineffective.


Patients who successfully completed MOIT achieved similar improvements in food tolerance compared with those treated with omalizumab. The challenge was that many participants were unable to complete treatment because of side effects.


This highlights the importance of selecting therapy based on an individual's medical history, allergy severity, treatment goals, and ability to tolerate potential risks.


## Are More Studies Needed?


Yes.


Researchers noted several limitations, including a smaller study population than originally planned and the highly selective group of participants with severe multifood allergies.


Future research will likely compare long-term outcomes, treatment costs, quality of life, and whether combining biologic medications like omalizumab with oral immunotherapy can further improve safety and effectiveness.


Additional studies may also identify which patients benefit most from each treatment approach.


## The Bottom Line


This clinical trial adds important evidence supporting omalizumab as an effective treatment option for people living with multiple food allergies.


While both therapies increased food tolerance in participants who completed treatment, omalizumab demonstrated a better overall safety profile and higher treatment completion rate.


For families managing multiple food allergies, these findings may help guide discussions with allergy specialists about the most appropriate treatment strategy. As research continues, personalized approaches that balance effectiveness with safety are expected to become increasingly important.


## Frequently Asked Questions


### Is omalizumab a cure for food allergies?


No. Omalizumab does not cure food allergies. It is designed to reduce the risk and severity of allergic reactions caused by accidental exposure.


### Does oral immunotherapy permanently eliminate food allergies?


Not necessarily. Oral immunotherapy aims to increase tolerance while treatment continues. Ongoing maintenance is often required.


### Which treatment is safer?


In this study, omalizumab showed fewer serious adverse events and lower treatment discontinuation rates than multiallergen oral immunotherapy.
