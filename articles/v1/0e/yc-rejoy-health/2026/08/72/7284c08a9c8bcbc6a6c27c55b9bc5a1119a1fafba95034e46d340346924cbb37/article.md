# Frozen Berry Recall Update: CDC Warns Consumers After E. Coli Cases Linked to Publix Products

CDC Issues Warning After Frozen Organic Berries Linked to E. Coli Outbreak Across Eight States


The CDC is investigating an E. coli outbreak connected to recalled GreenWise frozen organic blueberries and mixed berries sold at Publix stores. Learn about affected products, symptoms, and safety guidance.


Federal health officials are investigating a multistate E. coli outbreak connected to frozen organic berries sold at Publix stores. The Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) has reported illnesses linked to GreenWise frozen organic blueberries and mixed berries, prompting a nationwide recall of affected products.


According to health authorities, 12 people have been reported sick, with four requiring hospitalization. Officials are advising consumers who may have purchased the recalled berries to avoid eating them and dispose of the products immediately.


The recalled items were produced by Frutas y Hortalizas del Sur S.A., a berry company based in San Carlos, Chile. The products were distributed to Publix stores in eight states, including Alabama, Florida, Georgia, Kentucky, North Carolina, South Carolina, Tennessee, and Virginia.


## CDC Investigates Frozen Berry Related E. Coli Outbreak


The CDC and other federal agencies began investigating reports of E. coli infections potentially connected to GreenWise frozen organic berries. The agency confirmed that all lots of GreenWise frozen organic blueberries and mixed berries are included in the recall because of possible contamination.


Publix removed the affected products from store shelves after receiving the recall notice issued through the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA). Customers can no longer purchase the recalled frozen berry products from Publix locations.


Health officials reported that most cases were identified in Florida, with one case reported in Georgia. Investigators continue to monitor the situation and determine whether additional illnesses may be connected to the recalled products.


## What Products Are Included in the Recall?


Consumers should check their freezers for the following recalled products:


• GreenWise Organic Whole Blueberries, 10-ounce package, UPC 41415-06453
• GreenWise Organic Whole Blueberries, 48-ounce package, UPC 41415-12053
• GreenWise Organic Whole Mixed Berries, 10-ounce package, UPC 41415-06753
• GreenWise Organic Whole Mixed Berries, 48-ounce package, UPC 41415-12153


The affected products have best-by dates of February 9, 2028. They were distributed only through Publix stores located in the eight states identified by officials.


The CDC recommends that anyone who has these products at home should throw them away and avoid consuming them. Consumers should also clean any surfaces or containers that may have come into contact with the recalled berries.


## Understanding E. Coli and Its Symptoms


Escherichia coli, commonly known as E. coli, is a type of bacteria that can cause illness in humans. While many strains of E. coli are harmless, certain types can lead to serious infections and health complications.


The CDC states that common symptoms of E. coli infection may include:


• Severe stomach cramps
• Diarrhea, which may become bloody
• Vomiting
• Mild fever
• Loss of appetite


Symptoms can appear after consuming contaminated food and may vary from person to person.


Some individuals may experience more serious warning signs. These can include a fever of 102 degrees Fahrenheit or higher, diarrhea or vomiting lasting more than two days, bloody diarrhea, blood in urine, dehydration, dizziness when standing, dry mouth, or reduced urination.


People who experience severe symptoms should seek medical advice as soon as possible.


## Who May Face Greater Health Risks?


Although anyone can become infected with harmful E. coli strains, certain groups may have a higher risk of developing serious complications. Young children, older adults, pregnant individuals, and people with weakened immune systems are often more vulnerable to severe illness.


Some E. coli strains produce Shiga toxin, which can damage the body and may lead to a dangerous condition called hemolytic uremic syndrome (HUS). This condition can affect kidney function and may require medical treatment.


Health experts encourage consumers to pay attention to symptoms after eating recalled products and contact a healthcare provider if concerning signs develop.


## How Consumers Can Stay Safe


Food safety experts recommend several steps to reduce the risk of foodborne illness:


1. Check your freezer for recalled products and discard them immediately.
2. Do not taste recalled food to determine whether it is safe.
3. Wash your hands after handling recalled items.
4. Clean surfaces, utensils, and storage areas that may have contacted the berries.
5. Monitor yourself and family members for possible symptoms.


Following recall instructions helps prevent additional illnesses and reduces the chance of spreading harmful bacteria.


## Investigation Continues as Officials Monitor Cases


The CDC, FDA, and other public health agencies are continuing their investigation into the source of contamination. Officials may provide additional updates if more information becomes available.


Food recalls are issued as a precaution when products may pose a health risk. Consumers are encouraged to follow official guidance and rely on updates from public health agencies for the latest information.


## Final Thoughts


The recall of GreenWise frozen organic blueberries and mixed berries highlights the importance of food safety awareness. Even products that appear healthy and natural can sometimes become contaminated during production or distribution.


Consumers who purchased the affected berries should remove them from their homes and watch for symptoms associated with E. coli infection. Anyone experiencing severe symptoms should contact a healthcare professional.


Staying informed about food recalls and following safety recommendations can help protect individuals and families from preventable illnesses.
