# FDA Issues Highest Risk Warning for Recalled Cheese Products Over Listeria Concerns

Consumers in several U.S. states are being urged to avoid certain cheese products after federal health officials issued the highest risk classification for a recall involving Clover Hill Dairy cheeses. The warning follows concerns that the products may be contaminated with Listeria monocytogenes, a bacteria that can cause serious illness, hospitalization, and in some cases, death.


The U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) classified the recall as a Class I event, its most serious recall category. This designation means there is a reasonable possibility that consuming the affected products could result in severe health consequences or fatalities.


The recall involves cheeses produced by Clover Hill Dairy, a dairy company based in Mechanicsville, Maryland. The company voluntarily removed its products from the market in June after health officials began investigating a possible connection between the cheeses and a Listeria outbreak.


## Listeria Outbreak Linked to Cheese Products


According to information released by federal health officials, the outbreak investigation identified multiple illnesses connected to Listeria monocytogenes. Reports indicated that 12 people were infected with the outbreak strain, with 10 individuals requiring hospitalization and one reported death.


Health investigators collected information from affected individuals to determine possible sources of exposure. Among those interviewed, most reported eating cheese before becoming ill. Some individuals specifically reported consuming requeson cheese produced by Clover Hill Dairy.


Listeria infections can be especially dangerous for older adults, pregnant women, newborns, and people with weakened immune systems. Symptoms may include fever, muscle aches, fatigue, nausea, vomiting, and diarrhea. In severe cases, the infection can spread beyond the digestive system and lead to life-threatening complications.


## Products Included in the Cheese Recall


The recall covers a wide range of Clover Hill Dairy cheese products. These products were available in different sizes, from small consumer packages to large bulk containers used by businesses.


Affected products include:


• Ricotta cheese
• Soft cuajada in brine
• Vacuum-sealed soft cuajada
• Cheddar cheese varieties
• White Colby cheese
• Monterey Jack cheese
• Marble Jack cheese
• Fresh cheddar curd
• Pepper jack cheese varieties
• Flavored cheeses, including horseradish cheddar and Old Bay cheddar


Some recalled products may have been sold under different brand names. Consumers should also check products labeled as Kesso, Quesos La Ricura, Izalco, De Mi Pueblo, and Rio Lindo, as some items may have been repackaged or distributed under those names.


The recalled cheeses were distributed through several sales channels, including Clover Hill Dairy’s retail market, farmers’ markets, and third-party distributors. The products reached customers in Maryland, Virginia, North Carolina, New York, New Jersey, and Washington, D.C.


Health officials have reported Listeria cases in Illinois, Maryland, New York, and Virginia.


## What Consumers Should Do


Clover Hill Dairy has advised customers not to eat, sell, or serve any recalled cheese products. Consumers who have purchased these items should check their refrigerators, freezers, and storage areas carefully.


If recalled cheese is found, it should be discarded or returned according to retailer instructions. People should avoid tasting the product to determine whether it is safe because Listeria contamination cannot be identified by appearance, smell, or taste.


Surfaces that came into contact with recalled cheese should also be cleaned and sanitized. This includes refrigerator shelves, containers, cutting boards, and kitchen tools.


Anyone who has eaten recalled cheese and develops symptoms associated with Listeria infection should contact a healthcare professional, especially if they belong to a high-risk group.


## Understanding the FDA Class I Recall Classification


The FDA uses different recall categories based on the level of potential health risk.


A Class I recall is the highest level and applies when exposure to a product may cause serious health problems or death. This classification is used for situations involving the most significant safety concerns.


A Class II recall applies when a product may cause temporary or medically reversible health effects, while a Class III recall involves products that violate regulations but are unlikely to create health risks.


The Class I classification for Clover Hill Dairy cheeses highlights the seriousness of the investigation and the importance of consumers following safety guidance.


## Company Responds to Recall


Clover Hill Dairy announced that it was cooperating with health officials and expressed regret over the recall situation.


The company stated that it apologized for the inconvenience and difficulties caused by the recall and said it hoped to resolve the issue safely before returning products to customers.


Food safety recalls can create challenges for both businesses and consumers, but removing potentially contaminated products from circulation is an important step in preventing additional illnesses.


## Why Listeria Prevention Matters


Listeria monocytogenes is a bacteria that can survive in certain food production environments, including areas where refrigerated foods are processed and stored. Unlike some bacteria, Listeria can continue growing at refrigerator temperatures, which makes prevention and proper food handling especially important.


Consumers can reduce risks by practicing safe food habits, including keeping refrigerators clean, separating raw and ready-to-eat foods, washing hands regularly, and following recall announcements from trusted health agencies.


Dairy products, particularly soft cheeses, have historically been monitored closely because contamination risks can lead to serious outbreaks if bacteria enter the production process.


## Final Reminder for Consumers


The Clover Hill Dairy cheese recall remains an important food safety issue because of the reported illnesses and the FDA’s highest risk classification. Consumers should review the recall information carefully and avoid eating any products that may be affected.


Checking food labels, staying informed about recalls, and following official safety recommendations can help protect individuals and families from preventable foodborne illnesses.
