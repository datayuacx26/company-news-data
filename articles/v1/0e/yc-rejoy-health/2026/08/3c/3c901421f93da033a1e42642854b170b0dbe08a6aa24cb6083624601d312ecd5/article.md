# Salmonella Outbreak Linked to Mexican Jalapeños Sickens 345 People Across 27 US States

## CDC and FDA Investigate Widespread Foodborne Illness Connected to Imported Jalapeño Peppers


A major Salmonella outbreak linked to imported jalapeño peppers from Mexico has affected hundreds of people across the United States, according to the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC). Health authorities have confirmed that at least 345 people in 27 states have become ill, leading to dozens of hospitalizations as federal agencies continue their investigation.


The outbreak has raised new concerns about food safety, imported produce, and supply chain monitoring in the restaurant industry. Authorities are working with suppliers and restaurants to contain the issue while advising consumers and businesses to avoid recalled products.


## Hundreds Sickened Across Multiple States


The CDC reported that 345 confirmed cases of Salmonella infection have been identified in 27 states. So far, 36 people have been hospitalized, although no deaths have been reported.


According to the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA), the illnesses began between June 19 and July 20. Officials believe the contaminated jalapeño peppers are the common source of the outbreak.


Salmonella is one of the most common causes of foodborne illness in the United States. Symptoms usually include diarrhea, fever, stomach cramps, nausea, and vomiting. While many people recover within a few days without medical treatment, infections can become serious for young children, older adults, pregnant women, and individuals with weakened immune systems.


## Imported Jalapeños From Sinaloa Identified as the Source


Federal investigators traced the outbreak to jalapeño peppers grown in Sinaloa, Mexico. The peppers were distributed in the United States by Coast Citrus Distributors.


The FDA said the distributor has agreed to recall all remaining affected products and has started notifying customers who may have received the contaminated peppers.


Officials also stated that the recalled peppers were mainly supplied to restaurants and food service businesses rather than grocery stores. As a result, consumers are less likely to encounter the recalled products through retail supermarkets.


The FDA continues to advise restaurants, distributors, and businesses not to sell or serve recalled jalapeños originating from Sinaloa while the investigation remains active.


## Restaurants Respond to the Recall


Several restaurant chains that received the imported jalapeños have already taken action to reduce potential risks.


Chipotle Mexican Grill replaced its jalapeño supplier beginning on July 20 after learning that some of its restaurants had received the affected peppers.


Similarly, QDOBA stopped serving the imported jalapeños on July 28 and confirmed it has removed the product from its restaurants.


The FDA emphasized that there is currently no ongoing consumer risk associated with these restaurant locations because the affected ingredients have already been removed from service.


These precautionary measures are intended to prevent additional illnesses while health officials continue tracing the contaminated products through the supply chain.


## Chipotle Faces Renewed Food Safety Attention


The latest outbreak has once again placed Chipotle under public scrutiny, although the company is not believed to be the source of the contamination.


Chipotle has experienced several high-profile foodborne illness outbreaks over the past decade, prompting the company to implement stricter food safety procedures and supplier monitoring systems.


Following news of the current investigation, Chipotle's stock price declined by more than 8 percent after the company disclosed that it had removed jalapeños from restaurants in Minnesota and other affected locations.


While federal regulators noted there is no current threat from Chipotle restaurants involved in this investigation, investors reacted cautiously as memories of previous outbreaks resurfaced.


## Food Safety Concerns Continue to Grow


The Salmonella investigation comes at a time when U.S. health agencies are already managing another produce-related outbreak.


Earlier this year, federal officials linked a multistate cyclosporiasis outbreak to lettuce served at Taco Bell restaurants. Investigators later identified Taylor Farms' operations in central Mexico as the source of the contaminated lettuce.


On the same day the latest Salmonella update was released, the FDA and CDC announced that the cyclosporiasis investigation had expanded to 15 states.


The back-to-back investigations have renewed concerns about produce safety, international supply chains, and the importance of food safety inspections throughout the import process.


## What Consumers Should Know About Salmonella


Salmonella bacteria can contaminate fruits, vegetables, poultry, eggs, and other foods if proper hygiene and handling practices are not followed.


Common symptoms include:


- Diarrhea
- Fever
- Stomach cramps
- Nausea
- Vomiting
- Headache


Symptoms generally appear between six hours and six days after exposure and may last up to a week.


Most healthy individuals recover without treatment, but severe dehydration or bloodstream infections may require hospitalization. Anyone experiencing persistent high fever, bloody diarrhea, or signs of dehydration should seek medical care promptly.


Proper food preparation, hand washing, and safe storage remain important steps in reducing the risk of foodborne illness.


## FDA Investigation Remains Active


Federal investigators continue working with distributors, growers, and restaurant operators to determine whether additional products may be affected.


The FDA stated that Coast Citrus Distributors does not appear to have supplied the recalled jalapeños directly to grocery stores. However, restaurants and commercial food businesses that received the peppers are expected to follow recall guidance and remove affected inventory immediately.


Officials may release additional information as laboratory testing and supply chain tracing continue.


Consumers who believe they may have eaten contaminated food and develop symptoms consistent with Salmonella infection should contact a healthcare provider.


## Final Thoughts


The Salmonella outbreak connected to imported Mexican jalapeño peppers highlights the ongoing challenges of maintaining food safety across complex global supply chains. Although no deaths have been reported, hundreds of illnesses and dozens of hospitalizations demonstrate how quickly contaminated produce can affect consumers across multiple states.


Health officials continue monitoring the situation closely, while affected suppliers and restaurants have taken steps to remove potentially contaminated products from circulation. Consumers should remain informed about food recalls and follow official guidance from public health agencies to reduce the risk of illness.
