# Rare Rabbit Fever Cases in New York Raise Health Concerns: What You Need to Know About Tularemia

## Three Suspected Tularemia Cases Reported in Suffolk County, New York


Health officials in Suffolk County, New York, have reported three probable cases of a rare bacterial infection known as Tularemia, commonly called “rabbit fever.” The development has drawn attention because the illness can become serious if not recognized and treated quickly.


Tularemia is caused by the bacterium Francisella tularensis, an organism that can infect humans through several types of exposure. While the disease is uncommon, health experts warn that even limited contact with the bacteria may lead to infection.


Public health officials are reminding residents to take precautions when spending time outdoors, especially in areas where ticks, wild animals, and rodents are present.


## What Is Tularemia or Rabbit Fever?


Tularemia is a bacterial disease that primarily affects animals but can spread to humans. The infection is sometimes called rabbit fever because rabbits and hares are among the animals that can carry the bacteria.


The disease is also known as deer fly fever because bites from infected deer flies can transmit the bacteria. Other common sources of exposure include tick bites, contaminated water, and direct contact with infected animals.


According to health authorities, Tularemia is not considered a contagious disease between people. A person with Tularemia generally does not spread the infection through normal contact, coughing, or being near others.


However, because the bacteria can cause serious illness, early medical attention is important when symptoms appear after possible exposure.


## How Do People Become Infected With Tularemia?


People can contract Tularemia in several ways. Understanding these risk factors can help reduce the chances of infection.


Common causes of Tularemia exposure include:


- Tick bites from infected ticks
- Deer fly bites
- Handling infected rabbits, rodents, or other wildlife
- Drinking water contaminated with the bacteria
- Breathing in contaminated dust or aerosols


Outdoor activities such as hiking, gardening, hunting, landscaping, or handling wildlife may increase exposure risk in areas where the bacteria are present.


Health officials advise people to use insect repellent, wear protective clothing, and avoid direct contact with sick or dead animals.


## Symptoms of Rabbit Fever to Watch For


Tularemia symptoms can vary depending on how the bacteria enter the body. Some people may develop symptoms within a few days, while others may notice signs later.


Possible symptoms include:


- Fever and chills
- Skin ulcers near the infection site
- Swollen lymph nodes, especially in the armpit or groin area
- Eye irritation or inflammation
- Sore throat
- Mouth ulcers
- Tonsillitis
- Coughing
- Chest pain
- Difficulty breathing


In some cases, Tularemia can affect the lungs. This may happen after breathing in bacteria or when an untreated infection spreads through the bloodstream.


Because some symptoms can resemble other illnesses, doctors often consider a person’s recent activities, such as tick exposure or contact with wild animals, when making a diagnosis.


## Why Tularemia Diagnosis Can Take Time


Diagnosing Tularemia may require specialized laboratory testing. Health officials explain that confirmation can take several weeks because specific tests are needed to safely identify the bacteria and evaluate the body’s immune response.


Despite the delay in laboratory confirmation, doctors may begin antibiotic treatment if they strongly suspect Tularemia based on symptoms and exposure history.


Early treatment can help reduce the risk of complications and improve recovery.


## Prevention Tips for Staying Safe Outdoors


Although Tularemia is rare, prevention remains the best protection. People who spend time outdoors can take several simple steps to lower their risk.


Health experts recommend:


### Use Tick and Insect Protection


Apply insect repellent when spending time outdoors, especially in wooded areas, grassy locations, or places known to have ticks and biting insects.


Wear long sleeves, long pants, and closed shoes when possible. Checking your body for ticks after outdoor activities can also help identify exposure early.


### Avoid Contact With Wild Animals


Do not handle sick or dead animals without proper protection. If you must move an animal, use gloves and wash your hands thoroughly afterward.


Pets can also bring ticks or infected materials into homes, so regular checks and preventive care are important.


### Be Careful During Yard Work


Activities such as mowing lawns or clearing brush may disturb infected animals or contaminated materials. Avoid mowing over dead wildlife and use caution when working in areas where rabbits or rodents may live.


### Drink Safe Water


Avoid drinking untreated water from lakes, streams, or other natural sources unless it has been properly filtered or treated.


## Recent Tularemia Concerns Beyond New York


The New York cases are not the only recent Tularemia concerns in the United States. Public health officials have previously issued warnings in areas where the bacteria were detected in wildlife.


In Colorado, health officials reported Tularemia concerns after the bacteria were identified in a dead rabbit. Residents were encouraged to take precautions while outdoors, particularly in areas with rabbits, rodents, and ticks.


These reports highlight the importance of awareness, especially during warmer months when outdoor activities increase.


## When Should You Contact a Doctor?


Anyone who develops symptoms such as unexplained fever, swollen lymph nodes, skin sores, or breathing problems after a tick bite or contact with wild animals should seek medical advice.


When speaking with a healthcare provider, mention any recent outdoor activities, animal exposure, insect bites, or travel history. This information can help doctors determine whether Tularemia or another infection may be responsible.


## Final Thoughts


Tularemia, also known as rabbit fever, is a rare but potentially serious bacterial infection. The recent suspected cases in Suffolk County, New York, serve as a reminder that outdoor safety measures remain important.


While the illness is uncommon and does not spread easily between people, awareness of symptoms and prevention methods can help reduce health risks.


Simple steps such as using insect repellent, avoiding contact with wild animals, wearing protective gear, and seeking medical care after possible exposure can make a significant difference.
