# Louisiana Reports Five Deaths Linked to Vibrio vulnificus as Officials Warn About Coastal Water Exposure

Louisiana health officials are urging residents to take precautions around saltwater and brackish water after nine people in the state were diagnosed with infections caused by *Vibrio vulnificus* so far in 2026. Five of those patients have died, according to the Louisiana Department of Health.


The cases have prompted renewed warnings about a bacterium that can cause severe infections, particularly when it enters the body through an open wound. Health officials say all nine Louisiana cases reported this year involved wounds that had been exposed to seawater, and every patient required hospitalization.


The warning comes during the warmer months, when *Vibrio* bacteria are more commonly found in coastal waters.


## Five deaths reported in Louisiana


The Louisiana Department of Health announced the latest figures on Aug. 6, reporting nine confirmed cases of *Vibrio vulnificus* among Louisiana residents in 2026. Five of the nine patients have died.


According to the department, all of the cases were associated with wounds that came into contact with seawater. Officials also noted that every affected patient had an underlying health condition.


The state is currently seeing more cases and deaths than its recent average. During the comparable period over the previous decade, Louisiana recorded an average of seven cases and one death.


Health officials are encouraging residents to take extra care when swimming, fishing, boating, or participating in other activities in coastal waters.


The Louisiana Department of Health's official update is available[here](https://ldh.la.gov/news/vibrio-update-aug26?ref=private-content.ghost.io) .


## What is Vibrio vulnificus?


*Vibrio vulnificus* is a type of bacteria that naturally occurs in warm coastal waters. Although infections are relatively uncommon, they can become extremely serious and progress rapidly.


A person can become infected when the bacteria enter an existing cut, scrape, puncture, or other open wound after contact with saltwater or brackish water. Brackish water is a mixture of saltwater and freshwater and can occur in areas such as estuaries.


The bacteria can also cause illness when someone eats raw or undercooked shellfish, especially oysters.


The[Centers for Disease Control and Prevention](https://www.cdc.gov/vibrio/about/index.html?ref=private-content.ghost.io) says severe *Vibrio vulnificus* infections can lead to wound infections or bloodstream infections. In serious cases, treatment can involve intensive medical care and, in some circumstances, amputation.


The CDC estimates that approximately one in five people with a *Vibrio vulnificus* infection dies, with some deaths occurring within a very short period after symptoms begin.


## Why warmer water matters


*Vibrio* bacteria are naturally present in coastal environments, but their numbers tend to increase as water temperatures rise.


The CDC says *Vibrio vulnificus* is found in greater concentrations between May and October, when coastal waters are generally warmer.


That seasonal pattern is particularly relevant to states along the Gulf Coast and Atlantic Coast, where residents and visitors frequently come into contact with coastal water during the summer.


Louisiana's latest figures therefore serve as a reminder that people do not necessarily need to be swimming to face exposure. Activities such as fishing, boating, handling marine animals, or walking along coastal areas can potentially result in a wound coming into contact with contaminated water.


## People with certain health conditions may face greater danger


While anyone can potentially develop a *Vibrio* infection, severe illness is more likely among people with certain underlying health conditions.


Louisiana officials said all nine patients reported in the state this year had underlying medical conditions.


Because the infection can worsen quickly, health authorities emphasize the importance of seeking medical attention promptly when symptoms develop after potential exposure.


People with a wound that has been exposed to saltwater or brackish water should pay close attention to changes around the affected area.


Warning signs can include increasing redness, swelling, warmth, pain, discoloration, or drainage. More serious infections can be accompanied by fever, chills, low blood pressure, or blistering skin lesions.


Anyone experiencing concerning symptoms after coastal-water exposure should tell a medical professional about the exposure. That information can help clinicians consider *Vibrio* infection when evaluating the illness.


## Other forms of Vibrio infection


*Vibrio vulnificus* is not the only species of *Vibrio* capable of causing illness in humans.


According to the CDC, several species are responsible for human infections in the United States, including *Vibrio parahaemolyticus* , *Vibrio vulnificus* , and *Vibrio alginolyticus* .


Some infections occur after consuming raw or undercooked shellfish. Symptoms can include watery diarrhea, abdominal cramps, nausea, vomiting, fever, and chills.


Wound infections represent a different risk. They may develop when an open injury comes into contact with coastal water containing the bacteria.


This distinction is important because someone does not necessarily need to eat contaminated seafood to become infected.


## Louisiana is not alone


Other coastal states have also reported *Vibrio vulnificus* infections this year.


The Florida Department of Health has reported 14 *Vibrio vulnificus* cases and two deaths in 2026. Mississippi has reported two cases, according to information from the state's health department.


Historical reports also demonstrate that the bacteria can cause serious infections outside the Gulf Coast.


In 2023, the CDC issued a health alert after severe and fatal *Vibrio vulnificus* infections were identified in Connecticut, New York, and North Carolina during a period of unusually warm coastal waters.


The Mobile County Health Department in Alabama has also reported cases of vibriosis this year.


These reports underscore that the risk is not limited to Louisiana or one particular coastline.


## How residents can reduce their risk


Health authorities recommend taking practical precautions when spending time around coastal water.


People with open cuts or wounds should avoid exposing those injuries to saltwater or brackish water. If contact occurs, the affected area should be cleaned thoroughly.


People should also exercise caution when handling raw seafood and avoid eating raw or undercooked shellfish, particularly oysters.


Those who develop signs of a wound infection following coastal-water exposure should seek medical attention without delay. Informing the healthcare provider that the wound was exposed to saltwater or brackish water is also important.


For residents who enjoy Louisiana's coastal waters, the message from health officials is not necessarily to avoid the water altogether. Rather, it is to understand the circumstances that can increase the risk of infection and recognize symptoms early.


With five deaths among nine reported Louisiana cases this year, officials are emphasizing that seemingly minor wounds can become a serious concern when exposed to contaminated coastal water.
