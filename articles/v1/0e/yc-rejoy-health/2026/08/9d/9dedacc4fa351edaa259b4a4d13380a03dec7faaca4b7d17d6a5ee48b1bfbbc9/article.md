# Prolon Almond and Cocoa Spread Recalled Nationwide After Undeclared Nut Allergens Discovered

A popular almond and cocoa spread sold online across the United States has been recalled after testing found additional tree nut allergens that were not identified on the product label. The recall affects a specific lot of Prolon Apulian Almond & Cocoa Spread, according to L-Nutra and the U.S. Food and Drug Administration.


The California-based company said the affected product may contain cashew, pistachio, and hazelnut, creating a potentially serious health risk for people who are allergic or highly sensitive to those nuts.


## Which Product Is Affected?


The recalled product is Prolon Apulian Almond & Cocoa Spread, which comes in an 8.47-ounce glass jar. The product carries UPC number 850040360530.


Consumers should check the lot information printed on the packaging. According to the recall notice, the affected lot is **L2755** , with an expiration date of **Jan. 2, 2027** . The expiration information is printed on the jar cap beneath the date code.


At this time, L-Nutra says it has not identified any other lots affected by the recall.


The spread was not broadly distributed through traditional supermarkets. Instead, it was sold through L-Nutra's ProlonLife.com online store. Customers could purchase the spread on its own or receive it as part of certain Prolon product bundles.


Consumers who have the product at home should therefore check their jars carefully rather than assuming that an item purchased directly online is unaffected.


## Why Was the Spread Recalled?


The recall followed laboratory testing that detected tree nut allergens that were not declared on the product's label.


The ingredients identified in testing include cashew, pistachio, and hazelnut. These are all members of the tree nut category and can cause allergic reactions in susceptible individuals.


Food labels are particularly important for people with food allergies because even a relatively small amount of an allergen can trigger a reaction in some individuals. When an allergen is not listed on the packaging, consumers may unknowingly eat a product they would otherwise avoid.


L-Nutra said it has recorded **one confirmed allergic reaction** associated with the issue. The company has not yet determined how the undeclared nuts entered the product.


The fact that only one reaction has been reported does not mean the product is safe for people with relevant allergies. Consumers who have an allergy to cashews, pistachios, or hazelnuts should avoid the recalled product altogether.


## What Can Happen During a Tree Nut Allergy?


A tree nut allergy occurs when the immune system incorrectly identifies proteins found in nuts as harmful. The body's immune response can produce symptoms ranging from relatively mild skin irritation to a medical emergency.


The[Cleveland Clinic's information on tree nut allergies](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/diseases/tree-nut-allergy?utm_source=chatgpt.com) explains that possible symptoms can include hives, swelling, vomiting, and other digestive problems.


More serious reactions can affect breathing and circulation. A person experiencing anaphylaxis may develop throat or airway swelling, wheezing, difficulty breathing, dizziness, or a dangerous drop in blood pressure.


Symptoms can appear quickly after exposure. In some cases, signs develop within minutes, although reactions can also occur later.


Importantly, previous reactions do not necessarily predict what will happen during a future exposure. Someone who has experienced only mild symptoms in the past can potentially experience a more serious reaction later.


That uncertainty is one reason food recalls involving undeclared allergens are treated seriously by health authorities and manufacturers.


## What Should Consumers Do?


Anyone who has purchased the recalled Prolon spread should first check the jar for the affected lot number and expiration date.


If the product is marked **L2755** and has the specified Jan. 2, 2027 expiration date, consumers should not eat it, particularly if they have a tree nut allergy.


L-Nutra advises customers to dispose of the product or return it through the company's recall process. The company has also established a response form for customers who need to provide information about an affected product.


Consumers with questions can contact L-Nutra's customer service department during its listed business hours.


People who do not have a known allergy should still follow the recall instructions. Food recalls are issued because a product does not meet expected safety or labeling requirements, and consumers should not rely on the absence of symptoms in another person as evidence that a recalled food is safe.


## Why Checking Food Labels Matters


For people managing food allergies, reading labels is an important part of avoiding accidental exposure. However, recalls demonstrate why checking official safety notices is also valuable.


Manufacturers can update consumers when a problem is discovered after a product has reached the market. A food may appear familiar and have been purchased safely in the past, yet a particular production lot can still be subject to a recall.


Consumers should pay attention to lot numbers, expiration dates, package sizes, and UPC codes when reviewing recall notices. Those details help distinguish affected products from other versions made by the same company.


The current recall is limited to a specific lot of Prolon Apulian Almond & Cocoa Spread. L-Nutra says it is not currently aware of additional affected lots.


## The Bottom Line


The nationwide recall of Prolon Apulian Almond & Cocoa Spread highlights the importance of accurate allergen labeling. Testing found undeclared cashew, pistachio, and hazelnut in one identified lot, prompting the company to remove the affected product from the market.


Consumers who purchased the spread through ProlonLife.com or received it in a Prolon bundle should inspect their jars for **lot L2755** and the **Jan. 2, 2027** expiration date.


Anyone with a relevant tree nut allergy should avoid the recalled product and follow L-Nutra's instructions for disposal or return. People who develop symptoms of a serious allergic reaction after eating a food containing an allergen should seek emergency medical attention.


For the latest information, consumers should rely on the official FDA recall notice and communications from L-Nutra, since recall details can change as investigations continue.
