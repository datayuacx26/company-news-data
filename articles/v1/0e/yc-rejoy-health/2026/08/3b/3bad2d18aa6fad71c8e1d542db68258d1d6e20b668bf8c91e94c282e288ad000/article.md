# Bettergoods Pistachio Nut Butter Recall: Walmart Customers in 19 States Urged to Check Their Jars

Food safety recalls are an important reminder for shoppers to regularly check the products stored in their kitchens. A recent recall involving Bettergoods Pistachio Nut Butter has drawn attention after routine testing identified possible **Salmonella contamination** in a limited batch of the product. While no illnesses have been reported, consumers are being advised to avoid eating the affected product and return it for a refund.


The recall affects a single lot of Bettergoods Pistachio Nut Butter sold exclusively at Walmart stores across 19 U.S. states. Here is everything you need to know about the recall, the health risks associated with Salmonella, and what customers should do if they purchased the product.


## Bettergoods Pistachio Nut Butter Recalled After Positive Salmonella Tests


The U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) announced that one specific lot of Bettergoods Pistachio Nut Butter has been voluntarily recalled after Walmart was informed that three jars tested positive for **Salmonella** during a routine inspection.


According to the FDA, the contamination was discovered through standard quality testing. The recall only applies to one production lot, meaning other batches of Bettergoods Pistachio Nut Butter are not affected.


At the time of the announcement, there were **no reported illnesses or injuries** linked to the recalled product.


## How to Identify the Recalled Product


Consumers should check their jars for the following information:


- **Product:** Bettergoods Pistachio Nut Butter
- **Size:** 6.7-ounce glass jar
- **UPC:** 194346207961
- **Lot Number:** LB028ACP04
- **Expiration Date:** January 28, 2027


If your jar matches all of these details, you should not consume the product.

## Who Manufactured the Product?


The recalled Bettergoods Pistachio Nut Butter was produced by **Gustibus Alimentari Srl** , a manufacturer based in Assoro, Italy. The product was imported into the United States by **Botticelli Foods** , a New York company responsible for distributing the item.


The FDA emphasized that the recall is limited to the identified lot and does not involve other Bettergoods nut butter products.


## What Is Salmonella?


Salmonella is a type of bacteria that can cause foodborne illness. Infection typically occurs after consuming contaminated food or beverages.


Common symptoms include:


- Diarrhea
- Stomach cramps
- Fever
- Nausea
- Vomiting
- Headache
- Chills


Symptoms usually appear between six hours and six days after exposure and often resolve within several days. However, some infections can become severe and require medical treatment.


## Who Is Most at Risk?


Although many healthy adults recover without complications, certain groups face a higher risk of serious illness, including:


- Young children
- Older adults
- Pregnant women
- Individuals with weakened immune systems


In rare cases, Salmonella bacteria can enter the bloodstream, leading to severe medical complications such as:


- Bloodstream infections
- Arterial infections
- Reactive arthritis
- Endocarditis, an inflammation affecting the heart's inner lining


Anyone experiencing severe symptoms such as persistent fever, dehydration, bloody diarrhea, or prolonged vomiting should seek medical care promptly.


## Why Was the Product Recalled?


According to the FDA, Walmart was notified on July 17 after three jars from the affected lot tested positive for Salmonella during routine quality testing.


Officials have not disclosed exactly how the contamination occurred, and no additional manufacturing issues have been announced.


Food recalls like this are often initiated out of caution to prevent potential illnesses before they occur.


## What Should Consumers Do?


If you have the recalled Bettergoods Pistachio Nut Butter, the FDA recommends taking the following steps:


1. Do not eat the product.
2. Check the lot number and expiration date.
3. Return the jar to the Walmart store where it was purchased.
4. Request a full refund.
5. Clean any utensils or surfaces that may have come into contact with the recalled product.


Even if the product appears normal, contaminated food may not show visible signs of bacterial growth.

## Food Recall Safety Tips


Food recalls occur regularly and are an important part of protecting public health. To reduce your risk of foodborne illness:


- Stay informed about FDA food recalls.
- Check product labels before consuming recalled items.
- Wash hands after handling recalled food.
- Disinfect kitchen surfaces that may have been exposed.
- Store food properly to reduce bacterial growth.
- Seek medical advice if symptoms develop after eating a recalled product.


Remaining aware of recall notices can help prevent illness and ensure potentially contaminated foods are removed from your home quickly.

The recall of Bettergoods Pistachio Nut Butter serves as a reminder that routine food safety testing plays an essential role in protecting consumers. Consumers in the 19 affected states should carefully inspect their jars using the listed lot number and expiration date. Taking quick action can help reduce the risk of foodborne illness and keep households safe.
