# NHS Surgeon Struck Off After Serious Surgical Error Left Patient Fighting for Life

A former NHS consultant surgeon has been permanently removed from the medical register after a professional tribunal found that a serious surgical mistake during an emergency bowel operation placed a patient’s life at risk.


The case, involving Dr Yasser Adly Abdel Rahman at Royal Oldham Hospital in Greater Manchester, raised concerns about surgical decision-making, patient safety, communication with families, and the importance of seeking assistance during complex medical procedures.


According to findings from the Medical Practitioners Tribunal Service (MPTS), the operation resulted in a life-threatening complication after the wrong parts of the digestive system were connected. Another surgeon later carried out emergency corrective surgery, which saved the patient’s life.


## NHS Surgeon Removed From Medical Register Following Tribunal Findings


The Medical Practitioners Tribunal Service concluded that Dr Abdel Rahman committed serious professional misconduct following an emergency bowel surgery performed in August 2020.


At the time of the incident, Dr Abdel Rahman was working as a locum consultant general and colorectal surgeon at Royal Oldham Hospital. He had reportedly been working at the hospital for only a short period before carrying out the operation.


The patient, identified as Patient A during tribunal proceedings, required emergency surgery after doctors discovered a tumour in the small bowel along with a perforated and damaged section of intestine.


The tribunal heard that the four-hour operation resulted in a major surgical error. It found that Dr Abdel Rahman connected the wrong section of the small intestine to the stomach, creating a serious blockage in the digestive system.


## Life-Threatening Complication After Bowel Surgery


Medical experts told the tribunal that the procedure created a situation where bowel contents could not pass normally through the digestive tract.


Instead, material was forced back towards the stomach, creating what was described during the hearing as a closed-loop situation.


The patient reportedly suffered increasing and severe abdominal pain, vomiting, and a lack of bowel movement for several weeks after the surgery.


The tribunal heard that nursing staff raised concerns about the patient’s condition. However, concerns about the lack of improvement were not acted upon quickly enough, according to the findings.


A second surgeon, consultant Anthony Rate, later took over the patient’s care after concerns were raised by medical staff and the patient’s family.


During a further emergency operation, Mr Rate discovered the surgical problems and carried out corrective treatment, including creating a stoma to help restore bowel function.


## Expert Describes Error as Extremely Serious


During the tribunal hearing, an expert witness for the General Medical Council described the surgical mistake as being at the most serious end of medical error.


The expert said that the procedure carried out was highly unusual and that the patient could have died without further intervention.


The tribunal also heard that complex surgery involving previous operations and a large tumour can present significant challenges. However, medical experts stated that a surgeon facing unexpected difficulties should seek help from colleagues where necessary.


The findings highlighted the importance of teamwork and communication in high-risk surgical situations.


## Concerns About Communication With Patient’s Family


The tribunal also examined how the situation was communicated after the operation.


According to the findings, Dr Abdel Rahman told the patient’s family that the surgery had been successful and suggested the patient could soon be discharged once pain levels improved.


However, the patient continued to experience serious symptoms, including severe pain and vomiting.


The tribunal found that Dr Abdel Rahman failed to recognise the warning signs that the surgery had not achieved the intended result.


It also concluded that there was no sufficient evidence of apology, remorse, or recognition of the impact on the patient and family.


## Doctor Claimed There Was a “Witch Hunt”


During proceedings, Dr Abdel Rahman reportedly rejected the criticism against him and claimed that he was the target of unfair treatment.


He argued that he had decades of experience and had previously maintained a strong professional record.


However, the tribunal determined that the seriousness of the surgical mistakes, failure to respond appropriately to concerns, and issues relating to professional conduct meant that his fitness to practise medicine was impaired.


As a result, he was removed from the medical register.


## Additional Professional Conduct Concerns


The tribunal also considered separate concerns involving conditions placed on Dr Abdel Rahman’s medical registration.


In 2021, restrictions were placed on his registration, including requirements to inform the General Medical Council about certain employment arrangements outside the UK.


The tribunal found that he failed to comply with these requirements when working as a locum doctor in Ireland in 2022. It also found that inaccurate information was provided to the regulator about the start date of that role.


These issues contributed to the tribunal’s decision that his professional conduct remained seriously impaired.


## Importance of Patient Safety in Surgery


This case highlights the critical importance of safety procedures in modern healthcare.


Complex operations require careful planning, accurate surgical techniques, communication between medical teams, and immediate action when unexpected complications occur.


Hospitals rely on systems designed to reduce human error, including surgical checklists, teamwork, second opinions, and post-operative monitoring.


While medical professionals work in challenging environments where complications can occur, regulatory systems exist to ensure that serious mistakes are investigated and that patient safety remains the priority.


## Conclusion


The removal of Dr Yasser Adly Abdel Rahman from the medical register followed a tribunal investigation into a serious surgical error during an emergency bowel operation.


The case demonstrates the consequences of failures in surgical practice, communication, and professional responsibility. It also underlines the importance of accountability within healthcare and the need for medical professionals to act quickly when patients show signs of complications.


For patients and families, the case reinforces the importance of transparency, proper monitoring, and confidence that concerns about medical treatment will be taken seriously.
