# Woman Wins Nearly £50,000 After Bosses Mistook Medication Side Effects for Intoxication

A transport manager has been awarded £49,612 by an employment tribunal after she was dismissed by her employer, which wrongly assumed she had returned from lunch under the influence of alcohol or drugs.


Samantha Dallimore, who worked as a Customer Liaison Manager at AT Transport Solutions in Corby, Northamptonshire, was experiencing the effects of medication for bipolar disorder when colleagues became concerned about her behaviour at work.


## Medication Side Effects Mistaken for Alcohol or Drugs


Dallimore had been diagnosed with bipolar disorder in 2017 and took medication to manage the condition. On 14 March 2024, she went to a local café for lunch with a friend. The café did not serve alcohol, and Dallimore said she did not consume alcohol or recreational drugs.


She normally took her medication in the evening, but on this occasion took it during her lunch break. She later said the medication could make her feel drowsy.


After returning to work, she initially appeared well. Around 30 to 45 minutes later, however, she began experiencing impaired speech, confusion and significant drowsiness.


Dallimore told a colleague that she did not feel right and felt drowsy before falling asleep for a period of time.


Several colleagues witnessed her condition, but according to the tribunal findings, nobody asked whether she was unwell or needed medical assistance.


## Employer Accused Her of Being Under the Influence


At the end of the working day, Dallimore was allowed to leave and drive herself home. Her colleagues later reported their concerns to management.


She was suspended the following day. A subsequent letter alleged that she had returned from lunch while under the influence and described the incident as a potential serious health and safety breach.


Dallimore denied drinking alcohol or taking drugs and explained that medication could have caused her symptoms.


During the disciplinary process, her employer considered various possible medical explanations for her condition. However, according to the tribunal, the manager ultimately indicated that she would have been dismissed regardless of whether her symptoms were caused by medication.


Her dismissal was subsequently confirmed on grounds of gross misconduct.


## Tribunal Finds Disability Discrimination


Dallimore appealed the decision, but her appeal was unsuccessful. She then brought claims against the company before an employment tribunal.


Employment Judge Isabel Manley found in Dallimore's favour on claims including disability discrimination, harassment related to her disability and unfair dismissal.


The tribunal concluded that the employer had failed to carry out a reasonable investigation and had not properly considered Dallimore's explanation concerning her bipolar disorder and medication.


The judge also found that the allegation that Dallimore had been under the influence of alcohol or drugs was based on limited evidence.


## £49,612 Tribunal Award


Dallimore was awarded a total of £49,612.


The award included compensation for discrimination, injury to feelings, future losses and other employment-related losses.


The case highlights the importance of employers properly investigating unusual behaviour in the workplace, particularly when an employee raises a possible medical or disability-related explanation.


It also demonstrates why employers should avoid making assumptions about whether a worker is intoxicated when symptoms could potentially have a medical cause.
