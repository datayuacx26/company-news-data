# Senate Investigation Gains Access to Phone Used by Dr. Anthony Fauci During Government Service

## Device May Provide New Information in Ongoing Congressional Review


A Senate investigative panel has obtained a copy of the government-issued phone used by Dr. Anthony Fauci during his decades of federal service, including the years when he was one of the nation’s leading public health officials during the COVID-19 pandemic.


The Senate Permanent Subcommittee on Investigations, led by Sen. Ron Johnson (R-Wis.), received a copy of the iPhone that Fauci used while serving as director of the National Institute of Allergy and Infectious Diseases (NIAID) at the National Institutes of Health (NIH). Fauci held that position from 1984 until 2022.


According to information provided by a spokeswoman for Johnson, the committee obtained the device from the Department of Health and Human Services (HHS), the federal department that oversees NIH and NIAID. The transfer occurred as part of a congressional oversight request.


Johnson said the device could help answer questions that investigators have raised regarding Fauci’s communications while serving in government.


“Hopefully, this device will address many of the questions he refused to answer at last week’s hearing,” Johnson said in a statement shared on social media.


HHS confirmed that it provided a copy of the government device to the committee, stating that the production was part of a lawful congressional oversight request.


## Senate Review Focuses on Fauci’s Pandemic-Era Communications


The phone has drawn attention because investigators have been examining Fauci’s role during the COVID-19 response and seeking additional records related to his government activities.


While thousands of Fauci’s emails have previously been released through Freedom of Information Act requests, access to his text messages and other mobile device records has been more limited.


Government records released through previous legal actions have included some text messages involving Fauci and other officials during the pandemic. However, many details about his private communications on the government-issued device remain unavailable.


Supporters of the investigation argue that reviewing the device could provide additional context about decisions made during the pandemic. Critics of the inquiry have questioned whether continued investigations into Fauci are politically motivated.


The Senate panel has not publicly released information about what data is contained on the phone or whether any messages, files, or other records have been reviewed.


## Fauci Declines to Answer Certain Senate Questions


The development follows Fauci’s appearance before a Senate panel in late July, where he declined to answer some questions by invoking his constitutional right against self-incrimination.


During that hearing, Sen. Rand Paul (R-Ky.) questioned Fauci about issues connected to the pandemic response and stated that he would refer Fauci for prosecution if he believed Fauci had provided false testimony under oath.


Fauci said he was following the advice of his legal representatives when declining to respond to specific questions.


Following the hearing, another Senate panel voted to hold Fauci in contempt. The decision was connected in part to his refusal to answer questions about a period covered by a preemptive pardon issued by former President Joe Biden in 2025.


That pardon covered conduct between Jan. 1, 2014, and Jan. 19, 2025.


Fauci has maintained that his actions during his government career were conducted in accordance with his responsibilities and professional guidance.


## Previous Records and Released Communications


The Senate investigation comes after previous releases of Fauci’s government records, including emails and diary entries.


Sen. Paul previously released portions of Fauci’s diary entries, arguing that some private writings appeared different from statements Fauci made publicly during his time leading NIAID.


Fauci has become one of the most recognized figures associated with the COVID-19 pandemic. As director of NIAID, he frequently appeared in public briefings and advised government officials on pandemic-related policies.


During the pandemic, Fauci communicated with numerous officials, medical professionals, and public figures. Some previously released messages showed individuals seeking his advice on public health decisions.


For example, former Surgeon General Vivek Murthy contacted Fauci seeking confidential advice related to decisions involving sporting events and public gatherings. Other communications included discussions about protecting vulnerable populations, including older Americans who faced higher risks from COVID-19 complications.


## Why the Phone Records Matter to Investigators


Government-issued electronic devices can contain a variety of information, including text messages, contact records, emails, notes, and other forms of communication. Investigators often review such records as part of oversight efforts involving federal officials.


The Senate panel’s access to Fauci’s phone does not automatically mean that wrongdoing occurred or that investigators have discovered misconduct. Instead, the device represents another source of information as lawmakers continue examining federal decisions made during the pandemic.


Congressional oversight investigations often involve reviewing documents, interviews, electronic records, and testimony from government officials. The findings from such investigations may depend on the evidence collected and how lawmakers interpret the information.


At this stage, the contents of the phone have not been publicly disclosed, and no conclusions have been announced based on the device.


## Public Interest in Government Transparency


The release and review of government records have remained a major issue since the COVID-19 pandemic. Many Americans continue to seek more information about how officials made decisions during the public health emergency.


Supporters of transparency efforts say public officials should provide access to relevant records when decisions affect millions of people. Others emphasize the importance of protecting privacy, legal rights, and the ability of officials to communicate while performing government duties.


The review of Fauci’s phone will likely become another chapter in the broader debate over pandemic accountability, government oversight, and public trust.


## Conclusion


The Senate’s acquisition of a copy of Dr. Anthony Fauci’s government-issued phone marks a significant step in an ongoing congressional investigation into his time as a federal health official.


While lawmakers hope the device may provide additional answers about Fauci’s communications and decisions, the actual contents of the phone have not yet been made public. Any conclusions will depend on the information investigators uncover and how it is interpreted.


The investigation continues as lawmakers examine records related to the government’s handling of the COVID-19 pandemic and the actions of officials involved in the response.
