# Lerochol Autoinjector Approved by FDA: What Patients Should Know About the New Monthly Cholesterol Treatment

The U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) has approved a new autoinjector option for Lerochol (lerodalcibep-liga), a once-monthly medicine used to lower LDL cholesterol in adults with hypercholesterolemia, including people with heterozygous familial hypercholesterolemia (HeFH).


The approval gives patients another way to receive Lerochol, which was previously available as a pre-filled syringe. The new single-use autoinjector is designed to make self-administration simpler and faster. According to LIB Therapeutics, the company behind Lerochol, the autoinjector is expected to become available in the United States in January 2027.


The FDA also approved an update to Lerochol's prescribing information concerning the relationship between LDL cholesterol reduction and the risk of major adverse cardiovascular events, commonly referred to as MACE.


## What Is Lerochol?


Lerochol, also known by its generic name lerodalcibep-liga, is a PCSK9 inhibitor. PCSK9 is a protein involved in regulating the number of LDL receptors available to remove LDL cholesterol from the bloodstream. By blocking PCSK9, medicines in this class can help the liver remove more LDL cholesterol from the blood.


Lerochol is intended to be used alongside diet and exercise to reduce LDL cholesterol in adults with hypercholesterolemia. It may also be prescribed for adults with HeFH, an inherited condition that can cause unusually high levels of LDL cholesterol.


Lerochol is administered as a subcutaneous injection once a month. Unlike some cholesterol-lowering treatments that require daily dosing, its monthly schedule may offer an alternative for patients who have difficulty maintaining frequent medication routines.


Clinical studies conducted as part of the LIBerate development program included more than 2,900 participants across different cardiovascular risk groups. The company reports that Lerochol produced sustained LDL cholesterol reductions of at least 60% in patients with cardiovascular disease or those at high or very high cardiovascular risk. In people with HeFH, LDL cholesterol reductions of at least 59% were reported.


## New Lerochol Autoinjector Offers Another Administration Option


The newly approved Lerochol autoinjector is designed for single-use administration. It is pressure activated and intended to deliver the full monthly dose within seconds using a straightforward motion.


This may be particularly useful for people who prefer an autoinjector rather than handling a conventional pre-filled syringe.


The pre-filled syringe will remain available, meaning patients and healthcare professionals will have more than one administration format to consider.


Another feature highlighted by the manufacturer is storage flexibility. Lerochol can be kept at room temperature for up to 90 days under the approved conditions. This may make the medication easier to transport during travel, although patients should always follow the storage instructions supplied with their prescription.


## When Will the Lerochol Autoinjector Be Available?


LIB Therapeutics expects the new Lerochol autoinjector to reach the U.S. market in January 2027.


The company also expects insurance coverage to develop gradually during 2027. When the autoinjector launches, LIB Therapeutics says it plans to offer the device alongside the existing pre-filled syringe through its direct-to-patient cash-pay program.


The announced cash price is expected to be $199 per month for eligible patients using the program. Availability, eligibility requirements, insurance coverage, and patient assistance options can change, so patients should confirm current information with the manufacturer and their healthcare provider.


Lerochol is a prescription medicine and should only be used under the direction of a licensed healthcare professional.


## Why LDL Cholesterol Reduction Matters


LDL cholesterol is often described as "bad cholesterol" because elevated levels can contribute to the buildup of fatty deposits in the arteries. Over time, this process can increase the risk of cardiovascular problems.


Statins remain an important treatment for reducing LDL cholesterol. However, some people do not reach their recommended LDL cholesterol levels despite taking the maximum statin dose they can tolerate. Others may require additional treatment because of their underlying cardiovascular risk or inherited cholesterol disorder.


PCSK9 inhibitors provide another approach to lowering LDL cholesterol. The updated Lerochol indication information reflects evidence that lowering LDL cholesterol with statins, as well as with monoclonal antibody PCSK9 inhibitors used with statin therapy, can reduce the risk of major cardiovascular events in appropriate adults.


For patients requiring long-term cholesterol management, convenience can also be an important consideration. A monthly injection may be easier for some people to incorporate into their routine than treatments requiring more frequent administration.


## Lerochol Safety and Side Effects


Like all prescription medicines, Lerochol can cause side effects.


In clinical trials involving adults with primary hyperlipidemia, including people with HeFH, commonly reported adverse reactions included nasopharyngitis, which refers to inflammation of the nose and throat, injection-site reactions, and peripheral edema.


Injection-site reactions were reported more often with Lerochol than with placebo. In HeFH clinical trials, other reported adverse reactions included diarrhea, nausea, and peripheral edema.


Injection-site reactions were also the adverse reaction most frequently associated with treatment discontinuation in trials involving adults with primary hypercholesterolemia.


Lerochol is a recombinant fusion protein. As with other therapeutic proteins, there is a potential for immunogenicity, meaning the immune system may respond to the medication.


Patients should discuss side effects, allergies, medical history, and other medications with their healthcare provider before beginning treatment.


## What the Clinical Trial Program Found


The FDA approval of Lerochol was supported by the global Phase 3 LIBerate Clinical Trial Program. The program evaluated Lerochol in a broad patient population, including people with cardiovascular disease, individuals at high or very high cardiovascular risk, and patients with familial hypercholesterolemia.


Participants received monthly treatment in the registration trials for periods of up to 52 weeks. More than 2,400 participants continued into a longer open-label extension lasting up to 72 weeks.


The company reports that Lerochol provided sustained LDL cholesterol reductions and was generally well tolerated during the clinical development program. These findings helped support the medicine's role as an additional treatment option for adults who need further LDL cholesterol reduction.


## What the New Approval Means for Patients


The FDA's autoinjector approval is primarily a change in how Lerochol can be administered rather than a change to the fundamental purpose of the medicine. Patients who are prescribed Lerochol will have access to a device designed for quick self-administration, while the pre-filled syringe remains an option.


The monthly schedule, room-temperature storage flexibility, and new autoinjector format could make Lerochol more convenient for some people requiring long-term LDL cholesterol management.


However, the best cholesterol treatment varies from person to person. Factors such as LDL cholesterol levels, cardiovascular risk, family history, other medical conditions, current medications, tolerability, insurance coverage, and treatment goals all need to be considered.


Patients should not start, stop, or change cholesterol medication without speaking with their healthcare professional.
