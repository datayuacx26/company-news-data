# Whole Foods Recalls Jalapeño-Containing Foods Over Potential Salmonella Contamination

Whole Foods Market has recalled a range of prepared foods containing jalapeño peppers amid an ongoing multistate investigation into a potential **Salmonella outbreak** . The recall affects select refrigerated products, including salsa, guacamole, pico de gallo, and other prepared foods sold through Whole Foods stores and online.


The precautionary recall comes after fresh jalapeños supplied by Coast Citrus Distributors were recalled because of potential contamination with Salmonella. Products made with the affected peppers have also been recalled by other companies, including Taylor Fresh Foods, which operates as Taylor Farms.


## Whole Foods Jalapeño Recall Affects Multiple States


According to information released by Whole Foods and the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA), the recalled products were sold in the Produce and Prepared Foods departments and through online orders.


The affected states include:


- Texas
- Oklahoma
- Louisiana
- Arkansas
- Wisconsin
- Michigan
- Illinois
- Indiana
- Iowa
- Missouri
- Kentucky
- Ohio


Consumers should pay particular attention to products with Best Before or Best By dates ranging from **August 7 through August 16, 2026** .


Because the recall applies to specific products and dates, shoppers should review the FDA's product-specific recall information for names, UPC codes, dates, and distribution details before deciding whether an item is affected.


## Why Were Jalapeños Recalled?


The Whole Foods recall is connected to an investigation into Salmonella contamination involving fresh jalapeño peppers.


Coast Citrus Distributors previously announced a voluntary recall of jalapeños because of the potential presence of Salmonella. Taylor Fresh Foods subsequently recalled products made with the affected peppers.


Whole Foods said its own recall was prompted by the use of jalapeños included in the Taylor Farms recall.


The FDA investigation has identified a grower in Sinaloa, Mexico, that supplies Coast Citrus Distributors as a potential source connected to the outbreak investigation. Federal health authorities continue to investigate how the contamination occurred and whether additional products could be affected.


The outbreak investigation is significant because it involves multiple states and a large number of reported illnesses.


## Salmonella Symptoms to Watch For


Salmonella is a type of bacteria that can cause gastrointestinal illness. People who become infected may experience symptoms such as diarrhea, fever, stomach cramps, nausea, and vomiting.


Symptoms can vary from person to person. Some infections may be relatively mild, while others can become serious.


Young children, older adults, people who are frail, and individuals with weakened immune systems can face a higher risk of severe illness from Salmonella infection.


Consumers who believe they may have become ill after eating a recalled food should contact a medical professional for appropriate advice, particularly if symptoms are severe or persistent.


## What Consumers Should Do With Recalled Whole Foods Products


If you have recently purchased prepared foods from Whole Foods containing jalapeños, check the product information against the official recall notice.


If the item is included in the recall, consumers should not eat it. The safest approach is to discard the recalled food rather than attempting to remove the jalapeños and eat the remaining ingredients.


Consumers should also take care when handling recalled food and clean surfaces or containers that may have come into contact with it.


The FDA has published detailed product information to help shoppers identify affected items. Checking the official notice is especially important because not every salsa, guacamole, pico de gallo, or prepared food sold by Whole Foods is necessarily included in the recall.

## Restaurants Also Remove Jalapeños


The concerns surrounding the recalled jalapeños extend beyond grocery stores.


Restaurant chains, including Chipotle Mexican Grill and QDOBA, have removed jalapeño peppers from distribution as the investigation continues.


The broader response illustrates how a potential contamination issue involving a single ingredient can affect grocery retailers, prepared-food manufacturers, restaurants, and consumers across multiple states.


For consumers, the most important step is to follow official recall announcements and avoid eating products identified by federal or company recall notices.


## No Illnesses Reported From Recalled Whole Foods Items


Whole Foods has said that no illnesses connected specifically to the recalled products sold at its stores or online have been reported.


The recall is therefore described as precautionary. However, the precaution is important because Salmonella contamination may not change the appearance, smell, or taste of food.


That means consumers should not rely on the condition of a product to determine whether it is safe. If a product matches the official recall information, it should be discarded even if it looks and smells normal.


## Separate Cyclospora Outbreak Also Under Investigation


The jalapeño-related Salmonella investigation comes as federal health authorities are also monitoring a separate multistate outbreak of cyclosporiasis.


Cyclospora is an intestinal illness caused by the parasite *Cyclospora cayetanensis* . The CDC has linked the separate outbreak to iceberg lettuce sourced from central Mexico.


As of the latest information cited in the supplied report, the Cyclospora outbreak had resulted in thousands of reported cases, hundreds of hospitalizations, and two deaths.


The two investigations involve different pathogens and different suspected food sources, so consumers should not assume that the outbreaks are connected. They do, however, highlight the importance of food-safety monitoring and prompt recalls when a potentially contaminated ingredient enters the food supply.

## What Comes Next in the Salmonella Investigation?


Federal and state health officials are continuing to investigate the source and scope of the jalapeño-related Salmonella outbreak.


As investigations develop, additional recalls may be announced if other products are found to contain the affected peppers. Consumers should therefore monitor updates from the FDA, CDC, retailers, and food manufacturers.


The Whole Foods recall also demonstrates why consumers should keep track of packaged and prepared foods in their refrigerators. Having product packaging available can make it easier to compare UPC codes, product descriptions, and dates with an official recall notice.


For now, shoppers who have recalled Whole Foods products containing jalapeños should avoid eating them and follow the instructions provided by the retailer or FDA.
