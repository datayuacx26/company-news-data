# Man Fined After Climbing Hospital Roof in Grim Reaper Style Costume and Alarming Patients

A bizarre incident at a hospital in North Wales has ended with a 26 year old man being fined after climbing onto the roof wearing what many believed was a Grim Reaper costume. The unusual event caused concern among patients, visitors, and hospital staff, prompting an emergency response from police and firefighters.


Although the outfit was later described by the man as a crow costume, its dark appearance and the circumstances surrounding the incident left many people frightened. Authorities confirmed that the individual had no reasonable excuse for his actions, leading to legal consequences.


## Hospital Roof Incident Sparks Emergency Response


The incident took place at Ysbyty Glan Clwyd Hospital in St Asaph, Denbighshire, on June 6. Witnesses reported seeing a man dressed entirely in black standing on the hospital roof above the main entrance. Many described his clothing as resembling the iconic Grim Reaper figure because of its dark appearance.


According to reports presented in court, the man also had a long bladed object while making loud noises from the rooftop. His actions quickly attracted attention from patients, healthcare workers, and visitors entering and leaving the hospital.


Emergency services responded rapidly. Several police vehicles and fire service crews attended the scene to ensure public safety and safely remove the man from the roof. Thankfully, no injuries were reported during the incident.


## Costume Described as a Crow Outfit


The individual was later identified as 26 year old Leon Gillespie. During legal proceedings, he explained that the outfit was intended to represent a crow rather than the Grim Reaper.


Despite this explanation, the unusual costume combined with his location on the hospital roof understandably caused alarm. Hospitals are places where vulnerable patients receive treatment, and any unexpected disturbance can increase stress for patients, families, and medical staff.


Authorities stated that Gillespie did not provide a clear explanation for why he climbed onto the building.


## Guilty Plea Results in Financial Penalties


The case was heard at Llandudno Magistrates' Court in North Wales.


Gillespie pleaded guilty to causing a nuisance without reasonable excuse on NHS premises. The court ordered him to pay:


- £100 in fines
- £100 towards Crown Prosecution Service costs
- £50 in compensation


The total financial penalty amounted to £250.


The offence relates specifically to causing disruption on National Health Service property without lawful reason, an offence designed to protect hospitals from unnecessary disturbances that could interfere with patient care.


## Additional Shoplifting Offences


The hospital rooftop incident was not the only matter before the court.


Reports revealed that Gillespie also admitted to separate shoplifting offences. These included stealing approximately £30 to £40 worth of cat food and cat litter from a Pets at Home store in March.


He also admitted taking food and drink items from a Sainsbury's supermarket in Llandudno during May.


These offences were considered alongside the hospital nuisance charge during sentencing.


## Hospital Maintains Zero Tolerance Policy


Following the incident, representatives from Betsi Cadwaladr University Health Board reiterated their commitment to protecting hospital staff and patients.


The health board confirmed that it has a strict zero tolerance policy toward abuse, aggression, and disruptive behaviour across all NHS sites.


Hospitals are designed to provide safe environments for medical treatment and recovery. Any activity that distracts healthcare professionals or causes fear among patients can place additional pressure on already busy emergency services.


The response from the health board reflects the importance of maintaining calm and secure healthcare facilities for everyone.


## Why Hospital Security Matters


While unusual public incidents often attract attention online, events that occur at hospitals carry greater significance because they directly affect vulnerable people.


Patients recovering from surgery, receiving emergency treatment, or visiting seriously ill relatives may already be experiencing anxiety. Unexpected disturbances such as someone climbing onto a hospital roof can increase stress levels and potentially disrupt hospital operations.


Emergency responders must also divert valuable resources to handle situations like these. Police officers, firefighters, and hospital security personnel all become involved, reducing their availability for other emergencies within the community.


## Public Reactions to the Incident


News of the incident quickly spread across social media and news websites because of the unusual circumstances.


Many readers commented on the striking appearance of the costume, while others expressed concern over the unnecessary use of emergency services. Some questioned why anyone would choose a hospital as the location for such behaviour, particularly given the presence of vulnerable patients.


Although the event may seem unusual, authorities treated the matter seriously because of the potential risks involved.


## Legal Consequences for Public Nuisance on NHS Premises


UK law provides protections for healthcare facilities against behaviour that interferes with their operation.


Individuals who intentionally cause disturbances at NHS hospitals without reasonable excuse may face prosecution. Penalties can include fines, compensation orders, or more severe consequences depending on the circumstances.


In this case, the court determined that Gillespie's behaviour amounted to causing a nuisance on NHS premises. His guilty plea avoided the need for a full trial but still resulted in financial penalties.


## A Reminder About Respecting Healthcare Facilities


Hospitals play a critical role in every community, providing care to people during some of the most challenging moments of their lives.


Regardless of the intention behind unusual behaviour, actions that create fear or disrupt medical services can have far reaching consequences. Healthcare staff already face demanding workloads, and unnecessary disturbances only add further pressure.


Authorities continue to encourage members of the public to respect hospital property and ensure emergency services remain available for genuine emergencies.


The case serves as a reminder that even behaviour intended as a prank or personal expression can result in criminal charges when it affects public safety or healthcare operations.

The unusual case involving Leon Gillespie attracted widespread attention because of the striking imagery of a dark costume on a hospital rooftop.
