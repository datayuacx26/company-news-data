# Urgent Search Underway After Giant Monitor Lizard Spotted in Italy 13,000 Kilometres From Its Natural Habitat

A rare and giant monitor lizard has become the focus of a large search operation in northern Italy after it was reportedly spotted roaming freely near the town of Montichiari. The unexpected appearance of the reptile has attracted widespread attention, with local authorities deploying drones, cage traps, and thermal imaging equipment to locate the elusive animal.


The sighting has surprised wildlife experts and residents alike because the reptile belongs to a species native to Papua New Guinea, located more than 13,000 kilometres away. Officials believe the animal may have escaped from illegal captivity or been deliberately abandoned.


The unusual case has sparked concerns about public safety, wildlife protection, and the illegal exotic pet trade.


## Where Was the Giant Lizard Spotted?


The large reptile was first photographed in early July near the Gasparina farmhouse in Montichiari, a town in Italy's Lombardy region. The image was captured by a local worker and later verified by authorities, confirming that the sighting was genuine.


Since then, there have been multiple reported sightings in the surrounding countryside. Although the animal has remained difficult to track, local officials have taken the reports seriously and temporarily restricted pedestrian access to parts of the area where it was last seen.


The restrictions were introduced to reduce potential risks while search teams continue their efforts.


## Authorities Launch Extensive Search Operation


The search for the giant monitor lizard has grown into one of the most unusual wildlife operations seen in Italy in recent years.


Search teams have reportedly used:


- Drones to scan fields and wooded areas
- Cage traps placed at strategic locations
- Thermal imaging cameras
- Night vision equipment
- Local forestry and provincial police patrols


Because monitor lizards are cold blooded, thermal cameras can be challenging to use. Officials hope the reptile's body temperature will contrast enough with its surroundings when it basks in the sun, making it easier to detect.


Television crews and journalists have also joined the search, bringing national attention to the mysterious reptile.


## Mayor Expresses Frustration


Montichiari Mayor Marco Togni has spoken publicly about the intense media attention surrounding the incident.


According to Italian media reports, the mayor said he has been overwhelmed by constant calls from reporters while stressing that the situation is not a joke for local authorities.


He also appealed to anyone with information to come forward. If the animal has died, he urged anyone who finds it to leave it in a visible location so officials can officially close the investigation and redirect resources elsewhere.


## What Is a Papuan Monitor Lizard?


The reptile is believed to be a Papuan monitor, also known as a crocodile monitor. It is one of the world's longest lizard species and is native to the rainforests and swamp regions of southeastern Papua New Guinea.


These impressive reptiles are known for their:


- Long muscular tails
- Powerful curved claws
- Sharp teeth
- Excellent climbing ability
- Highly intelligent hunting behaviour


Adult crocodile monitors can reach lengths of up to 2.5 metres, making them among the largest lizards on Earth after the Komodo dragon and Asian water monitor.


Their natural diet includes birds, eggs, rodents, small mammals, reptiles, and carrion.


## How Did the Lizard End Up in Italy?


Authorities have not determined exactly how the reptile arrived in northern Italy.


However, officials believe the most likely explanation is illegal ownership as an exotic pet. The animal may have escaped from captivity or been abandoned by its owner.


Keeping protected monitor lizard species without proper authorization is illegal in many countries, including Italy.


The incident has once again raised concerns about the international exotic animal trade, where rare reptiles are sometimes imported and sold despite strict conservation regulations.


## Is the Giant Monitor Lizard Dangerous?


Although crocodile monitors are not known to hunt humans, they are powerful wild animals capable of defending themselves aggressively if threatened.


They possess:


- Extremely sharp claws
- Strong jaws
- Long serrated teeth
- Powerful tails used for balance and defence


Wildlife experts generally advise people to avoid approaching any large monitor lizard.


Officials in Italy have encouraged residents to immediately report sightings instead of attempting to capture or scare away the reptile.


## Protected Species Under International Law


The Papuan monitor is considered a protected species under the Convention on International Trade in Endangered Species of Wild Fauna and Flora, commonly known as CITES.


The species faces several threats in its natural habitat, including:


- Illegal wildlife trafficking
- Habitat destruction
- Deforestation
- Hunting


International regulations are designed to prevent uncontrolled trade that could further reduce wild populations.


## Local Residents Remain Concerned


People living near the reported sighting have expressed understandable concerns.


Workers who irrigate farmland during the evening have reportedly become more cautious, while nearby property owners believe the reptile may be hiding in underground canals or abandoned waterways connected to the Medio Chiese River.


Although no attacks or injuries have been reported, uncertainty about the animal's location has kept residents alert.


## Why This Story Has Captured Global Attention


Stories involving exotic animals appearing far outside their natural range often attract worldwide interest, but this case stands out because of the extraordinary distance involved.


Finding a Papuan monitor in rural Italy is highly unusual and highlights broader issues involving illegal wildlife ownership and conservation.


The ongoing search also demonstrates how seriously local authorities treat reports involving potentially dangerous exotic animals, even when the likelihood of human conflict remains relatively low.


Whether the lizard is eventually captured safely or remains hidden, the incident has already become one of Europe's most unusual wildlife stories of the year.
