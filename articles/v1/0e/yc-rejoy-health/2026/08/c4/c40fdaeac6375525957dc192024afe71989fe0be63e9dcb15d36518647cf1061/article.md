# Woman Dies After Accidental Paracetamol Overdose Following 19-Hour Hospital Treatment Delay

A 75-year-old woman died after an accidental paracetamol overdose and a 19-hour delay in receiving treatment. Learn about the risks, symptoms and safe use of paracetamol.


A recent inquest has highlighted the serious dangers of paracetamol overdose after a 75-year-old woman died following an accidental overdose and a lengthy delay in receiving potentially life-saving treatment.


Barbara Cope arrived at Rotherham Hospital’s A&E department on June 11, 2025, after experiencing symptoms including slurred speech, reduced consciousness and a significant reduction in how much she was eating and drinking.


The case has raised concerns about how quickly abnormal blood test results are identified, communicated and acted upon, while also reminding the public that paracetamol, despite being one of the most widely used painkillers in the UK, can cause severe liver damage when too much is taken.


## What happened to Barbara Cope?


According to evidence presented at the inquest, Barbara was admitted to Rotherham Hospital after becoming unwell. A blood sample was eventually taken at around 4pm on June 12, with the results available at approximately 5pm.


The test showed a high level of paracetamol in her blood.


However, the coroner heard that the result was not reviewed or acted upon until around 10am the following day. Treatment with N-acetylcysteine, commonly known as NAC, was then started at approximately 12:30pm.


This meant that around 19 hours passed between the blood test result becoming available and the administration of the time-critical medication.


Barbara's condition deteriorated during the night and she underwent two further medical reviews. The inquest heard that the abnormal blood result was still not identified and acted upon during those reviews.


She later died from multiple organ failure, acute liver failure and inadvertent paracetamol toxicity.


The coroner concluded that her death was accidental.


## Why is paracetamol overdose so dangerous?


Paracetamol is commonly used to relieve pain and reduce a high temperature. It is available under several brand names and is found in a number of combination medicines.


When taken correctly, paracetamol is generally considered safe for most adults. However, taking more than the recommended amount can cause serious and potentially fatal liver damage.


The danger is that a person may not immediately realise that an overdose has occurred. Symptoms can initially be mild or absent, while serious liver injury develops later.


This is one reason suspected paracetamol overdose requires urgent medical assessment rather than waiting for symptoms to appear.


The NHS advises anyone who has taken more paracetamol than stated on the packet or leaflet to seek medical advice.


## How much paracetamol can adults take?


For standard 500mg paracetamol tablets, the NHS says adults can usually take one or two tablets per dose, leaving at least four hours between doses.


Adults should not take more than eight 500mg tablets in a 24-hour period.


However, the appropriate dose can vary depending on factors such as body weight, existing health conditions and other medicines being taken.


People who weigh less than 50kg may require a lower dose. Certain medical conditions and other risk factors can also affect how much paracetamol is appropriate.


For this reason, people should always read the instructions supplied with their medicine and speak to a pharmacist or doctor if they are unsure.


## Accidental overdose can happen more easily than people think


One of the major risks is accidentally taking paracetamol from more than one medicine.


Paracetamol is included in some cold and flu remedies and combination painkillers. Someone who takes a standard paracetamol tablet alongside another medicine containing paracetamol may unknowingly exceed the recommended amount.


Checking the active ingredients on medicine packaging can help prevent this mistake.


It is also important not to take an additional dose simply because pain has not improved. Increasing the amount without professional advice can increase the risk of harm.


## What are the symptoms of paracetamol poisoning?


Paracetamol poisoning does not always produce obvious symptoms immediately.


Possible symptoms can include nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, loss of appetite and generally feeling unwell. More serious liver injury can later result in jaundice, confusion and other severe complications.


However, symptoms alone cannot determine whether someone has taken a dangerous amount.


Anyone who believes they have taken too much paracetamol should seek urgent medical advice, even if they currently feel well.


The NHS recommends contacting NHS 111 if more paracetamol has been taken than the amount stated on the medicine packaging. If someone is seriously unwell or requires emergency assistance, emergency medical services should be contacted.


## Why early treatment matters


The treatment highlighted in Barbara Cope's case was N-acetylcysteine, or NAC.


NAC is used in hospitals to help protect the liver following paracetamol poisoning. The decision to administer treatment depends on factors including the amount taken, timing, blood test results and the patient's clinical circumstances.


The case demonstrates why suspected overdose is treated as a medical emergency.


The coroner's findings also focused on communication and responsibility within healthcare settings. The Prevention of Future Deaths system allows coroners to raise concerns when they believe action should be taken to reduce the risk of similar deaths occurring in the future.


In response to the case, Rotherham NHS Foundation Trust said it had introduced measures aimed at improving the management of critically abnormal blood results. The trust also said it planned to pilot a system to alert staff to critical results within its urgent and emergency care centre.


## The key lesson for patients


The case is not a reason to avoid paracetamol when it is appropriately used. Instead, it highlights why the medicine should be treated with the same care as any other medication.


Always follow the dosage instructions on the packet or as prescribed by a healthcare professional.


Do not take more than the recommended amount.


Leave the required gap between doses.


Check whether other medicines contain paracetamol before taking them.


If you have taken too much, do not wait for symptoms to develop before seeking advice.


People with liver problems, low body weight or other health conditions should ask a doctor or pharmacist whether their usual dose is appropriate.


## A wider warning about medication safety


Barbara Cope's death has also highlighted an important issue within hospital care: abnormal test results need to be communicated clearly, assigned to the appropriate healthcare professional and acted upon promptly.


The coroner's concerns went beyond the individual blood test. The report emphasised the importance of clear communication, record keeping and understanding who is responsible for ongoing patient care.


For the public, the message is equally important. A medicine being available without a prescription does not mean that taking more than the recommended amount is harmless.


Paracetamol remains an effective and widely used painkiller, but an overdose can become a medical emergency.


If you or someone else may have taken too much, seek medical advice immediately rather than waiting for symptoms.
