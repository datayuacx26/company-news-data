# Outshine Fruit Bars Recall: What Consumers Need to Know About the Possible Glass Contamination

A nationwide recall involving select **Outshine Fruit Bars** has prompted consumers to check their freezers after the manufacturer announced concerns about possible glass contamination in certain batches.


The voluntary recall affects specific packages of five Outshine fruit bar flavors: **strawberry, watermelon, grape, tangerine, and black cherry** . According to the manufacturer, Dreyer's Grand Ice Cream, Inc., the affected products were distributed to retailers across the United States.


Consumers who regularly purchase frozen fruit snacks may want to examine their packages carefully before eating them. The recall is limited to particular products, package sizes, and production batches, meaning that not every Outshine fruit bar is affected.


## Outshine Fruit Bars Recalled Over Possible Glass


Dreyer's Grand Ice Cream announced the voluntary recall after identifying a potential foreign-material concern involving glass. The company said the recall applies only to selected lots of five Outshine Fruit Bars flavors.


The recalled products were sold in **six-count boxes containing 2.5-ounce fruit bars** . The manufacturer said the products were distributed nationwide, although it did not release a complete list of individual stores where the recalled items were sold.


The affected flavors are:


- Strawberry
- Watermelon
- Grape
- Tangerine
- Black Cherry


Dreyer's emphasized that other Outshine products and fruit bar varieties are not included in this recall.


Consumers should therefore avoid assuming that every Outshine product has been recalled. Instead, shoppers should compare the product information on their packages with the details provided in the official recall notice.


## How to Identify the Recalled Products


Several identifying details can help consumers determine whether a product is included in the recall.


The affected **strawberry Outshine Fruit Bars** carry UPC **041548610047** and have best-by dates of September 30, 2027, October 31, 2027, or November 30, 2027.


The recalled **grape** fruit bars have UPC **041548244044** and include products with best-by dates of September 30, 2027, and October 31, 2027.


For **watermelon** , the UPC is **041548413624** , with affected products carrying best-before dates of June 30, 2027, or July 31, 2027.


The recalled **black cherry** fruit bars have UPC **041548000121** and a best-by date of July 31, 2027.


Finally, affected **tangerine** fruit bars carry UPC **041548612041** and have a best-by date of October 31, 2027.


In addition to UPC codes and dates, consumers should check the **batch or lot number** printed on the packaging. The complete list of affected batch numbers is available in the official recall announcement from Dreyer's.


Because recalls can involve specific production lots, checking the complete product information is important before deciding whether a package is safe to consume.


## Why Is Glass Contamination a Food Safety Concern?


Glass is considered a foreign material when it unintentionally enters a food product. During food manufacturing and packaging, contamination can potentially result from damaged containers, broken equipment, packaging problems, or other environmental sources.


Food safety specialists note that glass can enter food products through a variety of pathways during production. Potential sources can include damaged glass containers, broken fixtures, windows, or glassware in a manufacturing environment.


The presence of glass in a frozen food product is particularly concerning because consumers may not immediately notice a small fragment before eating the product.


According to the U.S. Food and Drug Administration, swallowing sharp foreign material can potentially cause injuries to the mouth, throat, teeth, or digestive tract. Depending on the size and shape of a fragment, more serious injuries can also occur.


For that reason, consumers who discover that they have a recalled product should not attempt to inspect it by eating or tasting it.


## No Injuries or Illnesses Reported So Far


Dreyer's said it had not received reports of illnesses or injuries associated with the recalled fruit bars at the time of the announcement.


Even without reported injuries, the company is advising consumers who have purchased affected lots to take the recall seriously. Customers should dispose of the recalled products or return them to the retailer where they were purchased to request a full refund.


Anyone who believes they may have been injured after consuming a recalled product should seek medical attention promptly.


The company also apologized to consumers and retailers for the inconvenience caused by the recall and said product safety and quality remain priorities.


## What Should Consumers Do With Recalled Outshine Fruit Bars?


If you have Outshine Fruit Bars in your freezer, start by checking the flavor, package size, UPC, best-by date, and batch number.


If all of the information matches an affected product listed in the recall, do not eat the fruit bars. The safest approach is to discard the product or return it to the store for a refund, following any retailer instructions.


Consumers should also avoid giving recalled products to family members, friends, or children. If a package has been opened, use caution when handling and disposing of the remaining product.


If you are uncertain about whether your product is covered, compare the package details against the official recall information before consuming it.
