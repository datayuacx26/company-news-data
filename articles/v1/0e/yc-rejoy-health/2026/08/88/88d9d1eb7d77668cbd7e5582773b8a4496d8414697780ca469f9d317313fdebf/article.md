# Jessica Bang Dies at 18 After Brain Haemorrhage While Preparing for Thailand Golf Tournament

Australian golf prodigy Jessica Bang has died aged 18 after suffering a brain haemorrhage while preparing for a tournament in Thailand. Read about her rising career, achievements and tributes.


Australian golf has been left mourning the sudden death of rising star Jessica Bang, who passed away at the age of 18 after suffering a brain haemorrhage while preparing for a tournament in Thailand.


Bang collapsed on August 1 while in Bangkok and was taken to hospital for emergency treatment. She underwent brain surgery and spent almost two weeks in intensive care before her death on Thursday, August 13. Her passing has prompted an outpouring of grief from her family, fellow golfers and the wider Australian sporting community.


The teenager had been building an exciting career in professional golf and was regarded as one of Australia's promising young talents. Her death has brought an abrupt end to a career that had only recently begun but had already produced several significant achievements.


## Jessica Bang collapsed while preparing for a tournament


Bang was in Thailand as she prepared for an international qualifying event connected with the Korean women's professional golf tour. According to reports, she suddenly collapsed on August 1 after suffering a brain haemorrhage.


She was rushed to a hospital in Bangkok, where doctors performed emergency brain surgery. Bang remained in intensive care for almost two weeks as her family and friends hoped she would recover.


Despite the medical team's efforts, Bang died on August 13.


The news has shocked the golf community because of her age and the speed with which her promising sporting career was cut short. Reports from multiple outlets have confirmed the circumstances surrounding her illness and death.


## A remarkable start to her professional golf career


Jessica Bang had only recently entered professional golf, but she had already made a strong impression.


She earned her place on the WPGA Tour of Australasia through qualifying school at the end of 2025. Her progression followed an impressive amateur career, which included victory in the Queensland Amateur.


Earlier in 2026, Bang produced one of the biggest results of her young career when she won the Ford Women's NSW Open Regional Qualifying Event at Moss Vale.


Remarkably, it was only her fifth professional start.


Her victory demonstrated the potential that had made Bang such an exciting prospect in Australian golf. She had quickly moved from promising amateur to professional winner, suggesting that she could have enjoyed a long and successful career in the sport.


## WPGA Tour pays tribute to Jessica Bang


The WPGA Tour of Australasia expressed its deep sadness following Bang's death and paid tribute to her ability and character.


The organisation described her as an "outstanding young talent" who had made a significant impression during her short time on the professional tour.


The tribute reflected the sense of loss within Australian golf. Bang had only recently begun competing at the professional level, yet she had already established herself as a player with considerable promise.


Her death is therefore not only the loss of a young athlete but also the loss of a career that appeared to have a bright future.


## Family remembers her personality and passion


Bang's family also confirmed her death through a fundraising campaign established to help with medical expenses.


Her cousins said they were "truly heartbroken" by the news while stressing that they wanted Jessica to be remembered for her achievements, passion and dedication to golf.


They also highlighted her personality away from the course, remembering her sense of humour and cheerful, vibrant character.


The fundraiser was created to help Bang's parents with medical costs following her sudden illness in Thailand. It had raised close to A$50,000, equivalent to around £26,000, according to reports.
