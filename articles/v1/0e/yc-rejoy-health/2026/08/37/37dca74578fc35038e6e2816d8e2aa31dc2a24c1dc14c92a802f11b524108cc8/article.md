# FDA Announces Massive Recall of Rohto Eye Drops Over Sterility Concerns: What Consumers Need to Know

## More Than 11 Million Eye Drop Packages Recalled Across the United States


Millions of consumers across the United States are being urged to check their medicine cabinets after a major recall involving Rohto Cooling Eye Drops. The U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) has announced that more than 11 million packages of several Rohto eye drop products are being recalled because of concerns related to product sterility.


Eye drops are designed to come into direct contact with one of the body's most sensitive organs. Because of this, any potential contamination or manufacturing issue can increase the risk of eye irritation, infection, or other health complications. While the FDA has not reported confirmed injuries linked to this recall, the agency has classified the action as a Class II recall, indicating that temporary or medically reversible health effects are possible.


This article explains why the recall was issued, which products are affected, what consumers should do next, and why sterility is essential for eye care products.


## Why Are the Rohto Eye Drops Being Recalled?


According to the FDA enforcement report, Vietnam-based Rohto-Mentholatum Co., Ltd. voluntarily initiated the recall after identifying a lack of assurance of sterility in certain eye drop products.


Sterility is one of the most important quality standards for ophthalmic medications. Since eye drops are applied directly into the eyes, they must remain completely free of harmful microorganisms or contaminants during manufacturing and packaging.


Although the FDA did not release additional technical details regarding the manufacturing issue, the concern was significant enough for multiple product lots to be recalled nationwide.


The recall began on July 14, 2026, and the FDA officially classified it on July 23, 2026.


## What Does a Class II Recall Mean?


The FDA uses different classifications to communicate the level of health risk associated with recalled products.


A Class II recall means that using the affected product may cause temporary or medically reversible health problems. The likelihood of severe or life-threatening consequences is considered low, but consumers are still advised not to use the recalled products.


While this category is less serious than a Class I recall, it should not be ignored, particularly when the products are intended for eye care.


## Which Rohto Eye Drops Are Included?


The recall affects several popular Rohto Cooling Eye Drops sold throughout the United States.


The impacted products include:


- Rohto Cooling Eye Drops All-In-One
- Rohto Cooling Eye Drops Max Strength
- Rohto Cooling Eye Drops Optic Glow
- Rohto Cooling Eye Drops Digi Eye
- Rohto Cooling Eye Drops Dry Aid
- Rohto Cooling Eye Drops Cool Relief


According to the FDA, the recall covers approximately 11.96 million cartons containing more than 12.46 million bottles.


Consumers should carefully inspect product packaging and expiration dates before using any Rohto Cooling Eye Drops.


The affected products have expiration dates ranging from July 2026 through February 2029.


## Why Sterility Matters in Eye Drops


Unlike many over-the-counter medications, eye drops bypass many of the body's natural protective barriers. Even tiny amounts of bacteria, fungi, or other contaminants can potentially lead to:


- Eye irritation
- Redness
- Pain
- Blurred vision
- Eye infections
- Corneal damage in severe cases


Maintaining sterility throughout the manufacturing process helps ensure that products remain safe until they reach consumers.


If sterility cannot be guaranteed, manufacturers often issue recalls as a precaution to reduce potential health risks.


## Previous Eye Drop Safety Concerns


This is not the first major eye drop recall in recent years.


In 2023, the FDA warned consumers to stop using more than two dozen over-the-counter eye drop products sold under several retail brands after concerns that contamination could lead to serious eye infections and possible vision loss.


That same year, health officials investigated an outbreak involving drug-resistant *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* bacteria linked to contaminated artificial tears and eye drops. The outbreak resulted in severe infections and highlighted the importance of strict manufacturing standards for ophthalmic products.


These incidents have increased public awareness regarding eye drop safety and manufacturing quality control.


## What Should Consumers Do?


If you currently own any Rohto Cooling Eye Drops, take the following precautions:


- Check the product name and expiration date.
- Compare your product with the FDA recall notice.
- Stop using any affected product immediately.
- Contact the retailer or manufacturer regarding return or refund instructions.
- Speak with your healthcare provider if you experience eye pain, redness, swelling, unusual discharge, blurred vision, or other concerning symptoms after using the product.


Consumers should never continue using a recalled eye care product, even if it appears unopened or unused.


## Are All Rohto Products Affected?


No.


The recall only applies to specific lots of selected Rohto Cooling Eye Drops listed in the FDA enforcement report.


Other Rohto products that are not included in the recall are not affected based on the current FDA announcement.


Consumers should verify lot information using official FDA recall notices or contact the manufacturer if they are uncertain.


## FDA Continues Monitoring Product Safety


The FDA routinely oversees recalls involving medications, medical devices, and consumer health products to help protect public health.


Manufacturers may voluntarily recall products when quality issues are discovered during internal testing or inspections. The FDA then evaluates the situation and assigns an appropriate recall classification based on the potential health risks.


Although no widespread injuries have been publicly linked to this Rohto recall, consumers are encouraged to remain informed and follow official guidance.


## Final Thoughts


The recall of more than 11 million packages of Rohto Cooling Eye Drops serves as a reminder of the importance of manufacturing quality and product safety in healthcare products. While the FDA has categorized the recall as presenting a relatively low risk of serious harm, eye health should never be taken lightly.


Consumers should review their eye care products, discontinue use of any recalled items, and consult a healthcare professional if they notice unusual symptoms after using the affected products.


Staying informed about FDA recalls can help reduce unnecessary health risks and ensure that the products you use meet established safety standards.
