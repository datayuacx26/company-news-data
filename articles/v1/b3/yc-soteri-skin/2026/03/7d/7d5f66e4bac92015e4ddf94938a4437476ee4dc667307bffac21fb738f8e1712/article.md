# We Tested the pH of the Most Popular Eczema Creams, Here’s What We Found.

Many of the[eczema creams](https://soteriskin.com/products/eczema-cream) available in the market are sold by emphasizing familiar concepts: Our[eczema cream](https://soteriskin.com/products/eczema-cream) has[ceramides](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/skincare-ingredients/ceramide-np) , contains[colloidal oatmeal](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/skincare-ingredients/colloidal-oatmeal) , works on “[skin barrier repair](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/skin-ph/skin-barrier-repair) ,” or is “dermatologist recommended.” But one critical variable is rarely discussed: the[pH](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/skin-ph) of the product. Let’s discuss in detail.


## What is the[pH](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/skin-ph) of the product?


In a healthy human skin, the skin surface[pH](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/skin-ph) typically remains mildly acidic, around **4.7–4.9** . It happens when external factors are under control. The surface acidity is not a random thing or serves any cosmetic purpose. It is required to:


- Regulate the itch signalling
- Optimize ceramide generation
- Stabilize the skin microbiome
- Suppress serine proteases
- Organize proper lamellar membrane


In[atopic dermatitis](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/eczema/eczema-types) ([eczema](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/eczema) ), the skin surface[pH](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/skin-ph) rises. It often reaches **5.2 to 6.0** or higher. That means applying a moisturizer that has a[pH](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/skin-ph) of 5.5, 6.0, or 6.7 may further push the skin into a biochemical range that destabilizes the[skin barrier](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/skin-ph/skin-barrier-repair) and immune function.


To understand how common this issue is, we measured the[pH](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/skin-ph) of several widely used[eczema creams](https://soteriskin.com/products/eczema-cream) .

## What is the Best pH for Eczema-Prone Skin?


When healthy skin is allowed to heal and recover without frequent washing or harsh surfactant exposure, it naturally stabilizes at approximately[pH](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/skin-ph) **4.7–4.9** .


In[atopic dermatitis](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/eczema/eczema-types) , the[skin is different](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/skin-conditions/skin-types) :


- The activity of Proteases increases as the[pH](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/skin-ph) rises
- Recovery of the[skin barrier](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/skin-ph/skin-barrier-repair) is delayed at higher[pH](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/skin-ph)
- Skin with no lesions is frequently mildly elevated
- Skin that has lesions often measures between **5.2 and 6.1**


This means that the skin prone to[eczema](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/eczema) is already and inherently operating in a compromised biochemical environment.


The application of a product that has a[pH](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/skin-ph) of 5.5, 6.0, or 6.7 does not contribute to restoring the physiological acidity of the skin. In many cases, it may either maintain or even worsen the alkalinity.


## Why[pH](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/skin-ph) 5.0 and Above Is Not Optimal for[Eczema](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/eczema/)


A difference of 0.5 or 1.0[pH](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/skin-ph) units may sound small, but it is not. Each shift in the unit represents a tenfold change in hydrogen ion concentration. Hence,[pH](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/skin-ph) is logarithmic. Even slight changes or shifts produce measurable biochemical effects on the skin.


### 1. Effects on Lipid-Processing Enzymes


Two enzymes are essential for ceramide production in the stratum corneum:


- β-glucocerebrosidase
- Acidic sphingomyelinase


Both these enzymes operate optimally in mildly acidic conditions.


When the[skin pH](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/skin-ph) is ≥ 5.5:


- The activity of ceramide enzymes declines
- Ceramide conversion becomes incomplete
- Lamellar membranes become disorganized
- Recovery of the[skin barrier](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/skin-ph/skin-barrier-repair) slows down


[Eczema](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/eczema) -prone skin already exhibits ceramide deficiencies, and a moisturiser that sits above physiological[pH](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/skin-ph) does not support enzymatic correction in such a situation.


### 2. Effects on Serine Proteases (Kallikreins)


Kallikrein-5 (KLK5) and kallikrein-7 (KLK7), serine proteases, are produced by keratinocytes in the epidermis. They function to regulate the desquamation process in the skin. Under acidic conditions, their activity is also affected.


As the[pH of skin](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/skin-ph) rises:


- KLK activity increases exponentially
- Corneodesmosomes degrade prematurely
- Stratum corneum cohesion weakens
- Micro fissures develop


More importantly, KLK5 activates **protease-activated receptor-2 (PAR2)** .


PAR2 activation triggers:


- Thymic stromal lymphopoietin (TSLP) release
- Pro-inflammatory cytokines
- Direct stimulation of sensory nerves


Elevated[skin pH](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/skin-ph) , therefore, amplifies PAR2-activated itch signalling, even if no visible inflammation is present.


### 3. Effects on the Microbiome


Skin acidity also shapes the skin's microbial ecology. It supports beneficial microbiomes and prevents the growth of harmful bacteria.


At[pH](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/skin-ph) 4.7–4.9:


- Commensal organisms flourish
- Colonization resistance is maintained
- Organic acid production reinforces acidity


At[pH](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/skin-ph) 5.5 and above:


- *Staphylococcus aureus* persistence increases
- Virulence factors become more active
- Dysbiosis becomes more likely


Numerous studies show that elevated[skin pH](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/skin-ph) correlates with greater *S. aureus* colonization in[atopic dermatitis](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/eczema/eczema-types) .


If[eczema](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/eczema) -prone skin is already vulnerable to dysbiosis, applying a cream at[pH](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/skin-ph) 6.2 or 6.7 may create more favourable conditions for pathogenic organisms.


## Why[pH](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/skin-ph) ≥ 6 Is Particularly Concerning for Atopic Skin


The products measured at or above pH 6 included:


- Skinfix – 6.2
- Aveeno Baby – 6.2
- First Aid Beauty – 6.7


At[pH](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/skin-ph) 6.7, the product is over 100 times less acidic than the physiological[skin pH](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/skin-ph) 4.7.


In[eczema](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/eczema) -prone skin, where:


- Protease activity is already elevated
- [Skin barrier](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/skin-ph/skin-barrier-repair) recovery is impaired
- Microbial imbalance is common


A product in the 6+ range may further:


- Sustain kallikrein activation
- Delay ceramide regeneration
- Increase TEWL (Trans epidermal Water Loss)
- Support dysbiosis shifts


Even if the formulation applied contains beneficial ingredients such as[colloidal oatmeal](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/skincare-ingredients/colloidal-oatmeal) or[ceramides](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/skincare-ingredients/ceramide-np) , the biochemical environment still is not suitable to support optimal function.


## What about[pH](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/skin-ph) 5.4–5.8?


Many products cluster in the 5.4–5.8 range:


- [Eucerin Eczema Relief](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/skin-treatments/eucerin-eczema-relief-body-cream-review) – 5.4
- Eucerin Baby – 5.5
- [CeraVe](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/skin-treatments/cerave-eczema-relief-creamy-oil-review) – 5.5–5.6

These are closer to the physiological[pH of skin](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/skin-ph) but still fall above the natural equilibrium range of 4.7–4.9.


At[pH](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/skin-ph) 5.5:


- β-glucocerebrosidase activity is reduced relative to 4.8
- KLK activity is higher than at 4.7
- Re-acidification may be incomplete


A non-[eczema](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/eczema/) skin may tolerate this well, but for atopic skin already shifted[pH](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/skin-ph) upward, maintaining a 5.6–5.8 surface may not be sufficient to restore enzymatic balance.


## The Importance of Active Buffer Capacity


Active buffer capacity refers to a formulation's ability to resist[pH](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/skin-ph) shifts and maintain sustained acidity on the skin surface. The concept here also relates to the buffer capacity of the formulations we tested.


There is a difference between:


- A product that measures[pH](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/skin-ph) 4.8 in the bottle
- A product that maintains[skin pH](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/skin-ph) at 4.8 after application


Products measured below[pH](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/skin-ph) 5 in this testing included:


- [Soteri Long-Term Relief Cream](https://soteriskin.com/products/eczema-cream) – 4.7
- [Soteri Active Flare-Up Relief Cream](https://soteriskin.com/products/active-flare-eczema-relief-cream) – 4.8
- [Soteri Baby Eczema Relief Cream](https://soteriskin.com/products/baby-eczema-relief-cream) – 4.9


These values fall within the physiological equilibrium range for healthy skin.


When applied to elevated atopic skin, the goal is not a temporary acidic shift but stabilization within this optimal range.


## The Protease–[pH](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/skin-ph) –Itch Axis in[Eczema](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/eczema/)


[Atopic dermatitis](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/eczema/eczema-types) is characterized by a self-amplifying loop:


Elevated[pH](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/skin-ph) → Increased kallikrein activity → PAR2 activation → Cytokine release → Itch → Scratching →[Barrier damage](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/skin-ph/skin-barrier-repair) → Further[pH](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/skin-ph) elevation


Moisturizers are meant to break this loop, but if the moisturizer’s pH sits above 5.5 or 6.0, it may fail to suppress the upstream biochemical trigger.


Lowering the[pH](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/skin-ph) toward 4.7–4.9 helps to:


- Suppress KLK activity
- Reduce PAR2 signaling
- Support lamellar repair
- Stabilize microbiome composition


Addressing[pH](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/skin-ph) on the skin is therefore not a cosmetic requirement but a mechanistic relevance.


## Why This Is Especially Important for Babies


Infant skin:


- Has a thinner stratum corneum
- Has an immature barrier function
- Has reduced buffering capacity
- Is more susceptible to TEWL


[Baby eczema products](https://soteriskin.com/products/baby-eczema-relief-cream) with a[pH](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/skin-ph) of 6.2 may not support[acid mantle](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/skin-ph/acid-mantle) restoration.


Early-life[skin barrier](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/skin-ph/skin-barrier-repair) instability is associated with a higher risk of progression of atopic disease. It is very important to maintain the physiological acidity, particularly in infancy.


## Summary of What We Found


What we found was that most of the popular[eczema creams](https://soteriskin.com/products/eczema-cream) we tested fall into one of two categories:


1. **[pH](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/skin-ph) 5.4–5.8** — closer to neutral than physiological acidity
2. **[pH](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/skin-ph) ≥ 6.0** — clearly outside the optimal range for[atopic skin](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/eczema/eczema-types)


Very few formulations were found to sit within the natural equilibrium range of **4.7–4.9** .


For[eczema](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/eczema) -prone skin, where[pH](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/skin-ph) is already elevated, this difference is clinically very meaningful.
