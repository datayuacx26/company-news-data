# Understanding Eczema Types: Symptoms, Causes, and Treatment

[Eczema](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/eczema) is a group of[skin conditions](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/skin-conditions) characterized by a compromised[skin barrier](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/skin-ph/skin-barrier-repair) , resulting in the appearance of[dry](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/skin-conditions/dry-skin) patches with bumps, itching, inflammation, and sensitivity. It is a chronic[skin condition](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/skin-conditions) that needs care and management throughout life to avoid flare-ups.[Eczema](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/eczema) can affect different parts of the body, such as the scalp, face,[lips](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/eczema/eczema-on-lips) ,[hands](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/eczema/hand-eczema) , and[feet](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/eczema/foot-eczema) .


[Eczema](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/eczema) is not a life-threatening[skin condition](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/skin-conditions) , but it is very uncomfortable. If you are one of those affected by[eczema](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/eczema) and struggling to understand which **type of eczema** you have, what the specific symptoms for each type are, and how to manage them, then you have come to the right place. This article will answer all of these questions in detail. So read till the end and understand your[skin condition](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/skin-conditions) in depth.


## Common Eczema Symptoms


There are multiple **eczema symptoms** associated with[eczema](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/eczema) . Some of them are discussed below.


### [Dryness](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/skin-conditions/dry-skin)


The[eczema](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/eczema) patch is[dry](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/skin-conditions/dry-skin) and rough.[Dryness](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/skin-conditions/dry-skin) is caused by trans epidermal water loss due to a damaged[skin barrier](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/skin-ph/skin-barrier-repair) .


### Inflammation


Skin becomes irritated and inflamed by minor irritants due to[pH](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/skin-ph) balance disruption, causing redness and other inflammatory **eczema symptoms** .


### Swelling


Swelling is caused by inflammation of the skin.


### Itching


Slight itching is present in almost all **eczema types** ; however, severe itching signifies an intense eczema flare-up, irritation, and inflammation.


### Bumps or blisters


Some[eczema](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/eczema) patches are accompanied by small bumps, while others might have blisters. If the patch develops oozing or non-oozing blisters, please seek medical help.


### Scales


The[eczema](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/eczema) patch may sometimes develop scales, flakes, or crusts.


## Different Types of Eczema


As discussed earlier, there are **different types of eczema** depending on the cause and location of the patch. These[conditions](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/skin-conditions) have almost similar symptoms. Many people fail to understand the **type of eczema** they are experiencing, which is crucial for management. Some of these **types of eczema** are explained below so that you can understand the difference between them.


### 1. Atopic Dermatitis (Chronic Eczema)


According to the[American Academy of Dermatology Association (AAD)](https://www.aad.org/public/diseases/eczema/types/atopic-dermatitis) , atopic dermatitis is the most common form of eczema. Although atopic dermatitis and[eczema](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/eczema/) are often used as synonyms, you must know that[eczema](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/eczema/) is an umbrella term that has several types, one of which is atopic dermatitis.


Atopic dermatitis is a chronic, non-contagious condition. It is characterized by the appearance of rough and[dry](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/skin-conditions/dry-skin) patches with **eczema bumps** that may ooze. It is more common in children under 5 years of age. The exact cause of atopic dermatitis is unknown, but it can be caused by,


- Genes
- [Dry skin](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/skin-conditions/dry-skin)
- An immune system issue
- Triggers, such as irritants, stress, and allergens.


### 2.[Contact Dermatitis](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/skin-conditions/contact-dermatitis)


[Contact dermatitis](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/skin-conditions/contact-dermatitis) , as the name suggests, is caused by a reaction to a substance you touch. It can be if two[types](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/skin-conditions/contact-dermatitis) ,


- Allergic[contact dermatitis](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/skin-conditions/contact-dermatitis) : an immune system reaction to allergens.
- Irritant[contact dermatitis](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/skin-conditions/contact-dermatitis) : a chemical reaction or a substance that directly damages the skin.


The cause of[contact dermatitis](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/skin-conditions/contact-dermatitis) is different for everyone. Some of the most common causes of[contact dermatitis](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/skin-conditions/contact-dermatitis) are listed below,


- Detergents
- Bleach
- Jewelry
- Latex
- [Nickel](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/skin-conditions/nickel-allergy)
- Paint
- Poison ivy and other poisonous plants
- Skin care products, including makeup
- Soaps and perfumes
- Solvents
- Tobacco smoke


### 3. Dyshidrotic Eczema


Dyshidrotic Eczema, also known as pompholyx, is a[condition](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/skin-conditions) in which tiny fluid-filled blisters are present on the hands and feet. These[eczema](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/eczema/) tiny colorless itchy bumps on the skin and tiny blisters are itchy and painful and may burst to form crusts. About[5% to 20%](https://nationaleczema.org/types-of-eczema/dyshidrotic-eczema/) people with[hand eczema](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/eczema/hand-eczema) have dyshidrotic eczema. Some common symptoms of dyshidrotic eczema are,


- Small, firm blisters
- Pain or soreness
- Itching
- Peeling or scaling
- Changes in color
- Extra sweating
- [Dry skin](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/skin-conditions/dry-skin) and cracking


The exact cause of dyshidrotic eczema is not known; however, they are thought to be caused by,


- allergies
- damp hands and feet
- exposure to substances such as nickel, cobalt, or chromium salt
- stress
- smoking tobacco products


### 4.[Nummular Eczema](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/children-eczema/nummular-eczema)


Nummular means coin.[Nummular eczema](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/children-eczema/nummular-eczema) , also known as[Discoid eczema](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/children-eczema/nummular-eczema) or[discoid dermatitis](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/children-eczema/nummular-eczema) , is characterized by its round or oval coin-like patches. These skin patches mostly appear on legs, forearms, or the back of the[hands](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/eczema/hand-eczema) .


According to the[National Eczema Association](https://nationaleczema.org/types-of-eczema/nummular-eczema/) ,[nummular eczema](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/children-eczema/nummular-eczema) is a chronic[condition](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/skin-conditions/) , and if it is left untreated, the symptoms might remain for years. However, if proper treatment is provided, then it resolves in 3 to 4 weeks.


[Nummular eczema’s](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/children-eczema/nummular-eczema) cause is unknown, and it is often confused with[ringworm](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/skin-conditions/types-of-rashes) and[psoriasis](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/skin-conditions/psoriasis-vs-seborrheic-dermatitis) .


### 5.[Seborrheic Dermatitis](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/skin-conditions/seborrheic-dermatitis-black-people)


[Seborrheic dermatitis](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/skin-conditions/seborrheic-dermatitis-black-people) is a chronic and relapsing eczema type, also called scalp eczema.[Seborrheic dermatitis](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/skin-conditions/seborrheic-dermatitis-black-people) mostly occurs on the scalp but can appear anywhere sebaceous glands are present. In[infants](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/newborn-skincare/baby-eczema-vs-acne) , seborrheic dermatitis is called[cradle](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/newborn-skincare/cradle-cap) cap, and it does not reappear later in life.[Seborrheic dermatitis](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/skin-conditions/seborrheic-dermatitis-black-people) has different manifestations on[black](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/skin-conditions/seborrheic-dermatitis-black-people) skin.


Some of the common symptoms of[seborrheic eczema](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/skin-conditions/seborrheic-dermatitis-black-people) are,


- [Eczema rashes](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/skin-conditions/types-of-rashes) that can be pink, red, purple, tan, brown, or white, depending on your skin tone.
- Greasy skin or a ring-shaped rash.
- Raised appearance and forms under the body or facial hair.
- Skin irritation
- Patchy or flaky white or yellow scales.


The most common causes of[seborrheic dermatitis](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/skin-conditions/seborrheic-dermatitis-black-people) are,


- Hormonal changes
- Stress
- Illness
- Harsh detergents or chemicals
- Harsh weather
- Health conditions, like Parkinson’s disease, psoriasis, HIV, and acne
- Medications


### 6. Stasis Dermatitis (Eczema on Legs)


It is an inflammatory **eczema on the legs** that occurs due to poor blood flow. According to the[American Academy of Dermatology](https://www.aad.org/public/diseases/eczema/types/stasis-dermatitis) , the blood accumulates in the lower legs, where it damages capillaries due to pressure. It is also called gravitational dermatitis, venous eczema, or varicose eczema.


The most common symptoms of leg eczema are listed below,


- Skin discoloration
- **eczema bumps on legs**
- Itching
- Scaling
- Ulcers
- Leg swelling
- Pain
- Tenderness
- A dull ache or heaviness in your legs that gets worse when you stand.
- Varicose veins


Eczema on the legs can be treated by,


- Avoid standing and sitting for long periods of time.
- Prop up your feet when sitting.
- Wear compression stockings.
- Wear loose-fitting clothing to avoid irritating your skin.
- Topical steroid creams and ointments.


### 7. Neurodermatitis (Lichen Simplex Chronicus)


Neurodermatitis is a **type of eczema** that begins with an itchy patch. This itchy patch turns into a rash. The intense itching may cause lichenification of the rash. The **eczema rashes** mostly appear on the arms, legs, back of your neck, scalp, bottoms of your[feet](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/eczema/foot-eczema) , backs of your[hands](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/eczema/hand-eczema) , and genitals.


According to[AAD](https://www.aad.org/public/diseases/eczema/types/neurodermatitis) , the exact cause of Neurodermatitis is not known; however, most patients recall being stressed when the condition starts. Common symptoms of the disease include,


- [Itching](https://www.mayoclinic.org/diseases-conditions/itchy-skin/symptoms-causes/syc-20355006)
- Pain
- Raised and roughed patch
- Open sores
- Secondary infection
- Scarring


## Identifying Eczema Spots and Bumps


[Eczema](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/eczema/) can manifest differently on people depending on their skin color, age, and **type of eczema** . Some of the common signs of[eczema](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/eczema/) are discussed below.


### Eczema Spots


**Eczema spots** on the skin are usually the first sign of a flare-up, which may later turn into a rash or patch.


### Rash


The appearance of a[rash](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/skin-conditions/types-of-rashes) is usually the first sign of[eczema](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/eczema/) . The color of the rash may appear pink, red, or purple on light skin, and grey, dark brown, or purple on[dark](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/eczema/eczema-on-black-skin) skin.


### Texture


The rash may feel[dry](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/skin-conditions/dry-skin) cracked, scaly, crusty, or thickened and leathery.


### Location


[Eczema](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/eczema/) can appear anywhere on the body, but it is most commonly found on the[hands](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/eczema/hand-eczema) , neck, elbows, ankles, knees, and face. In[kids](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/newborn-skincare/baby-rashes) and[infants](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/newborn-skincare/baby-eczema-vs-acne) , eczema mostly appears on the face, especially the cheeks.


### Blisters


**Different** **types of eczema** also form blisters, which are small, fluid-filled bumps that may or may not ooze.


## Mild Eczema


Mild eczema or mild **eczema types** are not specific[skin conditions](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/skin-conditions) . They simply mean that the same **eczema types** are in a milder form. Mild[eczema](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/eczema/) signifies that the[condition](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/skin-conditions) is not very severe and under control. A chronic and aggressive[eczema](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/eczema/) can become milder with proper management and treatment. The mild **eczema types** are similar to those discussed above.


## When Eczema Becomes Chronic


Long-standing and recurring[eczema](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/eczema/) can progress to the chronic stage.[Eczema](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/eczema/) enters the chronic phase when the flare-ups last for three or more months. Many people wonder how[eczema](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/eczema/) starts. The answer is that it starts with[dry skin](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/skin-conditions/dry-skin) , skin sensitivity, and a damaged[skin](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/skin-ph/skin-barrier-repair)[barrier](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/skin-ph/skin-barrier-repair) , which may progress to chronic eczema later.


### Symptoms of Chronic Eczema


Chronic eczema has some signs that distinguish it from the acute[eczema](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/eczema/) phase. Some of the **symptoms of[eczema](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/eczema/)** are listed below.


- Cracks in the skin
- Dark, dull, or discolored skin
- Deeper skin lines
- Larger areas of skin breakdown (excoriations)
- Itching
- Thickened, leathery-looking skin or lichenification.

## FAQ’s


### Can[eczema](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/eczema/) cause tiny, colorless, itchy bumps on the skin?


Yes,[eczema](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/eczema/) can cause tiny, colorless, itchy bumps on the skin. It is common, like dry[eczema](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/eczema/) , which causes small, fluid-filled **eczema bumps** that are itchy and often colorless in appearance.


### Do eczema bumps go away on their own?


No,[eczema](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/eczema) bumps will not go away on their own. They can calm down and flare up, but they will not disappear. **Eczema bumps** need management during the calm phase and treatment during flare-ups. The condition can worsen if left untreated.


### How can you tell the difference between eczema rashes and other[skin conditions](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/skin-conditions) ?


You can tell the difference between **eczema rashes** and other skin conditions by simply closely inspecting the signs and symptoms. **Eczema rashes** are severely itchy, red, and scaly. They may ooze liquid, while other[conditions](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/skin-conditions) like[psoriasis](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/skin-conditions/psoriasis) have thicker, more silvery-white plaques. Scabies itching may worsen at night. Hives typically appear as welts that resolve within 24 hours.


### Is dry skin a sign of eczema?


Yes,[dry skin](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/newborn-skincare/newborn-dry-skin) eczema rash is common.[dry skin](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/skin-conditions/dry-skin) is a sign of eczema, but not every[dry skin](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/skin-conditions/dry-skin) signifies eczema. Persistent, extremely[dry](https://soteriskin.com/blogs/skin-conditions/dry-skin) itchy, and rough skin with inflammation and redness might be a sign of eczema.
