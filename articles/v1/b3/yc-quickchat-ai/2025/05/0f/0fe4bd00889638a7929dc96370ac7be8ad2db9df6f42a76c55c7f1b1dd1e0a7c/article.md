Most businesses land somewhere in between, and the final number depends on a handful of concrete choices: how many conversations you handle, which AI model runs them, how many channels and integrations you connect, and whether you buy a platform or build your own.


Here is the short answer. **You can start for free.** Several platforms include a free plan, and Quickchat AI’s gives you 50 AI replies a month with no card. **Paid plans commonly run from $9 to $999 a month** , and most small and mid-sized businesses settle on a plan between $29 and $99. ## How much does an AI chatbot cost?


AI chatbot costs run from completely free basic tools to enterprise builds topping[$1 million](https://www.lindy.ai/blog/chatbot-pricing) . Here is a simpler way to look at the ranges by business type in 2026.


#### Basic chatbot for a small or mid-sized business


- **Low:** around **$9 to $30 a month** on a no-code AI platform, or free to start.
- **Typical:** **$29 to $99 a month** on subscription, or a one-time build of **$5,000 to $30,000** for a custom rule-based bot.
- **High:** **$150 to $300 a month** with advanced features, or build costs up to **$30,000** .
- Handles FAQs, basic lead capture, and simple routing.


#### AI chatbot for a mid-market company


- **Low:** around **$99 a month** on subscription for an AI platform with natural language understanding.
- **Typical:** roughly **$299 to $999 a month** on subscription, or a custom build of **$75,000 to $150,000** .
- **High:** **$999+ a month** on subscription, or build costs up to **$500,000** for advanced AI and deeper integrations.
- Handles personalized support, resolves complex queries, connects to your CRM or helpdesk.


#### Enterprise generative AI chatbot


- **Low to typical:** usage-based from **$0.50 to $2.00 per resolved conversation** , which at scale often works out cheaper than a fixed high-tier subscription.
- **Custom build:** usually **$150,000 to $500,000** .
- **High:** **$1,000,000 and beyond** for proprietary models with extensive data training, top-tier security, and complex integrations.
- Handles sophisticated multi-turn conversations, hyper-personalization, and internal knowledge management.


These numbers are guideposts. Actual costs shift with vendor pricing, the features you choose, and how complex your build gets. The single biggest factor is the chatbot’s core nature: whether it follows predefined rules, uses AI, or runs on a large language model (LLM).

## Chatbot pricing models explained


Vendors price chatbots in a few different ways, and each affects your upfront and ongoing cost differently.


### Subscription plans


You pay a recurring monthly or annual fee for a platform and its features, usually in tiers that unlock more replies, seats, or capabilities as you grow.


Tier Typical monthly cost Common features and limits


**Free** $0 Core features, a monthly reply cap, platform branding. Good for testing and low volume.


**Small business** $9 to $99 Full AI features, common channels, basic analytics.


**Mid-market** $99 to $999 Higher reply limits, more integrations and seats, branding removal.


**Enterprise** $999+ or usage-based Dedicated support, SLAs, security and compliance, built for scale.


Subscriptions give you predictable costs and ongoing updates. Watch the limits on lower tiers; you upgrade as your volume grows.


### Usage-based pricing


Also called pay-as-you-go, usage-based pricing charges by how much you use the bot, measured in conversations, messages, or resolutions. A **resolution** is a request the chatbot successfully handled. Costs run from a fraction of a cent to[several dollars](https://crescendo.ai/blog/how-much-do-chatbots-cost) per unit. This suits businesses with seasonal traffic, because your cost tracks demand, but it makes monthly bills less predictable during spikes. For a deeper comparison of per-resolution versus per-seat models, see[AI agent pricing models compared](https://quickchat.ai/post/ai-agent-pricing-models) .


### Hybrid pricing


Hybrid pricing pairs an upfront **setup fee** , often[$5,000 to $30,000](https://www.crescendo.ai/blog/how-much-do-chatbots-cost) , with ongoing usage fees. Regulated industries like finance and healthcare lean toward this model, because the setup fee funds tailored compliance and security work.


### Custom builds


A one-time custom build gives you full ownership and can run from[$10,000 to over $1 million](https://denser.ai/blog/how-much-chatbot-cost/) . You own the intellectual property, but you are also responsible for all maintenance, updates, and infrastructure. Subscription platforms handle those for you but keep ownership of the core technology.

## Hidden chatbot costs people miss


The sticker price is not the whole bill. A few costs sneak up on your total cost of ownership.


-


**Testing spends your allowance.** As we saw building Fern & Petal, the replies you generate while testing and tuning count against your monthly quota just like a customer’s. Budget some headroom above your expected volume.


-


**Cloud and model bills on a custom build.** If you build your own bot or use pay-as-you-go AI services,[compute and storage costs](https://waterfieldtech.com/insights/the-cost-of-generative-ai-vs-ivr-vs-human/) fluctuate and traffic spikes can produce surprisingly high monthly bills. Generative AI has higher per-interaction compute cost than simpler models.


-


**The cost of a bad experience.** A poorly designed bot that gives wrong answers, loops, or blocks the path to a human frustrates users and can[increase churn and cart abandonment](https://www.stryde.com/the-hidden-cost-of-chatbots-why-ai-driven-customer-service-is-killing-your-conversions/) . Winning back a lost customer usually costs far more than the bot ever saved.


-


**The cost of not having one.** Without a bot, you miss leads and questions outside business hours. A chatbot answers around the clock and captures leads overnight, which can[save significantly](https://quidget.ai/blog/ai-automation/hidden-costs-of-not-having-an-ai-chatbot-in-2025/) compared with staffing humans 24/7. For more on cutting support cost specifically, see[how to reduce customer support costs](https://quickchat.ai/post/reduce-customer-support-cost) .


## How to estimate your monthly chatbot cost


Estimating your budget is a matter of working through your real needs rather than guessing. If you want a quick number first, the free[chatbot ROI calculator](https://quickchat.ai/chatbot-roi-calculator) estimates monthly savings from your ticket volume and support costs. Then work through these six steps.


1.


**Define scope and objectives.** Decide what problems the bot will solve, who it is for, what tasks it must perform, and how you will measure success. Aim for a clear list of what it must do.


2.


**Pick a bot type and pricing model.** Rule-based, AI-powered, or generative AI, and subscription, usage-based, hybrid, or custom. This gives you a preliminary cost for the core platform.


3.


**Estimate user and agent counts.** How many customers will use it monthly, and how many internal seats will manage it. Seat counts drive subscription tiers on some platforms.


4.


**Forecast conversation volume.** Estimate conversations or replies per month, including peaks and growth. On a platform like Quickchat AI, translate this into AI replies per month to pick a tier; on usage-based pricing, it sets your per-resolution bill.


5.


**List integrations and advanced features.** Note the systems the bot must connect to and any advanced features. On a no-code platform most are included; on a custom build, price each.


6.


**Add contingency and ongoing costs.** Include a contingency of about **15%** , plus maintenance, support, retraining, and possible tier upgrades.


A quick worked example on a platform: a small store expecting about 800 customer conversations a month, at a few replies each, needs a few thousand AI replies a month. That maps to the **$99 Essential plan (3,000 replies)** with room to test, for a predictable **$99 a month** all in, no engineering required.


## Do chatbots pay for themselves?


For most support and lead use cases, yes, and usually within months. A chatbot pays back through cost savings, extra revenue, and softer benefits like faster response times.


### Cost per chat: bots versus humans


The clearest saving is support cost. A chatbot interaction is estimated at[$0.50 to $0.70](https://fastbots.ai/blog/impact-of-chatbots-on-reducing-customer-support-costs) , while a live-agent interaction typically runs $8 to $15. If a bot handles 1,000 questions a month at $0.60 each ($600), and human agents would handle them at $10 each ($10,000), the monthly saving is $9,400.


### Revenue, not just savings


Chatbots also lift revenue: they capture and qualify leads 24/7, guide shoppers through a purchase, and[reduce cart abandonment](https://crescendo.ai/blog/how-much-do-chatbots-cost) , and they can upsell based on the conversation. To measure it, track leads and sales the bot influenced.


### A simple ROI formula


Use this basic formula:


```text
ROI (%) = (Net benefit from chatbot - Cost of chatbot) / Cost of chatbot x 100
```


A worked annual example for a mid-sized support bot:


Line Amount


Cost savings (24,000 chats/yr at $9.40 saved each) $225,600


Revenue uplift (10 sales/mo at $500 profit) $60,000


Total annual benefit $285,600


Cost of the chatbot (build plus a year of operation) $60,000


**ROI** **376%**


Your real figures will vary with volume and timeframe, but the pattern holds: for support automation, the payback is usually fast. For a detailed walkthrough, see[how to calculate chatbot ROI](https://quickchat.ai/post/calculate-chatbot-roi) .


## How to lower your chatbot costs


A few habits keep the bill in check without sacrificing results.


- **Start small.** Launch a minimum viable bot that solves one or two core problems well, then expand based on real usage instead of paying for features nobody uses.
- **Try no-code first.** For a first version, a no-code AI platform removes development cost and launches in days. Many, including Quickchat AI, have a free tier.
- **Reuse existing APIs.** Before commissioning custom integration work, check whether your systems already offer APIs or webhooks. Using them is cheaper and faster than building new connections.
- **Right-size your plan.** Review usage regularly. If you consistently use a fraction of your tier, downgrade; if you keep hitting the limit, a higher tier is usually cheaper than overage.
- **Negotiate at volume.** For large deployments, ask about volume discounts and custom enterprise pricing, which is often not listed publicly.
- **Streamline conversations.** Tight, well-designed flows resolve questions faster, which matters most on usage-based pricing. For more, see[how to scale customer support efficiently](https://quickchat.ai/post/customer-support-scalability) .
