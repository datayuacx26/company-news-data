## What Coldreach does differently


Most AI SDR tools start with a list and then generate copy. Coldreach starts one step earlier: it asks whether the account is worth contacting at all.


Before Coldreach writes a message, it researches the company and qualifies the lead against three questions:


1.


**Pain:** Is there a visible problem, pressure, growth motion, hiring pattern, tech stack clue, or business change that makes the account relevant?


2.


**Status quo:** What is the prospect likely doing today, and why might that current approach be insufficient?


3.


**In-market:** Is there evidence that the account is actively moving, investing, hiring, replacing tools, expanding, or entering a moment where outreach makes sense?


That matters because buyers can tell when an email was generated from a shallow token like industry, title, or company size. They are more likely to respond when the message reflects a real business reason for reaching out.

Coldreach monitors a large account and contact universe, then turns that research into outbound that feels specific to the company receiving it. The goal is not to make every email longer. The goal is to make every email easier for the buyer to recognize as relevant.


## Signal quality matters more than AI copy quality


AI can write fluent outbound copy. That is no longer the hard part.


The hard part is deciding what the email should be about. If the underlying signal is weak, the email still reads like automation, even if the sentence structure is polished. A message based on a generic trigger like "hiring sales reps" usually performs differently from a message based on a specific account situation, such as a company expanding into a new segment while hiring RevOps and changing its CRM stack.


Coldreach is designed around that second kind of signal. It uses research to decide whether there is a credible reason to reach out, then writes from that reason.
