# 8 Best Intent Data Providers for Outbound (2026)

If you are searching for the best intent data providers, you are usually trying to answer a simpler question.


Do you need raw buying signals? Or do you need a workflow that turns those signals into outreach your team can actually send?


That distinction matters.


Most intent data providers are built to surface in-market accounts. They are useful when you already have GTM ops, account scoring, routing, and reps who know how to translate weak signals into sharp messaging.


Most lean outbound teams do not have that setup.


They buy intent data, enrich a list, and still send generic emails.


So this guide is not a generic martech roundup. It is a practical look at which intent data providers make sense for outbound teams, where each tool fits, and when a research-driven AI SDR workflow is the better answer.

## 1. 6sense


6sense is one of the strongest options if you want enterprise-grade account prioritization.


Its public sales pages position the product around predictive AI, buyer discovery, sales alerts, buyer-stage visibility, and third-party intent. That makes it more than a raw intent feed. It is a broader revenue intelligence system.


This is useful when your sales and marketing teams already work in an account-based motion. You can score accounts, surface buying teams, and decide where to focus effort before a form fill happens.


The trade-off is operational weight. Small outbound teams often buy platforms like this before they have the process to use them well.


6sense does publish a free Sales Intelligence tier. Paid packaging is still sales-led.


## 2. Bombora


Bombora is still one of the clearest pure-play intent data providers.


Its core product is Company Surge data built from Bombora's B2B Data Co-op. The company says that co-op includes 5,000+ B2B sites, and that the data is collected directly with consent rather than scraped.


That makes Bombora useful when you want a standalone signal layer you can push into your own scoring, routing, enrichment, or outbound systems.


The limitation is the same one that applies to most pure intent feeds. A topic surge tells you interest exists. It does not tell you what changed inside the account or what message will land.


Bombora keeps pricing sales-led.


## 3. Demandbase


Demandbase is a strong fit for mature ABM teams that want intent as one layer inside a broader GTM platform.


Its public positioning is clear. Demandbase One combines account-based GTM software with data solutions, and the company also sells data as a standalone input into existing systems. On the intent side, Demandbase highlights global buyer intent data, 100M company records, and 810K+ intent keywords.


That matters if your team wants to connect ad targeting, account prioritization, sales activation, and measurement in one system.


It matters less if your team is just trying to help reps write better cold emails next week.


Demandbase pricing is sales-led, with a platform fee and a flat fee per user.


## 4. ZoomInfo


ZoomInfo is best understood as a sales intelligence platform that includes intent features, not as a pure intent-data specialist.


Its official sales pages emphasize a large B2B database, CRM workflows, buyer intent, and website visitor tracking. That bundled workflow is why many teams buy ZoomInfo. You can prospect, pull contacts, prioritize accounts, and move records into your CRM from one system.


For an outbound leader, that convenience is real.


The downside is that bundled platforms tend to become expensive fast, especially when your team only uses part of the system well. ZoomInfo also works best when your reps are disciplined about follow-up and data hygiene.


If you want a broad sales stack with intent layered in, ZoomInfo makes sense. If you want a focused pure intent provider, it is not the cleanest fit.


## 5. Cognism


Cognism is strongest when your team wants prospecting data first and intent signals second.


Its pricing page frames the product around two packages, Standard and Pro, plus contextual signals such as hiring, funding, job changes, and intent. That is a useful setup for teams that want prioritization signals inside the same workflow they already use for prospecting.


Cognism is especially relevant for teams that care about broad international coverage and a more structured sales workflow.


The trade-off is category fit. If your only goal is to buy the deepest standalone intent dataset possible, Cognism would not be my first pick. It is more compelling as a prospecting platform with timing signals layered in.


Pricing is sales-led.


## 6. G2 Buyer Intent


G2 Buyer Intent is different from most tools on this list because it is based on first-party software research behavior.


G2 says its signals come from real buyer actions such as category research, competitor comparisons, pricing page visits, and direct engagement with your profile. For software categories where buyers already use G2 heavily, that can be a much more actionable signal than broad topic consumption.


This is why G2 Buyer Intent can punch above its weight for SaaS teams.


The catch is obvious. If your buyers do not really evaluate vendors on G2, the signal quality drops with the traffic.


So I would not call G2 a universal answer. I would call it a category-dependent answer with very high upside in the right market.


Pricing is sales-led.


## 7. Apollo


Apollo is one of the more practical options for lean outbound teams.


Its buying intent product is available on all plans, including free, and Apollo positions it as part of the same workflow as search, Chrome prospecting, sequences, and engagement. The company says its intent layer includes signals from public web activity, social networks, events, hiring posts, and technology installs.


That makes Apollo appealing for teams that want useful prioritization without adding another system.


The limitation is depth. Apollo is good when you want an affordable all-in-one workflow. It is less compelling if you want the deepest enterprise-grade intent modeling in the market.


Still, for founders and small SDR teams, Apollo is often easier to operationalize than heavier platforms.

## Where intent data providers usually fall short


This is the part vendors usually skip.


Intent data helps with timing. It does not automatically help with message quality.


A rep still has to answer basic questions:


-


Why this account now?


-


What changed?


-


Who is likely driving the project?


-


What angle is relevant enough to earn a reply?


When those questions stay unanswered, intent data becomes another filter on a list.


That is why some teams buy better signals and still get mediocre outbound results.


The best workflows use intent as one input, then layer real account research on top. Hiring trends, funding, product launches, team changes, new markets, and competitive context often matter more than a generic "high intent" score.


## FAQ


### What are intent data providers?


Intent data providers collect and package signals that suggest which accounts are researching a problem, category, or vendor. Those signals can come from content consumption, review sites, web activity, topic surges, or platform behavior.


### Which intent data providers are best for small outbound teams?


Apollo is usually the easiest low-friction option. ZoomInfo and Cognism can also work if you want intent inside a broader sales workflow. If your real need is better outbound execution rather than another data feed, Coldreach is often the better fit.


### Are intent data providers worth it for outbound?


Yes, if your team can actually activate the signals. No, if the data just creates another dashboard and the messaging stays generic. Intent data is only valuable when it improves prioritization and relevance.


### What is the difference between Bombora and G2 Buyer Intent?


Bombora is mainly a third-party intent provider based on broad B2B content consumption. G2 Buyer Intent is first-party software research behavior from G2. Bombora is broader. G2 is narrower but often closer to active vendor evaluation.


### When should I choose a research-driven AI SDR workflow instead of a pure intent-data provider?


Choose the workflow when your team does not have the time or structure to translate weak buying signals into strong messaging. In that case, execution quality matters more than buying one more dataset.


## Final take


There is no single best intent data provider for every outbound team.

If you want a pure signal layer, start with Bombora. If you want enterprise account prioritization, look at 6sense or Demandbase. If you want intent inside a larger sales workflow, ZoomInfo, Cognism, and Apollo are practical options. If your buyers live on G2, G2 Buyer Intent can be one of the sharpest signals in software.
