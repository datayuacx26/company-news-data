## **Understanding the Kaiser Permanente Medical Records Request Process**


Kaiser’s regional structure matters because the applicable records workflow depends on where the patient received treatment. Northern California, Southern California, Georgia, Colorado, Hawaii, Washington, and the Northwest publish separate medical-record request pages and instructions.


For legal teams, the safest process is to identify the treatment location first, confirm the appropriate Kaiser region, and then follow the current instructions published for that region. If treatment occurred across different Kaiser regions, confirm whether additional requests are necessary instead of assuming that one submission will automatically retrieve every relevant record.


### **Processing Timelines Vary**


There is no single nationwide Kaiser turnaround time. Published estimates include:


-

**Hawaii:** Kaiser says to allow 10 to 15 business days for detailed electronic records.


These estimates relate to the specific workflows described on Kaiser’s regional pages and should not be treated as guaranteed attorney-request turnaround times. The completeness of the authorization, request scope, applicable fees, and regional procedures can affect the actual timeline.

## **Required Forms for Kaiser Permanente Medical Record Release**


### **Kaiser Permanente Authorization Form**


Kaiser’s current NS-9934 Authorization for Use or Disclosure of Patient Health Information, revision 08-21, is designed for disclosures to third parties. The same form identifies the Spanish version NS-1614 and the Chinese version NS-6274. Attorneys can review the current[authorization form](https://healthy.kaiserpermanente.org/content/dam/kporg/final/documents/forms/authorization-to-disclose-health-information-ca-en.pdf) directly through Kaiser.


The form should not be confused with every regional patient-access request form. Kaiser regions may provide separate workflows for patients requesting copies for themselves.


### **Completing the Authorization**


The current NS-9934 asks for core patient identifying information, including the patient’s name, medical record number, birth date, and email address. It also requires information identifying the third-party recipient.


For a law firm, recipient information should be complete and accurate so Kaiser knows where the requested material should be delivered. The form includes Legal among the available purposes for disclosure.


### **Selecting the Records**


The current NS-9934 provides options covering categories such as:


-


Form completion


-


Medical records


-


Diagnostic images


-


Itemized billing records


-


Pharmacy copays


-


Medical copays


The form also contains time-range selections for the material being authorized. Attorneys should review the actual[Kaiser authorization](https://healthy.kaiserpermanente.org/content/dam/kporg/final/documents/forms/authorization-to-disclose-health-information-ca-en.pdf) rather than relying on older summaries of the form.


### **Sensitive Records**


Kaiser’s authorization includes separate selections for certain categories of sensitive information, including:


-


Mental Health Treatment Records


-


Addiction Medicine Treatment Records


-


HIV Lab Test Results


-


Genetic Testing for Oregon locations


Kaiser’s form warns that certain protected information will not be included if the applicable authorization selection is not made.


For litigation teams, these selections can be important when the requested medical history involves behavioral health, substance-use treatment, HIV testing, or other specially protected information. The legal team should also consider applicable federal and state confidentiality laws before requesting or using sensitive records.


Incomplete authorizations are a major source of avoidable record-request problems. Missing patient signatures, incomplete dates, unclear expiration information, and unchecked selections for relevant sensitive records can result in rejection or require a corrected submission. ## **HIPAA and Patient Access Rights**


The HIPAA Privacy Rule generally gives individuals the right to inspect and obtain copies of medical and billing records held by covered healthcare providers and health plans, subject to limited exceptions. HHS provides an overview of these[HIPAA access rights](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-individuals/medical-records/index.html) .


For an individual exercising the HIPAA right of access, a provider may charge only certain reasonable, cost-based fees permitted by the Privacy Rule. HHS explains that covered entities cannot include search and retrieval costs in the individual’s access fee. The agency provides additional[HIPAA fee guidance](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/faq/2024/may-a-covered-entity-charge-individuals-a-fee/index.html) .


These HIPAA patient-access rules should not automatically be applied to every attorney, subpoena, or litigation request. The applicable legal framework can depend on who is requesting the information, the form of authorization, state law, and whether compulsory legal process is involved.


### **Who Can Act for a Patient?**


HIPAA recognizes circumstances in which a legally authorized personal representative can exercise rights on behalf of an individual. HHS explains that the scope of access can depend on the representative’s legal authority.


Kaiser or applicable law may require supporting documents in situations involving:


-


Legal guardians


-


Personal representatives


-


Powers of attorney


-


Deceased patients


-


Minor patients


Law firms should confirm the documentation needed for the specific patient and jurisdiction instead of assuming one representative document works nationwide.

## **Frequently Asked Questions**

Processing times vary by Kaiser region and request type. Northern California currently tells users to allow 5 business days for its online medical-record request, Georgia says requests may take up to 10 business days, and Hawaii says detailed electronic medical-record requests may take 10 to 15 business days. These are regional processing estimates rather than guarantees for every attorney request, so firms should confirm current instructions through the appropriate[Kaiser records](https://healthy.kaiserpermanente.org/southern-california/support/medical-requests) page before submitting a request.


### **Is there a fee for Kaiser medical records?**


Fees depend on the region, request type, and legal basis for the request. Georgia, for example, states that some requests are subject to a fee and that payment is required before release when a fee applies. For individuals exercising their HIPAA access rights, federal regulations generally limit providers to specified reasonable, cost-based charges and do not allow search and retrieval costs to be included. HHS explains the applicable rules in its[fee guidance](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/faq/2024/may-a-covered-entity-charge-individuals-a-fee/index.html) .


### **What should I do if Kaiser records appear incomplete?**

Look for treatment referenced in one document that does not appear elsewhere in the production, and contact the correct Kaiser ROI office when specific material appears to be missing.
