## **Understanding the Evolution of Medical Record Retrieval**


### **The Shift from Manual to Automated Retrieval**


A paralegal managing 20 active cases with five providers each is looking at more than 100 record requests a year, and every one means preparing authorizations, submitting via fax or portal, tracking dates, making follow-up calls, organizing what comes back, and spotting gaps that need supplemental requests.


That manual approach often stretches to 30 to 90 days. Applicable deadlines, meanwhile, vary by state, provider type, and request pathway. Texas physicians generally have 15 business days in certain circumstances, while HIPAA generally allows 30 calendar days for an individual access request. That gap creates case preparation bottlenecks and settlement delays.


### **Why Legal Tech Matters for Paralegals**


Modern platforms tackle these pain points through automated multi-channel request submission, AI-powered authorization review, real-time status tracking, automatic follow-up, and chronologically sorted delivery.[Codes Health reports](https://www.getcodeshealth.com/) that some client firms have achieved up to 80% less manual review time and the ability to handle more cases without proportional staff increases. These are reported customer results, and individual results may vary.

## **Minimizing Delays: Proactive Error Prevention and Persistent Follow-Up**


Provider rejections do not just delay retrieval; they reset the clock.


### **Preventing Common Retrieval Pitfalls**


Incomplete authorizations are a leading cause of denied requests. Missing patient signatures, unclear expiration dates, or unchecked boxes for sensitive records can force a resubmission and push your timeline back.[Codes Health's AI review](https://www.getcodeshealth.com/) catches these errors before submission, automatically flagging misspellings, missing dates of service, and signature issues that would otherwise cause provider rejections.


Common causes of rejected requests include:


-


**Incomplete authorization** : AI review of all form fields before submission helps prevent this


-


**Missing or inadequate photo ID** : verification of ID clarity and expiration catches issues early


-


**Insufficient patient information** : full legal name, date of birth, and dates of service need to be complete


-


**Improper representative authorization** : death certificates and guardianship documentation prevent delays


-


**Expired authorization** : automatic expiration date flagging prevents submission errors


### **The Value of Dedicated Follow-Up Teams**


Providers often miss deadlines without persistent follow-up, and manual processes depend on a paralegal remembering to make the call. Automated follow-up keeps consistent pressure through daily outreach across calls, faxes, emails, and portals, escalation when providers stay unresponsive, documentation of every contact attempt, and alerts when human intervention is needed.


## **Ensuring Completeness: Verifying Records for Legal Accuracy**


### **The Critical Role of Completeness in Litigation**


Incomplete records cause a chain of problems: missing causation evidence when initial treatment records are absent, understated damages when billing records are left out, expert review delays, and defense surprises when records surface in discovery that you never received.


### **Using Technology to Identify Missing Information**


Codes Health checks records against the original request and available patient history before marking retrieval complete. When records look incomplete, the service works with facilities to resolve the gaps before returning the completed set.
