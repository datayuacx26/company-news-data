## **Accessing HCA Medical Records Online**


### **Using MyHealthONE**


HCA's[MyHealthONE portal](https://myhealthone.com/mh1/public/mh1) allows patients to view many health records, lab results, imaging reports, and other available information online. HCA states that the portal provides access to most medical records, although certain records may still need to be obtained through the hospital's Medical Records office.


For attorneys, portal records may provide useful preliminary documentation when a client already has access. They should not automatically be treated as a substitute for a formal request when the case requires a complete record set, billing records, radiology images, certifications, or other materials that may not appear in the portal.


HCA also supports[proxy access](https://hcahealthcare.com/patients/myhealthone-proxy-forms) under defined circumstances. Its proxy guidance explains requirements for adult patients, minors, and individuals acting under appropriate legal authority.


## **Requesting Medical Records From HCA Facilities**


### **Separate Patient and Attorney Workflows**


One of the most important distinctions is between a patient's own request and an attorney's or another third party's request.


On the HCA facility pages reviewed, patient requests may be submitted through an online request portal and may also have fax, email, and mail options. HCA generally requires the authorization to be signed and dated and asks for a legible copy of a valid photo ID for the patient-request process.


Attorney, insurance, disability, and other third-party requests have separate instructions. The HCA Houston, Medical City, HCA Florida, and HealthONE pages reviewed direct those requests to the applicable SSC mailing address.

## **HCA Turnaround Information**


HCA's public pages should not be read as promising a fixed end-to-end turnaround.


For the facilities reviewed, HCA says:


-


Records delivered by email are received within 1 to 2 business days after processing.


-


Records delivered by mail are shipped within 5 to 7 business days after processing.


The pages do not provide a universal processing period that can simply be added to those delivery estimates.


Under[HIPAA access rules](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/faq/2037/are-there-any-limits-or-exceptions-to-the-individuals-right/index.html) , an individual's qualifying right-of-access request generally requires a covered entity to act within 30 days. That rule also applies when an individual makes a qualifying written direction to have the information sent to a designated third party. Attorney-initiated authorization requests should not automatically be treated as identical to individual right-of-access requests.


Texas also has a 15-business-day requirement for certain physician medical record requests under[Texas law](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/OC/htm/OC.159.htm) . That physician statute should not be described as a universal deadline for every HCA hospital request.

## **HCA Authorization Forms**


### **What a HIPAA Authorization Needs**


A valid[HIPAA authorization](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/privacy/guidance/access/index.html) generally must identify the information to be disclosed, who may disclose it, who may receive it, an expiration date or event, and the appropriate signature and date, along with other required Privacy Rule statements.


Avoid presenting a fixed 90-day or 180-day expiration period as a HIPAA requirement. The authorization must contain an expiration date or a qualifying expiration event, but federal law does not impose a universal 90-to-180-day rule.


HCA's current patient-request instructions also state that the authorization must be signed and dated and that a legible, valid photo ID is required to verify identification and validate the authorization.


For legal requests, use the current authorization form and instructions published by the specific HCA market or facility.


### **Avoid Common Authorization Problems**


Before mailing an attorney request, confirm:


-


The patient's name and identifying information are accurate.


-


The authorization is signed and dated.


-


Dates of service are accurate where requested.


-


The correct HCA facility is identified.


-


The requested record categories are clear.


-


Any legally required sensitive-record provisions are completed.


-


The authorization has not expired.


-


The request is being sent to the correct SSC.


Do not automatically include a Social Security number unless the applicable form or provider specifically requires it.

## **HIPAA Considerations for Law Firms**


Attorney medical record requests can involve several different legal pathways, so avoid treating every request as if the same HIPAA provision controls it.


A patient's[HIPAA access right](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/privacy/guidance/access/index.html) differs from a third party making its own request under a patient's authorization. That distinction can affect deadlines and permissible fees.


HHS explains that HIPAA access fee limitations apply to qualifying individual access requests, including qualifying requests directing records to a third party. A third party independently requesting records under an authorization may be treated differently.


The[minimum necessary](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/privacy/guidance/minimum-necessary-requirement/index.html) standard also does not apply to a disclosure made pursuant to an individual's authorization. Law firms may still choose to tailor requests to their case needs, but this should not be characterized as a HIPAA minimum-necessary requirement.


Whether a retrieval company is a[business associate](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/privacy/guidance/business-associates/index.html) depends on the relationship, parties involved, and function being performed. Handling medical information does not automatically create the same HIPAA business associate relationship in every circumstance.

## **Finding Older HCA Records**


HCA's network has changed over time, so an older treatment location may have operated under another hospital or system name.


When a response indicates that no records were found:


-


Verify the exact treatment location and dates using bills, EOBs, and case documents.


-


Confirm whether the facility changed names or ownership.


-


Ask the applicable records department which entity is the current custodian.


-


Verify the appropriate SSC instead of assuming another HCA hospital possesses the historical record.


Do not rely on a general six-year HIPAA medical record retention rule.[HHS guidance](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/faq/580/does-hipaa-require-covered-entities-to-keep-medical-records-for-any-period/index.html) states that the HIPAA Privacy Rule does not establish a general medical record retention period. State laws and other applicable requirements typically determine how long records must be maintained.

## **HCA Healthcare Record Fees**


### **Texas Hospital Records**


Under the current[Texas fee schedule](https://www.hhs.texas.gov/sites/default/files/documents/hospital-medical-record-fees.pdf) , hospital paper copy maximums effective September 1, 2025 include:


-


First 10 pages: up to $61.79


-


Pages 11 through 60: up to $2.09 per page


-


Pages 61 through 400: up to $1.02 per page


-


Remaining pages: up to $0.56 per page


-


Electronic records: retrieval or processing fees may apply under the state schedule


A 200-page paper record under that state maximum calculates to $309.09 before any separately permitted costs. These are statutory maximums, not a statement that HCA will charge that amount in every case.


HIPAA's[access fee rules](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/faq/2033/when-do-the-hipaa-privacy-rule-limitations-on-fees/index.html) may apply when the request qualifies as an individual's right-of-access request, including certain properly directed third-party requests.


### **Florida Hospital Records**


For Florida licensed facilities,[Florida law](https://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0300-0399/0395/Sections/0395.3025.html) currently provides that paper patient record copies may generally be charged at no more than $1 per page, with permitted sales tax and actual postage. The statute addresses nonpaper records separately.


The previous $75 example for a 200-page HCA Florida hospital record should not be used because it relied on a fee framework that does not apply to these hospital requests.


As with Texas, federal HIPAA access fee rules may affect what can be charged when the request qualifies as an individual's right-of-access request.


Before selecting a delivery format, ask the facility or records vendor what fee applies. Do not assume electronic delivery will always be cheaper.

There is no single verified HCA-wide end-to-end turnaround for every request. HCA states on the facility pages reviewed that records delivered by email are received within 1 to 2 business days after processing and mailed records are shipped within 5 to 7 business days after processing, so those figures should not be presented as the complete processing timeline. No, not under the current published HCA Florida process for attorney and other non-patient requests.[HCA Florida](https://www.hcafloridahealthcare.com/locations/university-hospital/for-patients/medical-records) directs attorney, insurance, disability, and similar third-party requests to Tampa SSC, PO Box 292409, Nashville, TN 37239-2409, and states that fax and email are not accepted for this category.

### **Can I request records for someone who has died?**


Yes, when the requester has appropriate legal authority. Under[HHS guidance](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/privacy/guidance/health-information-of-deceased-individuals/index.html) , an executor, administrator, or another person legally authorized to act for the decedent or estate may qualify as the deceased individual's personal representative for relevant HIPAA purposes, but the exact documents required to establish that authority can depend on state law and the facility's process.


### **Does HIPAA require hospitals to keep records for six years?**


No.[HHS guidance](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/faq/580/does-hipaa-require-covered-entities-to-keep-medical-records-for-any-period/index.html) states that the HIPAA Privacy Rule does not establish a general medical record retention period. The six-year HIPAA rule applies to certain compliance documentation, not to every patient's medical record, so state law and other applicable requirements generally control record retention.
