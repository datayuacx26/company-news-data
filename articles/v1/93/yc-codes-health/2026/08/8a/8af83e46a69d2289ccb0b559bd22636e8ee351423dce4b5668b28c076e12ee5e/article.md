## **Accessing VA Medical Records Online**


For eligible veterans, online access is usually the fastest way to see records already available electronically. The current My HealtheVet experience runs through VA.gov, where veterans can review lab and test results, vaccine and allergy information, provider notes, and care summaries.


### **What Is the VA Blue Button Report?**


The VA Blue Button feature lets veterans build customized reports from available VA health information. Depending on the record and tool, that can include:


-


Lab results and test findings


-


Medications, allergies, and immunizations


-


Care summaries and clinical notes


-


Other available treatment information


Blue Button reports can be downloaded as PDF or text files. The VA Health Summary, which is a Continuity of Care Document, is available in PDF and XML formats.


### **How to Sign In**


VA retired the legacy My HealtheVet username and password sign-in after March 4, 2025. Veterans now use an identity-verified Login.gov or ID.me account. Typical steps:


1.


Go to VA.gov and sign in with Login.gov or ID.me.


2.


Open My HealtheVet.


3.


Select Medical Records.


4.


Review or download the available records.


Veterans generally need to be enrolled in VA health care and registered at a VA health facility to manage records online.


### **One Important Limitation**


An online download is not proof that every record relevant to litigation has been obtained. Compare what came back against known dates of service, facilities, referrals, imaging, and billing. When completeness matters, a formal Release of Information request is still the right move.


## **Requesting VA Medical Records by Mail, Fax, or in Person**


Records can also be requested through the Release of Information office at the facility holding them.


### **VA Form 10-5345a**


**Form name:** Individuals' Request for a Copy of Their Own Health Information


**Current revision:** July 2021


**Purpose:** A veteran or other individual requesting a copy of their own VA health information. You can download[VA Form 10-5345a](https://www.va.gov/find-forms/about-form-10-5345a/) directly from VA.


Identify the patient, the records requested, the relevant dates, and the other information the form calls for. Submit by mail, fax, or in person. For an in-person request, bring a valid photo ID, such as a Veteran Health Identification Card or driver's license.


### **VA Form 10-5345**


**Form name:** Request for and Authorization to Release Health Information


**Current revision:** May 2026


**Purpose:** Authorizes VA to disclose health information to a non-VA individual or organization. The current[VA Form 10-5345](https://www.va.gov/find-forms/about-form-10-5345/) is the version to use, since older copies hosted on third-party sites are often out of date.


This is the form that matters when a firm needs the veteran to authorize VA to send records to an attorney or other authorized third party. The authorization identifies:


-


The VA facility and the patient


-


The person or organization receiving the information


-


The purpose of disclosure


-


The records or information to be disclosed


-


The patient's signature and signing date


-


The applicable expiration date, event, or condition


For information protected by special federal confidentiality rules, the authorization has to properly cover those categories. The current form includes a sensitive-diagnoses section covering drug abuse, alcoholism or alcohol abuse, sickle cell anemia, and HIV.


Authorization accuracy is where time gets lost.[Codes Health's AI review](https://www.getcodeshealth.com/) flags problems such as misspellings, missing dates of service, and signature issues before requests go out.


Incomplete authorizations are a leading cause of denied record requests. Missing signatures, unclear expiration information, or an incorrectly completed sensitive-records section can force a correction and a resubmission, and a resubmission restarts the clock. Under the federal[HIPAA right of access](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/privacy/guidance/access/index.html) , a covered entity generally must act on a request within 30 calendar days, with one 30-day extension available. Some states set shorter deadlines, so confirm the rule that applies where the records sit.


## **Where to Send a VA Medical Records Request**


Requests should go to the Release of Information office at the VA health facility holding the records. VA recommends finding the facility, opening its website, and locating the "Access your health records" section for current contact information.


### **Pittsburgh VA Example**


For requests by mail, the[official VA Pittsburgh page](https://www.va.gov/pittsburgh-health-care/medical-records-office/) currently lists:


VA Pittsburgh Medical Center Attention: Release of Information 1010 Delafield Road (136H4-A) Pittsburgh, PA 15215


For in-person visits, the same VA page identifies a Release of Information office at:


1010 Delafield Road, Pittsburgh, PA 15240-1005 Ambulatory Care Center Building 71, Room 1C106


These are two different addresses, so don't mail an authorization to the in-person office. Hours are 8:00 a.m. to 4:30 p.m., Monday through Friday. Because phone and fax numbers change, confirm the current number on the official VA facility page before sending authorizations.


### **Community-Based Outpatient Clinics**


If treatment happened at a Community Based Outpatient Clinic or other outpatient clinic, VA recommends calling the medical-records office first to ask whether the clinic has the records. The request may need to go through a VA health facility instead.


## **Preparing a Complete Request**


Before submitting, confirm that:


-


The correct VA facility and the patient's identifying information are accurate.


-


All applicable required fields are completed, and the identifying, record, recipient, and authorization information is accurate.


-


The requested records and date ranges are specific enough to identify what is needed.


-


The recipient of a third-party disclosure is clearly identified.


-


The authorization covers any applicable protected categories.


-


The required signature and date are present, using the method the facility accepts.


-


Any required expiration date, event, or condition is correctly stated.


-


Representative documentation is included when acting through legal authority.


A photo ID is required for in-person requests. Third-party, personal-representative, or deceased-patient requests may also require government-issued identification and documentation establishing authority.

## **Contacting the VA About Medical Records**


For the status of an existing request, the most useful contact is the Release of Information office at the facility handling it. Other VA numbers worth having:


-


**VA Health Benefits Hotline:** 877-222-8387, for health-care benefit questions


-


**My HealtheVet Help Desk:** 877-327-0022, for technical support with VA online health tools


-


**VA Benefits Hotline:** 800-827-1000, for benefit and claim questions


None of these substitute for the facility's Release of Information office when checking a specific request.


## **Requesting Records Through Secure Messaging**


VA also allows some requests to start through secure messaging. The veteran selects a care team tied to the facility where care was received and with "Release of Information" in the team name.


VA says the message will be acknowledged within three business days, after which the veteran gets next-step information. That is an acknowledgment window, not a promise that records arrive in three days. If no Release of Information team is available, VA directs requesters to mail, fax, or an in-person request.


## **Understanding Military Service Records and VA Health Records**


Not all military medical information lives in the same place, and mixing the two up stalls requests.


### **Service Treatment and Military Records**


Military service records document a period of active service. Depending on branch, service dates, and record type, they may sit with the National Personnel Records Center or another designated custodian.


The National Archives provides Standard Form 180, Request Pertaining to Military Records, for mailed or faxed requests. Its instructions identify the correct destination based on service history and the records requested. VA also explains how to[request military service records](https://www.va.gov/records/get-military-service-records) from the National Archives.


Many older military personnel's health and medical records sit with the Center in St. Louis, but the correct custodian still depends on the specifics.


### **VA Health Records**


VA health records are created or maintained as part of treatment through the Veterans Health Administration, and are generally requested through:


-


Online VA medical-record tools or secure messaging to a Release of Information team


-


VA Form 10-5345a for the individual's own records


-


VA Form 10-5345 for an authorized third-party disclosure


VA compensation, pension, and benefits records are a separate category from VHA treatment records.


## **VA Disability Claims and Private Medical Records**


VA's duty to assist can include helping claimants obtain relevant evidence for qualifying claims, and the process differs depending on who holds the records. VA generally makes efforts to obtain records held by federal agencies, including VA medical and military records. When records sit with a private physician, hospital, or other non-federal source, VA needs authorization and enough information to go get them.


### **VA Form 21-4142**


[VA Form 21-4142](https://www.va.gov/find-forms/about-form-21-4142) authorizes the disclosure of relevant private medical information to VA.


The current official form states plainly that it is meant for private treatment records and is **not needed to request VA medical records** .


### **VA Form 21-4142a**


[VA Form 21-4142a](https://www.va.gov/forms/21-4142a/) supplies information about the private providers VA should contact, including facility information and treatment dates.


These forms do not create VA's duty to assist. They supply the authorization and provider information VA needs to carry out a duty that already exists.


## **Complete VA Medical Records in Legal Cases**


Complete records matter most when medical history drives causation, damages, disability, pre-existing conditions, or future medical needs. That plays out across:


-


**Personal injury litigation:** prior conditions, treatment, causation, and damages.


-


**Disability claims:** relevant service and post-service medical evidence.


-


**Workers' compensation:** prior medical history against competing causes of an alleged occupational injury.


-


**Medical malpractice:** treatment timelines, gaps, and prior care.


-


**Insurance litigation:** treatment history, diagnoses, and medical expenses.


-


**Wrongful death:** medical care and health history before death.


### **Common Sources of Missing Records**


Gaps appear when:


-


Treatment occurred at multiple VA facilities.


-


Older records remain in paper storage.


-


Treatment happened through community care or another non-VA provider.


-


A request covered only part of the treatment period, or a provider supplied only part of the record set.

## **Privacy and Authorization Requirements**


VA medical-record disclosure is governed by overlapping federal requirements, including HIPAA, the Privacy Act, VA-specific statutes, and rules covering sensitive medical information.


### **Patient Access and Third-Party Disclosure**


Individuals generally have a right to copies of their own health information, and VA provides Form 10-5345a for that purpose. For disclosure to an attorney or another non-VA third party, VA provides Form 10-5345, which must carry the authorization elements described above. Special federal rules apply to sensitive information.


The HIPAA "minimum necessary" rule should not be oversimplified here. Among other exceptions, it does not apply to disclosures made to the individual or under a valid HIPAA authorization.

## **Choosing the Right VA Records Request Method**


-


**Online medical records:** best for veterans accessing what is already available through VA.gov. No form needed.


-


**Secure messaging:** best for starting a request with a participating Release of Information team. VA acknowledges the message within three business days, then provides the next steps.


-


**Mail, fax, or in person for own records:** VA Form 10-5345a. Up to 30 calendar days for electronic records, up to 60 for paper.


-


**Third-party disclosure:** VA Form 10-5345, for attorneys and other authorized third parties. Timing depends on the facility and how records are stored.


-


**Private records for a disability claim:** VA Forms 21-4142 and 21-4142a, authorizing VA to obtain relevant non-VA treatment records.

## **Frequently Asked Questions**


### **How long does it typically take to receive medical records from the VA?**


VA's current guidance says records stored electronically may take up to 30 calendar days, and paper records up to 60 calendar days, which usually applies to records created before 1998. If processing will take longer than expected, VA says it will send a letter. If no letter arrives within 20 calendar days, the requester can contact the facility's medical-records office.


### **Can a family member or attorney request VA medical records for a veteran?**


Yes, when the requester has valid authorization or legally recognized authority. VA Form 10-5345 is the current authorization form for disclosure to a non-VA person or organization. Requests involving a personal representative or deceased veteran may also require government-issued identification and legal documents establishing authority.


### **What should a law firm do if VA records appear incomplete?**


Compare what arrived against known treatment facilities, dates of service, referrals, imaging, billing, and references to other encounters buried in the notes. If a gap remains, submit a focused supplemental request rather than assuming the original production covered everything. Codes Health's Missing Record Review helps pinpoint the gaps worth chasing.


### **Is there a cost for veterans requesting their own VA medical records?**


VA's guidance does not identify a routine charge to veterans obtaining their own records through this process. Fees and requirements can differ when records are requested for other purposes or through other legal mechanisms, so confirm the procedure with the facility when requesting records for litigation.


### **What is the difference between military medical records and VA medical records?**


Military service and treatment records may be held by the National Personnel Records Center or another custodian depending on service history and record type. VA medical records document treatment through the Veterans Health Administration and come from the appropriate VA health facility or VA's online tools.


### **Can a general HIPAA authorization be used instead of a VA form?**


Federal regulations do not make one particular form universally mandatory for every protected-record consent, provided the legal requirements are otherwise satisfied. That said, VA provides Forms 10-5345 and 10-5345a specifically for its health-record processes, and using the current VA form is safer because it is structured around the elements VA expects. When sensitive information or unusual representative circumstances are involved, confirm requirements with the Release of Information office first.
