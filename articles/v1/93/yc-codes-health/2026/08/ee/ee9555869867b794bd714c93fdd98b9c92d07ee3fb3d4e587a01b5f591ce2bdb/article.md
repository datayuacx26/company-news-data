## **Understanding the Foundation: Your HIPAA Authorization Form for Atlanta Medical Records**


Most records requests from a PI firm begin with a properly executed HIPAA authorization. Under O.C.G.A. § 31-33-3, Georgia providers subject to the statute generally must furnish records within 30 days of the request or receipt of required payment, whichever is later.


Keep that separate from the federal rule. HIPAA requires a covered entity to act on an individual's right-of-access request within 30 days, with one permitted extension of up to 30 additional days on timely written notice. HHS distinguishes that from a third party, such as a law firm, making its own disclosure request under an authorization, which does not carry the same timeliness requirement. Building a calendar on the assumption that every attorney request gets a federal 30 day clock leaves you exposed.


### **Key Elements to Include in Your Authorization**


An authorization must contain all core elements and required statements under 45 C.F.R. § 164.508, including:


-


**Patient identification** : enough for the provider to locate the chart, typically full legal name and date of birth. A Social Security number is not a HIPAA requirement, though providers may ask for identifiers beyond the statutory minimum


-


**Provider information** : the specific facility name and address releasing records


-


**Authorized recipient** : your firm's name, address, and contact information


-


**Specific information disclosed** : a meaningful description of what is released, such as treatment notes, billing, imaging, and labs


-


**Date range** : the dates or records sought, as precisely as practicable


-


**Purpose of disclosure** : for example, legal representation in a personal injury matter. HHS notes that when the individual initiates the authorization, "at the request of the individual" satisfies this element


-


**Expiration** : a date or qualifying expiration event consistent with HIPAA and the provider's form


-


**Patient signature and date** : the patient or authorized representative signs and dates. Check the provider's current form for specific instructions


-


**Required statements** : notice of the right to revoke, whether treatment or payment can be conditioned on the authorization, and potential redisclosure


**Specially protected records need extra attention** :


-


Psychotherapy notes are subject to special HIPAA rules and generally require a distinct authorization, which may generally only be combined with another psychotherapy-notes authorization


-


Records subject to 42 C.F.R. Part 2 may require a Part 2-compliant consent containing the elements required by current federal regulations, including electronic-signature provisions where applicable law permits them


-


Georgia separately restricts disclosure of certain HIV-related confidential information. Review O.C.G.A. § 24-12-21 and the provider's current requirements


Treating these as checkboxes on a generic form is how requests come back unfulfilled weeks later.

## **Georgia Fee Schedule: What Atlanta Providers Can Legally Charge**


Georgia's fee schedule, effective July 1, 2026, sets these maximums:


-


Search, Retrieval and Admin: $25.88 per request


-


Certification Fee: $9.70 per record


-


Pages 1 to 20: $0.97 per page


-


Pages 21 to 100: $0.83 per page


-


Pages 101 and up: $0.66 per page


The Georgia Department of Community Health warns that these state rates should not simply be applied to an individual's HIPAA access request, where federal fee restrictions can preempt them.


### **What That Adds Up To**


A 500-page paper record could reach roughly $385 if the maximum search, retrieval, copying, and certification charges all apply. Without certification, it runs closer to $376, so check whether certification is actually being charged before accepting an invoice at face value.


Electronic delivery can reduce cost and handling, but do not assume a $6.50 cap applies to an attorney-initiated request. HHS says covered entities may elect a flat fee of no more than $6.50 for an electronic copy of PHI maintained electronically when fulfilling an individual's access request, or may instead calculate actual allowable costs or use an average cost methodology. That option is not a universal cap. Treatment depends on whether the request qualifies as an individual's right-of-access request or is a separate third-party disclosure, and HHS distinguishes a patient directing records to a third party from a third party requesting under an authorization.

## **Frequently Asked Questions**


### **How long do Georgia providers have to release medical records?**


Under O.C.G.A. § 31-33-3, providers subject to the statute generally must furnish records within 30 days of the request or receipt of required payment, whichever is later. That is separate from HIPAA, which requires covered entities to act on an individual's right-of-access request within 30 days with one limited extension. Attorney-initiated authorization requests do not automatically qualify. In practice, manual retrieval can take weeks or months.


### **Can I use a single HIPAA authorization to request records from multiple Atlanta providers?**


Provider policies vary. Emory states that if a request identifies multiple Emory facilities, one receiving facility can forward it to the others, and Piedmont accepts requests across its network through its Datavant portal. Outside a single system, expect to submit a separate authorization to each provider.

### **What is the difference between a HIPAA authorization and a medical records release form?**


A HIPAA authorization is the federally required document permitting disclosure of protected health information. A medical records release form is the broader category, covering HIPAA authorizations plus provider-specific forms. For Atlanta PI cases, use an authorization that satisfies 45 C.F.R. § 164.508 and the relevant Georgia requirements. Many Atlanta hospitals accept a standard HIPAA form, while others process their own templates faster.
