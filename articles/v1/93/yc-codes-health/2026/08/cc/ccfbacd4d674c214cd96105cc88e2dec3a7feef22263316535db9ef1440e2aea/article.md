## **Understanding the HIPAA Authorization Form for Medical Records in Personal Injury Cases**


For a PI firm seeking a client's records through a voluntary third-party disclosure, a valid authorization is typically required unless another legal basis applies. Under the[HIPAA Privacy Rule](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/privacy/index.html) , providers need valid written authorization before releasing protected health information to third parties like law firms.


Keep the two pathways straight. A patient's own right-of-access request under Section 164.524 carries a federal response deadline. A disclosure on a conventional authorization under Section 164.508 is a permitted disclosure, and HHS has said HIPAA imposes no comparable timeliness requirement there. Most attorney requests take the authorization path, so planning a calendar around a guaranteed federal clock leaves you exposed.


### **Key Elements of a Valid HIPAA Authorization**


An authorization must contain all core elements and required statements under Section 164.508, including:


-


**Description of the information** : specific record types, such as treatment notes, billing, and imaging


-


**Authorized disclosure party** : the provider releasing records


-


**Authorized recipient** : your firm's name and contact information


-


**Purpose statement** : for example, personal injury litigation


-


**Expiration date or event** : a specific date or triggering event


-


**Patient signature and date** : original or valid electronic signature


-


**Required statements** : notice of the right to revoke, whether treatment or payment can be conditioned on the authorization, and the potential for redisclosure


Separately from HIPAA's mandatory elements, facilities routinely ask for identifiers that help them locate the right chart: date of birth, medical record number, current address, and sometimes the last four of a Social Security number. These are provider requirements, not statutory ones, which is why the same form sails through at one hospital and stalls at another.


### **Avoiding Common Pitfalls with HIPAA Forms**


Codes Health identifies incomplete or defective authorizations as a leading cause of provider rejection. Common errors that restart your retrieval clock include:


-


Missing patient signatures or dates


-


Incomplete handling of specially protected information such as mental health, HIV and STD, or substance use records


-


Expired authorization dates


-


Insufficient identifying information for common names


-


Missing representative documentation for deceased patients or minors


Requests involving specially protected information may require additional consent language or provider-specific documentation under federal or Florida law. Substance use records from covered programs, for example, fall under 42 C.F.R. Part 2, whose revised consent requirements became mandatory in February 2026. Treating every sensitive category as a checkbox on a generic form is how requests come back unfulfilled.
