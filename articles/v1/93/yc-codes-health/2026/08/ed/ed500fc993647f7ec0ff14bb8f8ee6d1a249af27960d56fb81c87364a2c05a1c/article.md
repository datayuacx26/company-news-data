## **Frequently Asked Questions**


### **How long does it typically take to get medical records from CVS MinuteClinic?**


CVS does not publish a guaranteed normal processing time for each MinuteClinic records-request channel on its current resources page. For an individual exercising the HIPAA right of access, federal law generally requires a covered entity to act within 30 calendar days after receiving the request. A qualifying extension of up to another 30 days may be available if the covered entity provides the required written notice. These are maximum federal timeframes, not an estimate of how long every MinuteClinic request will take.


### **Can I request my MinuteClinic medical records for free?**


HIPAA permits covered entities to charge individuals a reasonable, cost-based fee for copies of PHI, subject to specific limitations. Those permissible costs can include certain copying, supply, and postage costs. State law may provide additional protections. The HIPAA rule providing one free accounting of disclosures during a 12-month period concerns a different privacy right and should not be confused with medical-record copy fees.


### **What information do I need on a MinuteClinic release form?**


The current MinuteClinic form requests patient name, address and date of birth; requested record types; dates of service; the required sensitive-record selection; recipient information; purpose where applicable; signature and date; and supporting authority documentation when a representative signs.


### **Is there a difference between a HIPAA authorization and a medical records release?**


The terminology is often used loosely, but the legal mechanism matters. MinuteClinic’s[release form](https://www.cvs.com/content/dam/enterprise/cvsretail/minuteclinic/service-category-pages/resources/MinuteClinic-DISCLOSURE-AUTHORIZATION-FORM.pdf) is an authorization for the release of PHI. However, HIPAA does not require the same authorization mechanism for every disclosure. For example, qualifying treatment disclosures can occur without individual authorization, and an individual exercising the HIPAA right of access has separate rights under 45 CFR 164.524.


### **Can a family member request my MinuteClinic medical records?**


Possibly, depending on the family member’s legal authority and the circumstances. CVS permits patients to exercise rights through personal representatives as allowed by applicable law and may require evidence establishing that authority. Marriage or family relationship alone should not automatically be treated as sufficient authority for access to an adult patient’s PHI.
