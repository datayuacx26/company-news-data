## **Understanding Why Medical Record Retrieval Matters in Malpractice Litigation**


Medical malpractice claims require proving four elements: duty of care, breach of that duty, causation, and damages. Each element depends on comprehensive medical documentation. Missing records create gaps that defense counsel can exploit, while incomplete analysis leaves breaches of care buried in voluminous files.


The complexity multiplies when cases involve multiple providers. A surgical malpractice claim might require records from the primary care physician, referring specialist, surgical center, hospital, anesthesiologist, post-operative rehabilitation facility, and pharmacy, each with different authorization requirements and response timelines.


Federal data indicate that most hospitals and office-based physicians use electronic health-record technology: in 2024, 95% of office-based physicians reported using an EHR, and 91% used a certified EHR, according to the Office of the National Coordinator for Health Information Technology. Digital infrastructure exists for faster retrieval, yet manual retrieval can still take several weeks and may extend longer when providers delay responses or authorization forms contain errors.


Codes Health identifies incomplete or inaccurate authorizations as a common, preventable cause of rejected or delayed requests. Authorization errors can cause a provider to reject a request, forcing the requester to correct and resubmit it, which can restart the applicable response period. Codes Health's AI review is designed to catch these errors before submission, automatically flagging misspellings, missing dates of service, and signature issues that would otherwise cause provider rejections.

### **Is a medical record retrieval service HIPAA compliant, and why is this important for legal cases?**


Law firms should verify that each vendor maintains appropriate safeguards for protected health information and determine whether a business associate agreement is required. Independent reporting, such as SOC 2 Type II, can provide additional assurance regarding security controls. For malpractice attorneys, security compliance protects against data breaches that could expose client information and create liability.

### **What types of medical records are typically retrieved for a medical malpractice lawsuit?**


Malpractice cases require treatment notes, operative reports, nursing notes, medication administration records, lab results, imaging studies, consultation notes, discharge summaries, and billing records. Completeness matters: missing operative reports or nursing notes can contain the breach-of-care documentation that determines case outcome.
