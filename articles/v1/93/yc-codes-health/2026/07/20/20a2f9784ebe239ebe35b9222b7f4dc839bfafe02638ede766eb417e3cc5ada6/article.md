## **Understanding the Landscape of Medical Record Retrieval Software**


The medical record retrieval market for legal practices has evolved beyond simple document collection. Modern platforms promise AI-powered analysis, automated chronologies, and accelerated turnaround, but the focus and target customer vary considerably between providers.


**Three distinct approaches define the current market:**


-


Speed-first platforms (LlamaLab) prioritize rapid delivery


-


Insurance-focused platforms (DigitalOwl) primarily serve carriers, with some tools also marketed to law firms for claims and demand review


-


Litigation-focused platforms ([Codes Health](https://www.getcodeshealth.com/) ) build specifically for plaintiff attorneys handling personal injury, mass tort, and medical malpractice cases


For plaintiff firms, the platform choice affects case preparation. A missed breach-of-care indicator or an incomplete operative report can affect settlement positioning.


## **Expediting Legal Processes: The Role of Medical Record Retrieval Companies**


Medical records form the foundation of every personal injury case. They establish causation, document damages, support expert opinions, and ultimately influence settlement values. Traditional retrieval methods can take 30 to 90 days and consume substantial staff resources through manual faxes, phone follow-ups, and provider-by-provider tracking.


**What plaintiff attorneys generally need:**


-


Complete records, not summaries or patient portal excerpts alone


-


Certified documentation admissible at trial


-


Organized chronologies ready for expert review


-


Litigation-specific insights identifying case-building opportunities


-


Reliable turnaround that doesn't jeopardize statute of limitations


[Codes Health](https://www.getcodeshealth.com/) positions itself as a "premier pre-litigation department without the overhead," combining retrieval operations with AI-powered case analysis. The platform serves seven legal practice areas: personal injury, mass torts, medical malpractice, workers' compensation, disability law, insurance litigation, and wrongful death.


### **LlamaLab's Speed-First Model**


LlamaLab markets itself on rapid turnaround, stating that a majority of requests are completed same-day for electronically connected providers. Codes Health notes that platforms built around same-day delivery can sometimes return records that are less complete than a fully verified set, particularly for providers without direct digital connections, and that this can require additional client follow-up to fill gaps.


For firms building cases toward trial, record completeness and certification matter. Firms considering any speed-focused vendor may want to confirm directly how records are certified for evidentiary use and what recourse exists if a delivered set turns out to be incomplete.


### **DigitalOwl's Insurance Focus**


DigitalOwl built its platform primarily for insurance underwriting and claims processing. In a deal that closed in October 2025,[Datavant acquired DigitalOwl](https://www.datavant.com/press-release/datavant-completes-acquisition-of-digitalowl) , integrating it into a broader health data ecosystem alongside Datavant's Ontellus retrieval business. Financial terms were not officially disclosed, though market estimates have put the price at around $200 million.


DigitalOwl's publicized client relationships include RGA, Nationwide, AAA Life Insurance, and Legal & General America, primarily insurance carriers and reinsurers. DigitalOwl does also market some tools toward law firms, including demand package review and mass tort or personal injury record analysis, but its case studies and partnerships center on insurance underwriting rather than plaintiff-side litigation features like breach-of-care detection or future medical expense extraction.

## **Automating Accuracy: Error Prevention and Verified Completeness**


Incomplete authorizations are a leading cause of denied requests. Missing patient signatures, unclear expiration dates, or unchecked boxes for sensitive records can restart the clock on provider response times. The federal HIPAA standard for record production is 30 days, though some states set shorter statutory windows, for example Texas requires production within 15 business days, and delays from resubmission can add real time to any retrieval timeline.


### **AI Validation for Request Submission**


Codes Health's AI review catches errors before submission:


-


Flags misspellings in patient names or provider information


-


Identifies missing dates of service


-


Detects signature issues that cause provider rejections


-


Verifies authorization completeness for sensitive records (mental health, substance abuse, HIV/AIDS)


This proactive error prevention is designed to reduce the back-and-forth that can delay traditional retrieval processes.


### **Ensuring Complete and Accurate Record Sets**


[Codes Health](https://www.getcodeshealth.com/) verifies completeness before closing any retrieval:

This expands the value proposition from simply obtaining documents to helping ensure the record set is actually complete, a distinction that matters when preparing for trial or settlement negotiations.
