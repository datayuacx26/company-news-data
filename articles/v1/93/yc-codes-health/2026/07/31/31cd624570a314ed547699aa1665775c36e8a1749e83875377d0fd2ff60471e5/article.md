Supio has established itself as a capable AI legal document review platform, but many firms also need a solution for the upstream challenge: obtaining comprehensive medical records from providers. This guide examines the top Supio alternatives, with particular emphasis on how[Codes Health](https://www.getcodeshealth.com/) combines medical record retrieval with AI-powered analysis, addressing the foundational retrieval step that document review platforms depend on.

## **Understanding the Medical Record Retrieval Landscape**


Before examining the alternatives, it helps to understand how the market roughly divides, though the categories increasingly overlap:


**Category 1: AI Legal Document Review Platforms**


These platforms (Supio, EvenUp) primarily work with medical records you already have, focusing on generating litigation work product like demands, chronologies, and complaints, though some, like EvenUp's PLAAS service, now also offer retrieval-linked options.


**Category 2: Traditional Record Retrieval Services**


These vendors (National Record Retrieval, Ontellus, Lexitas, Compex) obtain records from providers, with varying levels of built-in analysis.


**Category 3: Combined Retrieval Plus Analysis**


This category (Codes Health, LlamaLab) handles both obtaining records and providing AI-powered insights.


The key question for your firm: do you need a platform that analyzes records you already have, or do you need help obtaining complete records in the first place? For many plaintiff firms, upstream retrieval is a real bottleneck, since a demand letter platform can't add much value if a firm is missing critical treatment records.

## **Why General AI Tools Have Limits for Medical Record Analysis**


Some firms consider using general-purpose AI platforms like ChatGPT for medical record review. General-purpose AI tools can analyze uploaded documents, summarize files, and answer questions about their contents, but they aren't automatically validated for unsupervised, litigation-grade medical record review and may produce errors or omit important context.


**Considerations for Firms:**


-


**Accuracy** : General AI models aren't specifically trained on medical-legal contexts, and outputs used in court-submitted documents generally require independent verification


-


**Compliance** : Protected health information requires secure handling and clear audit trails; consumer use of general AI tools shouldn't automatically be assumed to meet these standards without confirming the applicable safeguards


-


**Context** : Legal significance of clinical findings often requires specialized training that general-purpose tools weren't built around
