## **Legal AI Software: Understanding What Each Platform Actually Does**


Before comparing features, it's critical to understand that these three platforms serve different primary functions within the medical records workflow.


**Codes Health** operates as a full-service platform handling both retrieval and analysis. The system gathers medical, billing, and imaging records through claims clearinghouses, custodian integrations, proprietary provider networks, and traditional retrieval channels. Once records arrive, AI-powered analysis extracts diagnoses, treatment patterns, breaches of care, and future medical expenses. All findings are verified by human specialists before delivery.


General AI platforms such as ChatGPT are not designed for litigation-grade medical record review. Codes Health's AI is purpose-built for litigation contexts, which supports high precision in extracting case-relevant insights from complex medical documentation.


**LlamaLab** focuses primarily on speed-optimized retrieval. The platform emphasizes same-day turnaround capabilities and provider discovery features. Analysis features exist but are secondary to the retrieval-speed value proposition.


**Supio** operates purely as an analysis and drafting platform. Firms must obtain medical records through other means before uploading them to Supio for chronology generation, demand letter drafting, and litigation document preparation. The Thomson Reuters Westlaw Advantage integration strengthens its legal research capabilities, but the lack of retrieval creates a workflow gap.


### **Why This Distinction Matters**


For firms seeking a single-vendor solution,[Codes Health](https://www.getcodeshealth.com/) reduces the coordination overhead of managing separate retrieval and analysis tools. Firms using Supio must still solve the retrieval problem independently through in-house staff, traditional retrieval services, or a platform like LlamaLab.


## **Speed and Accuracy: How Retrieval Timelines Actually Compare**


Medical record retrieval speed varies dramatically across platforms, but raw speed isn't the only factor that matters.


### **LlamaLab's Speed-First Approach**


LlamaLab markets itself on turnaround time. The platform states that approximately 60% of its record requests are sent and returned the same day. Additional features include:


-


LlamaLab states its provider discovery feature identifies roughly 3x more treating providers than traditional services


-


Mass tort optimization processing large case volumes over a period of weeks


-


Clinical case qualification the company states typically completes in under 7 days


This speed advantage comes from direct electronic connections to provider systems and portal-based retrieval. Codes Health notes that platforms optimizing for same-day turnaround can sometimes deliver incomplete record sets. Missing imaging studies, absent billing records, or partial treatment documentation may require additional client follow-up, which can create friction and contribute to client churn.


### **Codes Health's Completeness-First Philosophy**


[Codes Health](https://www.getcodeshealth.com/) takes a different approach,[delivering records](https://www.getcodeshealth.com/blogs/supio-alternatives) in a couple of weeks rather than months, while prioritizing verified completeness. The platform's workflow includes:


-


AI-powered request validation catching authorization errors before submission


-


Persistent provider follow-up across calls, faxes, emails, and portals


-


Completeness verification comparing delivered records against the original request


-


Missing Record Review identifying documentation gaps proactively


Incomplete authorizations are a leading cause of denied requests. Missing patient signatures, unclear expiration dates, or unchecked boxes for sensitive records can restart the clock on provider response times, which follows the 30-day federal HIPAA standard, though state rules can vary. Codes Health's AI review catches these errors before submission. The system automatically flags misspellings, missing dates of service, and signature issues that would otherwise cause provider rejections.


This approach addresses a common consideration with speed-optimized retrieval: records that arrive fast but incomplete. Missing imaging studies, absent billing records, or gaps in treatment documentation can affect case value, and problems discovered weeks later can be harder to correct.


### **Supio's Non-Factor in Retrieval Speed**


Because Supio doesn't retrieve records, turnaround time depends entirely on whatever retrieval method the firm uses upstream. Supio's value emerges only after records exist in a usable format.
