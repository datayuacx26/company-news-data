## **Elder Abuse Cases: Why Medical Record Retrieval is Critical**


Elder abuse litigation presents unique evidentiary challenges that distinguish it from standard personal injury work. Proving neglect, abuse, or facility misconduct requires documenting patterns over extended timeframes, often months or years of care, across multiple providers and facility types.


### **The Role of Medical Records in Proving Elder Abuse**


Medical records serve as the foundation for elder abuse claims in several critical ways: documenting physical indicators such as pressure ulcers, malnutrition, dehydration, unexplained injuries, and medication errors; establishing timelines showing when symptoms appeared and how they progressed under facility care; proving causation by linking facility negligence to patient harm through treatment gaps and missed interventions; and calculating damages through billing records and documented suffering.


Elder abuse litigation generally requires substantial documentation showing a pattern of neglect rather than isolated incidents. This means attorneys must gather records from nursing homes, hospitals, specialists, pharmacies, and emergency services to build a complete picture.


### **Challenges in Obtaining Records for Vulnerable Adults**


Several factors complicate medical record retrieval for elder abuse cases: patients often move between nursing homes, rehabilitation centers, and hospitals; clients may have cognitive impairment that limits their ability to recall treatment locations or sign authorizations; nursing homes facing liability may delay or obstruct records access; deceased patient records require estate representative authorization with death certificates; and long-term care generates thousands of pages requiring systematic organization.
