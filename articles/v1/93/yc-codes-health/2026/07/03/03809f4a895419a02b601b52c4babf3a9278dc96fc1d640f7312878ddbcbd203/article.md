## **Understanding the Role of Medical Records in Slip and Fall Cases**


Medical records serve as the foundation for every slip and fall claim, establishing three critical elements:


-


**Causation evidence** : Records from the first treatment after an incident create a temporal connection between the fall and resulting injuries. Emergency room notes, urgent care visits, and initial physician evaluations document the mechanism of injury in the patient's own words and capture treating provider causation opinions.


-


**Damages documentation** : Complete billing records, prescription histories, and treatment progression notes help quantify economic damages while supporting pain and suffering claims. Physical therapy records demonstrate functional limitations, and specialist consultations establish injury severity requiring advanced care.


-


**Pre-existing condition defense** : Defense attorneys routinely argue that injuries existed before the incident. Comprehensive baseline records covering a case-appropriate period before the fall help establish the plaintiff's prior health status and address alternative causation theories.


For slip and fall cases specifically, imaging records (X-rays, MRIs, CT scans) documenting fractures, soft tissue damage, or spinal injuries carry particular weight. Missing even one critical imaging study can undermine settlement negotiations or trial presentation.


## **Challenges of Traditional Medical Record Retrieval for Personal Injury Lawyers**


Traditional retrieval methods create bottlenecks that delay case resolution and consume staff resources:


-


**Extended timelines** : Manual processes can take many weeks and, in some cases, months. Certain patient-directed access requests are generally subject to a 30-day federal response period, but attorney and litigation requests may follow different federal or state requirements


-


**Provider rejections** : Missing signatures, incorrect dates of service, and authorization deficiencies can require resubmission and restart request timelines


-


**Incomplete records** : Partial deliveries, missing billing records, imaging CDs, or specialist notes require follow-up requests


-


**Administrative burden** : Managing multiple simultaneous provider relationships can consume substantial staff time, particularly for firms handling numerous concurrent requests


-


**Cost unpredictability** : Per-page fees and provider charges create billing surprises, especially for hospital records


Codes Health identifies incomplete or defective authorizations as a leading, often preventable cause of rejected requests. Missing patient signatures, unclear expiration dates, or unchecked boxes for sensitive records can cause rejection and restart the applicable response period, which varies by jurisdiction and request type. Platforms with AI review are designed to catch these errors before submission, automatically flagging misspellings, missing dates of service, and signature issues that would otherwise cause provider rejections.


Same-day claims should be examined carefully. In some cases, they refer to request submission or access to records available through participating EHR networks, rather than delivery of a complete record set from every provider, which can create additional follow-up work when components are missing. Codes Health prioritizes complete documentation, including billing records, imaging, and specialist notes, within a couple of weeks.


These challenges compound for slip and fall cases involving multiple treatment facilities: emergency rooms, orthopedic specialists, physical therapists, pain management clinics, and imaging centers. A single premises liability case might require records from 5 to 10 providers, multiplying the administrative complexity.
