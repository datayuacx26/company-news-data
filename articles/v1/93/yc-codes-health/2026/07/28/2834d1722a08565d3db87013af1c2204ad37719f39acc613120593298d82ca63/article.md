## **Understanding the Unique Challenges of Mass Tort Cases for Medical Records**


Mass tort litigation differs fundamentally from individual personal injury cases in ways that compound medical records complexity. A single mass tort involving a defective pharmaceutical product or toxic exposure may require records from thousands of plaintiffs, each with unique medical histories spanning multiple providers, treatment facilities, and geographic regions.


### **Distinguishing Mass Tort from Class Action**


Unlike class actions, which proceed through a representative structure when class-certification requirements are met, mass tort claims generally retain individualized questions of causation and damages even when coordinated. This means each claimant typically needs comprehensive medical documentation establishing:


-


**Pre-exposure baseline health status** to rule out alternative causes


-


**Post-exposure treatment records** demonstrating injury timeline


-


**Specialist consultations** supporting causation opinions


-


**Future treatment recommendations** for damage quantification


### **The Scale of Medical Documentation**


Consider the scope: Lexitas reports that the Zantac litigation involved approximately 156,000 plaintiffs and tens of thousands of medical records, with Lexitas selected as a lead retrieval provider. For illustration, if a 1,000-plaintiff mass tort averages 5 to 15 providers per claimant, it could generate 5,000 to 15,000 individual record requests, each requiring authorization management, provider follow-up, and quality verification.


## **The Need for Speed: Expediting Medical Record Retrieval for Mass Tort Litigation**


Turnaround time isn't merely a convenience factor in mass tort cases; it affects case qualification, settlement timing, and firm workflow.


### **The Cost of Delays**


Traditional retrieval methods that stretch from weeks to months can create cascading problems:


-


**Statute of limitations pressure** on late-signed cases


-


**Case qualification delays** are preventing accurate portfolio valuation


-


**Settlement timeline extensions** reducing the present value of recoveries

**Staff resource drain** from manual follow-up management

## **Preventing Delays: Proactive Error Checking in Record Retrieval**


Codes Health reports that incomplete or defective authorizations are a leading cause of provider rejection. Missing patient signatures, unclear expiration dates, or unchecked boxes for sensitive records can require resubmission and effectively restart the applicable response timeline. Codes Health's AI review is designed to catch these errors before submission, automatically flagging misspellings, missing dates of service, and signature issues that would otherwise cause provider rejections.


**Common rejection triggers:**


-


Missing or illegible patient signatures


-


Expired authorization dates


-


Missing consent or language required for specially protected records, where applicable


-


Insufficient patient identifying information


-


Improper representative authorization for deceased patients

## **Frequently Asked Questions**


### **What makes mass tort cases uniquely challenging for medical record retrieval?**


Mass tort cases generally require individualized causation and damages proof for each plaintiff, meaning comprehensive medical documentation across potentially thousands of claimants. Each claimant may have a number of healthcare providers (5 to 15 is a common planning assumption), generating many individual requests requiring authorization management, provider follow-up, and quality verification at scale.


### **How does AI improve the efficiency and accuracy of medical record retrieval for mass torts?**


Some platforms, including Codes Health, use automation to review requests for errors, follow up with providers, and organize received records into chronological formats, while adding case insight extraction that flags potential breach-of-care indicators and buried diagnoses for attorney review.


### **What are the potential advantages of a hybrid AI-human approach for mass tort medical record analysis?**


Pure AI solutions can raise reliability concerns for high-stakes litigation, while purely manual processes can struggle to scale efficiently. A hybrid approach, AI insights verified by humans, is designed to combine the speed and consistency of automation with added human validation.


### **What key features should mass tort law firms look for in a medical record retrieval service?**


Worthwhile features include proven mass tort experience at scale, turnaround measured in weeks rather than months, AI-powered organization and analysis, proactive error prevention, automated provider follow-up, real-time status tracking, HIPAA compliance (with independent security assurances such as SOC 2 as an additional consideration), and pricing transparency for portfolio budgeting.


### **Can medical record retrieval services help identify gaps or missing information in plaintiff medical histories?**


Yes. Platforms like[Codes Health](https://www.getcodeshealth.com/) include Missing Record Review functionality that cross-references patient medical history to identify documentation gaps before trial, working toward complete records rather than discovering missing evidence during litigation.


### **How does a medical record retrieval service ensure compliance with HIPAA regulations?**


Legitimate services maintain HIPAA-compliant platforms, and many pursue independent security assurances such as SOC 2 Type II reports, along with encrypted data transmission, secure document storage, proper authorization handling, and audit trails. It's worth confirming a vendor's specific compliance certifications and safeguards directly before engaging them to handle protected health information.
