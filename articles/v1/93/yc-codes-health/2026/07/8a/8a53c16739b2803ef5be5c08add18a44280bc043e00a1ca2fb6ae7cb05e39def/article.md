# Eve Legal Reviews

Eve Legal has emerged as one of the most talked-about AI platforms in plaintiff litigation, reaching a[$1 billion-plus valuation](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/eve-raises-103-million-at-1-billion-valuation-to-help-plaintiff-firms-deliver-justice-through-ai-transformation-302570807.html) and serving over 1,200 plaintiff firms. But for law firms evaluating legal AI tools, the question isn't just whether Eve Legal works; it's whether the platform addresses the actual bottlenecks slowing down your cases. For many personal injury and mass tort practices, a persistent challenge is getting[complete medical records](https://www.getcodeshealth.com/) in the first place.


This review examines Eve Legal's capabilities, reported strengths and limitations, and where plaintiff firms may want to pair it with a dedicated retrieval platform like[Codes Health](https://www.getcodeshealth.com/) to address the upstream part of the workflow.

## **What Eve Legal Offers Plaintiff Firms**


Eve Legal positions itself as a full-lifecycle AI platform for plaintiff litigation. The company has raised a total of $164 million in funding, including a $14 million seed round, a $47 million Series A, and a $103 million Series B that valued the company at more than $1 billion. Eve reports that its platform supports more than 200,000 cases, though its public materials have described that figure both as annual case volume and as active cases on the platform.


### **Core Capabilities**


Eve Legal's platform includes:


-


**AI voice intake** : 24/7 automated intake in more than 28 languages with lead scoring and qualification


-


**Medical chronology generation** : AI-powered summaries are generally produced in 15 to 20 minutes after records are available to the platform


-


**Demand letter drafting** : Automated demand packages that use firm templates, tone, and writing preferences


-


**Discovery support** : Document organization, interrogatory and discovery-response drafting, and exhibit assembly


-


**Built-in legal research** : Case law, statutes, regulations, medical literature, and other research sources


-


**Case management integrations** : Eve documents integrations with systems including Filevine, Clio, Litify, SmartAdvocate, and Growpath; its intake product also works with CRMs such as Clio and MyCase


### **Where Eve Legal Shines**


Eve's official G2 listing includes customer feedback praising the platform's "customer support team has been consistently excellent." Other reviewers describe the platform as useful across case evaluation, legal research, and day-to-day document review, note that it helps attorneys work through documents and facts more quickly, and highlight that its drafting adapts to a firm's preferred tone and format for demands, discovery documents, and similar filings.


For firms that already have reliable medical record retrieval processes in place, Eve Legal offers substantial workflow automation for everything that happens after records arrive.


## **Eve Legal Limitations: What to Confirm Before Signing**


Alongside its strengths, a few areas are worth confirming directly with Eve during evaluation.


### **Document Upload and Processing**


Older reviews reported lower upload limits, around 200 PDF pages, that required manual document splitting. Eve's current materials describe considerably higher capacity: the company says it can process files containing thousands of pages and automatically splits PDFs of up to 10,000 pages, with exceptionally large matters uploaded in batches. Firms that evaluated Eve some time ago should confirm current limits rather than relying on older reviews.


### **Medical Record Retrieval Scope**


Eve doesn't publicly document a full-service, end-to-end medical record retrieval offering comparable to a specialist retrieval platform's HIE, custodian, provider-network, and traditional outreach model. That said, Eve states that its current agents can assist with requesting records, and its communications agent can follow up on outstanding requests. Firms should confirm the scope of that functionality directly with Eve, since it's less clear whether it's positioned as a complete substitute for a dedicated retrieval workflow.


Where retrieval remains a firm's primary bottleneck, that's a gap worth discussing directly with Eve or addressing through a dedicated retrieval vendor:


-


Record retrieval bottlenecks can delay case progression


-


Incomplete records can contribute to missed diagnoses or treatment gaps


-


Authorization errors can cause provider rejections


-


Staff time spent chasing records can limit case capacity


### **Pricing Transparency**


Eve doesn't publish general platform subscription tiers and directs firms to contact sales. It does disclose roughly $350 per case for a specific medical-malpractice chronology use case on its website, but firms need a customized quote to understand broader platform pricing and how it compares with other legal AI products.


### **Implementation Timeline**


Eve states that most firms are fully operational within 90 days, with timing depending on integrations, templates, workflows, training, and firm size. Users also report that upfront configuration, teaching the system firm preferences, and setting up templates takes meaningful onboarding effort even after go-live.


## **The Medical Record Retrieval Question**


For firms where medical record retrieval is a bottleneck, that's worth weighing separately from Eve's downstream analysis and drafting strengths, since even sophisticated AI analysis can't work with records a firm doesn't have yet.


### **Why Retrieval Matters**


Common retrieval challenges include:


**Authorization errors causing rejections:**


-


Missing patient signatures


-


Unclear expiration dates


-


Missing consent language for sensitive record categories


-


Misspellings in patient or provider names


-


Missing dates of service


Authorization errors can force a request to be corrected and resubmitted, delaying the applicable provider response period. Under federal HIPAA, covered entities generally have 30 calendar days to act on a valid request, subject to a possible extension, while some states impose shorter deadlines in certain circumstances.


**Missing records creating trial risks:**


-


Treatment gaps that opposing counsel may surface at deposition


-


Diagnostic records from facilities that the plaintiff forgot to mention


-


Prior condition documentation affecting causation arguments


-


Billing records needed for accurate damage calculations


**Coordination overhead with multiple vendors:**


-


Managing separate relationships with retrieval and analysis vendors


-


Ensuring data formats work between systems


-


Determining accountability when records arrive late or incomplete


-


Handling separate invoicing from multiple service providers
