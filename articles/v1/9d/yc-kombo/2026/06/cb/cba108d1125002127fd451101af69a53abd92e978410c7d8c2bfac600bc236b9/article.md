# The Best Unified API Platforms for ATS and HRIS Integrations in 2026

## **The integration challenge in HR Tech**


If you’re building in HR tech, you know the pain: your customers use dozens of different HR tools, and they all need to talk to each other. The typical enterprise often uses[more than 80 different HR solutions](https://joshbersin.com/podcast/you-have-153-different-hr-technology-systems-wow-what-to-do/) . But here’s where it gets interesting: according to recent industry data, 81% of companies experience higher customer retention and 68% see improved close rates as a direct result of adding integrations.


However, developing these integrations is no simple task. It typically takes companies 4–5 weeks to develop a single integration, and several months for complex enterprise systems like Workday and SAP SuccessFactors. And this doesn’t include the waiting period for approval and sandbox access.


Unified APIs have emerged as a solution to this integration challenge. Instead of building separate integrations to each system, you build once to the unified API and gain access to dozens of systems. The unified API handles the complexity of normalizing data models, authentication flows, and rate limits, and often provides sandbox access for testing. But not all unified API solutions are built equal — especially when it comes to the specific demands of HR tech.


## **Why HR tech needs specialized unified API platforms**


Generic integration platforms might work fine for syncing marketing tools or CRM data, but HR data comes with its own challenges:


- You’re dealing with sensitive employee and applicant information, putting higher emphasis on compliance and security.
- The data models are complex and come with many edge cases — especially for enterprise systems.
- Many use cases require real-time, bi-directional capabilities at scale.
- It’s a very fragmented market, where each system comes with unique quirks (that are frequently not publicly documented).


## **How to evaluate unified APIs for ATS and HRIS integrations**


When evaluating unified APIs for HRIS and ATS integrations, product and engineering leaders typically consider three factors:


- **Deep workflow capabilities:** Can they solve your specific use case, and have they done it for similar companies before? You don’t want to discover during a customer onboarding that critical workflows aren’t supported, or that you’re their first customer to use and debug an integration. Look for relevant customer references and explicit coverage for each integration in their documentation.
- **Integration coverage:** How many ATS and HRIS systems are currently supported, and how likely is the company to add coverage when you request it? This determines whether you can scale with them and confidently say “yes” to new integration requests from customers. Check their changelog and track record with existing customers.
- **Reactive support:** When something breaks, how responsive and experienced is their support team? Especially when integrating new systems, you need knowledgeable engineers who understand HR workflows. Test the quality of their support during your evaluation by asking specific HR workflow questions and system-specific edge cases.

## **Making your decision**


Picking the right solution for your HRIS and ATS needs can be challenging. Most teams focus on the following criteria during their testing:


- **Validate your use cases:** Test the exact use cases you want to build with live integrations. This often surfaces gaps that companies might not communicate openly — such as unsupported workflows for key systems.
- **Fit for scale:** Assess how each company’s focus aligns with your growth plans and how likely they are to prioritize your future system requests. Switching providers after 1–2 years typically leads to high costs and disrupts product development.
- **Pricing and costs:** Weigh total costs against coverage, support quality, and implementation guidance. When building the business case, teams typically compare expected costs against three key benefits: unlocking new customer segments for upselling and acquisition, reducing ongoing maintenance burden, and freeing engineering resources for core product development.
- **Support quality:** If the support engineers aren’t responsive and knowledgeable during your POC, it likely won’t improve once you’ve signed.
