## Slack-native ITSM vs. Slack-connected ITSM: why this distinction matters


Most ITSM platforms let employees submit a request from Slack. The key difference is where the work happens after that request is created.


**Slack-native ITSM:** Employees request help through a message, DM, or support channel. Agents can manage the ticket in Slack, with approvals, escalations, and updates handled in the same workspace. A separate portal may exist, but it’s not required for day-to-day support. Risotto and Console are examples of Slack-native platforms.


**Slack-connected ITSM:** Employees may be able to create tickets and receive updates in Slack, but agents manage the queue and complete most of the work in a separate ITSM portal. Jira Service Management, Freshservice, and ServiceNow follow this model to varying degrees


The clearest way to tell the difference is to ask one question: *Can an IT agent manage a request from intake through resolution without leaving Slack?* If the answer is yes, the platform is Slack-native. If the agent needs to return to a separate portal to manage or complete the request, it is Slack-connected.

That difference has a direct impact on adoption. A Slack-native experience removes the extra steps and makes the approved support process easier to follow.


## Do you need to replace your existing ITSM to go Slack-native?


No. You can add a Slack-native support experience without replacing your existing ticketing system.


In this hybrid model, employees use Slack to request help, answer follow-up questions, and track the ticket through to resolution. IT teams can manage the request in Slack or continue working in[Jira Service Management](https://www.tryrisotto.com/blog/jira-service-management-slack) , Freshservice, ServiceNow, or another ITSM.


Bi-directional sync keeps both interfaces up to date. Changes to the ticket’s status, assignee, comments, and attachments are reflected in Slack and the ITSM, regardless of where the work happens.


The existing ITSM remains the system of record for reporting, governance, and compliance. This gives teams a Slack-native support experience without losing the processes and records already built into their ITSM.


## Augment or replace? How to decide for your team


Smaller teams with straightforward ticket volume can often run Slack-native end-to-end. Teams with established compliance or change management requirements may be better off keeping their current ITSM and adding a Slack-native support layer on top of it.


Consider these factors before deciding:


- **How much have you already invested in your ITSM?** Replacing an established platform may mean migrating ticket history, rebuilding workflows, retraining the team, and ending existing contracts. If the system still works well behind the scenes, augmentation can deliver a better Slack experience without taking on a full migration.
- **Which processes and records depend on your current ITSM?** Teams that rely on their ITSM for compliance, audit records, reporting, or change management will usually want to preserve it as the system of record. A Slack-native layer can improve how requests are handled without disrupting those controls.
- **What problem are you actually trying to solve?** If employees avoid the portal or IT wants to handle more support inside Slack, replacing the backend may be unnecessary. One 600-employee company wanted a Slack front door while keeping Freshservice as its system of record, so the two platforms ran in parallel with tickets synced both ways.
- **How many teams could use the same support model?** A Slack-native layer can extend the same request experience beyond IT to departments such as HR, Legal, and Finance. This lets each team improve how employees ask for help without forcing every department to migrate to a new system at once.


**The bottom line:** Replacing may make sense when the current ITSM adds little value and migration is straightforward. Augmentation is usually the safer choice when important workflows, records, and controls already depend on the existing platform.
