## **Types of Intent Signals You Should Track**


B2B sales teams that win keep track of five crucial intent signal categories to maximize their deal capture and conversion rates.


### **First-party signals from your own channels**


[First-party intent data](https://persana.ai/blogs/intent-data) flows directly from your **digital properties and serves as your most reliable** and accurate data source. These signals show up as website visits to product pages, webinar attendance, form submissions, email activity, and product usage metrics. Your prospects show real buying interest by repeatedly checking pricing pages or spending extra time with case studies.


### **Second-party signals from review and partner platforms**


[Second-party intent data](https://persana.ai/blogs/what-is-buyer-intent-data) comes from **platforms like G2 and TrustRadius where buyers research solutions** . These "downstream intent" signals track companies that view your business profile, pricing pages, or comparison guides. Many marketers see their ad performance jump up to 30% by using this intent data.


### **Third-party signals from external content and search behavior**


Third-party signals uncover research activities throughout publisher networks and show accounts consuming content about your solution category. You'll find **industry publication visits** , keyword searches, competitor research, and review site activity in this mix. While these signals offer a wider market view, you'll need to filter third-party data to spot genuine buying signals.


### **Technographic and hiring signals**


Technographic data shows **which technologies companies use now, plan to upgrade, or research** . These signals help you spot integration chances and possible competitive wins. Job postings often reveal upcoming projects, tool requirements, and strategic priorities.


### **Behavioral patterns that indicate buying readiness**


Watch for multiple stakeholders from one company that get **involved with your content** , frequent visits in short periods, and deep interest in commercial content. A strong sign of organizational buy-in appears when four or more active users from the same company show up.
