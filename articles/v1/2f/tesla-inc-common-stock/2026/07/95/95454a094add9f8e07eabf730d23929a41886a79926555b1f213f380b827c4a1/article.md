# Tesla’s 400-Stall V4 Supercharger Megahub: The Future of EV Travel Takes Shape in the California Desert

Tesla’s 400-Stall V4 Supercharger Megahub: The Future of EV Travel Takes Shape in the California Desert


Jul 31, 2026


**Introduction**


On a dusty stretch of Interstate 15 in California's Mojave Desert, something extraordinary is taking shape. What currently stands as a modest 22-stall charging stop at the Eddie World travel plaza in Yermo is poised to become the most ambitious electric vehicle charging project ever conceived. Tesla has submitted plans to expand this site into a 400-plus-stall V4 Supercharger megahub — a facility so vast it would more than double the size of the company's current record-holder


.


This is not merely an incremental expansion of Tesla's charging network. This is a paradigm shift. The Yermo project represents a fundamental rethinking of what an EV charging station can and should be — not a utilitarian pit stop but a destination in its own right, complete with restaurants, retail spaces, and amenities designed to transform the charging experience from a necessary chore into a genuinely pleasant part of the journey


.


For Tesla owners in the United States and Europe, this project signals something critically important: the company is doubling down on its commitment to charging infrastructure at a scale that matches its vehicle sales ambitions. With global Supercharger deployments surpassing 80,000 stalls in Q2 2026 and the network delivering 2.0 TWh of energy across 60 million charging sessions, Tesla's charging business has become a powerhouse in its own right


.


But the Yermo megahub is about more than just numbers. It is a statement. It says that Tesla is preparing for a future where EVs are not a niche product but the dominant form of transportation — and that the company intends to lead the infrastructure buildout that will make that future possible.


**II. The Site: Why Yermo, California?**


**2.1 A Strategic Location on America's Busiest EV Corridor**


The choice of Yermo is no accident. The site sits directly along Interstate 15, the primary artery connecting Los Angeles — the second-largest metropolitan area in the United States — with Las Vegas, one of the country's most popular tourist destinations


. This corridor sees millions of vehicles annually, with traffic surging dramatically on weekends, holidays, and during major Las Vegas events.


For Tesla owners, the I-15 corridor has long been a critical charging route. The journey from Los Angeles to Las Vegas covers approximately 270 miles — well within the range of most Tesla models, but close enough to the limit that charging en route is often necessary, particularly for drivers who plan to use their vehicles while in Las Vegas.


**2.2 The Current Infrastructure Crunch**


The existing charging infrastructure along this corridor, while extensive, has struggled to keep pace with growing EV adoption. The 20-mile corridor around Yermo already contains more than 200 high-power charging stalls, including 40 rated at 250 kW and 120 at 325 kW in Barstow, along with another 96 stalls in nearby Baker


. Despite this capacity, peak travel periods still produce long lines and frustrating waits


.


The current Eddie World Supercharger site offers just 22 older V2 and V3 chargers, limited to around 150 kW


. During busy holiday weekends, these chargers can become overwhelmed, with drivers facing significant delays. The problem is not a lack of chargers in the region but a mismatch between capacity and demand at specific times.


**2.3 Eddie World: From "Disneyland of Gas Stations" to EV Megahub**


Eddie World opened in 2018 as a 27,000-square-foot candy factory, ice cream parlor, gas station, and eatery


. Owner Alex Ringle famously described it as the "Disneyland of gas stations," marked by a 65-foot ice cream cone stationed along I-15


. The location was designed as a travel destination, a place where families could stop, stretch their legs, and spend money before continuing their journey.


Tesla's expansion plans will transform Eddie World into something far more ambitious. The new development, known as "Eddie World 2," will integrate the world's largest Supercharger station with an expanded commercial complex designed specifically for EV travelers


.


**III. The Scale: 400+ V4 Stalls in Six Phases**


**3.1 From 22 to 400+: A Transformation of Epic Proportions**


The expansion represents a 1,800% increase in charging capacity at this location. Tesla's current largest Supercharger station, the "Project Oasis" location in Lost Hills, California, has approximately 164 stalls


. The Yermo project would be more than twice that size


.


To put this in perspective, consider that many entire states have fewer than 400 Supercharger stalls. The Yermo megahub alone would rival the total Supercharger count of some mid-sized European countries. This is not just an expansion; it is a statement of intent.


**3.2 The Six-Phase Buildout**


The project will unfold in six distinct phases


:


**Phase 1** is scheduled to begin construction in late 2026 and will deliver 72 brand-new V4 Supercharger stalls


. This initial deployment alone would make the Yermo site larger than all but a handful of Tesla's existing Supercharger stations.


**Phases 2 through 5** will continue adding chargers along Calico Boulevard, creating large parking and charging areas


. Each phase will progressively increase the site's capacity until the total surpasses 400 stalls.


**Phase 6** remains in the design phase, with plans not yet finalized


. This suggests that Tesla is thinking beyond the initial 400-stall target, potentially expanding the site even further if demand warrants it.


3.3 Solar Integration and Sustainability


The plans also show multiple solar canopies atop the charging bays


. This is consistent with Tesla's broader strategy of integrating renewable energy generation with its charging infrastructure. The company's "Project Oasis" in Lost Hills already combines 164 Supercharger stalls with a solar farm and Megapack batteries


. The Yermo megahub could take this integration to an entirely new level.


**IV. The Technology: V4 Superchargers and 500 kW Charging**


**4.1 What Makes V4 Different?**


The Yermo megahub will exclusively feature Tesla's latest V4 Supercharger technology. This represents a significant leap forward from the V3 chargers that currently dominate Tesla's network.


V4 Superchargers operate at 400 to 1,000 volts, delivering faster speeds not just to Tesla vehicles but also to EVs from other manufacturers that support 800-volt platforms, including Hyundai, Kia, Porsche, and others


. The V4 cabinets can support up to eight stalls, double the capacity of the current four stalls per cabinet at existing V4 sites


.


**4.2 500 kW: Doubling the Power**


The most headline-grabbing specification is the 500 kW peak charging power


. This is double the 250 kW maximum of V3 Superchargers


. In practical terms, a 500 kW charger can add approximately 200 miles of range in just 10 to 15 minutes for vehicles capable of accepting that level of power


.


However, there is an important caveat: today's Tesla vehicles — including the Model 3 and Model Y — top out around 250 kW peak charging


. The Cybertruck is among the first Tesla vehicles that can take fuller advantage of V4's higher power output


. This means that for many current Tesla owners, the 500 kW capability is future-proofing — a promise of what their next Tesla will be able to do.


**4.3 1.2 MW for Commercial Vehicles**


Beyond passenger vehicles, the V4 system is designed to support 1.2 MW charging for Tesla's commercial Semi truck platform


. This is not merely a theoretical capability; Tesla has already demonstrated 1.2 MW charging in engineer demonstrations


and has begun deploying Megacharger sites specifically for the Semi


.


The inclusion of pull-through charging bays at the Yermo megahub suggests that Tesla is planning for larger vehicles, including the Cybertruck, vehicles towing trailers, and potentially the Tesla Semi itself


. This is a critical design choice that recognizes the diversity of Tesla's vehicle lineup and the need for charging infrastructure that accommodates all of them.


**4.4 Faster Deployment, Lower Costs**


Tesla has also introduced foldable V4 Supercharger units that can be pre-assembled before transport and unfolded on site


. These units can deliver eight stalls powered by a single 1.2 MW V4 power cabinet. The new approach improves transport efficiency by 33%, cuts installation time in half, and reduces overall costs by 20%


.


This innovation has profound implications for the Yermo project and beyond. Faster, cheaper deployment means Tesla can build larger sites more quickly and cost-effectively. The company is essentially industrializing Supercharger construction, treating it less as a custom engineering project and more as a standardized, scalable manufacturing process.


**V. Beyond Charging: The EV Travel Destination**


**5.1 A Full Travel Stop**


The Yermo megahub is not just about charging cars; it is about creating a complete travel experience. The plans include a 10,100-square-foot Cracker Barrel restaurant, a 4,300-square-foot McDonald's, a convenience store, and space for additional restaurants and drive-throughs


. Outdoor seating areas and retail spaces will allow drivers to stretch their legs, eat, and relax while their vehicles charge


.


This represents a fundamental shift in how Tesla approaches charging infrastructure. The company is moving beyond the model of "chargers in a parking lot" to a vision of "charging destinations" — places where travelers actually want to spend time.


**5.2 The Economics of Destination Charging**


This model makes sense on multiple levels. For Tesla, a more attractive charging experience encourages more EV adoption and more Supercharger usage. For the commercial partners — Cracker Barrel, McDonald's, and others — the steady flow of EV drivers represents a significant business opportunity. For drivers, the ability to eat, shop, and relax during a charging stop transforms what could be a tedious wait into a pleasant break.


5.3 Pull-Through Bays for Trailers and Larger Vehicles


The inclusion of pull-through charging bays is a thoughtful design choice that addresses a significant pain point for EV owners who tow trailers


. Traditional Supercharger stalls are designed for vehicles that can back into a parking space, which is difficult or impossible with a trailer attached. Pull-through bays allow drivers to keep their trailers attached while charging, making long-distance travel with trailers far more practical


.


This feature also accommodates the Cybertruck, which can be significantly longer than other Tesla vehicles, and paves the way for the Tesla Semi.


**VI. The Global Context: Tesla's Charging Network in 2026**


**6.1 80,000 Superchargers and Counting**


Tesla's Supercharger network has grown at an extraordinary pace. In Q2 2026, the company added 2,700 new stalls, bringing the global total past 80,000 — a 17% year-over-year increase


. In China alone, Tesla operates more than 2,600 Supercharger stations with over 13,000 stalls


.


The network delivered 2.0 TWh of energy in Q2 2026, a 30% year-over-year increase, across 60 million charging sessions


. Supercharger real-time availability stands at an industry-leading 99.95%.


**6.2 The End of the V3 Era**


March 2026 marked a significant milestone: Gigafactory New York concluded production of V3 Supercharger cabinets


. V4 is now the standard for all new Supercharger deployments. The Yermo megahub is the most dramatic expression of this transition, but it is part of a broader shift across Tesla's entire network.


**6.3 Opening the Network to Non-Tesla EVs**


The V4 Supercharger design includes longer cables, CCS connectors, and physical payment terminals, making them compatible with Tesla vehicles as well as EVs from other manufacturers


. This is a critical element of Tesla's strategy to position the North American Charging Standard (NACS) as the de facto standard for EV charging in North America.


**6.4 European Expansion**


While the Yermo megahub is a U.S. project, Tesla's European charging network is also expanding rapidly. The company opened 15 Supercharger locations to all EV brands in the UK for the first time


. V4 Superchargers capable of 500 kW have been deployed outside the U.S.


, and foldable Supercharger units are heading to Europe for more rapid deployment


.


For European Tesla owners, the Yermo megahub serves as a preview of what may eventually come to the continent. Tesla is clearly thinking at a global scale, and the lessons learned from the Yermo project — in terms of design, construction techniques, and operational experience — will inform Supercharger deployments worldwide.


**VII. Implications for Tesla Owners**


**7.1 No More Waiting on I-15**


The most immediate benefit for Tesla owners will be the elimination of charging queues on the I-15 corridor. With 400-plus stalls, the Yermo megahub will have more charging capacity than some entire metropolitan areas. Weekend trips to Las Vegas will no longer require strategic planning around charging stops or anxious monitoring of charger availability.


**7.2 Faster Charging for Future Vehicles**


While today's Tesla vehicles may not fully utilize 500 kW charging, future models almost certainly will. The Cybertruck is already capable of higher charging speeds, and future iterations of the Model 3, Model Y, Model S, and Model X will likely follow suit. The Yermo megahub is future-proofed for a decade or more of vehicle development.


**7.3 A Better Charging Experience**


The integration of restaurants, retail, and amenities transforms the charging experience from a necessary inconvenience into something genuinely pleasant. Instead of sitting in a car for 20-30 minutes, drivers can enjoy a meal, shop, or simply relax in outdoor seating areas. This is particularly valuable for families with children, who may appreciate the opportunity to stretch their legs and burn off energy during a long road trip.


**7.4 Implications for Tesla's Business Model**


Tesla's charging network is increasingly becoming a profit center rather than merely a customer-acquisition cost


. As the network grows and opens to non-Tesla vehicles, the revenue potential increases significantly. The Yermo megahub, with its integrated commercial amenities, could generate substantial revenue not just from charging fees but also from lease agreements with restaurant and retail partners.


For Tesla owners, this is good news. A profitable charging network is a sustainable charging network — one that will continue to expand and improve over time rather than being treated as a cost to be minimized.


**VIII. Challenges and Considerations**


**8.1 The Scale of Construction**


Building 400-plus V4 stalls in the California desert is a monumental engineering challenge. The site will require significant electrical infrastructure, including substations and transformers capable of handling megawatts of power. The six-phase buildout suggests that Tesla is approaching this cautiously, learning from each phase before committing to the next.


**8.2 Grid Capacity and Energy Storage**


A 400-stall Supercharger station operating at full capacity could draw tens of megawatts of power — enough to power a small town. Ensuring that the local grid can handle this load will be a significant challenge. Tesla's experience with the "Project Oasis" site in Lost Hills, which combines solar generation with Megapack battery storage, may provide a template


. Integrating solar canopies and battery storage at the Yermo site would reduce grid demand and provide backup power during outages.


**8.3 Competition from Other Charging Networks**


Tesla is not the only company building high-capacity charging infrastructure. Other networks are expanding rapidly, and competition for prime locations and grid connections is intensifying. However, Tesla's first-mover advantage, proprietary technology, and vertically integrated business model give it significant advantages.


**8.4 The Transition to NACS**


As Tesla opens its network to non-Tesla vehicles, managing demand will become more complex. The Yermo megahub's massive capacity will help absorb this demand, but the company will need to carefully manage access and pricing to ensure that Tesla owners continue to have a positive experience.


**Conclusion**


Tesla's plan to build a 400-plus-stall V4 Supercharger megahub in Yermo, California, represents a watershed moment in the evolution of electric vehicle infrastructure. It is not merely the world's largest Supercharger station; it is a new model for what EV charging can and should be.


The project addresses a genuine need — the chronic congestion on the I-15 corridor between Los Angeles and Las Vegas — while simultaneously future-proofing Tesla's charging network for a decade or more of vehicle development. The integration of restaurants, retail, and amenities transforms the charging experience from a chore into a destination. And the use of V4 technology, with its 500 kW and 1.2 MW capabilities, positions Tesla at the forefront of charging innovation.


For Tesla owners in the United States and Europe, the Yermo megahub is a preview of what is coming: a future where charging is fast, convenient, and even enjoyable. A future where range anxiety is a distant memory. A future where the question is not "where can I charge?" but "where would I like to stop?"


Tesla has always been a company that thinks big. The Yermo megahub is proof that the company's ambition extends far beyond the vehicles themselves. Tesla is building the infrastructure for an electric future — and it is building it at a scale that no one else can match.
