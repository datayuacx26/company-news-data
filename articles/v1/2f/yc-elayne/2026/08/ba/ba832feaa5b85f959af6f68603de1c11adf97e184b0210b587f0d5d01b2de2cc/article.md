# NAIC Life Insurance Locator: A Practitioner's Guide (August 2026)

When determining if a decedent had a life insurance policy, the NAIC Life Insurance Policy Locator is one of the best free tools available to help. It sends an alert to participating insurers, who then check their own records for a match. If they find one, they contact you directly. This guide covers how to use the NAIC locator, who qualifies to submit a request, and what information to gather.

## What the NAIC Life Insurance Policy Locator Is


The NAIC Life Insurance Policy Locator is a free, centralized search service run by the National Association of Insurance Commissioners. It lets families[search for life insurance policies](https://www.elayne.com/resources/figure-out-if-someone-had-life-insurance-in-the-first-place) and annuity contracts that may have been purchased by someone who has since died.


When a search request is submitted, participating insurers receive an alert and check their records against the deceased's information. If a match is found, the insurer contacts the potential beneficiary directly.


The service covers policies from insurers who have opted in, which represents a broad share of the market.

## Who Can Use the NAIC Life Insurance Policy Locator


The service is free and open to the public, with no login required.[Beneficiaries](https://www.elayne.com/resources/does-a-will-supersede-a-beneficiary-understanding-which-document-takes-priority) , estate administrators, executors, trustees, and attorneys can all submit requests on behalf of a deceased person's estate.


Two conditions must be met before submitting.


First, the person whose policies you are searching for must be deceased. The NAIC locator is built for post-death searches and will not surface benefits from policies held by someone who is still living.


Second, you must have standing to request the information: you are either a named beneficiary on the policy or someone with legal authority over the estate, such as an executor or court-appointed administrator. Insurers only respond to parties with a legitimate claim or legal right to the information.

## Step-by-Step: How to Submit a Request


The NAIC Life Insurance Policy Locator is free to use and[accessible here](https://content.naic.org/article/naic-life-insurance-policy-locator-helps-consumers-find-lost-life-insurance-benefits) .


Here is what to gather before you begin:


- The full legal name of the deceased, including any name variations or maiden names they may have used over their lifetime, since insurers search their records using exactly the name provided.
- The Social Security number of the deceased, which is the primary identifier insurers use to match records.
- Date of birth and date of death, both of which help narrow results when common names are involved.
- Your own contact information, including a valid email, because all insurer responses are sent to you directly instead of through the NAIC portal.


Once you have gathered that information, the submission process follows these steps:


1. Going to the NAIC Life Insurance Policy Locator and creating a free account using your email. There is no login app; the service runs entirely through a web browser.
2. Completing the online request form with the deceased's identifying details. The form also asks for your relationship to the deceased and your contact information.
3. Submitting the request. NAIC then forwards it to all participating insurers, each of which searches its own records independently.
4. Waiting for insurer responses. Participating companies have 90 days to respond, though many reply sooner. Responses arrive by email or mail directly from each insurer, not from NAIC itself.
5. If an insurer locates a policy or annuity contract in the deceased's name, they will contact you with instructions for filing a claim.


## What Happens After You Submit a Request


After submitting a request through the NAIC Life Insurance Policy Locator, the process moves into a matching phase that can take up to 90 business days.


Participating insurers receive the submission and search their records for any policies or annuity contracts that name the person as an insured or owner. If a match is found, the company contacts the requestor directly, not through NAIC.

## Searching Without a Social Security Number or With Limited Information


The NAIC locator asks for a[Social Security number](https://www.elayne.com/resources/how-to-contact-the-social-security-administration-after-a-death) , but submitting a request without one is still possible. The search form includes a field for partial information, and NAIC reviewers can work with a name, date of birth, and last known state of residence when no SSN is available.


For searches without a Social Security number, the following details strengthen the request:


- Full legal name at the time the policy was issued, including any name changes or maiden names that may appear on older policies.
- Date of birth and, if known, the date of death for a deceased individual, since insurers match records against both.
- Last known state of residence, which helps narrow the search to insurers licensed in that jurisdiction.
- Any partial policy information, such as an insurer name, agent name, or approximate policy year.


Searches by name alone take longer to process and may return fewer matches, since insurers rely on SSN as the primary identifier.

## FAQ


### Can I use the NAIC life insurance policy locator for a living person?


The NAIC life insurance policy locator is built for post-death searches and will not surface active policy benefits for someone who is still alive.


### What's the best free life insurance policy locator for finding a deceased parent's policies?


The NAIC life insurance policy locator is the strongest free starting point: it reaches a broad range of participating insurers at no cost and requires no login app. Running a parallel search through your state's unclaimed property database and submitting a request to MIB Group can help cover policies the NAIC service may miss, since no single locator reaches every insurer.


### How do I search for a life insurance policy by name when I don't have a Social Security number?


You can submit the request through the NAIC life insurance policy locator using the deceased's full legal name, date of birth, date of death, and last known state of residence; the form accepts partial information when no SSN is available. Name-only searches take longer and may return fewer matches because participating insurers use the SSN as their primary identifier.


### NAIC life insurance policy locator vs. MIB life insurance policy locator: which should I use first?


One option is to start with the NAIC locator because it is fully free, reaches a wide range of participating insurers, and returns results within 90 business days. The MIB life insurance policy locator costs $75 per search, but searches member-insurer records going back to 1996 and can help surface policies the NAIC tool may not identify.


### Is the NAIC life insurance policy locator legitimate?


Yes. The NAIC (the National Association of Insurance Commissioners) is the U.S. standard-setting body for state insurance regulation, and the locator is a free, official service it operates on behalf of families searching for unclaimed policies. Insurers who participate are required to search their own records and respond directly to requestors within 90 days, making it one of the most credible free tools available for a post-death life insurance policy search by name or Social Security number.
