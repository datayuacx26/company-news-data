# NJ Inheritance Tax Guide for Families (August 2026)

During estate settlement in New Jersey, certain beneficiaries who inherit money or property may owe state inheritance tax. Whether tax is due depends on the beneficiary's relationship to the person who died. **** This guide explains key points for families and executors to be aware of, including: who owes tax and how much, what assets are covered, exemptions, how to file the return, how waivers work, and more.

## NJ Inheritance Tax vs. NJ Estate Tax


New Jersey repealed its estate tax in 2018. For deaths on or after January 1, 2018, no New Jersey estate tax applies, regardless of the estate's size. The inheritance tax was not repealed and remains fully in effect. The inheritance tax is owed by each individual[beneficiary paying taxes](https://www.elayne.com/resources/does-a-beneficiary-pay-taxes-what-every-inheritor-needs-to-know) , calculated based on their relationship to the person who died and the amount they personally receive.

## NJ Beneficiary Classes and Tax Rates


New Jersey organizes[beneficiaries](https://www.elayne.com/resources/does-a-will-supersede-a-beneficiary-understanding-which-document-takes-priority) into four active classes, and the relationship to the person who died determines whether tax is owed at all, and at what rate.


Class Who Qualifies Exemption Rate


A Spouse, civil union/domestic partners, children (including adopted and stepchildren), grandchildren, parents, grandparents Full exemption 0%


C Siblings, sons-in-law, daughters-in-law First $25,000 exempt 11% to 16%


D All others: nieces, nephews, cousins, friends, unmarried partners not qualifying as Class A None 15% to 16%


E Qualifying charities, religious organizations, educational institutions Full exemption 0%


### Example


A sibling who inherits $75,000 as a Class C beneficiary pays nothing on the first $25,000, then 11% on the remaining $50,000, for a tax bill of $5,500. An unmarried partner who inherits the same $75,000 as a Class D beneficiary owes 15% from dollar one: $11,250.


## What Assets Are Subject to NJ Inheritance Tax


New Jersey's inheritance tax applies to most assets a resident owned at the date of death, valued at fair market value. For non-residents, the tax still reaches real property and certain physical personal property located within the state.


Assets subject to the tax include:


- Real estate located in New Jersey
- Bank and savings accounts
- [Bank and investment accounts](https://www.elayne.com/resources/how-to-find-all-bank-and-investment-accounts-after-someone-dies) , stocks, and bonds
- Vehicles, boats, and other titled property
- Personal property such as jewelry, artwork, and collectibles
- Business interests and partnership shares
- Digital assets, including cryptocurrency, reported at fair market value on the date of death


### How Assets Are Valued


Assets are valued as of the date of death, not the date they are later sold or distributed. For publicly traded stocks and bonds, the value is the market price on that date. For real estate or business interests, there is no single quoted price to look up, so a formal appraisal by a qualified professional is typically needed to establish the correct figure.


## What Is Exempt from NJ Inheritance Tax


Life insurance proceeds paid directly to a named beneficiary fall outside the inheritance tax entirely, regardless of the beneficiary's class. The policy pays the beneficiary, not the estate, so the transfer is not taxable under New Jersey's inheritance tax rules.


Several government retirement benefits are also exempt:


- New Jersey Public Employees Retirement System (PERS) benefits paid to a surviving beneficiary
- New Jersey Teachers' Pension and Annuity Fund (TPAF) survivor payments
- New Jersey Police and Firemen's Retirement System (PFRS) benefits
- Federal Civil Service Retirement benefits
- Annuities paid under certain federal survivor plans


## How to File the NJ Inheritance Tax Return


The executor or administrator of the estate is responsible for filing. Which form applies depends on where the decedent lived.


- Residents file Form IT-R.
- Non-resident decedents who owned New Jersey real property or physical personal property in the state file Form IT-NR.


- **Filing deadline:** The return and any tax owed are due within eight months of the date of death.
- **Extension to file:** Form IT-EXT provides a four-month extension for the paperwork only; the tax payment itself remains due at the original eight-month deadline.
- **Interest on unpaid balances:** 10% annually, accruing after the eight-month deadline.


If all beneficiaries are Class A, a full inheritance tax return is generally not required. Even so, waivers may still be needed to release certain assets, particularly real estate and financial accounts held in the decedent's name alone.

## NJ Inheritance Tax Waivers: How They Work and Why They Matter


Before any bank, brokerage, or financial institution releases estate assets to beneficiaries, it needs a New Jersey inheritance tax waiver. Which form applies depends on the beneficiary class.


Class A beneficiaries can self-certify in most cases using two forms:


- Form L-8, for financial accounts such as bank and brokerage accounts
- Form L-9, for real property held solely by the decedent


These forms do not require prior approval from the Division of Taxation. Class A executors complete them directly and submit them to the relevant institution, which speeds up the process considerably.


Class C and D estates follow a longer path. The executor must file the full inheritance tax return and wait for the Division of Taxation to issue Form 0-1 waivers, one per asset. That process typically takes four to eight weeks after filing, and the estate cannot distribute those assets until each waiver arrives.


Waivers are issued per asset, not per estate. A single estate with multiple bank accounts and a piece of real property needs separate waivers for each one, and tracking which have been requested, received, and submitted to which institution is key.

## FAQs


### Does New Jersey still have an inheritance tax in 2026?


Yes, New Jersey still has an inheritance tax in 2026. The state repealed its estate tax in 2018, but the inheritance tax remains fully in effect.


### Who pays NJ inheritance tax, and which beneficiaries are fully exempt?


The beneficiary pays, not the estate itself. Class A beneficiaries (spouses, civil union and domestic partners, children including adopted and stepchildren, grandchildren, parents, and grandparents) owe nothing. Class C beneficiaries (siblings, sons-in-law, daughters-in-law) pay 11% to 16% after a $25,000 exemption, and Class D beneficiaries (everyone else, including unmarried partners and cousins) pay 15% to 16% from the first dollar received.


### What assets are subject to NJ inheritance tax?


Most assets a New Jersey resident owned at the date of death are subject to the tax. Real estate, bank and brokerage accounts, investment accounts, vehicles, personal property, business interests, and digital assets including cryptocurrency are subject to the tax. Life insurance proceeds paid directly to a named beneficiary are exempt regardless of the beneficiary's class.


### How do I file the NJ inheritance tax return, and when is it due?


The executor files Form IT-R for resident decedents or Form IT-NR for non-residents who owned New Jersey property. Both the return and any tax owed are due within eight months of the date of death. A four-month extension to file is available through Form IT-EXT, but the tax payment itself remains due at the original eight-month deadline; unpaid balances accrue interest at 10% annually after that date.


### What is a NJ inheritance tax waiver, and why does a bank need one?


A New Jersey inheritance tax waiver is a form that releases a specific asset—such as a bank account or real property—so it can be distributed to a beneficiary. Banks, brokerages, and financial institutions will not hand over estate assets without one. Class A beneficiaries can use self-certification forms ([L-8 for financial accounts, L-9 for real property](https://www.nj.gov/treasury/taxation/prntinh.shtml) ) without waiting for state approval. Class C and D beneficiaries must wait for the Division of Taxation to issue Form 0-1 waivers after the full inheritance tax return has been filed, which typically takes four to eight weeks.


### Are IRAs and other retirement accounts subject to NJ inheritance tax?


It depends on the account type. Several New Jersey public employee retirement plans—including PERS, TPAF, and PFRS—and certain federal civil service benefits are fully exempt. Private retirement accounts such as IRAs and 401(k)s are generally included in the taxable estate and may be subject to inheritance tax for Class C and D beneficiaries. Because the rules vary by plan type, confirming the tax status of each account with a tax professional is especially helpful.
