# Life Insurance Policy Search Tools for Attorneys (August 2026)

There's no single database that holds all life insurance policies in the U.S. The NAIC locator, MIB, and state unclaimed property registries each draw from different data sets. This guide covers all three tools, what they search for, timelines, and more.


**Key Takeaways**


- The NAIC locator is free and searches across participating carriers.
- The MIB locator searches application activity from a different data set than the NAIC; running both is standard practice for a thorough diligent search.
- State unclaimed property databases capture benefits that have already escheated.
- Elayne's Verified Asset Search includes life insurance discovery through the NAIC locator as one component of a broader search across 100+ sources, billed as a reimbursable estate expense.


## The Diligent Search Obligation in Estate Administration


Personal representatives have a fiduciary duty to identify all estate assets before distribution. Life insurance is part of that inventory, and a documented search is part of meeting that standard.


Most jurisdictions expect a reasonable, multi-source effort for a[decedent life insurance policy search](https://www.elayne.com/resources/decedent-life-insurance-search-guide) . That often means running the NAIC locator, checking MIB, querying state unclaimed property registries, and reviewing physical records, with each step and result recorded along the way.


For attorneys advising a personal representative, the locator tools covered here can be helpful resources. Running them and recording the results can assist with building a clear record that the search obligation was taken seriously.

## The NAIC Life Insurance Policy Locator


The NAIC Life Insurance Policy Locator is a[free nationwide service](https://content.naic.org/article/learn-how-use-naic-life-insurance-policy-locator) allowing beneficiaries, executors, and authorized representatives to request that participating insurers search their records for policies owned by a decedent.


Key mechanics attorneys should note:


- The search window is 90 business days, during which participating insurers review their records against the submitted request.
- If a policy is found and the requester is the named beneficiary, the insurer contacts them directly instead of routing the result back through the NAIC.
- If no policy is found, or the requester is not the beneficiary, no response will come.
- The NAIC itself holds no policy or beneficiary data; it only routes requests to participating companies.
- Insurer participation is voluntary, so a negative result does not confirm that no policy exists.


## The MIB Policy Locator Service


MIB Group operates an industry-wide database that tracks application activity submitted to its fraud detection service, searching a fundamentally different data set than the NAIC locator. Where the NAIC routes requests to participating insurers, MIB works from the other direction: it checks whether an insurer submitted an application query for the decedent, then reports which company did and when. The report returns the company name, the application submission date, and contact information for follow-up.


A few points to note:


- MIB returns application activity, not confirmation of an in-force policy. Direct follow-up with the identified carrier is required to confirm whether a policy was issued and remains active.
- Beneficiary information is not included in the report.
- Coverage is limited to individually underwritten life insurance policies.


Because the MIB locator and the NAIC locator draw from different sources, running both is standard practice in a thorough diligent search.


## State-Level Locators and Unclaimed Property Searches


[Unclaimed property and inheritance searches](https://www.elayne.com/resources/unclaimed-property-and-inheritance-search-guide) capture policies where the insurer already tried to pay but could not locate the beneficiary. When that search period expires, insurers transfer the death benefit to the state as unclaimed property under escheatment laws.


Every state maintains an unclaimed property database. MissingMoney.com, sponsored by the National Association of Unclaimed Property Administrators, indexes many of those databases in a single search, though not every state participates.


Some states also operate separate insurance department locator programs. New York, for example, offers a[Lost Policy Finder](https://www.dfs.ny.gov/consumers/life_insurance/lost_policy_finder) through the Department of Financial Services, a free service for locating unclaimed benefits on life insurance policies, certificates, and annuity contracts insuring or owned by a deceased immediate family member.


For matters involving a decedent who lived in multiple states over their lifetime, each state of prior residence is worth searching.


## Manual Search Methods That Support Locator Searches


Sources worth reviewing:


- Personal papers, contact books, and correspondence for names of insurance agents, estate planners, or financial advisors who may have sold or managed coverage
- Safe deposit boxes and home files for policy contracts, premium notices, annual statements, or insurer letters
- Bank and credit card statements for recurring premium payments or billing descriptors tied to an insurer
- Federal and state tax returns, which may surface 1099 forms, policy loan interest, or deductible premium payments
- Mail received after death, including premium notices, anniversary letters, and lapse warnings that can arrive months later

## What Happens After a Policy Is Located


Once a locator search returns a result,[life insurance claims after death](https://www.elayne.com/resources/life-insurance-claims-after-death) begin with documentation. The insurer contacts the requester or named beneficiary directly, but contact alone does not start the clock on payment. The personal representative or beneficiary must[file a life insurance claim](https://www.elayne.com/resources/how-to-claim-someones-life-insurance-benefits) with a formal claim package, typically including certified copies of the death certificate, the claim form provided by the insurer, and identity verification. Where the estate is the claimant, Letters Testamentary are generally required before the insurer will release information or process payment.


Life insurance proceeds pass outside probate when a living, named beneficiary exists. Several scenarios route benefits back through the estate, though. If the primary beneficiary predeceased the decedent and no contingent beneficiary was designated, the death benefit passes to the estate and becomes subject to probate, where creditors may reach those proceeds before distribution to heirs. The same outcome can follow when the named beneficiary is the estate itself.


Contested beneficiary designations are a separate category. Where a surviving family member disputes whether a designation was made under undue influence, or where competing designations exist across multiple policy versions, court involvement may be necessary before the insurer will pay.

## FAQ


### Does a negative result from the NAIC life insurance policy locator mean no policy exists?


No. The NAIC life insurance policy locator routes requests to participating insurers, but insurer participation is voluntary. A negative result only confirms that no participating carrier found a match, not that no policy exists.


### NAIC life insurance policy locator vs MIB policy locator: which should I run for a probate matter?


You can run both, at the same time. The NAIC locator contacts participating insurers directly and works from their active policy records; the MIB policy locator searches application activity submitted to its fraud detection database and identifies which carrier received an application for the decedent, then requires direct follow-up to confirm whether a policy was issued. Because they draw from entirely different data sets, neither one should be considered a substitute for the other.


### How do I find a life insurance policy for a deceased parent when I have no paperwork and no insurer name?


A free life insurance policy locator submission through the NAIC requires only basic decedent information and reaches a wide range of participating carriers at no cost. At the same time, bank and credit card statements can be reviewed for recurring premium payments, tax returns may surface 1099 forms or policy loan interest, and each state where the parent lived has an unclaimed property database worth searching.


### What happens to a life insurance death benefit if the named beneficiary died before the policyholder?


When the primary beneficiary predeceased the decedent and no contingent beneficiary was designated, the death benefit passes to the estate and becomes subject to probate, where creditors may reach those proceeds before distribution to heirs. The same outcome follows when the estate itself is named as beneficiary.


### Can a life insurance policy search be done by name without a Social Security number?


Yes. The NAIC life insurance policy locator accepts submissions without a Social Security number, though including one improves matching accuracy. State unclaimed property databases also support life insurance policy searches by name, and MIB's locator works from application activity records that can be identified through name and date-of-birth information alone.
