# Searching for a Decedent's Life Insurance Policies (July 2026)

Billions of dollars in[life insurance benefits](https://www.elayne.com/resources/life-insurance-claims-after-death) go unclaimed every year because beneficiaries never knew a policy existed. Insurance companies generally do not contact beneficiaries after someone dies. If no one files a claim, a paid-up policy can remain unclaimed indefinitely.


Fortunately, there are several free ways to search for a deceased person's life insurance policies. The NAIC Life Insurance Policy Locator, the MIB Policy Locator, and state unclaimed property databases cover the most common places to look. In most cases, you'll only need the person's name, Social Security number, and date of death to begin your search.


This guide explains how each search tool works, what it can and cannot find, and how to use it effectively.


**Key Takeaways**


- Life insurance proceeds go unclaimed when beneficiaries don't know a policy exists; a paid-up policy can sit dormant for years.
- The NAIC Life Insurance Policy Locator is free, requires no login, and covers participating insurers in one request with a 90-day response window.
- The MIB Policy Locator searches application records back to 1996.
- State unclaimed property databases are free to search by name; MissingMoney.com covers dozens of states in a single search.
- Elayne's Verified Asset Search pulls the NAIC locator, state unclaimed property registries, and financial record analysis together in one search.


## What to Gather Before You Search


The death certificate covers most of what locator services ask for. What you will typically need:


- The decedent's full legal name, including any maiden name or prior surname
- Social Security number
- Date of birth and date of death
- Prior states of residence

## The NAIC Life Insurance Policy Locator


The NAIC Life Insurance Policy Locator is a free service run by the National Association of Insurance Commissioners that helps families search for life insurance policies and annuity contracts a deceased person may have held.


To use it, families submit a request through[the NAIC Life Insurance Policy Locator](https://content.naic.org/article/learn-how-use-naic-life-insurance-policy-locator) with the decedent's name, Social Security number, and date of birth. Participating insurers then have 90 days to search their records and contact the requestor directly if a match is found.


A few things worth knowing before submitting:


- The locator only searches participating insurers, so a policy held with a non-participating company will not appear in results.
- Results are not returned through the NAIC portal itself. If a match is found, the insurer contacts the requestor directly.
- There is no login required and no fee to submit a request.
- One request covers all participating insurers, so families do not need to submit separately for each company.


## The MIB Policy Locator Service


The MIB (Medical Information Bureau) Policy Locator Service is a separate search option for families. MIB is a member-owned cooperative used by life insurance companies to share applicant data, and its policy locator draws on records submitted by participating insurers going back decades.


To use the MIB locator, families submit a request online and pay a small fee. MIB searches its database for any life insurance or individual annuity applications the deceased may have submitted to member companies, then reports back which insurers, if any, have records on file.


Key points to know:


- MIB only holds application data, not policy confirmation. A match means the person applied with a given insurer; it does not confirm the policy was issued or is still active.
- Coverage extends only to applications submitted to MIB member companies, so policies from non-member insurers will not appear.
- Records go back to 1996 for most searches, meaning older policies may fall outside the searchable window.


The MIB search works best as a complement to the NAIC locator, not a replacement for it. Used together, the two tools cover a broader range of potential policies than either one does alone.


## State Unclaimed Property Databases


Each U.S. state runs an unclaimed property database where life insurance proceeds that were never claimed get turned over after a dormancy period, typically three to five years. These databases are free to search and often require only a name to get started.


The best starting point is MissingMoney.com, a multi-state search portal endorsed by[the National Association of Unclaimed Property Administrators](https://unclaimed.org/who-we-are/) . A single search there can surface results across dozens of participating states simultaneously.


For states not covered by MissingMoney.com, families can search the individual state controller or treasurer website directly.


## Searching Personal Records and Contacting Key Sources Directly


[Tax returns](https://www.elayne.com/resources/taxes-after-someone-dies-who-gets-the-tax-return-of-a-deceased-person) from the past two to three years frequently list insurance premium payments or dividend income tied to a policy. Bank statements may show recurring premium withdrawals. Also, mail and email archives sometimes contain annual statements, renewal notices, or correspondence from insurers.


### Who to Contact Directly


- Employers and former employers, since group life insurance through a workplace is common.
- The decedent's financial advisor, estate attorney, or accountant, each of whom may have helped structure or document coverage.
- [Bank and investment accounts](https://www.elayne.com/resources/how-to-find-all-bank-and-investment-accounts-after-someone-dies) should be reviewed, as some institutions offer life insurance products to their members.
- The state's unclaimed property office, which may hold funds from policies that insurers weren't able to pay out after a policyholder passed away.

## State Insurance Department Resources


Every state insurance department maintains a consumer services division that can help families search for life insurance policies a loved one may have held. These offices are free to contact and can guide families through the search process, including how to access state-specific unclaimed property databases where unpaid policy proceeds may be held.


### Finding Your State's Insurance Department


The National Association of Insurance Commissioners (NAIC) maintains a directory of all state insurance department contacts. Families can search by state to find the correct office, phone number, and online resources available to them.


Most state insurance departments offer:


- A dedicated consumer helpline staffed by representatives who can explain the policy search process and point families toward helpful resources
- Referrals to the state's unclaimed property office, where insurers are required to remit unpaid death benefits after a set dormancy period
- Guidance on filing a formal complaint if an insurer fails to respond to a policy inquiry


### State Unclaimed Property Offices


When a life insurance policy goes unclaimed, insurers are generally required by state law to transfer the proceeds to the state's unclaimed property fund. Families can search these funds directly through each state's official unclaimed property website. The National Association of Unclaimed Property Administrators (NAUPA) provides a central directory at MissingMoney.com.

## FAQ


### How do you search for an unclaimed life insurance policy when you don't have the Social Security number?


Families can start with state unclaimed property databases. Most accept a name-only search, making them accessible even when documentation is limited. MissingMoney.com lets you search across dozens of states simultaneously without a Social Security number.


### What's the fastest way to find life insurance policies of a deceased parent using free tools?


Submitting a request to the NAIC life insurance policy locator. It's free, covers all participating insurers in a single submission, and requires only the decedent's name, Social Security number, and date of birth. Other options include running a parallel search on MissingMoney.com for unclaimed property across multiple states. And, checking the decedent's tax returns and bank statements for premium payments or dividend income that point to policies the locator services may not have surfaced.


### How does the NAIC life insurance policy locator work?


Families submit a request through the NAIC website, and participating insurers have 90 days to search their records and contact the requestor directly if a match is found. Results do not come back through the NAIC portal itself, and there is no login required and no fee to submit.


### NAIC life insurance policy locator vs. MIB policy locator: which one should I use?


Families can use both. The NAIC locator searches active policy records across participating insurers, while the MIB service searches application data going back to 1996 for member companies. A match through MIB confirms only that the deceased applied with a given insurer, not that a policy was issued or remains active, so MIB results require follow-up directly with the insurer. Together, the two tools cover a broader range of potential policies than either one does on its own.


### Does the NAIC locator cover annuity contracts?


Yes. The NAIC locator searches for both life insurance policies and individual annuity contracts held by participating insurers.
