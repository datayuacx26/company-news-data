# Florida vs. New York Ancillary Probate: A July 2026 Guide

Florida and New York are two of the most common destinations for ancillary probate filings. Both states have a high percentage of nonresident property ownership: vacation homes, investment condominiums, rental properties, and retirement purchases held by people who live somewhere else. When those owners pass away, their home state probate proceeding cannot transfer title to out-of-state real estate. Each state where property is located has exclusive jurisdiction over land within its borders, which means a separate court proceeding opens in that state.


For each state, this guide will cover: when ancillary probate is required, which procedural path applies, what the filing package needs to contain, who can serve, and how long the process lasts.


**Key Takeaways**


- **Florida:** Ancillary probate is required whenever a nonresident died owning real property there, regardless of estate size.
- **Florida:** Summary administration can close in two to four weeks; formal administration generally lasts around six months, paced by the mandatory creditor notice period.
- **Florida:** The 90-day creditor window runs independently from the home state's probate timeline, even if that proceeding has already closed.
- **New York:** Ancillary probate in New York runs through the Surrogate's Court in the county where the decedent's New York property is located.
- **New York:** There is no summary administration equivalent; formal administration is generally required regardless of estate size.
- **New York:** A bond is typically required, and timelines range from several months to over a year.


## What Ancillary Probate Is


Ancillary probate is a secondary[probate](https://www.elayne.com/resources/probate-explained-what-happens-after-someone-dies-and-how-to-plan-ahead) proceeding opened in a state where a decedent owned real property but was not a legal resident. When someone dies domiciled in one state but holds title to real estate in another, that out-of-state property cannot pass to heirs through the primary estate proceeding alone. The state where the property sits has its own jurisdiction over land within its borders, so a separate court process is required there.


Florida and New York are among the most common destinations for ancillary proceedings because both states attract substantial nonresident property ownership through vacation homes, investment real estate, and retirement purchases.

## When Ancillary Probate Is Required in Florida


In Florida, ancillary probate is required whenever a nonresident decedent owned real property or physical personal property located in Florida at the time of death. The domiciliary proceeding in the decedent's home state has no authority over Florida-sited assets, so a separate Florida proceeding is the only way to legally transfer title or distribute those assets to[beneficiaries](https://www.elayne.com/resources/does-a-will-supersede-a-beneficiary-understanding-which-document-takes-priority) .


Two conditions lead to the requirement: the decedent was not a Florida resident, and the estate includes property with a Florida situs. Personal property generally follows the domicile, but real estate follows the law of the state where it sits.


### What Counts as Florida-Sited Property


Not every asset a nonresident owned with[Florida](https://www.elayne.com/resources/how-long-does-probate-take-in-florida) connections requires ancillary probate. The distinction turns on whether the asset has a legal situs in Florida.


- Real property located in Florida, including vacation homes, condominiums, undeveloped land, and timeshare interests held as tenancy in common, requires ancillary administration regardless of estate size.
- Physical personal property present in Florida at death, such as vehicles titled in Florida or boats registered here, falls under the ancillary requirement.
- Intangible personal property, including bank accounts, brokerage accounts, and life insurance proceeds, generally does not require Florida ancillary probate because intangibles follow the domicile of the owner, not the state where the institution is located.
- Assets held in a revocable living trust, jointly with right of survivorship, or with a named beneficiary designation pass outside probate entirely.


## The Florida Ancillary Probate Process: Filing Requirements and Key Steps


Formal ancillary administration in Florida opens in the circuit court of the county where the decedent's Florida property is located.


The required filing package typically includes:


- Two original[certified death certificates](https://www.usa.gov/death-certificate)
- An authenticated copy of the domiciliary will, or certified proof of the domiciliary intestate proceeding if the decedent died without a will
- Certified copies of the foreign letters or the domiciliary court order confirming the primary personal representative's authority
- A petition for ancillary administration and a petition to appoint the ancillary personal representative
- The personal representative's oath and, for nonresidents, a designation of a Florida-resident agent for service of process under Florida Probate Rule 5.110


Once the court accepts the filing and the personal representative qualifies, the clerk issues ancillary letters of administration, which serve as the legal authority to act on Florida-sited assets.


## Personal Representative Qualification and Selection Under Florida Law


### Who Qualifies Under Florida Statute 733.303


Florida Statutes section 733.303 bars certain individuals from serving as personal representative, including anyone who has been convicted of a felony, is under 18, or has been adjudicated incapacitated. Non-residents face an additional restriction: under Florida Statute 733.304, a non-resident can only serve if they are a legally adopted child, adoptive parent, spouse, sibling, parent, child, or other lineal descendant of the decedent.


### Priority of Appointment


Florida probate rules follow a statutory order of preference for appointment:


- The person nominated in the decedent's will takes priority, provided they meet Florida's qualification requirements.
- If no will exists, or the nominated person cannot serve, a surviving spouse has first priority among heirs.
- Adult children and other heirs follow in descending order under Florida's intestate succession framework.


For ancillary estates, out-of-state family members who do not fall within the qualifying relationship categories must identify a Florida-resident co-personal representative or seek appointment of a professional Florida fiduciary.


## Creditor Notice and Claims in Florida Ancillary Administration


Florida's ancillary probate rules require the personal representative to notify creditors and allow them to file claims against the estate. Under Florida Statute 733.2121, notice to creditors must be published in a local newspaper once a week for two consecutive weeks. Known creditors must also receive direct written notice. Creditors generally have 90 days from the date of first publication, or 30 days from the date of direct notice, to file a claim, whichever occurs later. Creditors who miss that window may lose the right to collect from the estate, though the exact consequences depend on the circumstances and applicable law.


### How This Applies to Out-of-State Estates


For estates being administered in another state with Florida property as a secondary matter, the ancillary personal representative carries the same notice obligations as a domiciliary representative would. The 90-day creditor claim period runs independently of any timeline in the home state.


## Ancillary Probate in New York: Surrogate's Court Procedure and Governing Law


Ancillary probate in New York runs through the Surrogate's Court in the county where the decedent's New York property sits. The governing statute is Article 16 of the Surrogate's Court Procedure Act (SCPA), which sets out how a foreign[executor](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/information-for-executors) or administrator obtains authority to act on New York assets.


### Who Can File and What They Need


A foreign personal representative generally must petition for ancillary letters in New York before managing any in-state asset. The petition requires:


- A certified copy of the domiciliary will and the letters testamentary or letters of administration issued by the home state court, authenticated under SCPA Article 16.
- Proof that the domiciliary proceeding is still open or has been formally closed, depending on the county's local rules.
- A New York-based registered agent or designation of the Surrogate's Court clerk for service of process, if the foreign representative has no New York location on file.

## Florida vs. New York Ancillary Probate: Key Procedural Differences


### Jurisdiction and Filing Venue


In Florida, ancillary proceedings are filed in the county where the decedent's Florida property is located. New York requires filing in the Surrogate's Court of the county where the New York property sits. Both states treat the domiciliary state's probate as the primary proceeding, with ancillary administration operating as subordinate.


### Summary Administration vs. Full Formal Proceedings


Florida offers summary administration as an expedited alternative when the estate's non-exempt assets are valued under $75,000 or the decedent passed away more than two years ago. New York has no direct equivalent, generally requiring formal administration regardless of estate size.

## FAQ


### What leads to ancillary probate in Florida for a nonresident's estate?


Florida ancillary probate is required when a nonresident decedent owned real property or physical personal property located in Florida at the time of death. Intangible assets like bank accounts and brokerage accounts generally do not lead to the requirement, because intangibles follow the owner's domicile, not the state where the institution sits. Assets held in a revocable living trust, jointly with right of survivorship, or with a named beneficiary designation pass outside probate entirely and avoid the ancillary requirement.


### Florida summary administration vs. formal ancillary administration: which applies to an out-of-state estate?


Summary administration applies when Florida property is valued at $75,000 or less in non-exempt assets, or when the decedent passed away more than two years ago: no personal representative is appointed, and the proceeding can close in as little as two to four weeks in smaller circuits. Formal administration is required when Florida assets exceed $75,000 and the decedent passed away within the last two years. There is a mandatory creditor notice period, and the entire process typically lasts six months to a year.


### Can a non-resident family member serve as personal representative in Florida ancillary probate?


A non-resident can serve only if they fall within a specific category under Florida Statute 733.304: a legally adopted child, adoptive parent, spouse, sibling, parent, child, or other lineal descendant of the decedent. Out-of-state family members who do not meet these relationship requirements must identify a Florida-resident co-personal representative or seek appointment of a professional Florida fiduciary.


### When is ancillary probate required in New York for a nonresident's estate?


New York ancillary probate is required when a nonresident decedent owned real property or other assets with a New York situs at the time of death. The foreign personal representative must petition the Surrogate's Court in the county where the New York property is located before taking any action on those assets.


### Which court handles ancillary probate in New York?


Ancillary proceedings in New York are filed in the Surrogate's Court of the county where the decedent's New York property sits, not the county where the decedent lived or died. Each Surrogate's Court sets its own scheduling calendar and local filing requirements.


### Does New York have a summary administration option for small ancillary estates?


New York does not have a summary administration procedure equivalent to Florida's. Formal administration through the Surrogate's Court is generally required regardless of the value of the New York assets. There is no expedited threshold that allows a nonresident's estate to bypass the standard ancillary petition process based on asset size alone.
