# How to find new immunology indications with AI and multiomics data

#### A blockbuster therapy’s patent expiration is often seen as the end of its commercial journey, but it doesn’t have to be.


The trick lies in finding new opportunities, which requires a comprehensive understanding of the interactions between drugs, pathways, drugs and biological mechanisms.


Discovery scientists are starting to combine curated biological knowledge, multiomics data and AI-supported interpretation to build a more complete understanding of human biology and support indication expansion.

### In action: New opportunities for ustekinumab (Stelara®)


Ustekinumab, a monoclonal antibody targeting the IL-12/IL-23 signaling pathway from Janssen, has been approved for plaque psoriasis, psoriatic arthritis, Crohn’s disease and ulcerative colitis. It offers a practical example of how integrated biological knowledge can uncover new therapeutic opportunities.


Although the therapy effectively suppresses key inflammatory pathways in psoriasis and other immune-mediated diseases, not all patients achieve a sustained response. Initial non-response, loss of response over time and persistent disease activity suggest that not all inflammatory mechanisms were affected by the treatment.


### Mapping persistent disease mechanisms


Here's one approach for using public datasets1,2 to identify treatment-responsive pathways and mechanisms that remain active despite therapy (Table 1).


#### 01


##### Which biological pathways respond to ustekinumab treatment?


**


Canonical pathway analysis integrates transcriptomic data with curated biological knowledge to predict pathways that are activated or inhibited by therapy.


#### 02


##### Which disease mechanisms are active even with treatment?


**


Comparative analysis reveals pathways that persist after IL-12/IL-23 inhibition, highlighting potential mechanisms of treatment resistance.


#### 03


##### What regulates these persistent pathways?


**


Upstream regulator analysis identifies IL1A, IL1B, IL1R, IL4 and IL17A as potential drivers of residual disease activity and novel therapeutic targets.


#### 04


##### Which candidates are the most promising?


**


Integrated evidence helps prioritize biomarkers, identify existing drugs targeting these pathways and select appropriate models for validation.


While ustekinumab effectively inhibited many inflammatory pathways, several disease-associated pathways remained active, providing a starting point for identifying new therapeutic targets and combination strategies that could improve patient response.


After identifying persistent inflammatory pathways and shared upstream regulators, researchers can validate their findings with the curated data in DKB.
