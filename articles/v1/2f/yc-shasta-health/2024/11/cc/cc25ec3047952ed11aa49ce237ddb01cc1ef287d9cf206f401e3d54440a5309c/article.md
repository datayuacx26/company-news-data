## Understanding Frozen Shoulder: Causes and Symptoms


### Understanding Frozen Shoulder


Frozen shoulder, or *adhesive capsulitis* , is a condition characterized by stiffness and pain in the shoulder joint. Typically, this condition finds its root in inflammation of the shoulder capsule, leading to gradual loss of mobility. Common causes include injury, prolonged immobilization, or underlying conditions like diabetes or thyroid disorders. Symptoms often start subtly and can escalate to debilitating pain, particularly at night. The most affected demographics are women aged 40 to 60, making it vital to recognize early signs, as timely treatment can significantly influence recovery. Familiarity with[frozen shoulder symptoms](https://www.shasta.health/conditions/frozen-shoulder) is essential for timely intervention.

## Stages of Frozen Shoulder: Physical Therapy Interventions


### The Freezing Stage


The journey of recovery from frozen shoulder begins with the freezing stage. During this time, individuals experience increasing pain and stiffness, which can significantly impair mobility. Typically lasting 6 to 12 weeks, it is essential to initiate **gentle stretching exercises** early on. These exercises promote blood flow without aggravating the shoulder. For example:


- **Pendulum swings:** Lean forward and let the affected arm hang. Gently swing it back and forth.
- **Crossover arm stretch:** Use the unaffected arm to pull the affected arm across the chest.


Practicing these stretches for **15-20 minutes daily** can help maintain some range of motion. Expert insight suggests remaining cautious—aim for a *gentle* stretch without pushing through pain, which can worsen the condition.


### The Frozen Stage


Next comes the frozen stage, which can last anywhere from 4 to 6 months. At this point, pain may start to diminish, but stiffness becomes more pronounced. Focus shifts to maintaining functionality. Introducing[manual therapy](https://www.shasta.health/therapists/christina-catalano) at this stage can be beneficial.


Techniques like **joint mobilizations** aim to restore mobility by applying gentle pressure at the shoulder joint. Also, incorporate resistance exercises, such as:


- **Isometric shoulder exercises:** Push your arm against a wall without moving.
- **External rotation:** Use a resistance band to strengthen surrounding muscles.


These exercises can be performed **2 to 3 times a week** , gradually increasing intensity based on comfort level.


### The Thawing Stage


In the thawing stage, recovery may span from 6 months to 2 years. Patients generally start to regain lost range of motion, and the primary goal shifts to strengthening and functional recovery. Incorporating[strengthening exercises](https://www.shasta.health/conditions/frozen-shoulder) becomes critical.


Examples include:


- **Wall slides:** Stand with your back against a wall; slide your arms up to a “Y” position.
- **Scapular retractions:** Squeeze your shoulder blades together while holding a light weight.


As you progress, an expert may advise increasing the intensity and frequency, suggesting workouts up to **5 times a week** . Monitoring personal comfort levels is essential to avoid any setbacks.

## Frequently Asked Questions


### What is frozen shoulder?


Frozen shoulder, also known as adhesive capsulitis, is a condition characterized by pain and stiffness in the shoulder joint, leading to limited mobility.


### What causes frozen shoulder?


Common causes include shoulder injury, prolonged immobilization, and certain health issues like diabetes or thyroid disorders.


### What are the symptoms of frozen shoulder?


Symptoms often start with a gradual onset of pain and stiffness in one shoulder, which may worsen over time, particularly during nighttime. Tasks that require arm movement may become difficult.


### How does physical therapy help?


Physical therapy helps reduce pain and increase mobility through targeted exercises, manual therapy, and education on proper body mechanics, tailored to each individual's needs.


### What are the stages of frozen shoulder?


Frozen shoulder progresses through three stages: freezing (increasing pain and stiffness), frozen (more pronounced stiffness with reduced pain), and thawing (gradual recovery of mobility).


### Is virtual physical therapy effective?


Yes, virtual physical therapy can be effective for frozen shoulder, offering personalized care in a comfortable setting and allowing patients to engage with their therapists from home.


### What should I do if I think I have frozen shoulder?


If you suspect you have frozen shoulder, it’s important to consult a healthcare provider. Early diagnosis and intervention can improve recovery outcomes.


### Can frozen shoulder return after treatment?


Yes, recurrence is possible. Ongoing care, including exercises and proper body mechanics during daily activities, can help prevent the return of symptoms.
