# The European Community of Alsace Equips Strasbourg City Headquarters With a Solar Power Plant to Boost Its Energy Self-Sufficiency

- **Installed on the roofs of the Hôtel d’Alsace, this new photovoltaic power plant marks a major milestone in the CeA’s long-term strategy for clean energy and cost control.**


- **The solar power plant achieves a self-consumption rate of 100%, covering just under 10% of the site’s energy needs, representing an annual saving of~ 24 000 € on its electricity bill in 2025.**


- **As with most sites already fitted with photovoltaic systems by the local authority, SolarEdge’s innovative solar technology with Power Optimizers was chosen to overcome the limitations of traditional string solar systems, with the aim of optimising yield and improving safety.**


**Strasbourg, France – 06 July 2026** – The[European Community of Alsace (CeA)](https://www.alsace.eu/) announces the commissioning of a self-consumption solar power plant on the roofs of the Hôtel d’Alsace, its headquarters located in the heart of Strasbourg. This new installation forms part of a long-standing strategy to promote the energy transition and control the operating costs of its property portfolio.


Against a backdrop of changes to the S21 regulations and high volatility in electricity prices, the CeA sought to secure its energy expenditure in the long term whilst strengthening its energy independence. To this end, the local authority chose to invest heavily in solar energy. Since 2021, it has installed solar panels at 50 sites (including 46 secondary schools), totaling 5.3 MWp and has already seen positive results in terms of solar self-consumption. Indeed, the equipped sites self-consume all or part of the photovoltaic output, and the surplus is used to supply other sites within the local authority, via a short circuit known as ‘heritage self-consumption’, which is currently being trialed with Energies Strasbourg.
Encouraged by these positive results, CeA councillors have decided to continue rolling out photovoltaic installations, and the fitting of at solar panels the Hôtel d'Alsace enables this commitment to be realised at one of the local authority’s main sites.


**Estelle Le Roy, Head of the Renewable Energy Department at the CeA, emphasises** : “The Hôtel d’Alsace is a public-access building of significant heritage value, which means there are high standards regarding electrical safety and risk management. It was essential to ensure the protection of both the occupants and the building itself. We have, in fact, adopted the same strategy in secondary schools developed at the outset of the photovoltaic scheme in collaboration with the fire and rescue services. This is why we chose to utilize the innovative technology of Power Optimizers. Installed beneath each pair of panels, these devices enhance safety by ensuring a very low residual voltage at the panel output in the event of a power cut. Furthermore, they allow us to monitor the performance of each panel, which helps us to optimise the operation of our power stations.”


**A bespoke installation for a particularly complex project**
Located in the city centre and featuring unique architecture with a curved roof, the Hôtel d’Alsace presented a high level of complexity in harmoniously integrating a solar installation. expertise Technical and effective communication between the client, project manager, installer, Architectes des Bâtiments de France and technical supervisorenabled to the project’s feasibility be confirmed and the most suitable solution to be identified. The building’s heritage value required a genuine aesthetic approach in addition to the standard requirements location and applicable to all the local authority’s. photovoltaic projects


The curvature of the roof creates few areas shaded, but these are nevertheless taken into account to optimise production. Shading significantly reduces the efficiency of conventional solar systems. Indeed, with traditional string inverters, when a single panel is shaded and its performance is reduced, all the panels connected to the same string are limited to the performance of the weakest panel, leading to a significant drop in overall production.


This is why the European Community of Alsace has opted for SolarEdge’s innovative DC optimisation solution, which relies on the use of Power Optimizers installed beneath each pair of photovoltaic panels. This approach allows each pair of panels to be ‘individualized’, so that the performance of modules adequately exposed to the sun is not affected by those in the shade, thereby maximising energy production and ensuring the project’s economic viability.
Over the course of a year, the solar power plant covers just under 10% of the site’s total consumption, representing an annual saving of ~ €24,000 in 2025.


**Maximum safety for an exceptional public access building**
As a public access building (ERP), the structure is subject to particularly stringent safety requirements. In this regard, the solar system is equipped with SolarEdge’s SafeDC™ function, designed to automatically reduce the voltage of the photovoltaic array to 1 V (a voltage safe to touch) as the inverter or the grid is switched off. This feature is essential for ensuring the safety of occupants, maintenance operations and emergency response teams. This safety requirement is now further reinforced by the CNPP Certified accreditation obtained by SolarEdge for its electric arc detection solution for photovoltaic installations*: an accreditation recognised by the APSAD D20 standard for fire risk protection.


* The certification covers SolarEdge three-phase commercial inverters and three-phase inverters with Synergy Technology (SE20k to SE120K), as well as the S Series commercial power optimizers (S1000, S1200 and S1400).

**A flagship project that positions the CeA at the forefront of a sustainable energy transition serving the region**


With this flagship project, the European Community of Alsace confirms its role as a key public driver in the regional energy transition, combining technological innovation, respect for heritage and sound public finance management.


**Martin Wolf, co-founder and partner at Enerios Associés** , explains: “This approach offered greater flexibility for the client and for us, the installer. Given the building’s complex multi-facade design, this installation, equipped with Power Optimizers, enabled a significant reduction in the volume of solar cabling compared to a traditional string-based installation. Clearly, this had an impact on the project budget. The SolarEdge solution we chose also enabled very rapid commissioning, thanks in particular to its ultra-simplified SetApp (configuration) application. It really is straight forward! Finally, thanks to the dedicated app, the highly precise monitoring that allows faults to be analysed in advance helps reduce operational costs for corrective maintenance. More specifically, monitoring at the module pair level saves us both time and money.”


**Romain Rocamora, Head of Sales and Channel Manager at SolarEdge Technologies France** , adds: “Projects such as the Hôtel d’Alsace demonstrate how advanced solar technologies can unlock the full potential of some of the most complex and sensitive buildings. By combining panel-level optimisation with enhanced safety features such as SafeDC™, we enable public bodies such as the European Community of Alsace to maximise their energy production, ensure the highest safety standards, simplify long-term operation and maintenance, and support local authorities in their transition towards greater energy independence.”
