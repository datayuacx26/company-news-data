# ZoomInfo vs. Apollo: Which should you choose in 2026?

ZoomInfo is the better fit for enterprise teams that need deep, US-heavy data with rich intent signals and can absorb an annual contract. Apollo is the better fit for startups and mid-market teams that want a large database plus sequencing and a dialer in one low-cost, self-serve seat. It comes down to data depth versus all-in-one value.

## What is the core difference between ZoomInfo and Apollo?


Depth versus breadth of use, at very different price points. ZoomInfo is a data platform first; Apollo is a workflow tool that includes data.


[ZoomInfo](https://www.artisan.co/blog/artisan-vs-zoominfo) built its reputation on the accuracy and richness of its US enterprise data, plus a signals layer most rivals cannot match: WebSights for website visitor identification and Scoops for human-verified sales intelligence. It repositioned around a broader go-to-market (GTM) intelligence story and now trades publicly under the ticker $GTM. Execution features exist, but they are add-ons to the data core.


[Apollo](https://www.artisan.co/blog/artisan-vs-apollo) inverts this. It leads with an accessible all-in-one experience: a large database wrapped in sequencing, a dialer, deal management, and assistive AI, so a rep can go from search to sent email in one tool. It is used by over 600,000 companies (over 100,000 paying), reported roughly $200M ARR, and acquired Pocus in March 2026 to strengthen its signals. Where ZoomInfo sells depth to enterprises, Apollo sells value to the broad market.


## Which has better B2B data, ZoomInfo or Apollo?


For enterprise US coverage and verification, ZoomInfo is widely considered the stronger source; for value at scale, Apollo is hard to beat.


[ZoomInfo](https://www.artisan.co/blog/clay-vs-zoominfo) claims over 321M profiles (a vendor figure) and invests heavily in verification and its Scoops and intent layers. Enterprises pay a premium precisely because the data holds up under scrutiny in the segments ZoomInfo covers best. This depth is the reason it commands the contracts it does.


Apollo claims over 230M contacts (a vendor figure). Reviewers report accuracy varies by region and segment, which is common at Apollo's price point, but the coverage is broad enough that many teams, including some enterprises, use Apollo as a low-cost data layer regardless of what they run for outreach. If your priority is verified depth in North American enterprise accounts, ZoomInfo leads. If it is broad coverage at a fraction of the cost, Apollo wins. ## How do ZoomInfo and Apollo compare on price in 2026?


This is the starkest contrast between them. Apollo is transparent and cheap; ZoomInfo is opaque, annual, and enterprise-priced.


[Apollo](https://www.artisan.co/blog/apollo-vs-lusha-which-should-you-choose-in-2026) publishes its tiers openly, from a free plan up to about $119/user/month, per its pricing page, with credits governing exports and enrichment. You can start on a monthly plan without talking to sales.


ZoomInfo is quote-based and sold on annual contracts, with buyer-reported entry points around $15K per year and rising steeply with seats, data volume, and add-ons like WebSights and intent. Its contract terms, including auto-renewal and negotiation dynamics, have a reputation buyers should go in aware of. For a small team, the price gap alone often decides this; for an enterprise where data quality drives real revenue, ZoomInfo's cost can be justified.


## Which is better for enterprise teams?


ZoomInfo, in most cases, but not automatically. The answer depends on whether you are buying data depth or workflow consolidation.


[ZoomInfo](https://www.artisan.co/blog/zoominfo-vs-rocketreach) is built for the enterprise motion: deep data, mature admin and governance, and signals that feed large revenue operations (RevOps) teams. If your competitive edge comes from knowing more about accounts than rivals do, and you have the budget, ZoomInfo is the safer enterprise data foundation.


[Apollo](https://www.artisan.co/blog/apollo-vs-outreach-which-should-you-choose) has moved upmarket and some larger teams adopt it, often as an affordable data and sequencing layer alongside other systems. But enterprises typically treat Apollo as a value data source rather than their intelligence backbone, and lean on ZoomInfo or a dedicated data platform for the depth and verification their pipeline depends on. Match the tool to whichever of these two problems is actually yours.

## Frequently asked questions


### Is ZoomInfo or Apollo better for a startup?


Apollo, in most cases. Its tiers, from free to about $119/user/month, include sequencing and a dialer, you can start monthly without sales, and the coverage is broad enough for early pipeline. ZoomInfo's enterprise depth is real but comes with an annual, quote-based contract that usually outsizes a startup's needs and budget. Startups that later need verified enterprise data can add ZoomInfo when the motion justifies it.


### Which has more accurate data, ZoomInfo or Apollo?


For verified US enterprise coverage, ZoomInfo is widely regarded as the stronger source, which is what its premium pricing buys.[Apollo](https://www.artisan.co/blog/clay-vs-apollo-which-should-you-choose-in-2026) offers far broader value but reviewers report accuracy varies by region and segment. If verification in North American enterprise accounts is critical, ZoomInfo leads; if broad coverage at low cost matters more, Apollo is the pragmatic choice. Test both on your real target list.


### How much do ZoomInfo and Apollo cost?


Apollo publishes tiers from free to about $119/user/month, per its pricing page, and offers monthly plans. ZoomInfo is quote-based and annual, with buyer-reported entry points around $15K per year that climb with seats, data volume, and add-ons like intent and WebSights. The transparency and price gap is one of the clearest reasons buyers pick one over the other.


### Does Apollo replace ZoomInfo?


Sometimes, for teams whose data needs are broad rather than deep. Apollo's database plus its bundled outreach tools can cover a startup or mid-market motion at a fraction of ZoomInfo's cost. But enterprises that depend on verified US data, Scoops intelligence, and WebSights typically keep[ZoomInfo](https://www.artisan.co/blog/zoominfo-vs-uplead) , because Apollo is positioned as value-first coverage, not an enterprise intelligence backbone.
