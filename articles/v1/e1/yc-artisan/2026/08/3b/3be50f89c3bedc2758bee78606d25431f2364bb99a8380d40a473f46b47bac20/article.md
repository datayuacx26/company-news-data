# Champion tracking: How to catch past buyers when they change jobs

Champion tracking watches your past buyers and users for job changes, then alerts you the moment one lands at a new company. It is the highest-converting outbound signal there is, because a champion who already bought and liked your product arrives at a new employer with budget, authority, and a reason to bring you in. Artisan tracks champion job changes natively and runs the outreach the instant the signal fires.


## Why is a champion's job change the highest-converting signal?


Because it removes the two things that make cold outbound hard. Normal prospecting has to build trust from zero and convince a stranger the category is worth buying. A champion who changes jobs has already done both. They ran your product at their last company, they know it works, and they carry that conviction into a new role that comes with fresh budget and often more authority than they had before.


That is why these signals sit near the top of most outbound playbooks. You already have an advocate who now has a new problem you know how to solve. The message writes itself, reply rates clear cold benchmarks by a wide margin, and sales cycles compress because the hardest objection, "why should I trust this," is gone before you send. A single tracked champion moving into a buying role can be worth more than a hundred cold accounts.


## When should I reach out after a champion changes jobs?


Inside the first 90 days, and sooner is better. A leader stepping into a new role spends that first quarter auditing what they inherited and deciding what to keep, cut, and add. That is the window when tools are chosen and stacks rebuilt, and a champion who loved your product is primed to bring it in, if you reach them before the slot fills with something else.


Wait past the honeymoon and the read weakens. Budgets are committed, the new hire's priorities harden, and your warm advantage cools into just another vendor email. So of all the buying signals, champion tracking is the one where speed is least optional. Reaching a moved champion in week 2 rather than week 10 is often what separates a fast yes from a "we just signed with someone else." That urgency is exactly why the signal is worth automating, because a human tracking a contact list by hand will always be slower than a system that fires the moment a change is detected. The same timing logic runs through[intent signals](https://www.artisan.co/blog/intent-signals) generally.

You need a maintained list of the people worth watching and a way to detect when their employer changes. Start by building the list: past buyers, power users, and internal advocates from closed-won and even closed-lost deals, pulled from your CRM. Then you need monitoring, because manually checking a few hundred contacts' profiles is not a real process.

## What is the champion job-change playbook?


Work both sides of the move. The first side is obvious: reach the champion at their new company with a warm, specific note that references your shared history without being heavy-handed, and offer to help them stand up what worked before. Because they already trust the product, keep it short and human, and let the relationship do the selling.


The second side is the seat they left. When a champion moves, their old role is usually backfilled by someone new who has no loyalty to your product and may be evaluating alternatives. That is both a retention risk to defend and, viewed from the other direction, the same signal in reverse: a new decision-maker just landed in a role that touches your category. The complete playbook treats every champion move as two events, a warm opportunity at the destination and an open seat at the origin, and works them together.
