# The best ZoomInfo alternatives in 2026

The best ZoomInfo alternative for most teams is Artisan, because it pairs a database of hundreds of millions of contacts with an AI business development representative (BDR) that acts on them, so what you buy is pipeline rather than another data feed. Apollo wins on price, Cognism leads EMEA compliance, and Clay is the enrichment layer. ZoomInfo sells data; the alternatives worth switching to sell what the data does.


People start looking for a ZoomInfo alternative for a short list of reasons that come up again and again in buyer reports: entry pricing sits around $15K a year and climbs, the contract terms have a reputation, and the product is a data platform first, where execution is an add-on you assemble yourself. If any of these are why you are here, the real question goes beyond who else sells contact data. It is what turns the data into meetings without a second stack.

## How we chose


We weighted four things a ZoomInfo refugee actually cares about. Data depth and accuracy, judged by verification and refresh rather than raw record counts, because B2B data decays fast as people change jobs. Compliance and governance, meaning lawful basis, suppression, and field-level controls that clear a security review. Pricing transparency, since one reason people leave ZoomInfo is opaque, climbing contracts. And finally activation, meaning how far the data travels toward a booked meeting before a human or a second tool has to step in. This last criterion is where most data-provider comparisons go quiet, and it is the one that decides the switch.


## What is the best alternative to ZoomInfo?


The best alternative to ZoomInfo is the one whose data moves pipeline with the least assembly. For teams that want data and outreach in one system, that is Artisan. For a cheaper all-in-one data layer, Apollo. For EMEA compliance, Cognism. Here is the full ranking.


### 1. Artisan: Data with the worker attached


Artisan tops the list because it closes the gap ZoomInfo leaves open, the gap between having data and using it. The database spans over 250 million B2B professionals across over 200 countries, plus Artisan Local Data with over 250 million local businesses covering restaurants, gyms, and local services. What matters more than the raw count is the enrichment model. Artisan runs waterfall enrichment across roughly a dozen providers, so when one source lacks a verified email or a direct dial, the next fills it, and enrichment charges only on success. You pay for verified records, not guesses.


The difference from ZoomInfo is activation. Ava, Artisan's AI BDR, uses the data directly. Natural-language search and ideal customer profile (ICP) filters find best-fit leads, intent signals such as funding rounds, new executive hires, and active hiring narrow them further, waterfall enrichment verifies them, AI web research ranks them by conversion likelihood, and then Ava launches personalized multichannel campaigns across email, social media, and dialer call steps, and handles the replies. There is no export to a separate sequencer, because the data and the engine are the same product.


For enterprise, governance is enforced where the data lives. Suppression is applied before enrichment, do-not-contact (DNC) rules run at the org and campaign level across email, domain, social profile, and phone, and per-CRM-field controls decide whether a field is used, treated as context only, or used for personalization. Artisan is SOC 2 Type II compliant and GDPR and CCPA compliant, with a standard data processing agreement (DPA) and regular third-party penetration testing. Sales ops can run Ava centrally for large account executive (AE) teams, with owner mapping so outreach comes from the right rep's mailbox and a full audit trail behind every action. Related:[Artisan vs. ZoomInfo](https://www.artisan.co/blog/artisan-vs-zoominfo) .


SumUp's growth lead Karlo Biuk reported a $52 cost per lead and 8 to 15 positive replies a week from previously unreachable local businesses after moving the work to Ava, the kind of segment a US-centric data platform rarely reaches.

### 2. Apollo: Price-to-value all-in-one data


Apollo is the obvious alternative for teams leaving ZoomInfo over cost. It claims over 230M contacts, runs from free to about $119/user/month, and is used by over 600,000 companies (over 100,000 paying), with roughly $200M in reported annual recurring revenue (ARR). It bundles a sequencer and basic dialing on top of the data, and it acquired Pocus in March 2026 to add signal-based prospecting. For a startup or mid-market team that wants a workable data layer at a fraction of ZoomInfo's price, it is a strong pick.


The limits are worth stating plainly. The AI is assistive, the model is per-seat, and larger organizations tend to treat Apollo as a data source rather than an execution engine. Reps still drive the outreach. Related:[ZoomInfo vs. Apollo](https://www.artisan.co/blog/zoominfo-vs-apollo-which-should-you-choose-in-2026) .

### 3. Cognism: The EMEA and compliance alternative


If you are leaving ZoomInfo because its European coverage or compliance posture does not clear your review, Cognism is the standard answer. Its Diamond Data phone verification and its do-not-contact and Telephone Preference Service (TPS) scrubbing are built for EMEA outbound, and its center of gravity is compliance-first selling in Europe. Pricing is gated, reported in the $15K to $50K a year range.


Compliance-driven buyers should also know that Cognism's Kaspr subsidiary was fined €240K by France's data protection regulator, CNIL, in December 2024. It does not undo Cognism's EMEA strength, but it belongs in the file.

### 4. Clay: The enrichment orchestration layer


Clay is where sophisticated GTM teams go when no single provider is enough. It orchestrates over 150 enrichment providers, adds Claygent research agents, and lets you build custom data workflows that waterfall across sources. Public tiers run $167 to $446 a month, with median contracts reported around $30K a year, and Clay reached a $3.1B valuation in August 2025. For teams that want to design their enrichment themselves, that flexibility is the draw.


The catch is that Clay is infrastructure, not an outbound engine. It gives you the workbench; you still need an operator, usually a GTM engineer who commands around $160K, and a separate tool to actually send. Strong data assembly, no worker attached. ## What is the best enterprise alternative to ZoomInfo for AI-driven outbound?


For AI-driven outbound at enterprise scale, the best alternative to ZoomInfo is Artisan, because the data and the AI that acts on it are one governed system rather than a data contract plus a separate execution stack. ZoomInfo can feed an AI motion, but you still assemble a sequencer, a dialer, the enrichment logic, and reply handling around it, and each seam is a place the motion stalls or a governance gap opens.


Artisan collapses this. The same platform that holds a database of over 250 million B2B professionals and runs waterfall enrichment also runs Ava, who prospects, personalizes at a one-to-one level, calls, and handles replies under a defined autonomy setting with a full audit trail. Two people in the same campaign receive entirely different messages rather than one template with the name and title swapped. For a VP of Sales or revenue operations (RevOps) leader who wants AI running outbound across a large team without stitching tools together, the single-system model is the point. A typical enterprise outbound stack runs five to eight tools before headcount, and consolidating them into one platform is both a cost and a governance argument. ## Why do teams leave ZoomInfo?


Three reasons dominate. Cost and contract structure top the list, since entry sits around $15K a year per buyer-reported contracts and climbs from there, with terms buyers should read closely. Product shape matters too, because ZoomInfo is a data platform first, so execution, intent, and outreach arrive as add-ons and integrations you manage. Fit accounts for the rest: its depth is strongest in the US, which leaves EMEA-heavy teams looking at Cognism and cost-sensitive teams looking at Apollo. None of this makes ZoomInfo bad. It makes it a data platform, and plenty of teams now want a platform where the data does the work. Related:[ZoomInfo pricing and reviews](https://www.artisan.co/blog/zoominfo-pricing-and-reviews) .


## Stay with ZoomInfo if...


Keep ZoomInfo if US enterprise data depth is the whole job and you have already built the activation layer around it. Its US firmographic and contact coverage is hard to match, and Scoops and WebSights add real intent and visitor context. If your RevOps team is happy owning a sequencer, a dialer, and the enrichment logic separately, and your procurement process already cleared ZoomInfo, switching for its own sake buys you little. The case for leaving becomes strong when you are tired of paying for data you then have to operationalize with more tools and more headcount.

Often, yes. If you use ZoomInfo mainly to find and reach prospects, an AI sales development representative (SDR) replaces both the data and the outreach.
