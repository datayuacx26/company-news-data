## What are the five types of buyer intent data?


Five distinct signal families are sold under the label "intent," and knowing which is which prevents most bad purchases:


1. **First-party intent** : behavior on your own properties, including website visits, pricing-page views, and product usage. Highest actionability, smallest volume. See our guide to the[best B2B website visitor identification tools](https://www.artisan.co/blog/b2b-website-visitor-identification) .


2. **Second-party intent** : buyers researching on review platforms (G2 Buyer Intent; TrustRadius signals, now inside HG Insights). Highest purchase fidelity, since these buyers are actively comparing vendors.


3. **Third-party topic intent** : content-consumption surges across publisher networks, dominated by Bombora's ~5,000-publisher co-op. Broadest coverage, account-level only.


4. **Bidstream intent** : inferred from ad-exchange data. Cheapest and noisiest, and declining with cookie deprecation.


5. **Event and signal-layer data** : observable facts such as funding rounds, hiring and job postings, executive hires, tech installs (HG Insights, BuiltWith), champion job changes (native in Artisan, standalone via UserGems and Champify), and community activity (Common Room). Verifiable and contact-addressable, which is why outbound teams increasingly start here.


The types are complements, not substitutes. Layering all three major intent categories correlates with roughly 47% better conversion than single-source intent, per Autobound's category roundup. For a primer, see[what is intent data](https://www.artisan.co/blog/what-is-intent-data) .


## Why is everyone's intent data the same?


Because much of it literally is. Bombora's co-op is the upstream source for the topic-surge intent resold inside ZoomInfo (per multiple third-party comparisons), and licensed across Apollo, Cognism, 6sense, and much of the rest of the category. When two vendors both show "surging on data enrichment," they are often reading the same co-op differently.


Full disclosure: Artisan's topic-intent signal is Bombora-powered too. We license the same co-op everyone respects, and we would rather say so than imply we invented a publisher network. The useful basis for comparing intent tools is therefore not "whose topic data is better" but two different questions: what proprietary signals surround the co-op data (website visitors, hiring, funding, job changes, custom AI signals), and what happens automatically when a signal fires. On topic data alone, buy Bombora direct; on everything after the signal, the platforms diverge sharply.
