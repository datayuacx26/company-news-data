# The best La Growth Machine alternatives in 2026

The best La Growth Machine alternative for most teams is Artisan, because it does the part La Growth Machine leaves to you: Ava, its AI business development representative (BDR), writes the multichannel outreach, sends it with managed deliverability, and answers the replies autonomously. La Growth Machine builds elegant sequences. You still run them.


La Growth Machine earned its following on social-first multichannel outreach: one sequence spanning email, LinkedIn, and calls, orchestrated per sending identity. This model is smart, and for a small team that likes steering its own campaigns it holds up well. But its per-identity pricing scales with the number of personas you send from, and the writing, targeting, and reply-handling stay your job. Teams shopping for an alternative usually want less of that manual load, not a prettier way to carry it.

## How we chose


Four criteria, weighted for a buyer who is tired of running multichannel campaigns by hand:


1. **How much of the job the tool does.** Sequencing across channels is table stakes now. The real question is whether the tool writes and replies, or just schedules the words you supply.


2. **Deliverability at scale.** Mailbox provisioning and management, inbox placement testing, sender reputation monitoring, and automatic pausing of unhealthy mailboxes.


3. **Pricing transparency and shape.** Published tiers beat quote-on-request, and we flag per-identity or per-seat models that tax growth.


4. **Deployment fit.** Whether one operator can run it, or whether it needs a dedicated specialist steering every persona.


## Why do teams look for a La Growth Machine alternative?


Most switch because La Growth Machine's model is orchestration-first, and orchestration is only half the job. It gives you a clean canvas for multichannel sequences and lets you assign each one to a sending identity. What it does not do is invent the messaging, choose the accounts, or work the inbox once replies land. This is a fair trade if you have people who enjoy that work. It is a poor one if the reason you bought software was to stop doing it.


The triggers we hear most:


-


**The manual load doesn't shrink.** You still write each variant, build the branching logic, and answer every reply by hand. More channels can mean more places to keep up with, not fewer.


-


**Per-identity pricing taxes scale.** Because the model bills per sending persona, cost rises every time you add a rep or a mailbox, which pinches teams trying to widen their footprint.


-


**Social-first is a strength and a ceiling.** The multichannel design shines for high-touch, low-volume motions. Teams that need real email volume alongside it often bolt on a second tool. Our guide to[cold emailing](https://www.artisan.co/blog/cold-emailing) covers that email side.


None of this makes La Growth Machine a weak product. It makes it the wrong product for a team that wants outbound to run without a person steering every persona.

### 3. Reply: An AI agent on a sequencer


Reply is the pick for a team that wants an AI agent layered onto multichannel cadences without leaving the sequencer world. Its Jason AI agent drafts messages and handles some reply logic on top of Reply's email and multichannel sequences. For teams moving cautiously from manual sequencing toward agent-run email, it is an accessible on-ramp.


The limit is that Jason is an email-centric agent sitting on a classic sequencer, so a human still owns much of the motion. Reply's platform sequences start around $59 per month, with its AI agent tier at roughly $500 to $800 per month, per Reply's pricing pages.


**Best for:** teams that want a light AI agent bolted onto familiar multichannel sequencing.


**Who should pick something else:** teams that want the sourcing, writing, and replying owned end to end rather than assisted.


**Pricing:** sequences from about $59/mo; AI agent tier about $500-800/mo, per Reply's pricing pages.

## Stay with La Growth Machine if...


La Growth Machine is the right call for a specific profile. Keep it if:


-


You run high-touch, lower-volume multichannel motions and enjoy hand-crafting each sequence.


-


Your persona count is small, so per-identity pricing stays affordable.


-


Social-first outreach on LinkedIn is central to how you sell, and email volume is secondary.


Switching tools to fix a copy-and-reply problem that La Growth Machine was never built to solve is a mistake. If the manual load is the problem, this is the signal to move to an autonomous[AI BDR](https://www.artisan.co/blog/ai-bdr) instead.


## Frequently asked questions


**What is the best alternative to La Growth Machine?** For teams that want multichannel outreach written, sent, and answered for them, Artisan is the strongest alternative, because Ava runs the full job autonomously with managed deliverability. For a like-for-like multichannel sequencer with stronger personalization craft, Lemlist is the closest direct rival.


**What is the best enterprise alternative to La Growth Machine?** Artisan. It adds what larger teams need on top of sequencing: central sales-ops deployment for AE teams who never log in, cross-channel do-not-contact governance, CRM owner routing, an audit trail, and SOC 2 Type II compliance. La Growth Machine's per-identity model is built for hands-on operators rather than governed at-scale deployment.


**How does La Growth Machine's per-identity pricing work?** La Growth Machine bills per sending identity, roughly one persona's email and LinkedIn accounts, so your cost rises with each rep or mailbox you add. Usage-based pricing like Artisan's, where you pay for work performed rather than per persona, keeps costs from multiplying as you widen your footprint.


**Is Lemlist better than La Growth Machine?** For personalization depth, often yes: Lemlist's custom images and variables are stronger. Both are manual multichannel sequencers with the same limit, though. You still write and reply. Lemlist runs $69 to $109/user/month.


**Can I replace La Growth Machine with an AI SDR?** Yes, if you want the job done rather than the sequence enabled. An autonomous[AI sales agent](https://www.artisan.co/ai-sales-agent) like Ava, an AI sales development representative (SDR), sources leads, writes multichannel outreach, manages deliverability, and answers replies, which covers La Growth Machine's function plus the manual work it leaves to you.


**Which La Growth Machine alternative is cheapest?** Woodpecker's usage model (about $7 per 100 prospects contacted) and Saleshandy's low published tiers are the most budget-friendly at small volumes. Cheapest per message is not the same as cheapest per meeting booked, which is where autonomous tools change the math.


**Does La Growth Machine handle replies for me?** No. La Growth Machine sequences outreach across channels, but qualifying and answering replies is your job. This reply-handling gap is the main reason teams evaluating[Artisan](https://www.artisan.co/) look past sequencers toward an autonomous BDR.
