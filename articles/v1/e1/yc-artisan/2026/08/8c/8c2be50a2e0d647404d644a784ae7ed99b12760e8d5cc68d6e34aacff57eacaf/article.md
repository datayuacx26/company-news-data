# 6sense pricing and reviews (2026)

6sense does not publish prices for its paid plans. It is gated account-based marketing (ABM) software sold on annual contracts, and buyers report a median contract of $62,820 a year via Vendr, with a wide range from roughly $11,500 to $175,000 by scale. There is a free tier. Reviewers praise the predictive intent and flag the price and complexity.

Paid figures are gated. The most useful public anchor is Vendr's buyer data: a median annual contract of $62,820, ranging from roughly $11,500 to $175,000. This spread reflects how much the price depends on data volume, seats, credits, advertising, and which modules you turn on.


## How much does 6sense cost in practice?


The median is a starting point, not a quote. 6sense pricing scales with the number of accounts and contacts you unlock, the seats you provision, the credits you consume, and whether you add advertising and the heavier analytics and orchestration modules. Two companies of similar size can land far apart depending on that configuration, which is why the reported range is so wide.


Then add the work to make it pay off. Reviewers report that 6sense is a substantial platform with a long implementation and a real learning curve, so most buyers budget for onboarding, integration with your CRM and marketing stack, and internal ownership from a revenue operations (RevOps) or marketing-ops function. Advertising spend, when you use the media side, is its own line on top of the license. The fully loaded first-year cost is meaningfully higher than the software subscription alone.


## What do the 6sense tiers include?


The tiers step up along data depth, activation, and analytics. The free tier gives limited intent and account insight as an entry point. Team covers the core predictive intent and account identification most buyers start with. Growth adds orchestration, advertising, and deeper data and workflows. Enterprise opens the full platform, with advanced analytics, governance, and the fullest RevvyAI capability.


RevvyAI is the 2026 headline. 6sense's agent layer is generally available and applies AI across identification, prioritization, and action. It is a real addition, and it also sits inside the same gated, module-based pricing, so unlocking more agent capability generally means a larger contract. As with the rest of the platform, what you pay depends on how much of it you switch on.


## What are the hidden costs of 6sense?


The line items buyers underestimate are credits and volume caps, advertising spend, implementation, and the annual commitment. Because pricing is usage- and module-based, teams that grow their account universe or lean harder on the platform can hit overages or need a mid-term expansion. The negotiated, opaque pricing also means bargaining power matters, and comparable buyers do not always pay comparable rates.


There is also a fit cost worth naming. 6sense is analytics-and-advertising-heavy ABM. It does well at identifying which accounts are in-market and reaching them with paid media, and it leans on your existing execution tools and reps to run the direct outreach. If you were hoping one contract would both identify the accounts and run the personalized outreach to them, budget for the execution layer separately, because this is not where 6sense's weight sits. ## What do reviewers say about 6sense?


The consensus is that 6sense is a powerful, data-rich ABM platform that large marketing and revenue teams rely on to find in-market accounts. Reviewers report that the predictive intent, account identification, and segmentation are standout strengths, that the analytics are deep, and that RevvyAI is a promising addition. For teams running account-based programs at scale, it is a category leader for a reason.


The complaints are consistent: it is expensive, the pricing is opaque, implementation is long, and the learning curve is steep enough that value depends on dedicated ownership. Reviewers also report that data accuracy can vary and that the platform is heavy for smaller teams. The balanced read is that 6sense is strong at analytics and identification, priced and operated like enterprise software, and most powerful in the hands of a team with the resources to run it.


## Who is 6sense best for, and not for?


6sense best fits mid-market and enterprise marketing and revenue teams running account-based programs, with a RevOps or marketing-ops function to own it and budget for paid media. If your motion is built on identifying in-market accounts and orchestrating advertising and plays against them, it is a strong choice.


It fits less well for small teams, for buyers who cannot absorb a five-to-six-figure annual contract plus implementation, and for teams that want the account signals and the personalized outreach to come from one system that also runs the sending. 6sense identifies and orchestrates. Direct outbound execution still leans on your reps and separate engagement tools. If activation, not analytics, is your bottleneck, weigh that carefully.

### Is 6sense worth the price?


For mid-market and enterprise teams running account-based programs with the resources to implement and own it, reviewers generally find 6sense worth the investment for its predictive intent and account identification. For small teams, or teams that cannot absorb a five-to-six-figure contract plus a long implementation, the value is harder to justify. It depends on whether account-based marketing is central to your motion.
