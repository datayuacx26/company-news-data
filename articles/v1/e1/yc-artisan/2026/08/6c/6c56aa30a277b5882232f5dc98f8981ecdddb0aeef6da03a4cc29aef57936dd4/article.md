## How we chose


Four criteria, weighted for agency economics specifically.


**How much of the per-client job the tool does.** An agency's cost is labor: writing, targeting, replying, reporting. Tools that remove this work score highest, because they change the unit economics of adding a client. Tools that only send score lower.


**Multi-client management and separation.** Can you run many clients from one place, keep their data and rules separate, and report per client? White-label presentation, per-account audit trails, and central ops are the features that matter here.


**Pricing transparency and margin.** Published, predictable pricing lets an agency price its own service confidently. We flagged add-on costs that accumulate and gated contracts that make margin hard to model.


**Deliverability and control at scale.** Managing many domains and mailboxes safely, with placement testing, DNS checks, health monitoring, and automatic protection when a mailbox goes bad.

## Why does consolidation matter so much for agencies?


Because the agency cost curve is brutal without it. A typical outbound stack runs five to eight tools before headcount: a data provider, an enrichment layer, a sequencer, a deliverability tool, a dialer, a reporting layer, sometimes a GTM engineer to wire it together. Now multiply the operational overhead by every client you serve. Each new logo means more mailboxes to manage, more copy to produce, more replies to triage, and more logins to context-switch between. Labor, not software, is where agency margin leaks.


Consolidation attacks this directly. When one platform finds, enriches, writes, sends, replies, and books across all your clients, the per-client overhead collapses and an account manager can carry more accounts profitably. That is why the ranking puts a system that removes labor above sending engines that add it back. If you want the underlying concept, our explainer on[what an AI BDR is](https://www.artisan.co/blog/ai-bdr) covers how one agent owns the full job, and the[AI sales agent](https://www.artisan.co/ai-sales-agent) page shows the deployment pattern agencies use to run it centrally.


## What changed for agency outbound recently?


The infrastructure layer became commoditized and the AI layer matured. Mailbox providers like Maildoso, Inframail, and Mailreef now sell inboxes at $0.50 to $5 each, and sequencers are vertically integrating that supply, which pushed pure-sending prices down and made sending a commodity. Apollo acquired Pocus (March 2026), adding intent to its data layer. Clay's $3.1B valuation (August 2025) confirmed enrichment orchestration as its own category. And the AI SDR wave sorted itself out: 11x's 2025 reporting and leadership change (per TechCrunch) cooled some of the hype, while the surviving question for agencies shifted from "can an AI send" to "can an AI run the client's whole motion under my supervision." That is the ground the ranking is fought on now.
