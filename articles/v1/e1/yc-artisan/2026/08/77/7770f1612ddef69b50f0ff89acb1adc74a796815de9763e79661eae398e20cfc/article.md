## How did the 2023-2025 deliverability crackdown change cold email?


For years, senders leaned on artificial engagement networks: tools exchanged automated emails between real inboxes and marked them as important to inflate sender reputation. In January 2023, Google enforced against the major networks and cut their Gmail API access, ending the practice's officially tolerated era. Then came the February 2024 Google and Yahoo bulk-sender rules: SPF, DKIM, and DMARC authentication became mandatory, one-click unsubscribe (RFC 8058) became required for bulk senders, and a 0.3% spam-complaint ceiling became a hard line. Cross it and your domain's mail stops landing. Microsoft followed with similar requirements for senders of over 1,000 daily emails in 2025. Apollo dropped artificial engagement entirely in 2024 and replaced it with send pacing, and while most of this list still bundles sender reputation tools, the center of gravity has moved to what mailbox providers actually reward: gradual volume ramp for new inboxes, clean DNS (SPF, DKIM, DMARC), low per-inbox volume, and list quality. None of it rescues bad targeting or spammy copy from a 0.3% complaint ceiling. Treat reputation tools as a supplement to authentication, list quality, and volume discipline rather than a substitute.


## How many cold emails can you safely send per day?


The 2026 norm is 15 to 50 emails per day per inbox (a hard ceiling of 50, though conservative operators keep closer to 20 or 30), sent from 3 to 8 secondary domains with 2 to 4 mailboxes each, never from your primary domain. This architecture yields roughly 300 to 1,500 sends per day at safe per-inbox volumes while quarantining reputation risk away from the domain your company actually depends on. New inboxes need 2 to 4 weeks of gradual volume increase before full throughput. This is why "unlimited sending" plans are a half-truth: the software is unlimited, the inboxes are not.
