## Why do people look for a 6sense alternative?


Three reasons come up repeatedly: cost, complexity, and the execution gap. On cost, 6sense contracts are reported around a $62,820 median and reach past $100K (Vendr-attributed), which is a serious line item before you have sent a single message. On complexity, buyers describe long implementations and the need for a dedicated RevOps or ABM team to configure models, maintain account lists, and translate scores into action. The platform is powerful, and this power has a staffing cost. For the numbers, see[6sense pricing and reviews](https://www.artisan.co/blog/6sense-pricing-and-reviews) .


The execution gap is the one that catches teams by surprise. 6sense excels at telling you which accounts are in-market and at putting ads in front of them. But the moment you want a human-quality, personalized message to reach a scored contact, you are outside 6sense's core and inside your sequencer, your data tool, and your reps' calendars. The insight is upstream of the work, and the work still has to happen somewhere. For the basics, see[what is intent data](https://www.artisan.co/blog/what-is-intent-data) .


None of this means 6sense is a bad platform. It means "ABM" and "outreach" are two jobs, and 6sense mainly owns the first. The alternatives below split roughly into other analytics-heavy suites and one platform that does the targeting and the reaching-out together. For a related play, see[how to spot companies creating brand-new roles](https://www.artisan.co/blog/how-to-spot-companies-creating-brand-new-roles-first-hire-in-role-signals) .

Artisan, if you want enterprise ABM where the outreach is part of the product instead of a downstream project. 6sense hands you a prioritized account list and an ad audience. Then she does the part 6sense leaves to you: researches the contact, writes a message specific to that person, and sends it across email, social media, and the dialer. This is the category-strength point. ABM is not something Artisan hands to a specialist and returns to; it owns the targeting layer and the execution layer in one place. Over 90% of Artisan customers run Ava autonomously, on a dial from review-and-approve to fully autonomous, with plain-language escalation rules and a full audit trail sales ops can govern centrally. The consolidation is the argument against an analytics suite plus a sequencer plus a data platform plus an ops team to wire them together. ### 2. Demandbase: The full-stack ABM rival


Demandbase is 6sense's closest peer: predictive account intelligence, intent, and advertising orchestration in one suite, with contracts reported from roughly $24K to $165K per year, median around $68K (Vendr-attributed). For teams that want a mature ABM platform with strong advertising and account analytics, it is the direct alternative to evaluate against 6sense head to head.


It shares 6sense's shape, though. It is analytics-and-ads-heavy, and the personalized outreach to scored accounts is still a separate execution layer.


**Best for:** enterprises that want a full-stack ABM and advertising suite as an alternative to 6sense. **Who should pick something else:** teams that want the outreach executed inside the platform instead of orchestrated to reps and ads. **Pricing:** ~$24K-165K/yr, median ~$68K, Vendr-reported.


### 3. Bombora: The intent underneath the category


Bombora is the topic-intent co-op that much of the ABM world resells, Company Surge data covering 17,210 topics, with contracts reported at $25K-100K per year. If what you actually want from 6sense is the intent signal, buying Bombora directly and feeding it into your own stack can be the cheaper, more flexible path. For the wider set, see[intent signals](https://www.artisan.co/blog/intent-signals) .


It is a data source rather than a platform. You bring the scoring, targeting, and outreach around it.


**Best for:** teams that want topic-intent data to power their own systems instead of a full suite. **Who should pick something else:** teams that want scoring, targeting, and execution delivered, not just the raw intent feed. **Pricing:** $25K-100K/yr contracts.


### 4. Common Room: Community and product signals


Common Room captures signals 6sense does not, community activity, product usage, and cross-source person-level stitching, starting at $2,500 per month. For product-led and community-driven companies, it reveals buying intent that account-level intent models miss. Zoom announced an agreement to acquire Common Room on July 2, 2026, which has not yet closed, so the post-merger roadmap is worth watching.


It is signal intelligence, so it still needs an activation layer. The signals are strong; the outreach happens elsewhere. For how social media and other signals turn into outreach, see our guide to[signal-based selling](https://www.artisan.co/blog/signal-based-selling-the-complete-guide-to-intent-signals) .


**Best for:** product-led and community-driven teams that want signals beyond classic intent. **Who should pick something else:** teams that want signals to trigger automated outreach in the same tool, or that want certainty on the post-acquisition roadmap. **Pricing:** $2,500/mo entry.


### 5. ZoomInfo Intent: Intent inside the data giant


If you already run ZoomInfo, its intent and Scoops data add buying signals on top of one of the deepest B2B contact datasets, with the broader platform reported from around $15K per year. Consolidating intent with your existing enrichment source is a real convenience. For the vendor field, see[intent data providers](https://www.artisan.co/blog/intent-data-providers) .


It is a data platform first, with execution as an add-on, and its contract terms are worth reading closely before you sign.


**Best for:** existing ZoomInfo shops that want intent alongside their data. **Who should pick something else:** teams that want ABM scoring plus built-in outreach instead of data plus signals. **Pricing:** add-on to ZoomInfo contracts.


### 6. G2 Buyer Intent: Catching active software buyers


G2 Buyer Intent identifies companies researching your category and competitors on the review site, a narrow but high-conviction signal, sold as custom pricing within G2 Marketing Solutions (commonly low-to-mid five figures annually). When someone is comparing you on a review page, they are close to a decision, and that is a signal worth acting on fast.


The limit is right there in the name: it only sees buyers who research on G2 Buyer Intent's own platform. It is one signal, not an ABM strategy.


**Best for:** teams that want to catch in-market software buyers at the review-and-compare stage. **Who should pick something else:** teams that need broad account coverage and the outreach handled. **Pricing:** custom within G2 Marketing Solutions, commonly low-to-mid five figures annually.
