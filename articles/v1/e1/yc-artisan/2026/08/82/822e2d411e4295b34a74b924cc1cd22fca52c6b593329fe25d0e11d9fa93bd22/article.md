# ZoomInfo vs. Clay: Which should you choose in 2026?

ZoomInfo and Clay solve different problems. ZoomInfo is an enterprise data platform with its own deep, US-heavy database and intent signals, sold on annual contracts. Clay is a programmable enrichment layer that waterfalls across over 150 providers and needs an operator to run it. Pick ZoomInfo for verified depth out of the box, Clay for custom data workflows.

## What is the core difference between ZoomInfo and Clay?


One owns the data; the other orchestrates it. ZoomInfo is a destination you search; Clay is a layer you configure.


ZoomInfo built its reputation on a large, verified first-party database, strongest in US enterprise coverage, wrapped in signals most rivals cannot match: WebSights for website visitor identification and Scoops for human-sourced sales intelligence. It repositioned around a broader go-to-market intelligence story and now trades publicly under the ticker $GTM. You log in, search, and export. The data is already there. For more ZoomInfo matchups, see[ZoomInfo vs. Cognism](https://www.artisan.co/blog/zoominfo-vs-cognism-which-should-you-choose-in-2026) and[ZoomInfo vs. LinkedIn Sales Navigator](https://www.artisan.co/blog/zoominfo-vs-linkedin-sales-navigator-which-should-you-choose-in-2026) .


Clay is the opposite shape. It holds no first-party database. Instead it connects to over 150 data providers, runs them in a waterfall so you only pay when one source fills a field the last one missed, and lets you enrich, score, and format records with spreadsheet-style logic and its Claygent research agents. Then it pushes the result into your sequencer or CRM. ZoomInfo gives you the store. Clay gives you the workbench. ## Which has better data, ZoomInfo or Clay?


It depends on what "better" means to you. For verified depth in North American enterprise accounts out of the box, ZoomInfo leads. For breadth of sources and hard-to-find records, a well-built Clay table wins.


ZoomInfo's advantage is one large, heavily verified database plus its Scoops and intent layers. Enterprises pay a premium precisely because the data holds up under scrutiny in the segments ZoomInfo covers best, and any rep can search it without setup.


Clay's advantage is that it is not one source but many. Because it waterfalls across over 150 providers, it can find records and fill fields no single database holds, and it lets you verify one provider against another. For niche personas, thin international coverage, or hard-to-find phone numbers, a well-built Clay table usually beats any single vendor, including ZoomInfo. ## How do ZoomInfo and Clay pricing compare in 2026?


Clay is transparent and starts cheap; ZoomInfo is opaque, annual, and enterprise-priced. This contrast is one of the clearest reasons buyers pick one over the other.


Clay publishes its tiers openly: a free plan, Launch at $167 per month, and Growth at $446 per month, per its pricing page, with an Enterprise tier that is custom. The model is usage-based, so enrichment credits are consumed fast at scale, and reported median contracts land around $30,000 per year once serious usage kicks in. You also pay implicitly for operator time to build workflows.


ZoomInfo is quote-based and sold on annual contracts, with buyer-reported entry points around $15K per year that climb steeply with seats, data volume, and add-ons like WebSights and intent. Its contract terms, including auto-renewal and negotiation dynamics, have a reputation buyers should go in aware of. For a small team, the price and transparency gap often decides this; for an enterprise where data quality drives real revenue, ZoomInfo's cost can be justified.


## Which is easier for a team without a data engineer?


ZoomInfo, clearly. It is built so a rep or revenue operations (RevOps) analyst can search, filter, and export on day one. Clay is powerful precisely because it is programmable, and this is also its cost.


Extracting real value from Clay usually assumes someone who thinks in data, whether that is a dedicated go-to-market (GTM) engineer, a role that can run north of $160,000 fully loaded, or an ops-minded rep who enjoys building. Teams that buy Clay expecting a turnkey list tool tend to underuse it. Teams that staff it properly obtain outputs a single database cannot match. ZoomInfo asks far less of the operator; you are buying finished depth, not a system to assemble.


## Which is better for enterprise teams?


ZoomInfo, in most cases, if the job is a verified data foundation. Clay earns its place in the enterprise stack too, but as a precision enrichment layer rather than the backbone.


ZoomInfo is built for an enterprise motion: deep data, mature admin and governance, and signals that feed large RevOps operations. If your competitive edge comes from knowing more about accounts than rivals do, and you have the budget, it is the safer foundation. Many enterprises then run Clay alongside it to enrich specific high-value segments, waterfall multiple sources, and fill the gaps a single vendor leaves. They are not really rivals at this tier so much as different jobs. The question is which job is actually yours.


## What is the shared limitation of both ZoomInfo and Clay?


Neither one runs your outreach. Both are data-first, and this is the honest limit they share.


ZoomInfo gives you verified records and signals, but a person still writes the messages, sends them, and works the replies. Clay produces beautifully enriched rows, then hands them off to whatever sequencer a person still operates. Both improve the inputs to outbound. Neither runs it. If your real bottleneck is not the quality of the data but the humans required to act on it, adding either tool sharpens the input without touching that constraint.

## Frequently asked questions


### Is ZoomInfo or Clay better for a startup?


Clay, for many startups, if you have someone data-literate to run it, because you can start on a free or $167-per-month plan and pay for what you use. ZoomInfo's enterprise depth is real but comes with an annual, quote-based contract that usually outsizes an early team's needs and budget. A startup without an operator, though, may gain more from a self-serve database than from a workbench it cannot staff.


### Which has more accurate data, ZoomInfo or Clay?


For verified US enterprise coverage out of the box, ZoomInfo is widely regarded as the stronger single source, which is what its premium pricing buys. Clay can beat any one vendor for specific, hard-to-find records because it waterfalls over 150 providers and lets you cross-check them, but this quality depends on how well the table is built. Test both on your real target list before deciding.


### How much do ZoomInfo and Clay cost?


Clay publishes tiers from a free plan to Launch at $167 per month and Growth at $446 per month, per its pricing page, on a usage-based model, with reported median contracts around $30,000 per year at scale. ZoomInfo is quote-based and annual, with buyer-reported entry points around $15K per year that climb with seats, data volume, and add-ons. The transparency gap is one of the clearest differences between them.


### Does Clay replace ZoomInfo?


Not exactly. Clay has no first-party database; it orchestrates other providers, so it can substitute for ZoomInfo only if the sources you plug in cover your targets well. Many teams run both: ZoomInfo as the verified backbone, Clay as the precision layer that waterfalls extra sources and fills gaps. Whether you need both depends on how specialized your targeting is.


### Do ZoomInfo or Clay run outreach for you?


No. ZoomInfo is data-first with execution as an add-on; Clay enriches data and hands it to a sequencer someone else operates. Neither writes messages, sends them, or works replies on its own.
