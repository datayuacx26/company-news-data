## Why do teams look for a LeadIQ alternative?


Usually one of three gaps. The first is data breadth, since LeadIQ is strong for point capture but teams outgrow it when they need a larger, better-verified base or intent signals. The second is cost per outcome, because paying for a capture tool plus a separate sequencer plus a dialer adds up while the capture step is only a sliver of the work. The third is execution. LeadIQ hands the contact to your sequencer, but a rep still writes the messaging, works the replies, and books the meeting. As reply rates fall industry-wide, faster capture into the same manual motion stops moving the number.


The right replacement depends on which gap hurts. Sometimes it is more or cleaner data. Sometimes it is compliance. Often it is wanting the whole motion handled instead of just the first click.


## How we chose


Four criteria, weighted for a LeadIQ buyer. How much of the outbound job the tool runs after capture. Data and signal depth, including verification quality. Pricing transparency, since stacking capture and execution tools is a common frustration. And deployment fit, from a single SDR to a central revenue operations (RevOps) team. ### 2. Apollo: Capture plus sequencing at a low price


Apollo is the closest low-cost analog to the LeadIQ flow, because it does capture and sequencing in one tool. It offers over 230M contacts, is used by over 600,000 companies (over 100,000 paying), publishes pricing from free to $119/user/month, and acquired Pocus in March 2026. For an SDR who wants to find a contact and drop them straight into a cadence without a second subscription, it is efficient and affordable.


Its AI assists a rep instead of acting autonomously, the model is per-seat, and enterprises tend to treat Apollo as a data layer. But as a direct, cheaper replacement for the capture-and-sequence habit, it is the obvious first look. ### 3. ZoomInfo: Enterprise data depth


If your reason for leaving LeadIQ is data breadth, ZoomInfo is the depth play. Repositioned as a go-to-market intelligence platform and trading as $GTM, it claims over 321M profiles (a marketing figure) and adds WebSights and Scoops. Enterprise teams that need the widest US coverage and an intent layer build around it.


It is a data platform first: capture and execution are add-ons, entry contracts are reported at over $15K per year, and its contract terms have a reputation. It is a heavier commitment than a nimble capture tool. For a direct comparison, see[ZoomInfo vs. LeadIQ](https://www.artisan.co/blog/zoominfo-vs-leadiq) .


-


**Best for:** enterprises that need the deepest US data and will build execution around it


-


**Who should pick something else:** small teams that want speed and simplicity, or anyone who wants the outreach run for them


-


**Pricing:** over $15K per year reported, gated


### 4. Cognism: Compliance-first EMEA data


Cognism is the alternative when your capture problem is really a compliance problem, especially in Europe. Diamond Data phone verification, do-not-call (DNC) and Telephone Preference Service (TPS) scrubbing, and GDPR-aligned processing make it the EMEA data leader. Reps obtain verified mobile numbers they can actually dial.


Pricing is gated and reported at roughly $15-50K per year, and it is a data provider, so you still need the sequencer and dialer. Its Kaspr subsidiary was also fined 240,000 euros by France's data protection regulator, the CNIL, in December 2024, per the regulator, which is worth weighing in diligence.


-


**Best for:** EMEA-focused SDR teams that need compliant, verified phone data


-


**Who should pick something else:** US-centric teams, or anyone who wants execution bundled in


-


**Pricing:** roughly $15-50K/year reported, gated

Clay is for teams whose capture needs are too custom for a fixed tool. You can design a capture-and-enrichment waterfall exactly how you want it.


That power is also the cost: Clay is infrastructure that needs a skilled operator, often a go-to-market (GTM) engineer around $160K a year, and median contracts land near $30K. It gives you the workbench; you supply the worker. For a direct comparison, see[Artisan vs. Clay](https://www.artisan.co/blog/artisan-vs-clay) .


-


**Best for:** RevOps and GTM engineers who want fully custom capture and enrichment


-


**Who should pick something else:** SDR teams that want a fast, ready-made capture flow out of the box


-


**Pricing:** $167-446/month public tiers; median contracts around $30K/year


### 6. Seamless.ai: Cheap real-time search


Seamless.ai is the budget real-time option, searching the web live to assemble contact data on demand. For price-sensitive teams that want a lot of lookups cheaply, it is tempting as a LeadIQ substitute.


The caveats are documented. Reviewers report bounce rates in the 20-30% range, which forces re-verification. Its plans renew annually on auto-renew terms that users report as hard to exit, and in March 2025 it faced reported removal of its company page from a major professional network over scraping. Weigh data quality against the low headline price.


-


**Best for:** very price-sensitive teams that want high-volume real-time lookups


-


**Who should pick something else:** teams that need reliable deliverability or clean contract terms


-


**Pricing:** only a Free plan is published; Pro and Enterprise are gated, on annual auto-renewal terms

## Stay with LeadIQ if...


LeadIQ is the right tool to keep in a few cases. Stay if your SDRs live in a sequencer and CRM you are committed to, and the one thing you need is faster, cleaner capture into them. Stay if your volumes are modest and a lightweight per-seat capture tool is cheaper than a platform. And stay if your data needs are US-centric and mid-depth, where LeadIQ is comfortable. The case to switch strengthens when you are paying for capture plus a sequencer plus a dialer, when reply rates make the manual motion around capture the real bottleneck, or when you want an AI to run the whole thing.
