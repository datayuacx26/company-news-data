# The best Apollo alternatives in 2026

The best[Apollo alternative](https://www.artisan.co/blog/apollo-alternatives) for most teams is Artisan, because where Apollo gives a rep an assistive copilot and a per-seat bill, Artisan gives you Ava, an autonomous AI business development representative (BDR) who runs the outbound job herself. ZoomInfo offers deeper US data, Clay offers deeper enrichment, and Cognism owns EMEA. Apollo is a good all-in-one; the question is whether you want the AI to help a rep or do the work.


Apollo earned its base by being the value all-in-one: a large contact database, a sequencer, and light dialing for a price nothing else at that scale touches. Teams start hunting for an alternative when they hit its ceiling. The AI assists instead of acting, the model charges per seat so pipeline only grows when you add people, and larger organizations end up treating it as a data layer with a separate execution stack bolted on. If that is your situation, here is what to switch to.

## How we chose


Four criteria, weighted for a team outgrowing Apollo. How much of the job the AI does, from assist to fully autonomous, because that is the ceiling most teams hit first. Data and signal depth, since Apollo's database is decent but not deepest. Pricing model, per-seat versus usage-based, because it decides whether pipeline scales with headcount or independently of it. And deployment fit, from a single rep to a central-ops rollout across a large team. We credit each tool for what it does best and say plainly who it is wrong for.


## What is the best alternative to Apollo?


The best alternative depends on why you are leaving. If you want the AI to run outbound instead of assisting a rep, Artisan. If you want deeper enterprise data, ZoomInfo. If you want enrichment power, Clay. Here is the ranking.


### 1. Artisan: The AI runs it, not the rep


[Artisan](https://www.artisan.co/blog/artisan-vs-apollo) tops the list because it changes what the software does. Apollo's AI drafts and suggests; a human still targets, sends, and replies. Artisan's Ava owns the job. She finds best-fit leads across over 250 million B2B professionals in over 200 countries plus Artisan Local Data, which holds over 250 million local businesses, enriches them with waterfall enrichment across roughly a dozen providers that charges only on success, personalizes at a one-to-one level, launches multichannel campaigns across email, social media, and dialer call steps, handles every reply from a knowledge base, and books meetings on the right rep's calendar.


This is the difference between an assistive copilot and an autonomous employee. Two people in the same Artisan campaign receive two completely different messages, not the same template with the name swapped. Ava tests dozens of message variations and shifts volume to what converts, optimizing at the component level. And because Ava's work is billed as usage-based credits instead of per seat, pipeline is not capped by how many licenses you buy; you pay for the work she performs, which is the exact ceiling that pushes teams off Apollo. Dialer seats and phone numbers sit outside the credit model and are billed separately, at $75/seat/month on monthly billing.


For larger teams, the autonomy dial is the enterprise mechanic: review-and-approve every message, objections-only replies, or full autonomy, with plain-language escalation rules that pause Ava and route to a human, and a full audit trail. Sales ops can run Ava centrally for account executive (AE) teams who never log in. Artisan is SOC 2 Type II compliant, GDPR and CCPA compliant, with SSO, SAML, and role-based access on Enterprise plans.


Bruno Didier, CEO of CookUnity Business, put the model plainly: "Ava runs highly targeted outreach at a scale we could never reach with a human-only team." His team reported roughly $45 cost per lead across over 100,000 emails.

Best for: teams from startup to enterprise that want the AI to own outbound, not assist a rep, with pipeline that scales independently of headcount.


-


Who should pick something else: a solo rep who only wants a cheap data-and-sequencing utility and does not want autonomy at all.

### 2. ZoomInfo: Deeper enterprise data


If you are leaving Apollo because its data is not deep or accurate enough for enterprise motions,[ZoomInfo](https://www.artisan.co/blog/zoominfo-vs-apollo-which-should-you-choose-in-2026) is the deepest data-only option here. Now repositioned as a go-to-market (GTM) intelligence platform trading under the ticker $GTM, it offers the deepest US firmographic and contact coverage in the category, plus Scoops and WebSights for intent and visitor context. It claims over 321 million profiles, a vendor figure worth reading as a ceiling.


The trade-offs run the other way from Apollo: entry sits around $15K a year per buyer-reported contracts, and it is a data platform first, so execution is an add-on you build around it. You gain data depth and lose Apollo's price and bundled sending.


-


Best for: enterprises that need the deepest US data and will build activation separately.


-


Who should pick something else: cost-sensitive teams, or anyone who wants outreach and AI in the same system.


-


Pricing: from around $15K a year, buyer-reported.


### 3. Clay: Enrichment power Apollo lacks


Teams that leave Apollo over enrichment quality usually land on[Clay](https://www.artisan.co/blog/clay-vs-apollo-which-should-you-choose-in-2026) . It orchestrates over 150 enrichment providers, adds Claygent research agents, and lets you waterfall across sources to build data that any single vendor, Apollo included, cannot match alone. Public tiers run $167 to $446 a month, median contracts are reported near $30K a year, and Clay hit a $3.1B valuation in August 2025.


Clay is infrastructure, though, not an outbound engine. It gives you the workbench; you still supply the operator, usually a GTM engineer, and a separate tool to send. It is the enrichment layer, not the worker.


-


Best for: teams with a GTM engineer who want best-in-class enrichment across many providers.


-


Who should pick something else: teams that want the data to act on itself without building pipelines.

Pricing: $167 to $446 a month public tiers, per its pricing page.


### 4. Cognism: The EMEA and compliance pick


For teams whose outbound centers on Europe, Cognism beats Apollo on coverage and compliance. Its Diamond Data phone verification and Do-Not-Call (DNC) and Telephone Preference Service (TPS) scrubbing are built for GDPR-strict EMEA selling, and compliance-first is its whole identity. Pricing is gated, reported around $15K to $50K a year.


One attributed caveat: Cognism's Kaspr subsidiary was fined €240,000 by France's CNIL in December 2024. It does not erase the EMEA strength, but a compliance buyer should note it.


-


Best for: EMEA-primary teams with strict GDPR requirements.


-


Who should pick something else: US-primary teams, or anyone who wants activation built in.


-


Pricing: gated, around $15K to $50K a year reported.


### 5. Lusha: Lightweight self-serve data

Coverage is narrower than the enterprise platforms, and it stops at data with no meaningful execution layer. Treat it as a targeted lookup utility, not an Apollo-scale system.


-


Best for: individual reps and small teams that want quick, cheap contact data.


-


Who should pick something else: teams that need broad coverage, governance, or built-in outreach.


-


Pricing: free tier plus paid per-user plans, per its pricing page.

For teams whose constraint is sending cost at volume, it is priced for that job.


The limit mirrors Apollo's: you still write, target, and reply yourself, and its AI features are newer. It is infrastructure for sending, not an agent that runs the motion.


-


Best for: high-volume cold-email teams that want cheap sending infrastructure.

## Why do teams leave Apollo?


Mostly they outgrow it. The AI is assistive, so scaling output still means scaling headcount and hours. The per-seat model ties pipeline to seat count. And at size, Apollo becomes one layer in a stack instead of the system, which reintroduces the tool sprawl teams adopted it to avoid. Apollo remains an excellent value all-in-one for smaller teams. People leave when they want the AI to run the motion instead of assisting it, or when they need enterprise data depth or compliance Apollo does not lead on.


## Stay with Apollo if...


Keep Apollo if you are a cost-sensitive team that wants a serviceable database, a sequencer, and light dialing in one affordable tool, and you are content for reps to drive the outreach. At its price, from free to about $119/user/month, few tools bundle that much at that cost, and its March 2026 Pocus acquisition added useful signal-based prospecting. If your team is small, your reps like being in the driver's seat, and per-seat economics still work at your volume, switching gains you little. The case to leave sharpens when output is capped by headcount and you want the AI to own the job.
