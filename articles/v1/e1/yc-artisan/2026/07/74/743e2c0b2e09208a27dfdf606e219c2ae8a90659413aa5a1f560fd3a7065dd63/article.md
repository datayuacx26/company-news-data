# The 10 best AI BDRs in 2026

Artisan's Ava is the best AI BDR (business development representative) in 2026: she is the only agent that owns every stage of the outbound job (sourcing, enrichment, multichannel outreach, reply handling, and booking) inside one platform, with autonomy you dial up or down. AiSDR and Amplemarket are the strongest alternatives. Most other tools cover part of the job.


A BDR's job is outbound pipeline generation, nothing else. So this guide, updated July 2026, does something different from the usual listicle. It defines the AI BDR category, breaks the outbound job into the five stages an AI BDR must own, and ranks only outbound-capable platforms against one question: can it actually do the whole job?


## What is an AI BDR?


An AI BDR is an AI agent that does the business development rep's job (generating outbound pipeline) instead of assisting a human who does it. Artisan coined the term's modern usage when it launched Ava, and the definitional guide lives here:[what is an AI BDR](https://www.artisan.co/blog/ai-bdr) . One test separates an AI BDR from AI-assisted software: if the agent disappeared tomorrow, would outbound stop, or would a human just lose a helper? A true AI BDR is accountable for meetings booked, not tasks completed.


## What does an AI BDR have to own end to end?


Five stages, in order. Miss one and a human, or another tool, has to fill the gap.


1. **Sourcing.** Finding net-new best-fit leads from its own database and buying signals, not just working lists you upload.


2. **Enrichment.** Verifying emails and phone numbers (ideally waterfall-style across providers) so outreach doesn't bounce, plus researching each prospect.


3. **Multichannel outreach.** Writing and sending personalized messages across email, social, and phone, backed by deliverability infrastructure (sending ramp-up, placement testing, bounce detection) to land in inboxes.


4. **Reply handling.** Qualifying interest, answering objections, and knowing when to escalate to a human.


5. **Booking.** Putting qualified meetings directly on the right rep's calendar.


Use this as your evaluation checklist. In demos, ask vendors to show each stage running without a human touch. The stage they demo with slides instead of the product is the stage you'll be doing yourself.

Outreach means individually written messages per lead (not templates with merge fields) across email, social, and native dialer call steps, with power dialing and parallel dialing up to three lines, AI answer detection, AI voicemail drop, and automatic area-code matching for true local presence. Deliverability is managed too: secondary mailboxes with automated sending ramp-up, inbox placement testing, and bounce-pattern auto-pause. When replies come in, Ava qualifies, answers objections from a knowledge base, and escalates on rules you write in plain English. Booking puts meetings on the right rep's calendar via CRM owner mapping, with native[Salesforce](https://www.artisan.co/blog/the-best-ai-sdr-for-salesforce-users) and[HubSpot](https://www.artisan.co/blog/the-best-ai-sdr-for-hubspot-users) sync.


Because every stage is native, there's no stitching tax: no separate data contract, sequencer, dialer, and deliverability vendor to integrate. It runs at enterprise scale on the same rails: sales ops operates Ava centrally for large account executive (AE) teams whose reps never log in, governed by escalation rules, a full audit trail, org-level do-not-contact, and field-level CRM controls, and Enterprise plans add single sign-on (SSO), SOC 2 Type II, a forward-deployed strategist, and a dedicated customer success manager (CSM). Raise sourced over $700K of ARR in six months with Ava, 20x its investment. SumUp generates leads at a $52 cost per lead in a local-SMB segment its human team couldn't economically reach.

**Best for:** any team whose bottleneck is outbound pipeline, from founders doing first outbound to fintechs running Ava centrally across a thousand-plus AEs.


-


**Who should pick something else:** teams that only want software for reps to drive (see Apollo), or teams whose whole motion is maximum-volume parallel calling. Ava's parallel dialing runs up to 3 lines with compliance guardrails built in, while dedicated parallel dialers like Nooks and Orum push to more lines, up to 10 on Orum's Ascend.

### 2. AiSDR


**Verdict: the best value full-loop agent for small teams.**


AiSDR covers sourcing through booking with published pricing ($250/month Solo, $900 Explore, $2,500 Scale, per aisdr.com/pricing) plus Ami, a go-to-market (GTM) strategist agent. Y Combinator-backed with a $3M seed. Reply handling is present but lighter-touch than Ava's, and the governance story is thin for enterprise.


-


**Best for:** startups and SMBs that want the whole loop at a fixed monthly price.


-


**Who should pick something else:** enterprises that need CRM depth, audit trails, and data governance.


### 3. Amplemarket (Duo)


**Verdict: strong native data, but Duo assists more than it owns.**


Amplemarket pairs its own contact database with multichannel engagement and the Duo agent, which gives it real sourcing and enrichment coverage. The gap is stages four and five: Duo accelerates reps instead of autonomously closing the loop, so humans still carry replies and much of the booking. Entry pricing sits around $600/month, unpublished.


-


**Best for:** mid-market teams that want one vendor for data plus engagement with AI assistance.


-


**Who should pick something else:** teams that buy an agent to run outbound without a rep driving daily.


### 4. Reply.io (Jason AI)


**Verdict: real reply handling on a sequencer chassis.**


Jason AI actually answers replies and books meetings, which is rarer than it should be, on top of Reply.io's mature multichannel platform. Sourcing leans on add-on data instead of a flagship database. Agent tiers run $500-800/month starter and $1,500-3,000/month growth, per Reply's pricing page.


-


**Best for:** teams with their own lead lists that want an agent to work them end to end.


-


**Who should pick something else:** teams that need the agent to source net-new pipeline from scratch.


### 5. Salesforge (Agent Frank)


**Verdict: the whole loop, one channel, one low price.**


Agent Frank sources, sends, replies, and books autonomously for a flat $499/month, but essentially within email. Platform tiers are $40-80/month. As a single-channel AI BDR it's honest and cheap. It just can't touch phone or social motions.


-


**Best for:** budget-conscious teams whose ideal customer profile (ICP) responds to email.


-


**Who should pick something else:** anyone whose outbound needs calls or social touches to convert.


### 6. Regie.ai (RegieOne)


**Verdict: deliberately not an autonomous BDR, and clear about it.**


Regie's Auto-Pilot agents warm cold accounts while reps work engaged ones inside RegieOne's sales engagement platform with a built-in dialer. Reps own replies and booking by design. Regie's stated philosophy is augmentation. Pricing is reportedly around $180/user/month, or $499/user/month for the Force Multiplier tier. $30M Series B (February 2025).


-


**Best for:** enterprises keeping a human-led cadence motion and adding AI where it's safe.


-


**Who should pick something else:** teams that shop this list for an agent that owns the job. That's explicitly not the product.


### 7. Apollo


**Verdict: the best software for humans doing the BDR job, which is the point.**


Apollo gives a human BDR everything: over 230M contacts, enrichment, sequences, a dialer, and AI assists, at free-$119/user/month. It acquired Pocus in March 2026 and reportedly passed $200M ARR. But the motion is human-run. Apollo's AI drafts and recommends while your team supplies the judgment, sends, replies, and books. It's in this list because many buyers comparing "AI BDRs" are really choosing between an agent and better tooling for people.


-


**Best for:** teams with reps who have capacity and need better, cheaper tooling.


-


**Who should pick something else:** teams that buy headcount-free pipeline. A per-seat toolkit doesn't generate meetings on its own.

Tiers run $94-555/month (sending-only from $47), per instantly.ai. Its AI agent features are new, and replies land in a unified inbox for humans to answer, so as an AI BDR it covers roughly two of five stages.


-


**Best for:** agencies and volume senders who own their targeting and copy strategy.


-


**Who should pick something else:** teams that want outreach answered and meetings booked without staffing the inbox. See our cold email tools guide for that category on its own terms.

## Is an AI BDR the same as an AI SDR?


Vendors blur the titles, but the buying decision differs. "[AI SDR](https://www.artisan.co/blog/ai-sdr) " (sales development representative) has become the umbrella label, covering inbound agents like Qualified's Piper that convert existing website demand and follow-up specialists like Conversica. "AI BDR" is the narrower, harder claim: an agent that generates net-new outbound pipeline end to end, which is the usage Artisan established with Ava. Artisan now spans both sides of the split: Ava Inbound, its AI inbound agent, greets and qualifies website visitors by live chat and books meetings from the same platform that runs outbound, priced separately (talk to sales for inbound pricing). Everything ranked above is outbound-capable. If your gap includes inbound conversion or you want the full category tour, read our companion ranking of the 12 best AI SDRs.


## What does an AI BDR cost in 2026?


Four pricing models dominate, and the model tells you what the vendor believes it's selling:


-


**Flat agent fee:** Salesforge at $499/month, AiSDR at $250-2,500/month. Priced like a subscription employee, predictable but capacity-capped.


-


**Annual-commit platform:** 11x publishes Growth from $3,750/month (billed annually), with Pro and Enterprise custom. A real commitment even though its center of gravity is lower mid-market.


-


**Per-seat software:** Apollo ($49-119/user/month published), Regie (reportedly $180-499/user/month), Instantly ($94-555/month). Priced as tooling for humans, which is what it is.


-


**Usage-based:** Artisan's model. Credits per action performed, no platform fee, free trial, so spend tracks the work actually done instead of seats or a flat retainer ([pricing](https://www.artisan.co/pricing) ).


For context on the alternative, a fully loaded US BDR runs $120K-200K/year, ramps for 3-6 months, and averages roughly 14 months of tenure. Teams using Ava report generating pipeline at about one-fifth the cost of a human BDR, adding capacity alongside the team instead of replacing it.

### Can an AI BDR do the whole outbound job without a human?


The best ones can run the day-to-day loop unsupervised (Artisan reports most customers operate this way), but every serious deployment keeps humans on strategy, ICP definition, and escalated conversations. In practice the AI owns the five mechanical stages while your team reviews a task queue or just takes the booked meetings. Treat any vendor that claims zero human involvement anywhere, ever, as a red flag instead of a feature.


### What is a digital AI BDR agent?


Another label for the same category: an AI agent employed to do business development work. Vendors also say "AI sales agent," "digital worker," or "autonomous BDR." Labels aside, apply the five-stage test. Does it source, enrich, reach out on multiple channels, handle replies, and book meetings on its own? Products that pass all five are rare. Most "digital BDRs" are sequencers with an AI writing layer.


### How is an AI BDR different from a cold email tool?


A cold email tool (Instantly, Smartlead) is stage-three infrastructure: it sends at volume with good deliverability, and you supply the leads, the strategy, and the humans to answer replies. An AI BDR owns the surrounding stages too, from sourcing and enrichment through reply handling and booking. Many teams graduate from cold email tooling to an AI BDR when inbox management, not sending capacity, becomes the bottleneck.
