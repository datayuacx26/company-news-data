## Who is Monaco and why does it attract attention?


Monaco earns its buzz honestly. It's led by Sam Blond, a widely respected go-to-market (GTM) operator, and it's pitched as an AI-native revenue engine rather than a bolt-on assistant. In May 2026 it raised a $50M Series B led by Benchmark, bringing total funding past $85M. This is serious capital and serious backing, and it signals investors believe the category is heading toward AI that runs the motion rather than just augmenting reps. On thesis, Monaco and Artisan agree. For the wider field, see[the best AI GTM platforms](https://www.artisan.co/blog/the-best-ai-gtm-platforms) .


But maturity is the whole story here. Monaco is in public beta. Its pricing is gated and undisclosed, an unspecified flat fee, so you can't evaluate cost without a sales conversation, and beta pricing rarely reflects what a product will charge at scale. There's no meaningful published customer proof yet, because the product is young. None of this is a knock on the team; it's simply where an early-stage product sits. If you like backing a strong founder early and you can absorb the risk of a beta, Monaco is a reasonable bet. If you need pipeline this quarter, a public beta is a hard thing to stake a number on.


## What does Artisan ship today?


Artisan's Ava is a fully deployed autonomous AI BDR, not a beta. She owns the BDR job end to end: she finds best-fit leads across a database of over 400M contacts with natural language processing (NLP) search, ideal customer profile (ICP) filters, and intent signals; enriches and prioritizes them with waterfall email and phone data that charges only on success; launches personalized multichannel campaigns across email, social outreach, and dialer call steps; tests dozens of message variations and shifts volume to what converts; handles every reply by qualifying, answering objections from a knowledge base, and chasing follow-ups; and books meetings on reps' calendars with CRM-owner routing. For how the category compares, see[the best AI BDRs](https://www.artisan.co/blog/the-best-ai-bdrs) and[the best AI SDRs](https://www.artisan.co/blog/the-best-ai-sdrs) .


The autonomy is a dial, not a switch. You can run review-and-approve on every message, objections-only replies, or full autonomy, with plain-language escalation rules ("if the lead mentions pricing, escalate") and a complete audit trail. Artisan reports over 90% of its customers run Ava autonomously. This is not a preview of what the product will do. Paying customers rely on it right now, at scale, including global teams that run Ava centrally from sales ops for account executive (AE) teams that never log in. ## FAQ


### Is Monaco better than Artisan?


Monaco is well-led and well-funded, but it's in public beta with undisclosed pricing and little published proof. Artisan ships a proven autonomous AI BDR today with named customers and attributed results. If you're comfortable backing an early product, Monaco is credible; if you need results now, Artisan is the safer pick.


### Is Monaco available yet?


Monaco is in public beta as of 2026. This means you can access it, but you're working with an early-stage product still being built out, and its pricing is a gated, undisclosed flat fee. Artisan is fully live with usage-based pricing and a free trial.


### How much does Monaco cost?


Monaco's pricing is not published; it's a gated flat fee, and you'd need a sales conversation to learn it. Beta pricing also may not reflect eventual list pricing. Artisan's core platform is usage-based with no platform fee, so you pay for the work performed.

Not yet, because it's early. Artisan publishes named customers and numbers: Raise's over $700K of ARR in six months, SumUp's $52 cost per lead, SaaStr doubling open rates. For a buyer who wants evidence before committing, this track record is the difference.
