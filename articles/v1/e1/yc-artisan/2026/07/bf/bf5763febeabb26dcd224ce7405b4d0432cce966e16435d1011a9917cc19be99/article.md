# The best People Data Labs alternatives in 2026

The best People Data Labs alternative depends on whether you want raw data through an application programming interface (API) or an outcome. If you want records to build with, Clay and ZoomInfo are closest. If you want the data plus an AI that runs the outreach, Artisan is the pick: over 250M B2B professionals, waterfall enrichment, and an AI business development representative (BDR) that books meetings.


People Data Labs (PDL) is an API-first data company. It ships person and company datasets through developer endpoints so teams can embed enrichment into their own products and pipelines. This composability is its whole reason to exist, and for engineering-led teams it is useful. But an API is not an outbound motion. Most teams evaluating PDL are choosing between building on raw data and buying something that already turns data into meetings, and the right alternative sits on one side of this line or the other.

## Why do teams look for a People Data Labs alternative?


A few reasons. Build cost comes first, since PDL gives you raw data and you supply the engineering to clean, match, and activate it, which is fine for a data team and painful for a go-to-market (GTM) team that just wants pipeline. Coverage and freshness matter too, because some teams want a provider whose match rates and recency are easier to evaluate without building test harnesses. And, most often, scope. PDL has no sequencer, no dialer, no reply handling, and no interface a rep logs into. If your goal is meetings instead of a data pipeline, an API is the long way around.


So the alternative you want depends on your builder appetite. Keep building on raw data, orchestrate it with less code, or stop building and buy the motion.

### 2. Clay: Orchestrate raw data with less code


Clay is the best alternative if you want to keep building but write less glue. Where PDL is one API you code against, Clay is a workbench that composes many providers with a spreadsheet-like interface.


It is still infrastructure, not an engine. You, or a GTM engineer whose time runs about $160K a year, design and maintain the workflows, and median contracts land near $30K per year.
