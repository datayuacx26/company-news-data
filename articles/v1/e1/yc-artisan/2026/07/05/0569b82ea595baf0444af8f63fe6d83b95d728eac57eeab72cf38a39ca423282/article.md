# 11x pricing and reviews (2026)

11x now publishes a starting price: its Growth plan begins at $3,750 per month billed annually, roughly $45K a year, with Pro and Enterprise quoted custom. That buys Alice, its[AI SDR](https://www.artisan.co/blog/ai-sdr) (sales development representative), plus optional access to Julian, its AI phone agent. Reviewers report strong multichannel automation but recurring deliverability and CRM-sync friction, and the company carries a bumpy public record.


## How much does 11x cost in 2026?


11x's Growth plan starts at $3,750 per month, billed annually, which comes to about $45K for the year. Pro and Enterprise are not published; the company quotes them based on volume and team size, so you request pricing through sales. For most of its history 11x kept all pricing gated at a reported figure near $5K per month, so the public Growth number is a recent change.

## What does 11x actually cost in practice?


The sticker price is just the floor. Because Growth is billed annually, the real commitment is a full year, paid up front or on an annual contract. Buyers weighing 11x should account for a few things beyond the base fee.


First, there is onboarding. Reviewers report that making Alice productive takes setup work, so the first several weeks go to ramp-up before pipeline appears. Second, there is the phone agent. Julian is a separate capability, and if calling is part of your motion, expect that to change the quote. Third, there is data and volume. An AI SDR is only as good as the contacts and signals feeding it, and higher volumes push you toward the custom tiers. Fourth, there is the annual lock-in itself. A yearly commitment on a category that is still maturing carries more risk than a month-to-month tool, which is worth weighing if you are testing whether an AI SDR fits your team at all.


## What do reviewers say about 11x?


Reviewers report a split picture. On the positive side, they credit 11x with genuine multichannel automation: Alice can research prospects, draft outbound across email and social channels, and keep sequences running with little day-to-day input, and Julian extends that into AI phone calls. Teams that want an agent to own the mechanical parts of outbound tend to like the breadth.


The complaints cluster around execution. Reviewers report deliverability problems, where sends do not land as expected, and CRM-sync issues, where records and activity do not flow cleanly back into Salesforce or HubSpot. Several note that message quality needs oversight and that the tool rewards a hands-on operator rather than running fully hands-off. Setup time comes up often.


The company's public record is part of any honest review. TechCrunch reported on March 24, 2025 that 11x had listed customers it could not support, a story worth reading before you sign. Weeks later, on May 5, 2025, founder-CEO Hasan Sukkar stepped down to a non-executive chairman role and CTO Prabhav Jain became CEO. Leadership changes are normal in fast-moving startups, but paired with the reporting, they are context a buyer should have.


## Who is 11x best for, and who should look elsewhere?


11x fits a lower mid-market team that wants a multichannel AI SDR and is comfortable signing an annual contract sold through a sales conversation. If Alice and Julian match your channel mix and you have someone to manage deliverability and CRM hygiene, the platform can automate a lot of outbound motion.


It is a weaker fit if you need self-serve, month-to-month flexibility, or a light footprint to test the category before committing. Despite the enterprise-style contracts, 11x is not an enterprise governance platform; buyers who need deep audit trails, granular autonomy controls, and a hardened security posture should scrutinize those areas closely. And any team burned by deliverability or CRM-sync issues in the past should ask pointed questions and request a proof-of-concept before the annual signature.
