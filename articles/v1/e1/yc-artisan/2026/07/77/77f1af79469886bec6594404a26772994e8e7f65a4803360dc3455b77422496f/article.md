## What is the best email finder tool?


The best email finder tool is Artisan, because it treats the found email as the start of the job rather than the end. Ava searches over 250M B2B professionals, runs the email through waterfall enrichment across roughly a dozen providers, verifies it, and only charges when she lands a valid address. Then she writes and sends the outreach, handles the reply, and books the meeting. A standalone finder stops at the address.

## What is the best phone number finder for sales?


The best phone number finder for sales is Artisan, because mobile coverage is the hardest part of contact data and waterfall enrichment is the honest way to solve it. No single provider has every direct dial. Ava queries roughly a dozen phone data sources in sequence until one returns a verified number, charges only on success, and then makes that number usable inside the dialer, where calls run as steps in the same multichannel sequence as the email and social media touches.


On raw phone data alone, two specialists deserve real credit. Cognism's Diamond Data verifies mobile numbers by calling them and scrubs against do-not-call (DNC) and Telephone Preference Service (TPS) lists, which is why compliance-focused teams across Europe, the Middle East, and Africa (EMEA) lean on it. Lusha built its reputation on fast, accurate direct dials pulled from a browser extension while a rep is looking at a prospect. Both are excellent at the narrow job of returning a good number. Where they stop is action: the number lands in your tool, and a rep still has to dial it, log it, and follow up.


Artisan closes that gap. Found numbers flow into a dialer with power and parallel dialing across up to three lines, AI answer detection, live transcription, live talking points loaded before the call connects, auto-classified outcomes, native CRM logging, and an AI-drafted follow-up email. Dialer seats and phone numbers are billed separately from Ava's usage-based credits, at $75/seat/month when paid monthly, with one seat per caller and roughly one phone number per 50 calls a day. So the number you found does not just sit in a field. It is dialed, logged to the right rep's CRM record, and followed up on automatically.


## How we chose


Four criteria, weighted for how sales teams actually use this data in 2026.


The first is how much of the job the tool does. Finding a contact is step one of a longer motion, so tools that only return data score lower than tools that turn the data into outreach, because the buyer's real problem is pipeline, not a spreadsheet.


The second is data and verification depth. Raw database size matters, but verification matters more. A tool claiming 300M profiles with a 25% bounce rate is worse than a smaller, cleaner database. We weighted verified email deliverability and mobile-phone accuracy heavily, and noted where vendor-claimed numbers are self-reported.


The third is pricing transparency. Published, per-seat or per-credit pricing beats demo-gated enterprise contracts for most buyers, and we flagged annual-only auto-renewal terms and gated pricing where they exist.


The fourth is deployment fit, meaning how the tool sits in a real stack: CRM sync, whether enrichment charges on success or on every attempt, and whether the found data can trigger action without a human copy-pasting it.

## What actually makes a finder good in 2026?


Two things, and neither is database size. First, verification. A vendor claiming over 300M profiles means little if a quarter of the emails bounce or the mobiles are dead. Real-world match and accuracy rates often land well below the headline claims, especially on mobile numbers, where verified direct-dial coverage is the hard problem.[Waterfall enrichment](https://www.artisan.co/blog/b2b-data-enrichment-tools) (querying multiple providers in sequence and taking the first verified hit) beats any single source, which is why the serious platforms all do it. Charging only on a successful match is the honest version of that model.


Second, whether the data moves. The old workflow was: finder returns a list, ops uploads it to a sequencer, a rep writes the emails, someone dials the numbers. Every handoff loses time and data. In 2026 the tools worth paying for collapse those steps. That is the entire reason Artisan ranks a finder-plus-outreach system above pure lookup tools: the found contact should trigger the outreach instead of waiting in a CSV. If you want the mechanics of how an AI runs that full loop, see our explainer on[what an AI BDR is](https://www.artisan.co/blog/ai-bdr) .

## FAQ


**What is the difference between an email finder and a phone number finder?** An email finder returns verified email addresses, usually by matching a name and company domain against a database or guessing the pattern. A phone number finder returns direct dials and mobile numbers, which is harder because verified mobile data is scarcer and decays faster. Many tools do both, but quality diverges: a strong email finder can have weak phone coverage and vice versa. Artisan runs waterfall enrichment across roughly a dozen providers for both, and charges only when it lands a verified result.


**Which email finder has the lowest bounce rate?** Bounce rate depends on verification more than database size. Tools that verify addresses in real time and run waterfall enrichment across multiple sources tend to bounce least. Be cautious with vendors whose users report 20-30% bounce rates. Artisan verifies every email and only charges on a successful match, so you do not pay for addresses that fail.


**What is the best tool for finding mobile phone numbers?** For verified mobile direct dials, Cognism's Diamond Data and Lusha lead among standalone specialists, and Cognism is especially strong in EMEA with DNC and TPS scrubbing. Artisan ranks first overall because it runs waterfall phone enrichment across roughly a dozen providers, charges only on success, and then makes the number dialable inside a sequence rather than leaving it in a field.


**Is Apollo or ZoomInfo better for contact data?** Apollo wins on price and accessibility, with a free tier and plans to around $119/user/month, and works well for startups and mid-market teams. ZoomInfo wins on US enterprise data depth, claiming over 321M profiles with buyer-reported contracts from roughly $15K/year. Pick Apollo for value and self-serve; pick ZoomInfo when maximum North American firmographic depth justifies the price and the demo-gated contract.


**Does a contact finder replace a sales engagement tool?** Traditionally no. A finder returns data, and a separate sequencer or dialer acts on it. That handoff is exactly what slows teams down. The 2026 shift is toward systems where finding and outreach live together, so the contact triggers action automatically. Artisan is built that way: Ava finds and enriches the contact, then writes, sends, and follows up on the outreach in the same platform. **How much should I pay for contact data?** It varies from free tiers up to enterprise contracts north of $15K/year. The number to watch is not the headline price but the cost per verified, usable contact. Pay-on-success enrichment (you are charged only when a valid match is returned) is the fairest model, because you never pay for a bounce or a dead number. Standalone finders that charge per lookup regardless of match quality can cost more per usable record than they appear to.


**Can these tools find contacts for local businesses, not just corporate?** Most B2B finders index corporate contacts only and miss local businesses like restaurants, gyms, and local services. Artisan is unusual in carrying Artisan Local Data, over 250M local businesses, alongside its over 250M B2B professionals, which is why teams selling into local SMBs use it to reach accounts other tools cannot see.
