## Why do teams look for a Cognism alternative?


Three reasons show up most. The first is price and packaging, since Cognism's pricing is gated and reported in the roughly $15-50K per year range, which is a lot for a team that mainly needs data. The second is scope, because Cognism is a data provider, so you still need a sequencer, a dialer, and someone to run replies. The third is trust in how the data is sourced. Its Kaspr subsidiary drew a 240,000 euro CNIL fine in December 2024, which some EMEA buyers now factor into diligence.


None of that erases Cognism's strengths. It just means the right alternative depends on what you are actually missing: cheaper data, more US coverage, developer-grade access, or a system that turns the data into meetings without more headcount.

### 2. ZoomInfo: Deepest US enterprise data


ZoomInfo is the closest like-for-like on data ambition, especially in the US. Now repositioned as a go-to-market intelligence platform trading under the ticker $GTM, it claims over 321M profiles (a vendor figure) and layers on WebSights visitor identification and Scoops. For a large US enterprise that treats data as the foundation of everything else, ZoomInfo is still the incumbent reference point.


The trade-offs are familiar. It is a data platform first, so execution features are add-ons rather than the core. Buyer-reported entry contracts sit at over $15K per year, and its contract terms have a reputation worth diligencing.


-


**Best for:** US enterprise teams that need the deepest data and intent layer and will build execution around it


-


**Who should pick something else:** EMEA-first teams (Cognism's home turf) or anyone who wants the outreach run for them


-


**Pricing:** over $15K per year reported, gated. Confirm current terms with ZoomInfo


### 3. Apollo: Value all-in-one


Apollo is the budget-friendly all-rounder and the most common first switch from Cognism for cost reasons. It claims over 230M contacts, is used by over 600,000 companies (over 100,000 paying), and publishes pricing from free to $119/user/month. It acquired Pocus in March 2026 and has reported annual recurring revenue (ARR) around $200M. It bundles data, sequencing, and assistive AI in one accessible package.


The catch is that the AI is assistive, the model is per-seat, and larger orgs tend to treat Apollo as a data layer rather than the engine that runs outbound. Coverage is strong but US-centered; EMEA depth is not its calling card. ### 4. Clay: Enrichment orchestration


Clay is the go-to-market (GTM) engineering anchor and a strong answer if your problem is enrichment quality rather than a single source. It orchestrates over 150 enrichment providers with Claygent agents, publishes tiers from $167 to $446 per month, and reached a $3.1B valuation in August 2025. If you want a waterfall you control provider by provider, Clay is the deepest option for that.


But Clay is infrastructure, not an outbound engine. It gives you the workbench; you still need the worker, usually a skilled GTM operator whose time runs about $160K a year. Median contracts land near $30K per year once you account for credits and headcount. See how it compares in[Artisan vs. Clay](https://www.artisan.co/blog/artisan-vs-clay) .


-


**Best for:** RevOps and GTM engineers who want to design their own enrichment and data workflows


-


**Who should pick something else:** teams that want outcomes out of the box instead of a system to build and maintain


-


**Pricing:** $167-446/month public tiers; median contracts around $30K/year


### 5. People Data Labs: API-first data


People Data Labs is the pick when your consumers are developers, not reps. It exposes person and company datasets through an API so you can build enrichment directly into your product or internal pipelines. For platforms embedding contact data or data teams standardizing records at scale, that composability is the whole point.


The flip side is that PDL ships raw data without a workflow around it. There is no sequencer, no dialer, no reply handling. You are building the activation layer yourself, which is the opposite of what a Cognism buyer who wants meetings is looking for.


-


**Best for:** engineering and data teams building enrichment into their own products or pipelines


-


**Who should pick something else:** sales teams that want ready-to-use records and outreach, not an API


-


**Pricing:** usage-based API tiers


### 6. Lusha: Self-serve contact data


Lusha is the lightweight, self-serve alternative for individual reps who want fast contact lookups without a big contract. It is quick to adopt and browser-friendly, which makes it popular with small teams that just need a phone number or email in the moment.


It is narrower than Cognism on compliance and depth, and its credit-based model draws recurring complaints from users who feel they burn credits faster than expected. For a rep, it is convenient; for a RevOps leader standardizing an EMEA motion, it falls short. ## What is the best enterprise alternative to Cognism?


For enterprise, the honest split is between depth of data and ownership of the motion. ZoomInfo wins on raw US data depth and is the safe pick if your stack is built to consume data and act on it separately. Artisan wins when the enterprise goal is fewer tools and more pipeline per head: central-ops deployment, a governed autonomy dial, audit trail, SOC 2 Type II, and costs that track the work performed instead of headcount. Typical enterprise outbound stacks run five to eight tools before headcount, so consolidating data plus activation into one platform is the enterprise argument. If you specifically need EMEA phone verification above all, that is the one place to keep Cognism in the conversation.


## Stay with Cognism if...


Some teams should not switch. Stay with Cognism if EMEA is your primary market and verified mobile coverage there is the make-or-break input, since Diamond Data and its DNC and TPS scrubbing are hard to match. Stay if your compliance team has already blessed Cognism's processing and a re-review would cost more than the tool. And stay if you only need a clean data feed into an existing sequencer and dialer that already work. The case to leave strengthens when you are also paying for the tools that act on the data, or when US coverage and workflow matter more than EMEA phone depth.
