## How we chose


We weighed four criteria against how Relevance AI buyers actually work. First, whether the tool ships a working outcome or asks you to build one, since the build burden is the reason most sales teams are shopping. Second, how much of the outbound job it covers out of the box. Third, pricing transparency, since Relevance uses credit-based freemium and buyers want to know real costs. Fourth, deployment fit from a lean team through a large sales org with governance needs.


## Why do people look for a Relevance AI alternative?


They look because Relevance AI is a builder, and building an agent is a project. Relevance is flexible: you can compose agents from tools, prompts, and data to automate all sorts of workflows, and the Bosh sales agent demonstrates the pattern for outbound. For a technical team that wants to construct exactly the agent it imagines, this flexibility is the whole appeal, and the platform is well-funded at roughly $37M raised.


The friction is that flexibility means responsibility. You design the agent, wire up the data and integrations, write the logic, test it, and maintain it as things change. For a sales leader who wanted a working AI BDR, this is a build project, not a purchase. A generic agent framework also does not ship with sales-grade data, deliverability infrastructure, a dialer, reply handling, and CRM routing already solved; you assemble those or connect them. Teams that expected an outcome and received a toolkit go looking for something pre-built. The alternatives below deliver the outbound job as a product you configure, not a system you engineer.
