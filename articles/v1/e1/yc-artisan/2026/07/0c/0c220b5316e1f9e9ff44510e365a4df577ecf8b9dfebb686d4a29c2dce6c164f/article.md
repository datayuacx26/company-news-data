# AiSDR vs. Alta: Which should you choose in 2026?

AiSDR and Alta are both[AI SDR](https://www.artisan.co/blog/ai-sdr) tools, software that stands in for a sales development representative (SDR) and runs outbound for you. AiSDR is the more transparent and established of the two, with published pricing from around $250/month and a fast, self-serve setup. Alta is newer, raised a $25M Series A in July 2026, and claims a broader "AI GTM System of Actions" scope on a thin track record.


In short, AiSDR wins on price transparency and speed to launch, while Alta appeals if you want a newer platform with a wider "system of actions" scope and its security claims fit your checklist. Here is the honest, scenario-by-scenario breakdown.

## What is the core difference between AiSDR and Alta?


Transparency and maturity versus scope and newness. AiSDR sells a clear, fast-to-start AI SDR: read the price, sign up, and be sending quickly, with outbound and inbound covered and Ami shaping strategy. It is proven and easy to trial. Alta is a younger company that raised its Series A in July 2026 and frames itself more broadly as a "GTM System of Actions," with Katie as its agent and AI calling in the mix.


For a buyer, AiSDR is the lower-risk, lower-friction way to test whether an AI SDR earns its place. Alta asks you to bet a little more on an earlier product with a wider ambition. The right choice depends on whether you want something proven and transparent now, or a newer platform you are willing to grow with.


## How do AiSDR and Alta price?


AiSDR is the transparent one. Its pricing page lists tiers at roughly $250, $900, and $2,500/month, so you can size a plan without a sales call and defend a budget internally. This openness helps when you are comparing tools or watching spend. For the full breakdown, see[AiSDR pricing and reviews](https://www.artisan.co/blog/aisdr-pricing-and-reviews) .


Alta's pricing is gated. It sells on a "pay for what you use" basis quoted by team size, and routes buyers to a conversation rather than a self-serve tier. If published, predictable pricing matters to you, AiSDR is the clearer choice here. For teams that do not mind a sales process, Alta may have room to negotiate, but you trade away the certainty of a listed price. For the full breakdown, see[Alta pricing and reviews](https://www.artisan.co/blog/alta-pricing-and-reviews) .


## Which is better for a team that wants to launch fast?


AiSDR. Its whole design favors speed: transparent tiers, a largely self-serve setup, and outbound plus inbound coverage out of the box. A small or mid-market team can start near $250/month, watch reply rates for a few weeks, and expand or leave with minimal friction.


Alta can also start you sending, but as a newer product with a guided motion and less published pricing, its on-ramp is less predictable. If your priority is a fast, low-commitment start, AiSDR fits this better. If you are less time-pressured and more interested in Alta's broader scope, the extra setup consideration may be worth it.


## Which handles channels and calling better?


Alta leans harder into calling as part of its pitch. It offers AI calling and positions Katie across a wider set of GTM actions, so voice is part of the story from the start. AiSDR is email-led, covers outbound and inbound with Ami, and is a strong email engine that does not pitch a voice agent as a headline.


Match this to your motion. If a phone channel is central and you want it native to the platform, Alta's calling focus is worth a look, though it is the younger product. If your pipeline comes from email with inbound support, AiSDR covers this well and has the longer record, and you avoid paying for a voice channel you may not use.


## Which is more established and lower risk?


AiSDR. It has a longer track record, a proven self-serve motion, and no notable public scrutiny, though its center of gravity sits in SMB and lower mid-market buyers with a thinner enterprise governance story. This is a known, ordinary limitation rather than a red flag. If AiSDR is not the fit, see[the best AiSDR alternatives](https://www.artisan.co/blog/the-best-aisdr-alternatives) .


Alta is earlier. It raised its $25M Series A in July 2026 and claims SOC 2 and ISO 27001 compliance on its site, which are meaningful if verified during procurement. The honest limitation is a thin history: a young product has less evidence behind it, so references, security documentation, and a trial carry more weight before you commit. If you want the safer, more proven pick today, AiSDR is it.

## FAQ


### Is AiSDR better than Alta?


For most teams shopping today, AiSDR is the safer pick: transparent pricing from around $250/month, fast self-serve setup, and a more established track record. Alta is newer, with a broader "AI GTM System of Actions" scope and SOC 2 and ISO 27001 claims, but a thinner history. Choose Alta only if its scope and security claims outweigh AiSDR's transparency and maturity.


### How much do AiSDR and Alta cost?


AiSDR publishes tiers around $250, $900, and $2,500/month on its pricing page. Alta publishes almost no pricing and typically requires a sales conversation. If cost transparency matters, AiSDR is the clearer choice.


### Which is newer, AiSDR or Alta?


Alta is the newer company and closed a $25M Series A in July 2026. AiSDR has a longer track record and a proven self-serve motion. This makes AiSDR the more established option, with Alta the earlier, less proven one.


### Does Alta do cold calling?


Yes. Alta offers AI calling and positions Katie across a range of GTM actions, so voice is part of its pitch. AiSDR is email-led, covering outbound and inbound with the Ami strategist agent. ### Are AiSDR and Alta enterprise-ready?


Both sit below enterprise in their center of gravity. AiSDR is weighted toward SMB and mid-market buyers with a thinner enterprise governance story. Alta claims SOC 2 and ISO 27001 but is young enough that large teams should validate references and security documentation carefully before committing.
