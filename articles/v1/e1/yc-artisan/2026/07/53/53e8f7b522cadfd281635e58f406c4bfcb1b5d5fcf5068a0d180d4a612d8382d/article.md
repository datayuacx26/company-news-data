# Apollo vs. Outreach: Which should you choose in 2026?

Apollo and Outreach both run sales sequences, but they answer different problems. Apollo is an affordable all-in-one that bundles a large contact database with sequencing and light dialing, best for teams that are assembling their first stack. Outreach is an enterprise sequencing platform with deep workflow governance, best for large sales orgs that already own their data.


The two invite comparison because their sequencing features overlap. Underneath this, they sit in different categories. Apollo starts from data and adds cadence on top. Outreach starts from cadence governance and expects you to bring data. Which one is right turns on where your team is in its growth, how disciplined your process needs to be, and whether you want to buy leads and outreach in one place. Here is the honest comparison.

## What is the core difference between Apollo and Outreach?


Apollo is a data-first all-in-one. Its center of gravity is the contact database, and the sequencer, light dialing, and enrichment are built around moving those contacts into outreach cheaply. This is why smaller teams reach for it as a first stack: leads and cadence in one bill, often starting free.


Outreach is a cadence-first enterprise platform. It assumes you already have a data source and a defined sales process, and it exists to run this process at scale with control. Sequence management, team permissions, step-level analytics, and reporting are the product. Many enterprises run Outreach on top of a separate data provider precisely because Outreach is the execution and governance layer, not the data layer.


## Which has better data and prospecting?


Apollo, clearly, because a native database is its whole premise. It claims over 230M contacts and is used by more than 600,000 companies per its own figures, and its March 2026 acquisition of Pocus added signal-based selling to the prospecting side. For a team that wants to search for leads, enrich them, and drop them into a sequence in one tool, Apollo does this at a price point Outreach does not try to match. Outreach does not ship a prospecting database. It is designed to sequence contacts you supply from a CRM or a data vendor. This is not a weakness in its category; it is a deliberate scope. ## Which is better for enterprise sequencing and governance?


Outreach. Its reputation is built on workflow discipline for large teams: granular sequence controls, role and team permissions, approval structures, and reporting that revenue operations leaders can actually govern. When a sales org has dozens or hundreds of reps and needs consistent process enforcement, Outreach is the more mature answer, and reported pricing around $100 to $165/user/month reflects that positioning.


Apollo can run sequences at scale too, and plenty of mid-market teams do. The difference shows up in governance depth and how enterprises treat the tool. Larger buyers often use Apollo as a data layer that feeds a more controlled execution platform, rather than as the system of record for a disciplined outbound process. If tight process control across a big team is the priority, Outreach earns its premium.


## What about AI features?


Both added AI, and both kept a human in the driver's seat. Apollo's AI is assistive: it helps write and research, and Pocus brings signal-based selling into the prospecting flow after the March 2026 acquisition. Outreach introduced its Omni agent in April 2026 to pull more AI into sequencing and rep workflows.


The shared limit is the model. In both tools a rep still owns the motion. The AI drafts and suggests, but a person decides, sends, and follows up. Neither is trying to run the outbound job on its own, which matters if what you actually want is fewer manual steps rather than better manual steps.


## How do Apollo and Outreach compare on price?


Apollo is the cheaper entry by a wide margin. It runs from free to about $119/user/month per its pricing page, which is why first-stack teams gravitate to it. This number covers data, sequencing, and light dialing.


Outreach is an enterprise purchase. Reported pricing sits around $100 to $165/user/month, sold through a sales process rather than self-serve, and this does not include the separate data source most Outreach customers run alongside it. So the real stack cost for an Outreach shop is the seat price plus a data vendor, where Apollo folds both into one lower bill. Price alone favors Apollo; governance and scale are what justify Outreach.

## Frequently asked questions


### Is Apollo or Outreach better for a small team?


Apollo, in most cases. It bundles a contact database with sequencing and light dialing from free to about $119/user/month, so a small team covers leads and cadence in one affordable tool without buying a separate data vendor. Outreach is built for large, disciplined orgs and priced accordingly at a reported $100 to $165/user/month, with data supplied separately, which is usually more platform than a small team needs.


### Does Outreach include a contact database like Apollo?


No. Outreach is a cadence and governance platform that expects you to bring contacts from a CRM or a data provider. Apollo's whole premise is the native database, claimed at over 230M contacts. If having leads inside the same tool matters to you, this is a real point for Apollo; if you already have a strong data source, Outreach's execution layer is the reason to choose it.
