# The best Conversica alternatives in 2026

The best Conversica alternative for most teams is Artisan, because it runs the whole outbound job autonomously instead of nudging leads you already have. Conversica helped invent the AI sales assistant back in 2007, and it still does lead follow-up well. The gap is scope. It responds and re-engages; it does not source, prospect, and run modern multichannel outbound.


That distinction is why teams go shopping. Conversica's assistant excels at chasing known contacts, marketing qualified leads (MQLs), and dormant records with polite persistence. It was not built to find net-new best-fit accounts, enrich them, write personalized cold outreach, and book meetings without a rep steering. The alternatives below close this gap from different angles.

## How we chose


We weighed four things against how Conversica buyers actually work. First, how much of the job the tool does on its own, since Conversica's assistant ceiling is the reason most people are shopping. Second, whether it sources net-new pipeline or only works existing lists, which is Conversica's core limit. Third, pricing transparency, given Conversica's entry is gated and enterprise-priced. Fourth, deployment fit from a lean team through a large sales org with governance needs.


## Why do people look for a Conversica alternative?


They look because Conversica is an assistant, and an assistant works the leads you feed it. Conversica pioneered conversational AI for sales in 2007 and still nurtures inbound and dormant contacts with steady, human-sounding follow-up. In automotive and higher education, where the job is re-engaging huge lists, that is real value, and the tenure means the follow-up flows are mature.


The ceiling is scope. Conversica does not prospect. It does not build a net-new target list from an ideal customer profile (ICP), run waterfall enrichment, write cold outreach across channels, or optimize a campaign the way a modern outbound platform does. It picks up where your list already exists and re-engages it. Teams that expected an AI to generate pipeline, not just revive it, hit this wall. Add gated, enterprise-style entry pricing, and the search for something that owns more of the motion begins. The alternatives below either run outbound as an autonomous job or give you a modern agent for less commitment. ### 3. Amplemarket: Data and a copilot in one bundle


Amplemarket suits teams that want modern data and engagement with an AI copilot, rather than a nurture assistant. It combines a contact database, multichannel outreach, and the Duo copilot, with a real emphasis on signal-based selling. Entry is reported around $600 a month, though it is not publicly listed. The trade is that Duo assists rather than owns. A human still decides, approves, sends, and works every reply, so output per rep rises but pipeline stays tied to headcount. It is a step up from Conversica in prospecting breadth, but it does not remove the rep from the loop the way an autonomous BDR does.

### 4. Reply.io: An accessible agent on a sequencer


Reply.io is the low-friction way to add an email agent without a gated enterprise contract. Its Jason AI agent sits on top of native sequencing, so you can start with classic cadences from about $59 a month and layer the AI SDR on for roughly $500 to $800 a month. For a team that wants to test agent-run email after living inside Conversica's assistant, it is an easy on-ramp.


The constraint is that it is an email-centric agent on a traditional sequencer. The deep multichannel sourcing, autonomous reply handling, and enterprise governance of a purpose-built AI BDR are not the core. It is a modern agent for email specifically, not a full owner of the outbound job.


-


**Best for:** teams that want an affordable email agent bolted onto a sequencer they understand.

**Who should pick something else:** teams that want multichannel, fully autonomous outbound should choose Artisan.
