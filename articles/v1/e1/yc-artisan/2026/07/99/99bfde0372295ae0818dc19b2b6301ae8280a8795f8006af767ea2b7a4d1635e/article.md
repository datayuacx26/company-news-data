# AI SDR vs human SDR: Which should you hire?

Hire an[AI SDR](https://www.artisan.co/blog/ai-sdr) (sales development representative) for consistent, high-volume top-of-funnel work (sourcing, research, personalized outreach, first-line replies) and a human SDR for complex, relationship-led deals and live discovery. Most teams should not choose one; they should pair them, letting the AI remove the grunt work so human reps spend their time selling. The AI augments the team; it does not replace it.


Here is the honest both-sides breakdown, including where a human clearly wins.

## Where does an AI SDR win?


An AI SDR wins on the parts of the job that are volume, consistency, and speed, the parts that make human reps miserable and cost the most in wasted six-figure salary.


-


**Volume and coverage.** It sources from a database of over 270M contacts, enriches and prioritizes, and runs personalized multichannel campaigns across far more accounts than a person can touch.


-


**Personalization at scale.** Marketo-scale with a one-to-one feel: two people in the same campaign receive completely different messages, not a name-and-title swap.


-


**Consistency.** No bad days, no ramp, no forgetting the follow-up. It replies to every inbound, qualifies, answers objections from a knowledge base, and chases follow-ups on schedule.


-


**Cost and scale economics.** Roughly one-fifth the cost of a human BDR, usage-based, flat as the team grows. SumUp reported a $52 cost per lead; CookUnity about $45.


-


**Removing the busywork.** SDRs spend 40-60% of hours on non-selling tasks and 66% of BDR time on outreach mechanics. That is exactly what the AI absorbs.

## Where does a human SDR win?


This is the part vendors skip, so let's be direct. A human SDR wins wherever the deal depends on a person.


-


**Complex, high-consideration deals.** Multi-stakeholder enterprise sales where the early relationship is the wedge. A human reads the room; the AI does not.


-


**Live discovery and objection handling.** Real-time conversation, reading tone, improvising, and building trust on a call are human strengths.


-


**Creative and strategic judgment.** Deciding which accounts to chase, reworking a broken message strategy, spotting a non-obvious angle. AI optimizes within a strategy; humans set it.


-


**Long-term relationships.** Champions, referrals, and the trust that closes and renews deals live with people.


If your motion is a handful of large, relationship-led deals a quarter, then a human SDR, or an account executive (AE), is the right hire, and an AI SDR is a support tool, not the driver.


## AI BDR vs human BDR: Is it any different?


No. BDR (business development representative) and SDR describe the same function under different titles (BDR often skews to net-new outbound, SDR to inbound qualification, but the split varies by company), and the AI-vs-human tradeoffs are identical. An AI BDR excels at net-new sourcing and personalized outbound at volume; a human BDR excels at the deals and conversations that need a person. The decision framework below applies to both terms. ## Should you hire an AI SDR or a human SDR? A decision framework


Ask three questions:


1. **What is your deal shape?** High-volume, transactional, or product-led? Lean AI-first. Large, complex, relationship-led? Keep humans central and use AI for support.


2. **Where is your team's time going?** If reps spend 40-60% of their time on list building, research, and sending, you are paying six figures for busywork. Hand that to AI and redeploy the humans to selling.


3. **What are you scaling?** Need 3x the top-of-funnel volume? Adding three human BDRs costs $360K-600K plus ramp and turnover. An AI BDR scales volume without the seat multiplier.


For most teams the answer is both, sequenced right: the AI owns sourcing, outreach, and first-line replies; humans own qualified conversations and closing. ## FAQ


### AI SDR vs human SDR: Which is better?


Neither is universally better; they are better at different things. An AI SDR wins on volume, personalization at scale, consistency, and cost (roughly one-fifth of a human's). A human SDR wins on complex deals, live discovery, rapport, and strategy. For most teams the strongest setup pairs them: AI owns top-of-funnel, humans own relationships and closing.


### AI BDR vs human BDR: Which is better?


Same answer as AI vs human SDR, since BDR and SDR describe the same job. An AI BDR is better for high-volume net-new sourcing and personalized outbound; a human BDR is better for relationship-led, multi-stakeholder deals. The best result usually comes from augmentation: the AI handles the mechanical 40-60% of the work, and people handle the conversations that need judgment.


### Should I replace my SDRs with AI?


Usually not outright. The stronger move is to remove the busywork (list building, research, sending, first-line replies) with AI so your reps spend their time selling and closing. AI amplifies a good team; it does not substitute for human judgment on complex deals. Replacing wholesale only makes sense for purely transactional, high-volume motions, and even then humans typically handle qualified conversations.


### Is an AI SDR cheaper than a human SDR?


Yes, for top-of-funnel work. A human SDR costs $120K-200K fully loaded per year with a 3-6 month ramp and about 14-month average tenure. An AI SDR generates pipeline at about one-fifth the cost, usage-based, with no ramp or turnover. But cost is only decisive where the work is volume-driven; for relationship-led deals the human's higher cost buys something the AI cannot.


### Can an AI SDR and human SDR work together?


Yes, and that is the recommended model. The AI sources, enriches, personalizes, sends, and handles first-line replies, then books qualified meetings onto reps' calendars by CRM ownership. Humans take those conversations, run discovery, handle complex objections, and close. The AI removes the 40-60% of time reps waste on busywork so they focus on selling.


### What can a human SDR do that an AI SDR cannot?


Read a room on a live call, build genuine rapport and long-term relationships, improvise through complex multi-stakeholder deals, and set strategy (which accounts to pursue, when to change the approach). AI executes and optimizes within a defined strategy and excels at volume and consistency, but the creative and relational judgment that closes big deals remains human.
