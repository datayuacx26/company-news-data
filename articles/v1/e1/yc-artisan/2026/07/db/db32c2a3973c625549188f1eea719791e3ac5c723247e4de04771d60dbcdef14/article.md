# The best Artisan alternatives in 2026

If you are comparing Artisan to alternatives, the honest shortlist is AiSDR, Apollo, Clay, Amplemarket, Regie.ai, and 11x. Each is good at a slice of the job. The reason most teams that evaluate this stay with Artisan is that its AI BDR (business development representative) Ava owns the whole outbound motion, inbound included, on one platform rather than one step of it.


## Why do people look for Artisan alternatives?


It usually comes down to one of three reasons: budget scoping, stack politics, or wanting a single narrow feature. Some teams want a cheaper published entry price and land on AiSDR or Apollo. Some already run Clay or a data tool and want to see whether they can stitch a motion together themselves. Others are handed a mandate to stay inside Salesforce or HubSpot and go looking for a native agent. All of these are fair reasons to shop around, and this page treats every alternative on its merits. For CRM-specific picks, see[the best AI SDR for HubSpot users](https://www.artisan.co/blog/the-best-ai-sdr-for-hubspot-users) and[the best AI SDR for Salesforce users](https://www.artisan.co/blog/the-best-ai-sdr-for-salesforce-users) .


The pattern worth naming is that most of these tools solve a piece of the outbound job, not the job. A data provider gives you contacts but not the outreach, a sequencer sends emails but does not source or reply, and a copilot makes a rep faster without removing the work. Artisan's pitch is the opposite: one AI employee owns the loop from finding a lead to booking a meeting, as hands-off or hands-on as you set it. If you want this, the alternatives below give something up to get their strength. This page says exactly what.

## How we chose


Four tests, in order. How much of the job the AI actually does (a full BDR, or one slice like drafting or dialing). Data and signal depth (can it source net-new leads, not just work a list you own). Pricing transparency (published and usage-based, or gated and per-seat). And deployment fit (does it carry the governance, security, and CRM depth a large team needs). Every entry receives a best-for verdict and a who-should-pick-else line, Artisan included, because a page that only flatters one tool does not earn anyone's trust.

## 2. AiSDR: Transparent pricing, fast setup


AiSDR is the cleanest alternative if what you want is a published price and a fast start. Its tiers are public ($250, $900, and $2,500 a month per its site), setup is quick, and it covers outbound and inbound with an "Ami" strategist agent. For an SMB or lower mid-market team that wants to see the number, sign up, and go, this transparency is the appeal, and it is a real one.


What it gives up is depth at scale. AiSDR's center of gravity is SMB, and the enterprise governance story (deep CRM controls, central-ops deployment for large account executive (AE) teams, audit depth) is thinner than a big org needs. It is an honest tool a growing team can outgrow.


**Best for:** SMB and lower mid-market teams that value transparent pricing and speed. **Who should pick something else:** large orgs that need central ops, deep CRM controls, and a heavier audit trail. **Pricing:** $250-$2,500/mo per its site.


## 3. Apollo: Value data with assistive outreach


Apollo is the value pick, and a strong one. It pairs a large database (over 230M contacts claimed) with an all-in-one workspace at an accessible price (free through $119/user/month on annual billing), and it serves over 600,000 companies. It acquired Pocus in March 2026 and reports approaching $200M ARR. If your priority is affordable data with basic sequencing attached, Apollo is hard to beat on price.


What it gives up is autonomy. Apollo's AI is assistive, the model is per-seat, and larger orgs tend to treat it as a data layer that feeds other tools rather than an engine that runs the motion. You still target, write, and reply. This is the line between a workspace and a worker.


**Best for:** cost-conscious teams that want a big database and light outreach in one place. **Who should pick something else:** teams that want the outreach and reply handling owned, not assisted, and pipeline that scales without more seats. **Pricing:** free-$119/user/mo (annual, three-seat minimum); Enterprise custom.


## 4. Clay: The enrichment workbench


Clay is the best enrichment orchestration in the market, full stop. It runs waterfalls across over 150 providers, its Claygent agents research at scale, and it anchors modern go-to-market (GTM) engineering, which is why it reached a $3.1B valuation in August 2025. Public tiers start free and run from around $167 to $446 a month and up. If you have a skilled operator and want to build exactly the data flow you need, nothing enriches like Clay.


The catch is in this word, operator. Clay is infrastructure, not an outbound engine, and obtaining outcomes from it usually means a GTM engineer at roughly $160K a year to run it. Clay gives you the workbench; Artisan gives you the worker. Teams that want the job done rather than a system to build tend to want the second.


**Best for:** teams with a GTM engineer who want to build a custom enrichment and data flow. **Who should pick something else:** teams that want outreach run for them, not a workbench to operate. **Pricing:** free; Launch from ~$167/mo, Growth from ~$446/mo, Enterprise custom.


## 5. Amplemarket: Signals with a copilot


Amplemarket is a solid all-rounder that combines data, engagement, and its Duo AI copilot, with strong signal-based selling built in. Entry runs $600 a month for two users, with Growth and Elite custom. For a team that wants good buying signals and a copilot to help reps act on them, it is a capable step up from a plain sequencer.


The word "copilot" is the tell of what it gives up. A human still runs the motion; Duo assists. For teams that want that hands-on control, it fits well. For teams that wanted the work taken off their plate entirely, it stops short of that.


**Best for:** teams that want signal-based selling with a human still in the driver's seat. **Who should pick something else:** teams that want an agent to own the motion, not assist it. **Pricing:** $600/mo entry (two users); Growth/Elite custom.


## 6. Regie.ai: Rep augmentation on a modern stack


Regie.ai (its RegieOne platform) pairs a sequencer, a dialer, and Auto-Pilot agents, and it carries real enterprise credibility. Its published pricing runs $180/user/month for the AI SEP (sales engagement platform) and $499 for the Force Multiplier Rep tier. The philosophy is rep augmentation: sharpen the reps you have and make them more consistent. If your reps are good and you want to speed them up rather than replace the motion, it is a strong pick.


What it gives up is independence from headcount. Reps still drive. Output per rep scales, but pipeline does not scale without more people, so if your goal is more volume without more hires, you end up back at recruiting. This is the difference between augmenting reps and owning the job.


**Best for:** teams that want to augment existing reps on a modern stack with published per-seat pricing. **Who should pick something else:** teams that want pipeline to scale without adding reps. **Pricing:** $180-$499/user/mo per its site; Enterprise custom.


## 7. 11x: An agent-led email challenger


11x offers Alice, an AI SDR, plus Julian, an AI phone agent, and it is backed by roughly $74M from investors including a16z and Benchmark. Its Growth plan is now public from $3,750 a month billed annually. For a team that wants an agent-led email challenger and likes this phone-agent angle, it is on the list.


The reasons to weigh it carefully are attributed. TechCrunch reported in March 2025 that some customers disputed claims 11x made about them, the founder-CEO stepped down on May 5, 2025 to non-executive chairman, and the CTO became CEO, and reviewers report deliverability and CRM-sync complaints. It sits in the lower mid-market while selling through enterprise-style contracts. Well funded, but the track record and control questions are real when the job you are handing over is your pipeline.


**Best for:** teams that want an agent-led email challenger with an AI phone agent. **Who should pick something else:** teams that need a proven deliverability and CRM-sync record and self-serve terms. **Pricing:** Growth from $3,750/mo (annual); Pro/Enterprise custom.


## When an alternative is the better call


Switching honesty runs both ways, so here is when another tool is the right answer. If you only need raw contact data to feed tools you already run, a data platform like Apollo or a workbench like Clay is a cleaner buy than a full agent. If you have a GTM engineer who wants to build a bespoke enrichment flow, Clay is the right tool for that person. If your entire mandate is a published sub-$300 monthly price for a small team, AiSDR's transparency will feel simpler. And if your reps are your differentiator and you want to augment rather than automate them, Regie.ai or Amplemarket fit this philosophy better than a fully autonomous BDR does.


Artisan's case is strongest when you want the whole job done: sourcing net-new pipeline, running multichannel outreach, handling every reply, dialing at $67 a seat, and catching inbound on the same platform, with a control dial and audit trail over all of it. If this is the shape of what you need, the alternatives above each ask you to give up part of it. If it is not, one of them may be a better fit, and that is a fine answer.

## FAQ


### What is the best alternative to Artisan?


For most teams, none of them replace the whole job, which is why they stay. If you need one slice, the honest picks are AiSDR for transparent agent-led email, Apollo for value data, Clay for enrichment orchestration, Amplemarket for signals with a copilot, Regie.ai for rep augmentation, and 11x for an agent-led email challenger. Artisan differs by owning sourcing, outreach, replies, and booking end to end on usage-based pricing.


### Is there a cheaper alternative to Artisan?


Apollo (free through $119/user/month) and AiSDR (from $250 a month) have lower published entry prices. The catch is scope: these numbers buy a data workspace or agent-led email, not a full BDR that sources, dials, replies, and books. Artisan's usage-based credits mean you pay for work performed rather than seats, so cost stays flat as a team grows instead of climbing per head.


### What is the best Artisan alternative for enterprise?


Most alternatives narrow at the top of the market. If you need central-ops deployment where sales ops runs an agent for large AE teams, SOC 2 Type II, SSO and SAML, a full audit trail, and per-seat economics that stay flat at scale, Artisan is built for exactly that. Apollo and Clay serve enterprises as data layers, and 11x sits in the lower mid-market despite enterprise-style contracts.


### Which Artisan alternative has the best data?


Clay orchestrates over 150 enrichment providers and Apollo claims over 230M contacts, both strong. Artisan carries over 270M B2B contacts plus over 130M local businesses (over 400M total) with waterfall enrichment across roughly a dozen providers, charged only on success. The difference is what happens next: Artisan turns the data into live outreach automatically, where a pure data tool hands you records to work yourself.


### Do I need a dialer if I use an Artisan alternative?


Most of these alternatives do not include serious calling, so you would add a dialer separately at over $200-300/user/month for a dedicated parallel one. Artisan's dialer is built in at $67 per seat with unlimited minutes, running as call steps inside the same multichannel sequence, with compliance (calling hours, do-not-call lists, consent-aware recording) enforced automatically per lead.


### Can any alternative do both inbound and outbound?


AiSDR covers both at the SMB end. Most of the others are outbound-only or data-only. Artisan runs inbound and outbound as one AI SDR on one knowledge base, so anonymous website traffic is identified and engaged, and any non-booked chat with a captured email flows into Ava's outreach sequences automatically.


### Should I switch away from Artisan?


If you only need raw data, a custom enrichment build, or a rep copilot, a focused tool may serve you better, and that is an honest answer. Artisan is the stronger choice when you want the whole job owned end to end with governance you can see. The reason to stay is scope: one AI employee running sourcing, outreach, replies, dialing, and inbound beats stitching five tools that each do one part.
