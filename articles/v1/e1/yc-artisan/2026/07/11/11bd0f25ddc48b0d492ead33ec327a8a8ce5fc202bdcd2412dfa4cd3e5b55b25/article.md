## How we chose


We ranked on five criteria: coverage (contacts, companies, phones, regions), tested accuracy versus claimed accuracy, compliance posture (GDPR, CCPA, DNC handling), pricing transparency, and activation, meaning whether the data can actually be put to work without stitching more tools together. Every negative claim is attributed to its source. There is one more bias to disclose beyond our own: nearly every "best B2B data" list you will read is written by a vendor that ranks itself first. So is this one. The difference we aim for is that every other entry receives an honest verdict, a real strength, and a named reason to choose it over us.

## Should you buy a static database or use waterfall enrichment?


Waterfall enrichment beats a static database for most teams in 2026. A static database is one vendor's snapshot: you inherit their coverage, their gaps, and their decay rate. A waterfall queries multiple providers in sequence. If provider one has no mobile number, provider two is asked, then provider three, which adds roughly 20% to 40% coverage versus any single source. The shift to waterfalls was the defining change in this category between 2024 and 2026.


The trade-off is operational. Running your own waterfall through Clay gives maximum control but requires an operator and stacked provider credits. Buying a single database is simple but caps your coverage at one vendor's reach. A third path is a product with the waterfall built in. Artisan, for example, runs enrichment across roughly a dozen providers internally, validates emails with bounce tests, and charges for phone numbers only on success, so you receive the coverage of a waterfall without the assembly work. Budget standalone waterfalls (FullEnrich, BetterContact) cover the middle ground for teams that just need enrichment. For a wider roundup, see the best[B2B data enrichment tools](https://www.artisan.co/blog/b2b-data-enrichment-tools) .


## Which B2B data provider has the most accurate contact data?


No provider's headline accuracy claim survives contact with reality, so the real answer is the most accurate setup, not the most accurate vendor. Vendor claims cluster at 95% or better. Independent-style testing consistently lands lower, roughly 65% to 90% for emails and 45% to 70% for phone numbers, and B2B contact data decays at roughly 20% to 30% a year as people change jobs. One more caveat is worth stating: essentially every published head-to-head benchmark in this category was run by a vendor, so treat all of them, including claims repeated in this article, as directional.


What actually moves accuracy is verification at the point of use (UpLead's export-time verification, Artisan's bounce-testing before send), phone-verified sourcing (Cognism's Diamond Data), and waterfall redundancy, which lets a second provider correct the first one's stale record. If a vendor quotes one accuracy number for their whole database, ask how it was measured and when.


## Which provider has the best mobile phone number coverage?


Cognism has the strongest single-vendor claim on mobiles. Diamond Data numbers are human- and phone-verified, the company claims up to 3x higher connect rates on them, and coverage is strongest in EMEA. ZoomInfo's 70 million direct dials (vendor figure) lead for US enterprise desk lines and mobiles. But because phone data is the weakest layer at every provider (independent-style tests put phone accuracy at just 45% to 70%), waterfall approaches tend to beat any single vendor on total mobile coverage. That logic is why Artisan charges for phone enrichment only when the waterfall actually returns a number, and why phones cost roughly 10x email credits at nearly every provider: scarce, decaying, and expensive to verify. ## How compliant is your B2B data provider?


Compliance became a real buying axis in 2025 and 2026, not a checkbox. The spread within a single corporate family shows how uneven the category is. Cognism markets DNC and TPS scrubbing across markets and holds ISO 27001, ISO 27701, and SOC 2 attestations, while its own subsidiary Kaspr was fined €240,000 by CNIL, France's data regulator, in December 2024 for GDPR violations. Meanwhile, Seamless.AI saw the reported removal of its company page from a major professional network over scraping practices (March 2025), per industry reporting.


A few questions separate vendors quickly. Where is the data sourced, and is there a lawful basis under GDPR? Are DNC registries scrubbed before phone records reach reps? How are deletion requests handled downstream? If your outreach platform sits on top of the data, its handling matters too. Artisan, for instance, applies suppression lists before enrichment and enforces federal DNC checks on its dialer.
