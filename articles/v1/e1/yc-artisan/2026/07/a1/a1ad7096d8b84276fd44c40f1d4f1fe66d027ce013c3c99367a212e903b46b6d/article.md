## What is Alta and where is it in its journey?


Alta positions itself as an "AI GTM System of Actions," with an SDR agent named Katie and AI calling capabilities. In July 2026 it closed a $25M Series A, which is a real vote of confidence and gives the team runway to build. On category thesis, Alta and Artisan are aligned: both believe an AI should take actions across the GTM motion rather than sit beside a rep as a copilot.


The caveats are about maturity. Alta is a young product with a limited public track record; there aren't the kind of named customers and attributed results that let you judge it before buying. Its pricing is gated, quoted by team size on a "pay for what you use" basis, so you can't evaluate cost without a sales conversation. And while Alta references security standards like SOC 2 and ISO 27001, those attestations aren't clearly confirmed in independent public sources, so an enterprise buyer should ask to see them directly. This does not make Alta a bad product, only an early one, and early products carry evaluation risk you have to price in.

## How does personalization and scale compare?


Artisan's personalization runs from light to agentic, with research-agent swarms for hard criteria, and it operates at Marketo scale with a one-to-one feel. Two people in the same Artisan campaign receive two completely different messages; in a legacy sequencer only the name and title change. That's a mature capability, tuned across large volumes of real sends and self-optimized at the component level.


Alta's agent, Katie, and its AI calling are newer and less battle-tested by definition. An early product may do impressive things in a demo, but the question for a buyer is how it holds up across thousands of live sends, edge-case replies, and deliverability realities over months. Artisan has that operating history; Alta is still building it. If you're an early adopter who enjoys shaping a product's direction, that's part of the appeal. If you need dependable output now, it's a risk.
