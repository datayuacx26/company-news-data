# The best Qualified alternatives in 2026

The best Qualified alternative is Artisan, the platform that runs both inbound and outbound: Ava, Artisan's AI BDR, greets, qualifies, and books website visitors, while the same platform sources and runs outbound. Salesforce Agentforce, HubSpot Breeze, Warmly, RB2B, and 6sense are the other real options. This page routes you by need and stack.


## Why look for a Qualified alternative?


It comes down to two things: Salesforce and inbound. Salesforce closed its acquisition of Qualified on April 1, 2026, and the product (its Piper AI sales development representative (SDR) and the conversational inbound suite) is folding into Agentforce. Acquisitions of this kind reliably raise two concerns for buyers. First, the roadmap: where a standalone product's priorities go once it becomes a feature inside a much larger suite is never certain, and teams renewing a premium contract want that certainty. Second, lock-in: Qualified already required Salesforce, and being absorbed into Agentforce deepens that dependency for anyone not committed to the Salesforce stack.


The other reason is scope, and it predates the acquisition. Qualified is inbound only. It is very good at the inbound conversion playbook (chat, routing, meeting booking for the visitors who arrive on your site) with real enterprise polish, historically priced around $42,000 to $100,000 a year. But it does nothing about outbound, so a team that wants to convert inbound and generate pipeline has to buy and stitch a second system. For a lot of buyers, the honest question is not "what is another inbound chat tool" but "what covers both without two contracts." This page answers both versions.

## How we chose


Inbound tools are usually bought to answer one question (convert the visitors already on your site) but evaluated against a bigger one (where does pipeline come from). So we weighed four things: coverage (inbound only, or inbound plus outbound), stack fit (which CRM it requires or prefers), how much of the work the AI does versus your team, and pricing transparency. Because Qualified's defining traits are "inbound only" and "Salesforce required," the alternatives split naturally by whether you want to stay in Salesforce, move to HubSpot, or have inbound and outbound in one place. Every entry receives a best-for verdict and a who-should-pick-else line.

## 2. Salesforce Agentforce: If you are staying in Salesforce


If your team is fully committed to Salesforce, Agentforce is the natural landing spot, and it is where Qualified is heading anyway. It is a CRM-resident conversational agent with Salesforce's security posture behind it, priced consumption-based at roughly $2 per conversation or Flex Credits at about $0.10 per action. For a Salesforce-first org that wants everything native, this residency is the appeal, and it removes the "will this integrate" question.


The caveats are the ones the whole Agentforce line carries: setup is steep, consumption pricing is still evolving, the product is young, and it deepens Salesforce dependency instead of reducing it. If Salesforce lock-in was your reason for leaving Qualified, this does not solve it.


**Best for:** Salesforce-committed teams that want inbound handled natively in the CRM. **Who should pick something else:** teams trying to reduce Salesforce dependency, or that want outbound sourcing too. **Pricing:** $2/conversation or Flex Credits ($0.10/action).


## 3. HubSpot Breeze: The HubSpot-native route


For HubSpot-first teams, Breeze is the cleanest alternative, and it comes with a pricing model worth citing: outcome-based, at roughly $1.00 per qualified lead as of April 2026, an industry first. You pay for results instead of seats or conversations, which changes the risk math for inbound qualification. If your world is HubSpot, this keeps everything native and aligns cost to outcome.


The limit is scope: it lives in the HubSpot world, so it is the right answer for HubSpot shops and a poor fit for anyone on a different stack or wanting a platform-agnostic tool.


**Best for:** HubSpot-native teams that want outcome-based inbound qualification. **Who should pick something else:** teams not on HubSpot, or that want inbound and outbound in one platform not bound to a CRM. **Pricing:** about $1.00 per qualified lead (April 2026).


## 4. Warmly: Identification plus warm outbound plays


Warmly is the alternative for teams that want to identify site visitors and act on them with automated plays, not just chat. It pairs visitor identification with orchestration, so a recognized account can trigger a play, with tiers reported around $10,000 to $30,000 a year per its site. For a team whose inbound problem is really "who is on my site and how do I reach them," this identification-plus-orchestration combination is a better fit than a chat widget alone.


It is lighter on the deep enterprise conversion polish Qualified built, and its identification, like every honest vendor's, is strongest on US person-level traffic. As a system of warm-outbound plays off visitor signals, it is capable.


**Best for:** teams that want visitor identification plus automated warm outbound plays. **Who should pick something else:** teams that want a full inbound conversion suite, or one platform that also sources net-new outbound. **Pricing:** about $10-30K/year per its site.


## 5. RB2B: Cheap person-level identification


RB2B is the budget answer to one narrow question: who is on my site right now. It pioneered person-level identification on US traffic, pushes names to Slack, and starts at $79 to $199 a month per its site. If all you need is the identity signal and you will act on it yourself, it is inexpensive and fast.


It stops at identification. There is no conversion, no qualification, no booking, and no outbound, so it is a signal source instead of an inbound platform. Pair it with something that acts, or pick a tool that does both.


**Best for:** teams that just want cheap US person-level visitor identification pushed to Slack. **Who should pick something else:** teams that want the tool to qualify, book, and follow up, not just identify. **Pricing:** $79-199/month per its site.


## 6. 6sense / Demandbase: Heavy full-stack ABM


If your inbound question is really an account-based one, 6sense or Demandbase are the full-stack ABM suites: intent data, account scoring, and orchestration across paid media and web, with contracts reported from roughly $12,000 to $175,000 a year and medians around $63,000 for 6sense and $68,000 for Demandbase (Vendr-attributed). For large enterprises running a serious ABM program, they are powerful.


They are also heavy: analytics-and-ads-first, with a long implementation and a team to run them. Worth noting that named-account ABM with signals, visitor identification, and the outreach attached is something Artisan runs natively, so account-based selling need not be handed to a separate heavyweight suite.


**Best for:** large enterprises running a dedicated, analytics-heavy ABM program. **Who should pick something else:** teams that want ABM with outreach built in on one lighter platform, or that need inbound chat conversion specifically. **Pricing:** roughly $12K-175K/year; medians about $63K (6sense) and $68K (Demandbase), Vendr-attributed.


## Stay with Qualified if...


Honesty first. Stay with Qualified if you are a committed Salesforce shop, inbound conversion is your entire need, and its playbook (the chat, routing, and booking that made it the premium inbound specialist) is producing for you. The Salesforce acquisition also means tighter native Agentforce integration as the products merge, which is a plus if Salesforce is your center of gravity. If the roadmap and lock-in questions do not worry you because you are all-in on Salesforce anyway, and you do not need outbound, there is no reason to move.


Switch if you want to avoid deepening Salesforce dependency, if the post-acquisition roadmap uncertainty is a real risk for you, or if buying inbound and outbound as two separate systems no longer makes sense. That last reason is the most common: most teams do not want a conversion tool and a pipeline tool with a seam between them.
