*Artificial intelligence is reshaping how people work, and much of the conversation centers on productivity. How can AI make us faster? Cut costs? Reduce fatigue? These benefits matter, but at Workday, we’re focused on the human element. Workday has been asking, “How can AI make the workplace smarter* ***and*** *more accessible?”*


*To explore this opportunity, Workday partnered with Carnegie Mellon University’s Tepper School of Business to study how Agentic AI (AI that can act on behalf of people) can improve accessibility, enhance employee experiences, and strengthen enterprise performance.*


By Michael Blume with contributions from Dylan Homoya, David Yin, Sophia Bazo, David Czech, and Shon Soosman


## **Research Methodology**


The team took a multi-phase approach to identifying areas where agentic AI can be most effectively used to enhance accessibility while also increasing overall productivity and quality. Research streams included:


- **Market Landscape** **Analysis** : Researched the current landscape of agentic AI to understand how companies are using it, who their target audience is, and identifying accessibility related gaps.
- **Stakeholder Interviews** : Regularly met with internal Workday staff and customers to understand their current understanding of agentic AI and uncover potential use cases that would add significant value.
- **Jobs-to-be-Done Review** : Synthesized insights from interviews and competitive analysis to define high-impact opportunities for agentic AI while also uncovering fresh, unexplored ideas.


This approach helped surface key insights on how agentic AI can meet users where they are, identify meaningful needs, and highlight responsible ways forward.


## **Understanding Agentic AI and How to Move It Forward**


Automation traditionally removes repetitive tasks, such as through bots, scripts, or macros. Agentic AI goes further. It works in the background, understands context, anticipates needs, and takes action. It can be thought of as a trusted digital assistant that not only executes tasks but also recommends actions. This shift transforms automation from simple task execution to proactive, human-centered empowerment.


However, for agentic AI to be successful, it needs to be trusted. Three main themes were uncovered during the research phase that shows what characteristics are most needed for a successful agent:


### **Integrated**


Agents should work seamlessly across platforms so users stay in their natural flow of work. One example of this when using AI for writing could be the integration of agentic capabilities into corporate messaging platforms like Slack; agents need to exist where people are already doing the writing, not siloed in a separate portal and copy pasted over without full context.


Quotes from our interviews:


> “Don’t make a chatbot a destination. Build it into where people are already going.”
>
>
> “Having everything under one hood is the best option.”
>
>
> “Overlays are extremely problematic because they become cluttered and make sites less accessible. We don’t want to see Al become an overlay.”


### **Controlled**


Employees and managers must retain choice and visibility into AI actions to foster trust. This can look like requiring a human to kick off automated processes, as well as providing steps for human review of AI processes before and after work has taken place.


Some feedback we received:


> “We currently use AI as a thought partner. It’s great for research and editing, but it still needs a human to validate before rollout.”
>
>
> “You should be able to easily see what the sources are for decisions made by an Al agent.”


### **Grounded**


Every solution must solve a clear, real-world problem that improves user experience and business outcomes. A grounded solution is one that has a clear, measurable outcome and addresses a high-value business priority. For instance, reducing the average time-to-hire or simplifying the process for requesting workplace accommodations.


Related comments we heard:


> “We don’t need solutions to hypothetical problems. The best use for agentic AI would be to help solve known problems.”
>
>
> “I wouldn’t have been able to do a task without Al helping me. I had basic understanding, but not the details on how to do it.”
>
>
> “People with disabilities are often the most creative problem solvers, hardest workers, and determined. But it comes to them at a heavy load. I can see Al really helping them relieve their stress and mental capacity”


**Categories for Consideration**


Through the research, in addition to more granular use cases, Workday and Carnegie Mellon identified four broad opportunity areas where Agentic AI can enhance both accessibility and enterprise performance:


1. **Finance, Risk, and Compliance:** Increasing transparency and governance in financial and regulatory operations to ensure equitable treatments for all. Enhancing highly manual procurement and financial reviews for example could improve thoroughness, objectivity, and fairness of outcomes.


2. **Talent, Careers, and Learning Development** : Supporting equitable hiring, employee growth, and retention with a keen focus on accessibility and unique needs. AI can provide new avenues for access to help applicants highlight their qualifications and inclusively promote skill growth at scale.


3. **Support and Accessibility** : Reducing everyday friction while enhancing inclusion for all employees. While repetitive tasks and difficulty quickly scanning information can cause frustration for anyone, for people with disabilities, these challenges are sometimes amplified. Agentic AI is uniquely positioned to reduce or remove related barriers by automating mundane processes, synthesizing complex visual information which may be indecipherable for the blind or those with low vision, and providing personalized support where needed.


4. **Insights and Organizational Intelligence** : Transforming complex enterprise data into actionable insights that drive accessible work environments. Use cases could look like organizational accessibility policy management which leverages the ability to identify gaps, forecast needs, and proactively manage compliance risks.


An important theme in all of the categories is not a replacement of employees, but ways to enable them by making sure their needs are met.


**Risk and Governance**


To deploy Agentic AI responsibly, organizations must embed safeguards from the start. Our analysis identified key risk categories:


- **Overlooking Individuals** : Solutions created without input from people with accessibility needs. Mitigation: Involve individuals with lived experiences in design decisions.
- **Privacy and Security** : Protecting sensitive data. Mitigation: Build transparent, regional data governance from day one.
- **Bias and Fairness** : Avoid exclusion in AI. Mitigation: Apply accessibility by design principles throughout the lifecycle.
- **Transparency** : Addressing black box logic. Mitigation: Ensure explainable AI and clear audit trails.
- **Human Oversight** : Overreliance on automation. Mitigation: Maintain human in the loop checkpoints.
- **Regulatory Change** : Evolving global AI laws. Mitigation: Continuous risk monitoring and documentation standards.


The path forward is clear: **develop accessible, trustworthy Agentic AI that empowers human judgment rather than replaces it.**


**Continuing to Move Forward**


Agentic AI is the next step in how people work. It has great potential in amplifying human capabilities and creating equitable work environments despite a person’s accessibility needs. As organizations continue to innovate at scale with this new technology, the focus must remain on inclusion by design, transparency in operation, and grounding solutions in human need. Agentic AI needs to not only transform how work gets done, but also to redefine what it means to truly work better, together.
