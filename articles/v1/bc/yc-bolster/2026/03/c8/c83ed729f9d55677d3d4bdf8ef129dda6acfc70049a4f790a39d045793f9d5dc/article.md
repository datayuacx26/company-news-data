## Construction Needs More Talent and More Perspectives


Construction is evolving at a breakneck pace, and it's happening right in front of our eyes.


While the industry will always rely on skilled tradespeople, today's construction businesses are just as dependent on planning, communication, and project management as they are on physical skill.


And as the digital aspect of these parts of the job takes off, the industry is throwing open its doors to a whole new group of professionals – including women who want to start careers in construction.


Modern tools like construction estimating software are letting contractors work smarter, streamline their operations, and build companies that are spread across the next generation of builders.


## The Demand for Talent and Diverse Perspectives, Has Never Been Greater


One of the main headaches facing construction today is the never-ending struggle to find skilled workers.


Contractors all across North America are struggling to find people with the right skills to fill jobs, and meanwhile the industry still has a long way to go in terms of getting its workforce to look more like the country it serves.


That creates a clear opportunity for women to enter the industry – because construction companies that are more diverse tend to be stronger.


Diverse teams bring benefits such as:


- better client communication because people are being listened to


- improved project organisation because different people are bringing different ideas to the table


- better decision-making because you're not relying on just one perspective


- and more creative problem-solving because people are thinking of new ways to approach things


Construction projects are complicated. The more different perspectives you get in on planning and managing those projects, the stronger the outcomes are going to be.


## The Modern Construction Job is a Tech Job


You might think that a career in construction is all about getting your hands dirty.


But the truth is, modern construction businesses rely on computers and data just as much as they do on physical skill.


Today's contractors rely on technology to manage the following:


- project budgets


- how much they're spending on materials


- measuring up and taking off the materials they need


- coming up with proposals for clients, and selecting the right client


- and keeping track of schedules and all the relevant documentation


So in other words, construction is no longer just about building – it's about running a business.


And that shift is creating whole new types of jobs within construction companies, from estimating specialists to operations managers and project coordinators.
