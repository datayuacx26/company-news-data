# How to File a Duty Drawback Claim: A Step-by-Step Guide

How do I file a duty drawback claim? +


Duty drawback claims are filed electronically with CBP through the ABI (Automated Broker Interface) Drawback module. The process is: gather your duty-paid import data, match it to qualifying exports or destructions, prepare the drawback entry (CBP Form 7551), file any required Notice of Intent to Export/Destroy (CBP Form 7553), submit through ACE, and receive payment after CBP review. Most companies file through a licensed customs broker.


What forms do I need to file duty drawback? +


The core form is CBP Form 7551, the drawback entry, filed electronically through ABI. If you plan to claim on exported or destroyed goods, you may also need CBP Form 7553 (Notice of Intent to Export, Destroy, or Return Merchandise) submitted before the export or destruction. You'll also reference your import entry summaries (CBP Form 7501) and proof of export or destruction.


Can I file a duty drawback claim myself? +


Technically yes, but it's difficult. You'd need to establish an electronic connection to CBP's ABI system or use a certified service bureau, and the documentation and HTS-matching rules are unforgiving — a single error can disqualify a claim. In practice, nearly all drawback is filed by licensed customs brokers, and first-time filers are strongly advised to use one.


What is accelerated payment in duty drawback? +


Accelerated payment (AP) is a CBP privilege that lets approved claimants receive their drawback refund before CBP completes final review (liquidation) of the claim. It requires a bond. Without AP, you wait until the claim is fully liquidated, which can take considerably longer. First-time filers typically apply for AP and other privileges when they set up their drawback program.


How long does it take to get a duty drawback refund? +


With accelerated payment privilege, refunds can be paid within a few weeks to a couple of months of filing. Without accelerated payment, standard claims are paid after final liquidation, which can take 6-18 months. Timelines depend on claim complexity, documentation quality, and CBP's review queue.


What documents do I need to file a drawback claim? +


You need import entry summaries (CBP Form 7501) showing duties paid, HTS classifications, proof of export (bills of lading, AES export filings) or CBP-witnessed proof of destruction, and records tracing the duty-paid imports to the corresponding exports or destructions. For manufacturing claims you also need a bill of materials. Records must be retained for 3 years from the date the claim is liquidated.


Do I need a customs broker to file duty drawback? +


Practically, yes. Drawback documentation is detailed — chain of custody, HTS matching, export proofs — and errors disqualify claims. Licensed brokers experienced in drawback handle the ABI filing, privilege applications, and recordkeeping. Most ongoing drawback programs are run by brokers under contract with the importer.
