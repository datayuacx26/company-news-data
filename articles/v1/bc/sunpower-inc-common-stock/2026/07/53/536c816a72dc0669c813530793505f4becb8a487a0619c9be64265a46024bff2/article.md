# How does solar panel efficiency impact your savings?

You’re considering installing solar panels. You’ve probably already heard about panel power in watts. But what truly determines your savings is **solar panel efficiency** .


This metric reflects the real performance of your system. Understanding this figure means making the right choice from the start and avoiding paying a high price for your watts.


## What is solar panel efficiency?


Solar panel efficiency is expressed as a percentage. It indicates what portion of the solar energy received is converted into electricity.


For example, an efficiency of 23% means that 23% of the sunlight hitting the panel is converted into usable electricity for your home.


The formula is simple:
**Efficiency = electrical power output ÷ solar power received × 100**


Don’t confuse efficiency with power:


- **Power (W or Wp : watt peak)** = maximum output under lab conditions


- **Efficiency** = real-world energy conversion performance


In practical terms, two solar panels with the same rated power of 500 Wp can produce very different amounts of electricity in real conditions. This is precisely why efficiency is a much more relevant indicator than power alone.


*Solar panel efficiency matters more than wattage: just like two 25W bulbs can deliver very different brightness, panels with the same power can produce vastly different energy output.*


It is similar to comparing two light bulbs based on their wattage rather than their brightness. Today, thanks to technological advancements, we know that an LED bulb can be ten times brighter than a traditional incandescent bulb with the same nominal consumption. Should we pay the same price for these bulbs simply because they both have a nominal power of 25 watts? Or does one have more value because it produces more light per watt due to a different technology?


As with these bulbs, the technological differences between two 500 W photovoltaic panels can lead to very different real-world results. And these results directly impact the long-term return on investment of your panels.

## What factors influence the efficiency of a solar panel?


The actual performance of a solar panel system does not depend solely on the chosen technology. Several external parameters also play an important role.


### Solar panel orientation and tilt


The orientation of your solar panels is critical.


- **South-facing** is ideal (in France / Northern Hemisphere)


- Best tilt: **30°–35°**


In this setup, photovoltaic cells capture the maximum solar radiation, both in summer and[winter](https://www.sunpowerglobal.com/blog/solar-panels-in-winter-performance-and-real-world-production) .


If your roof does not allow a south-facing orientation, choose south-west first, then south-east. A north-facing orientation should be avoided entirely, as the loss of production would be too significant to ensure a reasonable return on investment and savings.


### Sunlight exposure: a key variable depending on your region


Solar energy production varies greatly depending on geographic location.


- Northern Europe (Germany, Netherlands, Belgium, Nordics): **800–1,200 kWh/kWp/year**


- Central Europe (France, Austria, Czechia, Poland): **9,00–1,400 kWh/kWp/year**


- Southern Europe (Italy, Spain, Portugal, Greece): **1,000–1,800 kWh/kWp/year**


In practical terms, a well-oriented 3 kWp system produces roughly 2,700 to 4,500 kWh per year across Europe, depending on location.


### Temperature: an often overlooked factor


One might think that the hotter it gets, the more solar panels produce. In reality, the opposite happens. A photovoltaic solar panel performs optimally at 25°C. Beyond that, efficiency typically decreases by about 0.35% per additional degree.


This means that on a 35°C day, a panel loses roughly 3% of its efficiency.


This is why panels with a low temperature coefficient perform better.[SunPower back-contact panels](https://www.sunpowerglobal.com/uk/homeowners/products/sunpower-m-class-solar-panels) have a temperature coefficient as low as -0.26% per degree Celsius. They remain highly efficient even during periods of intense heat.
