# Embrace your bright new solar energy daily routine

You’ve chosen solar. Your solar panels are installed on your roof - a decisive first step.


But generating electricity isn’t enough. Knowing how to use it at the right time, gradually reducing your reliance on the grid, and maximising every kilowatt-hour produced by the sun - that’s where true solar energy benefits begin. And it starts with simple habits you can adopt every day.


So, if you’re looking for how to make renewable energy more efficient, read on. Here are our top tips for using your new solar panels, so you can get off to the best start.


Not just for the environment, but for your wallet too.


## Maximise your solar power during the day


Your solar panels produce the most electricity between **10am and 4pm** . This is the golden rule of solar self-consumption:
- *use energy when it’s being generated.*


​Identify your biggest energy guzzlers first.


And schedule your most energy-intensive appliances during these peak hours:


- Washing machines


- Dishwashers


- Electric vehicle charging


Every kilowatt-hour you use at that time is one you don’t have to buy from your energy supplier.


Even small devices make a difference. Charging your smartphone, laptop, or e-bike during daylight hours ensures you’re making the most of your solar generation.


Even these small appliances powered by the sun will add up to savings in the long run.


The bottom line: Always make the most of the electricity being made by your[SunPower solar panels](https://www.sunpowerglobal.com/uk/homeowners/products/solar-panels) during the daytime.


You’ll be on your way to making renewable energy efficient in no time.


### Which uses help reduce your dependence on energy suppliers?


If you’re upgrading your home, switching to electric appliances is a natural next step alongside your solar system.

## Managing grid dependence: think day and night


Solar panels don’t produce electricity at night - that’s unavoidable. But it’s a challenge you can manage effectively.


The key is to shift as much of your energy usage as possible to daylight hours. Without storage, your reliance on grid electricity returns in the evening.


This is where **solar energy storage** becomes essential for achieving real energy independence.

## Changing your habits for greener living


There are many other things you can do to reduce your household energy consumption. This is for everyone – even if you don’t have solar panels just yet.


Ditch the bathtub, and switch to a shower for your morning routine. If you can keep all showers to under five minutes, even better.


Also try to use your tumble dryer only when you have to. Always air dry your clothes outside on a line whenever possible.


If it gets too hot inside, try to manage the temperature by opening windows and doors, rather than reaching for the aircon remote.


​Finally, switch to smaller appliances that use less energy. Boil one cup of water in the kettle, don’t heat up a pan on the stove. Use an air fryer for small meals, rather than powering up your large oven. Always think about the minimum amount of energy you need to get the job done. ​


Even minor tweaks can make a significant impact and help keep your energy use in check.


These simple changes, over time, significantly improve your solar system’s efficiency and reduce your reliance on traditional energy suppliers.
