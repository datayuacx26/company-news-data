# The Stoic Letters, 27th July

Dear Stoics,


You’ve probably seen one this very week: a worn dirt line cutting across a lawn, ignoring the elegant paved walkway around it. Urban planners call these lines


**desire paths**


, the unplanned trails created by people walking where they actually want to go, rather than where the architects told them to. Every desire path is a wordless referendum: the official map on one side, human feet on the other. And the human feet usually win. Did you know that Broadway in New York is thought to


[follow a trail walked by the Wecquaesgeek people](https://www.newyork.com/articles/post/why-broadway-breaks-manhattan-grid/)


long before any city grid existed? The skyscrapers came much later and had to find their place around the route that was already there.


What is particularly interesting is that it takes very little to make a path. Studies suggest that as few as fifteen crossings can create a visible trail, which then invites everyone who comes after to follow it. The wisest city planners have stopped fighting this. In Finland, some visit parks after the first snowfall, when all official paths vanish under a thick layer of snow, to watch where the footprints go. Then they pave over those footprints to create new pathways. Some universities have done the same, leaving new campuses partly unpaved for a season and letting students’ feet draw the map. Designers call it


**paving the cowpaths**


: treating behaviour as information, not delinquency.


Finally, here’s the turn you probably saw coming: let’s look at our inner lives as landscapes too. We’ll discover that they are absolutely covered in desire paths. The official map says gym after work, but the worn trail leads to the sofa and the TV. The plan says meet new people, but the footprints lead to the same beloved friends. And so on; we’ve all been there. For years, we may treat these trails as evidence of weak will, something to be fought and overpowered. But what if we studied them like a Finnish city planner instead?


**Where your feet keep going, something true about your mind is showing itself.**


Listen to yourself for a while, and you may hear what you really need in order to feel better.


We should probably note that not every worn path deserves following. Some keep us in a rut and don’t allow us to grow. These are paths of least resistance rather than paths of desire, dug by fear and repetition. The test is how you feel at the destination. A desire path leads somewhere you’re happy to arrive; a rut path leads somewhere you’ve often been and feel stuck. The good news is that just as a path left alone in the park slowly grows green again, so will ours.


This week, be the planner after the snowfall. In your


[journal](https://www.newsletter.getstoic.com/p/getstoic.com/journaling)


, notice your footprints for a few days: where your time went, who you gravitated to, what you reached for. Then, at week’s end, make two decisions. Choose one trail that deserves paving, and make it official. And let the one that deserves ditching grow over with fresh green.


Yours truly,


The Stoic App Team

#### Traveller, there is no path; the path is made by walking.


*Antonio Machado*


Ask someone what they’re looking forward to, and you’ll usually hear the typical answers: the holiday, the promotion, the wedding, the weekend. This week’s stoic theme is


**On Looking Forward**


, but with a twist. We won’t be asking what you’re


supposed


to anticipate, but what genuinely quickens your pulse, which are often two very different lists.


Some of us feel completely neutral about milestones everyone insists are exciting; some of us have looked forward to something for so long that the anticipation has become more real than the thing itself will ever be. So, this week we’ll take an honest inventory of what we really want from our future. You’re
