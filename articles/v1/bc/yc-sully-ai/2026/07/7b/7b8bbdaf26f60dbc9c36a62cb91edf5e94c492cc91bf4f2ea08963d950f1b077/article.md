## What Abridge Does and Why Clinicians Look for an Alternative


Abridge is one of the best-known names in ambient AI documentation. It is worth being clear-eyed about what it does well before talking about where it stops.


### Where Abridge Is Strong


Abridge is built for the enterprise. It runs on a deep Epic integration across Haiku, Canto, and Hyperdrive, supports a wide range of specialties and languages, and carries enterprise security including HIPAA, SOC 2 Type II, and a BAA \[1\]. It has earned recognition in ambient AI from industry analysts and is deployed inside large, recognizable health systems \[2\]. If you are a large Epic shop and a scribe is genuinely all you need, it is a serious option. The catch is that most health systems find the work does not stop at the note.


### Where Abridge Falls Short for Growing and Multi-System Practices


The gaps show up once you look past the note. Here is how a physician described it on a recent call with our team:


> She had already tried both Abridge and Freed, and left both. Her reason was not price. She could not get the notes written the way she wanted, and she ran into accuracy problems. What won her over was being able to describe how she writes her notes once, save it as a template, and have every note come out the same way after that.


That story lines up with three patterns we hear again and again.


**It is built for large Epic enterprises.** Third-party reviews are blunt that Abridge is "not built for" solo clinicians or small practices, and rollout tends to run weeks to months with heavy IT involvement \[3\].


**Pricing is quote-only.** There is no public per-clinician price and no self-serve trial. Procurement goes through hospital IT and contracting, so buyers cannot evaluate cost on their own \[3\].


**It is a scribe, and only a scribe.** This is the one that matters most. Abridge produces documentation. Coding, claims, scheduling, and patient outreach are left to other vendors or to your staff. Many clinicians are even on Abridge because their hospital picked it, not because they chose it, and they start shopping the moment they change jobs.

## How to Switch From Abridge Without Disrupting Clinicians


Switching scribes sounds risky, which is why a lot of teams stay put. It does not have to be a big-bang cutover.


### Mapping Your Current Documentation Workflow


Write down where a note goes today. Who codes it, who bills it, who books the follow-up. The manual handoffs you find are exactly the steps a workforce can absorb, and they show you the real cost of a documentation-only tool.


### Running a Pilot With a Single Specialty


Start with one specialty or one clinic. Sully's[natural-language customization](https://www.sully.ai/help-center/articles/can-we-build-our-own-agents-or-workflows-on-top-of-sully.ai) lets a clinician describe how their workflow should run in plain language instead of waiting on a long build, so a pilot can go live fast and stay contained.


### Measuring Time Saved and Coding Accuracy


Decide the numbers up front: documentation minutes saved per encounter, coding accuracy and denial rate, and follow-up booking rate. Those three metrics tell you whether you bought a better scribe or a better workflow.
