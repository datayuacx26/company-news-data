# How to Add an AI Scribe to Jane App EHR in 2026

You run on Jane App, the allied-health practice-management and EMR platform, whether you are a solo physiotherapist, chiropractor, or massage therapist, or a multidisciplinary clinic billing insurance across many provider types. Documentation weighs on all of it. Physicians spend close to two hours on the EHR and deskwork for every hour of direct patient care \[1\], and clinicians across settings log about 86 minutes of nightly "pajama time" finishing charts \[2\].


Here is the fact worth knowing up front: Jane already ships its own native AI Scribe. So the real decision is no longer whether to add a scribe. It is whether the native scribe covers everything you need, or whether clean claims across a multidisciplinary clinic and the other systems you run need more. This guide covers that honestly.

## What an AI Scribe Adds to the Jane App Workflow


An[AI scribe](https://www.sully.ai/medical-scribe) listens to the appointment and turns the conversation into a structured note. The practitioner stays present with the patient. The scribe drafts the note in seconds, and the practitioner reviews it instead of typing it.


Jane's users span two kinds of work: cash-pay wellness like massage and some mental health, and insurance-billed care like physiotherapy and chiropractic. The numbers below are the burden a scribe is meant to remove.


The difference on Jane is that a native AI Scribe already exists. So the decision shifts from "do we add a scribe" to "does the native scribe cover everything, or do clean claims across providers, and the other systems we run, need more."


### Ambient Documentation Versus Manual Charting


The old flow is typing SOAP notes in Jane after each appointment. Either way the practitioner is still doing data entry.


The ambient flow is listen, draft, review, sign, and ambient scribes have cut documentation time in multi-site studies \[8\]. The split that matters here is payer mix: massage and wellness are often cash-pay, while physiotherapy and chiropractic are insurance-billed and coding-sensitive, so on that side the note has to feed a clean claim.


### Jane Ships Its Own AI Scribe, So the Question Is Claims and Scale


Jane offers a native AI Scribe, built in-house, that listens ambiently and drafts SOAP or progress notes into the chart. It is HIPAA and PIPEDA compliant and offered as a low-cost add-on at about $15 per practitioner per month with a free tier \[3\]\[4\].


That is a strong allied-health-native tool, and Jane also owns scheduling, booking, and billing. So the decision is not native versus nothing. It is whether clean claims across a multidisciplinary clinic, and coverage across other systems, needs more than a documentation scribe is built for. That framing drives the rest of this guide.

## How to Add an AI Scribe to Jane App Step by Step


Five steps take you from decision to a validated note in the chart.


### 1. Confirm Your Jane Setup and Payer Mix


Confirm your Jane setup, whether you have enabled the native AI Scribe, your disciplines, and your payer mix, cash-pay wellness versus insurance-billed physiotherapy and chiropractic. Decide what you want: SOAP documentation, which the native scribe may already cover, or clean claims across providers plus intake and follow-up, which points to a clinical workforce platform.


That payer mix determines the route.


### 2. Choose Your Integration Method


Map the methods to that goal. The native scribe gives you an in-workflow SOAP note. A third-party scribe documents. A clinical workforce platform handles documentation, clean claims, and intake through one integration.


The decision rule: if you are a solo or small allied-health practice, the native AI Scribe gets you there. If you bill insurance across a multidisciplinary clinic or run more than one system, you need a clinical workforce platform.


### 3. Connect the Scribe


For the native option, enable the AI Scribe in Jane \[3\]. For a third-party or workforce tool, have the vendor integrate with your Jane environment \[5\]\[6\].


Whichever route you pick, two things are non-negotiable before any session audio is recorded: a signed BAA and HIPAA-compliant data handling, plus client consent for recording.


### 4. Map Notes and Coding to Jane


Configure how SOAP notes land in the right Jane fields, and for physiotherapy and chiropractic how codes attach. The native scribe documents; a clinical workforce platform[writes coded, claim-ready data](https://www.sully.ai/help-center/articles/how-does-sully-ai-s-two-way-integration-with-ehrs-work) and submits it.


Verify it on an insurance-billed physio or chiro visit, not just a cash-pay wellness session, since that is where coding and claims actually matter.


### 5. Pilot Across Disciplines, Measure, and Scale


Pilot across a cash-pay wellness session and an insurance-billed physio or chiro visit, since they end differently. The insurance visit is the one that reveals whether coding and claims hold up.


Confirm notes land correctly, measure the[time saved](https://www.sully.ai/help-center/articles/what-is-the-roi-of-an-ai-medical-scribe) \[3\], and on the insurance side measure whether claims come out clean. Validate consent and governance before a clinic-wide rollout.

Match the tool to your disciplines and payer mix, not just the single visit.

Multi-location and mixed clinics often run Jane plus other systems. Native tools cover only Jane.


A workforce platform that integrates with Jane and more than 20 other EHRs covers them all through one approach. Weigh coverage against the systems you actually run.

The non-negotiables:[HIPAA compliance](https://www.sully.ai/help-center/articles/are-ai-scribes-hipaa-compliant) , a signed BAA,[encryption and de-identified PHI handling](https://www.sully.ai/trust) , MFA, SSO, role-based access, and clear retention controls.


Add client consent for recording sessions. Ask where audio is processed, how long it is retained, and who can access transcripts before the pilot starts.
