## Clinical Note Example (Filled In)


Templates make more sense when you see one completed. Here's the same template filled in for a routine primary care visit.


CLINICAL NOTE


Patient Name: J.M. (example) DOB: 03/14/1968


Date of Visit: 08/17/2026 Provider: Dr. A. Rivera, MD


Chief Complaint: Persistent cough x 3 weeks


HISTORY OF PRESENT ILLNESS


58-year-old male presents with a dry cough for 3 weeks, worse at


night. No fever, chills, or hemoptysis. Started lisinopril 6 weeks


ago for hypertension. No recent travel or sick contacts. Denies


shortness of breath, chest pain, or wheezing.


REVIEW OF SYSTEMS


Constitutional: No fever, no weight loss.


Respiratory: Dry cough as above; no dyspnea.


Cardiovascular: No chest pain, no palpitations.


All other systems reviewed and negative.


PAST MEDICAL HISTORY


Medications: Lisinopril 10 mg daily, atorvastatin 20 mg daily


Allergies: NKDA


Surgical History: Appendectomy (1994)


Social History: Never smoker; occasional alcohol.


PHYSICAL EXAMINATION


Vitals: BP 132/84 | HR 72 | RR 14 | Temp 98.2°F | SpO2 98%


General: Well-appearing, no acute distress.


Lungs: Clear to auscultation bilaterally. No wheezes or crackles.


ENT: Oropharynx clear, no postnasal drip visualized.


ASSESSMENT & PLAN


1. Chronic cough, likely ACE-inhibitor induced.


Plan: Discontinue lisinopril; start losartan 50 mg daily.


Cough expected to resolve within 1–4 weeks.


2. Hypertension, controlled.


Plan: Continue lifestyle measures; recheck BP at follow-up.


Follow-Up: 4 weeks, sooner if cough worsens or new symptoms.


Notice what makes this note effective: every problem has explicit reasoning and a concrete plan, pertinent negatives are documented, and a colleague could pick up care tomorrow without a phone call.


## Common Clinical Note Formats Compared


Not every encounter needs the same structure. Here's how the most widely used clinical note formats compare:


**Format**


**Structure**


**Best For**


**SOAP**


Subjective, Objective, Assessment, Plan


Routine visits and follow-ups across most specialties


**Problem-based**


HPI, ROS, Exam, numbered Assessment & Plan


Primary care, complex patients with multiple issues


**DAP**


Data, Assessment, Plan


Behavioral health and therapy sessions


**BIRP**


Behavior, Intervention, Response, Plan


Mental health, documenting interventions


**H&P**


Full History & Physical

## What Should a Clinical Note Template Include?


Regardless of format, a complete clinical note template should prompt you for:


-


**Patient identifiers and visit details:** Name, DOB, date, and provider; the basics that make the note legally valid.


-


**Chief complaint and HPI:** The patient's reason for the visit in their own words, followed by a structured history.


-


**Objective findings:** Vitals, exam findings, and any point-of-care results.


-


**Assessment with reasoning:** Not just a diagnosis, but why, this is what protects you medico-legally and helps colleagues follow your thinking.


-


**A concrete plan:** Orders, prescriptions, referrals, patient instructions, and follow-up timing.


**Pro tip:** The single most common weakness in clinical notes is an assessment that states a diagnosis without reasoning. A good template forces one line of clinical rationale per problem.


## How to Choose the Right Clinical Note Template


**1. Start from your visit mix.** If 80% of your encounters are follow-ups, optimize for a fast progress-note format and keep a comprehensive template for new patients and complex cases.


**2. Check it against your best note.** Pull up a note you're genuinely proud of, then ask whether the template you're evaluating would produce something as good. If it wouldn't, keep looking or customize.


**3. Keep your library small.** Most clinicians need only three to five templates: an initial assessment, a progress note, a procedure note, and one or two specialty-specific formats.


**4. Edit ruthlessly.** No template survives contact with real practice unchanged. Delete sections you never fill in, an empty field in every note is noise, not thoroughness.


## Frequently Asked Questions


### What is a clinical note template?


A clinical note template is a structured document with predefined sections, such as chief complaint, history, exam, assessment, and plan, that guides clinicians through documenting a patient encounter completely and consistently. It reduces the cognitive load of deciding what to write and ensures nothing essential is missed.


### What should be included in a clinical note?


At minimum: patient identifiers, date of service, chief complaint, relevant history, objective findings, an assessment with clinical reasoning, and a plan with follow-up. Specialty and payer requirements may add elements like time spent or medical decision-making complexity.


### What's the difference between a clinical note and a SOAP note?


A SOAP note is one specific *format* of clinical note, organized into Subjective, Objective, Assessment, and Plan sections. "Clinical note" is the broader category that includes SOAP, DAP, BIRP, H&P, and problem-based formats.


### Are clinical note templates free to use?


Yes, the templates on this page are free to copy or download as a PDF, with no signup required. Note formats like SOAP are industry standards, not proprietary structures.
