## What Is a Medical Report Template?


A medical report template is a pre-structured document that standardizes how a physician presents a patient's history, examination findings, diagnosis, treatment, and prognosis for an external audience. It typically includes fields for administrative details, the clinician's credentials, subjective and objective findings, a medical opinion, and a signed certification.


A medical report is usually requested by someone outside the treating relationship (e.g. an insurance company, a lawyer, an employer, a government agency, or another specialist). That's what separates it from day-to-day chart documentation like SOAP or progress notes, which are written primarily for you and your care team.


This means a medical report carries legal weight. It's an official statement of fact and professional opinion, and it needs to be accurate, complete, and defensible.


## The Standard Medical Report Format


Formats vary slightly by country, specialty, and requesting organization, but virtually every medical report contains these eight sections in this order:


1.


**Administrative details** : Date of request, requesting party, claim or reference number, patient name and DOB, practice details.


2.


**Clinician credentials** : Your qualifications, years of experience, and your relationship to the patient (treating physician vs. independent examiner).


3.


**Purpose and scope** : Who requested the report, why, and what sources it's based on (records, examination, or both).


4.


**Subjective findings** : What the patient reports: symptoms, functional limitations, and impact on work and daily life.


5.


**Objective findings** : What the record shows: diagnoses with dates, investigation results, and treatment to date.


6.


**Assessment and prognosis** : Your clinical assessment, expected trajectory, and planned management.


7.


**Medical opinion** : Direct answers to the specific questions the requester asked.


8.


**Certification and signature** : A declaration that the report is true and correct, plus your signature, contact details, and date.


**Pro Tip:** Before writing anything, re-read the request letter. Most rejected or returned reports fail because the clinician answered questions the requester never asked and skipped the ones they did.

-

-

## When You'll Need a Medical Report


Medical report requests come from a handful of predictable sources. Knowing which one you're writing for changes what you emphasize.


### Insurance and Compensation Claims


Insurers and compensation boards use your report to determine coverage, benefits, and return-to-work timelines. Focus on objective findings, functional capacity, and dates; vague language here delays claims and creates follow-up requests.


### Legal and Medico-Legal Matters


In personal injury cases and other legal proceedings, your report may become evidence. Stick strictly to what the record supports, separate fact from opinion clearly, and never speculate beyond your expertise.


### Work Capacity and Disability Assessments


Employers and government agencies need a clear answer to one question: what can this patient safely do? Spell out specific restrictions (weight limits, hours, movements) rather than general statements like "light duties."


### Referrals and Continuity of Care


When another clinician requests a report, they need the clinical story fast: diagnosis, what's been tried, what worked, and what you want from them. The specialist referral template above is built for exactly this.


## How to Write a Medical Report in 5 Steps


1.


**Read the request letter twice** : List every question the requester asks. Your opinion section will answer these, in order.


2.


**Gather the record** : Pull diagnoses, investigation results, and treatment dates before you start writing. Chronology errors are the most common reason reports get challenged.


3.


**Fill the template top to bottom** : Keep subjective and objective findings strictly separated. "Patient reports" belongs in one section; "MRI showed" belongs in the other.


4.


**Answer the questions directly** : One clear paragraph per question. If you can't answer something from the available record, say so explicitly.


5.


**Certify, sign, and keep a copy** : Add your declaration, sign, and file the report in the patient's record. You may need it again for a supplementary request.


A good medical report is boring in the best way: factual, chronological, and structured so the reader finds every answer exactly where they expect it.


## Common Medical Report Mistakes to Avoid


-


**Mixing fact and opinion:** Keep patient-reported symptoms, documented findings, and your professional opinion in separate sections. Blurring them undermines the whole report's credibility.


-


**Missing dates:** "Diagnosed with hypertension" is weak; "diagnosed with hypertension in March 2023" is defensible. Date everything.


-


**Copy-pasting clinical notes:** Raw chart notes are full of abbreviations and internal shorthand a claims assessor won't understand. The report should translate the record, not reproduce it.


-


**Ignoring the certification:** An unsigned or undeclared report is often legally invalid for the requester's purposes, which means you'll be writing it twice.

## Frequently Asked Questions


### What is the difference between a medical report and a medical record?


A medical record is the continuous, internal documentation of a patient's care over time. A medical report is a standalone document, written for a specific external requester, that summarizes and interprets the relevant parts of that record at a point in time.


### What should a medical report template include?


At minimum: administrative details, clinician credentials, the purpose of the report, subjective findings, objective findings with dates, an assessment and prognosis, direct answers to the requester's questions, and a signed certification.


### How long does it take to write a medical report?


With a template and an organized record, most routine reports take 20-45 minutes. Complex medico-legal reports can take several hours. Practices using an[AI medical scribe](https://www.sully.ai/scribe) cut this further because the clinical timeline is already structured when the request arrives.


### Can I charge for writing a medical report?


Usually, yes. Reports requested by insurers, lawyers, and employers fall outside standard clinical care and are typically billed to the requesting party. Check the fee rules for your jurisdiction and the requesting organization before invoicing.


### Is a medical report legally binding?


A medical report is a formal, certified statement that can be used as evidence in legal and administrative proceedings. That's why the certification section matters: you're declaring the contents true and correct, and inaccuracies can carry professional and legal consequences.
