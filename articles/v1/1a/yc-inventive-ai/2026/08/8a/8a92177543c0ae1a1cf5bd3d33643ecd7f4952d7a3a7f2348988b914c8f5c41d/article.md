# Govtech and It’s Role in Tender Management

Tender management is the process of actually preparing and submitting a tender in response to a request from a client. Most of the time in the form of an RFP. It involves everything from the tender document collection to writing a captivating proposal and ensuring all deadlines are adhered to.


## The Tender Process Explained


The tender process usually starts when there is a need for a particular company to procure goods or services. The company will write an inquiry letter to request suppliers or contractors in the market space to send them a tender. So that they can fulfill the needs of the client and then cost it to them.


Tender management in today's fast-moving technologically advanced world is especially interested in emerging technologies. Current trends in Government RFPs, especially for GWACs and IDIQ contracts, emphasize on experience with leading-edge technologies. This requirement underlines the realization by the government that technology plays a very significant role in response and recovery during crises.


In fact, companies should have the ability for quick adaptation, innovation, and delivery of solutions in high-stakes environments. This translates to ability in innovations such as 5G, Artificial Intelligence, Augmented Reality, Blockchain, and Cybersecurity, among others.


Effective tender management will not only require a documentation of projects executed in the past but also involvement in current research and development.

### Why is Tender Management Important?


Effective tender management becomes key to directly benefit a company's potential in acquiring new contracts. Thus, growing the business. Proper administration of the tender management process may result in winning the bid while the company loses opportunities and valuable resources otherwise.


### Key Benefits of Effective Tender Management:


Increased Success Rate: A methodical approach toward tender management helps in making a highly competitive, strong, and compliant bid.


Better Resource Allocation: With a structured approach, companies can optimize resources better and prevent the wastage of worthwhile time and money on bids that are likely to not be won.


Risk Mitigation: Proper tender management enables companies to identify any potential risks at the earlier stages of the process so that they can work to mitigate the risks before they become major issues.


Better Client Relationships: A professional and well-organized tender can speak well of the company and reflect positively on the client, potentially bettering future business relationships.


### Ideas for Improving Your Tender Management


Tender processes can be hard, from the point of view of the responder. By using some of the best practices, standing a better chance of winning those tough tenders.


Tender processes can be daunting for businesses looking to win lucrative contracts. From navigating complex requirements to facing fierce competition, responding to Requests for Proposals (RFPs) is no easy feat. However, by implementing proven best practices in tender management, RFP responders can significantly improve their chances of success.


1. **Completely Understand the RFP** : Every tender management process must begin with an understanding of the RFP. It's the document that gives all the pertinent details about client needs, project scope, and evaluation criteria. Spend time going through the RFP to ensure you understand it properly.


Tip: List key requirements and evaluation criteria in the RFP. Use these as a checklist which your services are to satisfy. Also you can check all about RFPs from our previous blogs.


**2. Do a Feasibility Study** : You need to do a feasibility study pertaining to your company and capability vis-à-vis the customers' requirements. There is a need to undertake a feasibility study regarding the company's technical ability, availability of resources, and its financial capability to undertake the project.


Tip: To be frank concerning the capabilities and weaknesses of your company. If the project scope is not within your current capability, very likely it's better for you to stay away from it; tendering for a weak reason is not in anyone's interest.


**3. Develop a Project Plan** : A good tender must have a project plan laid out very well, indicating how you will deliver the project, by when, and what key deliverables or outcomes are expected at major project delivery milestones. This should also cover any foreseen difficulties and possible ways of dealing with them.


Tip: Having a proper timeline in the project plan, ensure it is broken down to show the deliverable in phases. This gives the client the impression that, with a properly paved path, the project can easily meet the deadline.


**4. Concentrate on Compliance** : One of the main reasons an RFP does not work to drive success is noncompliance by your tender to the criteria set out in the RFP. To overcome this minor point, you must ensure that your tender communicates all criteria of the client in the RFP and addresses any specific instructions in the RFP.


Tip: Use a compliance matrix to cross-reference how well your proposal responds to specific RFP requirements. It will help you ensure nothing of importance is left out.


**5. Highlight Your Unique Value Proposition** : The most important point here is, of course, to find a way of saying how your company stands out from the other bidders in the competitive tendering environment. Stress what is different about your company: expertise, an innovative approach, or cost-effectiveness.


Tip: State your proposition with utmost clarity in your executive summary to the tender. This is usually the thing the client reads first; therefore, it has to be an eye-opener.


**6. Competitive Financial Proposal** : Your financial proposal must be competitive, transparent, and realistic. It should detail clear costs, which could include any contingencies. Underbidding or overbidding on your proposal are going to be huge red flags for the client.


Tip: Do a detailed cost-benefit analysis to help the client understand that your proposal provides the best value for money.


**7. Review and Revise** : After you are completely satisfied that there are neither errors nor omissions, you should also verify the tenders and do so on time. In many cases, you may need to have the tender reviewed by somebody not directly involved in the preparation to give a fresh, clear perspective.


Checklist: Make sure that there are no important documents missing. Your tender should be formatted according to the client's requirements.


**8. Submit on Time** : Make the deadline an absolute criteria. Late submissions and some other ones are usually disqualified, even if the tender is the strongest. Make sure to allow plenty of time for the unexpected delays, such as technical problems, or courier problems.


Hint: Try to put in your tender at least the day before the deadline, to eliminate the hassle of any last-minute problems.
