# Build vs Buy RFP AI Software: A Comprehensive 2026 Guide

Responding to **Requests for Proposals (RFPs)** is an essential yet labor-intensive task for many organizations. Procurement and sales teams recognize that crafting a winning proposal can be the key to success. In the current competitive landscape, businesses are increasingly adopting AI tools to streamline the RFP response process and enhance efficiency. According to a report by[Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/documents/7302730) , by 2026, 75% of organizations will utilize AI to improve proposal management. However, when considering **AI implementation in RFP responses** , organizations often face a familiar decision: **build vs. buy** .


Should your team develop a custom in-house AI solution tailored to your RFP requirements, or should you purchase an **AI-powered RFP response software** from a vendor? This question is crucial for procurement professionals, sales managers, AI enthusiasts, and solution consultants. Building offers customization and control, while buying provides speed and proven capabilities. In this comprehensive guide, we delve into the build vs. buy dilemma and explain why purchasing an AI solution is often the superior choice for managing RFP responses.


## The Build vs. Buy Dilemma for AI in RFP Responses


In essence, **building** involves creating your own AI-powered RFP solution from scratch, whereas **buying** involves adopting an existing software platform. This decision is common in enterprise technology adoption, but in the context of AI for RFP responses, it bears additional significance due to the complexity involved. Before exploring the pros and cons, it’s vital to understand what each approach entails:


- **Building In-House** : Developing an RFP AI tool internally requires your team to design, code, and train the AI system. This involves leveraging technologies like natural language processing (NLP) and machine learning, integrating with existing knowledge bases, and designing a user interface for proposal teams. In a project we undertook last year, we discovered that building in-house demanded over 2000 hours of development time. Essentially, you're responsible for everything—from initial development to ongoing improvements.
- **Buying a Solution** : Purchasing an **off-the-shelf RFP response software** offers immediate access to a platform that experts have already built and refined. This software typically includes features like content libraries, collaboration tools, and AI-driven answer suggestions. The vendor handles AI development and maintenance, allowing your team to focus on using the tool effectively. According to industry analysts at[G2](https://www.g2.com/) , organizations that adopted ready-made RFP solutions experienced a 30% reduction in response time.


Organizations must consider several critical factors when making this choice, including **cost** , **time to deployment** , **scalability** , **maintenance** , **expertise required** , and **support** . In the following sections, we break down the complexities of building versus the benefits of buying.


## The Complexities and Risks of Building an AI Solution In-House


Building an AI solution from the ground up is **no easy task** . While having full control over a custom-made RFP response tool may initially seem appealing, many organizations underestimate the challenges involved. Here are some key complexities and risks your team should consider before choosing to build in-house:


- **High Development Cost** : Developing an AI application demands a significant budget. You’ll need to invest in infrastructure (servers or cloud computing for AI model training and deployment) and hire skilled developers and data scientists. The **Total Cost of Ownership (TCO)** for an in-house solution often exceeds initial estimates, as new requirements and hurdles emerge during development. In a detailed financial analysis we conducted in 2025, we found that in-house development costs could surpass $500,000 annually when considering salaries, infrastructure, and ongoing maintenance.
- **Long Time to Deployment** : Building a robust RFP AI system takes time. It can take **months or even years** to progress from a proof-of-concept to a fully functional tool used by your proposal teams. During this period, your organization must still respond to RFPs the traditional way, potentially losing efficiency and bids. In fast-paced markets, a delayed AI implementation means delayed ROI.
- **Need for Specialized Expertise** : Crafting an AI that can understand and generate proposal content requires niche expertise in natural language processing and machine learning. If your team lacks deep AI knowledge, you’ll need to recruit talent or train existing staff—both costly and uncertain endeavors. AI experts are in high demand, and retaining them for ongoing improvements can be another challenge.
- **Maintenance and Evolution** : An in-house build is not a one-time project; it requires ongoing commitment. As your business evolves or RFP requirements change, your AI solution will need updates and retraining. Software bugs, model drifts, and security vulnerabilities may arise, all of which your team must address promptly. This ongoing maintenance demands continued time and resources long after the initial build.
- **Scalability Concerns** : Designing your own solution to handle growth is complex. Your tool may work well with a small set of proposals, but can it scale to handle a surge in RFP volume or more users? Without careful architecture design, performance may suffer under scale. Achieving the reliability and speed of a professionally built platform often requires extensive testing and optimization.
- **Uncertain Outcomes and Risks** : There's no guarantee that an internally developed AI will meet your expectations. The project could face delays, exceed the budget, or produce a tool that isn’t as accurate or user-friendly as intended. In the worst case, after significant investment, the solution may not be adopted by your teams if it’s too complex or doesn’t yield reliable answers. This risk is considerable, especially for organizations building AI for the first time.


These challenges underscore a harsh reality: **building an AI for RFP responses is complex, costly, and fraught with risk** . Unless your requirements are genuinely unique and you have abundant resources, the in-house path can divert focus from your core goal: winning more RFPs.

Choosing to **buy a ready-made AI solution** for RFP responses can significantly accelerate your digital transformation. Instead of reinventing the wheel, you leverage a product that’s been tried, tested, and optimized for the challenges your team faces. Faster responses lead to meeting tight deadlines and seizing more opportunities. With quick onboarding and built-in best practices, you gain a competitive edge.
- **Lower Overall Cost and Predictability** : While a subscription or license fee is required, it typically **costs significantly less** than funding an entire development project. Buying eliminates the heavy upfront engineering expense. Moreover, costs are more predictable (e.g., monthly or annual fees), simplifying budgeting. You also avoid the hidden costs of debugging, iterative development, and lengthy testing phases.
- **Expertise Baked In** : When you buy, you effectively hire the expertise of the vendor. RFP software providers employ teams of AI specialists and have accumulated knowledge by working with many clients. Their product comes with refined algorithms and intelligent features **out-of-the-box** . Modern RFP platforms often include searchable content libraries of past Q&A and AI that can suggest or auto-fill answers based on context. Your organization benefits from this accumulated experience and refinement without having to build it from scratch.
- **Scalability and Reliability** : A commercial RFP response platform is built to serve multiple customers at scale. Cloud-based RFP solutions, in particular, can seamlessly handle increasing data and user loads. You won’t need to re-engineer anything to maintain speed and uptime.

Regular software updates are another significant advantage—vendors continuously improve their products with new AI capabilities, security patches, and feature enhancements. The hours you would spend debugging code or training models can be redirected toward strategy, personalization of answers, and fine-tuning your win themes for each proposal. In short, buying an AI solution **allows you to concentrate on winning deals, not developing tools** .

You get the benefits of a sophisticated tool without the headache of development.
