### Traditional Banks & Credit Unions: Where the Minutes Disappear


Traditional lenders follow a multi-step process: qualifying, application evaluation, loan processing (document verification), underwriting, and final approval. Each handoff introduces potential delays.


Freddie Mac data shows that even during market stress,[average closing cycle times hit 44 days](https://sf.freddiemac.com/docs/pdf/fact-sheet/mortgage-cycle-time-benchmark-study.pdf) — a 10% increase from the prior year. The culprit: manual document verification and paper-based workflows.


Credit unions, while member-focused, face their own headwinds. The[delinquency rate at federally insured credit unions rose to 98 basis points](https://ncua.gov/newsroom/ncua-report/2024/quarterly-credit-union-data-summary-2024-q4) in Q4 2024, prompting tighter underwriting that can extend review times.


Lenders who maximize Freddie Mac’s Loan Product Advisor digital tools originate loans that are[on average $1,700 less costly](https://www.freddiemac.com/perspectives/sonu-mittal/how-freddie-mac-is-powering-efficiency-cost-savings) while shortening production timelines by five days. ## Borrower Playbook: Keep Your 30-Day Clock Ticking


Even the fastest lender can’t compensate for a disorganized borrower. Follow these steps to avoid self-inflicted delays:


1.


**Get pre-approved before house hunting.** As Colorado’s Division of Real Estate advises, buyers should[go to a lender to get pre-approved](https://dre.colorado.gov/the-transaction-process/lending-closing-understanding-the-real-estate-transaction-process) before shopping. This gives you a “heads up” on your borrowing capacity and signals serious intent to sellers.


2.


**Gather documents in advance.** Chestnut uses[proprietary technology to speed up mortgage preapproval](https://chestnutmortgage.com/news/know-before-buying-first-home) , cutting through the usual delays — but only if you provide accurate information upfront.


3.


**Lock your rate strategically.** Freddie Mac’s Loan Product Advisor results, including merged credit, are[valid for 120 days prior to the Note Date](https://sf.freddiemac.com/docs/pdf/fact-sheet/proc-va-mrtge-lpa5.pdf) , or 180 days for new construction. Don’t let your approval expire.


4.


**Respond to requests immediately.** The loan processing stage involves[verifying all documents the buyer provided](https://dre.colorado.gov/the-transaction-process/lending-closing-understanding-the-real-estate-transaction-process) . Delayed responses extend the timeline.


5.


**Avoid new credit applications.** Opening new accounts or making large purchases during underwriting can trigger re-verification and push your closing date.


**Key takeaway:** Borrower preparation is half the battle. Come organized, stay responsive, and protect your credit profile.


## Regulatory & Appraisal Roadblocks — And the Tech Work-Arounds


Colorado’s closing process involves specific legal instruments and regulatory requirements that can extend timelines.


Federal agencies including the OCC, FDIC, NCUA, CFPB, and FHFA have adopted[quality control standards for automated valuation models (AVMs)](https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/cfpb_automated-valuation-models_final-rule_2024-06.pdf) under the Dodd-Frank Act. These standards require lenders to adopt policies ensuring AVMs produce high-confidence estimates, protect against data manipulation, and comply with nondiscrimination laws.


While these requirements add compliance layers, they also validate the accuracy of tech-driven approaches. Institutions using AVMs must ensure systems[adhere to quality control standards](https://www.fhfa.gov/document/quality-control-standards-for-automated-valuation-models-final-rule) designed to ensure reliable property valuations.


The appraisal queue remains a common bottleneck. However, eClosings offer a workaround for the final stages.
