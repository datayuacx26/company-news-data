## What Factors Decide Your Conroe Mortgage Rate?


Your quoted rate depends on several personal and market-driven factors. Understanding these can help you position yourself for the lowest possible offer.


### Credit Score & DTI Benchmarks


Your credit score is the most influential factor lenders consider. According to NerdWallet,[FICO scores in the 740-850 range](https://www.nerdwallet.com/article/mortgages/how-to-get-the-best-mortgage-rate) are considered “very good” to “exceptional,” while scores from 670-739 are “good,” 580-669 are “fair,” and below 580 are “very poor.”


The Federal Trade Commission explains that[a credit score is a number typically between 300-850](https://consumer.ftc.gov/credit-scores) that estimates how likely you are to repay a loan on time. The higher your score, the better your terms.


Most conventional loans require a[minimum credit score of 620](https://www.bankrate.com/mortgages/improve-credit-before-mortgage/) , though you’ll need scores of 740 or higher to access the most competitive rates. Your debt-to-income (DTI) ratio matters too. Lenders typically want this number[no higher than 36%](https://www.nerdwallet.com/article/mortgages/how-to-get-the-best-mortgage-rate) .


### Timing the Market with a Rate Lock


A rate lock keeps your interest rate from changing between the offer and closing. The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau explains that[a rate lock ensures your interest rate won’t change](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/whats-a-lock-in-or-a-rate-lock-en-143/) as long as you close within the specified timeframe and make no changes to your application.


[Rate locks are typically available for 30, 45, or 60 days](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/whats-a-lock-in-or-a-rate-lock-en-143/) , and sometimes longer. According to Bankrate, you can[lock your rate for at least 30 days](https://www.bankrate.com/mortgages/what-is-mortgage-rate-lock/) , and in some cases up to 120 days depending on the lender and loan type.


The Federal Reserve notes that[lock-ins of 30 to 60 days are common](https://www.federalreserve.gov/pubs/lockins/default.htm) , though some lenders offer periods up to 120 days. Many lenders provide rate locks at no upfront cost, with the fee built into the rate itself.

Improving your credit score before applying can make a meaningful difference. ## Should You Refinance or Tap a HELOC at 2026 Conroe Rates?


If you already own a home in Conroe, you may be weighing whether to refinance or open a home equity line of credit (HELOC).


### Refinancing


Current refinance rates in Texas average[6.42% for 30-year fixed](https://www.lendingtree.com/home/mortgage/rates/texas/) and 5.93% for 15-year fixed loans. As Bankrate notes, the national average 30-year fixed refinance APR sits at[6.64%](https://www.bankrate.com/mortgages/refinance-rates/) .


Refinancing makes sense if you can lower your rate by at least 0.75 to 1 percentage point. As one Bankrate expert advises: “[If you have a chance to lower your rate](https://www.bankrate.com/mortgages/refinance-rates/) by a full percentage point or more, it is a good time to start the process.”


Refinance closing costs typically run[2% to 5% of the mortgage amount](https://www.bankrate.com/mortgages/refinance-rates/) , so calculate your break-even point before proceeding.


### HELOCs


A HELOC is[a revolving line of credit](https://sofi.com/heloc-rates-in-texas) that works like a credit card but is secured by your home’s equity. Current national HELOC rates average[8.22%](https://www.bankrate.com/home-equity/heloc-rates/) , according to Bankrate.


HELOC rates are tied to the prime rate, which has fluctuated significantly.[The prime rate ranged from 3.25% in 2020 to 8.50% in 2023](https://sofi.com/heloc-rates-in-texas) , directly influencing HELOC rates in Texas.

## How Much Are Closing Costs in Texas - and How Do You Lower Them?


Closing costs catch many buyers off guard. These fees[typically range from 2% to 5% of the total loan amount](https://www.bankrate.com/mortgages/what-are-closing-costs/) . For a $350,000 loan, that means somewhere between $7,000 and $17,500.


NerdWallet reports that average closing costs for buyers run[between about 2% and 6% of the loan amount](https://www.nerdwallet.com/article/mortgages/closing-costs-mortgage-fees-explained) . Texas-specific estimates from MortgageMark suggest[buyer closing costs of approximately $4,000](https://mortgagemark.com/mortgage-resource-library/closing-costs/estimated-closing-costs) for conventional financing, broken down as:


- **Lender fees:** $1,650
- **Other mortgage-related fees:** $900
- **Title fees:** $1,425
- **Miscellaneous fees:** $150


To reduce closing costs:


1.


**Negotiate seller concessions:** Ask the seller to cover a portion of your costs


2.


**Shop for title insurance:** Rates vary, so get multiple quotes


3.


**Compare lender fees:** This is where the biggest variations occur


4.


**Ask about lender credits:** Some lenders offer credits in exchange for a slightly higher rate


5.


**Time your closing:** Closing at month-end reduces prepaid interest charges
