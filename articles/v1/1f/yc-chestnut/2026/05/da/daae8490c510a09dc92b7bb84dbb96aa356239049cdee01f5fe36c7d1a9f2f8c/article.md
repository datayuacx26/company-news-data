## Should You Refinance or Tap a HELOC in Carrollton?


Carrollton homeowners with existing mortgages face a strategic decision: refinance the entire loan or access equity through a HELOC?


**When refinancing makes sense:**


- Your current rate is significantly higher than today’s rates
- You want to shorten your loan term
- You need a large lump sum for renovations or debt consolidation
- You prefer predictable fixed payments


**When a HELOC makes sense:**


- You have a low-rate mortgage you want to keep
- You need flexible access to funds over time
- You want to preserve your existing loan terms
- You only need a portion of your available equity


The numbers tell the story. The[average HELOC rate](https://www.lendingtree.com/home/home-equity/heloc/) for a $100,000 credit line sits near 8.75% as of May 2026, down significantly from 10.70% in late 2024. Meanwhile,[HELOC closing costs typically](https://www.lendingtree.com/home/home-equity/heloc/) range from 2% to 5% of your credit line amount.


To qualify for competitive HELOC rates, maintain a[credit score of 680 or higher](https://sofi.com/heloc-rates-in-texas) , ideally 700 or above. Most lenders also prefer a debt-to-income ratio below 36%.


A key consideration: HELOCs carry variable rates that fluctuate with market conditions. The[prime rate ranged from 3.25%](https://sofi.com/heloc-rates-in-texas) in 2020 to 8.50% in 2023, demonstrating how much payments can change over time.


Chestnut’s[tech-driven refinancing approach](https://chestnutmortgage.com/refinance) analyzes your situation to find the best deal while trimming weeks off the typical timeline. The platform uses smart tools to match you with rates that traditional brokers might miss.


**Key takeaway:** If you locked in a low rate before 2022, a HELOC often makes more sense than refinancing. If your current rate exceeds today’s market, refinancing may be worth exploring.


## What Texas Mortgage & AI Rules Must Borrowers Know?


Texas has specific regulations governing mortgage lending that protect consumers while enabling innovation.


**Licensing requirements:**


The[Texas SAFE Act](https://www.sml.texas.gov/mortgage-origination/faqs/) requires mortgage loan originators to be licensed unless exempt. The[Nationwide Multistate Licensing System (NMLS)](https://www.sml.texas.gov/mortgage-origination/faqs/) serves as the central database for licensing information, helping borrowers verify that their lender operates legally.


Two Texas agencies issue mortgage loan originator licenses: the Department of Savings and Mortgage Lending and the Office of Consumer Credit Commissioner. This dual oversight ensures comprehensive regulation of the industry.


**AI governance:**


Texas recently passed the[Texas Responsible Artificial Intelligence Governance Act](https://www.sml.texas.gov/news/announcement-2025-report-on-mortgage-lending-in-texas/) , which establishes requirements for AI systems used in lending. The[2025 Report on Mortgage Lending](https://www.sml.texas.gov/news/announcement-2025-report-on-mortgage-lending-in-texas/) includes a new section exploring how AI is reshaping underwriting, compliance, and consumer engagement.


The Federal Housing Finance Agency’s[2025 Scorecard](https://mortgageorb.com/fhfas-2025-scorecard-guides-the-gses-to-explore-the-use-of-ai-in-mortgage-lending) also guides Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac to “improve their risk management frameworks related to their use of artificial intelligence” and explore both benefits and risks of expanded AI use.


For borrowers, these regulations mean:


- AI-powered lenders must meet fair lending requirements
- You can verify your lender’s license through NMLS
- Transparency requirements help you understand how decisions are made
