## Remote Online Notarization (RON): The Final Frontier


### Understanding RON Technology


Remote Online Notarization represents the final piece of the digital closing puzzle. RON allows borrowers to complete their loan closing from anywhere with an internet connection, using video conferencing technology to connect with a licensed notary.


The RON process typically includes:


- Identity verification using government-issued ID and biometric matching
- Live video session with a commissioned notary
- Electronic signature of all closing documents
- Secure document storage and transmission


### RON State Adoption: Current Landscape


RON adoption varies significantly by state, creating a patchwork of availability across the country. As of 2026, the majority of states have enacted permanent RON legislation, but specific requirements and authorized notary qualifications differ.


**States with Full RON Authorization:**


- Most states now permit RON for real estate transactions
- Requirements vary for notary training and technology platforms
- Some states require in-state notaries, while others accept out-of-state commissioned notaries


**Limited or Pending States:**


- A handful of states still require traditional in-person notarization for real estate
- Some states allow RON but with restrictions on loan types or amounts
- Legislative changes continue to expand availability


### The SECURE Notarization Act: Game Changer Ahead


The pending SECURE Notarization Act could revolutionize the RON landscape by establishing nationwide standards for remote notarization. This federal legislation would:


- Create uniform RON requirements across all states
- Establish minimum technology and security standards
- Enable cross-state notarization without jurisdictional conflicts
- Provide legal certainty for lenders and borrowers


If passed, this act could make nationwide eClosings standard practice, eliminating the current state-by-state complexity that sometimes forces borrowers back to traditional closing methods.
