## What is contract review automation?


Contract review automation is the practice of using technology to streamline the review, analysis, negotiation, and approval of contracts.


Instead of requiring lawyers to manually review every agreement line by line, this approach relies on **rule-based systems, workflow automation, and AI** to identify key clauses, flag risks, extract critical information, and compare language against internal standards or legal playbooks.


In most organizations, contract review automation sits inside the broader **Contract Lifecycle Management (CLM) process** .


CLM covers the entire lifecycle of a contract, from intake and drafting to negotiation, approval, execution, renewal, and ongoing compliance management.


Contract review is one of the **most important workflow layers** inside that system because it determines whether an agreement is safe to execute.


So, how does this workflow become automated?


### How legal teams automate contract review


Legal teams automate contract review through several different approaches, depending on contract volume, workflow complexity, and risk tolerance.


These include:


- **Rules-based automation:** Use predefined templates, clause libraries, and keyword rules to identify standard terms, flag missing provisions, and detect deviations from approved language. This works best for routine, high-volume agreements with predictable language.
- **AI-powered contract review:** Use machine learning and natural language processing (NLP) to analyze contracts beyond basic keyword matching.
- **Hybrid review systems:** Combine rules-based logic with AI analysis to balance consistency, scalability, and flexibility.


Of these three approaches, AI-powered contract review has seen the fastest adoption and the biggest improvements in recent years. Understanding how it works in more detail explains why.


### How AI-powered contract review works


AI-powered contract review begins by converting a contract into machine-readable text. AI models then **analyze the agreement contextually** rather than relying on keyword detection.


This difference matters because rules-based systems depend on **predictable language** . So, once a clause is substantially rewritten, these traditional systems become less reliable at detecting legal risks. In contrast, AI models can still recognize the same legal meaning, even if the wording changes completely. In addition, the best AI systems **improve over time** because they learn from historical contracts, negotiation outcomes, redlines, and reviewer behavior.


That said, AI is still **far better at issue spotting than legal judgment.**


For example, AI can identify risky indemnity language. However, it can’t reliably decide whether accepting that risk makes strategic sense in the context of a specific customer relationship, deal size, or negotiation dynamic. That decision still requires **an attorney.**


However, most AI-powered contract review tools **only** handle the first-pass review.


After the software flags issues and surfaces risks, your internal legal team still has to interpret the findings, negotiate the agreement, and finalize the redlines. ## 8 biggest benefits of contract review automation


The eight benefits below explain why companies are replacing manual contract review with automated workflows.


### 1. It accelerates contract turnaround times


According to a[2023 academic study](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.16212v1) , junior lawyers take an average of 56 minutes to review a legal contract. Review time, however, **isn’t the same as turnaround time.** That’s because[commercial law firms](https://general.legal/blog/commercial-law-firms) continue to rely on manual workflows.


Contracts wait in queues, sit unread in inboxes, move between associates, and cycle through multiple rounds of redline before feedback is returned.


Contract review automation **removes much of that operational delay.**


The same study found that the fastest Large Language Model (LLM) outperforms junior lawyers by 77 times in speed, reducing first-pass review from nearly an hour to minutes. Even with a[hybrid review approach](https://general.legal/blog/contract-review-the-ai-native-model) like the one General Legal offers, contracts are typically returned in **hours instead of days or even weeks.**


### 2. It improves contract accuracy and consistency


A common criticism of automated systems is that they can make mistakes. However, the same is true of lawyers working through long review queues. As workloads increase, **consistency inevitably declines.**


By contrast, automated systems **apply the same review criteria to every agreement** , consistently flagging missing protections, outdated clauses, non-standard language, and deviations from internal playbooks.


The result is more consistent contract quality, fewer avoidable errors, and lower legal risk.


### 3. It strengthens compliance and risk management


Compliance problems rarely begin with a major legal failure; they stem from **small issues that go unnoticed** , such as:


- Outdated indemnity language
- A vendor agreement that violates internal policy
- A missing data processing clause


Manual review workflows are prone to missing these issues, especially under time pressure. That’s not the case with contract review automation, which creates a more controlled review process by:


- Analyzing every clause rather than prioritizing only the highest-risk sections
- Comparing contracts against approved templates, playbooks, and historical agreements to detect deviations
- Applying automated checks across large contract volumes without sacrificing coverage or consistency


### 4. It lowers legal operational costs


Reducing legal costs is[one of the biggest drivers](https://www.thomsonreuters.com/en-us/posts/wp-content/uploads/sites/20/2025/09/Legal-Department-Operations-Index-2025.pdf) behind contract review automation adoption.


The logic is straightforward.


Legal teams shouldn’t be paying thousands of dollars for attorneys to keep reviewing the same routine contract issues. Instead, AI systems can handle first-pass review, clause analysis, issue spotting, and document triage in minutes, allowing lawyers to focus on **negotiation strategy and higher-risk legal work** .


### 5. It increases visibility into contracts and obligations


Many companies have **limited visibility** into what their contracts contain, where agreements sit in the approval process, and which obligations create legal or commercial risk.


Contract review automation changes this by:


- Centralizing contract data
- Surfacing key obligations automatically
- Giving teams real-time visibility into contract status, renewal timelines, risk exposure, and non-standard terms


As a result, teams can spend **less time searching for information** and **more time acting on it.**


### 6. It scales legal workflows without adding headcount


It’s estimated that a typical Fortune 1000 company manages anywhere[between 20,000 and 40,000](https://businesslawtoday.org/2019/11/contract-analytics-new-frontier-gaining-sustaining-competitive-advantage/) active contracts at any given time. Most startups and growing companies never reach that level of contract volume. But even at lower volumes, **manual contract workflows become hard to sustain once the business starts scaling** .


Contract review automation allows businesses to absorb that growth without adding more reviewers and coordinators. That’s because automated systems can **process agreements in bulk** , automate first-pass review, standardize approval workflows, and route contracts automatically.


### 7. It improves collaboration across teams


Contract review can become increasingly difficult to coordinate **as more stakeholders become involved** . Without a centralized workflow, the legal department might review one version, sales might edit another, and finance might still approve an outdated draft.


Contract review automation addresses this risk by keeping edits, approvals, comments, and redlines synchronized, reducing duplicate work, version conflicts, and coordination mistakes.


### 8. It allows teams to focus on higher-value work


Legal expertise is most valuable when applied to judgment, not repetitive contract administration. By eliminating a huge amount of **low-leverage review work** , contract review automation allows legal teams to spend more time:


- Negotiating higher-stakes agreements
- Evaluating complex legal risks
- Advising clients on business strategy
- Handling sensitive commercial decisions
- Managing difficult counterparties
- Accelerating critical deals and partnerships

## 7 practical use cases for contract review automation


Contract review automation delivers the greatest value when legal teams review large volumes of repetitive agreements under tight deadlines.


Some of its most common use cases include:


- **Reviewing NDAs and vendor agreements:** High-volume agreements like[NDAs](https://general.legal/library/mutual-non-disclosure-agreement-nda) , procurement documents, and standard vendor contracts are ideal candidates for automated first-pass review because the same issues tend to appear repeatedly.
- **Handling M&A due diligence and bulk contract analysis** : Large-scale contract reviews become substantially more manageable when AI systems can sort, categorize, summarize, and extract key terms from thousands of agreements.
- **Running procurement and sales contract workflows:** Faster review workflows help sales, procurement, and operations teams move agreements through approval and signature without constant legal bottlenecks.
- **Comparing contracts against internal playbooks:** AI systems can automatically detect deviations from approved legal language, fallback clauses, and company risk standards before agreements are approved.
- **Extracting contract metadata and obligations:** Legal teams can pull renewal dates, payment terms, notice periods, and operational obligations from contracts without manually searching through documents.
- **Performing compliance and regulatory checks:** AI systems can identify contracts missing required protections or containing language that violates internal policy or regulatory requirements.
- **Identifying risky or non-standard clauses:** Contract review automation helps surface problematic language early, reducing the likelihood of risky terms reaching execution.


The use cases above make it clear that some tasks are ideal for automation, while others still require judgment. The good news is that **you don’t have to choose between the two.**

## FAQ


### How to perform a contract review?


Performing a contract review means analyzing an agreement for legal, financial, operational, and compliance risks before signing.


### Can I use AI to review a contract?


Yes, you can use AI to review a contract, as it can quickly identify risky clauses, compare language against legal playbooks, extract key terms, and flag non-standard provisions. Most companies use AI to accelerate first-pass review while lawyers handle final legal judgment.


### Is there a free AI tool for contract review?


Yes, there are several free AI tools for contract review. However, free tools are usually best suited for simple agreements and shouldn’t replace legal review for high-value or heavily negotiated contracts.


### Can ChatGPT review a contract?


Yes, ChatGPT can review a contract. However, it shouldn’t be treated as a substitute for qualified legal counsel, especially for complex or business-critical agreements.
