# 8 Major Challenges for Commercial Law Firms Today [Explained]

[Commercial law firms](https://general.legal/blog/commercial-law-firms) are the counsel businesses turn to for help navigating the **legal complexities of starting, operating, and growing a company.**


On paper, 2026 has[started strongly for these firms](https://www.thomsonreuters.com/en-us/posts/legal/lffi-q1-2026-strong-inputs-average-output/) .


In the first quarter, **demand grew across most core commercial practice areas** , including M&A (+4.4%), litigation (+2.9%), corporate law (+2.9%),[labor and employment](https://general.legal/employment) (+2.8%), and real estate (+2.6%). Firms are coming off a year of double-digit profit growth, and many continue to see healthy workloads.


However, **profit and stability aren’t the same thing** . The same forces driving revenue growth are also making it more difficult for firms to attract talent, deliver services efficiently, meet client expectations, and protect margins.


With that in mind, here are the **eight major challenges for commercial law firms** that will separate tomorrow’s market leaders from the rest.


## Key takeaways


- **AI is reshaping every aspect of commercial law**
AI is no longer optional for commercial law firms. The challenge is adopting it responsibly and balancing greater efficiency with human oversight, data security, and professional accountability.
- **Clients expect more value than ever before**
Today’s corporate clients want predictable pricing, faster turnaround times, commercial insight, and measurable business value. Firms that fail to meet these expectations risk losing work to more innovative competitors.
- **Operational efficiency has become a competitive advantage**
Fragmented systems, manual workflows, and outdated processes cost firms thousands of billable hours every year. Modernizing operations allows lawyers to spend more time on strategic legal work and less on administrative tasks.
- **Success requires more than legal expertise**
The firms pulling ahead are investing in technology, regulatory expertise, cybersecurity, and talent while remaining agile enough to adapt to changing client needs and market conditions.
- **AI-native law firms are built for the future**
Instead of retrofitting traditional ways of working, AI-native firms like[General Legal](https://general.legal/) combine technology with experienced lawyers to deliver faster contract turnaround, transparent flat-fee pricing, and commercially focused legal advice designed for modern businesses.


## 8 main challenges for commercial law firms amid rapid industry change


The eight main challenges for commercial law firms are largely driven by four forces: **technological change, shifting client expectations, growing operational demands** , and an **increasingly complex business environment** . Together, they are reshaping how legal services are delivered, priced, and evaluated.


### 1. The AI adoption dilemma


Few developments have affected the legal industry as quickly as[generative AI](https://general.legal/blog/generative-ai-for-law-firms) .


Today,[79% of lawyers](https://legal.thomsonreuters.com/blog/the-reality-check-why-in-house-counsel-are-moving-beyond-basic-ai-tools/) report using AI daily, and **firms are investing heavily to keep pace.** Industry spending on technology and technology-related staff grew by[10.6% in 2025 alone](https://www.thomsonreuters.com/en-us/posts/legal/lffi-q1-2026-strong-inputs-average-output/) as firms rushed to integrate AI into research,[contract review](https://general.legal/blog/contract-review-automation) , due diligence, drafting, and other traditionally time-intensive tasks.


The business case for using AI in legal service delivery is compelling, and **clients have noticed the technology’s benefits, too.**


[Nearly 80% of corporate clients](https://institute.thomsonreuters.com/en/institute/future-of-professionals-2026/report#intro) now say AI-enabled improvements are important when selecting professional service providers, and 32% are willing to reconsider relationships with law firms they believe are falling behind.


But if firms want AI, clients expect AI, and the value proposition of AI is undeniable, where exactly is the challenge?


The answer lies in the difference between just adopting AI and doing so **responsibly.**


Firms must contend with[numerous risks of AI in law](https://general.legal/blog/risks-of-ai-in-law) , including hallucinations, confidentiality concerns, governance failures, and the growing use of unsanctioned “shadow AI” tools by employees. At the same time, **lawyers remain professionally responsible** for the advice they give, regardless of how much technology assisted in producing it.


This is where the main challenge emerges for commercial law firms: Move too slowly and risk losing clients to more technologically advanced competitors; move too quickly and risk creating substantial operational, ethical, and legal problems.


Overcoming this challenge successfully requires **treating AI as a tool to augment legal expertise** , while building strong governance and human oversight into every stage of adoption.


### 2. The billable hour crisis


For decades, the billable hour has been the foundation of the commercial law firm business model. **More hours meant more revenue** , making it easy for firms to price work, measure productivity, and grow profits.


While that model still dominates today, with[roughly 90% of legal spend](https://legal.thomsonreuters.com/blog/the-2000-hour-problem-when-ai-efficiency-collides-with-billable-time-tri/) flowing through hourly billing arrangements, there are growing signs that it’s[coming under pressure.](https://general.legal/blog/the-death-of-the-billable-hour)


According to the[2026 Future Ready Lawyer Survey](https://www.wolterskluwer.com/en/know/future-ready-lawyer-2026#download) , 62% of corporate legal departments and 57% of law firms expect **AI-driven efficiencies to significantly reduce the prevalence of the billable hour.**


This shift is being driven by a simple reality: Clients want more certainty around legal spend, more transparency around pricing, and more value from every engagement, and **AI is making those expectations easier to meet.** The result is a growing tension between a business model that rewards time spent and a market that values efficiency more than ever.


Commercial law firms that effectively adapt will be those that find **new ways to price and demonstrate value** beyond hours worked.


### 3. The rise of the empowered client


Corporate clients are becoming more **sophisticated** , more **cost-conscious** , and more **demanding** . Equipped with stronger in-house legal teams, benchmarking data, and expanding AI capabilities, many no longer rely on outside counsel for routine legal support.


Instead, commercial law firms are being asked to provide:


- Commercial recommendations, not just legal analysis
- Industry-specific expertise and market context
- Faster turnaround times and responsiveness
- Seamless collaboration with in-house legal teams
- Data-backed insights rather than purely legal opinions
- Support on increasingly complex cross-border and regulatory matters
- Well-defined explanations that non-lawyer executives can act on


Clients are also becoming more **vocal about the value they expect from law firms** , with[one in four corporate buyers](https://www.thomsonreuters.com/en-us/posts/wp-content/uploads/sites/20/2026/01/2026-State-of-the-US-Legal-Market.pdf) reporting they’ve never experienced a firm that delivered excellent value despite premium pricing.


The commercial law firms that thrive will be those that understand their clients’ businesses as well as they understand the law.


### 4. The battle for legal talent


Attracting and retaining top legal talent remains a major challenge for commercial law firms, particularly as lawyers **gain more options** and become **more selective about where they work.**


Key drivers behind this challenge include:


- Growing competition from in-house legal teams, boutiques, and alternative legal service providers
- Rising expectations around flexibility, culture, and work-life balance
- Declining interest in the traditional partner-track model
- Increasing compensation costs and pressure to match market rates


AI is adding a **new layer of complexity.**


While this technology can reduce repetitive work, it’s also reshaping career expectations and professional development.[Nearly 3 in 10 mid-career professionals](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/69b41ee5dd715b5e9ce8c851/6a3594a2ba34e8bcdf2f1f5a_Future%20of%20Professionals%202026.pdf) say they would change jobs within two years if AI failed to deliver the value they expect, while 71% believe early-career lawyers need structured support to develop skills that AI can now automate.


The firms that win this battle will be those that **invest in both people and the systems** that help legal talent grow.


### 5. The growing cyber threat


Commercial law firms are prime targets for cybercriminals because they hold some of the **most valuable corporate information** , including M&A plans, intellectual property, litigation strategy, and confidential client communications.


A single cyberattack can lead to severe consequences, including:


- Regulatory investigations and compliance violations
- Loss of sensitive corporate and transaction data
- Business disruption caused by ransomware attacks
- Significant financial losses and remediation costs


While the average law firm data breach now costs[more than $5 million](https://programs.com/resources/law-firm-cyberattack-statistics/) , the greater risk is often **reputational** . A financial loss can be recovered; lost client trust can result in years of lost revenue and missed opportunities.


Avoiding this outcome requires firms to **build security into every layer of their operations** , from technology infrastructure to employee behavior.


### 6. The geopolitical ripple effect


Commercial law firms don’t operate in a vacuum. Trade disputes, sanctions, inflation, supply chain disruptions, and geopolitical conflicts **** now **shape both client priorities and legal demand** to a greater extent.


This geopolitical and economic volatility creates both **opportunity and uncertainty** for law firms **.**


For example, tariffs and trade restrictions are generating new work in areas like regulatory compliance, contract disputes, sanctions advice, and cross-border transactions. At the same time, many businesses are delaying acquisitions, investments, and expansion plans until market conditions become clearer.


This uncertainty is also adding **complexity** to legal work.


M&A transactions now require deeper scrutiny of supply chains, national security risks, and geopolitical exposure, while clients expect their lawyers to translate these risks into practical business advice.


Successfully navigating this challenge requires commercial law firms to **remain agile as client needs shift with changing market conditions.**


### 7. The regulatory burden


Commercial law firms are facing **growing regulatory pressure** as they must comply with increasingly stringent rules while also helping clients navigate an ever-expanding web of legal obligations.


Compliance includes keeping pace with:


- AI governance and[data privacy laws](https://general.legal/data-privacy)
- Environmental, social, and governance (ESG) reporting requirements
- Competition and antitrust regulation
- Cross-border sanctions and trade compliance
- [Anti-money laundering (AML) and Know Your Client (KYC)](https://legal.thomsonreuters.com/blog/overview-kyc-and-aml/) requirements


Beyond the **sheer volume** of regulation, the challenge also lies in the **speed** at which it evolves. New reporting deadlines, stricter compliance requirements, and overlapping rules across jurisdictions require ongoing investment in technology, training, and specialist expertise.


### 8. The efficiency gap


Operational inefficiencies continue to hold many commercial law firms back, reducing productivity, increasing costs, and slowing client service.


According to a survey of 133 senior decision-makers from top-ranked Am Law firms,[67% of firms](https://www.law.com/americanlawyer/2026/06/04/law-firms-ai-investments-for-now-are-failing-to-fix-operational-inefficiencies/) estimate they **lose between one and three billable hours** per lawyer every week due to operational friction. A further 25% estimate losses of **four hours per lawyer** . For a 500-lawyer firm, that equates to roughly 67,000 lost billable hours every year—and millions of dollars in lost revenue.


Common sources of inefficiency include:


- Fragmented systems and disconnected data
- Manual document, billing, and administrative workflows
- Complex client billing requirements
- Poor knowledge management and duplicated work


Closing the efficiency gap requires firms to streamline workflows, integrate their technology stack, and automate repetitive tasks so lawyers can focus on higher-value legal work.

## FAQ


### What is a commercial law firm?


A commercial law firm is a legal practice that advises businesses on matters such as contracts, corporate transactions, regulatory compliance, employment law, intellectual property, and commercial disputes.


### What is the biggest challenge facing law firms today?


Artificial intelligence is arguably the biggest challenge facing law firms today. Firms must adopt AI to remain competitive and meet client expectations, while managing the ethical, legal, and operational risks that come with using it in legal practice.


### What is the biggest challenge for a lawyer?


One of the biggest challenges for lawyers is adapting to rapid technological change while continuing to deliver accurate, commercially sound legal advice.


### What are the major threats confronting the legal profession?


The major threats confronting the legal profession include technological disruption, cyberattacks, talent shortages, and regulatory pressure.
