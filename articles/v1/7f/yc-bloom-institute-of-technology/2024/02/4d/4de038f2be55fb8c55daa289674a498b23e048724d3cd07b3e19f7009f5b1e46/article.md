# How to become a data scientist

So, you want to learn how to become a data scientist. Good choice! Data science is one of the buzziest and most lucrative fields to emerge in the past decade, and for good reason. It’s the secret sauce behind the innovations that make our lives more efficient – speech recognition, search engine optimization, food delivery, and self-driving cars, just to name a few.


Data scientists can be found powering nearly every industry in the world right now, not just tech, and there are a myriad of ways to dip your toes in.


# What is a data scientist?


“Data Science” is a nebulous term and data scientists wear many hats, including mathematician, statistician, and computer scientist. The main responsibility of a data scientist is to wrangle big sets of structured or unstructured data from sources like social media feeds, smart devices, and emails then transform them into a more usable format that can help solve business or organization-related challenges. Data scientists create software algorithms, analyze and process data, support machine learning, and create personalized data products for decision-making. They also communicate and work closely with IT teams and businesses to help them grow.


# How to become a data scientist


Becoming a data scientist has a lot of appealing perks—job security and generous pay, for starters—but it also requires extensive training and hard work. If you want to be a data scientist, you have to be fully committed.


So how do you begin? Here are some first steps.


### **Learn to love data.**


**‍** One of the foundational skills you’ll need to succeed as a data scientist is love for what you’re doing. It sounds pretty darn obvious, but without a lot of love for and curiosity about the work, it will be hard to find the motivation to move forward in a field that can be fairly challenging.


Think about what you’re curious about. Are you interested in what makes a person click on a news article, how many trails are in your city, how many women have served in government throughout history, or how much the cost of textbooks has risen overtime? There are an infinite number of questions you can ask that data can help solve. The first steps to loving data are to ask lots of questions and practice looking for the answers. You might even create your own databases to see how you can visualize and share what you learn.


### ‍ **Get familiar with the basics of data science.**


**‍** An important step that you can take right now is familiarizing yourself with basic data science terms and databases. Becoming a data scientist requires a solid understanding of coding languages, mathematics theory, and data modeling. On the technical side, you’ll need to understand or become familiar with things like:


- Statistics and machine learning
- Coding languages like SAS, R, or Python
- Writing SQL theories
- Data visualization and reporting
- Data mining and cleaning
- Workflow and collaboration skills


There are lots of languages and skills to keep track of, which is why structured courses are especially helpful to the budding data scientist.


### ‍ **Get your secondary education.**


Getting your foot in the door as an entry level data scientist often requires at least a bachelor’s degree in data science or a related field like mathematics or statistics for most employers. That said, if you’ve got a degree in a different field or even just a lot of grit, initiative, and innate curiosity, you can learn most data science skills by enrolling in a bootcamp or an online data scientist course.


### ‍ **Enroll in a free data science bootcamp or online course.**

### **Join a data science community.**


Data science is ever evolving, and it helps to have a network of peers by your side who are doing similar work. There are lots of data science communities you can participate in online that can help you find the code you need, build models, network and collaborate with other data scientists, and discover fresh content.


A few communities worth looking into are:


- [Kaggle](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets?utm_medium=paid&utm_source=google.com+search&utm_campaign=datasets&gclid=Cj0KCQiAqdP9BRDVARIsAGSZ8AlCfSbYQpo0WDi7VKgbTCq31Uklh2JaRLzELwnLRJrMULZfSl6uP9MaAgsTEALw_wcB) : With close to 3 million members, Kaggle is one of the largest data science communities in the world. Here you can use over[50,000 public datasets](https://www.analyticsinsight.net/top-10-data-science-communities-every-data-scientist-must-know/) and 400,000 public notebooks to aid in your analysis.
- [Data Science Central:](https://www.datasciencecentral.com/) DSC provides data scientists with a platform for social interaction with other data scientists, forum-based support, and the latest tools and trends in the industry.
- [Stack Overflow](https://stackoverflow.com/) : Stack Overflow is a great resource for all of your burning coding questions. It’s helpful for both web developers and data scientists.
- [IBM Data Community](https://community.ibm.com/community/user/ai-datascience/home) : Data scientists can find blogs, forums, webcasts, presentations, and the latest research on IBM.
