# What’s In My Bag: Sony Alpha 7 V + RX1R III for Travel & Street Photography

[Ming Han](https://www.instagram.com/minghan1004/) is a travel and street photographer based in Kuala Lumpur, Malaysia. "A lot of my photography comes from traveling – exploring a city on foot, observing everyday life, photographing architecture and street scenes, and finding those quieter moments in between. I also shoot hotels and hospitality, where I focus a lot on interiors, design details and the overall atmosphere of a space."


Because Han travels frequently, he tries to keep his camera bag versatile rather than carrying equipment for only one specific type of photography. "I want a setup that can take me from photographing a hotel interior in the morning, to walking the streets for the rest of the day, and then shooting during blue hour or at night without having to completely change my kit," he says.


Read below as Han walks through the setup he currently relies on for most of his travel, street, hotel photography and video work.

#### Lenses


[16-35mm f/2.8 G Master II](https://electronics.sony.com/imaging/lenses/all-e-mount/p/sel1635gm2?utm_source=AlphaUniverse&utm_medium=Web&utm_campaign=Article) : If I had to choose the lens that gets the most use in my bag, the


[16-35mm f/2.8 G Master II](https://electronics.sony.com/imaging/lenses/all-e-mount/p/sel1635gm2?utm_source=AlphaUniverse&utm_medium=Web&utm_campaign=Article) would be very high on the list.


It is especially important for my hotel photography. The wider focal lengths allow me to photograph rooms, architecture and interior spaces while still showing the relationship between different design elements.


Photo by Ming Han. Alpha 7 V. 16-35mm f/2.8 G Master II. 1/40-sec., f/2.8, ISO 2,500


But I don’t only use it for interiors. I also enjoy taking it onto the streets. At the wider end, I can get close to a subject while keeping plenty of the surrounding environment in the frame, which helps give the photograph more context.


It’s also one of my favorite lenses for video. I use it frequently when creating my “Moving Postcards” travel series because the wide perspective works well for establishing shots, architecture and movement through a city.


[28–70mm f/2 G Master](https://electronics.sony.com/imaging/lenses/full-frame-e-mount/p/sel2870gm?utm_source=AlphaUniverse&utm_medium=Web&utm_campaign=Article) : The


[28-70mm f/2 G Master](https://electronics.sony.com/imaging/lenses/full-frame-e-mount/p/sel2870gm?utm_source=AlphaUniverse&utm_medium=Web&utm_campaign=Article) is the lens I bring when I want one lens to cover most of the day.


When traveling, constantly changing lenses isn’t always practical. With the 28-70mm range, I can photograph wider street scenes and architecture, then quickly move into tighter details, portraits or everyday moments without changing lenses.


I also really like the image quality and sharpness I get from it. Having the constant f/2 aperture gives me another creative option, especially when I want more subject separation or when I’m shooting later in the day.


It is a larger lens, but when I know I want maximum versatility from a single setup, this is usually the one I choose.

[50–150mm f/2 G Master](https://electronics.sony.com/imaging/lenses/all-e-mount/p/sel50150gm?utm_source=AlphaUniverse&utm_medium=Web&utm_campaign=Article) : The


[50–150mm f/2 G Master](https://electronics.sony.com/imaging/lenses/all-e-mount/p/sel50150gm?utm_source=AlphaUniverse&utm_medium=Web&utm_campaign=Article) was the lens that really made me explore longer focal lengths more seriously.


A lot of my earlier photography leaned towards wider perspectives, so using 50-150mm changed the way I looked at a scene. Instead of always moving closer, I started looking for compositions from farther away and using compression to layer people, buildings and other elements together.


I especially enjoy it for street photography in busy cities. Shooting from a distance allows me to isolate small moments within a crowded environment, while the longer focal lengths can make different layers of the city appear much closer together.


It has opened up another way of seeing when I travel.

[50mm f/1.4 G Master](https://electronics.sony.com/imaging/lenses/all-e-mount/p/sel50f14gm?utm_source=AlphaUniverse&utm_medium=Web&utm_campaign=Article) : The

I especially like it for portraits and street photography. The 50mm perspective feels natural to me – it’s tight enough to isolate a subject but still gives enough context to tell a story about where the person is.


When I want stronger separation between the subject and background, the f/1.4 aperture gives me that softer look and beautiful bokeh while still keeping the subject very sharp.


It’s also a good lens for walking around at night, when I want to work with available light rather than carrying additional lighting equipment.

I really enjoy the 35mm focal length because it gives me a natural balance between the subject and the environment. It’s wide enough to include the surroundings and tell more of the story, but still lets me get close to a subject without making the frame feel too wide.


Its compact and relatively lightweight design also makes it easy to carry during long walks around a city. The f/1.4 aperture is also useful when shooting in lower light or when I want to separate a subject from a busy street background.


For street photography, it’s one of those lenses that lets me focus less on the gear and more on simply walking, observing and capturing what happens around me.

### Final Thoughts


At the end of the day, I don’t think about my camera bag as having a piece of gear for every possible situation. I would rather carry equipment that gives me different ways of seeing the same place.


Some days that means walking around almost unnoticed with the[RX1R III](https://electronics.sony.com/imaging/compact-cameras/all-vlog-compact-cameras/p/dscrx1rm3b?utm_source=AlphaUniverse&utm_medium=Web&utm_campaign=Article) . Other days I’ll use the[16–35mm](https://electronics.sony.com/imaging/lenses/all-e-mount/p/sel1635gm2?utm_source=AlphaUniverse&utm_medium=Web&utm_campaign=Article) to show the scale of an interior, the[35mm](https://electronics.sony.com/imaging/lenses/all-e-mount/p/sel35f14gm?utm_source=AlphaUniverse&utm_medium=Web&utm_campaign=Article) for a lightweight day of street photography, the[50–150mm](https://electronics.sony.com/imaging/lenses/all-e-mount/p/sel50150gm?utm_source=AlphaUniverse&utm_medium=Web&utm_campaign=Article) to compress a street scene from across the road, or the[28–70mm](https://electronics.sony.com/imaging/lenses/full-frame-e-mount/p/sel2870gm?utm_source=AlphaUniverse&utm_medium=Web&utm_campaign=Article) when I simply want one lens to cover almost everything.


The gear changes depending on the trip, but the goal is always the same:


to spend less time thinking about equipment and more time paying attention to what is happening around me.
