SECTION 02


## The Traditional Approach


The traditional approach is capital intensive.


Large corporations run vehicle development programs with $10 to $40 million budgets, dedicated engineering teams, and multi-year timelines.


Building a prototype vehicle from scratch requires solving every problem simultaneously: battery pack design, motor selection and integration, thermal management, vehicle control software, safety systems, and charging infrastructure.


Each component requires specialized engineering that most teams do not have.


For startups and small businesses, building affordable and reliable electric vehicles is even more difficult. If not impossible.


Today, we see startups relying on salvage Tesla parts, unreliable aftermarket battery and motor components, and trying to reverse engineer vehicle systems without proper documentation or safety validation.


The result is that prototype programs spend 80% of their budget on powertrain development before they ever validate their actual product idea.


By the time the vehicle runs, the company has burned through most of its runway.


Traditional development requires years of powertrain engineering before validation

The adjustable wheelbase (133-146 inches) supports trucks, vans, sedans, coupes, and custom designs. Payload capacity of 3,550 lbs accommodates most body configurations.

Yes. The platform is a real, functional vehicle with validated performance specifications. 174 HP, 121 mph top speed, and up to 255 miles range. Investors can ride in your prototype, not just look at slides.

Is this just for prototypes or can it scale to production?


+


The Olympus platform is production ready. Start with a single prototype, then scale to fleet volumes using the same architecture. No redesign required when you go from prototype to production.


05


Can I modify the platform software for custom vehicle behavior?


+


Yes. The vehicle control system provides API access for custom behaviors, telemetry logging, and integration with your own software systems. Development teams can tune throttle response, regenerative braking, and other parameters.

What documentation and technical support is included?


+


Every platform ships with complete CAD files, electrical schematics, mounting specifications, and integration guides. Engineering support is available for body mounting questions and custom integration challenges.

How do I integrate sensors or autonomous driving systems?


+


The platform includes CAN bus access and power provisions for aftermarket sensors, LiDAR, cameras, and computing hardware. Several autonomous vehicle development teams already use Olympus as their base platform.
