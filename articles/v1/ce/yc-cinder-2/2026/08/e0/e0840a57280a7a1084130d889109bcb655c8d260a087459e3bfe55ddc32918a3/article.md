# Navigating Trust and Safety in Online Marketplaces

Maintaining a reputable online marketplace is more complex than ever. Tackling complex risks like fraud, harmful content, and illicit activities requires innovative operational models, cutting-edge technology, and deep community engagement.


Join Cinder CEO Glen Wise as he dives into the complexities of trust and safety in online marketplaces with Depop's Sophie Walsh and Patreon's Chris Price. Together, they discuss protecting marketplace users while fostering growth.


Glen Wise speaks with Depop's Sophie Walsh and Patreon's Chris Price about trust and safety in online marketplaces.


## Understanding Trust and Safety in Online Marketplaces


Trust and safety are crucial in online marketplaces due to the inherent risks associated with transactions between strangers. Wise emphasized that marketplaces are among the most complex environments due to their operational scale, diversity of sellers and buyers, and regulatory landscapes.


Price highlighted that marketplaces involve a wide array of seller types, from digital creators to physical artisans, each operating under different legal regimes, tax laws, and content standards. This variability demands flexible policies capable of accommodating everything from virtual art to handmade crafts, while ensuring user safety and legal compliance.


## Trust and Safety Can Drive Significant Growth


Historically viewed as a cost center, trust and safety teams are increasingly seen as strategic partners capable of driving growth. Walsh shared how Depop shifted this perception over recent years by focusing on community education, transparency, and a reform-oriented approach.


An essential part of this transformation was treating moderation as a reform process rather than merely content removal. Depop engages users in understanding policies, fosters a community-centric approach, and ensures fair enforcement. These measures not only reduce harm but also enhance platform loyalty and economic activity.


## Building and Evolving Nuanced Policies


As platforms mature, they face increasingly complex challenges in creating policies. Price shared lessons learned from early policy missteps, like rushing to ban AI-generated explicit content without clear definitions. He highlighted the importance of transparency, community input, and iterative refinement. Engaging creators in policy discussions through regular feedback loops builds trust and ensures regulations align with community expectations.


## The Future of Trust and Safety: Trends and Opportunities


Looking ahead, Walsh and Price see exciting opportunities in emerging technologies that promise to reduce reliance on manual review. They are looking to tools like large language models and AI-driven reform processes to support the enforcement of their policies.


They also recognize the importance of building trustworthy, transparent policies rooted in community input. Walsh noted that companies are now more proactive, safety-focused, and community-centered in their approach. Price pointed out that as trust and safety teams grow in strategic importance, organizations are better equipped to handle complex risks and foster user trust, ultimately creating more sustainable, engaging platforms.
