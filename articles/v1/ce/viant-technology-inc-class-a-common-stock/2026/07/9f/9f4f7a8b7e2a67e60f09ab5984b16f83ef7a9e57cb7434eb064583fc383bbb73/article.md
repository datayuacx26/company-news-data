# Brand Safety vs Brand Suitability: What Advertisers Need to Know

# Brand Safety vs Brand Suitability: What Advertisers Need to Know


July 10, 2026 in[Blog](https://www.viantinc.com/insights/blog/)


## Brand Safety vs. Brand Suitability: What Advertisers Need To Know


In today’s fast-paced digital ecosystem, every ad placement is essentially a bet on context. Landing an advertisement next to the wrong content can inflict reputational damage that far outweighs any short-term campaign gains.


Because of these high stakes, brand safety and brand suitability have shifted from mundane checkboxes to strategic priorities. Especially when buying media at programmatic scale.


But there’s a core tension that often trips up even seasoned marketers: “the wrong content” actually means two entirely different things. Conflating universal safety with subjective suitability leads to over-blocking and missed opportunities, ultimately costing advertisers on both fronts.


Getting this critical distinction right separates a purely defensive posture from a modern strategy that protects your brand’s reputation while still effectively reaching your target audiences.


To help navigate this nuanced landscape, we chatted with[Sid Sharma, VP of Programmatic Strategy](https://www.linkedin.com/in/sid-sharma-57456a30/) . He explains the key differences between safety and suitability, and how advertisers can master both.


## What Is Brand Safety?


Brand safety is the practice of protecting a brand’s reputation by keeping its ads out of environments that could damage its image.


It has been a core concern in[programmatic advertising](https://www.viantinc.com/solutions/omnichannel/programmatic-linear-tv/) since automated buying made ad placement harder to predict. As Sharma puts it, in programmatic advertising, “Brand Safety refers to the notion that protecting a brand’s reputation is the top priority and, thus, ads associated with the brand should not be served in environments that could potentially damage the brand’s image.” Said simply, and per the Interactive Advertising Bureau (IAB), brand safety is defined as keeping a brand’s reputation safe when advertising online.


The discipline traces back to the earliest days of automated buying. As Sharma explains, “The origins of Brand Safety came about in the early days of programmatic advertising because buyers could not always predict where their ad would appear due to Real Time Bidding (RTB) dynamics. To help advertisers tackle the Brand Safety issue, the industry revolved around site lists and private-marketplaces (PMPs) to ensure that ads were served in brand-safe environments.”


That early approach, he notes, “evolved into pre-bid tools to manage Brand Safety, fraud (bot) detection and viewability,” alongside contextual targeting (keyword blocking), stringent data targeting, measurement and analytics.


## How Brand Safety Works Today


While the foundational goal of protecting reputation remains the same, the mechanics of brand safety have evolved significantly. Today, effective brand safety relies on a layered stack of automated tools, though each brand ultimately determines and sets its own unique standards.


The most notable sign of the industry’s maturation is that protection now spans multiple stages of the media buy. Advertisers use sophisticated pre-bid filtering to avoid purchasing inventory on risky sites, and pair that with post-bid measurement to verify that the placements delivered met expectations. The emphasis has decisively moved away from one-time blocking via static lists and toward continuous monitoring and dynamic refinement of safety protocols.


A critical component of this modern approach is the role of independent verification and content-adjacency measurement. Using these standard third-party checks provides an unbiased assessment, ensuring that an advertiser’s ads are not appearing alongside harmful content and that the automated tools are functioning as intended.


Sharma points to how far the tooling has come: “sophisticated supply path optimization tools, Machine Learning, Artificial Intelligence, Data Lake, clean rooms, etc, play a significant role in helping advertisers ensure Brand Safety. A combination of some or all of the above currently enable Brand Safety success within the industry.”


Still, technology doesn’t set the standard on its own. As Sharma cautions, “while the IAB and other firms have defined industry guidelines, each brand must define its own blueprint for Brand Safety.” Why? The reason, in his words: “Brand Safety could mean different things to people, and everyone in the ecosystem (brand, DSP, publishers, data, creative partners, etc,) will benefit when Brand Safety strategies are implemented correctly.”


## Brand Safety vs Brand Suitability: What’s the Difference?


While often grouped together, brand safety and brand suitability operate on fundamentally different levels. The core distinction comes down to who sets the standard and how far that standard extends. Brand safety serves as a universal, shared baseline across the industry, designed to protect all advertisers from clearly harmful content like violence or illegal activity.


In contrast, brand suitability is a highly personalized layer tuned directly to an individual brand’s unique values, tone, and strategic goals. Content that is perfectly acceptable for one advertiser may be entirely inappropriate for another based on their specific audience and brand identity.


Ultimately, safety and suitability are complementary, but they are not interchangeable. Brand safety establishes the essential floor of protection, while brand suitability builds the customized framework that aligns ad placements with a brand’s specific identity.


Here’s how they compare side by side:


Brand Safety Brand Suitability


Scope Universal baseline that blocks clearly harmful or unwanted content Brand-specific layer that aligns ads with fitting content


Who defines it Industry-wide standards and guidelines (e.g. IAB) Each individual brand, based on its own values and positioning


Tooling Universally accepted blocking tools and exclusion lists Market intel, brand profile, and custom suitability settings


Example Keeping any advertiser away from illegal or violent content A pharma brand allowing health-news adjacency an automaker would avoid


Sharma frames the relationship as complementary but distinct: “While Brand Safety and Brand Suitability go hand-in-hand, there are differences between the two.


As mentioned, Brand Safety is ensuring that a brand’s ads don’t serve on unwanted content, and most brands deploy universal tools to keep a brand safe. Brand Suitability, on the other hand, comes down to matching your company’s core values, goals and identity and thus is different for each brand.”


He illustrates it with a simple contrast: “a pharmaceutical company may find it suitable for their ad to run on news content related to health and wellness, while an automotive company may find it damaging to their company’s reputation to serve on news content focused on a car accident.”


The tooling differs accordingly, as Sharma notes: “unlike Brand Safety, which provides universally accepted industry-wide blocking tools to ensure ads are not featured in unsafe environments, Brand Suitability relies on market intel, brand profile and positioning, thus creating specific environments for advertising.”


## How People-Based Technology Supports Brand Safety and Suitability


People-based technology strengthens both brand safety and brand suitability by anchoring ad delivery to verified, real people rather than anonymous devices or fraudulent bot traffic.


By building media campaigns around confirmed individuals, advertisers gain far greater certainty regarding who sees their ad and the exact context in which it appears. This identity-first foundation reinforces a clean, safe baseline while simultaneously supporting nuanced suitability goals across every channel.


Sharma describes how that foundation works in practice: “[People-based advertising](https://www.viantinc.com/solutions/planning-and-buying/people-based-advertising/) technology enables the creation of a customer profile by combining online and offline attributes that are then utilized to reach the user on any device. The primary objective is to connect the brand with verified real people while identifying the right channels to advertise.


That verified approach, he explains, “enables the utilization of[cross device](https://www.viantinc.com/insights/blog/factuals-vp-agencies-strategic-partnerships-talks-cross-device-targeting-best-strategy-real-time-location-data/) , cross channel and patented household technologies in a brand safe (and in regulatory compliant) manner by ensuring that ads are served to real people. DSPs with people-based infrastructure can help with Brand Safety by utilizing[Identity Resolution](https://www.viantinc.com/solutions/planning-and-buying/identity-resolution/) -based consumer profiles to serve ads to opted-in real users as opposed to serving on devices or bot traffic.”


## How to Build a Brand-Safe and Brand-Suitable Strategy


Protecting your brand’s reputation doesn’t mean you have to sacrifice campaign reach. By following a repeatable, structured framework, advertisers can safeguard their image while continuing to engage high-value audiences at scale.


1. **Define what is unsafe vs. unsuitable** Separate universal safety baselines (e.g., hate speech, illegal acts) from brand-specific suitability preferences (e.g., avoiding specific news topics). Write down both as actionable, clear standards for your internal teams and media partners.
2. **Set strategic inclusion and exclusion lists** Implement exclusion lists to block untrustworthy domains, low-quality apps, and unsafe keywords. Concurrently, leverage inclusion lists—where brand suitability thrives—to actively prioritize high-value, relevant content that aligns with your brand’s core values.
3. **Choose a DSP with robust controls:** Use a DSP offering granular targeting, transparent supply paths, and integrated third-party verification. The platform should enforce your safety baseline while layering suitability controls without needlessly collapsing your audience scale.
4. **Audit placements regularly** Treat campaign execution as an ongoing discipline. Establish a regular cadence to review placement reports, update keyword and domain exclusion lists as new risks emerge, and verify that impressions are delivering in intended contexts.
5. **Revisit standards as your brand evolves** Brand safety and suitability guidelines should never be static. Treat your criteria as living documents, reassessing and refining them whenever your brand strategy, target audience, or product portfolio expands.


## The Future of Brand Safety and Brand Suitability


Looking ahead, the digital advertising landscape is undergoing a decisive shift toward brand suitability, with this evolution proving most consequential in CTV. As CTV continues to command a greater share of media budgets, evaluating context becomes exponentially more complex.


Unlike the traditional open web, a single CTV ad campaign runs across dozens of distinct apps, streaming devices, and content genres—frequently stitched together server-side where traditional page-level signals simply do not exist.


This technical fragmentation makes broad, blunt category blocking far more costly, often locking advertisers out of premium, high-performing inventory. As a result, the industry is placing far greater value on nuanced suitability approaches that can intelligently engage the right audience even inside complex or sensitive viewing environments.


Ultimately, the momentum is moving away from static blocking and toward continuous, context-aware decisioning. In the rapidly evolving CTV ecosystem, the winning brands will be those that protect their hard-earned reputation through intelligent precision, reaching the broad, high-value audiences that blunt exclusions would otherwise sweep away.


Sharma is emphatic that safety is never a finished job: “While Brand Safety standards could be more universal, it is by no means a “set-it and forget-it” medium. In fact, Brand Safety is a constant process and will always serve an essential role in programmatic ad buying.”


Looking ahead, he sees suitability as the growth area: “Over the past few years, there has been a paradigm shift towards Brand Suitability as brands are taking more control over the solutions that they deploy. The future for Brand Suitability is to identify avenues that appeal to their audience that could have otherwise been missed with current strategies.”


The goal, as he puts it, “is to avoid the vast blocking of entire categories and, in turn, focus on finding the right audiences within those.”

## Frequently Asked Questions


### What is the difference between brand safety and brand suitability?


Expand


Brand safety is a universal baseline that keeps ads away from clearly harmful or inappropriate content, while brand suitability is a brand-specific layer that aligns ads with content matching a company's values, tone, and audience. Safety is broadly the same for every advertiser; suitability differs from brand to brand. The two work together but are not interchangeable.


### What is brand safety in advertising?


Expand


Brand safety in advertising is the practice of protecting a brand's reputation by keeping its ads out of environments that could damage its image, such as content involving violence, hate speech, or illegal activity. It typically relies on exclusion lists, pre-bid filtering, and verification tools applied before and after a campaign runs.


### What is an example of brand suitability?


Expand


A common example is a pharmaceutical brand allowing its ads to run alongside health and wellness news, while an automotive brand avoids news content about car accidents. Both placements may be perfectly "safe," but only one is suitable for each brand's values and message. Suitability is about fit, not just avoiding harm.


### How can advertisers ensure brand safety?


Expand


Advertisers ensure brand safety by defining what content is unsafe or unsuitable for their brand, applying inclusion and exclusion lists, choosing a demand-side platform with transparent supply paths and verification, and auditing placements on a regular basis. Because standards evolve, brand safety works best as an ongoing process rather than a one-time setup.


### Are brand safety and brand suitability the same thing?


Expand


No. Brand safety avoids harmful or inappropriate content that nearly all advertisers would reject, while brand suitability selects for content that positively aligns with a specific brand. Treating them as identical is a common mistake that leads advertisers to either over-block inventory or run ads in technically safe but off-brand contexts.
