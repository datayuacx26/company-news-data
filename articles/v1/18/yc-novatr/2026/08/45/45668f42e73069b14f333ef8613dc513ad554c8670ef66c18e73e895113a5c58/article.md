# Master BIM Skills: What to Look for in a BIM Program {{year}}

## **Why Do Many BIM Learners Still Struggle in Real Projects?**


Limiting BIM upskilling to its theoretical concepts can result in a lack of confidence. Here are the key challenges such professionals face during career development.


**Difficulty Applying Knowledge:** Understanding


[BIM tools](https://www.novatr.com/blog/10-best-bim-software-and-tools-for-architects) is different from using them in real projects. Professionals often struggle to translate theoretical concepts into practical modelling, coordination, and documentation tasks.


**Limited Collaboration Experience:** BIM is a collaborative process that involves architects, engineers, contractors, and consultants. Without the experience of working in multidisciplinary teams, learners find it challenging to communicate.


**Lack of Workflow Understanding:** Real-world projects follow structured BIM execution plans (BEPs), modelling standards, naming conventions, and approval workflows. Professionals with only theoretical training may not be familiar with these industry practices.


**Low Confidence During Coordination:** Activities such as clash detection, model reviews, and design coordination require both technical and managerial skills. Without practice, professionals may hesitate to identify issues or suggest solutions.


**Difficulty Meeting Project Timelines:** Live projects involve strict deadlines and frequent design revisions, which can be fulfilled with appropriate BIM skills. Individuals who have only worked on isolated exercises struggle to manage changing project requirements.


**Slow Career Progression:**


Employers increasingly look for professionals who can contribute from the beginning of a project. Those without practical BIM experience may require additional training, which can delay career growth and reduce opportunities to work on complex projects.


## **What BIM Skills Do AEC Companies Expect Beyond Software Knowledge?**


****


BIM is not just software. It’s a process that involves designing, executing, and managing AEC projects using software as a tool. Let’s explore all the subjects and skills one needs to secure BIM jobs.


**BIM Coordination:** Professionals should be able to coordinate architectural, structural, and MEP models to ensure every discipline works with accurate real-time information. Effective coordination is one of the essential BIM skills that keeps projects moving smoothly.


**Clash Detection:** Identifying and resolving clashes before construction is one of BIM's biggest advantages. Using software like Navisworks, professionals should know how to detect clashes, evaluate their impact, and coordinate with teams to resolve them efficiently.


[BIM Execution Planning](https://www.novatr.com/blog/bim-execution-plan-for-mep-projects) **:** A BEP defines how BIM will be implemented throughout a project. Understanding BEPs helps professionals follow project goals, assign responsibilities, maintain consistent workflows, and meet client requirements.


**ISO 19650 Standards:** Many organisations now follow ISO 19650 for information management throughout a project's lifecycle. Familiarity with these standards helps professionals organise project data, maintain consistent documentation, and collaborate effectively across teams.


**Model Validation:** Creating a model is only part of the process. Professionals must also verify that it complies with project standards, modelling guidelines, naming conventions, and client requirements to improve the quality and reliability of project deliverables.


**Multidisciplinary Collaboration:** BIM projects involve architects, engineers, contractors, and project managers working together. Strong communication, coordination, and issue-resolution skills are key BIM skills ensuring seamless collaboration.


**Autodesk Construction Cloud:** BIM projects rely on cloud-based collaboration platforms such as


[Autodesk Construction Cloud (ACC)](https://www.novatr.com/blog/autodesk-construction-cloud-acc-complete-guide) .


Such platforms manage models, documents, reviews, RFIs, and issue tracking. Understanding these workflows enables professionals to collaborate in real time and maintain version control throughout the project lifecycle.
