# BIM Workflow in Construction: From Design to Handover {{year}}

## **What Is a BIM Workflow and Why Does It Matter?**


A BIM workflow is a sequence of activities that defines how project information moves between stakeholders from concept development to facility operations.


Rather than treating design, engineering, and construction as isolated tasks, it establishes clear processes for


[model creation](https://www.novatr.com/blog/everything-about-3d-modelling-in-construction) , coordination, revisions, and data exchange at every stage.


This ensures that project teams follow uniform standards, maintain construction documentation, and make informed decisions based on reliable information.


## **What Happens at Each Stage of a BIM Project?**


A construction or infrastructure project goes through various phases of work when integrated with BIM workflow. Let’s explore them:


**Concept Development:** The project starts by defining the client's requirements, site conditions, budget, and


[performance goals](https://www.novatr.com/blog/high-performance-building-design) . Early BIM models help teams evaluate design options, assess feasibility, and establish a strong foundation for the project's next stages.


**Design Development:** Architectural, structural, and MEP models are developed with increasing levels of detail. At this stage, teams refine layouts, include technical information, and coordinate across disciplines to produce a comprehensive design.


**Coordination:** Models from each domain are merged to identify clashes, verify constructability, and review compliance with project standards. Resolving conflicts during the design phase reduces disruptions and expensive modifications during construction.


**Documentation:** After design coordination, BIM models are used to generate plans, sections, elevations, schedules, and BOQs. Since the documentation is linked to the model, approved changes are reflected automatically, helping teams maintain consistency and reduce manual revisions.


**Construction Planning:** BIM models are integrated with project schedules, quantities, and sequencing to support construction planning. Contractors can visualise workflows, estimate resources, and coordinate site activities before work begins.


**Construction Execution:** During construction, BIM serves as a central reference for drawings, RFIs, site updates, and design revisions. Teams can monitor progress, communicate changes efficiently, and ensure on-site work aligns with the approved model.


**Project Handover and Operations:** The final BIM model is delivered with asset information, maintenance records, and operational data.


[Facility managers](https://www.novatr.com/blog/bim-mep-integration-for-facility-managers)


use this digital resource to support inspections, maintenance planning, renovations, and the long-term operation of the facility.


## **Which BIM Tools Support Different Workflow Stages?**


The various BIM workflow stages include conceptual design, BIM modelling, clash detection, project execution, and facility management. Here are the tools required at every level:


### **1. Idea Stage**


Software such as Autodesk Forma, Rhino, and


[SketchUp](https://www.novatr.com/blog/what-is-sketchup-and-why-do-architects-use-it) helps architects evaluate massing, site conditions, and design options before detailed modelling begins. These tools support quick iterations and enable architects to refine design decisions while considering project constraints from the outset.


### **2. Modelling Stage**


Revit software, Graphisoft Archicad, and Tekla Structures are typically used to develop discipline-specific BIM models. They combine geometry with project information, allowing architects, structural engineers, and MEP professionals to produce coordinated models that evolve throughout the design process.


### **3. Clash Detection Stage**


Coordination platforms like Navisworks, Revizto, and Solibri aggregate models from different disciplines to identify clashes and validate design compliance. Detecting conflicts before construction helps minimise rework, improve coordination, and keep projects on schedule.


### **4. Execution Stage**


Tools like


[Autodesk Construction Cloud](https://www.novatr.com/blog/autodesk-construction-cloud-shortcuts-for-productivity) ,


Bentley SYNCHRO, and Trimble Connect connect BIM models with construction planning, scheduling, document management, and site collaboration. This enables project teams to monitor progress, manage changes, and improve communication between office and field teams.


### **5. Maintenance Stage**


After project handover, platforms such as Autodesk Tandem, Archibus, and IBM Maximo use BIM data to support asset tracking, maintenance planning, and building operations. Access to construction documentation helps professionals make informed decisions throughout the building's operational lifecycle.


## **What Are the Key Deliverables at Every BIM Stage?**


The various phases of a BIM project provide key deliverables that are crucial for successful project implementation. Let’s have a look at them:


### **1. Concept Stage**


The pre-design stage produces site analyses, massing studies, conceptual BIM models, and feasibility reports. These deliverables help stakeholders evaluate design alternatives, define project objectives, and make informed decisions.


### **2. Design Development Stage**


As the design progresses, teams make architectural, structural, and


[MEP models](https://www.novatr.com/blog/top-10-trends-in-mep-design)


with increasing levels of detail. Material specifications, design calculations, and updated project information are also incorporated to prepare the project for coordination.


### **3. Project Coordination Stage**


This stage generates federated models, clash detection reports, issue logs, and coordination records. These deliverables identify conflicts, assign job roles, and approve resolutions before construction activities commence.


### **4. Documentation Stage**


Construction-ready drawings, elevations, sections, schedules, quantity take-offs, and BOQs are extracted directly from the BIM model. Since these documents remain linked to the model, updates can be generated with greater consistency and accuracy.


### **5. Construction Stage**


During project execution, professionals produce construction schedules, 4D simulations, site progress reports, RFIs, and updated models reflecting approved design changes. These records help monitor progress and maintain alignment between design intent and on-site execution.


### **6. Handover Stage**


The final stage delivers as-built


[BIM models](https://www.novatr.com/blog/complete-bim-guide-for-aec-professionals) , asset registers, equipment information, maintenance schedules, warranties, and operation manuals. Together, these resources provide facility managers with the information required to operate and maintain the building throughout its lifecycle.


## **What Common Challenges Affect BIM Workflows?**


BIM is a construction technology that’s still finding its ground in the AEC industry. So, here are the key challenges which can impact BIM workflows.


-


Transitioning from traditional


[CAD](https://www.novatr.com/blog/how-to-transition-from-cad-to-bim) -based processes to BIM can be tough without proper planning, training, and organisational support.


-


Using multiple BIM software platforms can create interoperability issues if project data is not exchanged seamlessly.


-


Inaccurate or incomplete project data can reduce the reliability of BIM models and affect downstream workflows.


-


Inconsistent modelling standards and naming conventions can lead to BIM coordination issues across project teams.


-


The upfront investment in software, hardware, and


[BIM training](https://www.novatr.com/blog/why-bim-training-is-crucial-for-architects) can be a hindrance for some organisations.


-


Managing multiple model revisions without proper version control can result in teams working with outdated information.
