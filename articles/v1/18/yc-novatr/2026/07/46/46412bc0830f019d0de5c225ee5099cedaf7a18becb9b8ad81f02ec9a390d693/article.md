# Top 5 Common BIM Workflow Mistakes and Their Solution {{year}}

## **Why Do BIM Workflows Break Down?**


BIM workflows disintegrate when the technology and the people using it do not align. So, while


[BIM enables seamless coordination](https://www.novatr.com/blog/bim-coordination-vs-traditional-coordination-and-why-bim-coordination-is-better) , here are the most common reasons why it can fail:


**Lack of BIM Standards:** In the absence of accurate modelling standards, naming conventions, and documentation protocols, every team may follow a different approach. This often leads to confusion, chaos, and patchy coordination across the project lifecycle.


**Poor Collaboration:** BIM relies on architects, engineers, contractors, and consultants working together. Delayed communication, isolated workflows, or limited data sharing can cause design conflicts and project delays.


**Incorrect Models:** When teams do not update the central model regularly, others may continue working with outdated information. This raises the likelihood of errors and discrepancies during construction.


**Limited BIM Skills:** BIM software is only as effective as the professionals using it. Insufficient training or a lack of understanding of BIM processes can lead to inaccurate modelling, weak workflows, and missed project requirements.


**Resistance to Process Changes:** Implementing BIM coordination requires organisations to change long-established workflows. When teams continue following traditional methods instead of adopting collaborative


[BIM practices](https://www.novatr.com/blog/what-is-building-information-modeling-bim) , the workflow becomes fragmented and loses efficiency.

## **How Do Standards and Collaboration Prevent Costly Errors?**


BIM projects rely on multiple teams working on the same project data throughout the design and construction lifecycle. This applies using proper software,


[BIM standards](https://www.novatr.com/blog/bim-standards-and-best-practices-for-architects) , and BIM collaboration processes that help avoid expensive rework. Here’s how this can be achieved:


**Uniform Modelling Methods:** Setting a project-wide modelling protocol for object parameters, detailing, and documentation ensures that every team member creates and interprets models in the same way. Aligning these standards with frameworks such as ISO 19650 improves model quality and abates coordination mistakes.


**Common Data Environment (CDE):** A Common Data Environment such as Autodesk Cloud Collaboration provides a central location where project information, models, drawings, and documents are stored. Using a single point of information helps teams avoid duplicate files, outdated information, and version conflicts.


**Regular Coordination Meetings:** Frequent coordination sessions allow project stakeholders to review project progress and resolve issues before they affect downstream activities. This keeps the entire team in sync throughout the BIM workflow.


**Clash Detection:** Routine BIM model coordination helps identify conflicts between architectural, structural, and


[MEP systems](https://www.novatr.com/blog/top-common-mep-engineering-challenges) before construction starts. Resolving these issues reduces rework, delays, and unexpected project costs.


**Defined Roles:** Assigning responsibilities for model creation, review, and approvals ensures accountability across the project team. When everyone understands their job, information flows more efficiently, and collaboration becomes more effective.


## **Which Tools Help Improve BIM Workflow Efficiency?**


BIM modelling software offers a dashboard where professionals can view, modify, and manage project components. They also help in enhancing construction documentation and keep project information consistent across teams. Let’s explore the key BIM tools that enhance operational efficiency:


**Revit:** It is one of the most widely used BIM authoring tools for architectural, structural, and MEP modelling.


[Revit software](https://www.novatr.com/blog/detailed-revit-guide) can generate coordinated drawings, schedules, and quantities from a unified model and maintain precise project documentation.


**Navisworks:** It enables teams to combine discipline-specific models for coordination, clash detection, and construction simulation. Identifying design conflicts before construction helps minimise rework and project delays.


**Revizto:** Revizto streamlines BIM coordination by bringing drawings and issue tracking onto a collaborative platform. It allows project teams to assign tasks, monitor progress, and resolve design conflicts more efficiently.


**Autodesk Forma:** This software supports conceptual and schematic phases of architecture and urban design projects. Its key features include site analysis, study of environmental factors, and building performance.


**Autodesk Model Checker:** Conceived on Forge, a cloud-based developer platform, this tool automates BIM model validation. It allows professionals to check BIM files against predefined standards and project requirements to streamline BIM workflow.


**Solibri:** Solibri is a quality assurance tool that validates BIM models for rule compliance, data accuracy, and coordination issues. It enables project teams to identify errors early and improve the reliability of project information.


**Autodesk Construction Cloud (ACC):**


[ACC](https://www.novatr.com/blog/autodesk-construction-cloud-acc-complete-guide) provides a cloud-based CDE where teams can store and manage project information. It improves collaboration by ensuring all stakeholders always work on the latest project data.


## **How Can Teams Build Better BIM Workflows?**


Building a practical BIM workflow depends on set processes, project coordination standards, and clear communication. Below are the key measures that can aid professionals in creating their workflow:


**Define Project Guidelines:** Establish modelling standards, file structures, and documentation protocols before commencing the project. A uniform approach helps teams work with clarity of thought and achieve timely milestones.


**Assign Responsibilities:** Every stakeholder should understand their scope, deliverables, and timelines. Explaining responsibilities for architects, engineers, contractors, and


[BIM managers](https://www.novatr.com/blog/guide-to-become-bim-manager) improves accountability and prevents duplication of work.


**Use a Common Data Environment:** An integrated platform ensures all project information and documents can be accessed from one location. This confirms every team member works with the latest project data and reduces version control issues.


**Host Coordination Reviews:** Schedule coordination meetings to review models, discuss design changes, and resolve clashes. Frequent reviews help identify issues early and keep the project aligned with its objectives.


**Leverage BIM Collaboration Tools:** Software such as Autodesk Construction Cloud, Revizto, and


[Navisworks](https://www.novatr.com/blog/navisworks-type) streamlines model sharing, clash detection, issue tracking, and communication between disciplines. These tools improve coordination and support informed decision-making.


**Invest in Continuous BIM Training:** BIM workflows continue to evolve alongside new software capabilities and industry standards. Regular upskilling helps teams stay competent and follow best practices, adopt efficient workflows, and make better use of BIM technologies.
