[Kiriti Rambhatla](https://au.linkedin.com/in/kiriti-rambhatla?trk=public_post_comment_actor-name) 2w


A thought-provoking perspective, Satya. But the reverse information paradox also exposes a growing tension: as the marginal cost of information approaches zero, the value of proprietary knowledge, engineering know-how, and trade secrets increases exponentially. Recent waves of AI-related copyright lawsuits, publisher disputes, and concerns from software developers and creators show that many believe this value is already being extracted without clear attribution or compensation. Innovation depends on incentives. If AI systems can internalize decades of proprietary human expertise while eroding the economic value of creating it, we risk weakening the very engine that produces tomorrow's breakthroughs. The defining challenge for this decade isn't building more capable AI ; it's building an AI ecosystem that respects provenance, IP, and the economics of innovation. If proprietary knowledge becomes free training data while its creators receive little in return, economists won't just be measuring productivity gains they'll be explaining why investment in frontier R&D slowed. I'll leave economists to measure the GDP impact.
