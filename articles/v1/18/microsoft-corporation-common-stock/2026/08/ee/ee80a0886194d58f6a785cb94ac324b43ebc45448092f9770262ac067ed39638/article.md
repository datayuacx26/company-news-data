# Hoop dreams: How a coach’s vision for a high-tech basketball gym is inspiring kids to aim for the perfect shot

Shoot 360 has had some real-life success stories come from their training, including top WNBA draft pick Cameron Brink and former Washington State guard Isaac Bonton. But Moody is just as excited about the trainee who makes their high school varsity team or the youngster who wants to come to training every week.


It’s not lost on Moody that in his quest to get his kids off video games and onto the court he’s created a gamified version of basketball that isn’t too far off from XBOX. But the shots don’t lie – the target audience is responding to this new way to play and practice.


“You meet kids where they’re at, right?” Moody says. “Video games and social media are a big part of their lives. And so, do you fight it or do you figure out a way to be creative with it and make it really positive? The old idea of just to grind, it’s not really relevant today. Kids will work hard, but they want immediate feedback. Like, that’s the world today. They expect that, right? And they want to have fun.”


As 9-year-old Henry Schlotthauer fires up shots at the Beaverton Shoot 360, he’s not paying close attention to the screens or monitoring every score on the leaderboard. He’s simply having fun playing basketball under the watchful eye of coach and GM Sara Schwanke.


“I’ve been going here every day and learning new skills and getting better,” Henry says. “I’m going to keep playing basketball.”
