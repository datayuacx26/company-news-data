## What are evergreen retainer fees and how do they work?


An evergreen retainer is a fee structure in which a client maintains a pre-agreed minimum[trust account](https://pointone.com/blog/best-legal-billing-software-for-solo-attorneys) balance by replenishing funds as they are used. Unlike a one-time payment drawn down to zero, an evergreen retainer keeps the account **funded throughout the legal relationship.** The firm bills against the trust balance as work is performed. When the balance falls below the agreed threshold, the client receives a replenishment invoice to restore the account to the required level.


Advance payments generally remain client funds until earned, so they usually must be held in trust unless a jurisdiction's rules provide otherwise. Labeling an advance payment "nonrefundable" does not necessarily exempt it from trust-account requirements. The evergreen structure reduces repeated renegotiation of payment terms and supports more predictable cash flow. It also requires careful tracking of trust balances,[timely invoicing](https://pointone.com/blog/how-to-craft-better-legal-bills) , and compliance with applicable ethical rules. Firms should follow the rules that apply in their jurisdiction.


## Benefits of evergreen retainer agreements


### For clients: predictable legal support and straightforward budgeting


For businesses and individuals, an evergreen retainer agreement provides highly predictable legal support. By maintaining a consistent trust balance, clients guarantee their legal counsel on retainer is always available to handle urgent legal issues without the delay of negotiating new contracts or processing sudden, large upfront payments.


This structure allows straightforward budgeting, as clients anticipate monthly replenishment invoices based on[actual hourly usage](https://pointone.com/blog/best-legal-billing-model) rather than unpredictable, lump-sum billing at the end of a lengthy litigation matter. **It transforms a potentially volatile legal cost into a manageable, recurring business expense.**


### For law firms: enhanced financial stability and operational efficiency


Evergreen retainers protect law firms from client non-payment and support long-term financial stability. Because the firm always holds funds in trust before executing work, the[collection realization rate](https://pointone.com/blog/billing-and-timekeeping) is significantly higher than when billing in arrears, where invoices can sit unpaid for months or end up written off entirely.


Industry experts strongly advise firms representing financially troubled corporations or distressed real estate entities to use these retainers[to minimize payment risk](https://www.abi.org/abi-journal/evergreen-retainers-for-a-debtors-professionals-a-market-driven-approach) . If a corporate client faces insolvency, the firm is not left as an unsecured creditor for unpaid hours. Furthermore, this model drives operational efficiency by[standardizing the billing cycle](https://pointone.com/blog/optimizing-legal-pre-bill-proforma-review) , saving countless hours otherwise spent chasing overdue accounts receivable.


## When to consider an evergreen retainer


Evergreen retainers are advantageous in high-stakes or unpredictable legal environments. Law firms should consider evergreen retainers when representing financially troubled entities, such as businesses undergoing restructuring or facing bankruptcy, to secure firm payment before services are rendered. They are also ideal for ongoing corporate counsel relationships, complex family law disputes, and protracted litigation where the risk of escalating fees is high.


## Five key evaluation criteria


### 1. Market norms for retainers in your practice area


An evergreen retainer must fit what clients expect in your practice area and local market. Bar regulators judge fee arrangements against prevailing norms, so unusual terms invite scrutiny. To measure this, benchmark peer firms' fee agreements and review your state bar's fee guidance.


### 2. Client financial sophistication and ability to maintain the balance


The two most important questions here are whether if the client understands the replenishment obligation and if they can fund it every month. Measure it at intake: ask about income stability, prior experience retaining a lawyer, and who manages their finances. Apply the same judgment you would use before extending credit.


### 3. Likelihood of prolonged representation


Evergreen retainers only pay off when the matter lasts long enough for multiple billing and replenishment cycles. Estimate the realistic duration and monthly billing pace using matter type, court timelines, and your firm's historical data.


### 4. Risk of client non-payment


This factor measures how likely the client is to default if billed in arrears. That is exactly the risk an evergreen retainer eliminates. Evaluate it like a lender: payment history with prior counsel, industry health, and warning signs like pending insolvency or distressed assets. Note that the logic inverts here: higher risk makes the evergreen structure more appropriate, not less.


### 5. Firm capacity to track trust balances


Evergreen retainers shift a compliance burden onto the firm. Every matter needs continuous trust balance monitoring, timely replenishment invoices, and records that survive a bar audit. Measure your readiness honestly: are three-way reconciliations completed monthly, and do invoices go out on schedule?

## FAQs about evergreen retainers


What does it mean when a lawyer is on retainer?


Having a lawyer is on retainer means the client has paid an upfront fee to secure the attorney's availability and future services. Ethically, this requires holding unearned advanced fees in dedicated trust accounts until the work is actually performed and billed.


Is having an attorney on retainer worth the investment?


Yes, keeping an attorney on retainer is highly valuable for businesses and individuals who require immediate, ongoing legal advice without the delay of negotiating new contracts for every unexpected situation.


How long does a lawyer retainer arrangement typically last?


The duration depends on the specific contract; it can last for the length of a single lawsuit or indefinitely for ongoing corporate counsel, provided the client continues to fund the trust account.


What happens if an evergreen retainer isn't replenished on time?


If a client fails to restore the required balance, the firm will typically pause services. Ultimately, failure to replenish can result in work stoppage or formal withdrawal from the case under established ethics rules.
