# ILTACON 2026: How to Navigate the Week Without Burning Out by Wednesday

ILTACON 2026 runs Sunday, August 23 through Thursday, August 27 at the Gaylord Opryland Resort in Nashville. ILTACON is a big conference with a lot competing for your attention. The best way to get value from the week is to arrive knowing which questions you want answered and which conversations you want to have. Otherwise, it’s easy to fall into the typical conference trap: going to everything, talking to everyone, and returning to your desk with little more than a tote bag full of pens, socks, and chargers.


This is a guide to spending the week well.


## What makes ILTACON 2026 different from every other legal tech conference


Most conferences are programmed by editorial teams and funded by sponsors. ILTACON works differently.


Its educational sessions begin as proposals submitted by practitioners—CIOs, innovation leaders, legal operations professionals, knowledge managers, and IT teams from law firms and legal departments. An ILTA member committee then selects the sessions based on what the community wants to learn.


The result is a conference focused on how law firms are putting technology into practice, including what worked, what failed, and what they learned along the way.


You'll hear from the team that rolled out a new document management system across 400 attorneys, the firm that rethought its pricing strategy, or the innovation leader who discovered halfway through an AI deployment that attorney adoption was the biggest challenge.


That same dynamic extends beyond the sessions.


The person sitting next to you is often facing the same challenges you are—or has just solved them. A conversation after a panel on AI governance can be as valuable as the presentation itself.


The best insights often come from conversations in the hallway, over coffee, or between sessions rather than from the stage itself.


## The shape of the week


**Sunday** is registration, orientation at 5:45pm, and the opening reception from 6:30 to 8:30pm.


**Monday** opens with the keynote from Jim Abbott at 9:00am, then sessions in three blocks through the afternoon. The exhibit hall opening reception is Monday night, 7:00 to 9:00pm.


**Tuesday** is the busiest day. Sessions start at 9:00am, the exhibit hall is open from 10:00am to 6:00pm, and there are four session blocks, so plan your schedule selectively.


**Wednesday** has the second keynote — Dr. Kevin Fong at 9:00am — plus a full session slate and the hall open again until 6:00pm.


**Thursday** is the half-day everyone underestimates. Hall closes at noon, the member lunch and annual meeting run midday, and there are still three session blocks. The closing reception will run from 6:30pm to 8:30pm. Thursday afternoon sessions are the least crowded of the week, which creates greater access to speakers.


All times are Central. Book your flight home Friday morning if you can. Thursday-evening departures mean skipping the part of the conference where people finally say what they really think.


## Pick your week by what you actually do


Trying to cover all of ILTACON is usually how you end up covering very little.


*Below are four routes to choose from, built from the published agenda. Times and rooms may move, so check the ILTACON agenda builder before you commit to anything.*


#### If you run IT or security


The infrastructure and cyber tracks offer the most depth. Prioritize sessions where firms share firsthand lessons from an incident or migration over broad framework discussions.


-


**Mon 11:00am, Davidson North — Break, Pivot, Exfiltrate: The Anatomy of a Breach.** A real incident-response walkthrough from initial access through exfiltration. Listen for detection gaps and escalation decisions.


-


**Mon 11:00am, Delta C — Passwordless: What, Why and How.** Hunton Andrews Kurth’s CIO covers hybrid Active Directory, Entra, conditional access, and attorney adoption. Worth considering if identity modernization is on your roadmap.


-


**Tue 10:30am, Davidson North — Copilot & Privilege: Using Purview to Assure Compliance and Data Security.** Put this near the top of your list if Copilot is already in your firm or likely to be soon. The focus is privilege, information barriers, and data access.


-


**Wed 2:00pm, Delta C — Evolving from VMWare.** Narrow, unglamorous, and probably the most immediately useful hour of your week if this is on your 2027 roadmap.


-


**Wed 3:30pm, Davidson North — Moving Risk Management from a Static to a Dynamic, Automated Discipline.** Law firm security leaders will share how to build a dynamic risk register that tracks, monitors, and addresses emerging threats and opportunities.


-


**Thu 3:30pm, Davidson North — Social Engineering 2.0: How Attackers Exploit Collaboration Platforms.** Attackers increasingly use trusted internal channels. This session examines the implications for collaboration platforms and user behavior. Thursday's least crowded slot, so you'll actually get to ask your question.


One conflict to plan around: Wednesday at 11:00am places the **ILTA Tech Survey 2026 Preview** (Delta A) against two solid IT sessions. Go with the survey. Over 500 firms responded and you'll reference the numbers all year.


#### If you're on the finance or operations side — CFO, COO, billing director


Your sessions are located in smaller rooms, resulting in better conversations. You'll get more out of six of these than out of any big-tent AI panel.


-


**Mon 11:00am, Delta D — CFO Roundtable: In the Box Office.** 90 minutes with Troutman Pepper Locke's CFO. For CFOs and COOs, this may be one of the best rooms of the week.


-


**Mon 2:30pm, Meet PointOne at Booth 624 to discuss AI-powered time entry and revenue capture.**

#### If you own Knowledge Management or Innovation


You're the audience the AI programming was built for, which means you'll be spoiled with choices and at risk of session-hopping through generalities. Anchor on the sessions where real numbers are reported or someone builds live.


-


**Mon 11:00am, Delta B — Whose Legal AI Workflow Is It Anyway?** Improv-style: the audience proposes use cases and the panel designs agentic workflows live, including ideas it'd reject. An unusually honest format.


-


**Mon 4:00pm, Delta D — Platform Wars: Which AI Tools Do Lawyers Really Want?** Five firm-side innovation leaders, no vendors on the panel. Listen for evidence on what lawyers still use after the initial rollout and where platforms overlap.


-


**Tue 10:30am, Delta B — KM Roundtable 2.0: KM+AI, From Inspiration to Implementation.** A 90-minute, table-based discussion focused on execution. Bring one deployment question and one adoption constraint from your firm.

**Tue 2:00pm, Volunteer North — Copilot Success Stories.** Actual deployments. Pay attention to repeatable deployment patterns.


-


**Tue 3:30pm, Delta D — Lights, Camera, Action! Build a Legal Copilot Agent Live and Take It Home.** A hands-on session on building four AI agents for common legal workflows.


-


**Wed 11:00am, Delta B — Does Experience Matter? Unlocking Hidden Expertise Using AI.**


-


**Thu 11:00am, Delta B — Escaping the Single-Vendor Paradigm: Firm-Led KM and AI Strategy.** A contrarian choice. Go if you've been told the platform decision is already made.


If I can only pick three: the improv workshop, Platform Wars, and the KM Roundtable.


#### If it's your first ILTACON


Go to orientation Sunday at 5:45pm. It's an hour and it saves you two days of wandering.


Then pick one track and aim to stay on it. Resist the urge to optimize every slot — the cost of a mediocre session is an hour, and the cost of running between buildings is your whole afternoon.


Three sessions worth going off-track for:


-


**Wed 11:00, Magnolia Mezzanine 11-12 — I'm a Young Professional, Get Me in There!** Small room, your peers, the most useful contacts you'll make all week.


-


**Mon 2:30, Davidson South — Influence Without Authority: Getting Work Done When You Don't Control the Levers.** Applies to almost everyone under director level.


-


**Wed 11:00, Delta A — ILTA Tech Survey 2026 Preview.** Instant context on what everyone else is running.


And set a target of five real conversations for the week. Five. Not fifty business cards.


## The exhibit hall, done in ninety minutes


The hall is open Tuesday and Wednesday 10:00am to 6:00pm and Thursday morning until noon. You do not need to spend six hours in there.


Before entering, write down the two or three problems you're actually trying to solve this year. Then walk the hall with that list and ask every vendor the same question: how does this work with the systems we already have? The answer separates the companies who've done the integration work from the ones who'll do it "on the roadmap."


Book the demos you care about in advance. Walk-up demos on Tuesday afternoon.


## Navigating the Gaylord Opryland


This deserves its own section, and every ILTACON veteran will back me up.


The Opryland is enormous. Nine acres of indoor gardens, multiple atriums, and a layout that can turn a straight line between two rooms into an obstacle course.


What this means in practice:


-


Book your rooms on the property, if possible. The commute from off-site hotels eats into parts of the day that matter most.


-


Build fifteen minutes of buffer into every meeting.


-


Wear shoes you'd walk five miles in, because you will.


-


Screenshot the venue map. Cell service inside the atriums is unreliable and you don't want to be reloading a map while a session starts.


-


Pick one landmark inside the resort as your default meeting spot and reuse it all week. "The lobby" is gigantic.


## ILTA side events in Nashville


Vendor receptions run every night.


Pick two. One with a vendor you're seriously evaluating, one with peers. Then get dinner somewhere off property with three or four people you want to know better. That dinner is consistently the highest-value two hours of the week, and it never appears on the agenda.


## Come back with a decision, not just a branded souvenir


The firms that get value out of ILTACON keep a running note with three columns: what you heard, who said it, and what your firm should do about it.


During your first week back, follow up while the conversations are still fresh and choose one idea for further review. One initiative that reaches the firm’s operating agenda is worth more than a long list of ideas that sounded interesting in Nashville.
