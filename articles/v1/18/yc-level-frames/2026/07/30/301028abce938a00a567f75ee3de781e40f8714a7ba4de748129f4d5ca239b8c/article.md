### Tips for Capturing and Framing Wedding Photos


For couples planning an upcoming wedding, we have some tips to nail your photos and wedding memory display:


##### What are the must-have shots for your wedding photo display?


The best framed collections highlight the most special moments for the couple, such as:


- The ceremony (particularly the kiss to seal the vows)
- Intimate shots of the couple soaking up their first married moments, often looking at one another or walking away holding hands
- Detail shots to capture the symbol of love and vows, such as the rings, either on or off the couple’s hands


…but your shot list should go beyond this and take you through the entire day, including:


- Getting ready
- Bride/groom solo shots
- First looks
- Family portraits
- First dance
- Toasts
- Cake cutting
- Other detail shots, such as the venue decor
- Reception fun and dancing


Mixing larger statement photos with smaller detail-led shots creates visual variety and helps a gallery wall feel personal and lived-in rather than overly staged.


##### Are posed, candid, or ‘plandid’ photos best?


A big debate and decision when choosing your wedding photography style and shots to frame is how posed or natural you want them to look.


Traditionally, wedding photos have been highly posed with the couple looking into the camera, but in recent years, more candid or ‘plandid’ shots - carefully executed poses intentionally made to look natural - have taken over.


Posed pictures will always have their place, but it’s often the candid, or seemingly candid, shots that capture the couples’ personalities and make the biggest impact.


The most well-curated wedding gallery walls tend to mix elements of both posed or ‘plandid’ shots of the couple, depending on their photography style of choice, alongside truly organic, candid moments that capture the essence of the day.


##### What do you need to consider when choosing your frames?


Framing is the final step that pulls it all together. The style of frame, spacing, and layout can completely change the impact of the display. It’s key to match the frame or gallery wall to your photography style for a cohesive finish.


For example, editorial-style posed shots often suit sophisticated black or white frames, whereas warm candid shots are often better suited to softer materials like high-end wood finishes.


Whatever style you go for, it's important to go for the[highest quality framing materials](https://www.levelframes.com/blog/what-goes-into-each-level-frame) so that your photos last a lifetime.
