# David Steiner Takes Helm as 76th Postmaster General: A New Era for USPS

In May 2025, the U.S. Postal Service Board of Governors appointed David Steiner as the 76th Postmaster General. He officially began his role in July following the resignation of Louis DeJoy earlier in the year. The Board highlighted Steiner’s track record in corporate leadership and positioned his appointment as part of a broader effort to bring strong management and strategic focus to the Postal Service at a critical time.


## Background and Early Reactions


Steiner brings decades of private sector experience, including senior positions at Waste Management and a seat on the FedEx Board of Directors. Supporters point to this experience as proof that he understands how to lead large, complex organizations. Critics, however, quickly raised concerns about potential conflicts of interest and the risk of importing a private sector mindset that may not align with the Postal Service’s public service mission. In response, Steiner stepped back from outside roles and emphasized his commitment to the independence and public purpose of USPS.


## Balancing Reform and Mission


Louis DeJoy, who served from 2020 to 2025, left a strong mark on the Postal Service. His tenure was defined by the launch of the Delivering for America plan, changes to delivery standards, and intense political scrutiny, particularly during the 2020 election. While DeJoy was praised for pursuing long-term structural reforms, his approach generated controversy and strained relationships with labor and policymakers.


Steiner has chosen to continue the Delivering for America plan, but with a different emphasis. Rather than driving immediate and sometimes disruptive change, he has positioned himself as a stabilizer. He has spoken about balancing the need for financial discipline with the Postal Service’s obligation to universal service. Unlike DeJoy, he has been explicit in rejecting full privatization or a shift to a purely government-funded model, framing USPS as a unique hybrid institution. His early tone with employees, unions, and Congress has been more collaborative, though skepticism remains. The test will be whether this measured approach can achieve the financial stability that DeJoy pursued, while avoiding the level of conflict that defined his predecessor’s tenure.


Recent developments illustrate this balancing act. In August, Steiner reaffirmed his support for the Delivering for America plan even as a new bipartisan Congressional Postal Service Caucus formed to question parts of it, particularly processing facility closures that some lawmakers warn could slow delivery in their districts. Critics continue to raise concerns about slower service, rising postage rates, and significant financial losses, with USPS reporting a $3.1 billion loss in the most recent quarter. In response, members of Congress have introduced the Protect Postal Performance Act, which would limit USPS’s authority to close buildings, including processing facilities. Steiner’s ability to navigate these challenges while maintaining both reform momentum and political support will be closely watched.


## Financial and Operational Challenges


The financial headwinds remain significant. Mail volumes continue to decline, costs are rising, and infrastructure needs are pressing. Steiner has inherited an organization that posted substantial losses in recent years, and he has signaled that the most immediate focus will be on operational efficiency. That includes facility consolidation, technology investment, and optimization of delivery networks. These measures are designed to improve service reliability and reduce costs, but they also generate debate in Congress and among local communities.


## Labor Relations and Political Scrutiny


Union leaders have voiced strong reservations about Steiner’s appointment. Their concern is that his corporate background could lead to service cuts or job reductions, particularly in rural areas where universal service is most vital. Lawmakers have echoed those concerns and indicated they will closely monitor how reforms are carried out. Steiner has responded by stressing that employees are the core of the organization and that he intends to work with labor to achieve needed improvements.


## Looking Ahead


David Steiner’s tenure begins at a pivotal moment for the Postal Service. He was chosen to bring business discipline and forward-looking strategy, but he must also maintain public trust and ensure universal service. The contrast with his predecessor highlights a shift in style from rapid and controversial changes toward a more measured approach. As reforms continue under Delivering for America and lawmakers push for greater accountability, the coming months will provide the first indications of whether Steiner’s leadership can stabilize the Postal Service while building stronger relationships with labor, policymakers, and the public.
