# Eliminating payment friction: The fast track to trade

Your business is moving faster than ever. You’ve streamlined your supply chain, digitised your operations, and expanded your reach to new markets. But when it comes to the financial infrastructure supporting that growth, many small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) find themselves working with banking processes designed for yesterday’s trade. With limited structure or resource bandwidth, bypassing the traditional banking walls can be a struggle. Today, modern, digital fintechs are creating new opportunities, especially in the emerging markets, for small and medium businesses to trade freely and scale their operations.


## How fast is trade moving across Africa?


At nearly £25 billion, UK to -Africa trade recorded an annual growth rate of roughly 7%. The UK maintains nine trade agreements covering 18 African nations, including roll-over economic partnership agreements (EPAs) and trading blocs. When trade volumes increase, payment speed needs to match. Yet, for many business owners, the reality involves weeks of waiting for onboarding documentation to clear, manual forms, and outdated approval cycles.


This friction creates a significant hurdle, turning agile opportunities into stalled growth. To compete in today’s landscape, SMEs need to rethink how they manage cross-border payments. For businesses in the UK, time is the most valuable currency. Every day spent waiting on documentation or clearance in emerging markets is a day your business isn't moving products or settling invoices with key suppliers.


## How optimised payments protect SME margins


Modern financial solutions are now prioritising straight-through processing for local businesses. By focusing on digital-first verification, your business can now bypass the traditional waiting game. When you remove the administrative bottlenecks, you regain the ability to execute trades instantly, ensuring your cash flow stays as dynamic as your business strategy.


**Remove the payment layers**


Hidden fees and FX markups silently kill SME profitability as conventional payment routes often come with 3-5% margins that erode your bottom line before the money even arrives at its destination. The currency fluctuations between the UK and Africa widen the gap that only increases with the payment layers. Instead, businesses should invest in infrastructure that can reduce this gap and the payment frictions.


**Invest in transparent infrastructure**


Protecting your profits shouldn’t be complex. When your payment costs are predictable and transparent, you have a clearer picture of your actual operational costs. Payment workflow that allows competitive FX rates and transparency lets you keep more revenue in your business.
