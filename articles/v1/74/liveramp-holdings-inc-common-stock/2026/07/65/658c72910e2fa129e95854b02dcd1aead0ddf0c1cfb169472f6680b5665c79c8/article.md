# Beyond the Seatback: How Airlines Can Turn In-Flight Connectivity into a Travel Media Network

## Key takeaways


- **Connectivity is becoming table stakes – and a launchpad.** Onboard Wi-Fi has shifted from a premium perk to a baseline expectation, giving airlines the foundation to build travel media networks.
- **Travelers are one of brands’ most valuable audiences.** With hours of focused attention and strong purchase intent, connected travelers offer brands a uniquely engaged audience.
- **A great audience isn’t a media business on its own.** Identity resolution, addressability, and measurement transform connected cabins into travel media networks that brands can confidently invest in.


Airlines are in fierce competition to improve in-flight experiences and connectivity is the battleground. From United and Starlink to American and AT&T, every major carrier is rolling out faster, more reliable Wi-Fi so passengers can stream, scroll, shop, and stay connected at 35,000 feet, just like they can everywhere else.


But the moment a plane’s cabin offers fast, open internet, it stops being just a better passenger experience and becomes the foundation for a modern travel media network (TMN) – the airline industry’s fast-growing answer to retail media. A logged-in traveler, focused for hours at a time, is one of the most valuable audiences in advertising. For airlines, that audience is a growth opportunity hiding inside an existing investment.


With cabin connectivity becoming table stakes, airlines have an opportunity to turn that connection into a media business brands want to invest in.


## Every connected flight is a media opportunity


Onboard Wi-Fi used to be a spotty, paid afterthought. Now it’s a core part of how travelers choose an airline. For example,[80%](https://aerospaceglobalnews.com/news/in-flight-wifi-trends-moment-benchmark/) of travelers say it’s as essential as seat comfort and schedule reliability, and[89%](https://www.onboardhospitality.com/7-out-of-10-airlines-offer-inflight-wi-fi/) of legacy carriers already offer it.


The bigger story for airlines is that every authenticated session creates media infrastructure: a logged-in portal, a screen, and an identity tied to an actual travel itinerary. Airlines have already invested in connectivity. Now the opportunity is to unlock more value for passengers, the airline, and advertisers.


What makes that infrastructure so valuable is the captive audience sitting in front of it. Travel puts people in an open, spend-ready state of mind:[85% of travelers](https://www.kinectivemedia.com/Knowledge/trends-and-insights/psychology-of-travel) make unplanned purchases during a trip, in everything from retail to dining to financial services. It also holds their attention in a way few channels can. Travelers spend an average of[3.5 hours](https://content-na1.emarketer.com/travel-networks-next-wave-commerce-media) engaged across airports, airline apps, and in-flight with fewer distractions than at home or on a desktop. A receptive audience with hours of focused attention is exactly what advertisers are starved for – and what a connected cabin can deliver. With the right data foundation, connected cabins can turn those moments into valuable advertising opportunities.


## A great audience isn’t a media business on its own


Building a TMN requires more than an engaged, high-intent audience. The difference is what happens between “we have Wi-Fi and seatback ads” and “brands can reliably reach the right traveler with the right message.”


Three capabilities help make this transformation possible:


1. **Identity resolution:** A traveler may appear across a booking, the airline’s mobile app, airport kiosks, and seatback screens; but unless an airline can resolve authenticated customer identifiers across every touchpoint, the audience remains fragmented and less valuable to advertisers.[Identity resolution](https://liveramp.com/blog/what-is-identity-resolution) securely connects those interactions into a complete view of customer behavior across devices, channels, and touchpoints.
2. **Addressability:**[First-party data](https://liveramp.com/explainer/explaining-first-party-data) is what makes airline inventory worth a premium. But first, it must be connected and turned into addressable audiences brands can activate through trusted data collaboration.
3. **Measurement:** Brands invest where they can prove results. Closing the loop – connecting an in-flight or in-app impression to a business outcome – is what moves an airline from selling impressions to selling measurable performance.


Together, these capabilities turn connectivity into a true media business. Skip any one of them, and you’re left with little more than an in-flight perk supported by limited ad inventory.


Here’s what those capabilities can look like when the connected cabin becomes part of the broader media network: The opportunity for Kinective Media by United Airlines goes beyond simply adding faster Wi-Fi alongside seatback ads. It’s connecting the in-flight experience across seatback screens, personal devices, the airline app, airport touchpoints, and post-flight re-engagement. With a shared identity framework across those moments, the connected cabin becomes a source of addressable audiences, richer engagement signals, and measurable outcomes that brands can activate before, during, and after the flight.


As Carey Piraino, Director of Strategic Partnerships at Kinective Media,[explained at RampUp](https://liveramp.com/rampup-2026?wchannelid=11f8uxy1i5&wmediaid=bdg4e4num6) , “We need to bring the value of the audience together – the value of the in-flight and the re-engagement. We see conversions are up to six times as high if you couple two channels together, ensuring that we’re not just the seatback team and the personal device powered by Starlink team. Combining it in a way that makes the most sense for our customers and for our partners.”


## Turning connected cabins into travel media networks


Travel media is one of the fastest-rising advertising industries. US advertisers will spend[roughly $71 billion](https://content-naf.emarketer.com/faq-on-commerce-media-how-capitalize-on-growth-beyond-retail) on retail media in 2026, with the sharpest growth coming from networks beyond traditional retail. But scale isn’t the only advantage. Airlines that build TMNs on a foundation of identity, addressability, and closed-loop measurement will transform a captive audience into a media business that brands trust for measurable performance.

For travel brands, the connected cabin turns the flight into a planning window. A passenger headed to the beach might see snorkeling excursions while scrolling social media or a rideshare offer for airport pickup while watching YouTube during the flight. Because the airline understands the traveler’s destination and intent, every message is more relevant.


For brands outside of travel, airline data unlocks audience targeting that a seatback screen alone never could. Instead of serving one generic entertainment ad to an entire cabin, a streaming platform could promote a live sports package to a traveler watching game highlights mid-flight while showing a new original series or free trial to another traveler browsing entertainment content. That’s the difference between buying an in-flight ad placement and reaching an addressable audience in a context where attention, intent, and engagement are already high.


Airlines that build a successful TMN will use connectivity as the foundation for recognizing travelers earlier, delivering more relevant experiences, and proving measurable value to advertisers.
