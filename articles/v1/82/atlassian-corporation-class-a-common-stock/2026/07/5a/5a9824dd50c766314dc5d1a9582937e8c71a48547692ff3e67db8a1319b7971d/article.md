# Stop stitching together HR tools. Start Connecting Them.

**HR doesn’t have a tool problem, it has a connection problem. The Atlassian Ecosystem turns fragmented systems into one intelligent system of work.**


HR has quietly become one of the most cross-functional operations in any organization. Onboarding a new hire touches IT provisioning, payroll, compliance training, manager coordination, and knowledge management, often across a half-dozen platforms that don’t talk to each other.


The tools exist. The problem is they’re disconnected. Onboarding checklists live in one system while the training content lives in another. Policy approvals move through email, while the policies themselves sit in a wiki that no one can find. Every handoff is a place where work stalls, data gets re-entered, and new hires wonder if anyone planned for their arrival.


This isn’t a niche frustration. It’s a structural challenge, and it’s where the[Atlassian Ecosystem](https://www.atlassian.com/ecosystem) offers something fundamentally different. One of the ecosystem’s practical strengths is flexibility in how teams adopt it. You can buy from thousands of Marketplace apps tuned to HR use case, build custom solutions with[Forge](https://developer.atlassian.com/platform/forge/) or Rovo Studio when no existing app fits, or you can compose solutions by combining Atlassian products, Marketplace apps, and Rovo agents into automated workflows specific to your organization.


## What the Ecosystem actually is


The[Atlassian Ecosystem](https://www.atlassian.com/ecosystem) is a network of Atlassian products,[7,000+ Marketplace apps](https://marketplace.atlassian.com/) and integrations, a[global partner community](https://www.atlassian.com/partners) , and an extensible platform, all built on the same foundation. Every Marketplace app,[Rovo AI agent](https://www.atlassian.com/software/rovo) , and partner-built solution operates on the same data model, permissions, and workflows that power Jira, Confluence, and Jira Service Management. You’re not stitching together separate platforms. You’re extending one.


## Four ways HR teams put this to work


Across hundreds of enterprise deployments, HR success with the ecosystem falls into four categories:


**Planning, alignment, and optimization.** Large-scale HR transformation programs, such as reorganizations, headcount planning, and recruiting surges, require a structure that connects high-level strategy to daily execution. Apps like[BigPicture](https://marketplace.atlassian.com/apps/1212259/bigpicture-portfolio-resource-management-for-jira?hosting=cloud&tab=overview) and[Financial Manager](https://marketplace.atlassian.com/apps/1221748/financial-manager-jira-project-cost-tracking-tempo?hosting=cloud&tab=overview) bring portfolio-level visibility directly into Jira, replacing standalone PPM tools and giving HR leaders a single view of initiative progress, budget, and staffing.


**Data intelligence and platform automation.** Metrics like time-to-hire, time-to-onboard, and retention rates shouldn’t require a data team to produce. With apps like[Custom Charts](https://marketplace.atlassian.com/apps/1220925/custom-charts-for-jira-reports-dashboards-graphs-tables?hosting=cloud&tab=overview) ,[ScriptRunner](https://marketplace.atlassian.com/apps/6820/scriptrunner-for-jira?hosting=cloud&tab=overview) , and native Power BI, HR teams build dashboards and automate reporting on their own terms. ScriptRunner handles repetitive ticket routing, so service desk volume drops without adding headcount.


**Tool connectivity and integration.** This is where fragmentation hits hardest. HR teams typically run a human resource information system (HRIS) such as Workday or SAP, a service desk, a learning management system (LMS), a communications platform, and various compliance tools. Marketplace connectors like[OnLink HR for JSM](https://marketplace.atlassian.com/apps/1231655/onlink-hr-and-identity-connectors-for-jira-jsm-assets?hosting=cloud&tab=overview) sync employee data directly from Workday into Jira Service Management, eliminating manual entry and the compliance gaps that come with it. Microsoft 365 integrations keep interview scheduling in Outlook while tracking candidate progress in Jira.


**Content experience and governance.** HR owns some of the most sensitive and most-referenced content in any company: employee handbooks, offer letter templates, compliance policies, org charts. Marketplace apps like[Comala Document Management](https://marketplace.atlassian.com/apps/142/comala-document-management?hosting=cloud&tab=overview) bring approval workflows to Confluence and[Refined Sites](https://marketplace.atlassian.com/apps/1221322/refined-sites-for-confluence-intranets-documentation-kbs?hosting=cloud&tab=overview) lets teams build branded HR portals that employees can actually navigate—no design team required.

## What this looks like in practice


[Zelis](https://www.zelis.com/) , a healthcare technology company, ran separate platforms for IT service management and HR service management. The result was predictable: fragmented employee experiences, manual data entry between systems, and compliance exposure from information falling through the cracks. By consolidating onto Jira Service Management with native Workday integration, Zelis eliminated data silos across departments, reduced technical debt, and replaced an entire additional tool, while giving employees a single, consistent service experience.
