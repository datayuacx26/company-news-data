# This SC-based stock outpaced all others in 2025


##### INGEVITY IN THE NEWS


*The following is an excerpt from a January 7, 2026 article published by John McDermott in The Post and Courier.*


A year of immense change at a North Charleston-based global chemical maker ended on a high note last week for shareholders.


Ingevity Corp.’s stock was the biggest gainer among South Carolina’s small group of publicly traded companies in 2025.


The O’Hear Avenue research and manufacturing business brought on a new CEO, sold a division and announced a change in strategic direction over the course of the past 12 months.


When all was said and done, the stock had climbed 44 percent to $59.18 when the closing bell rang on Dec. 31. Big Board-listed NGVT came out of the gate a year ago at around $41, and at one point it sank below $30.


Ingevity is the 13 Palmetto State-based stocks that trade on a major exchange — and one of six to rise in 2025.


Other top gainers included the parent of Southern First Bank and consumer lender World Acceptance Corp., both from Greenville. Their shares jumped 29 percent and 23 percent, respectively, over the 12-month period.


The biggest loser by far was United Homes Group, a Midlands-based residential builder that lost 63 percent of its value last year.


Much of the slide ay UHG coincided with the Chapin company’s decision in October to remain independent after a strategic review, coupled with the abrupt resignation of six directors. They included two high-profile board members: former South Carolina governor and U.S. Ambassador Nikki Haley of Kiawah Island and James Clements, the now-retired president of Clemson University.
