# Trusted Voices: How Clinicians Can Help Shape Safer Communities

Healthcare professionals are among the most trusted voices in society. Through routine safety conversations, risk identification, and community partnerships, clinicians can play an important role in gun violence prevention and building safer communities. This discussion explores how healthcare organizations are helping shift the focus from responding to violence toward preventing it.


## **Healthcare’s Longstanding Role in Injury Prevention**


Healthcare has long played a role in helping communities prevent injury and improve wellbeing. Clinicians routinely discuss topics such as child safety seats, bicycle helmets, smoking cessation, and overdose prevention. These conversations are viewed as an extension of patient care. Increasingly, healthcare leaders are recognizing that safety conversations around gun violence can follow a similar model.


## **Why Clinicians Are Trusted Voices on Safety**


I recently participated in a discussion with healthcare leaders, they noted that many people avoid conversations about gun violence because they worry the topic may be divisive. Yet doctors, nurses, and other clinicians are widely viewed as trusted and credible professionals. Families often turn to them for guidance during some of life’s most difficult moments. When a clinician raises a safety concern, patients tend to listen because the conversation is grounded in care.


## **The Healthcare Perspective on Violence Prevention**


Clinicians also have a unique perspective. They see the human impact of violence every day. Healthcare workers care for victims, support families, and witness the physical and emotional aftermath that follows a traumatic event.


They also have opportunities to identify risks and start important conversations. Questions about safety at home or potential hazards often occur as part of routine patient care. These moments can create opportunities to provide guidance.


## **Building Safer Communities Through Partnership**


Modern healthcare organizations increasingly recognize that health is shaped by factors beyond the walls of a hospital. Healthcare leaders have an opportunity to work alongside schools, community organizations, faith-based groups, public safety agencies, and other stakeholders to support safer communities.


## **Moving From Reaction to Prevention**


By making safety conversations routine, respectful, and rooted in care, healthcare professionals can help communities move from reacting to violence toward preventing it.
