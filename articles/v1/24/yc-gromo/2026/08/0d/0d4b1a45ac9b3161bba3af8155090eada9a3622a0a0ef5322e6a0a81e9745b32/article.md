# Moonlighting Rules for Salaried Employees in India: 2026 Guide

The moonlighting rules for salaried employees in India are simpler than most people fear. No central labour law bans a private-sector employee from earning outside office hours. What binds you is your employment contract, and what you must never skip is declaring the income in your ITR. Government employees are the exception: they need prior written sanction.


The part almost nobody explains is *how you get caught* . A second **salaried job** files a fresh EPF contribution against your single UAN and produces a second Form 16 on the same PAN. Commission income from independent distribution — what a[GroMo](https://gromo.in/?ref=gromo.in) Partner earns — is not employment at all, so neither of those records is created.


*Your contract, not the Indian Penal Code, decides what you can do after 7 pm.*


> **Key Takeaways**


- There is no central Indian statute banning a private-sector employee from taking outside work. The Factories Act, 1948 restricts same-day work in two *factories* under Section 60, which does not cover most office jobs ([Section 60, Factories Act 1948](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/161214/?ref=gromo.in) , retrieved 2026-08-07).
- Government servants must get prior sanction before engaging in any trade, business or other employment under Rule 15 of the CCS (Conduct) Rules, 1964 ([ISTM, DoPT](https://www.istm.gov.in/home/css_conduct_rules?ref=gromo.in) , retrieved 2026-08-07).
- EPF is the real detection channel: multiple Member IDs sit under one UAN, and one employer can see contributions filed by another ([Vidhikarya legal blog on EPFO dual entry](https://www.vidhikarya.com/legal-blog/double-entry-in-epfo-dual-employment-moonlighting--cause-and-resolution?ref=gromo.in) , retrieved 2026-08-07).
- TDS on commission under Section 194H is 2%, and applies only once commission crosses Rs 20,000 in a financial year from 1 April 2025 ([TaxGuru on the Budget 2025 amendment](https://taxguru.in/income-tax/budget-2025-amendment-section-194h-tds-commission-or-brokerage.html?ref=gromo.in) , retrieved 2026-08-07).

## Moonlighting Rules for Salaried Employees in India: The Short Answer


Is moonlighting legal in India? Yes, for most private-sector employees, with conditions.


India has no single "anti-moonlighting law". The rule people quote is Section 60 of the Factories Act, 1948, which says no adult worker shall work in a factory on a day he has already worked in another factory ([Indian Kanoon](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/161214/?ref=gromo.in) , retrieved 2026-08-07). That section applies to factories. If you work in an office, a bank branch or an IT firm, it almost certainly does not apply to you.


Some state Shops and Establishments Acts carry similar double-employment restrictions, so the wording varies by state. But the practical rulebook for a salaried professional is the offer letter they signed.


**Government employees are treated differently.** Rule 15 of the Central Civil Services (Conduct) Rules, 1964 says no government servant shall, except with previous sanction of the Government, engage directly or indirectly in any trade or business, or undertake any other employment ([ISTM](https://www.istm.gov.in/home/css_conduct_rules?ref=gromo.in) , retrieved 2026-08-07). If you are in central or state service, get written permission first. This article is general information, not legal or tax advice.

## How Employers Actually Detect a Second Income


Detection is the question behind the question, and it is not magic. Employers find out through four record systems.


*The PF trail is the one that gets people caught. Sources cited below.*


**1. EPFO and your UAN.** Your Universal Account Number links every EPF Member ID issued to you by any employer. When a second employer starts filing PF, that contribution appears under the same UAN — and one employer can see contributions filed by another ([Vidhikarya](https://www.vidhikarya.com/legal-blog/double-entry-in-epfo-dual-employment-moonlighting--cause-and-resolution?ref=gromo.in) , retrieved 2026-08-07). Overlapping EPS and EDLI entries can also trigger queries from the regional PF office. Your own passbook is visible on the[EPFO member portal](https://www.epfindia.gov.in/site_en/FAQ.php?ref=gromo.in) .


**2. Two Form 16s under one PAN.** Both employers report salary against the same PAN. Your ITR then shows two employers.


**3. AIS and Form 26AS.** Any TDS deducted on your income shows up in your Annual Information Statement. Commission is reported under Section 194H, which reads as commission, not salary.


**4. Public profiles.** A LinkedIn headline announcing a second role is the least technical and most common trigger of all.


EPF enrolment is rarely optional, which is why the PF trail is so reliable: membership is mandatory for employees earning basic wages up to Rs 15,000 a month at a covered establishment ([EPFO member FAQ](https://www.epfindia.gov.in/site_en/FAQ.php?ref=gromo.in) , retrieved 2026-08-07).


### The distinction most articles skip


A second **salary** creates employment records. **Commission** does not.


The absence of an employment record is why a commission-based, non-employment income stream is the lower-risk route for someone who wants to keep their job. Commission income does not override your contract — an exclusivity clause still binds you — but it removes the EPF and Form 16 trail entirely.

Section 44AD lets a small business declare 6% of digital receipts (8% for cash), up to Rs 2 crore turnover ([ClearTax on Section 44AD](https://cleartax.in/s/section-44ad-presumptive-scheme?ref=gromo.in) , retrieved 2026-08-07).

If you declare actual profit instead of using a presumptive scheme, genuine costs of earning that income — a share of your phone bill, internet, travel to meet a customer — are deductible. Keep the bills. Confirm your own position with a CA.

## Mistakes That Get Salaried People Into Trouble


*Most terminations come from the bottom three rows, not from the side income itself.*


The pattern is consistent. People rarely get fired for having a side income. They get fired for using company property, working during contracted hours, or selling to the employer's own customers. Add two financial mistakes — not declaring the income, and mixing it into your salary account — and a manageable situation becomes a disciplinary one.


## FAQs


**Can my company fire me for having a side income?** It can act on a breach of your contract. If your agreement has an exclusivity or conflict-of-interest clause and you breach it, that is a contractual issue and can lead to termination. Without such a clause, and with no use of company time or resources, the ground is much weaker.


**Do I need to tell my employer?** If your contract requires disclosure or prior approval, yes — in writing. Government employees need prior sanction under Rule 15 of the CCS (Conduct) Rules, 1964. Otherwise it is a judgement call, and many people simply ask HR.


**Will two Form 16s create a problem?** Not with the tax department, as long as you report both in your ITR. The problem is visibility: two Form 16s on one PAN make dual employment obvious. Commission income does not produce a second Form 16.


**Is commission income taxed differently from salary?** It is taxed under a different head — business or professional income rather than salary — and can be reported presumptively under Section 44AD or 44ADA if you qualify. It is still added to your total income and taxed at your slab rate.


**Does a side income affect my PF or gratuity?** Not if it is non-employment income. PF and gratuity are calculated on your salary with your employer. A second *salaried* job, however, can create overlapping EPS and EDLI records under one UAN and cause claim problems later.
