#### Year at Palantir


Year at Palantir is a full-year internship for students who want deeper ownership than a standard summer term. Current paths include forward-deployed and software work.


****Best proof:**** a substantial technical project, evidence that you can learn independently, and a clear reason the full-year format fits your degree plan.


#### Software Engineering


Software Engineers sit in Product Development and build Palantir platforms, product capabilities, and supporting systems. Teams are small, so interns are expected to write maintainable code and work across an existing codebase.


****Best proof:**** tested code, a design decision, a failure you debugged, and a measurable improvement.


#### Forward Deployed Software Engineering


FDSEs sit closer to customers. They translate an open-ended operating problem into data integration, applications, AI workflows, and production solutions, often while working onsite.


****Best proof:**** a deployed system where you clarified ambiguous requirements, made technical trade-offs, and iterated with real users.


#### Deployment Strategy


Deployment Strategists connect customer workflows, data, product possibilities, and adoption. The role combines quantitative analysis, product judgment, training, demos, and communication with users and leaders.


****Best proof:**** an analysis or product intervention that changed a decision, process, or user's behavior.


#### Privacy and Civil Liberties Engineering


PCL Engineers build software for privacy, data protection, AI governance, auditing, and accountability while collaborating with engineers, designers, lawyers, and policy specialists.


****Best proof:**** a technical project where security, governance, fairness, safety, or legal constraints changed the design.

## How Should I Prepare for a Palantir Internship Interview?


Palantir says interview format and count vary by candidate and role. Its published process begins with one or two phone interviews, usually 20–45 minutes. Engineering candidates should expect a coding question; candidates who advance may meet more team members in an onsite or virtual-onsite round.


**For SWE and PCL Engineering:** practice writing readable, testable code, explaining time and space complexity, and modifying an unfamiliar system. Use Palantir's[writing-good-code guidance](https://www.palantir.com/careers/getting-hired/writing-good-code/?ref=blog.simplify.jobs) and the[SWE interview roadmap](https://simplify.jobs/blog/swe-interview-prep-roadmap-2027/?ref=simplify.jobs) .


**For FDSE and Deployment Strategy:** practice an open-ended problem with incomplete requirements. State assumptions, identify the user and decision, compare trade-offs, ask for missing context, and propose a workable first version. Palantir's[open-ended-question guide](https://www.palantir.com/careers/getting-hired/navigating-open-ended-questions/?ref=blog.simplify.jobs) explicitly values pragmatic solutions over imaginary perfection.


**For every track:** prepare accomplishments, judgment calls, failures, and motivation. Make your individual contribution clear using the[behavioral interview guide](https://simplify.jobs/blog/prepare-behavioral-interview-without-sounding-rehearsed/?ref=simplify.jobs) .
