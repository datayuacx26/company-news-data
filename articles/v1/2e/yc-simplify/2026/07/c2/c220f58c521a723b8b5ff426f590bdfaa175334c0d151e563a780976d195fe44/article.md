# How to Make Sure Your Resume Gets Past AI Screening

**AI screening is not one universal robot, and many employers do not use it at all.** When AI-assisted matching is enabled, your best strategy is simple: submit a resume the hiring system can read, then make the evidence for each real requirement easy to find. Do not try to “beat” a secret score with keyword stuffing.


This guide focuses on the newer matching layer: tools that compare candidate information with employer-defined criteria or collect structured screening answers. For the formatting and file-handling basics, use our[complete 2026 ATS resume guide](https://simplify.jobs/blog/how-to-write-ats-resume-2026?ref=blog.simplify.jobs) .


## What is the difference between resume parsing and AI candidate matching?


A parser converts your file into fields. AI-assisted matching, when an employer uses it, compares information from your application with job-related criteria and gives a recruiter a recommendation, explanation, summary, or ranking. One can happen without the other.


Stage What it does What can go wrong Your best response


**Resume parsing** Extracts contact details, employers, dates, education, and skills into structured fields. Important text can be missing, mislabeled, or read in the wrong order. Use readable text, conventional headings, and a layout that preserves reading order.


**AI-assisted matching** May compare application evidence with employer-defined skills or qualifications. Vague claims can provide too little evidence for a useful match. State the relevant skill, where you used it, and what happened.


**AI-assisted screening** May ask structured chat or voice questions and summarize the responses. An answer may not address the role-specific criterion being assessed. Answer the question directly, use a concrete example, and stay truthful.


That distinction matters. A cleanly parsed resume does not guarantee a high match, and a strong candidate can still be screened out by a required application question. Likewise, an immediate rejection does not prove that an AI model judged your resume.


## What do hiring platforms actually say their AI tools do?


The honest answer is: it depends on the product and the employer’s setup.


In its own[recruiting privacy statement](https://www.workday.com/en-us/privacy/recruiting-privacy-statement.html?ref=blog.simplify.jobs) , Workday says it uses AI tools in its corporate recruiting process to compare information such as skills, experience, location, and education with an open role. The tool can suggest grades such as “exceeds” or “meets,” while Workday’s recruiting team considers those grades alongside other factors. That statement describes Workday hiring for Workday; it does not mean every Workday customer uses the same process.


[Greenhouse Real Talent](https://www.greenhouse.com/real-talent-candidate-matching?ref=blog.simplify.jobs) describes a different model. Its Talent Matching feature analyzes a resume against recruiter-defined calibration criteria and presents recommendations with explanations in a recruiter dashboard. Greenhouse says the recruiter stays in control.


[AI Screening by VONQ inside Lever](https://www.lever.co/vonq-ai-screening?ref=blog.simplify.jobs) is different again: it can use structured chat or voice conversations after someone applies, evaluate the answers against role-specific criteria, and give recruiters dossiers, summaries, and explainable scores.


These examples show why “an LLM reads every resume and decides who advances” is the wrong mental model. Some tools match resume data. Some support a separate screening conversation. Some employers use neither. The candidate-facing application notice and privacy statement are better evidence than the logo on the careers site.


## How do you make your experience easy to match?


Start with the job description, but do not copy it. Pull out the criteria that appear to be required, then connect each one to evidence you can defend.


**Separate eligibility from fit.**


Eligibility items are usually binary: work authorization, a license, a location requirement, a degree status, or willingness to work a stated schedule. Fit criteria are things such as a programming language, customer segment, analysis method, or level of ownership.


Answer eligibility questions accurately. If your resume should confirm one of them, use the employer’s recognizable term. “Series 7” is clearer than “securities credential” when the posting names Series 7. For education requirements, use a consistent month and year; our guide to[showing your expected graduation date on a resume](https://simplify.jobs/blog/expected-graduation-date-resume?ref=blog.simplify.jobs) covers the common student formats.


**Turn each important requirement into evidence.**


A skills list says what you claim. A bullet shows where the claim came from. Matching tools may use both, and recruiters certainly do.


- **Too vague:** Responsible for reporting.
- **Better:** Built three weekly Salesforce dashboards used by a 12-person sales team to review pipeline risk.


The stronger version gives the reader a tool, output, audience, and scale. Those details are useful even if the employer uses no AI matching at all.


Use the posting’s language where it is accurate, including both a full term and a common abbreviation when helpful: “machine learning (ML),” for example. Our guide to[resume skill keywords that matter](https://simplify.jobs/blog/most-important-skill-keywords-for-resume?ref=blog.simplify.jobs) explains how to choose terms without turning your resume into a list.


**Match the job, not an imagined algorithm.**


There is no defensible universal number of keywords, no required density, and no fixed “first 200 words” rule across hiring platforms. Prioritize the handful of qualifications that genuinely describe your experience. Put the strongest evidence in your recent, relevant work rather than repeating a term throughout the page.


If you are applying to different role types, create a truthful base resume and adjust the emphasis for each job. The practical workflow in our guide to[tailoring your resume to the job description](https://simplify.jobs/blog/tips-for-tailoring-your-resume-to-the-job-description?ref=blog.simplify.jobs) helps you do that without rewriting everything.

## How much should you worry about resume formatting?


Enough to protect the content, but not enough to let formatting take over the article.


Use text that can be selected, copied, and searched. Keep important details out of graphics. Use conventional headings such as Experience, Education, Skills, and Certifications. Follow the file-type instructions in the application instead of assuming PDF is always preferred.


A single-column layout is the lowest-risk choice because its reading order is obvious. A two-column resume is not automatically rejected, though, and parser behavior varies. If you are choosing a template, compare the tradeoffs in our[single-column versus multi-column resume guide](https://simplify.jobs/blog/single-column-vs-multiple-column-resume?ref=blog.simplify.jobs) .


As a quick check, copy all the text from the final file into a plain-text editor. Confirm that names, dates, headings, and bullets appear in a sensible order. This does not reproduce every ATS parser, but it can reveal an image-only PDF or an obviously broken reading sequence before you submit.


## Which AI-screening “hacks” should you avoid?


**Hidden or irrelevant keywords.**


White text, microscopic text, and blocks of unrelated skills do not make you more qualified. They can also create an incoherent application when the text is extracted or reviewed. Use only claims you can support in an interview.


**Copying the job description.**


Shared terminology is useful; copied sentences are not evidence. If the posting asks for stakeholder management, show which stakeholders you worked with, the decision you influenced, and the outcome.


**Inventing a perfect “AI score.”**


A score from a resume tool is not the employer’s score. Different tools use different inputs and criteria, and the employer may not use matching at all. Treat a score as editing feedback, not a promise that you will advance.


**Letting generated prose erase the facts.**


AI writing assistance can help you find a clearer verb or shorten a sentence. It becomes a problem when it adds numbers, tools, or ownership you did not have. Keep the details verifiable and read every bullet aloud before submitting it.


## What should you check before you submit?


- The final file opens, and its text can be selected.
- Your contact details and dates are correct.
- You answered every eligibility question accurately.
- The resume names the most relevant skills in the job’s recognizable language.
- Important skills are supported by examples, not only listed.
- The reading order remains sensible when copied as plain text.
- You saved the exact resume version sent for this role.


The goal is not to outsmart a machine. It is to reduce ambiguity for every reviewer in the process: the parser, any configured matching or screening tool, and the recruiter who makes the next decision.

## Frequently asked questions about AI resume screening


### Does every employer use AI to screen resumes?


No. Adoption, product features, and employer configurations vary. Some systems only store and organize applications. Others add matching, ranking, summaries, fraud signals, or structured screening conversations. Look for the employer’s own candidate notice or privacy statement.


### Can AI reject my resume automatically?


Some employers may automate parts of screening, including required-question workflows, but vendor features and human-review practices differ. A rejection email alone does not reveal what happened. Check the role’s stated minimum requirements and your application answers before assuming an AI model made the decision.


### How many keywords should I include?


There is no universal number. Cover the job’s genuine, important qualifications once where they fit, then support them with evidence in your bullets. Relevance and truthfulness matter more than repetition.


### Will a two-column resume fail AI screening?


Not automatically. The concern is parsing and reading order, not AI judgment. A single column is the simplest low-risk option; if you use columns, test the final file and make sure the extracted text remains complete and ordered.


### Should I use an AI resume writer?


Use it as an editor, not a witness. It can help with structure and clarity, but you are responsible for every claim. Reject any rewrite that adds experience, results, or tools you cannot verify.
