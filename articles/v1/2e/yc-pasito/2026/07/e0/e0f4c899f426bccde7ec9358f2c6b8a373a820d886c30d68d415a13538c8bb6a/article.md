# The open enrollment checklist consultants can hand their clients

Open enrollment has a lot of moving parts, and most of them stay invisible until one gets missed. This open enrollment checklist lays out the work phase by phase, from the plan decisions locked months ahead to the debrief after the window closes. Hand it to clients as it is, or fold it into your own renewal process.


## **Why a checklist beats a calendar reminder**


Employees give the enrollment decision very little time. EBRI found that half of enrollees spent less than an hour reviewing their benefits options, and three in ten spent less than 30 minutes.1 That hour is all you get, so everything employees see in it has to be ready before the window opens.


Getting materials in front of employees is its own battle. Three in four organizations (76%) told the International Foundation of Employee Benefit Plans (IFEBP) that their biggest open enrollment communication challenge is participants who don’t open or read the materials.2 That’s what happens when everything lands at once, in one channel, right as the window opens: rushed materials, confused employees, and an HR inbox that fills up on day one.


A checklist fixes that because it front-loads the planning work. And if enrollment is already underway and the timeline got away from you, start at the phase you're in, not at the top: knock out the deadline-critical items first and circle back to the rest when the dust settles.

## **The open enrollment checklist, at a glance**


Five phases, twenty-three boxes. Each phase below explains why the work matters and what slips when it gets skipped, with the checklist items underneath. Work through them in order: every phase depends on the one before it, so a box you can't check yet usually means the previous phase isn't finished. Consultants can run the list as-is with clients, or trim it to match how a given group runs its renewal.


### **Three to four months out: Lock the plan**


Everything downstream depends on final plan decisions, so waiting here pushes everything else.


- Finalize renewal decisions, carriers, and rates with the client.
- Set the contribution strategy and confirm what changes for employees, especially anything that touches paychecks.
- Clean up census and eligibility data so enrollment files don’t break later.
- Put the enrollment dates on the calendar and work every other deadline backward from them.


### **Six to eight weeks out: Build the materials**


Once plan data is final, the materials phase begins. This is where teams can lose lots of hours without the right tools and a standard single source of truth for data. Every document repeats the same plan details in a different format.


- [Benefits guides](https://pasito.ai/who-we-serve/benefits-consultants/benefits-guides) built from the final plan documents, not last year’s copy with new numbers pasted in.
- A[benefits microsite](https://pasito.ai/who-we-serve/benefits-consultants/benefits-microsites) employees can reach without a login hunt, with every plan, form, and deadline in one place.
- A[decision support](https://pasito.ai/who-we-serve/employers/decision-support) experience that helps employees compare their real options side by side.
- One source of plan data behind all of it, so the guide, the site, and the emails never contradict each other.


### **One to four weeks out: Launch communications**


Employees need to hear about open enrollment more than once before they act on it.


- Announce the enrollment dates and what happens if employees do nothing.
- Explain what’s changing in plain language: premiums, networks, new plans, dropped plans.
- Use more than one channel.[Year-round communications](https://pasito.ai/who-we-serve/employers/year-round-communications) across email, SMS, and meetings reach the people a single newsletter misses.
- Brief managers, since employees will ask them questions.


### **During the window: Answer fast and watch the data**


The window itself is about responsiveness. Most questions are simple; they just all arrive at once.


- Give employees an[AI benefits assistant](https://pasito.ai/who-we-serve/benefits-consultants/ai-benefits-assistant) trained on the actual plan documents, so “is my doctor in network” gets answered instantly instead of joining a queue.
- Watch engagement daily: who’s enrolled, who hasn’t started, which pages get traffic.
- Send a mid-window nudge to employees who haven’t acted.
- Keep a running list of the questions that come up anyway. They’re next year’s content plan.


### **After the window closes: Confirm and debrief**


The close-out phase is the one most checklists skip, and it’s where next year’s season gets easier.


- Confirm elections transmitted cleanly to carriers and the ben admin system.
- Verify payroll deductions match elections before the first affected paycheck.
- Review the engagement data: what employees read, what they asked, where they stalled.
- Debrief with the client while it’s fresh, and log what to fix before the next renewal.


## **Make the open enrollment checklist a repeatable process**


No single item on this open enrollment checklist brings 100% of the value. The same list, run through every year, is what makes each season easier than the last. The phases that feel optional, the close-out debrief, the running list of employee questions, are usually the ones that separate a scramble from a system. Skip them and every renewal starts from a blank page. Keep them and each season inherits the last one's work.


The hard part isn't knowing what to do. It's doing all twenty-three things well, at the same time, while the plan details keep shifting under you. That's where the tooling matters. When the guide, the microsite, the communications, and the employee answers all pull from different places, every change means updating four things and hoping they agree. When they pull from one, a single correction updates everything at once.

## **FAQ**


### **What is an open enrollment checklist?**


An open enrollment checklist is a phase-by-phase list of the tasks that make enrollment run smoothly: locking plan decisions months ahead, building accurate materials, communicating early across channels, answering questions fast during the window, and confirming elections and lessons learned after it closes.


### **When should the open enrollment timeline start?**


Three to four months before the enrollment window. Plan decisions, rates, and contribution strategy come first, because guides, microsites, and communications all depend on final plan data.


### **What should happen after open enrollment ends?**


Confirm elections reached carriers correctly, verify payroll deductions, review engagement data, and hold a debrief. Teams that close out properly start the next season ahead instead of from scratch.
