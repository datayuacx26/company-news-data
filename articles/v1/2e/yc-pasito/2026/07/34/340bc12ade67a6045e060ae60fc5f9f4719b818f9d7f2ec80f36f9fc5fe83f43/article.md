# Open enrollment best practices: planning a smoother season

Open enrollment runs smoother when the work starts months before the window opens. Really, it comes down to four things: a clear timeline, communication that doesn’t stop after November, education built from the actual plans, and some way to measure what employees actually understood. Get those in place, and employees make better choices, HR fields fewer last-minute questions, and the consultant looks like the person who kept the season calm.


This open enrollment guide walks through what prevents the annual scramble and where the right tools can take the manual work off your plate.


## **What is an open enrollment best practice?**


Open enrollment best practices are the planning, communication, and education steps that help employees choose the right coverage with less confusion and less pressure on HR. They turn open enrollment from a two-week fire drill into a repeatable process.


In practice: start early, write in plain language all year, make the plan differences easy to see, and track engagement so you know what to fix before the next renewal.


## **Why open enrollment best practices matter now**


Two pressures collide every fall: cost and confusion.


On cost, KFF reported that average annual premiums for employer-sponsored family coverage reached $26,993 in 2025, up 6% from the prior year.1 Employees feel those increases in every paycheck, which raises the stakes on the choices they make at enrollment.


On confusion, Aflac’s 2024–2025 WorkForces Report found that 51% of employees still didn’t fully understand their health insurance, and 71% wanted more information about their benefits.2 When people don’t understand their options, they default to last year’s plan or the lowest paycheck deduction, and the questions land on HR during the busiest weeks of the year.


The practices below close that gap before the enrollment window opens.


## **Start the open enrollment timeline early**


The single biggest predictor of a smooth season is how early you start. Teams that wait until the window opens spend the whole season catching up.


A workable open enrollment timeline builds backward from the enrollment dates: lock plan decisions and rates first, then build materials, then communicate, then enroll, then follow up.

3–4 months out Finalize plan design, rates, and carrier decisions Consultant + HR

## **Communicate year-round, not only during the window**


The most common open enrollment mistake is treating communication as a two-week event.


People need to see information several times before it sticks, and a single newsletter during the enrollment window won’t get there. Steady, relevant communication across the year means employees arrive at enrollment already informed.

## **Make plan choices easy to understand**


Employees can’t choose well if they can’t tell the plans apart.


Clear[benefit guides](https://pasito.ai/blog/employee-benefits-guide-template-how-to-create-better-guides-faster) and[decision support](https://pasito.ai/blog/what-is-a-benefits-decision-support-tool) translate deductibles, copays, networks, and prescription rules into a side-by-side choice people can actually make. When the education is grounded in the actual plan, employees stop guessing and start comparing.

## **Measure what employees understand**


Most teams end the season with opinions about what worked. But relying on the data’s a better and more sure approach.


Track which guides and pages employees view, which questions they ask, and where they get stuck. That tells consultants and HR exactly what to fix before the next renewal, so each season starts a little further ahead than the last.


## **Turn open enrollment best practices into a year-round process**


These open enrollment best practices share one idea: the season goes well because the work starts long before the window opens.


Plan early, communicate all year, make the choices clear, answer questions fast, and measure what lands. HR gets a calmer season, employees make better decisions, and consultants prove their value when it counts.

## **FAQ**


### **What are open enrollment best practices?**


Open enrollment best practices are the planning, communication, and education steps that help employees choose the right coverage with less confusion: starting early, communicating year-round in plain language, making plan differences clear, and measuring engagement.


### **When should open enrollment planning start?**


Lock plan decisions and rates three to four months before the enrollment window, then build materials and communications in the weeks that follow. Starting that early is what prevents the last-minute scramble.


### **How can HR reduce open enrollment questions?**


Communicate consistently before the window, give employees plan-accurate guides and decision support, and use an AI benefits assistant trained on the actual plans to answer common questions instantly.
