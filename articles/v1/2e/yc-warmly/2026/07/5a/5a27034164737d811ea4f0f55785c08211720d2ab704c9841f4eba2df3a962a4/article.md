## What are the factors to consider when buying sales intelligence software?


The main factors to consider when evaluating sales intelligence platforms include the breadth & accuracy of their data, the quality of intent signals, how actionable the insights are through automation and CRM integration, and the pricing structure.


Let’s dive deeper into each one of them: ⬇️


### #1: Broad, accurate data coverage


The first question you need to ask yourself is which data types you require, such as company firmographics, up-to-date contacts, job titles and hierarchies, technographics, and revenue/employee ranges.


Equally important is the cadence of data freshness, deduplication, and enrichment (stale or poorly merged records will waste your time and money).


➡️ During a demo, you should ask for sample feeds so you can validate coverage against known accounts.


**Pro Tip:** If you target narrow industries or geographies, prefer a vendor with depth and accuracy in those niches over one with broad but shallow coverage.


### #2: Real-time intent and engagement signal quality


The 2nd question to ask yourself is whether you need live intent (e.g., content consumption, product research), website visitor identification, or only periodic engagement indicators.


You should consider signal sources, how the vendor correlates multi-channel signals, and what they do to reduce noise, as many intent feeds flag activity that isn’t sales-ready.


Examples of signals to validate include:


- Website visits and page-level behavior (e.g., visited your pricing page).
- Content downloads or webinar attendance.
- Search or third-party intent topic spikes (e.g., prospects searching for your solution category, such as accounting software).


➡️ If I were you, I’d prioritize real-time intent only when your reps can act within hours; otherwise, batched signals work just fine for account prioritization.


### #3: How does the tool turn sales intelligence into actions?


Next up, you want to know how the platform turns intelligence into immediate actions for your team: account creation, scoring, playbook triggers, routing, and enrichment inside your CRM or sales engagement platform.


Integration quality matters more than a long connectors list: you should look for bi-directional sync, customizable field mapping, and low-latency updates.


If I were you, I’d check automation use cases, such as:


- Auto-create or update CRM records from intent events.
- Trigger cadences or tasks in sales engagement tools.
- Route high-value accounts to named AEs with notifications.


➡️ **Rule of thumb:** Tools that don’t live inside your SDRs’ daily workflows will have poor adoption.


### #4: Pricing structure


Last but not least, you should evaluate the platform’s pricing structure and make sure that it’s straightforward, predictable, and aligned to real value.


There are some tools on the market that I feel like are designed to make new users spend up with their opaque credit systems.


For example, today I was browsing LinkedIn and I saw a post of a content marketer who was upset with Clay’s pricing, explaining that in 1 week they spent $800 on phone credits, with about 24% of them being wrong.


➡️ **Pro Tip:** If you’re going to choose a tool with a credit-based pricing structure, I’d recommend you first learn how to use the platform via training or watching tutorials and then spend the credits so you don’t waste them in your learning process.
