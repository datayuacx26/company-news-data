# AI alone won't transform your business. Your people will.

"What happens when you stop chasing efficiency and start designing for effectiveness?" That's the question a room full of senior executives tackled on June 16, 2026, at a Zoom Executive Community Meetup in partnership with Chief Wine Officer. Over fine wine and frank conversation, leaders from global brands explored how AI can elevate human potential rather than simply replace it.


## The farmer's wisdom: rethinking what AI is really for


Benedetto Conversano, executive advisor at Zoom, opened the evening with a Zen parable about a farmer who greets every twist of fate with the same words: "Maybe so, maybe not."


His point landed immediately. We can't use old patterns to redesign a better future. We're overindexing on efficiency at the expense of effectiveness in the AI adoption race. Technology can save time, sure. The real question is what we do with that reclaimed capacity.


"Efficiency has a floor, while effectiveness has no ceiling," Benedetto said. "Nobody made an outstanding wine shaving hours off the fermentation cycle."


The true value of AI, he argued, isn't in the hours saved. It's in how those hours get redeployed to create new value, deepen relationships, and solve problems we couldn't tackle before. Most digital transformation initiatives have failed precisely because they optimized for speed without rethinking outcomes.


Could AI actually improve quality of life at work? Benedetto thinks so. Fear, though, kills the creative thinking needed to design a better future. If we let it dominate the transformation, we lose the mental power to imagine something different.


The room responded immediately. Executives shared stories of automation projects that delivered speed gains yet failed to move the business forward. The gap between efficiency and effectiveness was familiar territory for everyone.


## From efficiency to human elevation


Laura Ball, global CX AI sales and GTM lead at Zoom, picked up the thread with a direct challenge: technology doesn't transform businesses. People do.


With characteristic Yorkshire candor (tempered, she joked, by sticking to water to avoid "oversharing"), Laura pushed the audience past efficiency metrics toward what she called "effectiveness levers."


## Five human capabilities AI can amplify


Laura outlined five distinctly human strengths that define the next era of transformation:


- Empathy. Using freed-up time to truly listen to customers.
- Curiosity. Mining new insights from every interaction.
- Creativity. Designing new services, not just faster ones.
- Proactivity. Reaching out before customers realize they need help.
- Redeployment. Transforming agents into analysts, designers, and experience architects.


## Transformation stories from the field


Drawing on a decade of redesigning operations across industries, Laura shared examples that brought the theory to life.


In financial services, customer service agents were upskilled into AI agent designers and builders. These roles didn't exist before. Now they're industry standard. In retail, store associates became digital stylists, connecting with customers remotely while driving incremental revenue. In the public sector, teams freed from administrative tasks were redirected to provide emotional support for people in financial difficulty.


These weren't technology implementations. They were human redesign stories. Each began with a simple question: "What could this person do if we freed them from the repetitive?"


## The debate: measuring what matters


Following the presentations, the room erupted into animated discussion. Leaders debated how to measure human-centered ROI, how to build psychological safety for teams navigating change, and how to make the case for empathy and creativity in traditional business frameworks.


Common themes emerged across the conversations:


- Quantifying the value of empathy and creativity remains difficult in traditional business cases.
- Leadership courage is essential to redesign work rather than simply automate it.
- New frameworks are needed that measure effectiveness, not just efficiency.
- Purpose and a sense of ownership are critical to successful AI adoption.


## Three invitations for leaders


Laura closed with three invitations that resonated across the room:


- **Reframe ROI** . Stop asking "What can we cut?" and start asking "What can we create?"
- **Redesign work** . Don't just automate tasks. Elevate people by building roles around human strengths.
- **Reimagine leadership** . Lead with curiosity, not certainty. Design for possibility rather than predictability.
