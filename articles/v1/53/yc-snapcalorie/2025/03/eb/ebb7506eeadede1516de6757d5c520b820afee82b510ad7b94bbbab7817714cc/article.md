# Why Does MyFitnessPal Charge Now?

If you're a longtime user of MyFitnessPal, you might have noticed a significant change: features that were once free are now locked behind a subscription. This shift has left many users asking, "Why does MyFitnessPal charge now?" ## The Reason Behind MyFitnessPal’s Subscription Model


MyFitnessPal’s decision to charge for features stems from a trend many apps are adopting: subscription-based revenue models. While MyFitnessPal initially built its user base offering free tools, the company announced that advanced features like barcode scanning, custom macro goals, and detailed food insights are now available exclusively through its premium subscription, which costs approximately $20 per month or $80 annually.


There are several reasons behind this shift:


- **Increased Operating Costs** : Running a comprehensive app with features like a vast food database and integrations with wearables can be expensive.
- **Focus on Revenue Growth** : As MyFitnessPal competes with other health-focused technologies, premium subscriptions provide a steady income stream.
- **Monetizing Popular Features** : Tools like barcode scanning used to be free, but the company likely recognized that users depend on it and are willing to pay for it.


While it’s understandable for companies to seek sustainability, many users feel frustrated by the sudden costs—especially since many turned to MyFitnessPal for its free features in the first place.

## Why the Change is Frustrating for Users


For years, MyFitnessPal stood out as a free-to-use, accessible option for calorie tracking and nutrition management. Now, many feel that putting basic tools behind a paywall excludes users who can’t—or don’t want to—commit to a monthly fee.


Common concerns include:


- **Added Financial Pressure** : With a cost of nearly $20/month, MyFitnessPal’s premium subscription may feel out of reach for many.
- **Loss of Free Tools** : Features like detailed tracking, macronutrient breakdowns, and progress insights used to be staples of the free version but are now restricted.
- **Limited Alternatives** : Users may find it tough to switch apps after years of logging data—especially if other apps seem equally costly.
