# How Iteration Beats Innovation in Türkiye’s Mobile Gaming Industry

Türkiye’s most successful mobile game studios are not known for inventing new genres. They’re known for taking familiar formats and executing them exceptionally well.


That was one of the strongest themes from a recent conversation between Tenjin Country Manager and Account Executive[Tabarak Paracha](https://www.linkedin.com/in/tabarak-paracha-766b3a113/) and[Batuhan Avucan](https://www.linkedin.com/in/batuhanavucan/) , founder of[Mobidictum](https://mobidictum.com/) , on[Tenjin ROI 101](https://www.youtube.com/channel/UCL16MfWoUpIowC_59DprVDQ) .


Turkish mobile game studios have built a competitive advantage through rapid testing, disciplined execution, and data-driven product development. Instead of relying entirely on new mechanics, they improve retention, monetization, live operations, and user acquisition one iteration at a time. The next challenge is combining that execution advantage with greater differentiation.


## **Iteration Is Not Imitation**


Startup culture uses innovation as the ultimate goal, so founders are encouraged to create something entirely new. Mobile gaming operates under a different set of conditions. Batuhan returned to a line often credited to investor Akin Babayigit:


> “Gaming is not an innovative game. It’s an iteration game.”


An original idea is only the start. Long-term success comes down to how fast a team can test that idea, understand player behavior, improve the product, and repeat. But a game studio doesn’t need a new genre to build a successful product.


Mobile game players already know match-3, puzzle, idle, simulation, and casual mechanics; these formats have been tested across hundreds of millions of players. Instead, a successful game might improve:


- The first-time user experience
- Level progression and difficulty
- Live operations and events
- Reward systems
- Monetization timing
- Creative strategy
- Player segmentation
- Production quality


Batuhan describes Türkiye’s iteration strength directly:


> “We are very good at replicating success.”


That’s not the same as copying a game without understanding *why* it works. It means identifying a proven player need and finding ways to serve it more effectively. This distinction matters because while imitation reproduces, iteration improves.


## **Why Mobile Gaming Rewards Iteration**


Mobile games create unusually fast feedback loops. Teams can ship prototypes, test concepts with real audiences, measure behavior, and act on the results, often through A/B testing on gameplay, interfaces, engagement systems, monetization, and ad creative.


Batuhan summarizes the model in three phrases:


> “Very fast results, iteration, borderless business.”


The cycle is straightforward:


- Launch a prototype or product update.
- Measure acquisition, retention, and monetization.
- Identify where players engage or drop out.
- Adjust the product.
- Test the change.
- Scale what works.


Individual improvements may appear small or be unnoticeable.


A clearer onboarding step might increase the number of players who complete the tutorial. raises tutorial completion. A better difficulty curve improves Day 7 retention. A tweak to an in-app offer might lift conversion without hurting the player experience.


On their own, these gains may not look transformative, but together, they determine whether a game can acquire users profitably and scale.


This sort of iteration also lowers product risk because instead of placing one large bet on an unproven idea, teams can test smaller assumptions and increase their investment as the evidence becomes stronger.


That approach has become especially important as mobile games have shifted from short-lived launches toward longer-term products supported by live operations, regular content, hybrid monetization, and continuous creative testing. Industry guidance for hybrid-casual games now emphasizes ongoing A/B testing, retention optimization, creative iteration, and data-driven adjustments to monetization.


## **Mobile Game Development Is a Data Business**


One of Batuhan’s most direct observations:


> “Mobile game making is actually mathematics.”


While having a strong mathematical substance, every mobile game is still a creative product. Art direction, characters, storytelling, audio, and gameplay design all influence how players experience them. Yet, commercial success is also determined by measurable behavior.


Mobile product teams closely monitor metrics such as:


- Day 1 and Day 7[retention](https://tenjin.com/glossary/retention-rate-rr/)
- Player progression
- [Conversion rate](https://tenjin.com/glossary/conversion/)
- [Average revenue per user](https://tenjin.com/glossary/average-revenue-per-user-arpu/)
- [Lifetime value](https://tenjin.com/glossary/lifetime-value-ltv/)
- [Cost per install](https://tenjin.com/glossary/cost-per-install-cpi/)
- [Return on ad spend](https://tenjin.com/glossary/return-on-ad-spend-roas/)
- [Churn points](https://tenjin.com/glossary/churn-rate/)
- [Session](https://tenjin.com/glossary/session/) frequency


These numbers help teams understand whether players enjoy the game, where they encounter friction, and whether acquiring more users is economically sustainable.


A small improvement in progression pacing can create more commercial value than a novel mechanic that players do not understand or return to. Similarly, a high-performing ad creative has limited value if the users it attracts do not retain or generate revenue.


These metrics cannot make every decision for the team, but they do show where a problem exists, and understanding why it exists still requires product judgment.


That is where the strongest studios combine creativity with measurement. They use data to identify the opportunity, human judgment to develop a solution, and experimentation to determine whether it worked.


## **Shorter Feedback Loops for Faster Learning**


Data only helps when teams can access and interpret it quickly. If a team waits days for reports, attribution reconciliation, or revenue matched to campaign results, the loop slows down, and by the time the answer arrives, the campaign has already spent more, the cohort has already shifted, or the next decision has already been made without it.


Iteration works best as a tight cycle: measure, understand, change, test, repeat. That takes a connected view of acquisition, retention, monetization, and revenue. For example, a campaign with a low cost per install can still perform badly if its users churn fast or never generate revenue. So this means that creative performance is tied to what happens after the install, rather than being judged on install cost alone.


Early predictive LTV signals shorten that cycle further. Instead of waiting weeks to see whether a cohort turns profitable, teams can make an earlier read and adjust spend while the decision still matters. This is where attribution and analytics infrastructure becomes part of the iteration process itself: when attribution, cost, revenue, and predictive LTV can be evaluated together, teams spend less time assembling the picture and more time deciding what to improve.


## **How Peak Games Spread the Playbook**


Peak Games proved a mobile gaming company from Istanbul could compete globally. The major acquisition also helped spread a particular operating model across Türkiye’s mobile gaming industry. Former Peak employees carried experience in product development, monetization, user acquisition, live operations, and international scaling into new companies. That network became widely known as the “Peak Mafia.”


The effect was not simply a wave of teams attempting to reproduce Peak’s games. It was the distribution of knowledge about how to build a global mobile product, test it against demanding performance targets, and improve it continuously.


This is where this story connects to the wider development of **[Türkiye’s mobile gaming ecosystem](https://tenjin.com/blog/what-turkiyes-mobile-gaming-ecosystem-can-teach-us/) .** Peak provided an important proof point, but the repeatable advantage came from the knowledge that moved into the next generation of studios.


In this article, however, the most important inheritance is not the acquisition itself. It is the discipline of learning through execution.


## **Iteration is a Minimum Requirement**


Batuhan does not believe iteration will remain a sufficient advantage forever.


> “Now the market is changing. AI is helping on the level design, and now many Vietnamese studios can copy things faster than Turks.”


AI tools are reducing the time required to develop concepts, produce assets, generate level variations, analyze data, and test creative ideas.


At the same time, mobile gaming expertise is becoming more geographically distributed. Studios in markets such as Vietnam can adopt similar development models and compete for the same global audiences.


That changes the strategic value of speed.


When only a limited number of companies can prototype and optimize quickly, iteration is a differentiator. And, when every capable studio can do it, iteration becomes a minimum requirement.


The question is no longer simply: Who can copy and improve a successful model fastest?


It becomes: Who has the insight, brand, data, or creative point of view that competitors cannot reproduce as easily?


The next competitive advantages may come from:


- More distinctive intellectual property
- Stronger emotional connections with players
- Proprietary audience and product data
- Better live operations
- More sophisticated player segmentation
- Faster organizational decision-making
- Original combinations of familiar mechanics
- Brands that can extend beyond a single game


Türkiye’s studios should not abandon iteration, but they should learn how to pair it with stronger differentiation. As iteration remains the process through which products improve, innovation itself determines which direction those improvements should take.


## **What App Marketers Can Learn from Türkiye’s Studios**


The same operating model applies beyond game design.


Successful app marketing rarely comes from rebuilding the entire strategy around each new trend. More often, it comes from testing specific changes, measuring their effect, and scaling the versions that improve long-term performance.


That iterative mindset can be applied to:


- Ad creative
- Store listings
- Audience targeting
- Onboarding
- Subscription offers
- Live events
- Monetization
- Retention campaigns
- Re-engagement
- Budget allocation


The key is to connect each test to the right outcome.


A creative test should not be judged only by click-through rate or cost per install. Teams should also understand whether the acquired users retain, subscribe, make purchases, or generate ad revenue.


An onboarding experiment should not be judged only by completion rate. It should also be connected to longer-term engagement and lifetime value.


The goal is not to make more changes. It is to learn more from each change.


That requires four disciplines:


### **Test One Clear Hypothesis**


Every experiment should begin with a specific question. Changing too many elements simultaneously makes it difficult to understand what caused the result.


### **Measure Past the First Conversion**


Installs and sign-ups are only the beginning of the user journey. Retention, revenue, and LTV reveal whether the change created sustainable value.


### **Evaluate Cohorts Consistently**


Comparing users acquired under different conditions can produce misleading conclusions. Cohort-based measurement gives teams a clearer view of how each test performs over time.


### **Act While Insights are Relevant**


A perfect report delivered too late may be less valuable than a reliable signal delivered while the team can still adjust the campaign or product.


## **The Next Evolution of Türkiye’s Mobile Gaming Industry**


Türkiye’s mobile gaming success challenges the assumption that every winning company must begin with a revolutionary idea. Peak Games, Dream Games, Rollic, and other studios demonstrated the value of disciplined execution. They took familiar player behaviors seriously, measured performance closely, and built organizations capable of learning quickly.


As Batuhan puts it:


> “You don’t have to disrupt anything. Just make something a little bit better.”


That remains a powerful principle, but the modern environment around it is changing rapidly.


AI is accelerating production. Global competitors are adopting similar playbooks. Familiar mechanics can be reproduced more quickly, and operational speed is becoming less rare.


The next generation of successful studios will need both sides of the equation. They will need enough innovation to give players a reason to care, and enough iterative discipline to turn that idea into a product that retains, monetizes, and scales.


For app marketers, the lesson is similar. Sustainable growth rarely comes from one perfect campaign or one breakthrough creative. It comes from building a system that makes each test more informative than the last.


Innovation may determine what you build next. Iteration determines how good it becomes.

## **Frequently Asked Questions**


### **What is iteration in mobile game development?**


Iteration is the process of releasing a concept or product change, measuring how players respond, and using the results to improve the next version. Studios may iterate on gameplay, onboarding, progression, monetization, live operations, or marketing creative.


### **Why is iteration important in mobile gaming?**


Mobile games generate frequent behavioral and revenue data. This allows teams to test changes and evaluate their effect on retention, engagement, monetization, and lifetime value. Small improvements across several areas can compound into a significant commercial advantage.


### **Why have Turkish mobile game studios been successful?**


Many Turkish studios combine global ambitions with disciplined product development, data-driven decision-making, rapid iteration, and strong user acquisition expertise. Experience from companies such as Peak Games also spread into a new generation of studios.


### **Does iteration reduce creativity?**


Not necessarily. Data can identify where players encounter problems, but teams still need creative judgment to develop solutions. The strongest products combine distinctive ideas with continuous testing and improvement.


### **How can app marketers use an iterative approach?**


App marketers can test individual changes to creative, targeting, store listings, onboarding, pricing, and retention campaigns. Each test should be connected to downstream outcomes such as revenue and lifetime value, rather than judged only by clicks or installs.
