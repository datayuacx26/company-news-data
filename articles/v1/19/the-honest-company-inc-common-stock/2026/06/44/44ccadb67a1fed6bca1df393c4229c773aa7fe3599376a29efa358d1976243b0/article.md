### Is It Okay to Get the Umbilical Cord Stump Wet?


Getting your baby’s umbilical stump wet before it falls off can delay the healing process, and even increase the risk of infection, since the natural skin barrier isn’t yet present in that area.

Until then, it’s essential to keep the umbilical cord area clean and dry by gently wiping it with a damp cloth during sponge baths without submerging it in water.

## How to Hold a Newborn During Bath


t

Proper support is crucial for bath safety. Here are some tips on


how to bathe a newborn


safely:


****


- Always keep one hand supporting baby’s head and neck.


- Use your non-dominant hand as the “safety hand” that never leaves baby.


- For sponge baths, lay baby on a flat, comfortable surface with a towel underneath.


- For tub baths, support baby's head and shoulders with your arm and hand, cradling their body in your arm.


- Use your free hand to wash, keeping a secure but gentle grip.


- Never leave baby unattended, even for a second.


The “football hold” works well for many families. Just cradle baby’s head in the crook of your arm while your hand grips their thigh/bottom, keeping their body secure against your side.

### Getting the Ideal


Newborn Bath Temperature


Finding the right water temperature is one of the most important steps in bathing your baby—their skin is more sensitive than ours, and water that feels comfortable to you might be far too hot for your little one. Plus, water that is too cold can make your baby uncomfortable and cause them to lose body heat quickly.


The recommended


bath temperature for newborns


is 98.6°F to 100.4°F (37°C to 38°C)—close to your baby’s natural body temperature. Don’t stress—you don’t have to come to bathtime armed with a thermometer. The most reliable methods for nailing water temperature include:


****


- Mixing the water thoroughly to eliminate hot or cold spots before testing


- Testing with your elbow or wrist instead of your hand (these areas are more temperature sensitive)


- Filling the tub with cold water first, then adding hot water


Of course, a bath thermometer adds extra reassurance and accuracy if you want to make certain you’re in the right range. It’s a simple, easy step for some peace of mind.
