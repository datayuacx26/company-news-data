# What Is Coherent Optics?

# What Is Coherent Optics?


August 5th, 2026


Coherent optics is an advanced optical transmission technology that uses the modulation of light amplitude and phase, combined with digital signal processing, to transmit massive amounts of data over existing fiber optic cables, dramatically increasing network capacity by maximizing spectral efficiency without requiring new physical fiber deployments.


Next-generation transport networks face a constant challenge: scaling capacity to keep up with relentless data growth. Legacy optical transmission technologies such as Intensity Modulation and Direct Detection (IM-DD) cannot handle modern data rates or long transmission distances efficiently.


Imagine sending a message by blinking a flashlight using morse code. Traditional systems just turn the light on and off to signal ones and zeros. Coherent optics can change the brightness, the precise timing, and the angle of the light beam simultaneously to send entire libraries of information in a single flash.


This is the core technology enabling the high-speed internet backbone. High-speed fiber access is insufficient on its own; a sophisticated, converged[IP Optical aggregation and transport network](https://ribboncommunications.com/ip-optical-solutions) is essential to fully realize the benefits of high-capacity fiber and bridge the digital divide.


### What Are the Key Components of Coherent Optics?


**A coherent optical system relies on advanced digital signal processing and sophisticated lasers to manipulate multiple properties of light for maximum data throughput.**


To understand how this works, you must examine the underlying hardware, as the architecture requires several highly specialized elements working in unison.


- **Digital signal processor (DSP):** Serving as the computational brain of the system, the DSP electronically compensates for optical fiber impairments like chromatic dispersion and polarization mode dispersion.
- **Modulation:** This process alters the optical carrier signal to encode data. Advanced systems use complex modulation formats to pack more bits into every signal.
- **Amplitude:** Amplitude refers to the intensity or brightness of the light wave.
- **Phase:** Phase represents the timing or position of the light wave in its cycle.
- **Coherent detection:** At the receiver, the incoming signal is mixed with a stable reference laser called a local oscillator. Comparing the two recovers both the amplitude and the phase of the light, the information a simple on-off receiver discards, and it is what makes the system "coherent." Combined with polarization multiplexing, which carries two independent signals on perpendicular planes of the same wavelength, coherent detection roughly doubles capacity and gives the DSP the raw information it needs to reverse fiber impairments electronically.


By controlling both amplitude and phase simultaneously, systems achieve much higher spectral efficiency. Modern optical solutions architected for[coherent routing](https://ribboncommunications.com/company/media-center/blog/what-coherent-routing) integrate these components into[Digital Coherent Optic (DCO) pluggables and amplifiers](https://ribboncommunications.com/products/service-provider-products/apollo-optical-networking) , managed through a multilayer automation platform like[Muse](https://ribboncommunications.com/products/service-provider-products/muse-multilayer-automation-platform) .


### How Does Coherent Optics Increase Network Capacity?


**By manipulating multiple properties of light simultaneously, coherent optics multiplies the amount of information transmitted per wavelength.**


Coherent optics solves a key problem: how to get more data through existing fiber optic cables. Traditional systems transmit one bit per symbol, whereas advanced coherent systems transmit multiple bits per symbol using complex modulation formats like Quadrature Amplitude Modulation (QAM). While older optical transmission technologies cannot handle modern data rates, coherent optics changes several physical properties of the light beam at once.


You achieve very high bandwidth over long distances without laying new fiber. Network architects can select capacity-reach-optimized wavelengths approaching[1.2 Tbps on the newest platforms](https://ribboncommunications.com/products/service-provider-products/apollo-optical-networking/apollo-9400-series) (800G is broadly shipping today), or alternatively, you can deploy power-cost optimized[400G and 800G ZR+ wavelengths](https://ribboncommunications.com/company/media-center/press-releases/ribbons-innovative-400g-zr-solution-changes-optical-transport-economics) for different network applications like metro or regional transport.


400G ZR+ pluggable optics offer better cost-performance than embedded proprietary optics for metro and regional networks, and increasingly for long-haul routes where their reach is sufficient; the longest routes may still favor higher-power embedded transponders.


For ultra-long-haul Data Center Interconnect (DCI) needs, 400G wavelengths are now achievable across distances previously limited to 100G. This effectively extends data-center interconnect across entire economic regions.


### What Are the Primary Benefits for Modern Networks?


**Integrating coherent optics into a converged IP and optical architecture delivers major gains in scalability, cost efficiency, and reach for service providers and enterprises.**


Effective optical solutions require the integration of[IP routing](https://ribboncommunications.com/products/ip-routing/aggregation-routers) and programmable optical networking. The optimal network architecture is not a pure implementation of either Coherent Routing (IPoDWDM) or traditional Optical Transport. It is a flexible, hybrid approach that combines both to balance cost, complexity, and performance for specific use cases like aggregation and regeneration.


Benefit Impact on Networks


Increased Capacity Multiplies data throughput on existing fiber infrastructure up to 1.2T per wavelength.


Optimized Capacity for Extended Reach Transmits signals over thousands of kilometers without requiring expensive electrical regeneration.


Cost Efficiency Reduces the total cost of ownership by using 400G ZR+ pluggables instead of proprietary embedded optics.


Tunable Capacity Software-configurable wavelengths let operators dial capacity and reach to fit each route, from 400G metro to 800G long-haul.


Disaggregated optical networks can be monetized through new commercial models. Service providers can sell virtual optical network capabilities to other operators or large enterprises using "alien spectral pipes" and "spectrum slicing."


Because optical networking can also be delivered as a secure, private, as-a-service offering for enterprise customers, this creates a new, differentiated revenue stream for service providers. Ultimately, next-generation transport combines packet switching for service flexibility with optical transport for high-capacity, long-distance backbone connectivity.
