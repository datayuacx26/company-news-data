## Why teams look for a Profound alternative


Profound is the reference point for this category. It deserves that position.


The company has shipped a lot in a short period. Projects arrived on June 26, 2026, followed by Aim on July 2. Projects converts findings into ranked work. Aim runs in the background, turns signals into tasks, and measures movement. That's a genuine closed loop.


Ads Studio followed in July as an invite-only, ChatGPT-focused beta. Shopping analysis now reaches individual products and merchants. Profound is clearly building past the dashboard.


Teams still compare alternatives for four practical reasons.


### The action system is new


Projects and Aim have existed for weeks, rather than years. They may become excellent. Today, buyers have less public evidence about how the loop performs across long-running programs, larger teams, and repeated approval cycles.


That's a maturity question.


Profound already has stronger analytics evidence than most competitors. It also has financial backing.[Fortune reported in February 2026](https://fortune.com/2026/02/24/exclusive-as-ai-threatens-search-profound-raises-96-million-to-help-brands-stay-visible/) that Profound raised a $96 million Series C at a $1 billion valuation, bringing total funding to $155 million.


Funding proves that serious investors believe in the category and the company. It doesn't tell a marketing team how much work will get completed next month.


### Agent credits cap every self-serve plan


Profound Starter is listed at $99 per month billed annually and includes 100 agent credits. Growth is listed at $399 per month billed annually and includes 400 credits.


Those limits matter because agent work is the part buyers increasingly care about. A report can run every week without changing a page, earning a citation, or resolving a crawler problem. Each executed action has a cost in credits.


Before buying, ask Profound to map a representative month of work to its credit model. Use your own backlog. A generic product demonstration won't show whether the allowance fits your program.


### Engine coverage is tiered


Starter includes ChatGPT, 50 tracked prompts, and one seat. Growth includes ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Google AI Overviews, with 100 prompts and three seats.


Enterprise expands coverage to as many as nine engines and adds Shopping, SSO, SAML, and SOC 2 support.


This structure can work well for a focused program. It becomes restrictive when a self-serve team needs Gemini, shopping results, broader model coverage, or enterprise controls without entering a custom sales process.


### Off-site execution remains the harder problem


A brand can edit its own website whenever it wants. It can't directly edit the publisher pages, comparison articles, Reddit discussions, and earned-media coverage that answer engines cite.


That's why outreach depth matters. Ask each vendor to walk you through five things:


- Which third-party page to target
- Who the right author or editor is
- How it prepares contact-specific outreach
- Where the response gets recorded
- How it measures the citation change afterward


Profound has citation analysis, Agents, Sheets, Projects, and Aim. I haven't found public evidence showing author-contact citation outreach, deep user-generated-content targeting, or automatic site-audit fixes at the same level of detail. Given their release pace, that could change by the time you read this.


### Trial terms aren't published


Profound doesn't publish trial terms. That's different from having no trial.


Ask what data you can import, how many prompts you can test, which engines are included, whether agent credits are available, and whether you can export results before the evaluation ends. Those details determine whether a trial can answer the buying question.


## How I evaluated these Profound alternatives


I compared the products against the job a buyer hires them to do. The methodology uses six criteria.


1. **Execution depth.** I looked at how far each product carries a finding through prioritization, production, approval, delivery, and later measurement.
2. **Engine coverage.** I checked the engines available on the entry plan and separated self-serve access from enterprise access. A long engine list means little when the engines a team needs sit behind a custom contract.
3. **SEO connection.** AI citations still depend heavily on searchable pages, technical access, authority, and external coverage. Products received more credit when they connected answer data to those inputs.
4. **Data usefulness.** Large indexes help with discovery. Tracked prompts help with repeated measurement. Those are different jobs. I scored them separately and skipped the biggest-number contest.
5. **Customer evidence.** Named outcomes, a substantial independent review base, and long-running deployments count more than a logo page. Small review samples are shown with their counts.
6. **Real cost.** I compared prompt allowances, engines, seats, domains, refresh frequency, agent credits, and required add-ons alongside the advertised price.


The ranking favors products that finish more of the work. A team that already has analysts, writers, PR support, and developers has a fair case for weighting reporting depth higher.

## Is there a free Profound alternative?


Not a permanent one, and you should be sceptical of anything advertised that way.


What exists is sampling. AthenaHQ gives you a free credit allocation you can spend across several engines, which is the most useful way to see real answers about your brand before paying. Most other products run a time-boxed trial instead, so you get full features for a week or two and then choose.


Free checkers are a different thing. Several vendors publish a one-off "check your AI visibility" tool that returns a single score. Those are useful for a first look and useless for tracking, because one score on one day tells you nothing about movement. AI answers vary by model, region, and rerun.


If budget is the binding constraint, the honest path is a low-cost paid tracker rather than a free one. Otterly.AI starts at $29 per month, which is less than most teams spend on a single freelance article.


## How to switch from Profound without losing your baseline


A migration should preserve the old measurement long enough to explain any change. Switching prompts, engines, locations, and tools on the same day creates noise that no dashboard can untangle.


### 1. Export the current operating set


Save the prompts, prompt groups, competitors, cited domains, historical reports, engine settings, markets, and any open Projects.


Also export a list of completed actions. That prevents the new system from recommending work your team already finished.


### 2. Mark the prompts that affect revenue


Most accounts contain a mix of commercial prompts, category research, executive curiosities, and low-value variants.


Tag the prompts tied to product discovery, vendor comparison, purchase intent, and objections. Move those first. Broad research prompts can follow after the new baseline is stable.


### 3. Match the measurement settings


Use the same engines, locations, language, prompt wording, and schedule in both products. Keep these settings stable during the overlap period.


Exact answer matching is unrealistic because generated responses vary. The goal is to compare direction across a consistent prompt set.


### 4. Run both systems in parallel


Keep Profound and the alternative active for at least one normal reporting cycle. Compare source answers, citations, brand mentions, competitors, and the date each system collected its result.


A difference isn't automatically an error. It may come from model versions, geography, account state, retrieval method, or collection time. Ask each vendor to explain the sampling method.


### 5. Test one real action workflow


Select one content gap, one citation opportunity, and one technical issue. Follow each through to completion.


Record:


- What the product found
- What it recommended
- What it produced itself
- Which approvals it required
- Where a person had to take over
- How it measured the finished work
- Which allowance, credit, or add-on it consumed


This test reveals more than a broad feature checklist.


### 6. Rebuild reports after validating the data


Move executive reports last. Stakeholders care about continuity, and an unexplained score change can damage confidence in the whole program.


Show the old and new baselines side by side. Document the engines, prompts, cadence, and sampling differences. Then set a clear date after which the new system becomes the reporting source.


### 7. Cancel after checking the archive


Confirm that exports open correctly, historical charts are saved, pending work has an owner, and finance knows the renewal date. If an agency workspace contains client data, store each client's export separately.

## Frequently asked questions


### What is the best Profound alternative?


Writesonic is my top pick when execution carries the most weight. It combines AI visibility and SEO data with autonomous content, outreach, and technical workflows. Otterly.AI is the cheapest credible paid alternative at $29 per month. Its base plan covers core monitoring, but Gemini, Claude, and Google AI Mode require add-ons. AthenaHQ offers a free 300-credit sampler, although its first ongoing paid plan costs $295 per month.


### Which Profound alternative is best for agencies?


Peec AI is the clearest agency choice because every paid plan includes unlimited seats. Starter costs $95 per month. SE Visible also includes unlimited users and starts at $99, but refreshes weekly and provides less execution support. Agencies should still compare client-workspace and prompt costs.


### Does Profound have an execution layer?


Yes. Profound has Agents, Sheets, Projects, and Aim. Projects turns findings into ranked tasks, while Aim runs ongoing work and measures movement. These capabilities shipped in June and July 2026, so the fair concern is maturity and agent-credit capacity. The execution layer exists.


### Does Profound offer a free trial?


Profound doesn't publish clear trial terms. That doesn't prove a trial is unavailable. Ask the sales team which engines, prompts, exports, and agent credits an evaluation includes. AthenaHQ offers a free credit sampler, while several alternatives publish fixed seven-day or fourteen-day trial terms.


### How much does Profound cost?


Profound Starter is listed at $99 per month billed annually for ChatGPT, 50 prompts, one seat, and 100 agent credits. Growth costs $399 for three engines, 100 prompts, three seats, and 400 credits. Enterprise pricing is custom and can include broader engine coverage, Shopping, and SSO.


### Which alternative has the broadest research data?


Ahrefs Brand Radar is the strongest choice for broad research. Its prebuilt indexes help teams examine brands, domains, topics, citations, and competitors before creating a tracked prompt set. The tradeoff is price: one AI index starts at $199 per month, while the complete bundle costs $699.


### Which Profound alternative is best for ecommerce?


Searchable reports individual products, prices, merchants, ratings, and carousel appearances on a self-serve plan, and it's a common pick among European teams. Profound offers deep Shopping analysis on larger contracts. Writesonic and Peec AI cover shopping visibility inside broader systems, which is usually enough unless merchant-share detail is the main job.
