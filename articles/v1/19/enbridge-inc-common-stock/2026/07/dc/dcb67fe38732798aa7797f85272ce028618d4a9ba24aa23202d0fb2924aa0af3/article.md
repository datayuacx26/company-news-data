# ‘A true Houston footprint’: Enbridge turns up the heat in Texas

Enbridge Houston Oil Terminal (EHOT) provides Canadian producers greater access to global markets, USGC refineries


July 06, 2026


There’s Houston-in-the-summer humidity.


And then there’s EHOT.


The **[Enbridge Houston Oil Terminal](https://www.enbridge.com/Projects-and-Infrastructure/Projects/Enbridge-Houston-Oil-Terminal)** , that is—a pivotal step in our full-path pipeline delivery system from northern Alberta to the U.S. Gulf Coast. Now in service, EHOT consists of three lateral pipelines and four above-ground storage tanks with an initial shell capacity of 2.5 million barrels.


Envisioned as the **[premier heavy crude terminal in the Gulf Coast](https://www.enbridge.com/Reports/2026-Liquids-Pipelines-Customer-Handbook/North-America-and-beyond)** , EHOT offers access to all existing delivery points for the Houston market as well as dock loading facilities—providing Canadian producers greater access to global markets.

“EHOT gives Enbridge’s liquids pipelines business a true Houston footprint,” says Matt Gagnon, a Houston-based director of business development with Enbridge’s liquids pipelines business.


“It ties our system from Edmonton all the way to the Gulf Coast, creating optionality for our customers, future opportunities for export, and new strategic connectivity along the U.S. Gulf Coast.”


The emergence of EHOT helps ensure Canadian crude reaches the markets that need it most—direct access to tidewater and international markets, as well as the heavy-oil-hungry refining market in the Houston area ideally suited to Western Canadian heavy feedstock.

EHOT provides a vital link in this **[full-path, cross-continent Enbridge pipeline chain](https://www.enbridge.com/Reports/2026-Liquids-Pipelines-Customer-Handbook/The-Enbridge-value-proposition)** , which includes:


- Our Alberta-based Athabasca system, which includes the **[Athabasca](https://www.enbridge.com/map#map:infrastructure,search=%22Line%2019%22)** , **[Athabasca Twin](https://www.enbridge.com/map#map:infrastructure,search=%22Line%2045%22)** , **[Woodland](https://www.enbridge.com/map#map:infrastructure,search=%22Lines%2049/70%22)** and **[Waupisoo](https://www.enbridge.com/map#map:infrastructure,search=%22LIne%2018%22)** long-haul pipelines from Fort McMurray to Edmonton and Hardisty.
- Our **[Canadian and U.S. (Lakehead) Mainlines](https://www.enbridge.com/~/media/Enb/Documents/Reports/2026-LP-Customer-Handbook/2026_LPCH_ENB_pipeline_system_configuration.pdf)** , stretching from Edmonton to Pontiac, IL via Superior, WI.
- Our **[Flanagan South](https://www.enbridge.com/map#map:infrastructure,search=%22Line%2059%22)** and **[Spearhead](https://www.enbridge.com/map#map:infrastructure,search=%22Line%2055%22)** pipelines carrying product from Pontiac to Cushing, OK.
- The **[Seaway](https://www.enbridge.com/map#map:infrastructure,search=%22Seaway%20Crude%20Pipeline%22)** and **[Seaway Twin/Loop](https://www.enbridge.com/map#map:infrastructure,search=%22Seaway%20Twin/Loop%22)** lines connecting Cushing to Houston, in which Enbridge has a 50% ownership stake.


“This project was a unique opportunity to build from the ground up and proudly, we were able to deliver it on time, on budget, and with no safety incidents,” adds Chad Herring, manager of Enbridge’s USGC liquids projects.
