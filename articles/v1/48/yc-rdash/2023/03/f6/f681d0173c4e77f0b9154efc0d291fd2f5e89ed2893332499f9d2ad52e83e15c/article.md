One of the common mistake that people in early stage startups make, is to keep worrying about scale while building the technology. But the hard reality is that most of the technology that is going to be built early on, would be thrown away.


In fact, I deliberately take decisions to building things that don’t scale, I know their flaws. I know when they will break, but I still go ahead and build them anyway. In early stage startup, engineering bandwidth is the most precious resource you have. A lots of it is needs to go into product discovery, Iteration after iteration to find out what can make consumers tick.

Here is everything that I have learned:


- Find a cheapest way to test your hypothesis, it comes in many **** flavor from sharing mockups to building simple hacked up solution on no-code tools.
- Your speed of execution is your only competitive advantage. Meaningful and cohesive features which come close to solve customer’s problem will be far more valuable than smooth experience on the product in the first go.
- Don’t be afraid to do things manually in the beginning, even if it doesn’t scale. This will give you a deep understanding of your customers and their needs, which will ultimately help you build a better product.
- It’s okay to have some technical debt early on, as long as you’re aware of it and have a plan to address it in the future.
- Be willing to pivot and change direction if your initial hypothesis doesn’t pan out. This is a normal part of the startup process and can help you find a product-market fit faster.
- Always be learning and iterating. The more you can learn from your customers and adapt your product to meet their needs, the more successful you will be in the long run.


Early on startups should focus on building a minimum viable product (MVP) that solves a specific customer problem, rather than trying to build a fully scalable solution from the get-go. By doing this, startups can validate their assumptions and iterate quickly based on customer feedback, rather than wasting time and resources building something that may not even be needed. Remember, it’s better to have a product that solves a real problem for a few customers than a product that doesn’t solve any problems for anyone.
