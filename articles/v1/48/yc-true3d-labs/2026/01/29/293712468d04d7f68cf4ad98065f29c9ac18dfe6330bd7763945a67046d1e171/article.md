## #1. IBM RXN for Chemistry: Best Free Retrosynthesis Tool


IBM RXN is my top pick if you’re looking for a free, reliable tool that supports retrosynthesis, reaction prediction, and automated experimental procedures. It feels like having a lab assistant that never gets tired.


### Why I Recommend It


- You can input molecules and get retrosynthetic routes in minutes
- The system uses deep learning to mimic how chemists think
- You can convert retrosynthetic paths into lab-ready protocols


IBM RXN is ideal for both students learning synthesis and professionals running real drug design projects. And because it’s entirely browser-based, there’s no software to install.


**Pros**


- Completely free
- Predicts outcomes with high accuracy
- Converts plans into experimental steps


**Cons**


- Limited customization of routes
- Some outputs lack mechanistic explanation


**Price**
Free to use


## #2. SYNTHIA: Best for Route Comparison in Industry


SYNTHIA, formerly Chematica, is a commercial-grade retrosynthesis platform used by major pharma companies. Unlike other tools, it compares multiple synthetic routes, highlighting costs and feasibility.


### Why I Recommend It


- Helps you select the best route, not just any route
- Built-in risk scoring for scale-up
- Used by Pfizer, Merck, and top CROs


SYNTHIA stands out for its ability to weigh synthetic options like a human chemist. You can enter a target molecule and compare up to 10 viable pathways, each with literature references.


**Pros**


- Enterprise-grade retrosynthesis
- Highlights cost, complexity, and practicality
- Trusted by drug discovery teams


**Cons**


- Expensive
- Not open-source


**Price**
Custom pricing (starts around $5,000 per seat)


## #3. CAS SciFinder: Best for Literature & Reaction Search


SciFinder is a chemistry-first research platform used by students, educators, and pharma companies. The platform now includes AI tools to accelerate literature discovery and chemical insights.


### Why I Recommend It


- Search for molecules, reactions, and patents in seconds
- AI suggestions help uncover relevant data you might miss
- Great for students working on review papers or presentations


When I was mentoring a group of undergrads last year, SciFinder helped them complete three weeks’ worth of paper research in about two days. The tool does a lot of the heavy lifting in background reading.


**Pros**


- Covers over 130 million reactions
- Includes patent and substance data
- AI recommends related reactions


**Cons**


- Requires institutional license
- No synthesis tools included


**Price**
Available via academic and corporate licenses


## #4. Schrödinger Platform: Best for Molecular Modeling


Schrödinger is the top platform for property prediction, docking studies, and virtual screening. If your chemistry involves computational drug design, this is the tool most teams rely on.


### Why I Recommend It


- Simulates molecule behavior before synthesis
- Supports ADME prediction, toxicity, and optimization
- Used by companies like Bristol Myers Squibb and Eli Lilly


Its integration with LiveDesign allows chemists and biologists to work collaboratively. Schrödinger is expensive but offers unmatched capabilities in property modeling.


**Pros**


- Gold-standard in computational chemistry
- Wide range of simulation tools
- Supports enterprise R&D collaboration


**Cons**


- High cost
- Learning curve for beginners


**Price**
Starts at $15,000 per user per year
