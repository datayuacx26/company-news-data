# Evergy crews restore power to 75% of impacted customers after storm with 115mph winds

*More than 500 poles damaged as severe weather moved through overnight*


Kansas City, Mo., June 9, 2026 – Evergy crews have restored power to about 75% of customers impacted by strong storms that moved across Kansas overnight. Over the course of the storm, 118,000 customers lost power. As of 6 p.m. today, just over 27,000 remain without power.


Straight-line winds caused significant damage, resulting in outages in Salina, Abilene, Junction City, Manhattan, Topeka and surrounding communities. More than 500 poles across the region either snapped in half or were so severely damaged by the storm that they need to be replaced.


The most significant damage was in Salina, where 115 mph winds uprooted large trees, took down power poles and lines, and left the entire city without power at one point last night. Evergy crews and contractors have been repairing the damage, but large sections of the power grid have to be rebuilt, including 200 poles to be replaced in Salina alone. Crews will continue restoration work in the Salina and Abilene areas throughout the week.


In Topeka, about 25,000 customers were without power at the storm’s peak. High winds caused extensive damage to trees and the power grid. Evergy expects restoration for Topeka to be complete late Wednesday evening. As of 6 p.m. today, approximately 4,100 customers in the Topeka area are without power.


Additional areas with significant damage include Manhattan and Junction City, where power restoration is expected to be complete by late Wednesday afternoon.


With additional severe storms in the forecast for Kansas and Missouri this week, power restoration may take longer with inclement weather. Working safely and quickly, Evergy crews will work around the clock until they can restore power to all customers.


Customers should always stay at least 30 feet from downed power lines, and assume they are still electrified. In an emergency, call 911 and then contact Evergy.
