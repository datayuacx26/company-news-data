## **What Happens After You Find a Sponsor?**


Once an employer agrees to sponsor you, the immigration process begins.


The exact steps depend on the Green Card category and your circumstances, but an employer-sponsored case may involve:


1. **A permanent job offer**
The employer offers you a qualifying permanent position.
2. **Labor certification when required**
Some employment-based cases require the employer to complete a labor certification process.
3. **An immigrant petition**
The employer may file Form I-140 with U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services.
4. **Waiting for visa availability**
Depending on your category and priority date, you may need to wait until an immigrant visa is available.
5. **Applying for permanent residency**
When eligible, you may complete adjustment of status in the United States or immigrant visa processing abroad.


One important point to remember is that **an approved I-140 is not the same as receiving a Green Card.**


It is an important step, but the full permanent residency process still has to be completed.


## **How Long Does Green Card Sponsorship Take?**


There is no single timeline.


Employment-based Green Card processing can take years depending on factors such as:


- Your employment-based category
- Your priority date
- Your country of chargeability
- Visa availability
- Government processing times
- Your individual immigration circumstances


Because of this, be cautious of anyone promising a guaranteed Green Card or guaranteed timeline.


## **What Should You Look for in a Sponsorship Opportunity?**


Before accepting a job based on Green Card sponsorship, make sure you understand exactly what is being offered.


Ask:


- Does this specific job include Green Card sponsorship?
- Who will be my sponsoring employer?
- Which employment-based category will be used?
- When will the sponsorship process begin?
- Who will handle the immigration process?
- Which legal or immigration costs will the employer cover?
- Will I need to relocate?
- What employment commitment is expected?


Do not rely only on phrases such as “sponsorship available.”


You want to understand what the employer is actually agreeing to do.

### How do I get a company to sponsor my Green Card?


Start by looking for U.S. employers offering permanent jobs that include Green Card or permanent residency sponsorship. You still need to qualify for the position, and the employer must be willing to support the appropriate employment-based immigration process.


### How do I find jobs that sponsor Green Cards?


Search job boards, employer career pages, recruiters, and professional networks using terms such as "Green Card sponsorship," "permanent residency sponsorship," and "EB-3 sponsorship." If a posting only says "visa sponsorship," ask whether it specifically includes permanent residency.


### Can I ask an employer to sponsor my Green Card?


Yes. If Green Card sponsorship is important to you, ask whether the specific position includes employer-sponsored permanent residency. It is usually better to clarify this early instead of assuming the employer sponsors every role.


### Can a hospital sponsor a healthcare worker for a Green Card?


Yes. Some hospitals, long-term care facilities, and other healthcare employers offer Green Card sponsorship for qualified healthcare professionals. Availability depends on the role, credentials, employer needs, immigration circumstances, and current openings.

### Does getting sponsored guarantee I will receive a Green Card?


No. An employer can offer a qualifying job and support the employment-based immigration process, but final approval is determined by U.S. government agencies. Job placement, petition approval, and final permanent residency are separate steps.


### How long does employer-sponsored Green Card processing take?


There is no single timeline. Employment-based Green Card processing can take years depending on the immigration category, priority date, visa availability, government processing, country of chargeability, and the applicant's individual circumstances.
