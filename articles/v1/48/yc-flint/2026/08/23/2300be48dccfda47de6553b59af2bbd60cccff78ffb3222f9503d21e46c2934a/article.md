## **Can LPNs Get an EB-3 Green Card?**


Yes. An LPN or LVN may qualify for an EB-3 green card when a U.S. employer offers a permanent, full-time position and is willing to complete the sponsorship process.


LPNs and LVNs play an essential role in hospitals, long-term care facilities, rehabilitation centers, clinics, and residential healthcare settings. Employers seeking dependable nursing professionals may consider EB-3 sponsorship when the position and worker meet immigration requirements.


The process for an LPN or LVN is typically completed through standard PERM labor certification rather than Schedule A. This does not mean LPNs are excluded from EB-3. It simply means the employer follows a different labor-certification pathway.


The position may qualify under the EB-3 skilled-worker category when it genuinely requires at least two years of training or experience. A role requiring less than two years may qualify under the Other Workers category.


The correct classification depends on the actual requirements of the job—not only the worker’s title.


### **What May Strengthen an LPN or LVN EB-3 Case?**


An LPN or LVN may be well positioned for sponsorship when they have:


- A permanent, full-time job offer
- Relevant nursing education or training
- Documented clinical experience
- A current or obtainable state license
- A qualifying healthcare-worker certification
- An employer prepared to complete labor certification
- Clear and verifiable employment records


Healthcare-worker certification and state licensing are separate requirements. Receiving immigration approval does not automatically grant permission to practice nursing in a particular state.


For qualified LPNs and LVNs, EB-3 can provide a meaningful opportunity to build a long-term healthcare career in the United States.

## **Can CNAs Get an EB-3 Green Card?**


Yes. A certified nursing assistant may qualify for employer-sponsored permanent residence through the EB-3 category.


CNAs provide essential hands-on support in nursing homes, assisted-living communities, hospitals, rehabilitation centers, home health settings, and other patient-care environments. Their work is an important part of the U.S. healthcare system.


Many CNA positions may qualify under the EB-3 Other Workers category because they require less than two years of training or experience. Other positions may be reviewed differently depending on the employer’s genuine minimum requirements.

These differences should not be viewed as one occupation having a “better” immigration opportunity than another. Instead, each profession follows the pathway that matches the offered position.

## EB-3 Green Cards for Healthcare Workers


### Can healthcare workers qualify for an EB-3 green card?


Yes. CNAs, LPNs, LVNs, RNs, and other healthcare workers may qualify for EB-3 sponsorship when they have a permanent, full-time job offer from a qualifying U.S. employer and meet the requirements of the offered position. The classification and application process may differ by occupation.


### Can a CNA get an EB-3 green card?


A CNA may qualify for an EB-3 green card when a U.S. employer offers a permanent position and completes the required sponsorship process. Many CNA positions may be classified under EB-3 Other Workers because they require less than two years of training or experience.


### Can an LPN or LVN receive EB-3 sponsorship?


Yes. An LPN or LVN may qualify for EB-3 sponsorship through a permanent U.S. job offer. These positions generally use the standard PERM labor-certification process rather than Schedule A. The worker must also satisfy applicable licensing, credentialing, and immigration requirements.


### Can a registered nurse get a green card through EB-3?


Yes. Registered nurses may qualify for EB-3 sponsorship, and certain professional nursing positions may use Schedule A Group I processing. The employer and nurse must still meet the applicable job, licensing, credentialing, petition, and visa-availability requirements.


### Is Schedule A required for an EB-3 green card?


No. Schedule A is a specific labor-certification pathway available to qualifying occupations, including certain professional nursing positions. Healthcare workers whose positions do not qualify for Schedule A may still pursue EB-3 through the standard PERM labor-certification process.


### Do I need a U.S. employer to apply for EB-3?


In most cases, yes. EB-3 is an employer-sponsored immigration category that generally requires a permanent, full-time job offer from a qualifying U.S. employer. The employer normally completes the labor-certification process and files the immigrant petition.


### Can I qualify for EB-3 if I am already in the United States?


Potentially. Healthcare workers already in the United States may be able to pursue employer-sponsored permanent residence, but eligibility depends on their current immigration status, employment history, priority date, visa availability, and other individual circumstances.


### Do I need a U.S. healthcare license before beginning the EB-3 process?


The answer depends on the occupation, state, offered position, and stage of the case. RNs, LPNs, LVNs, and CNAs must satisfy the licensing or certification rules that apply where they will work. Immigration approval and authorization to practice are separate requirements.


### How long does the EB-3 process take for healthcare workers?


There is no single timeline. The process may include labor certification, an employer-filed immigrant petition, priority-date waiting, credentialing, licensing, consular processing, or adjustment of status. Timing also depends on the worker’s classification and country of chargeability.


### Can my spouse and children receive green cards with me?


A qualifying spouse and unmarried children under age 21 may be eligible to apply as derivative beneficiaries. Their eligibility depends on the principal worker’s approved case, visa availability, admissibility, and each family member’s circumstances.
