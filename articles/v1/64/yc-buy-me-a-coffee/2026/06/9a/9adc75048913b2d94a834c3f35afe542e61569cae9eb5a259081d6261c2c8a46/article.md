### 1. What Selling Learning Content Actually Looks Like on Each Platform


The clearest way to understand the difference between these two platforms is to walk through what actually happens when you set up and sell learning content on each one.


#### Podia: structured course delivery


On Podia, you work inside a dedicated course builder built for online education. You create a course, divide it into sections and lessons, upload video directly to Podia’s servers, and attach supporting files like worksheets or PDFs. You can also add quizzes within lessons.


Learners enrol, pay, and land in a learner portal where they can see their progress, track completed lessons, and work through your material in sequence.You can also drip content. For example, you might release one module per week after enrolment. This approach is useful if you want to guide the pace of learning rather than handing everything over at once.


For learners, it’s a clean, familiar experience. They log in, find their course, and move through it with a clear sense of where they are. For educators who want that structured, portal-style delivery, Podia handles it well. It’s purpose-built for the traditional online course experience.


#### Buy Me a Coffee: flexible creator monetization across multiple formats


On Buy Me a Coffee, the approach to learning content is more flexible. And, for many course creators, that flexibility is precisely the point. You’re not locked into a single course format. Instead, you have several ways to deliver learning content depending on how you naturally teach, and you choose which combination works for you.


**Exclusive posts** let you publish written lessons, image-based content, or audio directly to your page. If you use memberships, you choose which tier each post is available to and a $10/month member gets access to everything published at that level. You can build a curriculum this way: a series of posts published in sequence, each with downloadable materials attached, structured around a topic you’re teaching.


**The Shop** lets you sell packaged digital products with automatic delivery — a structured PDF guide, a workbook, a bundled resource pack, or a self-contained course delivered as downloadable files. Buyers purchase once and get instant access. There’s no upload cap and no limit on how many products you list. This works completely independently of any membership.


**Coaching sessions** can be listed directly in the Shop with custom pricing and details. For educators who combine content with direct access, it’s a clean way to offer both from the same page.


**Tips** let casual visitors support your work without committing to a membership or buying a product — a frictionless way to earn from the audience you’re already building.


These formats stack naturally and work independently of each other. A course creator on Buy Me a Coffee might have a free tier for general followers, a $10/month membership with exclusive written and audio lessons, a shop selling a structured workbook, and coaching sessions available for anyone who wants direct time — all on one page, with no subscription required to get started.

### 2. The Video Question


Video is often at the centre of online course content, so it’s worth being clear about how each platform handles it.


#### Buy Me a Coffee: works with your existing video workflow


Buy Me a Coffee is designed to work with the video platforms creators already use. Rather than requiring you to re-upload your content, you embed video directly from YouTube, Vimeo, Wistia, or any external host into posts on your page.


If you want to publish a video lesson for paying supporters, you keep it on your existing hosting platform, whether it’s public, unlisted, or private, and embed it into a BMC post that’s restricted to the right audience.


For many educators, this is a genuine workflow advantage. Your video content stays on the platform where your audience already finds it. You can gate extended or members-only content through tier access without duplicating your library. If you already teach through YouTube and want to offer deeper content to paying supporters, the setup is fast and familiar. There’s no migration, no re-uploading, and no need to start from scratch.


For audio content, Buy Me a Coffee does support native uploads. You can publish audio posts directly from the platform and make them available to everyone or to paying members only. It’s a clean path for podcasters, musicians, and educators who teach primarily through audio.


#### Podia: native video hosting for a course-first experience


Podia hosts video natively. You upload directly to Podia, it lives there, and learners watch through the course player without being sent to another platform. On Mover you can host up to 500 videos, Shaker goes to 1,000, and Earthquaker is unlimited. For a structured online course business where everything lives in one place, native hosting gives learners a consistent, self-contained experience inside the learner portal.

### 3. Membership-Gated Content: How It Works on Each Platform


Both platforms let you put content behind a paywall. The difference lies in how they do it, and that shapes the experience for your learners.


#### Podia: enrolment-based access


On Podia, content is gated primarily through course enrolment. Someone pays for a course, coaching package, or community membership, gets access to that specific product, and works through it inside the learner portal. The structure is vertical — you build a product, people buy into it, they access it. It maps well to how learners expect a traditional online course to work.


#### Buy Me a Coffee: optional tiers with flexible monetization


On Buy Me a Coffee, memberships are completely optional — one tool among several, not the foundation of the platform. If you choose to use membership tiers, you set them up at different price points and publish posts at each level.


A $5/month member sees all posts tagged to that tier; a $15/month member sees everything. You can build a curriculum this way, publishing a series of lessons with each one available to the right tier of members.


But you don’t have to. Many creators on Buy Me a Coffee earn entirely through one-time purchases, digital products, coaching sessions, and tips — with no membership structure at all. Others combine all of the above. The platform gives you the flexibility to build your monetization model around how your audience actually engages, rather than fitting your content into a predefined structure.


Podia’s model is built for structured online education where learners enroll in a defined course. Buy Me a Coffee is built for flexible creator monetization across multiple formats. Which one fits you depends on which of those describes how you teach.

### 4. Pricing: What You’re Actually Paying and When


#### Podia


Podia has three annual plans designed for the course creator at different stages of growth.


**Mover at $42/month ($500/year)** includes the full course builder, coaching, digital downloads, live and in-person events, community spaces (up to 25), website, blog, landing pages, custom domain, and email up to 100 subscribers. A 5% transaction fee applies to all sales on top of this.


**Shaker at $84/month ($1,000/year)** removes the transaction fee and expands limits: 500 email subscribers, 150 products, 1,000 videos, 100 community spaces. Adds affiliate marketing, upsells, PayPal, and Zapier. This is the plan where the full feature set unlocks for a growing online education business.


**Earthquaker at $150/month ($1,800/year)** removes all limits — unlimited products, videos, spaces, assistants, and 1,000 email subscribers included. For anyone running a large-scale education business, this is the tier that makes sense.


The break-even between Mover and Shaker — factoring in the 5% transaction fee versus the higher subscription — is around $840 in monthly course revenue. Below that, Mover costs less in total. Above it, Shaker does.


Email limits are worth planning around. If growing a newsletter to your learners is part of your strategy, you’ll hit Mover’s 100-subscriber cap fairly quickly. At that point, email becomes a paid add-on that scales with list size — an additional cost that doesn’t appear in the headline subscription price.


#### Buy Me a Coffee


Buy Me a Coffee charges a flat 5% platform fee on earnings, with no subscription. Payment processing through Stripe is separate at the standard rate. If you earn nothing this month, you pay nothing. If you have a strong month, 5% goes to the platform and the rest is yours.


There’s no subscriber cap on email, no limit on how many digital products you list, and no cap on uploads. There’s also no pricing tier that locks features behind an upgrade — everything is available from day one, whether you’re earning $50/month or $5,000/month.


The practical comparison: a creator earning $500/month on Podia’s Mover plan is paying $42 in subscription fees plus $25 in transaction fees — $67 total, keeping $433. The same creator on Buy Me a Coffee pays $25 in platform fees and keeps $475. At higher revenue, moving to Podia’s Shaker plan removes the transaction fee, but the $84/month subscription becomes the new baseline cost.


For creators who don’t yet have consistent, predictable course revenue, Buy Me a Coffee’s model means the platform cost is always proportional to what you earn — a meaningful advantage in the months when things are slow.

### 5. The Learner Experience: What It’s Like on the Other Side


Your learner’s experience reflects directly on you, so this matters more than it might seem at first.


On Podia, learners create an account, log in, and see their enrolled courses in a clean learner portal. They work through lessons in sequence, track their progress, and return to where they left off. It’s familiar and feels like other online learning platforms they’ve used before. For courses with lots of video content and structured progression, this experience is genuinely strong.


On Buy Me a Coffee, a member logs into their account and sees a feed of posts gated to their tier, plus any digital products they’ve purchased in the shop. It looks and works more like a membership content feed than a course portal. So, there’s no progress tracking, no module structure, no completion percentage.


For many learners this is perfectly fine; they access what they need, when they need it. For learners who rely on the structure and motivation of tracked progression, Podia’s learner portal is the better fit.


The tip experience is worth calling out specifically: a non-member who wants to send a quick payment for something they found useful can do so on Buy Me a Coffee in about 15 seconds, with no account required. That frictionless moment is where a lot of informal learning support happens.


Someone watched your free YouTube tutorial, got value from it, and wants to send $10. On Buy Me a Coffee, it takes one tap. On Podia, there’s no clean path for that to happen.

### 6. Tools That Matter for Educators Specifically


#### Coaching and 1-on-1 sessions


Buy Me a Coffee handles coaching through the Shop — you list a session with your own pricing, description, and availability, supporters purchase it, and you coordinate directly. It’s straightforward and gives you full control over how you run your time. Podia has a more structured coaching product with built-in scheduling, which suits educators who want that managed for them.


#### Email


Buy Me a Coffee lets you email your entire supporter base or specific groups with no subscriber cap and no added cost — ever. It’s simple, direct, and doesn’t get more expensive as your audience grows. Podia’s email tools are more sophisticated, with automations and course-based segmentation. However, the entry plan is limited to 100 subscribers, and costs increase as your list grows.


#### Digital products


List as many products as you want on Buy Me a Coffee with no upload limits and automatic delivery on every purchase. There’s no ceiling to grow into and no plan upgrade required. Both platforms support digital downloads, but Buy Me a Coffee imposes no restrictions on volume or file size.

### 7. Where Podia Has the Edge


If you’re building a structured online education business in which learners move through defined modules, track their progress, and learn within a purpose-built environment, Podia offers a more complete solution.


The course builder, learner portal, progress tracking, drip scheduling, quizzes, native video hosting, community spaces, and affiliate tools add up to an infrastructure that’s genuinely purpose-built for the course creator. If you want learners to have a polished, structured experience with completion tracking and a professional portal, Podia delivers that in a way Buy Me a Coffee is not designed to replicate.


The full website with custom domain, blog, and landing pages also matters if you want a proper home base for your education brand.
