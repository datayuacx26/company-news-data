# Ghost vs. Buy Me a Coffee: Which one is actually built for creators like you?

Ghost vs Buy Me a Coffee is a common comparison for creators trying to decide how to monetize their audience.


There’s a moment most creators know well.


You’ve built something people care about — a newsletter, a YouTube channel, a design practice, a podcast — and someone in the comments says: *“How can I support you?”*


That question sounds simple. But what comes next often isn’t.


Do you send them to a newsletter platform? A subscription page? A checkout flow? A website you still haven’t finished building?


Or do you just drop a link that works immediately?


Ghost and Buy Me a Coffee both help creators earn from their work. But they’re designed for very different workflows, different levels of setup, and different definitions of what “creator business” looks like.


Choosing the wrong one doesn’t just affect fees — it can affect how quickly you start earning, how often people convert, and how much time you spend setting things up instead of creating.


Here’s a grounded comparison of Ghost vs Buy Me a Coffee, and where each one actually fits.


---


### What each platform is built for: Ghost vs Buy Me a Coffee


**Ghost** is a publishing platform first.


It’s designed for creators who want a full editorial setup: a website, a newsletter, subscription content, and ownership over their audience data. It behaves less like a tip jar and more like media infrastructure.


If your work already resembles a publication — a weekly newsletter, essays, analysis, long-form writing — Ghost gives you structure, branding, and control.


**Buy Me a Coffee** starts from a different assumption entirely.


Instead of asking you to build a publication first, it asks: *what if your audience could support you immediately, without you setting anything up?*


No hosting. No domain. No email configuration. No theme selection. Just a link you can share anywhere your audience already is.


That difference in starting point shapes everything else.

### 1. You shouldn’t have to pay to get paid


Ghost’s pricing model introduces a fixed cost before you earn anything.


To accept paid subscriptions, you need at least the **Publisher** plan.


Plan Monthly cost Purpose


Starter $15 Publishing only


Publisher $29 Paid memberships enabled


Business $199 Larger-scale publications


This means you’re committing to a monthly expense before your first subscriber.


The trade-off Ghost offers is simple: no revenue share.


If you’re earning consistently, that becomes attractive. But early on, or during inconsistent months, fixed cost matters more than percentages.


Buy Me a Coffee works differently.


There’s no monthly fee. Instead, a **5% fee applies only when you earn** .


- No earnings → no cost
- Small earnings → small fee
- Larger earnings → comparable to subscription platforms


There’s no universally better model — just different timing assumptions about when income begins.

### 2. Setup should match your momentum


A platform only works if you actually finish setting it up.


Ghost setup depends on how you use it.


At minimum, you’ll likely need to:


- Choose hosting (Ghost(Pro) or self-host)
- Set up a domain
- Configure email delivery
- Select and customize a theme
- Create membership tiers
- Set up newsletters


Ghost(Pro) simplifies this, but it’s still a full publishing system that takes time to configure.


Self-hosting adds flexibility, but also maintenance.


Buy Me a Coffee takes a different approach:


- Create account
- Connect Stripe
- Add profile
- Share link


You can go from idea → live page in minutes.


The difference is activation time.

### 3. Not every supporter wants a subscription


Ghost is fundamentally subscription-first.


That works well for audiences who are already committed readers — people who expect ongoing content.


But most audiences are mixed.


They include:


- Regular readers
- Occasional visitors
- One-time discoverers


Subscription-only models treat all of them the same way: they must become members or they can’t support you.


Buy Me a Coffee expands support types:


- **One-time tips** — immediate support
- **Subscriptions** — recurring support
- **Shop** — digital products and services


Intent isn’t uniform. Support options shouldn’t be either.


---


### 4. Friction is often the difference between intent and action


Support rarely fails because of price. It fails because of steps in between.


Ghost typically requires:


- Account creation
- Email verification
- Payment setup


This supports subscriptions and recurring billing, but adds friction.


Buy Me a Coffee simplifies one-time support:


- Click link
- Pay
- Done


On mobile, Apple Pay and Google Pay reduce friction further.


Subscriptions still involve accounts but only when users choose recurring support.


---


### 5. Publishing rhythm should match how you create


Ghost works best when publishing is consistent.


When readers subscribe, they expect ongoing content. That naturally encourages structure and cadence.


But not every creator works that way.


Some:


- publish in bursts
- create irregularly
- work across formats
- only publish when there’s something meaningful


Buy Me a Coffee decouples income from publishing frequency.


On Ghost, people subscribe to a publication.


On Buy Me a Coffee, people support a creator.


That difference matters when output is irregular.

### 6. More ways to earn means more opportunities to connect


Ghost is built for one revenue model: paid subscriptions to your content. Buy Me a Coffee gives creators significantly more surface area to earn from — and each feature serves a different kind of creator:


**Gallery & portfolio**
Visual creators — illustrators, photographers, designers — can showcase their work directly on their support page. Not a project page buried in a publication structure, but a clean, scrollable showcase that’s front and centre when someone lands on your page.


**Stream alerts**
When a supporter tips you during a live stream, it shows up as an on-screen alert in real time on Twitch or YouTube. For streamers and live content creators, that visible, instant acknowledgment is a core part of how their community operates. Ghost has no equivalent.


**Shop**
Sell digital downloads, physical items, pay-what-you-want products, or services like 1-on-1 calls and art commissions. Every product is a standalone purchase — no subscription required to buy.


**One-time tips**
The simplest form of support. A reader sees your link, taps it, pays, and moves on. No account. No commitment. Just a direct, frictionless way to say thank you.


For creators working across video, illustration, music, or live content, these features can be more relevant to their audience than a subscription newsletter model.

### 7. Your audience shouldn’t have to move platforms to support you


Ghost works best when you bring your audience to your publication.


That’s powerful for long-term editorial growth.


But many creators start elsewhere:


- YouTube
- Instagram
- TikTok
- Twitch
- X


Buy Me a Coffee works inside those ecosystems:


- link in bio
- video descriptions
- pinned comments
- stream overlays
- email signatures


No migration required. Just a support layer on top of existing attention.


You don’t ask your audience to come anywhere new. You meet them exactly where they already follow you and give them a way to support you from there.


This is particularly relevant for creators whose primary platform is video or social. Those audiences don’t naturally migrate to newsletters or independent publications. They follow creators inside the platforms they already use. A Buy Me a Coffee link works inside those ecosystems without requiring your audience to go anywhere new. Ghost, as a standalone publication platform, requires building a separate destination.


The same applies to creators with smaller but highly engaged audiences. You don’t need 10,000 YouTube subscribers for a Buy Me a Coffee link to work. You need people who care about what you make — and a frictionless way for them to show it. A link in a video description or a pinned comment is often enough. The audience is already there. The link just has to be easy to find and easier to use.


There’s also a practical discoverability point. Ghost recently launched Ghost Explore, a discovery feature for publications on the platform. But for most creators, the audience they’re trying to reach is already following them somewhere else. A support link that works within those existing platforms tends to be more immediately useful than building a new destination and hoping people find their way there.

### When Ghost is the right choice


Ghost is best when your work functions like a publication.


It fits creators who:


- publish consistently (weekly or more)
- want a full website + newsletter system
- care about SEO and long-term discoverability
- are comfortable with setup or managed hosting
- already have an audience ready to subscribe


It works especially well when subscription revenue is predictable.


---


### When Buy Me a Coffee is the better fit


Buy Me a Coffee works best when:


- you already have an audience elsewhere
- you want to start earning immediately
- you prefer minimal setup
- your publishing is irregular
- you want multiple earning formats beyond subscriptions
- you value low-friction support flows


It’s often the fastest way to turn attention into support.

### You don’t have to choose


If you’re already on Ghost and it’s working for you, there’s no reason to abandon it. Many creators run both.


Keep your Ghost publication exactly as it is. Add your Buy Me a Coffee link to your newsletter footer, your about page, or the bottom of your posts. Readers who want to send a one-time tip, buy something from your shop, or support you without a subscription now have a door open to them that Ghost doesn’t provide.


Think of it as expanding your surface area for support rather than switching tools — capturing the supporters who were never going to subscribe, without changing anything about how your publication works.


Here’s how the split tends to work in practice:


- **Ghost** handles your publication and your subscribers
- **Buy Me a Coffee** handles everyone else — the casual reader, the one-time tipper, the person who wants to buy your ebook without joining a membership


Between the two, you’re leaving far fewer supporters without a way to reach you.
