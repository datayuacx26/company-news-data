# Buy Me a Coffee vs Memberful: A Guide for Membership-First Creators

Creators often lose momentum because of one problem: turning their work into a steady source of income. That’s where memberships come in.


Instead of relying on one-time support, memberships let your audience contribute on an ongoing basis, usually in exchange for exclusive content, early access, or work you don’t publish anywhere else.


Once you’ve decided to offer memberships, the next question is where to run them. There are dozens of platforms that promise the same thing on the surface, but they differ quite a bit once you start using them.


Two of the most common options creators compare are Buy Me a Coffee and Memberful. Both help you build a membership business, but they’re designed for different kinds of creators and different stages of that journey.


This guide breaks down how they compare and which is the better fit depending on how you work.

## 1. What Each Would Cost You


Let’s start with pricing, because the higher the cost, the higher the barrier to entry.


For creators who are just getting started, a fixed monthly fee can be difficult to justify. Before you’ve built an audience or proven that people are willing to pay, every recurring expense adds risk.


You can use Memberful for free in test mode while setting everything up. Once you’re ready to accept payments, the standard plan costs $49/month, plus a 4.9% fee on each transaction.


In return, Memberful gives you a powerful membership system that sits on top of your own website. But that monthly cost exists whether you have ten paying members or none.


Buy Me a Coffee is free to start and doesn’t require a monthly subscription. Instead, it charges a 5% platform fee when you earn money, in addition to standard payment processing fees.


That makes it easier for creators who are still experimenting. Your costs grow with your income instead of arriving before it.


---


## 2. What Each Platform Is


Memberful is a membership layer that plugs into infrastructure you already run. Instead of replacing your website, it adds subscriptions on top of it. It gives you:


- **A WordPress plugin** : Install it on your site, choose which posts, pages, or categories should be members-only, and Memberful handles checkout and access. Once someone pays, the plugin unlocks the content automatically.
- **Integrations with your existing tools** : Podcast hosts, Discord, Ghost, Mailchimp, ConvertKit, Squarespace, and others. The idea is to fit into your existing workflow.
- **A hosted page of its own** : Useful if you don’t have a website yet, but generally a fallback rather than the primary way people use Memberful.


Buy Me a Coffee works from the opposite direction. The page itself is the product. Instead of plugging into a website, it gives you a standalone page where people can support you. It includes:


- **A hosted page on Buy Me a Coffee** : Sign up, choose a username, and your page is live at buymeacoffee.com/yourname.
- **A link instead of a plugin** : Share it anywhere your audience already is. People can tip you, join memberships, or buy what you’re offering without visiting a separate website.
- **Optional integrations** : Discord, OBS, Streamlabs, and others. They’re there if you need them, but the page works perfectly well on its own.

## 3. How Quickly You Can Get Started


On Memberful, setup often starts before Memberful even enters the picture. You need a website already running, usually WordPress. Once that’s in place, you install the plugin, connect Stripe, and decide which pages, posts, or categories should sit behind each membership tier. None of those steps are particularly difficult on their own, but together they add up.


Buy Me a Coffee starts much closer to the point of earning. You sign up, choose a username, and your page is live. From there, you can turn on memberships, customize tiers, and start sharing your page. There is no website to configure or plugin to install, which makes it much quicker to go from signup to accepting your first payment.

## 4. What Running a Membership Actually Feels Like


Once you’re live, the difference shows up less in features and more in workflow.


On Memberful, running a membership means working across multiple tools. You publish content in WordPress or your CMS as usual, but Memberful handles access, billing, and subscriber management separately. If you’re running a podcast, that adds another system through your hosting platform. Email may sit in a third tool like Mailchimp or ConvertKit. Each part works well on its own, but you’re switching between dashboards depending on what you’re doing.


On Buy Me a Coffee, most of that lives in one place. You publish posts, manage memberships, send updates, and handle tips or digital products from the same dashboard. There are fewer moving parts, which means less context switching.


## 5. Everything Outside Memberships


Imagine you’re a creator publishing online.


You post your work regularly. Your audience reads it or follows along. Some of them subscribe, unlocking a paid tier of content on your WordPress site. That becomes the main system: content sits behind a paywall, and access is tied to recurring payments.


But not everyone wants to support a creator that way.


Now imagine a different setup.


Your content, your support options, and your monetization all live on a single page. Someone reads your work and, in the same place, leaves a small tip, buys a digital product, books a session, or joins a membership.


That is the real difference between Memberful and Buy Me a Coffee in practice.


Memberful is built for one thing: gated subscriptions. That focus is intentional, but it also means everything outside that model sits elsewhere.


There is no native way to accept one-time tips, which removes a common form of casual support. Digital products can be sold, but it feels more like delivering files or links than running a proper storefront. Coaching, consultations, and bookings are not supported natively, so you end up stitching together external tools to fill those gaps.


Buy Me a Coffee, on the other hand, treats memberships as one option inside a broader system. Tips, products, and services all live alongside memberships on the same page.


This matters because audience support is not uniform. Some people will never subscribe but will still want to contribute something small. Some prefer buying a single product over committing to a monthly membership. Others only want a one-time session or workshop.


Instead of assuming every supporter should become a member, Buy Me a Coffee gives people multiple ways to support your work. Memberships are one of them, not the only one.

## 6. Where Your Content Actually Lives


The two platforms also disagree on where your work should live.


On Memberful, your content stays exactly where it already is: your WordPress posts, your podcast feed, or your Discord server. Memberful’s job is simply to check who’s paid before granting access. Nothing about your content moves to Memberful itself.


On Buy Me a Coffee, you can host content directly on your page. Posts, galleries, and downloadable files all live in the same place your supporters already visit. Or you can keep publishing wherever you already do and simply share your Buy Me a Coffee page alongside it. Either approach works. Nothing has to move.


This difference matters most if you already care about separating your content from your payment platform. A podcaster with years of episodes on their own hosting service has little reason to move any of it. Memberful simply adds a membership layer on top of what’s already there.


A creator starting from scratch has a different problem to solve. They need somewhere to publish, somewhere to get paid, and something they can share with their audience. Buy Me a Coffee combines those pieces into a single page, which means there’s less infrastructure to manage from day one.

## 7. Who Each Platform Is Actually For


At this point, the difference stops being about features and starts being about setup philosophy.


**Memberful** fits creators who already have an established content business.


If you already have a website, a mailing list, a podcast, or a content archive you control, Memberful becomes a clean monetization layer on top of it. It assumes you’re not starting from zero. You already have content worth gating, and now you want to charge for access in a structured way.


It works best when:


- You already run a WordPress, Ghost, or custom website
- You want full control over how your content is delivered
- Subscriptions are your primary source of revenue
- You’re comfortable using separate tools for email, shops, tips, and bookings


**Buy Me a Coffee** fits creators who are still building an audience or simply don’t want to manage that infrastructure.


It doesn’t assume you have a website, a mailing list, or even a publishing system. It gives you a page where people can discover your work, support it, and buy from you. Memberships, tips, products, and posts all grow from that same place.


It works best when:


- You’re early-stage or audience-first
- You want income from more than just memberships
- You prefer one page that brings everything together
- You don’t want to build and maintain a website before you start earning


---


## 8. Can You Use Both?


Some creators run both, and the split usually follows a simple pattern.


Memberful gates the content that’s already substantial and already built: the show archive, years of essays, or the paid tier of a website that already exists.


Buy Me a Coffee handles everything around it. Tips from people who aren’t ready to subscribe yet, a digital product that doesn’t belong behind a paywall, and a simple way to message your audience without adding another tool.


A podcaster with a loyal but not-yet-paying audience is a good example. Memberful manages the paid podcast feed and subscriber access. Buy Me a Coffee gives casual listeners a way to support the show without committing to a monthly subscription, while also giving the creator a place to sell products or accept tips.


The main thing to watch for is stack creep. A website, a podcast host, Memberful, and a Buy Me a Coffee page is a lot of infrastructure for one person to manage. It only makes sense once each piece is earning its place. It’s probably not the setup you start with before you’ve proven your audience is willing to pay.


---


## 9. Which One Makes Sense for You


Memberful earns its place when a few things are already true: you have a content-heavy site worth gating, enough recurring revenue that its fees are a small operating cost rather than a meaningful chunk of what you’re making, and subscriptions are your primary source of income. At that point, its depth around churn management, large-scale billing, and integration with an existing tech stack is hard to replace.


For most creators evaluating these two, at least one of those conditions isn’t met yet. If you’re still building an audience rather than gating an established one, Memberful’s fees are overhead before the revenue justifies them. If your income comes from multiple sources like tips, products, occasional memberships, or coaching, Memberful handles only one of those cleanly and pushes you elsewhere for the rest.


Buy Me a Coffee works across those stages without a fixed monthly cost sitting in the background. It won’t replace Memberful’s subscription management depth for a large-scale membership publication. But for the wider range of creators who want a simple, low-friction way to earn from their audience, in whatever combination of tips, products, memberships, and sessions fits their work, it covers the basics without asking you to build infrastructure first.
