# 5 High-Risk Areas Burglars Commonly Exploit in Self-Storage Facilities

Security has become one of the most important differentiators in self-storage, and most break-ins can be traced back to a handful of common vulnerabilities. As facilities modernize and customer expectations rise, operators must identify and address the security gaps criminals commonly target. Here are five of the most common areas burglars exploit in self-storage facilities and practical ways to help reduce risk.


##### 1. Main Entry Gates Without Smart Security


The facility gate serves as the first layer of defense, yet many unauthorized entries occur through shared access codes, tailgating, or outdated gate entry systems. Criminals may even rent a unit to study facility traffic patterns and identify security gaps before returning to commit theft. Strengthening gate security with mobile access provides individualized credentials by phone number instead of gate codes that can easily be shared. Smart access paired with robust[security gates](https://www.janusintl.com/security-gates) , and activity tracking can help reduce opportunities for unauthorized entry while providing operators with greater visibility into who is entering and exiting the property.


##### 2. Storage Unit Doors Without Smart Locks


Many break-ins occur at the unit level, where criminals commonly target traditional padlocks, hasps, and door systems using bolt cutters, prying tools, or other simple methods. According to industry security reports, cutting locks and forcing doors remain among the most frequently used break-in techniques.[High-security doors](https://www.janusintl.com/ns-enhanced-security-brochure) paired with smart locks can provide an additional layer of protection at the unit level. Features such as reinforced bottom bars, anchored guides, and enhanced door security can help strengthen resistance to common break-in methods while improving overall facility security.


##### 3. Poorly Lit Areas, Blind Spots, and Secondary Entry Points


Criminals often seek out areas where activity is less likely to be noticed, including side entrances, interior hallways, elevator lobbies, fence lines, and poorly lit sections of a property. Limited visibility can create opportunities for theft, especially during overnight hours. Effective lighting, surveillance coverage, perimeter security measures, and smart security technologies help operators improve awareness across the property and reduce the likelihood that suspicious activity goes unnoticed.


##### 4. Facilities That Lack Smart Entry and Security Solutions


Criminals often target facilities that rely solely on traditional security measures such as padlocks, keypads, and surveillance cameras. While these tools remain important, they may not provide the level of deterrence needed to address increasingly sophisticated break-in tactics. Modern security technologies, enhanced door systems, and smart security solutions can help operators move beyond detection and take a more proactive approach to protecting their facilities.


##### 5. Legacy Access Control Without Smart Security Insights & Training


Many operators do not discover theft until after damage has occurred. Traditional security tools can provide valuable evidence, but they often lack the real-time visibility needed to identify suspicious behavior before a loss takes place. In addition, security is only as effective as the people managing it. Regular staff training, documented security procedures, modern reporting tools, and integrated security technology can help operators respond more quickly to incidents, improve investigation efforts, and maintain stronger oversight across the facility while helping address some of the[most common break-in methods facing self-storage facilities today.](https://www.modernstoragemedia.com/msm-exclusives/the-last-word-pete-frayser-janus-international-group)


##### Reducing Risk Starts With Addressing Common Vulnerabilities


Most self-storage burglaries can be traced back to a handful of recurring vulnerabilities: weak gate security, outdated locks, facility blind spots, a lack of modern security deterrents, and limited visibility into site activity.


The challenge is that even small security gaps can create opportunities for unauthorized access and increase the risk of theft. Whether it's a shared gate code, an aging unit door, inadequate lighting, or an overreliance on traditional security measures, vulnerabilities can quickly become larger security concerns.


As security expectations continue to evolve in self-storage, operators are investing in stronger physical security measures, smart locks, high-security doors, advanced gate solutions, and smart security technologies. Addressing these high-risk areas can help reduce opportunities for theft, build tenant confidence, and strengthen long-term facility performance.
