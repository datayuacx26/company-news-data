# Most Paywalls Fail Before the User Even Hits Subscribe

Your paywall is not the problem. Everything that happens before it is.


When apps struggle with conversion, they usually start tweaking the paywall itself — the price, the copy, the button color. But the real issue is almost always upstream. If a user hits your paywall without context, without feeling anything, they are going to bounce. Every time.


## The problem is sequence, not price


Think about what your user has experienced by the time they see a price tag. Did they get a taste of what the app can do? Did they have a moment where they thought, "oh, this is actually useful"? Or did they just see a loading screen and then a paywall?


The apps that convert well follow a pattern. First, they give users a sandbox experience where everyone gets the same polished version of the product. The user does something, accomplishes something, feels something. That is the foundation.


## Make the user sell themselves


After the initial experience, smart apps use a short questionnaire. The questions are designed to get users to identify their own problem. When someone types out what they are struggling with, they have done half your sales work for you. They are no longer a cold lead — they are someone who just admitted they need help.


Add social proof right before the paywall. Reviews from people who had similar problems. This closes the gap between "I need this" and "I trust this."


By the time the paywall shows up, it should feel like the obvious next step. If it feels like a wall, you did not do enough work before it.


Stop optimizing the paywall. Start optimizing everything before it.
