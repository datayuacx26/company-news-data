Every year between September and January, Indian businesses go through the same hiring frenzy. Retailers bring on extra floor staff for the Diwali rush. Restaurants hire additional waiters and kitchen hands for the wedding and banquet season. E commerce platforms scramble to onboard hundreds of delivery executives to handle the spike in orders. Warehouses add packers, loaders, and sorters to keep up with festive demand.


The numbers are staggering. Retailers and e commerce platforms see a 30 to 40 percent surge in hiring needs during the festive season. Forty three percent of employers report higher recruitment volumes during this period compared to the rest of the year. Warehouse helpers, delivery executives, store staff, and customer service professionals are the most in demand roles.


And almost none of them are verified before they start work.


The logic seems sound on the surface. These are temporary roles. They will be here for two or three months and then move on. We do not have time for background checks. We need them on the floor tomorrow.


This logic has cost Indian businesses crores in theft, fraud, accidents, and legal liability. Temporary staff who are hired fast, given access fast, and never verified are the single highest risk category of employees. And yet they are the ones who receive the least scrutiny.


This article is about why that needs to change and how to do it without slowing down your festive season hiring.


## Why Temporary Staff Are Higher Risk, Not Lower


There is a widespread assumption that temporary employees are “lower risk” because their access is limited and their tenure is short. This assumption gets the risk calculation exactly backwards.


Temporary employees know they are leaving soon. This fundamentally changes the incentive structure. A permanent employee who steals from the cash register risks their career, their reputation, and their long term income. A temporary employee who is planning to leave in six weeks anyway faces virtually none of those consequences, especially if their identity was never properly verified.


Temporary employees are often hired in bulk with minimal individual attention. When you are onboarding 30 people in a single day for a warehouse operation, the temptation to skip verification is enormous. But that bulk onboarding is also the moment when someone with fake documents is most likely to slip through, because nobody has time to look closely.


Temporary employees often receive the same access as permanent staff. A seasonal retail associate handles the same cash register, the same inventory, and the same customer interactions as someone who has been with the company for three years. A temporary delivery executive carries the same customer orders and collects the same cash on delivery payments. The access is identical. The accountability is not.


Temporary employees are harder to trace after they leave. If a permanent employee commits theft, they are still showing up to work the next day. You know where to find them. A temporary employee who steals on their last week of the season simply does not come back. If you never verified their identity, you have no way to trace them. The name on their file might be someone else’s name. The address might be fake. The phone number might already be disconnected.


## The Festive Season Fraud Patterns You Need to Know


Having worked with employers across retail, hospitality, logistics, and warehousing, certain fraud patterns repeat themselves every festive season with almost clockwork predictability.


### Cash skimming in retail


During the Diwali rush, daily footfall and transaction volumes spike dramatically. More transactions mean more opportunities for cash to disappear between the customer’s hand and the register. Temporary cashiers and floor staff who know their tenure is short and whose identity was never verified are the primary risk. A cashier who skims Rs 500 per day across a 60 day festive season walks away with Rs 30,000 from a single store. Multiply that across multiple locations and multiple unverified hires, and the numbers become significant.


### Inventory walkouts


Festive season means high value inventory. New product launches, premium gift sets, expensive electronics, and branded merchandise fill the stockroom. Temporary warehouse and store staff who handle this inventory have daily opportunities to pocket items. In the fashion retail segment, studies suggest that internal theft accounts for a significant portion of the 1.2 to 2.5 percent revenue shrinkage that Indian retailers experience annually. The festive season, with its higher inventory volumes and more temporary staff, sees a proportional increase.


### Delivery fraud


E commerce companies onboard delivery executives at massive scale during the festive season. These executives carry customer packages and collect cash on delivery payments. An unverified delivery executive with a fake identity can mark packages as delivered without actually delivering them, collect COD payments and disappear, or steal high value items and claim the package was damaged or lost. The black market for delivery platform credentials is real. Unverified individuals purchase login credentials and employee IDs from departing delivery executives, allowing them to work under someone else’s identity.


### Kitchen and supply pilferage


Restaurants, caterers, and cloud kitchens hire extra cooks, helpers, and servers for the wedding and event season. Temporary kitchen staff have access to raw materials, ingredients, and cooking supplies. Daily pilferage of a few kilograms of chicken, vegetables, or cooking oil might seem minor, but across a three month festive and wedding season, it adds up to tens of thousands of rupees. If the temporary cook is operating under a fake name and address, there is no accountability and no recourse.

## What to Verify for Seasonal Hires (And What You Can Skip)


You do not need to run comprehensive background checks on every seasonal hire. That would be impractical and unnecessary. But you do need to verify their identity. Here is a practical framework.


### For every seasonal hire, without exception


Run a PAN check. This confirms the candidate’s legal identity against the Income Tax Department database. It is the single most important check because it establishes who the person really is. If someone is working in your store under a fake name, a PAN check catches it instantly.


Run a Voter ID check. This provides an independent identity cross reference from the Election Commission. Comparing the name and date of birth across PAN and Voter ID gives you two source identity confirmation.


### For delivery, driver, and field roles


Add a Driving Licence check. This confirms the licence is active, valid, and authorizes the right vehicle class. For festive season delivery surges, this check is non negotiable. A single accident involving an unverified driver with a suspended licence can result in criminal liability, customer lawsuits, and reputational damage that outlasts the festive season by years.


### For candidates claiming previous experience


Add a UAN check. If a seasonal hire says they have done this work before, whether it is retail, warehousing, or delivery, the UAN will show whether previous employers actually contributed PF for them. This catches fabricated experience claims quickly and cheaply.


### What you can skip for seasonal hires


Education verification, criminal record checks, address field visits, and credit history checks are typically unnecessary for temporary frontline roles. These deeper checks are best reserved for permanent hires, senior roles, and positions in regulated industries. For seasonal staff, identity verification across PAN, Voter ID, DL, and UAN provides proportionate protection at a practical cost.


## How to Build Verification into Your Seasonal Hiring Playbook


The best time to integrate verification into your festive season hiring is before the season starts. Here is how to build it into your playbook so it runs smoothly when the hiring rush begins.


First, make verification a documented requirement. Add it to your seasonal hiring SOP alongside other intake steps like collecting Aadhaar copies, issuing ID cards, and assigning uniforms. When it is written into the process, it is not optional. It is just another step.


Second, set up your ID Verify account before the season starts. Create your account, familiarize yourself with the interface, and run a test check on a current employee to see how the process works. This way, when the bulk hiring begins, there is no learning curve.


Third, integrate verification into the intake flow. When candidates arrive for onboarding, have them fill out a form that includes their PAN number, Voter ID number, DL number (if applicable), and UAN (if they claim previous experience). While they complete the rest of their joining paperwork, run the checks. Results arrive before the paperwork is done.


Fourth, establish a clear policy for handling red flags. Decide in advance what you will do if a check comes back negative. If a PAN does not match, the candidate does not proceed. If a DL is suspended, they are not assigned to a driving role. If UAN contradicts claimed experience, a conversation happens before a decision is made. Having this policy in writing prevents ad hoc decisions under time pressure.


Fifth, file every report. Download the PDF verification report for each seasonal hire and store it with their temporary employee file. Even though they are temporary, the documentation protects you for the duration of their engagement and afterward if any issues surface.

## Frequently Asked Questions


### Is it practical to verify 50 or 100 temporary hires in a single week?


Yes. Digital verification takes minutes per candidate. With an efficient intake process, a single person can verify 15 to 20 candidates per hour by running checks while candidates complete other onboarding tasks. For 50 hires, you need roughly 3 to 4 hours of verification time spread across your onboarding sessions.


### What if seasonal candidates do not have all four documents?


Run whichever checks are available. Most working age adults have a PAN card and Voter ID at minimum. For delivery roles, a DL is essential regardless. UAN is a bonus check for candidates claiming prior experience. Even two checks (PAN and Voter ID) provide meaningful identity verification.


### Should I verify temporary staff sourced through a staffing agency?


Absolutely. Under the DPDP Act 2023, you as the employer remain the Data Fiduciary regardless of the sourcing channel. If a staffing agency sends you an unverified candidate who causes a data breach or theft, you bear the regulatory liability, not the agency. Always verify independently.


### What happens if I find a discrepancy in a seasonal hire’s documents after they have already started working?


If the discrepancy is serious, such as a completely fake identity, you should suspend access immediately and consult with legal counsel about termination. If it is minor, such as a name spelling variation, address it through a conversation. This is exactly why it is better to verify before the person starts work. Prevention is always simpler than remediation.


### Can I reuse verification results if I rehire the same seasonal worker next year?


Verification results are a snapshot of the candidate’s status at the time of the check. DL validity can change, UAN records will have additional entries, and PAN status can shift. It is best practice to run a fresh verification each season, especially for DL checks where licence status may have changed.


### Is the festive season too chaotic for a new process like this?


That is exactly why you should set it up before the season begins. Create your ID Verify account, test the process, and write it into your seasonal hiring SOP during a calm period. When the rush begins, verification is just another step in an already established workflow, not a new burden added during the busiest week of the year.
