**1. What Is Form 16? (And Why It Is Now Form 130)**


Form 16 is a TDS (Tax Deducted at Source) certificate that an employer issues to salaried employees, certifying the amount of tax deducted from their salary and deposited with the government. For over 60 years, Form 16 served as the primary document linking an employee’s salary income to their tax compliance under the Income Tax Act 1961.


However, **starting from Tax Year 2026-27, Form 16 has been officially replaced by Form 130** under the new Income Tax Act 2025. The government introduced the new Act to simplify and modernise India’s tax framework, and as part of this overhaul, most tax forms received new numbers and revised structures.


While the name and form number have changed, the core purpose remains identical: Form 130 certifies the salary paid, exemptions and deductions claimed, and the TDS deposited on behalf of the employee. Employees still need this certificate to file their Income Tax Return (ITR).


This guide covers **both Form 16 (for FY 2025-26) and Form 130 (for FY 2026-27 onwards)** , so whether you are filing for the current year or the previous year, you have everything you need.


**2. Form 16 vs Form 130: What Changed?**


The transition from Form 16 to Form 130 is not merely a renaming exercise. The new form introduces structural improvements that provide greater transparency to employees. Here is a side-by-side comparison:


**Parameter** **Form 16 (Old)** **Form 130 (New)**


**Governing Law** Income Tax Act 1961 Income Tax Act 2025, Section 395(4)(b)


**Structure** Two parts: Part A + Part B Three parts: Part A + Part B + Part C


**Part A** Employer/employee details, TDS deposited (auto-populated from TRACES) Employer/employee details, TAN, PAN, employment period, TDS deposited (auto-populated from TRACES)


**Part B** Salary breakup, deductions under Chapter VI-A, full tax computation TDS computation summary


**Part C** Not applicable Detailed salary computation with breakup of all components, deductions, and exemptions


**Generation** TRACES portal TRACES portal only (no offline generation)


**TDS Return Form** Form 24Q (quarterly) Form 138 (quarterly)


**Due Date** 15 June of assessment year 15 June of assessment year


**Applicable From** Up to FY 2025-26 FY 2026-27 onwards


**Key takeaway:** The addition of Part C in Form 130 means employees now get a far more detailed salary breakup directly in their TDS certificate, reducing confusion and the need to cross-reference multiple documents.


**3. Components of Form 130: Parts A, B & C Explained**


Form 130 comprises three distinct parts, each serving a specific purpose in documenting the salary-TDS relationship between employer and employee.


**3.1 Part A: Employer and Employee Details**


Part A is auto-populated from the TRACES portal based on the quarterly TDS returns (Form 138) filed by the employer. It contains:


- **Employer details:** Name, address, TAN (Tax Deduction and Collection Account Number)
- **Employee details:** Name, PAN (Permanent Account Number)
- **Period of employment:** Start and end dates with the employer during the tax year
- **TDS deposited:** Quarter-wise breakup of tax deducted and deposited with the government
- **Challan details:** BSR code, challan serial number, date of deposit, and amount for each TDS payment
- **Assessment year:** The assessment year to which the certificate pertains


Since Part A is system-generated, employers cannot modify it. This ensures data integrity and prevents discrepancies between what the employer reports and what the government records show.


**3.2 Part B: TDS Computation Summary**


Part B provides a consolidated summary of the tax computation. Think of it as the executive summary of the employee’s tax position for the year. It includes:


- **Gross total income:** Total salary income before deductions
- **Deductions claimed:** Summary of deductions under various sections
- **Taxable income:** Net income after all deductions
- **Tax payable:** Computed tax liability including surcharge and cess
- **Relief under Section 131:** If applicable (equivalent to old Section 89 for salary arrears)
- **Net tax payable:** Final tax after all adjustments
- **TDS deducted vs deposited:** Comparison to identify any shortfall or excess


**3.3 Part C: Detailed Salary Computation (New)**


**Part C is entirely new in Form 130** and did not exist in Form 16. This is the most significant structural change. It provides:


- **Salary component breakup:** Basic salary, HRA, special allowance, DA, conveyance, medical, LTA, and every other component paid
- **Exemptions claimed:** HRA exemption computation, LTA exemption, standard deduction, and other exempt allowances with detailed calculations
- **Perquisites:** Value of all perquisites (company car, housing, ESOP, etc.) with computation methodology
- **Profits in lieu of salary:** Gratuity, leave encashment, compensation, etc.
- **Deductions under Chapter VIII:** Detailed list of deductions (equivalent to old Chapter VI-A) including 80C, 80D, 80E, NPS contributions, etc.
- **Income from other sources:** If declared to the employer (interest income, rental income, etc.)
- **Tax regime elected:** Old regime vs new regime indication


**4. Complete Form Mapping: Old Act vs New Income Tax Act 2025**


The Income Tax Act 2025 renumbered and restructured most tax forms. Below is the complete mapping that every payroll professional and employer must know:


**Old Form (IT Act 1961)** **New Form (IT Act 2025)** **Purpose**


**Form 16** **Form 130** Salary TDS certificate issued by employer to employee


**Form 16A** **Form 131** Non-salary TDS certificate (interest, professional fees, etc.)


**Form 16B** **Form 132** TDS certificate for property transactions


**Form 16C** **Form 133** TDS certificate for rent payments


**Form 24Q** **Form 138** Quarterly TDS return for salary payments


**Form 26Q** **Form 139** Quarterly TDS return for non-salary payments


**Form 27D** **Form 134** TCS (Tax Collected at Source) certificate


**Form 26AS** **Form 168** Tax Passbook (consolidated tax credit statement)


**Form 12BB** **Form 124** Employee investment declaration to employer


**5. How to Generate Form 130 from TRACES (Step-by-Step)**


Generating Form 130 requires that you have already filed your quarterly TDS returns using Form 138 on the TRACES portal. Here is the complete process:


**Step 1: File Quarterly TDS Returns (Form 138)**


Before you can generate Form 130, you must file all four quarterly TDS returns for the tax year using Form 138. These returns report the salary paid to each employee, exemptions and deductions claimed, and TDS deducted and deposited. Ensure every quarterly return is filed and processed by TRACES without errors.


**Step 2: Verify TDS Challan Deposits**


Confirm that all TDS payments have been deposited with the correct challan details. Mismatched challans are the most common reason for Form 130 generation failures. Cross-check the BSR code, challan serial number, deposit date, and amount for every quarter.


**Step 3: Log In to TRACES**


Visit the TRACES portal at[tdscpc.gov.in](https://tdscpc.gov.in/) and log in using your TAN, user ID, and password. If you have not registered on TRACES, you must register as a Deductor first.


**Step 4: Navigate to the Download Section**


After logging in, go to **Downloads > Form 130** from the main navigation menu. This option becomes available only after your Q4 Form 138 return has been processed.


**Step 5: Select Tax Year and Enter Employee PAN(s)**


Select the relevant Tax Year (e.g., 2026-27), choose the quarter, and enter the PAN number(s) of the employees for whom you want to generate Form 130. You can generate certificates for individual employees or in bulk.


**Step 6: Download the Generated Form 130**


TRACES generates the Form 130 as a downloadable file. For bulk downloads, you will receive a zip file. Part A is auto-populated by TRACES based on your filed returns, while Parts B and C require employer input during the filing process.


**Step 7: Verify All Details**


Before issuing Form 130 to employees, meticulously verify every detail:


- Employee PAN and name are correct
- Salary breakup matches the payroll records
- Deductions and exemptions match the investment proofs submitted
- TDS deducted matches the amount shown in payslips
- Challan details are accurate for all quarters


**Step 8: Sign the Certificate**


Form 130 must be signed by the authorised signatory of the employer. This can be done digitally (using a DSC) or physically. Ensure the signatory’s details are updated on TRACES.


**Step 9: Issue to Employees by 15 June**


Distribute the signed Form 130 to all employees by **15 June 2027** (for Tax Year 2026-27). Late issuance attracts a penalty of Rs 100 per day per certificate under Section 272A.


**6. How to Generate Form 16 for FY 2025-26**


**Still Relevant** Form 16 is still the valid TDS certificate for FY 2025-26 (Assessment Year 2026-27). If you have not yet issued Form 16 for FY 2025-26, follow these steps. The deadline is 15 June 2026.


**Step 1: File Form 24Q Quarterly TDS Returns**


Ensure all four quarterly Form 24Q returns for FY 2025-26 have been filed and processed on TRACES. Q4 is particularly important as it contains the annual salary computation.


**Step 2: Log In to TRACES**


Go to[tdscpc.gov.in](https://tdscpc.gov.in/) and log in with your TAN credentials.


**Step 3: Navigate to Downloads**


Go to **Downloads > Form 16** . Select the Financial Year (2025-26) and the relevant quarter (typically Q4 for annual certificates).


**Step 4: Add Authorised Signatory**


Select or add the authorised signatory details. TRACES requires this information to generate the certificate with the correct signatory.


**Step 5: Download and Merge**


Download the zip file from TRACES. It contains Part A (auto-generated by TRACES). You must prepare Part B separately (containing salary breakup, deductions, and tax computation) and merge it with Part A to create the complete Form 16.


**Step 6: Issue to Employees**


Sign the complete Form 16 (Part A + Part B) and distribute to employees by 15 June 2026.


**7. Form 138: Quarterly TDS Returns (Replacing Form 24Q)**


Form 138 is the quarterly TDS return form for salary payments under the Income Tax Act 2025, replacing the erstwhile Form 24Q. This is the foundation upon which Form 130 is built, because TRACES uses the data from Form 138 to auto-populate Part A of Form 130.


**What Must Be Reported in Form 138**


- Employee-wise salary details for the quarter
- TDS deducted from each employee’s salary
- Challan details for TDS deposited with the government
- PAN of each employee (mandatory)
- Deductions and exemptions claimed by each employee
- Tax regime elected by each employee (old or new)


**Filing Schedule for Form 138**


**Quarter** **Period** **Filing Deadline**


**Q1** April – June 2026 31 July 2026


**Q2** July – September 2026 31 October 2026


**Q3** October – December 2026 31 January 2027


**Q4** January – March 2027 31 May 2027


**8. Due Dates and Penalties for Non-Compliance**


Timely compliance is critical. The Income Tax Act imposes strict penalties on employers who fail to meet TDS filing and certificate issuance deadlines.


**8.1 Key Due Dates**


**Obligation** **Due Date** **Applicable Form**


**TDS deposit (salary)** 7th of following month Challan


**Q1 TDS return filing** 31 July Form 138


**Q2 TDS return filing** 31 October Form 138


**Q3 TDS return filing** 31 January Form 138


**Q4 TDS return filing** 31 May Form 138


**Issue Form 130/16** 15 June Form 130 / Form 16


**8.2 Penalties**


**Offence** **Penalty** **Section**


**Late filing of TDS return (Form 138)** Rs 200 per day (max = TDS amount) Section 234E


**Late issuance of Form 130** Rs 100 per day per certificate Section 272A


**Incorrect information in certificate** Up to Rs 50,000 Relevant provisions


**Non-deduction of TDS from salary** Interest at 1% per month + penalty Section 201


**Example:** If an employer with 50 employees issues Form 130 on 30 June 2027 instead of 15 June 2027 (15 days late), the penalty would be: 50 certificates x Rs 100/day x 15 days = Rs 75,000. For small businesses, this can be a significant financial burden.


**9. Employee Guide: Using Form 130/16 to File Your ITR**


As an employee, your Form 130 (or Form 16 for FY 2025-26) is the most important document for filing your Income Tax Return. Here is how to make the best use of it:


**9.1 Verify Salary Details Against Payslips**


Cross-check every salary component listed in Part C of Form 130 with your monthly payslips. Discrepancies could indicate payroll errors. Pay particular attention to:


- Basic salary and HRA amounts
- Special allowances and bonuses
- Perquisite values (company car, housing, etc.)
- Deductions claimed (PF, professional tax)


**9.2 Check TDS Credits in Form 168 (New 26AS)**


Form 168 (the new Tax Passbook, replacing Form 26AS) shows all TDS/TCS credits, advance tax, and self-assessment tax payments. **Always verify that the TDS amount in Form 130 matches the credit shown in Form 168.** If there is a mismatch, notify your employer immediately, as it could mean the TDS was not deposited correctly.


**9.3 Multiple Employers in a Tax Year**


If you changed jobs during the tax year, you must collect Form 130 from each employer. Each employer will issue a separate certificate for the period you worked with them. When filing your ITR, you need to consolidate the income and TDS from all Form 130 certificates.


**9.4 Filing ITR Without Form 130**


While it is possible to file your ITR without Form 130 (using payslips, Form 168, and bank statements), it is strongly recommended to wait for the certificate. Form 130 provides the most accurate and comprehensive salary and TDS information. If your employer has not issued it by 15 June, remind them that late issuance attracts a penalty.


**9.5 Record Retention**


**Keep your Form 130/16 for at least 7 years** from the end of the relevant assessment year. The Income Tax Department can reopen assessments within this period, and you may need the original certificate as proof of income and TDS.


**10. Form 168: The New Tax Passbook**


Form 168, under the Income Tax Act 2025, replaces the well-known Form 26AS. It is officially called the Tax Passbook and serves as a consolidated statement of all tax-related transactions linked to your PAN.


**What Form 168 Contains**


- **TDS credits:** All TDS deducted by employers, banks, and other deductors
- **TCS credits:** Tax Collected at Source by sellers
- **Advance tax:** Self-paid advance tax instalments
- **Self-assessment tax:** Tax paid at the time of filing ITR
- **Refunds:** Any refunds processed by the Income Tax Department
- **High-value transactions:** Specified Financial Transactions (SFTs) reported by banks, mutual funds, property registrars, etc.


**How to Access Form 168**


Employees can view Form 168 by logging in to the[Income Tax e-Filing Portal](https://www.incometax.gov.in/) using their PAN and password. Navigate to **e-File > Income Tax Returns > View Form 168** to access the Tax Passbook.


**Why Employers Should Care About Form 168**


While Form 168 is primarily an employee-facing document, employers should be aware that any discrepancy between the TDS they report in Form 138 and what appears in the employee’s Form 168 will be flagged by the system. This creates compliance risk. Ensuring accurate and timely Form 138 filing is the best way to avoid such discrepancies.
