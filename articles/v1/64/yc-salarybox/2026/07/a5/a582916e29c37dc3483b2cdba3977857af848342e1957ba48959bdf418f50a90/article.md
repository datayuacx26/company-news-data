Every year, millions of salaried employees across India receive revised pay packages after their appraisal cycles conclude. According to a 2024 Aon India Salary Increase Survey, over 85% of Indian companies revise salaries between April and September, often with retrospective effect. When the difference between the revised salary and the old salary is paid for past months in a single instalment, it is called salary arrears.


For HR teams and business owners, processing arrears in salary correctly is critical. Miscalculations can lead to incorrect PF deposits, wrong TDS deductions, employee grievances, and even penalties from statutory authorities. This comprehensive guide covers everything you need to know about salary arrears: their meaning, step-by-step calculation, tax treatment, Section 89(1) relief, Form 10E filing, statutory implications, and best practices for seamless payroll processing.


## **What Are Salary Arrears?**


**Salary arrears are the difference between the revised salary and the old salary for past months, paid in the current month.** When an employer increases an employee’s compensation with retrospective effect (for example, a pay hike effective from April but processed in July), the employee is entitled to receive the differential amount for the months that have already passed. This differential payment is known as arrears in salary.


In simpler terms, arrears represent money that was owed to the employee but not paid on time. The payment “catches up” in a later pay cycle, and it must be processed with all applicable statutory deductions and tax calculations.


**Key Definition:** Arrears in salary = (Revised salary per month – Old salary per month) x Number of past months for which the revision applies. This amount is typically paid as a lump sum in the month the revision is processed.


Salary arrears are distinct from bonuses, incentives, or ex-gratia payments. While bonuses are performance-based or discretionary payouts, arrears are a contractual obligation arising from a delayed salary revision. Employers are legally bound to pay arrears once a revision is approved with retrospective effect.


## **Common Causes of Salary Arrears**


Understanding why salary arrears arise helps HR teams plan their payroll cycles more effectively. Here are the most common causes:


- **Delayed Annual Appraisals:** Most Indian companies follow an April-March financial year. Appraisal results are often finalised in June or July, but salary revisions are effective from April. The gap between the effective date and the processing date creates arrears for 2-3 months.
- **Retrospective Promotions:** When an employee is promoted with effect from an earlier date (for example, promoted in August but effective from April), the salary differential for the intervening months becomes arrears.
- **Dearness Allowance (DA) Revisions:** Central and state government employees frequently receive DA revisions with retrospective effect. The 8th Pay Commission recommendations, expected in 2026, will likely generate significant arrears for government employees across India.
- **Pay Commission Implementation:** Pay commissions in India (the 7th Pay Commission being the most recent) revise pay structures with retrospective dates, sometimes spanning 2-3 years. The resulting arrears can be substantial.
- **Payroll Errors and Corrections:** If an employee was underpaid due to a payroll error (wrong grade, missing component, incorrect basic salary), the correction payment is treated as arrears.
- **Union Negotiations and Wage Agreements:** In industries with active unions, wage agreements may be finalised after prolonged negotiations, with the revised wages applied retrospectively from the date of the expired agreement.
- **Court Orders or Tribunal Awards:** Labour courts or industrial tribunals may order salary revisions with retrospective effect, requiring employers to pay arrears.


According to a 2024 People Matters survey, approximately 67% of Indian companies process at least one round of salary arrears per financial year, with the average processing delay being 2.4 months after the revision effective date.


## **How to Calculate Salary Arrears**


The formula for calculating salary arrears is straightforward, but the execution requires component-wise precision:


**Formula:** Salary Arrears = (New Monthly Salary – Old Monthly Salary) x Number of Months of Arrears


However, in practice, arrears must be calculated component by component (basic salary, HRA, DA, special allowance, etc.) because each component has different statutory implications. For instance, PF is calculated on basic + DA, while HRA has separate tax exemption rules.


### **Worked Example: Component-Wise Arrears Calculation**


Let us consider an employee whose salary is revised from Rs 50,000 to Rs 55,000 per month, effective April 2025, but processed in July 2025. The arrears cover 3 months (April, May, June).


**Salary Component** **Old Salary (Rs)** **New Salary (Rs)** **Difference (Rs)** **Arrears 3 Months (Rs)**


Basic Salary 20,000 22,000 2,000 6,000


HRA 10,000 11,000 1,000 3,000


Dearness Allowance 5,000 5,500 500 1,500


Special Allowance 10,000 11,000 1,000 3,000


Conveyance 3,000 3,200 200 600


Medical Allowance 2,000 2,300 300 900


**Total (Gross)** **50,000** **55,000** **5,000** **15,000**


**Total Arrears Payable: Rs 15,000** (before statutory deductions). This amount will be added to the July 2025 salary, and PF, ESI, professional tax, and TDS will be recalculated on the revised figures.


## **Step-by-Step Arrears Processing in Payroll**


Processing salary arrears accurately requires a systematic approach. Here is the recommended 7-step process that payroll teams should follow:


**1. Identify the Effective Date of Revision** Obtain the official approval letter or HR notification that specifies the date from which the salary revision takes effect. This date determines how many months of arrears need to be calculated. Verify that the revision is approved by the competent authority (board resolution for senior management, HR head for others).


**2. Calculate Component-Wise Differences** Break down the salary into individual components (basic, DA, HRA, special allowance, etc.) and calculate the difference for each component separately. This is essential because different components attract different statutory deductions. For example, PF is computed on basic + DA only, not on the entire gross salary.


**3. Recompute Statutory Deductions on Revised Gross** Recalculate PF (employee and employer), ESI (if applicable), and professional tax for each arrears month based on the revised gross salary. The differential amounts must be deposited with the respective authorities.


**4. Calculate Differential PF and ESI Contributions** Determine the additional PF contribution (both employee share at 12% and employer share at 12% of revised basic + DA) and the additional ESI contribution (employee at 0.75% and employer at 3.25% of revised gross, if applicable) for each month.


**5. Apply TDS on Arrears** Calculate income tax on the arrears amount. Arrears are taxable in the year of receipt. The employer must deduct TDS based on the employee’s estimated total income for the year, including the arrears. If the employee is eligible for Section 89(1) relief (old regime only), the employer can consider this while computing TDS.


**6. Process Arrears in Current Month Payroll** Add the net arrears (after all statutory deductions) to the current month’s salary. Ensure that the payroll software or system clearly segregates regular salary and arrears in the payslip for transparency and audit purposes.


**7. Issue Revised Payslips and Reports** Generate revised payslips for the arrears months showing the updated salary structure. Update Form 16 workings, and if arrears cross quarter boundaries, prepare revised quarterly TDS returns (Form 24Q). Maintain a detailed audit trail of the entire revision and arrears processing.

## **Tax Treatment of Salary Arrears**


The taxation of salary arrears is one of the most critical aspects for both employers and employees. Understanding the rules correctly can save employees significant tax liability.


### **When Are Arrears Taxed?**


**Salary arrears are taxable in the financial year in which they are received, not the financial year to which they relate.** This is the receipt basis of taxation under the Income Tax Act, 1961. For example, if arrears relating to FY 2024-25 are paid in FY 2025-26, they are taxable in FY 2025-26.


### **Impact on Tax Bracket**


Since arrears are a lump-sum payment added to the employee’s regular income in the month of receipt, they can push the employee into a higher tax bracket. For instance, an employee earning Rs 9 lakh per year who receives Rs 1.5 lakh as arrears would have a total income of Rs 10.5 lakh, potentially moving from the 10% slab to the 15% slab under the new tax regime.


This is precisely why Section 89(1) relief was introduced under the old tax regime, to ensure that employees are not disadvantaged by the timing of when they receive income that was due to them earlier.


### **TDS on Arrears**


Employers are required to deduct TDS on arrears at the time of payment. The TDS rate is based on the employee’s projected total income for the financial year (including arrears). Employers should:


- Calculate the employee’s estimated annual income including arrears
- Apply the applicable income tax slab rates
- Deduct TDS proportionally from the arrears payment
- Consider Section 89(1) relief while computing TDS (if the employee opts for old regime and provides Form 10E details)


## **Section 89(1) Relief: Eligibility, Calculation & Form 10E**


Section 89(1) of the Income Tax Act provides relief to employees who receive salary arrears or advance salary, ensuring they do not pay higher taxes merely because of the timing of the payment. This section is a crucial safeguard for employees receiving large arrears.


### **Eligibility for Section 89(1) Relief**


To claim relief under Section 89(1), the following conditions must be met:


- The employee must have received salary in arrears or in advance in the current financial year
- The arrears must relate to one or more previous financial years
- The employee must be filing returns under the old tax regime (Section 89(1) relief is NOT available under the new tax regime, i.e., Section 115BAC)
- Form 10E must be filed online on the Income Tax e-filing portal BEFORE filing the Income Tax Return (ITR)


**Important:** Filing Form 10E is mandatory. If an employee claims Section 89(1) relief in their ITR without filing Form 10E first, the relief WILL be disallowed by the Income Tax Department during processing, and the employee will receive a demand notice for the differential tax.


### **How to File Form 10E**


Form 10E must be filed online through the Income Tax e-filing portal (incometax.gov.in). Here are the steps:


**1. Log in** to the Income Tax e-filing portal with your PAN and password


**2. Navigate** to e-File > Income Tax Forms > File Income Tax Forms


**3. Search** for Form 10E and select it


**4. Select** the relevant Assessment Year


**5. Fill in** details of arrears received, the year(s) to which they relate, and the tax computation


**6. Verify and submit** using digital signature or EVC/Aadhaar OTP


**Note:** Form 10E must be filed BEFORE submitting the ITR for the relevant assessment year. Once the ITR is filed, you cannot file Form 10E retrospectively.


## **Worked Example: Section 89(1) Relief Calculation**


Let us walk through a complete 7-step example to calculate Section 89(1) relief. This example uses simplified figures for clarity.


**Scenario:** Mr. Rajesh Kumar received salary arrears of Rs 1,80,000 in FY 2025-26, relating to FY 2024-25. His total income in FY 2025-26 (including arrears) is Rs 12,00,000. His total income in FY 2024-25 (without the arrears) was Rs 8,50,000. He is filing under the old tax regime.


**Step** **Description** **Income** **Tax**


**Step 1** Tax on total income INCLUDING arrears (FY 2025-26) Rs 12,00,000 Rs 1,72,500


**Step 2** Tax on total income EXCLUDING arrears (FY 2025-26) Rs 10,20,000 (12,00,000 – 1,80,000) Rs 1,27,500


**Step 3** Relief Step A = Step 1 – Step 2 Rs 45,000


**Step 4** Tax on FY 2024-25 income + arrears Rs 10,30,000 (8,50,000 + 1,80,000) Rs 1,30,000


**Step 5** Tax on FY 2024-25 income without arrears Rs 8,50,000 Rs 82,500


**Step 6** Relief Step B = Step 4 – Step 5 Rs 47,500


**Step 7** Section 89(1) Relief = Step A – Step B (if positive, else nil) Nil (negative)


In this example, Step A (Rs 45,000) minus Step B (Rs 47,500) gives a **negative result** . When the result is negative or zero, no Section 89(1) relief is available. This means Mr. Rajesh would actually benefit more from the arrears being taxed in FY 2025-26 itself. Relief under Section 89(1) applies only when the result is positive, i.e., when taxing the arrears in the current year causes a higher tax burden compared to taxing them in the year they relate to.


Let us modify the example to show a case where relief IS available:


**Modified Scenario:** If Mr. Rajesh’s FY 2025-26 income (including arrears) was Rs 15,00,000 and FY 2024-25 income was Rs 6,50,000, the relief calculation would yield a positive difference because the arrears push him into a significantly higher slab in the current year (30%) compared to the year they relate to (20%). In such cases, the positive difference is claimed as relief, reducing the current year’s tax liability.


## **Impact on Statutory Contributions (PF, ESI, PT)**


Salary arrears have a cascading effect on all statutory contributions. HR teams must recalculate and deposit the differential amounts accurately to avoid penalties.


### **Provident Fund (PF)**


Arrears on basic salary and dearness allowance attract additional PF contributions. Both the employee’s share (12% of basic + DA) and the employer’s share (12% of basic + DA, split between EPF and EPS) must be recalculated for each arrears month.


- The differential PF must be deposited with EPFO by the 15th of the month following the month in which arrears are processed
- Late deposits attract interest at 12% per annum under Section 7Q of the EPF Act and damages under Section 14B
- If the revised basic + DA exceeds Rs 15,000 per month, PF contributions on the amount above Rs 15,000 are voluntary unless the employee was already contributing on a higher amount


### **Employees’ State Insurance (ESI)**


ESI has a wage ceiling of Rs 21,000 per month for coverage eligibility. Salary arrears can have significant implications:


- If the revised gross salary exceeds Rs 21,000 per month but the old salary was below this threshold, the employee exits ESI coverage from the revision effective date
- If the employee was already above Rs 21,000, ESI does not apply to the arrears
- Contributions already deducted cannot be refunded for the covered period; coverage continues until the end of the contribution period


### **Professional Tax**


Professional tax slabs vary by state. Salary arrears may push the employee into a higher professional tax slab. HR must recalculate professional tax for each arrears month based on the revised gross salary and deposit any differential with the state authority.


### **Gratuity Implications**


Gratuity is calculated on the last drawn basic salary + DA. While arrears do not directly affect gratuity calculations (since gratuity is computed at the time of separation), the revised basic + DA will become the basis for future gratuity liability. HR teams should update their gratuity provision calculations accordingly.


## **Arrears Under New vs Old Tax Regime**


The choice of tax regime has a significant impact on how salary arrears are taxed. Here is a detailed comparison:


**Parameter** **Old Tax Regime** **New Tax Regime (115BAC)**


**Arrears Taxability** Taxable in year of receipt Taxable in year of receipt


**Section 89(1) Relief** Available NOT Available


**Form 10E Filing** Required for claiming relief Not applicable


**Tax Slab Rates** Higher rates but more deductions Lower rates, fewer deductions


**HRA Exemption on Arrears** Available (Section 10(13A)) NOT Available


**Standard Deduction** Rs 50,000 Rs 75,000 (from FY 2024-25)


**Recommended For** Employees with large arrears spanning multiple years Employees with small arrears within same FY


**Key Takeaway:** Under the new tax regime (Section 115BAC), there is no relief mechanism for salary arrears. Arrears are simply added to the total income and taxed at the applicable slab rates. Employees expecting large arrears should evaluate whether switching to the old regime for that financial year would result in overall tax savings, considering all deductions and exemptions.


### **Labour Codes 2025: Impact on Arrears**


The new Labour Codes (expected to be fully implemented by 2025-26) mandate that basic salary must be at least 50% of the Cost to Company (CTC). When calculating salary arrears, HR teams must ensure that the revised salary structure maintains this 50% ratio. Non-compliance can attract penalties under the Code on Wages, 2019.


This means that if an employer is revising salaries, the revised structure must allocate at least 50% to basic salary. Any arrears calculation must reflect this compliant structure, which will also increase PF and gratuity liabilities since both are computed on basic + DA.


## **HR Best Practices for Processing Arrears**


Efficient arrears processing protects both the organisation and its employees. Here are proven best practices that every HR and payroll team should follow:


- **Process Within the Same Financial Year:** Whenever possible, finalise and process salary arrears within the same financial year as the revision effective date. This eliminates cross-year tax complications, avoids the need for revised TDS returns, and simplifies Section 89(1) calculations for employees.
- **Use Payroll Software for Automated Calculations:** Manual arrears processing is error-prone, especially when dealing with component-wise calculations, differential PF/ESI, and TDS recomputation. Payroll software like SalaryBox automates the entire process, calculates component-wise arrears, recomputes statutory deductions, and generates compliant payslips automatically.
- **Maintain a Complete Audit Trail:** Document every step of the arrears process: the revision approval letter, component-wise calculation worksheets, differential statutory computations, and final payslip details. This audit trail is essential for internal audits, statutory inspections, and resolving employee queries.
- **Communicate Proactively With Employees:** Inform employees in advance about the expected arrears timeline, the gross arrears amount, applicable deductions, and the net amount they will receive. Clear communication reduces grievances and builds trust.
- **Re-File Revised TDS Returns If Needed:** If arrears cross quarter boundaries (for example, arrears for Q1 processed in Q2), the employer must file revised quarterly TDS returns (Form 24Q) to reflect the updated salary and TDS figures for each quarter.
- **Verify ESI Threshold Crossings:** Before processing arrears, check whether any employee’s revised gross salary crosses the Rs 21,000 ESI threshold. If it does, ensure proper transition documentation and update ESI records accordingly.
- **Reconcile PF Deposits Monthly:** After processing arrears, reconcile PF deposits with the EPFO portal to ensure all differential contributions are reflected accurately. Any discrepancies should be resolved before the month-end filing deadline.
- **Keep Payslips Transparent:** Ensure that payslips clearly separate regular salary from arrears. Each component of arrears should be itemised so that employees can verify the calculation and use the information for tax filing.
