### **What Is a Payslip? Definition & Legal Requirement**


A payslip is a document issued by an employer to an employee at the end of every pay period that provides a detailed breakdown of salary earned and deductions made during that period. Also referred to as a salary slip or wage slip, the payslip serves as an official record of compensation and is one of the most important financial documents for both employers and employees in India.


Under Section 50 of the Code on Wages, 2019, which came into force on 21 November 2025, every employer in India is legally required to issue a wage slip to every employee before the expiry of the pay period. This applies regardless of whether the employee is on the company payroll full-time, part-time, or on a contractual basis. Failure to issue a payslip is a punishable offence under the Code, with penalties that can include fines and, in cases of repeated non-compliance, imprisonment.


A payslip typically contains four sections: company details, employee details, a list of all earnings (basic salary, allowances, bonuses), and a list of all deductions (PF, ESI, professional tax, TDS). The difference between total earnings and total deductions gives the net pay or take-home salary that the employee actually receives in their bank account.


**Key Point:** Digital payslips issued via email, HRMS portal, or mobile app (such as SalaryBox) are fully legal and accepted under the Code on Wages 2019 and the Information Technology Act. No physical signature or paper copy is required as long as the payslip is issued through an authenticated system.


**Why Payslips Matter: Legal & Practical Importance**


Payslips are far more than just a monthly formality. They serve critical legal, financial, and administrative functions for both employers and employees. Here is why issuing accurate payslips should be a non-negotiable part of your payroll process.


**1. Legal Compliance Under the Code on Wages 2019**


As mentioned, the Code on Wages 2019 mandates payslip issuance for every pay period. Non-compliance can attract a fine of up to Rs 50,000 for the first offence and up to Rs 1,00,000 along with imprisonment of up to 3 months for subsequent offences. For businesses of any size, issuing payslips is no longer optional.


**2. Proof of Income for Financial Transactions**


Payslips serve as proof of income and are required for a wide range of financial transactions. Banks require the last 3 to 6 months of payslips for home loan, personal loan, and car loan applications. Landlords often ask for payslips before finalizing rental agreements. Embassies require payslips as part of visa application documentation to verify employment and income stability.


**3. Income Tax Filing and TDS Verification**


Employees need payslip details to file their Income Tax Returns (ITR) accurately. Payslips show the exact TDS deducted each month, allowances claimed, and PF contributions, all of which feed into tax computation. Without payslips, employees may file incorrect returns and face scrutiny from the Income Tax Department.


**4. Dispute Resolution and Legal Evidence**


In the event of a salary dispute, wrongful termination claim, or labour court case, payslips serve as primary evidence. They establish the agreed compensation structure, actual payments made, and deductions applied. Courts and labour tribunals give significant weightage to payslip records.


**5. Employee Trust and Transparency**


Issuing detailed payslips builds trust between employer and employee. When employees can see exactly how their salary is calculated, what deductions are being made and why, and what employer contributions (like PF and ESI) are being deposited on their behalf, it creates a culture of transparency. This directly impacts employee satisfaction and retention.


**6. PF and ESI Compliance Verification**


Employees can cross-verify their PF (Provident Fund) and ESI (Employee State Insurance) deductions shown on the payslip against their EPFO and ESIC portal records. This ensures the employer is actually depositing the statutory contributions and not just deducting them from the salary.


**Payslip Format: 4 Essential Sections Every Payslip Must Have**


A standard payslip format in India contains four essential sections. Whether you are creating a payslip manually in Excel or generating it through payroll software like SalaryBox, every payslip must include these sections to be compliant and useful.


**Section 1: Company / Employer Details (Header)**


The header section of a payslip contains all identifying information about the employer. This section establishes the authenticity and source of the payslip document.


• Company name (as registered)


• Company logo (optional but recommended for branding)


• Registered address of the company


• CIN (Corporate Identification Number) for companies registered under MCA


• PAN and TAN of the company (for TDS verification)


• Pay period (e.g., 01 July 2026 to 31 July 2026)


• Pay date (the date salary was credited)


• Payslip number or reference ID (optional but useful for record-keeping)


**Section 2: Employee Details**


This section contains all relevant information about the employee for whom the payslip is generated. It also includes attendance data that directly impacts salary calculation.


• Employee name (as per company records)


• Employee ID or Employee Code


• Designation and Department


• Date of Joining (DOJ)


• PAN number of the employee


• UAN (Universal Account Number) for PF tracking


• ESI IP number (if applicable, for employees with gross salary up to Rs 21,000)


• Bank name and account number (last 4 digits masked for security)


• Total working days in the month


• Days worked / Present days


• LOP (Loss of Pay) days, if any


• Paid leaves availed during the month


**Section 3: Earnings (Left Column of the Payslip)**


The earnings section lists every component of the employee gross salary. These are the amounts the employee has earned before any deductions are applied. We cover each earnings component in detail in the next section.


**Section 4: Deductions (Right Column of the Payslip)**


The deductions section shows all amounts subtracted from the gross salary. These include statutory deductions mandated by law and any voluntary or company-specific deductions. The detailed breakdown of each deduction is covered below.


The final line of the payslip shows the Net Pay (Take-Home Salary), calculated as:


**Net Pay = Gross Salary − Total Deductions**


**Earnings Components on a Payslip: Complete Breakdown**


Understanding each earnings component is essential for both employers designing salary structures and employees verifying their payslips. Here is a detailed breakdown of every common earnings component found on Indian payslips.


**Component** **Description** **Typical % of CTC**


**Basic Salary** Foundation of the salary structure. All statutory calculations (PF, gratuity, HRA) are based on basic salary. Must be at least 50% of CTC under the new Labour Codes. 40-50%


**Dearness Allowance (DA)** Cost-of-living adjustment linked to the Consumer Price Index. More common in government and PSU sectors. In private sector, often merged with basic. Variable


**House Rent Allowance (HRA)** Provided for rental accommodation expenses. Tax-exempt under Section 10(13A) of old regime: 50% of basic in metro cities, 40% in non-metro cities. 20-25%


**Conveyance Allowance** Fixed monthly allowance for daily commute between home and office. Rs 1,600/month


**Medical Allowance** Allowance for health-related expenses of the employee. Rs 1,250/month


**Special Allowance** Balancing figure to bridge the gap between CTC and other fixed components. Fully taxable. Variable


**Leave Travel Allowance (LTA)** For travel expenses within India. Tax-exempt under Section 10(5) of old regime for 2 journeys in a block of 4 years. Variable


**Performance Bonus** Monthly or quarterly incentive linked to individual or team performance targets. Variable


**Overtime Pay** Compensation for hours worked beyond normal schedule. Must be paid at 2x the normal hourly rate under the new Labour Codes. As applicable


**Other Allowances** Includes telephone allowance, internet reimbursement, education allowance, meal allowance, etc. Variable


**Gross Salary** **Sum of all earnings components listed above** **100%**


**Deductions Components on a Payslip: What Gets Subtracted**


Deductions are the amounts subtracted from your gross salary before the net pay reaches your bank account. Some deductions are mandatory under Indian labour law, while others may be company-specific or voluntary.


**Component** **Description** **Rate / Amount**


**Employee PF (EPF)** Employee contribution to the Provident Fund under the EPF Act. Deposited in the employee PF account. 12% of basic (capped at Rs 15,000 basic = Rs 1,800/month)


**Employee ESI** Employee contribution to the Employee State Insurance scheme. Applicable only if gross salary is Rs 21,000 or below per month. 0.75% of gross salary


**Professional Tax (PT)** State-level tax on employment/profession. Varies by state. Not all states levy professional tax. Rs 200/month max (varies by state)


**TDS (Income Tax)** Tax Deducted at Source based on projected annual income and applicable tax slab. New regime is the default since FY 2023-24. As per income tax slab


**Loan Recovery** EMI deduction if the employee has availed a company loan or salary advance. As per EMI schedule


**Other Deductions** Includes advance recovery, canteen charges, group insurance premium, union membership fee, etc. As applicable


**Total Deductions** **Sum of all deductions listed above** **Subtracted from gross**


**Employer Contributions: What Your Company Pays on Your Behalf**


Employer contributions are amounts paid by the employer on behalf of the employee. These are part of the CTC (Cost to Company) but are not deducted from the employee salary. They are shown separately on the payslip for transparency.


**Component** **Description** **Rate / Amount**


**Employer PF Contribution** 12% of basic salary (3.67% to EPF + 8.33% to EPS, capped at Rs 15,000 basic) Rs 1,800/month (at cap)


**Employer ESI Contribution** 3.25% of gross salary. Applicable only when gross is Rs 21,000 or below per month. 3.25% of gross


**Gratuity Provision** Employer sets aside 4.81% of basic salary (15/26 days per completed year of service). 4.81% of basic


**Labour Welfare Fund (LWF)** State-specific contribution towards worker welfare. Typically a small fixed amount. Rs 20-60 per half-year


**Important:** Employer contributions reduce your in-hand salary because they are part of CTC but not part of gross salary. For example, if your CTC is Rs 6,00,000 per annum, your gross salary will be CTC minus employer PF, employer ESI, and gratuity provision. This is why net take-home is always lower than what you might expect from the CTC figure.

**How to Make a Payslip: 10-Step Process**


Creating accurate payslips requires a systematic process. Whether you are making payslips manually in Excel or using payroll software, follow these 10 steps to ensure compliance and accuracy.


**1. Gather Employee Master Data.** Collect all employee master data including full name, employee ID, PAN number, UAN, bank account details, date of joining, and designation. This information forms the foundation of every payslip and must be verified before the first payslip is generated.


**2. Define the Salary Structure.** Based on the offer letter and company policy, define the salary breakdown into basic salary (at least 50% of CTC under new Labour Codes), HRA, conveyance allowance, medical allowance, special allowance, and any other applicable components.


**3. Record Attendance Data.** Document the total working days in the month, the number of days the employee was present, any Loss of Pay (LOP) days, and paid leaves availed. This data directly impacts the gross salary calculation.


**4. Calculate Gross Salary.** If the employee has LOP days, prorate each salary component proportionally. For example, if there are 30 working days and 2 LOP days, each component is multiplied by 28/30.


**5. Calculate Statutory Deductions.** Employee PF at 12% of basic (capped at Rs 15,000 basic), ESI at 0.75% of gross (if applicable), and Professional Tax as per state rules. These are non-negotiable and must be deducted correctly.


**6. Calculate TDS (Income Tax).** Project the employee annual income, apply the applicable tax slab (new regime is default), subtract any eligible deductions or exemptions, and divide the annual tax liability by 12 (or remaining months) to arrive at monthly TDS.


**7. Add Other Deductions.** If the employee has taken a company loan, salary advance, or has any other agreed deductions (group insurance premium, canteen charges), add these to the deduction list.


**8. Compute Net Pay.** Subtract total deductions from gross salary. This is the amount that will be credited to the employee bank account.


**9. Generate the Payslip Document.** Compile all four sections (company details, employee details, earnings, deductions) into a structured payslip document. Ensure all mandatory fields are populated and the calculations are accurate.


**10. Distribute to Employees.** Send the payslip to the employee via email, HRMS portal, or payroll app before the end of the pay period. SalaryBox automatically generates and distributes payslips to employees via the mobile app, saving hours of manual work every month.

**7 Common Payslip Mistakes Indian Employers Must Avoid**


Even well-intentioned employers make payslip errors that can lead to legal trouble, employee dissatisfaction, or compliance penalties. Here are the seven most common payslip mistakes and how to avoid them.


**1. Not Showing PF UAN on the Payslip**


The UAN (Universal Account Number) is the employee lifelong PF identifier. Without it on the payslip, employees cannot track their PF balance on the EPFO portal or verify that contributions are being deposited. Always include the UAN in the employee details section of the payslip.


**2. Wrong Basic Salary Split Violating the 50% Rule**


Under the new Labour Codes, basic salary must be at least 50% of CTC (excluding employer PF and gratuity). Many employers still keep basic at 30-40% to reduce PF liability, but this is now non-compliant. Review and restructure salary breakdowns to ensure basic meets the 50% threshold.


**3. Not Prorating Salary for LOP Days**


When an employee has Loss of Pay days, every component of salary must be prorated proportionally. Some employers only reduce the basic salary or special allowance for LOP, which is incorrect. Each component should be calculated as: Component Amount x (Days Worked / Total Working Days).


**4. Missing Professional Tax Deduction**


Professional tax is mandatory in most Indian states but is frequently missed, especially by employers with employees across multiple states. Each employee professional tax should be deducted based on their work location state, not the company registered state.


**5. Not Updating TDS After Investment Proof Submission**


Many employers calculate TDS based on estimated declarations at the start of the year but fail to adjust it when employees submit actual investment proofs (typically in January-February). This leads to higher TDS deduction throughout the year and last-minute bulk refunds, causing employee frustration.


**6. Showing Employer PF as an Employee Deduction**


Employer PF contribution (12% of basic) is paid by the employer and should be shown in a separate Employer Contributions section, not under employee deductions. Mixing these up inflates the deductions shown to the employee and creates confusion about actual take-home pay.


**7. Not Issuing Payslips At All**


Surprisingly common among small businesses, not issuing payslips is a direct violation of the Code on Wages 2019. Even if you pay salaries on time via bank transfer, you are legally required to provide a documented payslip for every pay period. The penalty for non-compliance can be up to Rs 50,000 for the first offence.


**Digital Payslip: Legal Validity & Advantages**


A digital payslip is a payslip generated and distributed electronically, typically via email, HRMS portal, or a mobile payroll application. With the increasing digitisation of payroll processes in India, digital payslips have become the standard for modern businesses.


**Legal Validity of Digital Payslips**


Digital payslips are fully legal and valid under Indian law. The Code on Wages 2019 does not mandate a specific format or medium for payslip issuance. Electronic payslips issued through authenticated systems (official company email, HRMS portal with employee login, or authorised payroll apps) satisfy the legal requirement. The Information Technology Act, 2000, further supports the validity of electronic records.


**Record Retention Requirements**


Under the Code on Wages 2019, employers must maintain payslip records for a minimum of 3 years from the date of the last entry. However, it is recommended to maintain records for at least 7 years for income tax and audit purposes. Digital payroll systems make long-term record retention effortless.

• Instant delivery: Employees receive payslips immediately after payroll processing

• Cost-effective: No printing, paper, or distribution costs


• Environmentally friendly: Reduces paper waste significantly


• Easy compliance: Digital trail makes audit and inspection easier


• Searchable: Employees can quickly find and download specific month payslips
