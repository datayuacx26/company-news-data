What if someone told you there is a single step you could add to your hiring process that takes ten minutes, costs less than Rs 500, and could save your business anywhere from Rs 3 lakh to Rs 50 lakh?


You would probably ask what the catch is.


There is no catch. The step is digital identity verification. And the reason most Indian businesses do not do it is not that it is expensive or time consuming. It is that they do not know it exists in this form.


When most business owners hear “employee verification” or “background check,” they picture a slow, expensive process involving a third party agency, stacks of paperwork, weeks of waiting, and a bill of Rs 3,000 to Rs 8,000 per candidate. That picture was accurate ten years ago. It is completely outdated today.


Digital identity verification platforms now let you check a candidate’s PAN, Voter ID, Driving Licence, and UAN against actual government databases in real time. No agency middleman. No waiting period. No bulk commitments. You enter the details, the platform queries the database, and you get results before the candidate has finished their second cup of chai in your reception area.


This article explains exactly what happens in those ten minutes, why the results matter more than most employers realise, and how this single step prevents the kind of costly hiring disasters that Indian businesses deal with every year.


## What Actually Happens in Those 10 Minutes


Let us walk through the process from start to finish, so you can see exactly how simple it is.


You sit down with a candidate who has cleared your interview. You have decided you want to hire them. Before you type up the offer letter, you open ID Verify by SalaryBox on your browser. No app to install, no software to configure.


You enter the candidate’s name, date of birth, and phone number. Then you enter the document numbers they have provided. Their PAN number. Their Voter ID number. Their Driving Licence number, if the role involves any driving. Their UAN, if they claim previous formal employment.


You select the checks you want to run and confirm. The platform sends each document number to the respective government database. PAN goes to the Income Tax Department. Voter ID goes to the Election Commission. DL goes to the Transport Department. UAN goes to EPFO.


Within minutes, results start coming back. For each document, you see whether the record exists, whether the name and date of birth match what the candidate provided, and the current status of the document. Is the PAN active or deactivated? Is the DL valid or suspended? Does the UAN show PF contributions from the employers the candidate mentioned?


Then comes the part that makes digital verification genuinely powerful. The platform compares identity details across all returned records. If the PAN says the candidate was born on March 15, 1995, and the Voter ID says March 15, 1995, and the DL says the same, you have strong multi source confirmation of their identity. If the PAN says March 15 but the Voter ID says November 22, you have a problem that deserves a conversation.


You review the results, download a PDF report, and save it to the candidate’s file. The entire process, from entering the first detail to downloading the report, takes about ten minutes.


That is it. Ten minutes. And in those ten minutes, you have accomplished something that no amount of interview skill, gut instinct, or personal recommendation can achieve. You have independently confirmed, through government databases, that this person is who they say they are.


## Why Those 10 Minutes Matter More Than the Entire Interview


This might sound like an exaggeration, but think about what an interview actually tells you versus what verification tells you.


An interview tells you how well someone communicates. It tells you whether they can articulate their experience convincingly. It tells you whether they are personable, confident, and culturally compatible with your team. These are all valuable things to know.


But here is what an interview cannot tell you. It cannot tell you whether the candidate’s name is actually their name. It cannot tell you whether they really worked at the companies they mentioned. It cannot tell you whether their Driving Licence has been suspended. It cannot tell you whether the PAN card photocopy they handed to your HR is real or printed from a template downloaded off the internet.


A skilled liar can pass any interview. A fabricated resume can sound completely convincing when presented by someone who has rehearsed their story. Only 19 percent of hiring managers say they feel confident in their ability to detect a fraudulent applicant during an interview. The other 81 percent are relying on luck.


Verification does not rely on the candidate’s presentation. It goes straight to the source. When the Income Tax Department confirms a PAN number, no amount of acting can override that result. When EPFO shows that a candidate’s PAN was never linked to PF contributions from the companies they claim to have worked at, that is an objective fact, not a judgement call.


This is why those ten minutes of verification are worth more than an hour of interviewing when it comes to protecting your business from fraud.


## The Real Numbers Behind Bad Hires in India


Let us put real numbers to the problem, because abstract risk does not motivate action the way concrete figures do.


According to industry estimates, a single bad hire costs an Indian business between Rs 5 lakh and Rs 50 lakh. That range is wide because it depends on the role, the duration before discovery, and the type of damage caused. But let us use a conservative middle ground scenario.


You hire a store supervisor at Rs 25,000 per month. They were recommended by a friend, interviewed well, and showed you their documents. You did not verify digitally. Four months later, you discover they have been skimming Rs 8,000 to Rs 10,000 from daily cash collections. By the time you catch it, the total cash loss is around Rs 35,000 to Rs 40,000.


But that is just the direct theft. Add the cost of hiring and training them in the first place, which is easily Rs 30,000 to Rs 50,000 when you include the job posting, interview time, onboarding, and the learning curve during which they were less productive. Add the cost of hiring their replacement, which restarts the entire cycle. Add the legal fees if you pursue the matter. Add the hit to team morale when other employees learn what happened.


The total cost of that one unverified hire lands somewhere between Rs 1.5 lakh and Rs 3 lakh. For a small business doing Rs 50 lakh in annual revenue, that is 3 to 6 percent of the entire year’s revenue, gone because of a step that would have taken ten minutes and cost Rs 500.


Now multiply that by the average incidence rate. If 30 to 35 percent of resumes contain discrepancies, and you hire 10 people a year without verification, statistically three of them have something wrong with their stated background. Not all of them will steal or cause problems, but the risk is there for every single one.


## Why Indian Business Owners Skip This Step


If verification is this fast and affordable, why do most Indian SMEs still not do it? The reasons are surprisingly simple, and they are all based on outdated assumptions.


The first assumption is that verification is expensive. This was true when the only option was a BGV agency charging Rs 3,000 to Rs 8,000 per candidate. Digital self serve platforms have reduced the cost to under Rs 500 for a comprehensive identity check across four government databases. Many business owners simply do not know this option exists.


The second assumption is that verification takes weeks. Traditional BGV involving court record searches, physical address visits, and manual employer contact does take 7 to 21 days. Digital identity verification takes minutes. The technology has evolved, but the perception has not caught up.


The third assumption is that “my business is too small for this.” Small businesses actually need verification more than large ones because each hire represents a larger fraction of the workforce. In a team of five, one bad hire is 20 percent of your people. The smaller the team, the more each person matters, and the more damage one wrong hire can do.


The fourth assumption is that interviews and references are sufficient. As the data shows, interviews test communication, not honesty. And references, whether professional or personal, are social endorsements, not identity checks. They tell you someone is likeable. They do not tell you someone is genuine.


## Industries Where This 10 Minute Step Has the Highest ROI


While every business benefits from verification, certain industries see an outsized return because of the nature of their workforce and risks.


Retail businesses deal with daily cash handling, high value inventory, and high employee turnover. Employee theft causes 35 to 40 percent of retail shrinkage in India. Verifying every retail hire creates accountability from day one and makes your store a harder target for serial offenders who cycle through unverified employers.


Restaurants and cloud kitchens face ingredient pilferage, cash skimming, and the constant pressure of fast hiring in a high turnover industry. Kitchen and service staff who know their identity is verified on government records behave differently from those who handed in a photocopy and were never checked.


Logistics and delivery companies put employees on the road with customer packages and cash on delivery payments. An unverified delivery executive with a suspended DL or a fake identity is a liability, both legally and financially. DL verification alone prevents accident related lawsuits that can cost lakhs.


Healthcare clinics and hospitals hire ward attendants, receptionists, and support staff who access patient records, medications, and sometimes cash. Identity verification ensures that the person handling sensitive patient information is traceable and accountable.


Professional services firms like accounting practices, legal offices, and consulting firms give employees access to client data, financial records, and privileged information. A single data leak from an unverified employee can destroy client trust and invite DPDP Act penalties.


## How to Make This Part of Your Hiring DNA


The key to getting value from verification is consistency. It should not be something you do occasionally or only for certain hires. It should be a standard step for every single person who joins your business, regardless of how they were sourced, what role they are filling, or how urgently you need them.


Here is a simple way to build it in. After the interview, before the offer letter, run the check. That is the moment. The candidate has cleared your assessment. You want to hire them. But before you commit, take ten minutes to confirm their identity. Make the offer conditional on successful verification, and mention this in the offer letter itself.


If you are doing bulk hiring, whether seasonal or for a new location, batch the verification. Collect identity details from all shortlisted candidates, run them through ID Verify in one session, and use the results to finalise the hiring list.


For contract and temporary workers, do not skip verification just because the engagement is short. Temporary employees often have the same access to cash, inventory, and customer data as permanent staff. A person who is going to work in your store for three months during the festive season needs verification just as much as a full time hire.


Over time, verification becomes as natural as checking references or conducting interviews. It stops being an extra step and becomes a standard part of how your business hires. And every time it catches a discrepancy, which it will, you will be grateful you built the habit.

## Frequently Asked Questions


### Is ten minutes realistic, or is that a marketing claim?


It is realistic. Digital identity verification queries government databases electronically. PAN and Voter ID results are near instant. DL and UAN checks may take slightly longer depending on database response times, but the entire four check process typically completes within a single sitting. The ten minute figure includes the time to enter candidate details, run checks, review results, and download the report.


### What if a check comes back with a discrepancy? Does that mean the candidate is fraudulent?


Not necessarily. Minor discrepancies, such as slightly different name spellings across government databases, are common and usually harmless. The key is the pattern. If PAN says “Rajesh Kumar” and Voter ID says “Rajesh Kumar Singh,” that is likely a clerical difference. If PAN says “Rajesh Kumar” and Voter ID says “Sunil Verma,” that is a serious red flag requiring immediate investigation.


### Can I verify a candidate who is from a different state?


Yes. PAN, Voter ID, DL, and UAN are all national databases. A candidate from Tamil Nadu applying for a job in Delhi can be verified just as quickly and accurately as a local candidate. Geography makes no difference in digital verification.


### What if the candidate is a fresher with no previous employment?


Freshers will not have a UAN since they have never had formal employment with PF contributions. Run PAN and Voter ID checks to confirm their identity. These two checks together provide strong two source verification. The absence of UAN is expected for freshers and is not a red flag.


### Do I need to inform the candidate that I am verifying their identity?


Yes. Under the DPDP Act 2023, you must obtain the candidate’s consent before processing their personal data, including identity verification. Use a simple consent form that explains which checks will be conducted. Legitimate candidates rarely object. Resistance to basic identity verification is itself informative.


### How does this compare to what large companies do?


Large companies typically run full background checks through BGV agencies, covering education, employment history, criminal records, and more. This costs Rs 3,000 to Rs 8,000 per candidate and takes weeks. Digital identity verification is the foundation layer of what large companies do, now available as a standalone product at a fraction of the cost. For most SME hires, it provides sufficient protection on its own.
