## The Scale of India’s Gig Economy and Why It Matters


Let us start with the numbers, because they paint an important picture.


India’s gig workforce grew from 7.7 million in 2020-21 to over 12 million by 2025, a 55 percent increase in just four years. The sector is growing at roughly 21 percent annually, and the government projects that gig workers will make up nearly 7 percent of the non agricultural workforce by 2030.


This is not just about food delivery and ride hailing anymore. The gig economy now spans dozens of categories: delivery partners (Swiggy, Zomato, Blinkit, Zepto), ride hailing drivers (Uber, Ola, Rapido), home service professionals (Urban Company, Housejoy), warehouse and logistics workers, freelance field sales agents, event staff and promotional workers, security personnel hired through apps, and healthcare workers on platform deployments.


Every single one of these categories involves workers who interact directly with customers, often in their homes or personal vehicles. The trust requirement is enormous, and yet the verification infrastructure has not kept pace with the growth.


## Why Gig Worker Verification Is Different from Traditional Employee Verification


Verifying gig workers presents unique challenges that traditional background verification processes were not designed to handle.


### Volume and Speed


A food delivery platform might onboard thousands of new delivery partners every week across dozens of cities. Traditional background verification that takes 7 to 15 days per candidate simply cannot keep up with this pace. Platforms need verification that happens in minutes, not weeks.


### High Turnover


Gig workers switch platforms frequently. A delivery partner who works for Swiggy today might move to Zomato next month and Blinkit the month after. Annual turnover rates in gig work can exceed 100 percent. This means verification is not a one time exercise. It needs to happen repeatedly as workers move between platforms.


### Informal Work Histories


Most gig workers do not have formal employment histories with relieving letters and experience certificates. Many come from informal sector jobs where no records were maintained. Traditional employment verification simply does not work for this population.


### Geographic Spread


Gig workers are spread across cities, towns, and increasingly, tier 2 and tier 3 locations. Running physical verification checks across such a wide geography is logistically challenging and expensive.


### Cost Sensitivity


Platform companies operate on thin margins and cannot spend Rs 2,000 to Rs 5,000 per worker on comprehensive background verification. The economics need to work at scale, which means verification costs need to be in the range of tens to hundreds of rupees per check, not thousands.


## The Real Risks of Not Verifying Gig Workers


The consequences of inadequate verification in the gig economy are not theoretical. They show up in police reports, news headlines, and customer complaint logs every single week.


### Safety Incidents


National Crime Records Bureau data for 2024-25 recorded over 700 road fatalities among delivery riders alone, a 30 percent increase since the pandemic. While many of these are accidents, the pressure to deliver quickly without adequate driver verification contributes to the problem.


A Centre for Internet and Society survey of 1,500 gig workers found that one in three fears theft or physical assault while working. This fear exists on both sides. Customers worry about unverified workers entering their homes, and workers worry about being sent to unsafe locations.


In Pune, a delivery partner scam case made national headlines when it was revealed that individuals were using fake identities to register on delivery platforms, completing a few legitimate deliveries, and then using the platform access to commit crimes.


### Brand Reputation Damage


For platform companies, a single safety incident involving an unverified worker can cause massive brand damage. When a customer is assaulted by a driver or delivery partner, the platform’s name is in every headline. Customer trust, which takes years to build, can evaporate overnight.


### Regulatory Consequences


The government is tightening regulations around gig worker verification. Starting February 2026, the Rajasthan High Court has made registration mandatory for gig workers on platforms like Ola, Uber, Swiggy, and Zomato. The labour ministry has set a June 2026 deadline for platforms to register their gig workforce on the eShram portal. New rules require gig workers to carry ID cards and wear uniforms with QR codes while on duty.


These regulations signal a clear direction: the era of unverified, anonymous gig work is ending. Platforms that do not build robust verification processes now will face compliance issues, potential fines, and operational disruption when enforcement begins.


### Liability and Insurance Issues


When an unverified gig worker causes harm, the question of liability becomes complicated. Courts and consumer forums are increasingly holding platforms responsible for incidents involving their workers, especially when it can be shown that adequate verification was not performed.


## What Background Checks Should You Run for Gig Workers?


Not every gig worker needs the same level of verification. The depth of checks should match the level of trust and access the role requires. Here is a practical framework.


### Tier 1: Basic Identity Verification (All Gig Workers)


Every gig worker, regardless of role, should go through basic identity verification. This is non negotiable.


**PAN Card Verification.** Confirms the worker’s name, date of birth, and PAN status against the Income Tax Department database. This catches fake PAN cards and identity mismatches immediately.


**Voter ID Verification.** Validates the worker’s identity and registered address through Election Commission records. This is particularly useful for workers who do not have a PAN card.


**Driving Licence Verification.** Essential for any role that involves driving, including delivery partners, cab drivers, and field sales agents. Confirms licence validity, vehicle class, and any endorsements or suspensions.


These checks can be completed in minutes through digital verification platforms. ID Verify by SalaryBox (verify.salarybox.in) runs all three checks against government databases and returns results almost instantly. The pay per check pricing makes it affordable even at high volumes.


### Tier 2: Enhanced Verification (Customer Facing Roles)


For workers who enter customer homes or have extended one on one interactions with customers, add these checks.


**UAN Verification.** Checks the worker’s Universal Account Number against EPFO records to verify their employment history. This reveals previous employers, employment duration, and PF contribution patterns. It is the fastest way to verify work history for workers who may not have formal experience certificates.


**Address Verification.** Confirms that the worker’s current address is legitimate. This can be done digitally through database checks or through physical verification for higher trust roles.


**Cross Record Analysis.** Compare details across multiple identity documents to check for inconsistencies. If the name on the PAN card does not match the name on the Voter ID, or if dates of birth differ, that is a red flag that needs investigation.


### Tier 3: Comprehensive Verification (High Trust Roles)


For workers who handle cash, high value goods, or work in sensitive environments, add criminal record checks and more thorough address verification.


**Criminal Record Search.** Check court records across relevant jurisdictions. This is the most time consuming check, but it is essential for roles where the worker has significant access to customer property or finances.


**Police Verification.** For roles like personal drivers, home service professionals, and security personnel, formal police verification provides an additional layer of assurance.


## How to Verify Gig Workers at Scale


The biggest challenge for platforms and businesses is not knowing what checks to run. It is running them quickly and affordably at scale. Here are practical strategies.


### Automate Identity Verification


The fastest way to verify gig workers at scale is to integrate digital identity verification into your onboarding flow. When a new worker signs up, they enter their PAN number, Voter ID, or Driving Licence details. The system automatically verifies these against government databases and flags any issues.


This eliminates manual document review, reduces onboarding time, and ensures consistent verification for every worker.


### Use UAN as a Work History Shortcut


Traditional employment verification requires calling previous employers and waiting for responses. For gig workers with informal work histories, this is often impossible.


UAN verification solves this problem. Any worker who has ever been employed in the formal sector and had PF deducted will have a UAN. Checking this number against EPFO records reveals their employment history instantly, without needing to contact any previous employer.


### Implement Periodic Re Verification


Background verification should not be a one time event. Implement periodic re verification to catch any changes in a worker’s status. A worker who was clean at onboarding could develop issues over time. Running identity checks annually, or when a worker returns after a gap, keeps your verification current.


### Batch Processing for Large Scale Onboarding


If you are onboarding dozens or hundreds of workers at once, use a platform that supports batch processing. Upload a list of document numbers and get verified results in bulk, rather than checking one worker at a time.


## The Regulatory Push Towards Mandatory Verification


The Indian government is moving decisively towards formalising the gig economy workforce. Here is what has changed and what is coming.


### eShram Registration


The labour ministry has set a June 2026 deadline for digital platforms to register all gig workers on the eShram portal. This creates a centralised database of gig workers with verified identities. Platforms that fail to comply face potential penalties and operational restrictions.


### State Level Regulations


Several states are implementing their own gig worker protection laws. Rajasthan has made registration mandatory for workers on platforms like Ola, Uber, Swiggy, and Zomato. Karnataka’s proposed gig worker bill includes provisions for worker verification and identification.


### Social Security Code 2020


The Code on Social Security, which came into effect in November 2025, requires platforms to provide social security benefits to gig workers. This effectively requires platforms to maintain verified records of their workforce, including identity details, work patterns, and payment history.


### Mandatory Identification


New regulations require gig workers to carry ID cards with QR codes while on duty. This means platforms need to verify identity before issuing these cards, creating a natural checkpoint for background verification.

For ride hailing and delivery platforms specifically, many cities require police verification for drivers. Regardless of legal requirements, verification is a business necessity for risk management.
