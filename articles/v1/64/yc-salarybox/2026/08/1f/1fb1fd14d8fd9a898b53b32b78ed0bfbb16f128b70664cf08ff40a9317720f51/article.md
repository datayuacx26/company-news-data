India recorded 1,87,702 crimes against children in 2024. That is 512 crimes against children every single day. In 97 percent of cases, the perpetrator was someone known to the child.


In Kerala alone, 72 teachers and non teaching school staff have faced charges under the Protection of Children from Sexual Offences (POCSO) Act since 2019. In August 2024, the Badlapur school case in Maharashtra shocked the nation when a school employee allegedly sexually abused two four year old girls in the school washroom. In Chhattisgarh, three government school teachers were booked for molesting girl students. In Uttar Pradesh, a teacher was arrested for physically burning a five year old child in class.


Every single one of these incidents happened inside a school. Inside a place where parents send their children believing they are safe.


India’s education system employs over one crore teachers and an even larger number of non teaching staff including administrative personnel, bus drivers, security guards, laboratory assistants, hostel wardens, peons, and cleaning staff. Every single one of these individuals has access to children. And in far too many schools, colleges, and coaching centres across India, most of them were hired without any background verification at all.


This guide is for school administrators, college management, coaching centre owners, and education sector HR teams. We will cover why background verification in education is not just a best practice but a moral and legal imperative, what checks you must run, and how to implement a verification system that protects your students and your institution.


## Why the Education Sector Has the Highest Stakes for Background Verification


Every industry benefits from background verification. But the education sector is different in one fundamental way: the people at risk are children.


### Children Cannot Protect Themselves


Adult employees in a corporate office can identify and report inappropriate behaviour. They can leave a dangerous situation. They can file complaints and seek legal recourse. Children, especially young children, often cannot do any of these things.


Young children may not understand that what is happening to them is wrong. They may be too scared to speak up. They may not have the vocabulary to describe their experience. They may fear punishment or disbelief. This vulnerability makes it absolutely essential that schools verify every adult who has access to children.


### Trust Is Implicit and Total


When a parent drops their child at school, the trust they place in the institution is total. They trust that the school has ensured every person on its premises is safe to be around children. They trust that the teachers are qualified. They trust that the bus driver is licensed and has no criminal history. They trust that the security guard at the gate is there to protect, not to harm.


This trust creates an enormous responsibility. When a school fails to verify its staff and an incident occurs, it is not just a failure of process. It is a betrayal of the most fundamental trust a parent places in any institution.


### Legal Liability Is Severe and Growing


The POCSO Act (Protection of Children from Sexual Offences Act, 2012) provides for strict penalties for sexual offences against children, including imprisonment of up to life. But the Act does not only target the perpetrator. Schools and institutions that fail to report offences or that enable them through negligent hiring can face legal consequences.


Additionally, multiple state governments have issued directives making background verification of school staff mandatory. In Karnataka, the government launched a specific process to verify the backgrounds of all school staff, using the Crime Criminal Information System (CCIS) to check for any criminal history. Staff with clean records receive a No Objection Certificate.


Schools that do not verify their staff are not just negligent. They are increasingly non compliant with government mandates.


## Who Needs to Be Verified in a School or Educational Institution?


The answer is simple: everyone who has access to students. This includes far more people than most school administrators initially think.


### Teachers


This is the most obvious category. Teachers spend hours each day with students, often in classrooms where they are the sole authority figure. Verify their identity, educational qualifications, professional certifications (B.Ed, D.El.Ed, etc.), and criminal records.


### Non Teaching Academic Staff


Lab assistants, library staff, art and music instructors, sports coaches, and special educators all interact directly with students. They often work with smaller groups or individual students, which can create isolated situations. Verify them with the same rigour as teachers.


### Administrative and Support Staff


Office administrators, accountants, receptionists, and other administrative personnel may not teach classes, but they are present on campus and have access to student records, contact information, and sometimes direct student interaction. Verify their identity and criminal records.


### Bus Drivers and Transport Staff


School bus drivers and attendants are responsible for children during transit, often the most vulnerable time in a school day. The child is away from both parents and teachers, in a vehicle with an adult whose background may never have been checked.


Verify their identity, Driving Licence (confirming they are licensed to drive the vehicle type), criminal records, and address. This is absolutely critical.


### Security Guards


Security personnel are stationed at school gates and patrol campuses. They are often hired through third party security agencies, and schools assume the agency has done the verification. This assumption is frequently wrong. Verify every security guard yourself, regardless of what the agency claims.


### Hostel Wardens and Caretakers


For boarding schools and colleges with hostels, wardens and caretakers have access to students during night hours when supervision is minimal. This is an extremely high trust role. Run comprehensive verification including criminal record checks.


### Cleaning and Maintenance Staff


Cleaning staff and maintenance workers access every area of the school, including washrooms, changing rooms, and classrooms. The Badlapur case involved a school sweeper. Do not overlook this category.


### Coaching Centre and Tuition Teachers


Coaching centres and private tuition classes often operate with minimal formal hiring processes. A coaching centre teacher might be alone with students in a small room for hours. This setting requires verification at least as rigorous as formal schools.


### Contractual and Temporary Staff


Substitute teachers, event support staff, guest lecturers, and any temporary personnel who will be on campus should be verified before they interact with students.


## What Background Checks to Run for Education Sector Staff


### Identity Verification (Every Staff Member)


Confirm that the person is who they claim to be. This is the baseline for every other check.


**PAN Card Verification.** Verify the PAN number against the Income Tax Department database. Confirms name and date of birth.


**Voter ID Verification.** Validate against the Election Commission database. Provides identity and registered address confirmation.


**Driving Licence Verification.** Essential for bus drivers and transport staff. Confirms licence validity, vehicle class authorization, and any suspensions.

### Criminal Record Check (Every Staff Member)


This is the single most critical check for the education sector. Every person who will have access to students should have a criminal record check before they start work.


Search court records across all jurisdictions where the candidate has lived and worked. Pay particular attention to any cases involving violence, sexual offences, offences against children, drug related offences, and any POCSO charges.


Do not skip this check for any role, including cleaning staff, security guards, and bus drivers. Many of the most serious incidents in Indian schools have involved non teaching staff.


### Education and Qualification Verification (Teachers and Academic Staff)


Verify that teachers and academic staff actually hold the qualifications they claim. Check their B.Ed, D.El.Ed, or other teaching certifications with the issuing university or board. Verify any subject specific degrees.


This matters not just for student safety but for educational quality. A teacher who does not actually have the qualifications they claim will deliver substandard education, which harms students in a different but very real way.


### Employment History Verification (All Staff)


Check previous employment to identify any patterns of concern. A teacher who has been terminated from three previous schools warrants further investigation, regardless of what their current references say.


**UAN Verification** through EPFO records provides instant, government sourced employment history. This shows previous employers and employment dates, revealing gaps and inconsistencies that the candidate may not have disclosed.

### Address Verification


Know where your staff actually lives. This is important for traceability and is increasingly required by school safety regulations.


## How to Build a Verification System for Your School or Institution


### Step 1: Create a Verification Policy


Document a clear policy that states every staff member (teaching and non teaching) must undergo background verification before starting work, the specific checks required for each role category, the process for handling red flags, periodic re verification requirements, and the consent and data protection procedures.


Get this policy approved by the school board or management committee and communicate it to all staff.


### Step 2: Collect Documents During the Hiring Process


Before making any offer, collect the candidate’s PAN card number, Voter ID number, Driving Licence number (for transport staff), educational certificates and registration numbers, UAN (if they have previous employment), and current residential address.


### Step 3: Run Digital Identity Verification Immediately


Before the candidate enters the campus, verify their identity documents through ID Verify by SalaryBox. This takes minutes and catches fake identities, document mismatches, and impersonation immediately.


### Step 4: Run Criminal Record Checks


Submit criminal record check requests for all jurisdictions where the candidate has lived. For schools in states where police verification is mandated, submit the police verification application simultaneously.


### Step 5: Verify Qualifications


Contact the universities and boards that issued the candidate’s teaching certifications and degrees. For NCTE recognized qualifications, verify through the issuing institution.


### Step 6: Run UAN Employment History Check


Verify the candidate’s UAN to get their complete employment history. Compare this with what they stated in their application. Gaps and discrepancies warrant investigation.


### Step 7: Make a Verification Based Decision


Only after identity verification and criminal record checks are completed should the candidate be allowed to start work. Other checks (education verification, address verification) can continue during the probation period but should be completed before confirmation.


### Step 8: Implement Periodic Re Verification


Background verification is not a one time exercise. Implement annual re verification, particularly criminal record checks, for all staff. Circumstances change, and someone who was clean at the time of hiring may develop issues later.


## Handling Third Party Staff: Agencies, Contractors, and Outsourced Personnel


Many schools outsource security, transport, cleaning, and sometimes even teaching to third party agencies. This creates a dangerous gap: the school assumes the agency verified the worker. The agency may or may not have done so.


Here is the rule: **if the person has access to your students, they are your responsibility to verify, regardless of who employs them.**


Add background verification clauses to all vendor and agency contracts. Require agencies to share verification reports for every worker deployed to your campus. Run your own identity checks as a second layer. Do not allow any agency worker on campus until their verification is complete.


## For Parents: What to Ask Your Child’s School About Verification


If you are a parent reading this, here are questions to ask your child’s school administration.


Does the school have a written background verification policy? Are criminal record checks conducted for all staff, including non teaching staff? How does the school verify staff hired through agencies? Is verification a one time process or is it done periodically? What is the school’s process for handling red flags in verification results?


If the school cannot answer these questions clearly, that should concern you. Schools that take child safety seriously have these answers ready.


## The CBSE and State Board Perspective


CBSE affiliated schools are expected to follow safety guidelines that include staff verification. Multiple circulars have emphasized the need for police verification of staff, especially in the wake of high profile incidents.


State boards have similar requirements, though enforcement varies. Karnataka has been particularly proactive, implementing a government led verification process for school staff using the Crime Criminal Information System.


Regardless of the specific board or regulatory requirement, the fundamental principle is the same: verify everyone who has access to children. The regulations are the floor, not the ceiling. Good schools exceed the minimum requirements.


## For Coaching Centres and Private Tuition: Verification Is Not Optional for You Either


Coaching centres and private tuition classes often fly under the regulatory radar. They may not be subject to the same formal requirements as CBSE or state board schools. But the risks are identical.


A coaching centre teacher who is alone with a student in a small room has even more unsupervised access than a school teacher in a classroom of thirty. The informal nature of coaching centres can make students even more vulnerable because there are fewer institutional safeguards.


If you run a coaching centre, verify every teacher’s identity, criminal record, and qualifications before they teach their first class. The process is the same as for schools, and the stakes are just as high.

## The Cost of Not Verifying Education Sector Staff


Let us be blunt about the consequences.


**Legal consequences.** Schools found to have hired staff without verification who subsequently harm students face lawsuits, POCSO proceedings, and potential closure orders.


**Reputational destruction.** A single child safety incident can destroy a school’s reputation permanently. Parents withdraw their children. Admissions drop. The school may never recover.


**Emotional and human cost.** Behind every statistic is a child who was harmed. The emotional trauma inflicted on a child by a trusted authority figure has consequences that last a lifetime. No amount of legal settlement or institutional reform can undo that damage.


**The cost of prevention is negligible by comparison.** Verifying a staff member’s identity and criminal record costs a few hundred rupees and takes a few minutes for the identity checks. This is the most important investment any educational institution can make.


## Frequently Asked Questions


### Is background verification mandatory for school staff in India?


Multiple state governments have issued directives making staff verification mandatory, particularly criminal record checks. CBSE has issued circulars emphasizing staff verification for affiliated schools. While there is no single national law that mandates verification for all educational institutions, the combination of state level directives, board requirements, and POCSO Act implications makes it effectively mandatory for any school that takes child safety seriously.


### Should we verify staff who have been working with us for years?


Yes. Many schools have long serving staff who were hired before verification became standard practice. Run retrospective verification on all existing staff, particularly criminal record checks. Explain to staff that this is a universal policy being implemented for everyone, not a targeted investigation. Most staff will cooperate willingly.


### What if a criminal record check reveals a minor offence from years ago?


Not all criminal records are disqualifying. A traffic violation from ten years ago is very different from a violence related offence. Evaluate each case based on the nature of the offence, how long ago it occurred, its relevance to the person’s role at the school, and whether there have been any subsequent incidents. Any offence involving violence, sexual misconduct, or harm to children should be treated as disqualifying regardless of when it occurred.


### How do we handle verification for guest lecturers and visiting faculty?


Any person who will interact with students on your campus should be verified. For guest lecturers who visit occasionally, run at minimum a PAN or Voter ID identity check. For visiting faculty who will be present regularly, run the full verification suite including criminal record checks. The check takes minutes and can be done while the guest waits.
