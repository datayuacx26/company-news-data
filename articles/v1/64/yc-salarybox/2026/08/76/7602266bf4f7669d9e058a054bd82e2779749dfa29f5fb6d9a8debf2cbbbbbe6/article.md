By the time the buyer discovers the fraud, the dealer has vanished. His office is empty. His phone number is disconnected. The company that hired him as a “senior property consultant” three months ago never verified his identity against any government database.


This is not a hypothetical story. Real estate fraud complaints in India surged 25 percent in 2025, with buyers losing an estimated Rs 15,000 crore in that single year. Mumbai accounted for 30 percent of cases, NCR for 25 percent, and Bengaluru for 15 percent. While RERA has brought significant regulation to the industry, it has not solved the fundamental problem of unverified individuals operating within property businesses.


India’s real estate sector is one of the largest employers in the country, contributing over 7 percent to GDP and projected to reach $1 trillion by 2030. Over 95,000 real estate agents have been registered under various state RERA portals. Behind these registered entities are hundreds of thousands of employees: agents, telecallers, site executives, cashiers, property managers, security guards, and maintenance staff. Most of them handle sensitive client information, access client properties, or manage significant amounts of money.


And yet, background verification in real estate remains almost non existent outside the top tier corporate developers. This guide explains why that needs to change and how property businesses of every size can implement effective verification.


## Why Real Estate Has Unique Verification Risks


The real estate industry creates verification challenges that are distinct from most other sectors.


### Employees Handle Very Large Sums of Money


No other industry routinely puts entry level employees in charge of transactions involving lakhs and crores of rupees. A junior property agent might collect booking amounts of Rs 5 to 50 lakh from clients. A cashier at a real estate office processes cheques and bank transfers worth crores. A site supervisor manages construction material purchases running into tens of lakhs every month.


In most other industries, this level of financial access would require extensive vetting. In real estate, especially in the mid market and affordable housing segments, these roles are filled with minimal verification.


### Agents and Brokers Access Client Homes


Property agents routinely enter client homes for site visits, property inspections, and handovers. They have keys to vacant properties being shown to prospective buyers. They know when properties are empty, what valuables are inside, and which owners travel frequently.


This level of access to private spaces is comparable to what domestic staff have, yet domestic workers are increasingly being verified while property agents are not.


### Client Data Exposure Is Enormous


A real estate CRM contains some of the most sensitive personal data imaginable. Client names, phone numbers, home addresses, Aadhaar numbers, PAN numbers, bank account details, property ownership records, and investment portfolios. An employee with access to this system has access to information that enables identity theft, financial fraud, and targeted burglary.


Under the DPDP Act 2023, every real estate company that collects this data is a Data Fiduciary with legal obligations to protect it. Hiring unverified employees who can access this data is a compliance failure.


### The Industry Has an Informal Workforce Culture


Despite RERA’s push toward professionalization, much of India’s real estate workforce still operates informally. Agents work on commission with loose contractual relationships. Telecallers are hired through staffing agencies with minimal documentation. Site executives move between developers without formal references. This informal culture creates gaps where unverified individuals can operate with significant authority and financial access.


## What Checks Real Estate Businesses Should Run


Different roles in real estate require different levels of verification. Here is a role based framework.


### Property Agents and Brokers


These are the front line employees who interact with clients, show properties, negotiate deals, and handle money.


**PAN Card Verification.** Confirms the agent’s identity and links them to their tax records. Essential for anyone handling financial transactions. Catches individuals using fake identities immediately.


**Voter ID Verification.** Validates identity and registered address through the Election Commission database. Provides address confirmation that is independent of the agent’s claimed address.


**Criminal Record Check.** Search court records for fraud, cheating, forgery, or property related offences. A real estate agent with a history of property fraud is an unacceptable risk to your business and your clients.

### Telecallers and Sales Support Staff


Telecallers have access to client databases containing phone numbers, addresses, property preferences, and budget information. They are also often the lowest paid and most transient employees in a real estate operation.


**PAN or Voter ID Verification.** At minimum, verify identity for every telecaller before granting them access to your CRM or client database. A telecaller with a fake identity who downloads your client list and sells it to competitors is a scenario that plays out more often than most real estate companies admit.


### Site Executives and Project Managers


Site executives manage construction progress, authorize material purchases, and supervise labour on development projects. They handle both physical assets and financial transactions.


**PAN Card Verification.** Identity confirmation linked to tax records.


**UAN Verification.** Check employment history through EPFO records. Verify that the site executive actually has the experience they claim. A person who fabricated their work history managing construction projects may lack the judgment to manage your site safely.


**Driving Licence Verification.** Essential if the role involves company vehicle use or site travel.


### Property Managers and Facility Staff


Property management companies employ staff who have ongoing access to residential and commercial properties. These include maintenance workers, cleaning staff, electricians, plumbers, and property managers themselves.


**Identity Verification (PAN or Voter ID).** Every person with property access needs verified identity.


**Criminal Record Check.** For property managers and any staff who access occupied or vacant properties unsupervised. A maintenance worker entering a flat while the owner is away is in a position of extraordinary trust.


**Address Verification.** Confirm the current residential address for all property management staff.


### Finance and Accounting Teams


These employees process payments, manage escrow accounts, handle client deposits, and prepare financial documentation.


**Comprehensive Verification.** PAN verification, criminal record check (especially for fraud and embezzlement), and employment history verification through UAN. Financial roles in real estate warrant the highest level of scrutiny because the amounts involved are massive.


## RERA Compliance and Background Verification


The Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, 2016 brought significant regulation to the Indian real estate market. While RERA does not explicitly mandate background verification of all employees, it creates an environment where verification is practically necessary.


### Agent Registration Under RERA


RERA requires all real estate agents to register with the state RERA authority. This registration involves submitting personal details and, in some states, passing a basic eligibility check. However, RERA registration applies to the agent (the business entity or individual), not to every employee of that agent.


A registered agent might employ twenty telecallers, five site executives, and three cashiers, none of whom go through any RERA registration. These unregistered employees still interact with clients, handle money, and access sensitive data.


Background verification fills this gap. It ensures that not just the registered agent but every person working under that agent’s registration is a verified, identified individual.


### Accountability Under RERA Complaints


When a buyer files a RERA complaint, the registered agent is held accountable. But if the fraudulent act was committed by an unverified employee who used a fake identity, the registered agent faces the legal consequences while the actual perpetrator disappears.


Verification creates a chain of accountability. Every employee is identified, traceable, and documented. If something goes wrong, you know exactly who was involved and how to find them.


### Client Trust and Competitive Advantage


In a market where 25,000+ RERA complaints have been resolved and Rs 4,500 crore refunded to buyers, trust is the scarcest resource. Real estate companies that can demonstrate thorough employee verification build stronger client relationships.


Some forward thinking developers and brokerages now mention their employee verification practices in client presentations, positioning it as a trust differentiator in a market plagued by fraud.

### Scenario 1: New Branch Office Opening


A real estate company opens a new branch and hires a local team of agents, telecallers, and a branch manager. The branch manager is given authority to collect booking amounts and sign agreements on behalf of the company.

### Scenario 2: Channel Partner Onboarding


Developers work with dozens of channel partners (independent brokers) who sell their projects. These channel partners bring their own sales teams who interact with the developer’s clients.


**Risk without verification.** Channel partner employees are essentially unknown individuals selling your project. If they defraud a buyer, the buyer’s first complaint is against the developer.

### Scenario 3: Property Management Company Hiring


A property management company needs to hire security guards, maintenance staff, and housekeeping workers for a residential complex. These workers will have access to common areas, building systems, and potentially individual apartments.


**Risk without verification.** Workers with fake identities gain access to residential buildings where families live. If theft or a safety incident occurs, the property management company faces both civil liability and criminal investigation.


**With verification.** Verify every worker’s identity before they are given building access. Maintain verification records as part of your security documentation.


### Scenario 4: Luxury Property Sales


High end real estate involves clients with significant wealth. These clients share financial details, investment plans, and family information with their agents. They invite agents into their current homes for property valuation visits.

**With verification.** Comprehensive verification of every agent who handles luxury clients. Criminal record checks, identity verification, and employment history review create a security baseline.


## The DPDP Act Connection


The Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 makes every real estate company that collects client data a Data Fiduciary. Data Fiduciaries have legal obligations including implementing reasonable security safeguards to protect personal data and ensuring that data processing is done only by authorized persons.


Hiring unverified employees who access client databases undermines both obligations. You cannot claim to have reasonable security safeguards if you do not even know the true identity of the people accessing the data. And you cannot ensure authorized processing if any of your employees are operating under false identities.


Background verification is the foundation of DPDP compliance for any data handling role in real estate.

## Frequently Asked Questions


### Does RERA require background verification of real estate employees?


RERA does not explicitly mandate employee background verification. However, RERA requires agent registration, transparency, and accountability. Background verification supports these requirements by ensuring that everyone working under a registered agent’s name is a verified, identified individual. Many state RERA authorities are moving toward stricter agent oversight, making verification increasingly important for compliance.

### Should we verify property agents who work on commission only?


Yes. Commission only agents have the same client access, financial handling ability, and data exposure as salaried employees. In fact, commission only agents may have higher risk profiles because their income depends entirely on closing deals, which can create pressure to cut corners. Verify their identity before giving them access to clients or properties.


### What checks are most important for real estate telecallers?


At minimum, PAN or Voter ID verification. Telecallers access client databases containing phone numbers, addresses, budgets, and property preferences. This data has commercial value and is protected under the DPDP Act. Verifying the identity of every person with database access is both a security measure and a compliance requirement.


### How do we handle verification for temporary or seasonal staff?


Real estate companies often hire additional staff during festive seasons or project launches. Verify every temporary hire through digital identity checks before they start. The speed of digital verification (minutes per check) makes it practical even for short term hires. A temporary employee who works for just two weeks has the same access to client data and properties as someone who has been there for two years.


### Can background verification help with DPDP Act compliance?


Yes. The DPDP Act requires Data Fiduciaries to implement reasonable security safeguards and ensure authorized data processing. Verifying the identity of every employee who accesses personal data is a foundational security measure. It demonstrates to regulators that you know who is handling client data and that every data handler is a verified, traceable individual.
