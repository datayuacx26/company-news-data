Somewhere in a fulfillment centre outside Bhiwandi right now, a contract worker is scanning packages, loading pallets, and moving inventory worth crores through a facility the size of multiple football fields. This worker was hired 48 hours ago during a peak season surge. The labour contractor said he was reliable. His Aadhaar photocopy is in a file somewhere. Nobody verified it against a database. Nobody checked if the name on the document actually belongs to the person wearing the warehouse vest.


This scene plays out thousands of times every day across India’s booming logistics sector. The industry employs over 22 million people and is growing at over 14 percent annually, fueled by e-commerce, quick commerce, and third party logistics. India’s e-commerce logistics market alone was valued at over $19 billion in 2025 and is projected to reach $103 billion by 2034.


But this explosive growth has exposed a critical vulnerability that costs the industry crores every year: inventory shrinkage caused by unverified workers.


Shrinkage, the gap between inventory records and actual stock, eats 2 to 5 percent of logistics margins. A significant portion of this shrinkage is internal, caused by pilferage and theft by workers who were never properly identified or verified. Organized cargo theft rings have been documented placing unvetted contract workers into fulfillment hubs during peak hiring spikes, specifically to exploit the chaos of high volume operations.


This guide is for logistics company owners, warehouse managers, supply chain heads, and HR teams in the logistics sector. We will cover why background verification is the most effective tool against shrinkage, what checks you need for different warehouse roles, and how to verify workers at the speed your operations demand.


## Why Logistics and Warehousing Has a Unique Verification Problem


The logistics industry faces verification challenges that most other sectors do not encounter.


### Extreme Hiring Velocity


During major peak periods like Diwali sales, Republic Day promotions, or end of season clearances, a single fulfillment centre may need to onboard 500 to 2,000 contract workers in less than 72 hours. Traditional background verification that takes a week per candidate is physically impossible at this speed.


This creates an impossible choice for warehouse managers: either slow down operations and miss delivery targets, or bring in unverified workers and accept the risk. Most choose the latter. And that is exactly when shrinkage spikes.


### High Turnover and Transient Workforce


Warehouse worker turnover in India can exceed 100 percent annually. Workers move between facilities, switch from one logistics provider to another, and alternate between warehouse work and other informal employment. Many work at multiple facilities simultaneously during peak seasons.


This transient workforce makes traditional verification approaches impractical. By the time a comprehensive background check returns results, the worker may have already moved on to another facility.


### Multiple Layers of Contractors


Most large logistics operations do not directly employ their warehouse workers. They use staffing agencies and labour contractors who recruit, deploy, and manage the workforce. This creates the same accountability gap seen in construction: the logistics company assumes the contractor verified the workers, the contractor assumes the sub contractor did it, and nobody actually verifies anyone.


### High Value, Portable Inventory


Warehouses are filled with products that are small, valuable, and easy to steal. Electronics, cosmetics, clothing, mobile accessories, and pharmaceutical products can be worth significant amounts per unit and are easy to conceal. Unlike raw materials in a factory, these are finished consumer goods with immediate resale value.


A warehouse worker processing 500 packages per shift has hundreds of opportunities to divert a product. Without proper identity verification, the worker is essentially anonymous, making theft a low risk proposition.


### Round the Clock Operations


Modern fulfillment centres operate 24 hours a day, 7 days a week. Night shifts typically have fewer supervisors and less management oversight than day shifts. This reduced supervision during night operations creates additional opportunities for unverified workers to exploit.


## The True Cost of Shrinkage in Indian Logistics


Let us quantify the problem.


If a warehouse processes Rs 50 crore worth of inventory annually and experiences 3 percent shrinkage, that is Rs 1.5 crore in losses every year from a single facility. A logistics company with ten warehouses could be losing Rs 15 crore annually to shrinkage across its network.


Not all shrinkage is caused by theft. Damaged goods, receiving errors, and administrative mistakes also contribute. But internal theft by workers is consistently identified as one of the top causes, and it is the one most directly addressed by background verification.


Beyond direct inventory losses, shrinkage creates several cascading costs. Insurance premiums increase as claims rise. Customer satisfaction drops when orders go missing. Brand reputation suffers when a logistics partner cannot guarantee delivery. Investigation costs accumulate as incident after incident requires security team involvement. And the cost of installing additional surveillance, security checkpoints, and loss prevention measures adds up quickly.


## What Background Checks Logistics Companies Should Run


The verification approach for logistics and warehousing needs to balance thoroughness with speed. Here is a practical framework designed for the realities of the industry.


### For All Warehouse Workers (Including Contract and Temporary Staff)


Every person who enters a warehouse floor should have their identity verified. No exceptions for peak season hires, no shortcuts for “the contractor’s people,” no skipping verification because the person is “only here for two weeks.”


**PAN Card Verification.** Verify the PAN number against the Income Tax Department database. Confirms the worker’s name and date of birth. This is the fastest way to confirm that the person’s identity document is genuine.


**Voter ID Verification.** Validate the EPIC number against the Election Commission database. Provides identity and registered address confirmation. Many warehouse workers hold Voter IDs even if they do not have PAN cards.

### For Drivers and Transport Staff


Anyone who operates vehicles, drives delivery trucks, or handles goods in transit needs additional verification.


**Driving Licence Verification.** Confirms licence validity, vehicle class authorization (critical for heavy goods vehicles), and any suspensions or endorsements. A driver operating a 16 wheel truck with an expired or fake licence is a liability and safety crisis waiting to happen.


### For Supervisors and Team Leads


Workers in supervisory roles have greater authority and often control inventory access points. Add deeper checks.


**UAN Verification.** Check against EPFO records to verify employment history. This reveals previous employers and employment dates, confirming whether the supervisor actually has the experience they claim. A supervisor who fabricated their work history may lack the judgment and skills needed to manage a warehouse team.


**Cross Record Analysis.** Compare details across multiple identity documents. ID Verify by SalaryBox does this automatically, flagging discrepancies in names, dates of birth, or other details across PAN, Voter ID, and Driving Licence records.


### For Warehouse Managers and Operations Heads


Senior roles with full facility access and financial authority warrant comprehensive verification.


**Criminal Record Check.** Search court records for any history of theft, fraud, or financial crimes. A warehouse manager with an undisclosed criminal background involving property crimes is an unacceptable risk.


**Address Verification.** Confirm current residential address through database checks or physical verification.

### Pre Deployment Verification by Contractors


Make digital identity verification a contractual requirement for all staffing agencies and labour contractors. Include a clause in your contractor agreements specifying that all workers must be verified through government database checks before deployment to your facility.


Provide your contractors with access to the verification platform. When a contractor recruits a new worker, they verify the identity before adding the person to their deployment roster. By the time the worker arrives at your warehouse, they are already verified.

During major sale events, you might onboard hundreds of workers in a single day. Use batch processing to handle the volume. Collect identity document numbers from all arriving workers, run verification in bulk, and sort workers into verified and pending categories. Deploy verified workers immediately and process pending cases as a priority.


### Biometric Integration


For warehouses with biometric access control, link the verification record to the biometric profile. This ensures that the person whose identity was verified is the same person who enters the facility each day. It also prevents a common trick where a verified worker shares their access credentials with an unverified friend.


## Addressing the Contractor Accountability Gap


The multi layered contractor structure in logistics creates one of the biggest verification gaps. Here is how to close it.


### Contractual Requirements


Add specific verification clauses to every contractor and staffing agency agreement. The clause should specify that all workers deployed to your facility must have PAN or Voter ID verified against government databases before deployment, the contractor must maintain verification records and make them available for audit, workers who cannot be verified must not be deployed, and the contractor is liable for any losses caused by workers they deployed without proper verification.


### Random Audits


Periodically audit your contractors’ verification records. Pull a random sample of deployed workers and check whether their verification records exist and are genuine. This keeps contractors honest and demonstrates that you are serious about enforcement.


### Direct Verification Layer


Even with contractor verification requirements, run your own identity checks at the gate level. This double verification creates a safety net. If the contractor’s process had gaps, your gate level check catches them.


## Industry Specific Scenarios


### E-Commerce Fulfillment Centres


E-commerce fulfillment centres process high volumes of consumer electronics, fashion, and personal care products. Workers handle individual items during picking, packing, and sorting. The opportunity for pilferage is highest during packing, when workers are handling open packages.


Verify every worker’s identity, and implement zone based access controls so workers can only enter the areas they are assigned to.


### Cold Storage and Pharmaceutical Warehousing


Cold storage facilities and pharmaceutical warehouses handle temperature sensitive and high value inventory. A single shipment of vaccines or specialty medicines can be worth crores. Workers in these facilities need enhanced verification including criminal record checks, because the potential financial impact of theft is enormous.


### Third Party Logistics (3PL) Providers


3PL companies manage inventory on behalf of their clients. When a client’s product goes missing from a 3PL warehouse, the 3PL company bears the financial and reputational liability. This makes verification not just a risk management tool but a competitive differentiator. 3PL companies that can demonstrate robust worker verification win more contracts and retain clients longer.


### Quick Commerce and Dark Stores


Quick commerce operations (Blinkit, Zepto, Swiggy Instamart) use small dark stores with minimal staff. Each worker in a dark store handles a larger proportion of the inventory compared to a mega warehouse. A single unverified worker in a dark store with 5 to 10 employees has significant access and minimal oversight. Verify every person.


### Last Mile Delivery Hubs


Last mile hubs are the final staging point before packages reach customers. Workers at these hubs sort and dispatch thousands of packages daily. Missing packages at the last mile stage are a major source of customer complaints and financial loss. Identity verification for every worker at these hubs is essential.


## Building a Shrinkage Prevention Strategy That Works


Background verification is not the only tool against shrinkage, but it is the foundation. Here is how it fits into a comprehensive shrinkage prevention strategy.


**Layer 1: Verify identity.** Every worker’s identity is confirmed through government database checks before they access the facility. This is the baseline.


**Layer 2: Control access.** Zone based access controls limit each worker to the areas they need for their role. Verified identity linked to biometric access ensures the right person enters the right zone.


**Layer 3: Monitor inventory.** Real time inventory tracking, CCTV coverage, and exception reporting catch discrepancies as they happen rather than during monthly audits.


**Layer 4: Investigate and act.** When discrepancies are detected, verified identity records make investigation faster and more effective. You know exactly who was in each zone at each time, and you have confirmed identity information for every person.


Without Layer 1 (verification), the other layers are significantly less effective. Camera footage of an unverified worker is almost useless because you do not know who you are looking at. Access logs for workers with fake IDs tell you nothing. Verification is what makes every other security measure actionable.
