# How Gatorade turned a dehydration problem into a 60-year science-backed legacy

In 1967, a deal was struck to begin producing and selling the drink across the United States. And as demand grew, the brand expanded beyond college athletics and became increasingly associated with sports at every level, from professional leagues to young competitors. The now-familiar bolt logo was first introduced in 1973, and by the 1980s Gatorade’s reach expanded to international markets.


In 1983, the company that produced Gatorade, Stokely-Van Camp, was acquired by the Quaker Oats Company. New flavors and product innovations followed, as did moments that cemented the brand’s place in pop culture — from celebrity spokespeople and major advertising to the tradition of the[Gatorade “dunk.”](https://www.pepsico.com/newsroom/stories/2026/how-the-gatorade-dunk-became-a-super-bowl-tradition)


The brand also committed itself to furthering sports science: In 1985, it established the Gatorade Exercise Physiology Lab, and then in 1988, it expanded and was renamed the[Gatorade Sports Science Institute (GSSI)](https://www.gssiweb.org/en) , bringing together experts dedicated to studying sports nutrition, hydration, and athletic performance. Since its inception, GSSI has continued its mission of helping people optimize their performance, reinforcing the scientific foundation that inspired the drink's creation two decades earlier.
