# Buy a Lightroom mobile premium subscription with Djamo


If you love editing your photos, you’ll love Lightroom.


Adobe Lightroom mobile for Android lets you organize your photos, sync them across devices, and plenty more.


You can create as many folders as you want to organize your photos, then sync them across devices (phone, tablet, computer, camera…) through a smooth, intuitive interface. On top of that, you can edit any photo with the built-in editing tools.


## How to pay for a premium subscription


The premium version of Lightroom unlocks some great features: Premium Presets, Healing tool, Selective Adjustments, Geometry tools, Raw file editing, and more.


Before paying, you’ll need to create a Lightroom account:


- Download the app on[Play Store](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.adobe.lrmobile) or[App Store](https://apps.apple.com/fr/app/adobe-lightroom-retouche-photo/id878783582)
- Open the app and follow the instructions to create your account


Premium subscriptions are paid through the Play Store or App Store. If your card is already added, you can pay right away. If not, you’ll need to add it.


To add your card on the Play Store, check out this[article](https://www.djamo.com/en-ci/learn/how-to-buy-an-android-app-on-the-google-play-store/) . For the App Store, here’s[how](https://www.djamo.com/en-ci/learn/how-to-make-payments-on-the-apple-store/) .


## The cool stuff you can do with the premium version


Lightroom’s premium version is way more complete than the free one and lets you do some incredible edits. A few examples:


- Presets let you transform your photo with different looks: portraits for various skin types, travel, cinematic, vintage, and more.


- Edit any part of a photo with your finger or stylus to apply precise improvements using Selective Adjustments. You can edit the sky or the subject of your photo, or simply remove an element.


- Easily fix and adjust your photo’s perspective with the Upright modes and geometry sliders in the Geometry panel. When you take a photo too close to a subject, or use certain types of lenses, perspective can get distorted and straight lines can look tilted, curved or leaning. For example, if you shoot a tall building from the ground, it can look like it’s leaning backwards.
