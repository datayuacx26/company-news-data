# Best Practices for Storing Camera Footage in the Cloud

Understanding how to store security camera footage is the first step toward picking the best option for your needs.


There are two primary ways to do this: either on local storage or in the cloud. Both options have their pros and cons, so you will need to weigh your personal security requirements against the cost of implementing the appropriate solution.


Relying on **local storage** gives you the most control over your data but requires a significant up-front cost to purchase the SD cards or hard drives necessary for storing a large volume of rolling video footage. If you run out of space or your storage needs grow, you’ll need to make additional investments to acquire and set up the drives, then migrate the data. And if your physical drives end up damaged or stolen, your data is permanently lost.


Meanwhile, a **cloud storage solution** requires a small monthly fee, but allows you to essentially “set it and forget it.” Cloud-powered cameras like the[Arlo Pro 6](https://us.arlo.com/products/arlo-pro-6) connect to your home network via Wi-Fi, automatically backing up footage with little to no manual intervention. The cloud server handles security and encryption for you, so your data remains private even if you lose your security cameras. Plus, if your storage needs grow, many security plans allow you to upgrade capacity for a small fee, without the need to swap out drives or migrate data.


A hybrid approach that stores your most valuable footage locally while pushing the rest to the cloud is also available, but implementation is usually more complex and costly than relying on either one solution or the other.
