# What Is Dear Passengers? The Viral Airline Chaos Game, Explained

If your feed is suddenly full of clips of ragdoll flight attendants, escaped crocodiles, and planes coming apart mid-air, you've met Dear Passengers - the co-op airline disaster game that's become one of the most talked-about upcoming indie releases. Here's everything we know.


## The pitch: crew the world's worst airline


Dear Passengers is a physics-based co-op game where you and your friends run an airline that should have been shut down years ago. In the developer's own words: your plane is falling apart, your cargo is illegal, and passenger safety barely makes the list.


## How the gameplay works


- One player takes the cockpit and flies through dynamic weather, turbulence, and air pockets.
- The rest of the crew works the cabin: serving meals, calming passengers, securing cargo, and repairing the plane as it breaks.
- Before takeoff you choose which passengers and cargo to accept - riskier manifests pay more, and crates can contain anything from contraband to live crocodiles.
- Everything runs on physics. The plane pitches and rolls under your feet, so every task is a balance challenge during an emergency.


## Who's making it


Dear Passengers is developed and published by FLEXUS, an indie studio based in Kyiv, Ukraine.


## Release date and platforms


Dear Passengers is targeting a 2026 release on Steam (PC), with reports pointing to a fall 2026 window. There's no demo and no announced price - the store page is wishlist-only for now. No console versions have been announced.


## Why it's going viral before launch


Dear Passengers slots into the 'friendslop' lineage - chaotic, physics-heavy co-op games built for group voice chat and clippable moments: Lethal Company, Content Warning, R.E.P.O., PEAK. Kotaku has already flagged it as a candidate for the genre's next viral obsession, and outlets have described it as Overcooked at 30,000 feet. The formula is proven; the setting is fresh.
