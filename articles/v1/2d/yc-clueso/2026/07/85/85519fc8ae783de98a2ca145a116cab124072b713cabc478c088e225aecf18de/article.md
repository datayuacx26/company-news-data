# Employee onboarding videos for frontline teams (with examples)

## Employee onboarding video examples that work for frontline roles


Search for employee onboarding video examples and you'll mostly get employer-brand films.


This Dunkin' Donuts video keeps turning up on those lists. An agency-produced piece posted in August 2013. It runs 3 minutes 12 seconds, it's genuinely warm, and it has never taught anyone how to work a register. It also can't be revised, because revising it means hiring the agency again.


The videos worth copying come from software vendors teaching the people who use their product on a shift.


### Example 1: Square


Square's 46-second walkthrough of processing a refund in Square for Retail shows the exact steps a retail worker has to take in the app, then stops. A new associate can watch that at the counter with a customer standing right there, which is the actual situation these get used in.


### Example 2: Toast POS


Toast POS' platform video covers payment handling for restaurant floor staff in 2 minutes 40 seconds with a short introduction from a representative who then dives into the exact steps the floor staff has to take to work with the platform.


### Example 3: Lightspeed


Lightspeed's how to make a sale in Lightspeed Retail does register basics in 85 seconds, and it has been doing that job since 2018 — which tells you how long a well-scoped task video stays useful.


### Example 4: Zendesk


For support teams, a walkthrough like this Zendesk escalations process video is the perfect way to navigate a technical task in the format. Though at just under five minutes it sits at the length where it might be helpful to break down complex actions into multiple videos.


### Example 5: Jobber


Field service is where the library idea shows up most clearly. The[Jobber Help Center video channel](https://www.youtube.com/@jobberhelp) is broken into playlists that follow a technician's workflow, narrated by Jobber's own support team and captioned in English and Spanish. On a crew where several techs read English as a second language, those captions do more work than any amount of production value.

None of these came out of a video team. They're screen recordings with a voiceover, and each one retires an explanation a manager would otherwise repeat out loud a few dozen times a year. What you're copying is the scope: one task per video, under three minutes, published next to the written steps.


## Where to start this week


Pick the one task your frontline managers explain most often — the return, the escalation, the job log — and record that first. Publish it with a matching step-by-step guide behind a single link, send it to your next new hire instead of scheduling a walkthrough, and watch whether they finish the task without asking. If they do, you've proven the model on the cheapest possible bet. Then do the next four, and stop re-teaching the same shift from scratch.
