# How to localize training and product videos without losing your mind

## Frequently asked questions


### How do you translate a video without re-recording it?


Keep the original screen recording as your visual source and replace only the language layer. Transcribe the audio, translate the script using your glossary, generate an AI voiceover in the target language, and update captions. You re-record nothing unless the product UI itself is localized and needs fresh screenshots.


### Is AI translation accurate enough for training videos?


For European languages —[English to Spanish](https://www.clueso.io/tools/translate-english-to-spanish) , for instance — it's usually strong enough that a reviewer polishes rather than rewrites. For languages like Japanese, Korean, and Arabic, treat the first pass as a draft and budget for human review. A glossary with per-language rules and examples closes most of the recurring gaps.


### Why does a translation glossary matter so much?


Without one, an automatic translator will translate your product and feature names, and different videos will render the same term differently. An account-wide glossary defines do-not-translate terms, preferred renderings, dialect rules, and pronunciation, so every translated video stays consistent and on-brand.


### How do you handle on-screen text and screenshots when translating?


If your product UI is localized, switch the product to the target language and re-capture the screen so the visuals match the voiceover. If the UI is English-only, keep the original screenshots and let translated captions and voiceover carry the steps. Pull any callouts or titles you add from the same glossary as the audio.


Pick one onboarding video, build the glossary for your top three languages, and translate that single video end to end this week. Once the glossary exists and the workflow is proven on one video, the fourth and fifth languages cost you a review pass, not another studio booking.
