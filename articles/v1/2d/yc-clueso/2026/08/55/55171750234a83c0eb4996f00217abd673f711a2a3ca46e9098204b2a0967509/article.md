# How to keep your training video library up to date with your product

## Auditing a video library to find what's outdated


Start the audit with a complete inventory of the training video library. For each video, record its owner, product area, intended audience, publication date, last review date, and location. Add usage data such as views and completion rate when your platform provides it. A shared spreadsheet or content database works well because reviewers can filter videos by product area or release.


Review each asset against five signals.


1.


**Compare the recorded interface with the current product.** Check navigation labels, button locations, screenshots, cursor paths, and the sequence of actions. You can often inspect thumbnails and key frames before watching the full video.


2.


**Search transcripts for changed features and terminology.** Product release notes provide a useful checklist for renamed features, removed options, and revised workflows. Transcript search can flag affected videos faster than manual viewing.


3.


**Check visual and spoken branding.** Look for old logos, colors, product names, taglines, and branded introduction sequences. A rebrand may affect the framing around a tutorial even when the product workflow remains accurate.


4.


**Use viewing data as a diagnostic signal.** A sharp drop in completion after a product release may indicate that viewers encounter instructions they cannot follow. Low completion alone does not prove that a video is stale, so compare the timing with product changes and viewer comments.


5.


**Connect support requests with specific videos.** Repeated tickets about a step covered in training can reveal missing or outdated guidance. When possible, ask support staff which articles or videos customers viewed before contacting them.


Place every reviewed video into one of three buckets:


-


**Keep** videos remain accurate and require no immediate work.


-


**Refresh** videos still teach a valid task but contain outdated screens, narration, captions, or branding


-


**Retire** videos cover removed features, duplicate newer material, or receive too little use to justify an update.


Prioritize refresh work by combining audience impact, degree of inaccuracy, and update effort. High-traffic onboarding videos and required employee training should come first because outdated steps affect many viewers. Videos linked to frequent support tickets also deserve early attention. Long-tail tutorials can wait unless they contain instructions that cause errors, security problems, or compliance risks.


Finish the video content audit by assigning an owner and target date to every flagged asset. Without ownership, a well-categorized inventory can still become another stale document.
