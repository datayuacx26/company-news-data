## Mainland China Overtakes Japan: Hong Kong Travel Data


*We tracked ~200,000 booking receipts by Hong Kong users across six major travel platforms 2019–2026, to tell us where Hong Kong travelers went.*


In the final quarter of 2023, two lines crossed in[Measurable AI](https://measurable.ai/) ‘s e-receipt panel: for the first time, Hong Kong travelers booked more mainland hotel trips than Japanese ones. And then, they never uncrossed. Three years on it isn’t close — of every 100 trips out of the city, the mainland now takes 45. Japan keeps 19. **This means nearly one in two trips by Hong Kong users in 2026Q2 end up in mainland hotels.**


Japan remains Hong Kongers’ second destination, and its share has been flat since the crossover — with one exception. In the summer of 2025, an “earthquake prophecy” went viral: a Japanese manga, *The Future I Saw* , predicted a catastrophic earthquake would strike Japan that July. Our receipts show it landed. Japan’s share fell from **28% to 19%** in a single quarter (the prophecy window ran April to July 5), while the mainland jumped from **34% to 41%** . The prophecy failed to produce an earthquake. It did produce a travel statistic.

Our data also captures several checkpoints that accelerates the northbound trips in Hong Kong, including the **Costco Shenzhen** open (where now 14% of Costco Shenzhen members are from Hong Kong according to Bloomberg), and the **Hong Kong cars go north policy** . Since then there has been no single new catalyst — the habit sustains itself.


## Where exactly is ‘north’? Mostly one province


Where exactly in “north” are Hong Kong users going? **Shenzhen, the sleepover next door is #1 every single year — ~46% of mainland trips, median stay one night.** The rest of the table is basically Guangdong: Zhuhai #2, and #3 Guangzhou. Although the majority choose the closest province, the beyond-Guangdong share is quietly rising (19% → 23%) over the last 3 years — users who started at the border towns and are traveling deeper.

## Staycation in Hong Kong is dying


Every boom across the border has a casualty at home. In Hong Kong, it’s the staycation — the harbour-view weekend that, for two sealed-border years, was the only trip in town.


Staycations predate the pandemic: 19% of trips in 2019, one in five. Then the border closed and they became the entire market — 89% at the 2021 peak. Nine trips in ten. For two and a half years the staycation wasn’t just a trend; it was *all* of Hong Kong travel (quarantine-length stays excluded). Today it’s 13% — one trip in eight, below where it started. And the luxury end died fastest: HK$2,500-plus-a-night bookings are down 83%, budget stays 46%. What’s left is the cheap resort weekend, not the harbour-view splurge.

## What does a night actually cost?


Based on 2026 data from Measurable AI’s proprietary transactional e-receipt data, **A Hong Kong staycation night costs around HK$811, while a mainland night costs around HK$653.** **Staying home costs 24% more per night than crossing the border** .


Look at other hot destinations, **Macao (HK$1,171) now runs 44% above a staycation** ; a Japan night is HK$1,187; Italy tops the table at HK$1,324. The gap is new. In the first half of 2023 a mainland night cost *more* than a Hong Kong one (≈HK$1,090 vs ≈HK$890). Three years of normalization cut the mainland price paid per night by ~40% — the weekend hop got cheaper as it got routine.

## What the Receipts say next


According to[Measurable AI](https://measurable.ai/) ‘s e-receipt data, mainland China now takes 45 of every 100 trips Hong Kong travelers make out of the city — up from 4 in early 2023, overtaking Japan for good in late 2023 and turning Guangdong into Hong Kong’s de facto holiday region, with Shenzhen the #1 destination every single year. The staycation era that once claimed nine trips in ten has ended below its pre-pandemic baseline, and the economics explain why it won’t reverse soon: a hotel night at home costs 24% more than one across the border. None of this came from surveys or checkpoint headcounts. It came from ~200,000+ real booking confirmations received by Hong Kong consumers across Agoda, Trip.com, Booking.com, Expedia, Klook — one slice of Measurable AI’s transactional e-receipt dataset, which tracks what consumers actually pay across food delivery, ride-hailing, e-commerce, streaming and travel. Hong Kong’s travel wallet is one question; the same panel answers thousands more — market share, pricing, retention, platform head-to-heads — in near real time.
