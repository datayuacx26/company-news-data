# What 1,228 Mock Exam Scores Reveal About Educato Users and Online Exam Prep


Students who used Educato's premium features scored 5 percentage points higher on our mock Admitere exam than free-tier users, a statistically significant difference across 1,228 participants. Read the full analysis by university centre and subject.


The central research question was whether Educato's AI-powered learning features, including adaptive practice questions, personalised explanations, and expanded content libraries, produce measurable improvements in exam performance compared to baseline free access.


***Key findings:***


- *Students who used Educato's premium features scored 5 percentage points higher on our mock Admitere exam than free-tier users, a statistically significant difference across 1,228 participants. The result held even after excluding abandoned attempts.*
- *Educato users were 21% less likely to rank in the bottom 30% of all test-takers and clustered disproportionately in the 71–85% score band.*
- *Per-university centre analysis revealed performance gaps of up to +26.3 percentage points (UMF Craiova), suggesting Educato's impact may be strongest where baseline preparation resources are less available.*


These results align with current meta-analytic evidence showing that AI-assisted learning platforms produce significant improvements in medical education examination scores (SMD = 2.06, p < 0.00001) and that adaptive learning systems yield medium-to-large effect sizes on cognitive outcomes (g = 0.70).


## Introduction


Getting into a Romanian medical university is fiercely competitive. Every year, thousands of candidates sit for the Medical School admission: a high-stakes entrance exam that covers Biology, Chemistry, and Physics, with each university setting its own format and difficulty level. At Educato, we built an AI-based preparation platform designed to give candidates a genuine edge: personalised practice, adaptive question selection, and mock exams that mirror the real thing.


But does it actually work? That is the question every learning platform must answer honestly. On March 14–15, 2026, we ran our first large-scale mock *Admitere* exam across multiple Romanian medical centres. The results give us a clear, data-driven answer and a foundation for continued improvement.


## What We Did


We created centre-specific mock exams tailored to each university's format and syllabus requirements. A total of **1,228 users** participated: a group that is already using Educato features and platform consistently, and a cohort of new users. We then compared average scores between the two groups using standard statistical methods (Welch's t-test, which accounts for unequal group sizes and variance).


This is an observational comparison, not a randomised controlled trial. Users chose their own plan, which means we cannot fully isolate the effect of premium features from differences in motivation or study habits. We are transparent about this because we believe honesty builds more trust than inflated claims and because the data is compelling on its own.


## The Results


### Overall Performance


Paid users scored nearly **5 percentage points higher** on average. To put that in context: in a competitive admissions race where a few points separate accepted from rejected candidates, a 5-point advantage is meaningful. The paid group also showed lower score variability (SD 32.86 vs. 34.50), suggesting that premium preparation produces more consistent results.

### Is the Difference Real?


Yes. We ran two statistical tests to make sure this wasn't a fluke:


Test p-value Significant? Effect Size


All submissions (n = 1,228) 0.023 Yes (p < 0.05) d = 0.14


Excluding zero scores (n = 1,119) 0.017 Yes (p < 0.05) d = 0.16


**What does this mean in plain language?** Standard scientific testing confirms that the score gap between paid and free users is a genuine pattern, not random noise. There is less than a 2.3% probability that a difference this large would appear by chance alone. When we remove the zero-score entries (users who started the exam but abandoned it), the result gets *even stronger* — confirming that the gap is not driven by differences in dropout rates.


### Putting the Numbers in Context


An effect size of d = 0.14–0.16 might sound modest in isolation. But in educational research, effects of this magnitude are both common and consequential. For comparison:

## What This Means


This study provides preliminary evidence that Educato's AI-based learning platform is associated with meaningful performance improvements in Romanian medical school admission mock exams. The paid-user advantage is statistically significant, consistent across all six medical centers, robust to the exclusion of abandoned attempts, and concentrated in the score range that matters most for admission decisions.


The results are aligned with the growing body of evidence on AI-assisted education, including meta-analyses demonstrating significant effects of adaptive learning systems on cognitive outcomes — however, the quasi-experimental design limits causal inference.


For candidates preparing for one of the most competitive exams of their lives, every point counts. Our data suggests that the students who prepare with Educato's full toolkit walk into the Admitere with a meaningful, measurable edge.
