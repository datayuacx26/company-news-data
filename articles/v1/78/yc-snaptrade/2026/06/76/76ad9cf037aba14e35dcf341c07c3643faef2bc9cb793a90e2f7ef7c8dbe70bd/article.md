# Best portfolio rebalancing tools (2026): A Canadian’s complete guide

# Highlights:


- The 5 best portfolio rebalancing tools available to Canadians in 2026
- How often you actually need to rebalance (it's less than you think)
- A simple framework to pick the right tool for you


If you’re a Canadian DIY investor, you’ve probably thought something like this: *“My portfolio has drifted again. Do I really have to pull out a spreadsheet, log into three different brokerage tabs, and figure out trades by hand? Isn’t there a better way?”*


There is.


Portfolio rebalancing tools have come a long way. In 2026, you don’t need to be a finance pro or live in Excel to keep your investments on track.


In this guide, you’ll see the best portfolio rebalancing tools available to Canadians right now, what each one is good at, and how to pick the one that actually fits your life.


## **What is a portfolio rebalancing tool, exactly?**


A rebalancing tool (or in some cases, a single fund) helps keep your asset mix on target. Some watch your accounts and tell you what to buy or sell. Others handle the rebalancing automatically inside the fund itself.


Why does it matter?


Because over time, your portfolio shifts. If your target is 70% stocks and 30% bonds and stocks have a great year, you might wake up holding 80/20 - which means you’re carrying more risk than you signed up for.


Even relatively small drift can change your portfolio’s risk profile over time, which is why disciplined investors set a plan and stick to it. ## **How we picked the tools on this list**


For Canadian investors specifically, the best rebalancing tool needs to:


- Connect to Canadian brokerages (not just U.S. ones)
- Handle registered accounts properly - RRSP, TFSA, RESP, FHSA
- Show your whole household portfolio in one view
- Keep fees low (or be free)
- Actually save you time, not add more steps


With that in mind, here are the tools worth knowing about in 2026, ordered roughly from simplest (least involvement) to most hands-on.


## **1. All-in-one ETFs (XEQT, VEQT, XGRO, VGRO) - best for “set it and forget it”**


Technically, an all-in-one ETF isn’t a *tool* in the software sense - it’s an investment that rebalances itself.


You buy one fund that holds Canadian, U.S., international, and emerging-market stocks (and sometimes bonds), and the ETF provider rebalances internally. You never touch it.


[Canadian Couch Potato](https://canadiancouchpotato.com/model-portfolios/) points out that “With this one-fund solution, you’ll never need to rebalance your portfolio.”


The trade-off: slightly higher MERs (typically around 0.18%–0.25%, depending on the ETF) than building your own portfolio, and limited flexibility to tilt your allocation.


**Best for:** people who are just getting started with DIY investing, or who want literally zero ongoing portfolio work.


## **2. Canadian robo-advisors - best if you want zero involvement**


Robo-advisors handle everything: investing your contributions, picking the ETFs, and rebalancing automatically. You just deposit money.


Popular robo-advisors are provided by[Wealthsimple](https://www.wealthsimple.com/en-ca/learn/what-is-robo-advisor) and[Questwealth](https://www.questrade.com/questwealth-portfolios/) .


Fees run around 0.20–0.50% per year on top of the underlying ETF MERs.


To give you an idea, a typical all-in cost would be something like 0.40% platform fee + ~0.20% weighted average MER = ~0.60% total.


The catch: you're trading control for convenience. You can't pick your own ETFs, and on a $200,000 portfolio, that ~0.60% all-in fee adds up to $1,200 per year - which compounds over decades.


**Best for:** investors who genuinely don’t want to make any decisions.

[Wealthica](https://www.wealthica.com/) is a portfolio tracker that connects to over 200 Canadian financial institutions. It won’t rebalance for you, but it gives you the consolidated view of your investments, savings, and crypto.


It pairs well with whichever rebalancing approach you choose: see the full picture in Wealthica, then make your trades wherever you actually invest.


**Best for:** people who want to *see* everything in one place before deciding what to do.


## **5. Spreadsheets - the old-school approach**


Yes, you can still rebalance with Excel or Google Sheets. MoneySense even publishes a[free rebalancing spreadsheet](https://www.moneysense.ca/save/investing/portfolio-rebalancing-tool/) .


But here’s the honest truth: spreadsheets break down as your portfolio grows. You have to manually update prices, log into each brokerage to check holdings, and recalculate every time you contribute. It works - until it doesn’t.


If you have one account and three holdings, a spreadsheet is fine. If you have an RRSP, a TFSA, an RESP, and your spouse has the same? It gets messy, fast.


**Best for:** very simple portfolios, or people who genuinely enjoy the math.

## **A word about timing: how often should you actually rebalance?**


A common question. The honest answer from[MoneySense’s research](https://www.moneysense.ca/save/investing/etfs/how-often-should-you-rebalance-your-portfolio/) : rebalancing matters, but the exact frequency matters far less than people think.


For most Canadian DIY investors, once a year is enough. Some prefer to rebalance only when an asset class is more than 5 percentage points off target. Both approaches work.


What *doesn’t* work: rebalancing every week, getting nervous, second-guessing, and trading too often. That’s how transaction costs and tax drag eat your returns.


## **What about Canadian tax considerations?**


Quick note for Canadians: rebalancing inside an RRSP or TFSA has zero tax consequences. You can sell and rebuy freely.


In a non-registered (taxable) account, selling can trigger capital gains.


The simplest workaround is to rebalance using new contributions - direct fresh cash to your underweight assets instead of selling overweight ones. This is sometimes called “rebalancing with cash flows” and Canadian Couch Potato calls it one of the most efficient strategies.
