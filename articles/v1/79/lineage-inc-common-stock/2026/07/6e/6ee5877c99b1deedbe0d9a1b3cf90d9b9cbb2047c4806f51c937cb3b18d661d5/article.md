A refrigerated shipment might spend a day on the road. But it can spend weeks being planned.


By the time the trailer pulls up to the dock, inventory has already been positioned, warehouse labor has been scheduled and transportation routes have been planned and probably adjusted more than once.


That's why experienced food companies don't think about refrigerated transportation as just a truck. They think about it as one part of a much larger cold chain.


At Lineage, refrigerated transportation connects with cold storage warehousing, transportation management and[global integrated logistics services](https://www.onelineage.com/services/global-integrated-solutions) to help customers keep their products moving, even as plans change.


## Successful refrigerated transportation solutions are delivered through smart decision-making up and down the cold supply chain


The trailer may be the most visible part of refrigerated transportation, but it's rarely where success or failure is decided in the supply chain.


A[refrigerated shipment](https://www.onelineage.com/news-stories/managed-transportation-spotlight) is often influenced by dozens of decisions before it ever reaches the loading dock. Where inventory is stored. Which facility it's shipped from. Whether it moves direct to the customer or through another distribution center. Every decision affects the cost, transit times and product quality.


That's why refrigerated transportation is about more than just maintaining temperature in transit. When done right—it 's about keeping the entire[refrigerated supply chain](https://www.onelineage.com/about-us/cold-chain) working together to deliver seamlessly to your customers.


Lineage supports refrigerated transportation through a[global network of warehousing](https://www.onelineage.com/services/global-warehousing) and integrated solutions, built specifically for temperature-sensitive food products. Transportation isn't managed separately from warehousing or logistics. It's planned as part of the bigger picture.


Many refrigerated transportation solutions to choose from for your temperature-sensitive products In the supply chain, there isn't a single "right" way to move food.


A frozen food manufacturer shipping across North America has different priorities than a regional dairy processor.[Fresh produce](https://www.onelineage.com/services/fresh-and-perishable-solutions) moves differently than frozen proteins.[Foodservice distributors](https://www.onelineage.com/services/foodservice-solutions) face different challenges than grocery retailers.


Depending on the operation, refrigerated transportation services may include:


- Refrigerated truck transportation for regional and long-haul freight
- Refrigerated freight services for full truckload and[LTL shipments](https://www.onelineage.com/services/less-than-truckload-shipping)
- [Temperature-controlled rail](https://www.onelineage.com/services/temperature-controlled-rail) for longer distances and larger volumes
- [Managed transportation](https://www.onelineage.com/services/managed-transportation) for coordinating multiple carriers and modes
- Refrigerated distribution supported by[strategically located cold storage](https://www.onelineage.com/services/cold-storage-warehousing)


The transportation mode matters, but so does knowing when to use it. That's where experience becomes valuable. Lineage helps customers build refrigerated transportation solutions around their products, customers and business goals instead of forcing every shipment into the same model.


## Leveraging refrigerated transportation means planning for change


Transportation plans rarely stay the same for long. Demand shifts, customers adjust orders, production schedules move, weather creates delays, and capacity tightens. When working in the supply chain, it’s wise to expect the unexpected.


The real question isn't whether those things will happen. It's whether your transportation partner can adapt when they do.


When evaluating refrigerated transportation providers, food companies often look for:


- Experience with temperature-sensitive products
- Flexible refrigerated capacity
- Visibility across shipments and inventory
- Connections to warehousing and refrigerated distribution
- Multiple transportation options as needs evolve
- Logistics expertise beyond moving freight


Those capabilities become even more valuable as supply chains grow more complex. Transportation is easier to manage when it's connected to people who understand the rest of the operation.


## Connected refrigerated logistics create better outcomes


Transportation decisions don't happen in a vacuum. A warehouse that's closer to customers can shorten transit times. Inventory that's positioned in the right market can reduce transportation costs. International shipments may require drayage, freight forwarding and customs brokerage before they ever hit the road.


Looking at each service independently only solves part of the challenge, and it doesn’t leave much room for quick adjustments and flexibility.


Looking at them together creates new opportunities to reduce handoffs, improve visibility and respond faster when conditions change.


That's why Lineage connects refrigerated transportation with cold storage, transportation management and integrated logistics services. Customers gain access to a network designed to support their entire refrigerated supply chain instead of a single shipment.


## Refrigerated transportation works best when it’s operating in sync with the other links of the cold supply chain


Refrigerated transportation in the food supply chain has always been about protecting food and getting it to where it needs to be. But today, it's also about building flexibility.


Whether companies are moving frozen foods across the country, replenishing regional distribution centers or expanding into new markets, transportation performs best when it's connected to the rest of the operation.


That's the role Lineage plays—bringing together refrigerated transportation, warehousing and logistics expertise into one integrated network to help customers build stronger, more connected cold chains.
