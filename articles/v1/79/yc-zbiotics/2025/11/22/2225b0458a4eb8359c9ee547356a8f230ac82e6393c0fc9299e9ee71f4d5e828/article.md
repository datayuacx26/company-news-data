# ZBioneer Brides Share Their Best Wedding Tips

Weddings are all about joy, connection, and celebrating the people you love most—values that hit close to home for all of us at ZBiotics. This year, two of our very own ZBioneers tied the knot, and between floral choices, family input, and nerves of the big day, they both found ways to make their weddings uniquely theirs. And, of course, worked[ZBiotics Pre-Alcohol](https://zbiotics.com/products/zbiotics?variant=26921652977764) into the mix to make sure their guests enjoyed[the next day](https://zbiotics.com/blogs/journal/tips-for-a-great-next-day-after-drinking-alcohol) just as much as the big day.


We sat down with both brides, Anna (AO) and Bentley (BMS), to talk about what worked, what didn’t, and how they stayed grounded through it all.


## Wedding planning and preparation tips


**1. Looking back, what’s one thing you’re really glad you did during wedding planning?**


**AO:** Looking back, I’m so glad we stuck with vendors I connected with. I never had to prove my business was right for them—they genuinely embraced my vision. For example, my photographer didn’t push for the whole “no sunset photos” thing since I’m not a big photo girl. Everyone I surrounded myself with at the wedding was kind, supportive, and truly wanted the best for me and Taylor.


**BMS:** I’m really glad my partner and I planned everything together because it made the whole process feel like a true team effort. We each made a list of our top priorities. That way, if churros were really important to me and music was really important to him, we made sure we both got what mattered most and let go of things that weren’t as meaningful to either of us. Also, I surprised my partner with my processional song (Song choice: Teenage Dirtbag (Violin and Piano Cover) by Charlotte Kennedy), and it was so much fun to plan. He told me afterward that the moment he heard his favorite song start playing, he thought, “Wow, okay, I’m loved”. It was such a special little moment that made the day even more meaningful.


**2. Was there anything you wish you had done differently (or sooner) in your planning process?**


**AO:** I wish we had poured beers for people coming into the cocktail hour or had more grab-and-go beers to lessen the initial line at the bar.


**BMS:** The trouble with being crafty is that whenever I see something cute, I immediately think, “Ooh, I could make that.” Luckily, I started a lot of my projects early, but definitely had to cut some craft ideas! The only thing we wished we had done differently was to tell the DJ specific songs we wanted him to play. We gave him a big playlist and assumed he’d pull from it, but he mostly just used it for “vibes.” We heard a bunch of songs we didn’t care about and missed out on some of our favorites.


**3. How did you balance your own vision with input from family and friends?**


**AO:** Luckily, my mom and I shared a similar vision. I chose my battles and let her have certain wins. As much as it’s about you and your partner, you don’t want to hurt feelings over something you won’t notice on the day. When setting boundaries with family and friends, lead with empathy.


**BMS:** While it was fun to share our ideas with friends and family, we really had a vision that we wanted to bring to life. We waited until we could pay for our own wedding. It was nice to be able to call the shots, but even better knowing that everyone we love could just sit back, relax, and enjoy the day. I keep joking with my partner that I wish I could have been invited to this wedding too!


## Wedding tips for enjoying the big day


**4. What was the most memorable moment of your wedding day?**


**AO:** Dancing my butt off during every single song.


**BMS:** The last song, hands down. We’re so glad we picked something big and fun to end the night with (Dog Days Are Over by Florence + The Machine). The people still on the dance floor at that point were the friends and family who just wanted to celebrate every last moment with us. We were surrounded by so much love, soaking up every second before the night came to an end.


**5. Did anything unexpected happen—and how did you handle it?**


**AO:** Honestly, it probably did, but I was too happy to notice. It was seriously the best day of my life.


**BMS:** I think I was probably shielded from most of the unexpected stuff (kudos to all the party planners of the world!). That said, someone did spill a little champagne on me—thankfully, it mostly ended up on my chair and not my dress. My sister, who was my maid of honor, immediately came to my rescue. She was the real MVP.


**6. How did you and your partner stay grounded and present amid all the excitement?**


**AO:** I went on a run with my best friend at 8am to start the day off. We ran my family’s farm (where the wedding was) and that kept me present. I also didn’t check my phone the whole day!


**BMS:** We kept looking at each other throughout the night and saying, “Be present!” We kept checking in with each other and taking little moments to soak it all in and be proud of all the work we’d put into the day. The night really does fly by!


## Guest experience tips: how to host with heart


**7. What did your guests seem to enjoy the most?**


**AO:** I heard guests thought it was incredibly personal to Taylor and me (for example, each table was named after one of our favorite restaurants, with a note on the menu explaining why). They also loved our band (shoutout West Coast Music!) and how fun the other guests were—no one left the dance floor. The avocado and slider bar during cocktail hour and the cake bar for dessert were also big hits!


**BMS:** The dessert! I’m obsessed with churros and my partner can’t go a day without bubble tea, so we thought, what better way to share our favorite treats with our favorite people? It was such a hit and a perfect reflection of us.


**8. Was there anything you did to make your guests feel especially welcomed or comfortable?**


**AO:** We wrote everyone at the rehearsal dinner a handwritten card, hung pictures from their weddings at our reception, and brought up all the women for a group dance to “Man I Feel Like A Woman.” It brought the house down!


**BMS:** We invited everyone to our rehearsal dinner, not just the bridal party. Since we had recently moved, most of our friends and family had to travel to be there, so it felt important to create more opportunities to spend time together. One little touch I loved was that we provided blankets for guests in case they got chilly as the night went on since our wedding was entirely outside.


**9. If you could give one piece of advice to a bride about hosting, what would it be?**


**AO:** Don’t think of the wedding as just your day—it’s also about your guests and thanking them for traveling to celebrate you. Make it easy for them to have a seamless experience. An open bar and transportation are money well-spent!


**BMS:** Yes, your wedding is for you, but it's also for everyone else coming.


## Pre-wedding self-care and nerves: staying grounded


**10. How did you take care of yourself in the days leading up to the wedding?**


**AO:** I invested in massages, facials, and sushi.


**BMS:** I made sure not to miss my therapy sessions that final week. Your mental health can really take a hit during wedding planning!


**11. Did you have any pre-wedding jitters, and how did you calm them?**


**AO:** I was so ready and excited because my bridal party made it feel like we were just getting ready for another fun night out. I also didn’t start taking photos until right before getting on the bus to go to the ceremony because I don’t love photos.


**BMS:** Keep your team close. I made sure to have my best friends and family nearby for moral support. I don’t love being the center of attention, so the thought of walking slowly in high heels in front of 100 people without tripping honestly terrified me. Luckily, I had a small bridal party made up of the most supportive people, and just having them around kept me grounded and laughing instead of stressing.


**12. What’s one small detail you did for yourself (not for guests) that made a big difference?**


**AO:** I ate a burrito at 2am and a bag of Takis the night before my wedding. I didn’t change into a second look but did change into cowboy boots to be more comfortable for dancing.


**BMS:** The iconic post-wedding trip to In-N-Out. I definitely didn’t get to eat as much as I wanted during the wedding, so those fries really hit the spot after a long day. It was the perfect low-key way to end the night.


## Personal touches and advice: making the day uniquely yours


**13. Was there a tradition you loved—or one you skipped—that made your wedding feel more “you”?**


**AO:** We did a 30-minute dance set with the band before sitting for dinner. We love to dance, and this brought the energy early in the night! We also cut the cake before the first dance, so everyone could see it and we didn’t have to stop the party later on.


**BMS:** As I mentioned before, our desserts broke from tradition since we skipped the classic wedding cake and went with something that felt much more like us. We had a churro cart with someone making fresh churros on the spot, bubble tea, and a DIY Mexican candy bar. It was such a fun and personal touch.


**14. If you could give upcoming brides one tip about enjoying their wedding day, what would it be?**


**AO:** Share with your partner what might upset you on the wedding day. Some things are out of your control, but others aren’t—and if they know what could stress you out, they can help make sure it doesn’t happen. Also, remember that someone will always have an opinion, no matter what you do, so focus on what makes you happy. And while everyone says to stay by your partner all night, I don’t think that’s necessarily true—some of my favorite moments were with my girlfriends and my parents’ friends.


**BMS:** Our DJ gave us some great advice: where the couple goes, so will the crowd. If you want people on the dance floor, get on the dance floor. If you want people to join in at the photo booth, go snap some pics. It turned out to be so true! I’d also say the classic advice really works. Stick with your partner as much as possible, know that something will probably go wrong, and have someone designated to deal with it so you don’t have to. Most of all, just be present and soak it all in. The day goes by so fast.


**15. What advice would you give for savoring the season (and not stressing the small stuff)?**


**AO:** If you truly want to savor the season and can swing it with work and life, go on your honeymoon the Sunday after your wedding. You will be on such a high! I was so happy to jump straight into a vacation and spend more time with my husband. It was blissful to reminisce and start making more lifelong memories together.


**BMS:** We really tried to plan for every detail, right down to the exact cocktail recipes. But vendors are going to vendor, and some things will always be out of your control on the night of. The best thing you can do is be super clear about what actually matters to you. We had a month-of coordinator, and she was amazing. She knew all the little things and helped with details you don’t even think about but totally make a difference.That being said, if you’re doing something nontraditional, it might take people a minute to get your vision. Give them examples, photos, or notes to help bring it to life.
