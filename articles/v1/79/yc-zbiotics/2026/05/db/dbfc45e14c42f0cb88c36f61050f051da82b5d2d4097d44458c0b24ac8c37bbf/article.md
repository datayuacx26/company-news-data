# How ZBiotics Landed On Our First Product

#### **How ZBiotics came to be**


When Zack Abbott, PhD, first had the idea that led to[Pre-Alcohol](https://zbiotics.com/products/zbiotics?variant=26921653076068&selling_plan=2925297898) , he wasn't trying to solve rough mornings after drinking. He was looking for a home for a groundbreaking idea—one that would get people just as excited as he was.


Zack knew[genetic engineering](https://zbiotics.com/pages/how-it-works) could make people's lives healthier and happier in entirely new ways, but convincing consumers of that was a different challenge. As he puts it, "Ultimately, my mission was to advocate for the incredible uses of genetic engineering, and the best way to do that was not to give everybody a science lesson. It's much easier to have people open their minds when you have something they want and that they understand."


#### **The beginning of the ZBiotics idea**


[The story](https://zbiotics.com/blogs/journal/the-zbiotics-founding-story) starts over a decade ago, when Zack was deep into his PhD in Microbiology & Immunology at the University of Michigan—becoming an expert in bacterial and microbial genetics, and growing increasingly fascinated by the way genetic engineering could change microbes' behavior to make them perform specific, beneficial functions.


At the time, protein drugs known as "biologics" were transforming the pharmaceutical industry. Scientists were engineering bacteria to produce proteins, then isolating those proteins for use as drugs. It was a compelling proof of concept: genetic engineering could benefit human lives in new, powerful ways. But as Zack continued his research, he identified two roadblocks preventing the technology from reaching its full potential.


#### **Problem #1: the technology was expensive and unnecessarily complicated**


Isolating a protein from a microbe makes it unstable, requiring special conditions like constant cold storage. Many of the microbes used in the process—certain strains of *E. coli* , for instance—needed lengthy, costly purification before they were safe for human use. The result was a technology accessible only to large companies, most profitably applied to treating serious diseases. Everyday people rarely benefited directly.


Zack saw a different path. Simplify the approach, and genetic engineering could be broadened far beyond pharmaceuticals—particularly into improving healthy people's daily lives.


#### **Problem #2: genetic engineering had a serious public image problem**


Every trip to the grocery store drove it home: non-GMO labels were everywhere. Zack understood why the skepticism existed—the technology had long been associated with bad business practices (especially in the agricultural industry), and the tool had become inseparable from the industries that wielded it. But he also knew that the same technology could be used to do genuine good,[from fighting climate change to helping people live healthier lives.](https://zbiotics.com/blogs/journal/how-bioengineering-is-saving-the-planet)


He wanted to find a simpler, more transparent way to bring genetically engineered products to everyday consumers—and challenge the black-and-white conversation around GMOs along the way.


Then the core idea clicked. What if you cut out the middleman entirely? Instead of engineering a microbe to produce a protein and then isolating that protein as a drug, why not deliver the factory itself? He could take a probiotic bacteria—a safely ingestible one—and engineer it to make a protein directly inside the body, and make it readily available to consumers.


#### **The pitch**


Before finishing his PhD in 2015, Zack entered a pitch competition to stress-test the concept. His proposed use case: a genetically engineered probiotic to help the gut recover from radiation exposure. Standing on stage, he quickly sensed the room. It was, in his words, "a really cool scientific idea, but objectively, a pretty terrible idea for a consumer business. It was all science and just not at all interesting to people. Eyes were glazing over."


So at the end, he pivoted. He listed about ten other potential applications—increasing nutrient absorption, protecting astronauts' bodies in space, and almost as an aside, helping people feel better the morning after drinking.


The room shifted.


At the post-event reception, people sought Zack out to ask about that last idea specifically. The reaction was immediate and clear. If he wanted to change how people thought about genetic engineering, he needed to start with something tangible—a problem people had actually lived, and a solution they actually wanted.


#### **The prototype**


A few years later—after finishing his PhD and a stint at a contract research organization designing clinical trials—Zack was ready to make it real. He rented lab space in Berkeley, California, and got to work. For the next twelve months he worked around the clock, sometimes sleeping in a sleeping bag on the lab floor to see an experiment through to the end.


It paid off. After a year, he had the first prototype.


"As far as I know, I was the first human to intentionally ingest a genetically engineered probiotic," Zack recalls. He tried it before a night of drinking and felt noticeably better the next morning. Friends who tried it reported the same. After additional testing to validate the results, he knew he had something real.


"At that point, I realized I needed to[turn the science experiment into a real business](https://zbiotics.com/blogs/journal/the-zbiotics-founding-story) ," he says. "I started looking for a co-founder because I knew I couldn't do it all myself. That's when I was introduced to Stephen Lamb."


Zack spent months convincing Stephen to come on full-time, ultimately closing the deal by taking him out for drinks and letting him try the prototype. The results spoke for themselves. Three years later, with a larger team, a bigger lab, rigorous safety testing, more funding, and a patent on the technology, the world's first genetically engineered probiotic hit the market:[ZBiotics Pre-Alcohol](https://zbiotics.com/products/zbiotics?variant=26921653076068&selling_plan=2925297898) .


#### **The product**


Since launch, ZBiotics Pre-Alcohol has kept growing. More people discover it every day. And one of Zack's original goals—to give people a new model for what a GMO product could look like—is showing real signs of coming true.


The success of Pre-Alcohol depended on one question: would people accept a transparently genetically engineered product, if the company behind it used that technology responsibly and for the customer's benefit? From day one, we've printed “Proudly GMO” on every bottle. It hasn't slowed us down. If anything, the opposite.


"I had this hypothesis that people would accept the product if it was transparent, and it's exactly what we've seen," says Zack. "Most people don't actually care one way or the other about GMOs and just want a good product. But some people will even say they usually avoid GMOs—but that ZBiotics is different because it benefits them directly, delivers on what it promises, and the science makes sense. That's been really validating."


That direct, tangible benefit was always the point. When you improve someone's life in a way they can actually feel, it makes heads turn. Now that we know we can build that kind of product—and that people want it—it's time to think bigger.


That was always the other half of the original pitch: the underlying technology can do way more than just this one thing, and we’re excited to be working on it.
