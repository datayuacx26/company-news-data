# ZBiotics' First Patent: Where It All Began

In January 2021, ZBiotics received its first patent:[US10849938B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10849938B2/en?oq=10849938) .


At the time, we resisted the urge to overcelebrate. One patent alone isn't earth-shattering news—Thomas Edison amassed over 1,000 in his lifetime. But as a team of microbiologists, we knew that big things start small. And looking back, this one was a genuine milestone.


The patent marked an important step forward for ZBiotics’[genetically engineered probiotic technology](https://zbiotics.com/pages/how-it-works-genetic-engineering) .


### **What the patent covered**


We started developing this technology in 2016 and filed a provisional application in September 2017. More than three years later, the US Patent Office issued ZBiotics a full, valid patent. It covered two things:


#### 1. A system for building genetically engineered microbes (GEMs)


The first was a unique gene expression system for probiotic microorganisms—the foundational platform that makes everything else possible.


It started with understanding how a microbe's DNA affects its behavior. From there, we used genetic engineering to introduce specific changes to that DNA, enabling the microbe to perform an additional, useful function on top of its normal behavior. Before deciding what that function would be, significant work went into transforming a regular microbe into something we could reliably engineer—while keeping it safe and viable.


Think of it like training a horse. Before you can teach it to jump or rope, you first need to train it simply to accept a saddle and rider.


#### 2. A GEM that breaks down acetaldehyde


With the platform in place, the next step was defining a specific function and engineering it into the microbe. The application covered in this patent—the one at the heart of[ZBiotics' first product](https://zbiotics.com/blogs/journal/how-zbiotics-landed-on-our-first-product) —is the ability of a genetically engineered microbe to break down[acetaldehyde](https://zbiotics.com/blogs/journal/what-is-acetaldehyde-and-its-link-to-alcohol) , a byproduct of alcohol metabolism.


In technical terms: the robust, constitutive expression of acetaldehyde dehydrogenase, an enzyme that breaks down acetaldehyde. Strip away the jargon, and you have something genuinely useful:[a probiotic drink that helps you feel better the day after drinking](https://zbiotics.com/products/zbiotics?variant=26921653076068&selling_plan=2925297898) .


### What it meant for the future


Patents are always good news for a team like ours. As scientists, we work hard to invent technology that both enables new products and advances the state of our field, and patents validate the novelty of our work and protect against copycats. But this particular patent felt like more of a milestone.


When ZBiotics launched publicly in 2019, it marked two firsts: the first time this[technology had been used to target acetaldehyde](https://zbiotics.com/pages/how-it-works) , and the first time a genetically engineered probiotic had ever been made available in a consumer product.


This patent laid the groundwork for everything that followed—a new generation of GEMs, each engineered for its own specific function, forming the foundation of an entirely new category of products. At the time, we described it as a starting point. Looking back, that's exactly what it was.


Every big vision has to begin somewhere. For ZBiotics, it began with a unique combination of Gs, As, Cs, and Ts.
