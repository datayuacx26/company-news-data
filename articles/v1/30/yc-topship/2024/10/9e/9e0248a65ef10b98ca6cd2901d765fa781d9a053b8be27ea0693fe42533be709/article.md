As the year winds down and we enter the “ember months”—September, October, November, and December—shipping trends shift noticeably. For shippers and businesses alike, this time of year is marked by an uptick in activity, higher demand for goods, and more frequent shipments. We call this the “Peak Period.”


In this post, we’ll explore what the Peak Period means for you as a shipper, what to expect, and how to ensure hassle-free shipping during the busiest season of the year.


### **What is Peak Period?**


The Peak Period is the time of year when


[international](https://topship.africa/imports) and nationwide shipping are at their highest. For most countries, this occurs between September and December.


During this period, business owners typically


[import large quantities of goods](https://topship.africa/sea-freight) ahead of the holiday season. Likewise, individual shoppers and consumers seize the opportunity to take advantage of sales, promotions, discounts, and the holiday season in general. This results in a


[highly competitive and busy shipping environment](https://topship.africa/shop-&-ship) .


Understanding how the Peak Period works and what factors impact your shipping timelines and costs is critical to staying prepared and avoiding unnecessary delays.


### **What to Expect as Peak Period Approaches**


Here are some key things to keep in mind as we head into the Peak Period:


#### **1. Potential Changes in Shipping Costs**


As more people ship products during the Peak Period, shipping costs may fluctuate. This is due to a combination of higher demand and limited space on freight and cargo planes. It’s essential to keep this in mind and plan your shipments accordingly. Early planning and shipping before demand reaches its peak can sometimes help you secure more favourable shipping rates.


#### **2. Flight Delays and Rescheduling**


With more goods being shipped, airlines and shipping companies must manage higher-than-usual cargo volumes. This can sometimes lead to rescheduling or rerouting flights to accommodate the extra load. As a shipper, it’s wise to ship as early as possible to avoid last-minute delivery delays.


#### **3. Fuller Warehouses**


Warehouse capacity is another factor that can affect shipping during the Peak Period. With more goods being stored and processed, warehouses may operate at full capacity. One simple but often overlooked tip is to ensure your packages are correctly labelled with all the correct shipping details. This helps expedite processing and increases the chances of your shipment being prioritised.


#### **4. Possible Changes in Customs Clearance Costs**


The increase in shipping volume also has an impact on customs. During the Peak Period, customs clearance can take longer, and there might be changes in customs fees. Prepare for the possibility of increased clearance costs, and factor these into your budget ahead of time.


### **How to prepare for it**


The key to successfully navigating the Peak Period is


**planning** —and then planning some more. As a shipper, it’s important to anticipate potential changes in costs, timelines, and shipping availability and build that into your strategy.


Here are a few actionable tips to get you prepared:


- **Book your shipments early** : Don’t wait until the last minute. Shipping rates and timelines can change as the Peak Period progresses, so book your shipments as early as possible to lock in favourable terms.
