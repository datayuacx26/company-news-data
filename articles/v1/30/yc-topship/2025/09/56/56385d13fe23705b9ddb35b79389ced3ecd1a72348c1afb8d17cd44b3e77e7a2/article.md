### **1. Understanding Shipping Delays in Nigeria**


Shipping delays in Nigeria are a longstanding issue that continues to affect importers and exporters. According to the Lagos Chamber of Commerce and Industry (LCCI), inefficiencies at Nigerian ports cost businesses an estimated


**$10 billion annually** in lost revenue and extra expenses. Source: Vanguard Nigeria.


These delays are not caused by one single issue but a combination of congestion, poor infrastructure, customs bottlenecks, and human-related challenges.


### **2. Port Congestion: The Number One Culprit**


Lagos ports Apapa and Tin Can handle more than


**80% of Nigeria’s imports** \[Source: NPA Annual Report\]. With such high volumes of cargo funneled through limited facilities, congestion is inevitable.


Ships often wait offshore for days or weeks before berthing. Trucks then face long queues outside the terminals. In fact, a World Bank report described Apapa port as one of the least efficient in Africa, with average container dwell times stretching over 20 days, compared to 4 days in Durban, South Africa.


This inefficiency directly contributes to shipping delays and increased costs for businesses.


### **3. Clearing Delays: Customs and Documentation Challenges**


Even after cargo berths, clearing goods is another hurdle. Nigerian customs procedures are known for being slow, highly bureaucratic, and sometimes inconsistent.


Importers frequently face:


- Documentation errors that force re-submission.


- Lengthy inspection processes that can take days.


- Multiple agencies demanding approvals (Customs, NAFDAC, SON, etc.).


A study by the Nigerian Economic Summit Group highlighted that importers often spend


**2–4 weeks clearing goods** due to these bureaucratic bottlenecks.


### **4. Infrastructural Limitations at Nigerian Ports**


Most Nigerian ports lack modern equipment and digital systems. Outdated cranes, poor container handling capacity, and limited warehousing facilities all contribute to inefficiencies.


Road infrastructure is also a nightmare. The notorious Apapa gridlock is caused by poor road conditions, limited truck parking, and inadequate rail connections. Until recently, moving containers in and out of Apapa by truck could take


**up to 10 days** .


### **5. Human Factors: Strikes, Bureaucracy, and Corruption**


Delays aren’t just caused by physical infrastructure; human factors play a huge role:


- Strikes by port workers, truck drivers, or customs officers frequently paralyze operations.


- Bureaucracy adds unnecessary steps to clearance.


- Corruption often means that those unwilling to pay “facilitation fees” face even longer delays.


Transparency International consistently ranks Nigerian ports as high-risk zones for corrupt practices, adding to importer frustration.


### **6. Seasonal Surges and Global Supply Chain Disruptions**


Global supply chain issues also trickle down to Nigeria. During peak seasons (such as the end-of-year holidays), cargo volumes surge dramatically, worsening congestion.


For example, in December 2022, Nigerian ports recorded a


**40% increase in cargo volumes** , which led to clearance times nearly doubling. Coupled with global shipping disruptions caused by the pandemic and geopolitical issues like the Russia-Ukraine war, Nigerian importers often face compounded delays.


### **7. Impact of Shipping Delays on Businesses**


The ripple effects of port delays are massive:


- **Increased Costs** : Importers pay extra demurrage and storage fees.


- **Stock Shortages** : Retailers struggle to keep shelves stocked.


- **Missed Deadlines** : Manufacturers dependent on imported raw materials face production downtime.


- **Reduced Competitiveness** : Nigerian businesses pay more and deliver slower than their international counterparts.


According to PwC Nigeria, port inefficiencies cost the Nigerian economy


**N2.5 trillion annually** in lost revenue.


### **8. How to Minimize Delays When Shipping to Nigeria**


While you can’t control all external factors, there are steps importers can take:


- Ensure proper documentation before cargo arrives.


- Work with experienced clearing agents who understand the system.


- Plan shipments early, especially before peak seasons.


- Use bonded terminals or inland container depots to ease congestion.


- Leverage digital shipping platforms for real-time tracking and faster coordination.
