## **1. Understanding Nigeria’s Customs System**


The Nigeria Customs Service (NCS) is the government body responsible for regulating and facilitating trade, collecting duties, and preventing smuggling. Every shipment entering Nigeria must be inspected and cleared by Customs before it can be released.


Customs clearance is not just a formality, it is a legal requirement under the


**Customs and Excise Management Act (CEMA)** . The NCS enforces import guidelines that protect the economy, ensure fair competition, and prevent the importation of prohibited items.


## **2. Key Agencies Involved in Customs Clearance**


When clearing goods in Nigeria, you’ll likely deal with multiple agencies beyond Customs, including:


- **Nigeria Customs Service (NCS)** – Oversees inspections and collects duties.


- **Nigeria Ports Authority (NPA)** – Manages port operations.


- **Standards Organisation of Nigeria (SON)** – Ensures products meet quality standards.


- **National Agency for Food and Drug Administration and Control (NAFDAC)** – Regulates food, drugs, and cosmetics.


- **Nigeria Agricultural Quarantine Service (NAQS)** – Handles agricultural and animal imports.


Knowing the right agency to engage with depending on your shipment type can significantly speed up clearance.


## **3. Step-by-Step Customs Clearance Process in Nigeria**


Here’s what happens from the moment your goods arrive in Nigeria to when they’re released:


**Step 1 – Pre-Arrival Documentation**


- Prepare your Bill of Lading/Airway Bill, Commercial Invoice, Packing List, and Form M (mandatory for imports).


- Ensure your goods are not on Nigeria’s list of prohibited imports.


**Step 2 – Arrival of Goods**


- Goods arrive at port or airport and are registered in the NCS system.


**Step 3 – Customs Declaration**


- Submit an Import Declaration Form (IDF) via the Nigeria Single Window Trade Portal.


**Step 4 – Risk Assessment**


- Customs assigns your shipment to Green, Yellow, or Red Lane:


- Green Lane: Cleared without inspection.


- Yellow Lane: Documentation review required.


- Red Lane: Full physical inspection.


**Step 5 – Duty & Tax Assessment**


- Customs calculates applicable import duties, VAT, levies, and other charges.


**Step 6 – Payment**


- Pay duties and taxes through an approved bank.


**Step 7 – Inspection & Release**


- Customs inspects (if required), stamps clearance, and releases goods.


**Pro Tip** : Always have a licensed customs agent handle your clearance to avoid costly errors.


## **4. Import Duties, Taxes, and Levies**


The cost of clearing goods depends on:


- **Customs Duty** – A percentage of the Cost, Insurance, and Freight (CIF) value.


- **Value Added Tax (VAT)** – Currently 7.5% on most imports.


- **Levies** – Vary by product category.


- **Excise Duties** – On specific goods like alcohol, tobacco, and luxury items.


**Example** : If you import electronics valued at ₦5 million, you might pay 20% duty + 7.5% VAT + other applicable fees.


## **5. Common Mistakes That Cause Delays**


- Incomplete documentation


- Under-declaring value to reduce duties (this can result in seizure)


- Importing restricted/prohibited items without proper permits


- Choosing inexperienced clearing agents


- Ignoring SONCAP/NAFDAC certifications for regulated goods


## **6. Tips for Smooth Customs Clearance**


- **Prepare Documents Early** – Start the clearance process before goods arrive.


- **Hire Licensed Agents** – Use NCS-approved agents for legitimacy.


- **Verify HS Codes** – Correct classification prevents overpayment or penalties.


- **Avoid Peak Season Delays** – Import before major holidays or year-end rush.


- **Use Digital Tracking** – Monitor clearance progress online.
