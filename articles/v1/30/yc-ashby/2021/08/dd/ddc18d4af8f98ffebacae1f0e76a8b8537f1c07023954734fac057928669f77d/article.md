# How Thoughtful Communication Makes Us Unreasonably Productive

1


Recurring open-ended meetings can make sense on an ad-hoc or infrequent basis (e.g., quarterly) to foster collaboration that might not happen otherwise. We currently don't see the need for them.


2


Ephemeral communication is communication where it's ok that there's no record of it and ok that it can't be triaged by the person(s) receiving the communication.


3


Negative customer feedback is important and often sparks a productive discussion that we don't want lost, so we don't use Slack to share it.


4


At our stage, an individual can be the owner of many things, but we avoid overlapping ownership


5


There may be some exceptions that make sense for #social. For example, you're welcome to use it to sign off for the evening (just like you might say bye when leaving the office). In short, keep #social, social.


6


We don't count shared channels with customers and vendors.


7


We use Slack messages to tag automated alerts with links to the related issue and try to factor that out

Focus time is measured as a period of more than two hours of uninterrupted time between meetings.


10


A focus day is a day in which there are 6 or more hours of focus time.
